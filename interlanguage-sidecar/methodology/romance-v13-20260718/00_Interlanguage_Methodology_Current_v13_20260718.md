# Interlanguage and Mathematical Translation Methodology Sidecar

## Public archive map and bounded research checkpoints

Version 0.13, 18 July 2026

This record is the methodology, provenance, corpus-control, and programme-state sidecar for the mathematical transcription and translation archive. It preserves the source-body baselines, automata and terminology work, decision and audit methods, language-manager state, reproducible checks, and bounded interlanguage experiments needed to understand the public readers honestly.

The sidecar is not a single translation and its public title is intentionally broader than the newest addition. Full mathematical readers remain on the author or series records; this record explains how they were built, what is actually checked, and what remains open.

## Romance checkpoint boundary

The current Romance package is a public-safe projection of a larger private operational tree.

- WordWeb: 60 concepts, 106 explicit senses, 39 extension nodes, 811 metadata-only evidence records, 954 sense-by-cohort rows, 78 supported senses, and 28 explicit gaps.
- Corpus metadata: 153 records, including 147 primary unique records and six aliases; 61 named standards or varieties, of which 11 have an active body and 50 remain zero-body.
- Controlled Romance: eight bounded source-keyed units, 19 aggregate PDF pages, and a continuation cursor at German R823 authority line 21309. The package includes the two-page T008 unit and its editable source and controls.
- Governance: an append-only decision ledger, validator, language-profile decisions, acceptance matrix, and reproducibility scripts.
- Rumantsch Grischun: metadata and QA for one deduplicated 30-page official school-mathematics body. Source bodies are excluded because redistribution rights are unresolved.

There are zero native-language validations, zero human intelligibility observations, zero pilot-eligible rows, zero form promotions, and no rights-clearance claim. Diagnostic proximity and access values are design evidence, not measured comprehension.

## Interslavic checkpoint boundary

The Noether Interslavic production corpus contains 221 canonical Latin-script TeX units and 221 paired Cyrillic-script units. The methodology package records normalization Tranches 002A through 007 and the final streaming audit.

Tranches 003-007 record 1,111 replacements across 247 distinct units. They cover the reviewed correspondence family, a declared simultaneity policy, four tokenized context queues, a bounded algebraic-ring competitor, and one final diacritic correction. The final audit covers all 221 Latin units. The remaining explicit external-authority blocker is the mathematical sense extension of 353 `vazi*|vaze*` probes in 101 files.

Mechanical closure, successful builds, render integrity, internal linguistic normalization, source fidelity, and community acceptance are separate statuses. This release verifies the first four only within each declared tranche; it does not claim native-speaker approval, sentence-by-sentence German-source certification, or community standardization.

## Reproducibility controls

The operating method requires:

- stable source, target, structural, decision, and difficulty IDs;
- machine-readable indices for sections, paragraphs, statements, proofs, equations, notes, and cross-references;
- append-only decision logs recording alternatives, evidence, uncertainty, consequences, and supersession;
- append-only difficulty and failure ledgers retaining hard units, failed approaches, resolutions, residual risks, and recurrence cues;
- explicit authority hashes, source and target locators, continuation cursors, build evidence, render review, and honest publication labels;
- a public projection separated from private paths, raw rights-restricted bodies, credentials, and internal task identifiers.

CSV is retained for easy inspection. JSON, JSONL, SQLite, or another schema-capable representation is required when hierarchy or one-to-many relations cannot be represented faithfully in a flat table.

## Claim boundary

The included materials are working translations, source controls, corpus evidence, normalization experiments, and reproducible audit artifacts at explicitly bounded levels. They are not critical editions, peer review, proof certification, native-language endorsement, universal mathematical validation, rights determinations, or completed translations unless a particular file states a narrower verified completion claim.

Generated OCR, VLM, and CUDA extraction are witnesses or locators unless a source-audited body is explicitly identified. A clean build does not establish linguistic correctness. A normalized interlanguage form does not establish community acceptance. A full-looking reader may still contain inherited or draft authority layers, which must remain visible.
