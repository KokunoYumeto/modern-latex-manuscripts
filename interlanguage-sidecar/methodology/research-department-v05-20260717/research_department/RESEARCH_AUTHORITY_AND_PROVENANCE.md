## Conflict-resolution rule

When artifacts disagree:

1. Preserve the earlier state as archaeology.
2. Identify the input versions, row identity rules, counting unit, and provenance tier.
3. Prefer a later artifact only when it explicitly reconciles the discrepancy or reproduces the authoritative inputs.
4. Keep projections, candidates, and current observations separate.
5. Never let “certified,” “reviewed,” or “authoritative” in an internal package imply community validation.

## Final weighted-object reconciliation

The debate ended with a layered answer:

- The language-family tree is the correct object for genealogical dependence, branch coverage, and diminishing returns.
- The typed evidence graph is the correct object for source-to-decision provenance and separate support/adverse/gap/candidate paths.
- Weighted automata are a legitimate later formalization only for a specified extraction or routing transducer. Calling a graph an automaton does not by itself define automaton semantics.

The final v3 and v6.2 packages therefore remain useful as evidence-graph serializations. Their names are preserved for provenance; their operational type is stated accurately here.

It ignores source class, support-versus-candidate status, gaps, row review, breadth, script policy, external review, and human comprehension. It is rejected as a readiness measure.
