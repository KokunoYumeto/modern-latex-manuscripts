# Interlinguistic method synthesis

This is the shared method for every language manager and every work. It governs translation into the manager’s languages; it is not a separate translation lane.

## 1. Research objective

Construct and maintain translation registers that maximize **marginal intelligibility over the dominant-language baseline** while preserving source meaning, mathematical function, local linguistic legitimacy, script integrity, and an inspectable evidence trail.

A bridge or controlled register is not good because it is globally familiar or resembles the dominant language. It is useful when it buys an additional reader cohort a usable channel without materially degrading service to existing readers.

The final judge is human comprehension. Corpus counts, source attestations, model geometry, form similarity, branch balance, and AI review are instruments and diagnostics.

## 2. Decide the intervention before constructing it

Use the family’s standards, scripts, and actual mutual intelligibility, not genetic labels alone.

| Situation | Default intervention |
| --- | --- |
| Related languages, multiple standards, substantial passive intelligibility | Zonal bridge or coordinated interlanguage register |
| One strong standard already serves the region | Controlled technical register in that standard |
| Closely connected register split mainly by scripts | Script bridge with deterministic conversion and invariant checks |
| High internal diversity or low mutual intelligibility | Local standards plus concept crosswalks; no pan-language claim |
| Insufficient native technical sources | Source-intake plan only |

The source floor is lexicographic: no amount of model output or formal scoring compensates for missing native target-language evidence.

Examples in the present programme:

- Slavic and Romance can support zonal comparison and bridge work.
- Arabic and Malay/Indonesian are controlled-register problems, not invitations to invent replacement languages.
- CJK is a local-standard and crosswalk problem, not a pan-CJK language.
- Horn of Africa, West Africa, Pacific, Indigenous, creole, and sign-language work must be partitioned by actual linguistic and community structure rather than administrative convenience.

## 3. Start from concepts, not English strings

Every record begins with a controlled concept and sense, then maps source and target realizations.

Required concept layers:

1. **Proof grammar** — therefore, however, let, suppose, follows, is called, remains.
2. **Curriculum mathematics** — ring, field, ideal, module, homomorphism, quotient.
3. **Work-specific historical register** — the concepts and metaphors of Noether, SGA, or another source corpus.

The 67-row C2 spine is the shared comparison minimum, not a universal vocabulary. Each lane must return `witnessed`, `gap`, or `not_applicable`; it must never invent a form merely to fill a cell.

The current consolidated interface contains 216 concepts across 25 language columns. It is a map and comparison surface. Raw cells, candidate cells, competitors, and adverse items retain their tiers.

## 4. Preserve evidence channels

For every concept-candidate-language tuple, record these independently:

- `support`: a permitted source supports the candidate in the intended sense.
- `candidate`: the form is useful for discovery or context review but is not yet a witness.
- `competitor`: the language uses a different form for the same concept.
- `adverse`: the form collides with another sense, register, script behavior, or community constraint.
- `absence`: the search has not produced evidence either way.
- `veto`: a typed prohibition such as false sense, do-not-inherit, or external-authority hold.

A competitor is not absence. Adverse evidence never becomes a negative count that enough positive mass can outvote. A collision can veto a candidate regardless of the number of weak hits.

## 5. Separate linkage, witness, and review

These statuses do not imply one another:

1. `linked_to_concept`
2. `candidate_after_context_check`
3. `concept_shelf_witness`
4. `row_verified_witness`
5. `internally_reviewed_for_use`
6. `externally_reviewed_for_use`

Generated text, bilingual drafts, dictionary prefix matches, folded spellings, and model suggestions may discover a candidate. They do not witness living usage.

An internal AI agreement can raise confidence that a record is correctly processed. It cannot set status 6.

## 6. Evidence collection loop

For each work and target language:

1. Freeze the current source and target state, including hashes and build identity.
2. Segment the source by stable work, section, and unit IDs.
3. Link each technical term and proof connective to a controlled concept and intended sense.
4. Search native sources, retaining context windows and bibliographic provenance.
5. Classify each return by source class and permitted use.
6. Run sense, homograph, false-friend, script, and register checks.
7. Record support, competitors, adverse evidence, and explicit gaps separately.
8. Compute branch diagnostics only after declaring the unit of evidence and target distribution.
9. Draft a candidate decision and reviewer question; do not auto-promote.
10. Apply accepted internal decisions to translations with source-fidelity, terminology, script, and build gates.
11. Preserve the pre-change state and the diff.
12. Submit held governance questions for appropriate external/community review.

This loop repeats for Noether, SGA, and every other work. A manager’s corpus and word web are shared infrastructure for all of its translations.

## 7. Sense and adverse-evidence controls

Mechanical lexical search is unsafe without context.

Standing F14 trap concepts:

- `modulus`: often a module/modulo collision.
- `ground form`: often generic “fundamental/basic form” or differential geometry.
- `complete system`: often ODE, residue-system, or generic completeness.
- `binary form`: often binary code or neural-computation material.
- `covariant` / `contravariant`: often tensor or category-theory senses rather than invariant theory.

Standing homograph control: a pan-branch form requires a cross-branch sense check. The Polish `jednak` “however” versus Croatian/Serbian `jednak` “equal” case is the canonical warning.

Standing normalization control: diacritic-folded variants may assist search but may not be summed as independent evidence.

## 8. Branch, dominance, and fairness controls

Declare what one unit means: document, source, language, cohort, concept-language presence, or row-verified witness. Never mix units in one statistic.

Use raw branch mass to expose the recorded evidence distribution. Use saturation within a language to stop repeated sources from masquerading as independent languages. Use family-tree edge coverage or a declared dependence model to measure marginal breadth.

Keep these questions separate:

- How much permitted evidence exists?
- How many independent branches or cohorts does it cover?
- How far does its distribution deviate from the declared fairness target?
- Which cohorts gain over the dominant baseline?
- What adverse or governance constraints remain?

Population weights are sensitivity analyses unless a community governance target explicitly adopts them.

## 9. Scripts and registers are first-class objects

Each lane declares:

- canonical target scripts and encodings;
- deterministic conversions, if any;
- invariants that conversion must preserve;
- register level and audience;
- formula/prose boundary;
- treatment of names, citations, quotations, and source-language fragments;
- punctuation, directionality, and line-breaking behavior;
- build and render checks.

In a dual-script lane, the second script is not a casual transliteration. It is a derived artifact with synchronization gates.

In RTL lanes, bidi behavior, mirrored punctuation, formula direction, embedded Latin identifiers, and citation layout are validation targets.

## 10. The global mathematical register

Use it as a comparator column, not a new manager and not an English disguise.

It has three layers:

1. Formula and notation: already substantially global.
2. Proof prose: a small controlled register, but highly branch-divergent in connectives.
3. Concept terms: partly international and measurable per concept.

The current mechanical attractor probe finds 16 of 59 tested concepts in a folded cross-family basin. That is a candidate-generation signal only. The 15-connective pilot finds no cross-family lexical attractor; formula substitutes such as `∃`, `∴`, `⇒`, and `≝` should be shown first where mathematically appropriate.

The operative comparison is:

> Does the zonal or local form produce more marginal intelligibility for the target readers than the global/international candidate, after dominance and adverse-evidence controls?

English is always a dominance-bearing carrier in this comparison, never a neutral default.

## 11. Model geometry and theoretical research

The ChatGPT Pro reconstruction provides a serious research programme, not current translation authority.

- **Interlingual Local Overlap (ILO)**: test whether aligned concepts preserve local semantic neighborhoods across languages and whether that predicts comprehension.
- **False-friend risk**: test the hypothesis that high form similarity combined with semantic distance predicts errors.
- **Regularized family barycenter**: model a bridge as a family-central solution with learnability and anti-dominance constraints, not as reconstruction of a proto-language.
- **Intrinsic dimension, persistent topology, and bridge complexes**: future comparative instruments only.
- **Latent-pivot audit**: mandatory before using multilingual model geometry, because model spaces may be English-shaped.

None of these model-geometry measurements has been run as validation on the present translation corpus. They may propose experiments and candidates; they cannot currently promote terms.

## 12. Decision output

Every consequential term or register decision should produce:

- source and work identity;
- controlled concept and intended sense;
- target language, script, and register;
- candidate forms and variants;
- evidence records by channel and provenance;
- declared branch/cohort model and diagnostic values;
- dominant-baseline comparison;
- adverse and veto controls;
- decision status and rationale;
- unresolved reviewer question;
- invariants and validation gates;
- pre-change state and applied diff, if any.

Use `OPERATIONAL_DECISION_INTERFACE.schema.json` for the machine-readable form.

## 13. Promotion rule

No term is promoted because one scalar is high. A production decision requires all applicable hard gates:

- source floor met;
- intended sense verified in context;
- target-language evidence permitted for the claimed use;
- competitors and adverse evidence handled;
- dominance and branch/cohort effects reported;
- script/register invariants defined;
- translation/source fidelity gates pass;
- internal review completed;
- external review or human-comprehension evidence present when the claim requires community legitimacy or intelligibility.

Until then, the correct output is a candidate, a held decision, a gap, or a reviewer question.
