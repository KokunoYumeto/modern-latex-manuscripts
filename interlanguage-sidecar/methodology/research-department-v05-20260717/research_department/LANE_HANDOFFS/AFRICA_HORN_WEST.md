# Horn of Africa / West Africa research handoff

## Manager scope

The manager translates every work into selected Horn-of-Africa and West-African target languages and organizes the corresponding corpora. This is one administrative manager, not one language family and not a pan-African construction programme.

Horn and West Africa must be separate research subprogrammes, and each must be subdivided further by actual language family, standard, script, educational ecology, and community.

## Current research state

- The current unified v6.2 package has no mature lane evidence for this manager’s scope.
- Absence of indexed evidence is a source-intake state, not adverse evidence and not permission to generate terminology.
- The programme’s siting rule rejects broad pan-family umbrellas where diversity is high and mutual intelligibility low.
- The default intervention is local standards plus cross-language concept crosswalks.

## Required controls

- Name every target language, standard, and audience before translation work begins.
- Map actual families and contact zones; never use geography as a branch model.
- Maintain Ge’ez, Arabic, Latin, N’Ko, Ajami, and other applicable script systems as distinct, tested sublanes rather than generic “non-Latin” variants.
- Include institutional PDFs, textbooks, university notes, and community terminology even when extraction is harder; machine-readability cannot become a bias against the lane.
- Distinguish colonial-language source material from native target-language witnesses.
- Model lingua-franca baselines such as English, French, Arabic, Amharic, Somali, Hausa, Swahili, or others only where relevant to a declared target community; none is a neutral African default.
- Do not aggregate population counts or geography into a fairness target without community governance.
- Treat sign languages, creoles, and Indigenous languages as separate programmes unless a concrete linguistic and community rationale establishes a shared sublane.

## Manager output

Begin with a target inventory, source-floor ledger, script/register policy, and concept crosswalk. Translation can proceed for targets with sufficient source and reviewer infrastructure. Where the floor is not met, the correct deliverable is a source-intake and gap record—not invented pan-African terminology.
