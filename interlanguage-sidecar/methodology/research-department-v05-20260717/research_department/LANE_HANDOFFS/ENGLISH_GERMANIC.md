# English / Germanic research handoff

## Manager scope

The manager translates every work into English and any selected Germanic targets. English is a target because not every source is in English. German may be a source, a target, or a comparison language depending on the work; those roles must not be conflated.

## Current research state

- German canonical/source authority is valuable for normalizing concepts in the Noether corpus. It does not witness English or another target language’s usage.
- Open Logic Project material supplies English source-side proof-literacy and concept normalization. It does not automatically witness other targets.
- The global-register comparator uses English as a dominance-bearing carrier, never a neutral baseline.
- The global connective pilot found no cross-family lexical attractor in its current 15 rows. Formula substitutes such as `∃`, `∴`, `⇒`, and `≝` should be listed first where mathematically exact.
- Proof prose remains a controlled-register problem even where concept nouns are international.

## Required controls

- Distinguish original-source English, translated English, source-side explanatory material, and target-language witness.
- Keep English dominance visible in any global or cross-family selection objective.
- Do not use “everyone speaks English” as a source-floor, accessibility, or fidelity argument.
- For non-English Germanic targets, create independent native source and terminology records; English cognates are candidates, not witnesses.
- Preserve source-language concepts and quotations where the source’s historical terminology matters.
- Record register level: historical mathematical prose, modern scholarly prose, educational prose, and controlled global proof prose are distinct.

## Manager output

Maintain an English/Germanic concept web that can accept French SGA, German Noether, and other sources directly. English should not be the mandatory pivot for the other managers; it is one target and one comparator column.
