# Malay / Southeast Asian / Pacific research handoff

## Manager scope

The manager translates every work into the selected Malay/Indonesian, Southeast Asian, and Pacific targets. This is an administrative grouping. Malay and Indonesian are closely related standard/register work; “Southeast Asian” and “Pacific” cover many unrelated families and require separate sublanes.

## Current research state

- The existing Malay-Indonesian table contains 60 source/body candidate rows in unified v6.2.
- That package reports 96.1 despite zero support rows and 60 candidates. Treat every row at its actual candidate provenance; ignore the readiness number.
- The siting analysis treats Malay/Indonesian primarily as a controlled technical-register and standard-pair problem.
- Current evidence does not justify a pan-Southeast-Asian or pan-Pacific bridge.

## Required controls

- Maintain Malay and Indonesian standards separately while recording genuinely shared forms and register differences.
- Declare additional SEA and Pacific targets by language and family; do not inherit Malay evidence into them.
- Use native educational and mathematical sources per target, including non-HTML/PDF sources under a documented extraction and context-review process.
- Model colonial and dominant-language influence explicitly; international-looking vocabulary may be hegemon-mediated rather than mutually transparent.
- Record Latin and non-Latin scripts, orthographies, and local typography per sublane.
- Use local standards plus crosswalks for unrelated languages.
- Require community-sensitive governance before proposing any new regional bridge.

## Manager output

The manager maintains one coordination index with independent linguistic sublanes. Reusable concept IDs and formula mappings are shared infrastructure; witness status, terminology promotion, and translation QA remain target-specific.
