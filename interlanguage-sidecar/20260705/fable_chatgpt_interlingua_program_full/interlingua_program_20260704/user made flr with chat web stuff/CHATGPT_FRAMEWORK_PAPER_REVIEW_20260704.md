## Verdict

The draft is viable as a methods paper if it is presented as **artifact-grounded methodology**, not as validation of a zonal language. Its strongest contribution is not the speculative bridge to multilingual model geometry; it is the measured corpus-process discipline:

1. independently grown term spines diverged by source genre;
2. the Interslavic lane's per-term evidence was effectively one-branch before backfill;
3. local backfill moved the branch-balance statistic substantially;
4. cross-system triangulation caught concrete concept-linking errors;
5. adverse evidence had to be represented as a typed channel rather than a negative scalar.

Those five results are sufficiently concrete to support a workshop/preprint-style methods paper. The paper should not yet be framed as a claim about human intelligibility, because no external review or comprehension study has returned.

## Required edits before circulation

### 1. Separate “measured result” from “interpretive bridge” in every result section

The draft currently does this mostly well, but it should become explicit at the beginning of Sections 4--8:

- **Measured:** the artifact count, ledger count, branch mass, or defect count.
- **Interpreted as:** source-genre drift, dominance drift, triangulation value, adverse-evidence need.
- **Not claimed:** external validity, community acceptance, or human comprehension.

This will prevent over-reading of phrases such as “pan-family corpus,” “recognizability,” and “access gain.”

### 3. Mark population weighting as sensitivity only

The paper should not use population weighting in any headline claim. The v3 score file now source-pins a Europe-native speaker proxy, but this remains a policy choice rather than a neutral fact. If included, state:

The main defensible weighting in the paper is branch-equal or dependence-corrected branch coverage, because the paper’s target is family-central evidence breadth, not demographic maximization.

## Abstract edit

The current abstract is strong but overfull. Suggested compression:

> We report an AI-assisted interlinguistic corpus pipeline as a measurable evidence system for zonal auxiliary-language construction. The pipeline’s operational access-gain ledger corresponds to a bridge-language objective over recognizability, source coverage, dominance regularization, and adverse evidence. Measuring the pipeline on its own artifacts yields five results: lane-specific term spines diverge by source genre; the Interslavic term ledger was initially an effective single-branch witness system; local West/South backfill substantially improves but does not close the gap; cross-system triangulation catches concrete concept-linking defects but is not external validation; and adverse evidence requires a typed channel distinct from support. We give a lexicographic siting model and publish all pre-repair and post-repair artifacts with hashes. No term is certified; the contribution is a method for making bridge-language construction auditable before community review.
