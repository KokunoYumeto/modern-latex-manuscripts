# Zonal auxiliary-language construction as measurable evidence discipline
## An AI-assisted interlinguistic corpus case study

## Abstract

We report an AI-assisted interlinguistic corpus pipeline as a measurable evidence system for zonal auxiliary-language construction. The pipeline's operational access-gain ledger corresponds to a bridge-language objective over recognizability, source coverage, dominance regularization, and adverse evidence. Measuring the pipeline on its own artifacts yields five results: lane-specific term spines diverge by source genre; the Interslavic term ledger was initially an effective single-branch witness system; local West/South backfill substantially improves but does not close the gap; cross-system triangulation catches concrete concept-linking defects but is not external validation; and adverse evidence requires a typed channel distinct from support. We give a lexicographic siting model and publish all pre-repair and post-repair artifacts with hashes. No term is certified; the contribution is a method for making bridge-language construction auditable before community review.

## 1. Introduction

Constructed bridge systems for related language communities are usually discussed in one of two registers: the philological tradition of zonal auxiliary languages, with a century of design practice and community governance; or, recently, the representation geometry of multilingual language models, which measures cross-lingual semantic alignment at scale. This paper reports what happened when the two were joined over a working system — an AI-assisted pipeline that has produced substantial technical corpora in an existing zonal language and staged construction programs across a dozen further language families, under written evidence and governance rules.

The contribution is threefold. First, a correspondence: the pipeline's operational selection record and the theoretical bridge-design objective are the same object in two vocabularies, each side supplying terms the other lacks (Section 2), under shared discipline (Section 3). Second, measurements: joining the vocabularies immediately produced five results on the pipeline's own artifacts (Sections 4–8) — including two cases where the audit found variance *inside* the generated corpus, not only against external shelves. Third, a siting model that decides which families warrant a bridge, a controlled register, a script layer, or deliberate non-intervention, with source availability as a lexicographic gate (Section 9). Sections 10–11 state the limits and the availability of the full record. Throughout, the posture is that of a case-study methods paper: internal triangulation before external review, and no claim of authority over any community's language.

## 2. Two vocabularies, one objective

Two bodies of work grew independently around the same problem and named its parts differently.

The first is an operating construction pipeline. Between 2026-06 and 2026-07 an AI-assisted workflow produced, among other artifacts, a complete Interslavic rendering of Emmy Noether's collected papers in both Latin and Cyrillic scripts, a Spanish source-audited edition, a French translation through forty papers, and staged construction programs for Pan-Romance, controlled Arabic, Persianate, Hindustani, Malay-Indonesian and further language-family lanes. The pipeline is governed by written operating rules: every term requires a source witness or a declared coinage; term choices are motivated and logged; script variants are deterministic build artifacts with validators; terms are promoted only under corpus pressure; and every artifact remains forkable, correctable and rejectable by the communities it might serve. Mid-program, the pipeline made its selection criterion explicit as a typed decision record, the *access-gain ledger*: a contested choice must state which reader cohorts gain a materially new usable channel, whether main-register readers remain served, how strongly sources support the choice, and what penalties apply for dominance, exclusion, opacity and ambiguity. The ledger is used as a record, not a scalar: its terms are named, argued and pointed at evidence, not summed.

The second body of work is a theoretical framework for bridge-language design, assembled from interlinguistics, intercomprehension research, controlled-natural-language studies, terminology governance and the representation geometry of multilingual language models. In that vocabulary, a zonal auxiliary language is an optimization object: for each concept one seeks a surface form that maximizes passive recognizability and semantic-neighborhood preservation across the source languages, sits near a regularized family barycenter rather than at any single member, and minimizes false-friend risk and acquisition cost. Multilingual model geometry appears as an instrument for measuring cross-lingual alignment — never as an oracle, because such models carry their own dominant-language bias.

The central observation of this paper is that these are the same object in two vocabularies. The correspondence is term-for-term. Passive recognizability and the ledger's marginal inter-intelligibility gain are the theory's recognizability and neighborhood-preservation terms. The Interslavic tradition's subgroup voting is a hand-executed regularized barycenter. The false-friend lists that zonal projects have curated for decades are the ambiguity penalty. The pipeline's rule that no bridge should be built where a strong written standard already serves its readers is the regularizer that the theoretical objective needs but rarely states. And the correspondence is productive in both directions. Practice contributes two terms the theory lacks: *source strength* — evidence quality as a first-class variable, because a selection criterion is useless where native technical sources are missing — and *lingua-franca gap*, the siting precondition that asks whether a new channel is needed at all. Theory contributes measurable counterparts for the ledger's editorial terms: neighborhood-overlap scores for recognizability, form-similarity-times-semantic-distance for false-friend risk, distance-from-dominant-member for the dominance penalty.

The correspondence extends to the failure mode. Every level of the program exhibits the same characteristic drift: a family centroid estimated from unbalanced evidence collapses toward the dominant member. In the human lanes this appears as the bridge that is "just Spanish in softened spelling," the Interslavic that reads as rough Russian, the Malay-Indonesian register that quietly becomes Indonesian; on the machine side it appears as multilingual models routing meaning through representation space shaped by their dominant training language. Section 5 reports a measured instance. The practical cure is also shared: regularize explicitly against the dominant member, and weight evidence by independence, not by volume — many witnesses from one branch are not many witnesses.

## 3. Objects and discipline

The program distinguishes four intervention types, and the siting decision is made before any language work begins. A *zonal auxiliary language* is warranted where a family is split across many written standards with high mutual recognizability (Slavic, Romance). A *controlled technical register* is warranted where one strong standard already serves readers and the need is stable technical prose, not a new language (Arabic, Chinese, the Malay-Indonesian standard pair). A *script bridge* is warranted where one register is divided by writing systems (Hindustani; the Turkic alphabet layer). Where internal diversity is high and mutual recognizability low, the correct output is *local standards with crosswalks*, and pan-family umbrellas are refused outright (Chinese-Japanese-Korean, Niger-Congo, the Indigenous American languages). Two rules order the decision: the source floor is lexicographic — below a minimum of native technical source evidence, no bridge recommendation may be issued, only a source-intake plan — and build type follows sociolinguistic structure, the arrangement of standards and scripts, not genetic closeness.

Evidence handling observes a three-state discipline that turned out to be the program's most consequential design choice: *support*, *absence*, and *adverse* are different states and are never collapsed into one number. A Czech source using a different lexeme for the same concept is not merely missing support for a candidate — it is support for a competitor. A form that resembles the candidate while meaning something else is stronger still: collision evidence. Adverse evidence is therefore carried in its own typed channel — false-friend or collision, semantic opposite, dominance risk, competitor support, register mismatch, authority-needed, and non-inheritance vetoes between lanes — and veto relations are relations, not negative numbers folded into a score. The companion linkage discipline separates three statuses that alignment work tends to blur: a term row may be *linked to a concept* (it can be associated with a concept entry), *witnessed for a branch* (a source branch actually attests a form), or *reviewed for bridge use* (cleared by review); no status implies the next, and coverage gains from alignment are never allowed to masquerade as evidence gains.

Three artifacts carry these disciplines across lanes. The *interlingual concept ledger* holds language-neutral concept entries with per-language labels, typed source-term match classes (exact, near, source-specific, compound, ambiguous, collision-risk), and the adverse channel; it exists because lanes cannot be compared through English substrings or source-language phrases, as the collision cases in Section 7 demonstrate. The *stratified core spine* replaces flat term lists: a comparison set balanced across three strata — proof grammar, curriculum algebra, and the corpus-specific historical vocabulary — with fill-list semantics under which every lane must eventually mark each row witnessed, gap, or not-applicable, never inventing a form to fill a cell. The *invariance ledger* states, per transformation the pipeline performs — script conversion, register shift, bridge mapping, controlled simplification — what must be preserved, what may change, the test, and the failure mode; the deterministic Latin-Cyrillic transliteration validators are its oldest working instance.

Finally, lane readiness is governed by thresholds rather than enthusiasm: a lane may not present itself as pilot-ready until corpus coverage, false-friend screening, cross-branch recognizability evidence, settled script policy, acceptable governance risk and — ultimately — human comprehension testing align. Below those thresholds a lane is exploratory by definition, whatever the volume of its output; the phrase in force throughout is *internal triangulation before external review*.

## 4. Result 1 — Independently grown term spines are not commensurable, and the drift is source-genre-driven

**Measured:** merging the four lanes' "60-term spines" (word-boundary matching, one alias corrected) yields 138 unique concepts: 17 in all four lanes, 32 in at least three, 83 in exactly one.
**Interpreted as:** source-genre drift — each lane's inventory mirrors its source genre (paper-translation vs textbook vs OER vs single-paper microdraft).
**Not claimed:** that any lane's spine is wrong; that the union defines a canonical vocabulary.

The Pan-Romance spine carries Noether's working vocabulary and proof connectives, because it grew from paper translation; the controlled-Arabic spine carries curriculum algebra (normal subgroup, tensor product, injective, surjective), because it grew from textbook shelves; the Malay-Indonesian spine carries undergraduate material (eigenvalue, equation, root), because it grew from open educational resources; the Persianate ledger carries the specialist vocabulary of the single paper it started from. The consequence is methodological: cross-lane evaluation requires a deliberately *stratified* core — here 67 rows balanced across proof grammar (22), curriculum algebra (28) and corpus-specific historical vocabulary (17), with fill-list semantics under which each lane marks each row witnessed, gap, or not-applicable. A flat "universal" term list would have re-imported the genre bias of whichever lane wrote it.

## 5. Result 2 — Witness monoculture, measured

**Measured:** 95 glossary files, 1,310 records, deduplicated to 1,229 term rows; witness fields are Ukrainian/Russian only; 7 of 1,310 rationales mention any West/South language; triage: 947 missing-witness, 19 broad-claim-on-East-evidence, 41 dominance-risk (one family), 19 declared coinages; branch witness mass 95.1% East / 2.5% West / 2.3% South; effective branch number 1.255 of 3.
**Interpreted as:** an effective single-branch witness system inside a formally pan-Slavic corpus — the dominance failure mode of Section 2, instantiated and quantified.
**Not claimed:** that the Interslavic renderings are wrong; that East-derived choices fail West/South readers (that is a comprehension question no internal measure answers).

Two qualifications matter. First, the bias was caught in-project: the human collaborator, reading the pipeline's logbooks, flagged the East-only seeding on 2026-06-24, which produced a 20-source West/South reference shelf (Czech 6, Polish 6, Slovak 1, Slovenian 2, Serbian 1, Croatian 2, Bulgarian 2; university and institute texts, hash-pinned), a provenance rule, and spot-triangulations — but no per-row backfill, so the records did not reflect the shelf. Second, the pre-repair state is deliberately preserved (frozen artifacts): it documents what an AI pipeline produces when seeded with single-branch sources, and makes the repair a measurable event rather than a silent fix.

## 6. Result 3 — Local backfill closes much of the gap, and the family is asymmetric

**Measured:** searching the shelf's extracted texts for branch forms of 37 core concepts; ~15 concepts supported in both branches; ring competitor-only in both branches; quotient field competitor-only West; write-back of shelf attestations through the concept ledger upgraded 182 rows across 20 concepts and moved branch balance from 1.255 to 1.754 effective branches (divergence-from-balanced 0.871 → 0.537).
**Interpreted as:** the witness gap is largely closable from material already on disk for the core vocabulary; the internationalism policy was correct where it was applied to genuinely international terms; and the family is asymmetric (below).
**Not claimed:** row-level verification (the write-back is concept-shelf-level provenance: the shelf attests the concept's branch forms; each row's form-in-context check is pending); any change to any term.

Boundary conditions of the write-back, stated exactly because they carry the credibility of the number: competitor hits stayed in the adverse channel and never counted as support; draft translations counted for nothing; the frozen baseline was not overwritten. The residual gap is the phrase-level long tail — witnessing at corpus scale, not method.

**The branch asymmetry (F12):** West Slavic is the calque-preferring branch — Czech and Polish use native lexemes even for *theorem*, *corollary*, *trace* and *extension*, where the South branch accepts internationalisms. Variant policy should therefore target West cohorts specifically, not "non-East" readers generically.

**Internal-variance addendum (post-review).** The audit also found variance *inside* the generated corpus, not only against external shelves. (a) The ring family is internally overwhelming but not perfectly uniform: against 1,059 `kolc*` occurrences across 125 Latin-script files, Paper 25 contains `prsten` twice in an algebraic passage (residue classes modulo a prime ideal forming a ring without zero divisors), verified in the rendered text by two independent toolchains. (b) Quotient field is the second internal-variance case: the corpus renders *Quotientenkörper* as `polje kvocientov` (papers 06, 24, 30) and `kvocientno polje` (paper 09, with a definitional footnote) — two word-orders for one concept, text-verified on both sides. An earlier placeholder for this row (`polje častnikov?`) was wrong and is withdrawn everywhere; it originated from the Ukrainian rendering (поле часток) rather than from the corpus's own Interslavic text. Both review-priority rows therefore combine external competitor evidence with internal variance evidence — the audit finds defects inside the corpus as well as against shelves.

## 7. Result 4 — Cross-system triangulation catches real defects; it is still not validation

**Measured:** one predicted-and-confirmed concept-linking defect; one unpredicted defect of reversed polarity; one transient false positive retracted; independent occurrence counts agreeing exactly (1,059) across two toolchains on the same corpus.
**Interpreted as:** cross-system review does real quality-assurance work, with a division of labor — systems catch each other's concrete errors, the human catches the systemic one.
**Not claimed:** validation. No community return exists; triangulation among AI systems and one human is internal QA by definition.

Two syntheses of the same corpus were produced independently — one blind pass and one language-only handoff written without sight of it — and converged on the same skeleton: strata, ladder, objective, priority sequence. Convergence is weak evidence; the caught defects are stronger. The second system predicted, sight-unseen, that a coarse gloss-linker would have mapped "ring" onto *Ränderung* (a bordering operation from Clebsch's transfer principle); inspection confirmed exactly that link. **Auditing failure modes (all three kept on the record):** substring collision (`ring` ← *Ränderung*, fixed with word-boundary matching); polarity reversal (`reducible` linked into *irreducible* material — a wrong-sign match, not a weak match); and a scan-path false positive (a claimed glossary-vs-text drift for two papers was retracted the same day after a full-tree search showed the forms present — the transient claim came from probing a directory layout those papers do not use). The retraction is reported as evidence of the discipline: honest coverage numbers went *down* after the fixes, which is the desired direction when removing contamination. Separately, the human collaborator caught the systemic defect (the seeding bias of Section 5) that neither system had surfaced.

## 8. Result 5 — Adverse evidence as a first-class channel

**Measured:** 123 typed adverse entries harvested from existing records: 62 non-inheritance vetoes, 41 dominance-risk rows, 16 authority-needed rows, plus collision, polarity, competitor and register templates.
**Interpreted as:** adverse evidence (competitors, collisions, vetoes) is a distinct evidence state that cannot be represented as low or negative scores.
**Not claimed:** that any flagged term is wrong; flags queue review.

The largest class was already being produced by the governance discipline of one lane — the Persianate rejection matrices — before the channel had a name: good governance generates adverse evidence naturally, and the ledger merely makes it shared infrastructure. The design principle is that vetoes are typed relations, not negative numbers: folding a collision into a lowered score would let a term with many positive mentions outvote its own false-friend warning, which is precisely the failure the three-state discipline exists to prevent. **Internal-variance addendum (post-review):** the Paper-25 `prsten` occurrence and the quotient-field word-order split (Section 6) enter this channel as *internal variance* entries — neither is adverse evidence against any candidate; both are review questions about variant policy that only an authority can settle. The channel distinguishes "this form collides" from "this corpus is not yet uniform," and the distinction changes what the reviewer is asked.

## 9. The siting model

Putting the pieces together yields a siting procedure that decides, per language family or region, which intervention — if any — construction effort should buy. The procedure is lexicographic. First the source floor: without sufficient native technical sources, the only permissible outputs are a source-intake plan, a format-relaxation pass (machine-readable sources are preferred; requiring them is itself a bias against academic cultures whose mathematics lives in PDF and institutional archives), or a marked hypothesis. Second the build-type rule of Section 3, read off the family's standard-and-script structure. Only then the access-gain scoring of candidate designs, with the marginal rule in selection: a contested choice must add usable inter-intelligibility over the dominant-standard baseline, and gains concentrated in the already-dominant cohort do not count in its favor. **Weighting note (post-review):** population weighting appears in the program's sensitivity analyses only to show dependence on cohort-prior choice; it is not a proposed governance weighting, and any decision-grade use requires a declared community target. The defensible weightings for this paper's purpose — family-central evidence breadth — are branch-equal and dependence-corrected branch coverage. Applied to the current queue the procedure re-derives the program's own decisions — bridges for Slavic and Romance, controlled registers for Arabic and the Malay-Indonesian pair, a script bridge for Hindustani, local standards for Chinese-Japanese-Korean, refusal of pan-family umbrellas elsewhere — and marks Pan-Turkic as source-blocked pending a format-relaxation retest, which is the correct, checkable state of that lane. The one addition the measurements force is the branch asymmetry of Section 6: siting must model branch-level reception asymmetries, and the analogous within-family checks belong in every lane's access ledger.

## 10. Honest limits

No lane has any accepted external review return; every recognizability statement in this paper is source-attestation or editorial judgment, not human comprehension data. Internal triangulation across three AI systems and one human sanity-checker is quality assurance, not validation, and the paper's standing phrase draws exactly that line. All witness upgrades reported in Section 6 carry concept-shelf provenance from a 20-source shelf; per-row form verification is pending, and the shelf itself is small — the effective-branch numbers are floors and trajectories, not endpoints. The flag taxonomy is mechanical triage over recorded rationales and can misfile unusual phrasings; its clean class is a lower bound. The large draft-translation archive used for candidate forms carries a known condensation defect from its generating era and is never counted as witness material. Multilingual model geometry has been used nowhere in the measurements reported here; it is future work, and when it is used, the dominant-language-pivot audit is a precondition, since the instrument exhibits the same failure mode the method exists to control. Nothing in this program claims authority over any language: every artifact is designed to be inspected, forked, corrected or rejected by the communities concerned, and the first external contact is planned around a prepared no-verdict review packet, not a fait accompli.

## Appendix B. Flag and status vocabularies (self-contained)

**Witness-triage flags (per term row):** F10-0 *clean* — West/South, project-authority, or international rationale adequately witnessed. F10-1 *missing witness* — no non-East evidence recorded either way; the choice may be fine. F10-2 *East-heavy claim* — broad family validity claimed on East-only evidence. F10-3 *dominance-risk* — the row belongs to a family whose surface is contested across branches; review before external showing. F10-4 *authority-needed* — declared coinage or specialist term awaiting community certification.

**Linkage statuses (per row, none implies the next):** *linked_to_concept* — the row can be associated with a concept entry. *witnessed_for_branch* — a source branch attests a supporting form (recorded per branch: East/West/South). *reviewed_for_bridge_use* — cleared by external review; only category-6 evidence (community returns) can set this.

**Evidence states (per candidate, per branch):** *support* — the branch attests the candidate's lexeme family. *absence* — nothing recorded either way. *adverse* — the branch supplies competitor or collision evidence; carried as typed relations (competitor_support, false_friend_or_collision, semantic_opposite, register_mismatch, dominance_risk, authority_needed, do_not_inherit_into_lane), never as negative scores; some relations are vetoes.

**Witness provenance levels:** *concept_shelf* — a reference shelf attests the concept's branch form; *row_verified* — this row's form checked in its own context; *reviewed* — human/community return accepted.

## Appendix D. Branch-balance statistics (self-contained, minimal)

For a term row or a corpus, count witness mass per family branch (East/West/South), normalized to a distribution. The **effective number of branches** is the exponential of the distribution's Shannon entropy: it equals 3.0 when the three branches carry equal mass and falls toward 1.0 as evidence concentrates in one branch — "how many branches is this corpus effectively listening to." The **divergence from balanced** is the Kullback–Leibler divergence from the target distribution (balanced across branches, or a declared voting-machine target): zero at target, growing with skew. The **dependence correction** (specified for the math lane; used in sensitivity analyses) down-weights witnesses that are close relatives on the family tree, so a second witness from the same branch adds less than a first witness from an unrepresented branch — the formal version of "many witnesses from one branch are not many witnesses." Measured values in this paper: baseline 1.255 effective branches (divergence 0.871); after concept-shelf write-back 1.754 (0.537).
