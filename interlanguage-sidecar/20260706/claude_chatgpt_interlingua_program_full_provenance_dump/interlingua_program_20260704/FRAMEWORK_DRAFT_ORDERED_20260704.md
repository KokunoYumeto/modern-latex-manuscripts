# Zonal auxiliary-language construction as measurable optimization
## Harmonizing an operational AI translation pipeline with cross-lingual evidence discipline

Ordered draft v0.2, 2026-07-04 (paper order per post-Fable steer Task A; content identical to v0.1 sections, mechanical reorder + appendix pointers). Provenance: AUTHORSHIP.md. Posture: internal triangulation before external review; no term is promoted or certified by this paper.
## Abstract

*An operating AI-assisted translation pipeline has built technical corpora in a zonal auxiliary language (Interslavic, both scripts), audited editions in several natural languages, and staged construction programs across further language families under explicit evidence and governance rules. We show that the pipeline's operational selection criterion (an "access-gain ledger") and the theoretical objective of bridge-language design (recognizability, neighborhood preservation, family-barycenter regularization, false-friend penalties) are the same object in two vocabularies, and we exploit the correspondence to measure the pipeline on its own artifacts. Five results: independently grown core-term spines diverge by source genre (138 concepts, 17 shared of four lanes), requiring stratified comparison sets; the flagship lane's witness base, formally pan-Slavic, is effectively single-branch (95% East-Slavic mass; 1.26 of 3 effective branches), a measured instance of the dominance failure mode shared by human bridge design and multilingual model geometry; the deficit closes substantially from material already in the corpus (to 1.75 effective branches), exposing a West-Slavic calque preference that variant policy must address; cross-system triangulation catches concrete defects (a predicted-and-confirmed concept collision; a polarity-reversed link) while human oversight catches the systemic one; and adverse evidence — competitors, collisions, non-inheritance vetoes — requires a typed channel separate from support scores. A lexicographic siting model (source floor, then standard-and-script structure, then access scoring) re-derives the program's build-type decisions. All artifacts, including pre-repair states, are published with hashes; nothing herein claims community authority.*

## 1. Introduction

Constructed bridge systems for related language communities are usually discussed in one of two registers: the philological tradition of zonal auxiliary languages, with a century of design practice and community governance; or, recently, the representation geometry of multilingual language models, which measures cross-lingual semantic alignment at scale. This paper reports what happened when the two were joined over a working system — an AI-assisted pipeline that has produced substantial technical corpora in an existing zonal language and staged construction programs across a dozen further language families, under written evidence and governance rules.

The contribution is threefold. First, a correspondence: the pipeline's operational selection record and the theoretical bridge-design objective are the same object in two vocabularies, each side supplying terms the other lacks (Section 2), under shared discipline (Section 3). Second, measurements: joining the vocabularies immediately produced five results on the pipeline's own artifacts — term-spine drift is source-genre-driven and demands stratified comparison sets (4); a formally pan-family corpus can be effectively single-branch, quantifiably so (5); the gap closes cheaply from existing material, exposing a branch-level reception asymmetry along the way (6); independent AI systems catch each other's concrete defects while the human collaborator catches the systemic one (7); and negative evidence — collisions, competitors, vetoes — needs its own typed channel rather than a lowered score (8). Third, a siting model that decides which families warrant a bridge, a controlled register, a script layer, or deliberate non-intervention, with source availability as a lexicographic gate (9). Sections 10–11 state the limits and the availability of the full record. Throughout, the posture is that of a working method paper: internal triangulation before external review, and no claim of authority over any community's language.

## 2. Two vocabularies, one objective

Two bodies of work grew independently around the same problem and named its parts differently.

The first is an operating construction pipeline. Between 2026-06 and 2026-07 an AI-assisted workflow produced, among other artifacts, a complete Interslavic rendering of Emmy Noether's collected papers in both Latin and Cyrillic scripts, a Spanish source-audited edition, a French translation through forty papers, and staged construction programs for Pan-Romance, controlled Arabic, Persianate, Hindustani, Malay-Indonesian and further language-family lanes. The pipeline is governed by written operating rules: every term requires a source witness or a declared coinage; term choices are motivated and logged; script variants are deterministic build artifacts with validators; terms are promoted only under corpus pressure; and every artifact remains forkable, correctable and rejectable by the communities it might serve. Mid-program, the pipeline made its selection criterion explicit as a typed decision record, the *access-gain ledger*: a contested choice must state which reader cohorts gain a materially new usable channel, whether main-register readers remain served, how strongly sources support the choice, and what penalties apply for dominance, exclusion, opacity and ambiguity. The ledger is used as a record, not a scalar: its terms are named, argued and pointed at evidence, not summed.

The second body of work is a theoretical framework for bridge-language design, assembled from interlinguistics, intercomprehension research, controlled-natural-language studies, terminology governance and the representation geometry of multilingual language models. In that vocabulary, a zonal auxiliary language is an optimization object: for each concept one seeks a surface form that maximizes passive recognizability and semantic-neighborhood preservation across the source languages, sits near a regularized family barycenter rather than at any single member, and minimizes false-friend risk and acquisition cost. Multilingual model geometry appears as an instrument for measuring cross-lingual alignment — never as an oracle, because such models carry their own dominant-language bias.

The central observation of this paper is that these are the same object in two vocabularies. The correspondence is term-for-term. Passive recognizability and the ledger's marginal inter-intelligibility gain are the theory's recognizability and neighborhood-preservation terms. The Interslavic tradition's subgroup voting is a hand-executed regularized barycenter. The false-friend lists that zonal projects have curated for decades are the ambiguity penalty. The pipeline's rule that no bridge should be built where a strong written standard already serves its readers is the regularizer that the theoretical objective needs but rarely states. And the correspondence is productive in both directions. Practice contributes two terms the theory lacks: *source strength* — evidence quality as a first-class variable, because a selection criterion is useless where native technical sources are missing — and *lingua-franca gap*, the siting precondition that asks whether a new channel is needed at all. Theory contributes measurable counterparts for the ledger's editorial terms: neighborhood-overlap scores for recognizability, form-similarity-times-semantic-distance for false-friend risk, distance-from-dominant-member for the dominance penalty.

The correspondence extends to the failure mode. Every level of the program exhibits the same characteristic drift: a family centroid estimated from unbalanced evidence collapses toward the dominant member. In the human lanes this appears as the bridge that is "just Spanish in softened spelling," the Interslavic that reads as rough Russian, the Malay-Indonesian register that quietly becomes Indonesian; on the machine side it appears as multilingual models routing meaning through representation space shaped by their dominant training language. Section 5 reports a measured instance. The practical cure is also shared: regularize explicitly against the dominant member, and weight evidence by independence, not by volume — many witnesses from one branch are not many witnesses.

## 3. Objects and discipline

The program distinguishes four intervention types, and the siting decision is made before any language work begins. A *zonal auxiliary language* is warranted where a family is split across many written standards with high mutual recognizability (Slavic, Romance). A *controlled technical register* is warranted where one strong standard already serves readers and the need is stable technical prose, not a new language (Arabic, Chinese, the Malay-Indonesian standard pair). A *script bridge* is warranted where one register is divided by writing systems (Hindustani; the Turkic alphabet layer). Where internal diversity is high and mutual recognizability low, the correct output is *local standards with crosswalks*, and pan-family umbrellas are refused outright (Chinese-Japanese-Korean, Niger-Congo, the Indigenous American languages). Two rules order the decision: the source floor is lexicographic — below a minimum of native technical source evidence, no bridge recommendation may be issued, only a source-intake plan — and build type follows sociolinguistic structure, the arrangement of standards and scripts, not genetic closeness.

Evidence handling observes a three-state discipline that turned out to be the program's most consequential design choice: *support*, *absence*, and *adverse* are different states and are never collapsed into one number. A Czech source using a different lexeme for the same concept is not merely missing support for a candidate — it is support for a competitor. A form that resembles the candidate while meaning something else is stronger still: collision evidence. Adverse evidence is therefore carried in its own typed channel — false-friend or collision, semantic opposite, dominance risk, competitor support, register mismatch, authority-needed, and non-inheritance vetoes between lanes — and veto relations are relations, not negative numbers folded into a score. The companion linkage discipline separates three statuses that alignment work tends to blur: a term row may be *linked to a concept* (it can be associated with a concept entry), *witnessed for a branch* (a source branch actually attests a form), or *reviewed for bridge use* (cleared by review); no status implies the next, and coverage gains from alignment are never allowed to masquerade as evidence gains.

Three artifacts carry these disciplines across lanes. The *interlingual concept ledger* holds language-neutral concept entries with per-language labels, typed German-to-English match classes (exact, near, source-specific, compound, ambiguous, collision-risk), and the adverse channel; it exists because lanes cannot be compared through English substrings or source-language phrases, as the collision cases in Section 7 demonstrate. The *stratified core spine* replaces flat term lists: a comparison set balanced across three strata — proof grammar, curriculum algebra, and the corpus-specific historical vocabulary — with fill-list semantics under which every lane must eventually mark each row witnessed, gap, or not-applicable, never inventing a form to fill a cell. The *invariance ledger* states, per transformation the pipeline performs — script conversion, register shift, bridge mapping, controlled simplification — what must be preserved, what may change, the test, and the failure mode; the deterministic Latin-Cyrillic transliteration validators are its oldest working instance.

Finally, lane readiness is governed by thresholds rather than enthusiasm: a lane may not present itself as pilot-ready until corpus coverage, false-friend screening, cross-branch recognizability evidence, settled script policy, acceptable governance risk and — ultimately — human comprehension testing align. Below those thresholds a lane is exploratory by definition, whatever the volume of its output; the phrase in force throughout is *internal triangulation before external review*.

## 4. Result 1 — Independently grown term spines are not commensurable, and the drift is source-genre-driven

Four lanes each maintained a "60-term spine" of core concepts. Merging them (word-boundary matching, one alias corrected) yields 138 unique concepts, of which only 17 occur in all four lanes, 32 in at least three, and 83 in exactly one. The spines were never one skeleton; each lane's inventory mirrors the genre of its sources. The Pan-Romance spine carries Noether's working vocabulary and proof connectives, because it grew from paper translation; the controlled-Arabic spine carries curriculum algebra (normal subgroup, tensor product, injective, surjective), because it grew from textbook shelves; the Malay-Indonesian spine carries undergraduate material (eigenvalue, equation, root), because it grew from open educational resources; the Persianate ledger carries the specialist vocabulary of the single paper it started from. The consequence is methodological: cross-lane evaluation requires a deliberately *stratified* core — here 67 rows balanced across proof grammar (22), curriculum algebra (28) and corpus-specific historical vocabulary (17), with fill-list semantics under which each lane marks each row witnessed, gap, or not-applicable. A flat "universal" term list would have re-imported the genre bias of whichever lane wrote it.

## 5. Result 2 — Witness monoculture, measured

The proven lane's machine-readable term records (95 glossary files, 1,310 records, deduplicated to 1,229 term rows) derive without exception through a German→Ukrainian/Russian→Interslavic path: the record schema contains Ukrainian and Russian witness fields and no West or South Slavic fields at all; 7 of 1,310 free-text rationales mention any West/South language; 1 mentions false friends. Row triage: 947 rows lack any non-East evidence either way ("missing witness" — under-recorded, not wrong); 19 claim broad-Slavic validity on East-only evidence; 41 form a single concentrated dominance-risk family (every compound of the contested ring term); 19 are declared coinages awaiting authority. As a concentration statistic, branch witness mass is 95.1% East / 2.5% West / 2.3% South — an effective branch number of 1.255 out of 3. The corpus was formally pan-Slavic and effectively single-branch, including under rationales that claim pan-Slavic motivation. Two qualifications matter. First, the bias was caught in-project: a human reading the logbooks flagged it on 2026-06-24, which produced a 20-source West/South reference shelf, a provenance rule, and spot-triangulations — but no per-row backfill, so the records did not reflect the shelf. Second, the pre-repair state is deliberately preserved (frozen artifacts) as an archaeological baseline: it documents what an AI pipeline produces when seeded with single-branch sources, and makes the repair a measurable event rather than a silent fix.

## 6. Result 3 — The gap closes cheaply from local material, and the family is asymmetric

Searching the 20-source shelf's extracted texts for branch forms of 37 core concepts required no new collection. About fifteen concepts — the international core: ideal, module, group, homomorphism, isomorphism, idempotent, basis, invariant, vector, kernel, norm, algebra, definition — are supported in both branches: the lane's internationalism policy was correct there, merely unevidenced. The contested cases concentrate elsewhere. The ring term is competitor-only in both branches: the lane's *kolco* (East continuity) has zero attestations against Czech/Slovak *okruh*, Polish *pierścień*, South-Slavic *prsten*, Slovenian *kolobar* — one decision that resolves all 41 dominance-risk rows, prepared as a review memo with typed options rather than decided unilaterally. A previously unflagged case surfaced (quotient field: West competitor-only). And a structural regularity emerged (F12): **West Slavic is the calque-preferring branch** — Czech and Polish use native lexemes even for *theorem*, *corollary*, *trace* and *extension*, where the South branch accepts internationalisms. Variant policy should therefore target West cohorts specifically, not "non-East" readers generically. Routing the shelf attestations through the concept ledger upgraded 182 term rows across 20 concepts and moved the corpus witness balance from 1.255 to 1.754 effective branches (divergence from balance 0.871→0.537), at concept-level provenance, from material already on disk. The residual gap is the phrase-level long tail and per-row verification — witnessing at corpus scale, not method.

## 7. Result 4 — Cross-system triangulation catches real defects; it is still not validation

Two syntheses of the same corpus were produced independently — one blind pass and one language-only handoff written without sight of it — and converged on the same skeleton: strata, ladder, objective, priority sequence. Convergence is weak evidence; the caught defects are stronger. The second system predicted, sight-unseen, that a coarse gloss-linker would have mapped "ring" onto *Ränderung* (a bordering operation from Clebsch's transfer principle); inspection confirmed exactly that link, plus an unpredicted sibling with reversed polarity — "reducible" linked into "irreducible". Both defects came from substring matching, both were fixed with word-boundary matching, and honest coverage went *down* (gloss links 28→25 concepts) — the desired direction when removing contamination. Separately, the human collaborator caught the systemic defect (the seeding bias of Section 5) that neither system had surfaced. This division of labor — systems catch each other's specific errors, the human catches the systemic ones — is what the phrase *internal triangulation before external review* denotes. It is quality assurance. It is not, and is never presented as, community validation.

## 8. Result 5 — Adverse evidence as a first-class channel

Harvesting the corpus's existing negative signals into one typed ledger yielded 123 entries: 62 non-inheritance vetoes (a bridge candidate must not propagate into named adjacent lanes without their own review), 41 dominance-risk rows (the ring family), 16 authority-needed rows (concepts whose only promoted-register evidence is a warning comparator), and singleton templates for collision (*Ränderung*→ring), polarity reversal (irreducible→reducible), competitor evidence (*razpadno* vs the West's *rozklad-* family) and register caution (*svijanje* for *Faltung*, which may behave as contraction). Two observations. The largest class was already being produced by the governance discipline of one lane — the Persianate rejection matrices — before the channel had a name: good governance generates adverse evidence naturally, and the ledger merely makes it shared infrastructure. And the channel's design principle is that vetoes are typed relations, not negative numbers: folding a collision into a lowered score would let a term with many positive mentions outvote its own false-friend warning, which is precisely the failure the three-state discipline exists to prevent.

## 9. The siting model

Putting the pieces together yields a siting procedure that decides, per language family or region, which intervention — if any — construction effort should buy. The procedure is lexicographic. First the source floor: without sufficient native technical sources, the only permissible outputs are a source-intake plan, a format-relaxation pass (machine-readable sources are preferred; requiring them is itself a bias against academic cultures whose mathematics lives in PDF and institutional archives), or a marked hypothesis. Second the build-type rule of Section 3, read off the family's standard-and-script structure. Only then the access-gain scoring of candidate designs, with the marginal rule in selection: a contested choice must add usable inter-intelligibility over the dominant-standard baseline, and gains concentrated in the already-dominant cohort do not count in its favor. Applied to the current queue this re-derives the program's own decisions — bridges for Slavic and Romance, controlled registers for Arabic and the Malay-Indonesian pair, a script bridge for Hindustani, local standards for Chinese-Japanese-Korean, refusal of pan-family umbrellas elsewhere — and marks Pan-Turkic as source-blocked pending the format-relaxation retest, which is the correct, checkable state of that lane. The one addition the measurements force is F12: siting must model *branch-level* reception asymmetries (a Slavic bridge must budget West-facing calque doublets), and the analogous within-family checks belong in every lane's access ledger.

## 10. Honest limits

No lane has any accepted external review return; every recognizability statement in this paper is source-attestation or editorial judgment, not human comprehension data. Internal triangulation across three AI systems and one human sanity-checker is quality assurance, not validation, and the paper's standing phrase draws exactly that line. All witness upgrades reported in Section 6 carry concept-level provenance from a 20-source shelf; per-row form verification is pending, and the shelf itself is small — the effective-branch numbers are floors and trajectories, not endpoints. The flag taxonomy is mechanical triage over recorded rationales and can misfile unusual phrasings; its clean class is a lower bound. The large draft-translation archive used for candidate forms carries a known condensation defect from its generating era and is never counted as witness material. Multilingual model geometry has been used nowhere in the measurements reported here; when it is, the dominant-language-pivot audit is a precondition, since the instrument exhibits the same failure mode the method exists to control. Nothing in this program claims authority over any language: every artifact is designed to be inspected, forked, corrected or rejected by the communities concerned, and the first external contact is planned around a prepared decision memo, not a fait accompli.
