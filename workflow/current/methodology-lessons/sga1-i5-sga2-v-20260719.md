# Lessons from the SGA1 I.5 and SGA2 Expose V release

## What worked

- Preserve producer freezes unchanged, then make a separately receipted public
  projection for audience wording and privacy hygiene.
- Rebuild projected TeX, compare unchanged mathematical pages pixel-for-pixel,
  and regenerate every dependent manifest and ZIP.
- Keep structural indices, formula/source/terminology ledgers, append-only
  difficulty records, and project-generated page renders together.
- Treat SGA1 and SGA2 as separate active lanes with exact authority spans and
  continuation cursors; do not let a cumulative SGA record imply either volume
  is complete.
- Publish project-generated target and QA images with stable IDs, dimensions,
  hashes, linked units, and dispositions while excluding French-source pixels
  whose redistribution rights are not established.

## Failure and repair

- A first hygiene pass caught `archive-maintenance` but missed the variant
  `archive-maintainer`. Audience-wording gates must use a reviewed family of
  variants and the rendered PDF must be inspected after replacement.
- Zenodo's deposition API returned a bare MD5 while the records API returned
  the same value prefixed by `md5:`. A post-publication assertion initially
  treated that formatting difference as a byte change. Normalize the algorithm
  prefix before comparing, then retain the stronger SHA-256 readback for every
  newly uploaded file.

## Public-claim boundary

- The SGA record rights field was changed from historical CC0 metadata to
  `License Not Specified`; no blanket license over French sources or new English
  translations is asserted.
- SGA1 is bounded through Expose I section I.5 and continues at I.6.
- SGA2 contains complete Expose V as a bounded checkpoint and continues at
  Expose VI.
- SGA5 is a selected ten-expose working edition, not a complete volume.
- SGA6 retains mixed authority layers and is not a uniform source-faithful
  whole-volume edition.
- None of these statuses constitutes a critical edition, peer review, proof
  certification, independent human review, accessibility certification, or
  rights clearance.
