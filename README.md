# Modern LaTeX Editions of Mathematics Manuscripts

Historical mathematics transcribed into modern LaTeX and translated into other languages, with reader PDFs and editable sources. The larger editions have their own repositories, listed below; this repository holds the overview, the smaller editions and earlier work.

## Status

This collection is a work in progress. Its transcriptions and translations are AI-produced, except where an edition credits human work. When the project started, AI tools could not yet do this work reliably, and much of what is here comes from experiments in finding a method that works; those results are rough. The tools have become more reliable since.

Some editions are in a readable state, but even the readable ones very likely contain errors, or at least inelegancies, and only some of them are of decent quality. Many are still worth reading, especially translations that exist nowhere else. Apart from a few tentative exceptions, none of it is at the level of a human mathematician's work. Read with discretion, and check important statements and formulas against the original.

### Carl Friedrich Gauss, collected works

The original-language *Werke*, Volumes I–VIII (Volume V in selected works, Volume VII to Article 130), are available in the [Gauss collected works repository](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/gauss-collected-works). Its English and modern German translations cover complete Volumes I, VIII and IX and works from Volumes II–VI.

Read the [English collection, 4997 pages](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/modern-latex-manuscripts/releases/download/gauss-english-german-2026-11-11g/Gauss_Collected_English.pdf) or [German collection, 5144 pages](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/modern-latex-manuscripts/releases/download/gauss-english-german-2026-11-11g/Gauss_Werke_Deutsch.pdf). **Volume IX — Geodesy. Continuation of Volume IV (1903).** The English and modern German readers contain the entire historical volume, pp. 1–528, with its preliminaries, Hanover foldout map, final blank leaf, and publisher’s advertisement. It covers the Göttingen–Altona latitude determination, the geodesic line and conformal mapping, network adjustment, the Oldenburg and Hanover surveys, height measurement, the heliotrope, and observation errors. L. Krüger prepared the 1903 volume with A. Börsch’s assistance, M. Brendel directed the general editorial work, and F. Klein signed the closing remarks. H. C. Schumacher’s introduction, Hartmann’s credit in the station observations, and the quotation from Goldschmidt retain their attribution. Lieutenant J. Gauss devised the Hanover map. Both editions were AI-translated and typeset by OpenAI GPT-6.1 Sol (Ultra), with editable LaTeX text, formulas, tables, and diagrams. [1903 edition at the Göttinger Digitalisierungszentrum](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/PPN23601515X) · [Hanover foldout at SUB Göttingen](https://webdoc.sub.gwdg.de/ebook/e/2005/gausscd/html/kapitel_landv_karte.htm)

The title lettering and arrangement follow the 1903 Göttingen copy. The unsigned Gauss vignette is documented in *Werke* I (1863). The image reproduced here comes from the matching vignette in the second printing of *Werke* I (1870), identified on Wikimedia Commons with Public Domain Mark 1.0. [1863 title leaf](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN235993352/PPN235993352.pdf#page=4) · [1870 image, Commons revision 1179960952](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?title=File%3AGauss%2C_Carl_Friedrich_-_Vignette_-_von_Werke_%281870%29_%28page_9_crop%29.jpg&oldid=1179960952) · [Public Domain Mark 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/)

For Volume IX, open the `volume_IX/english` or `volume_IX/german` directory and run `pdflatex -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error main.tex` twice from that directory. The sources use TikZ for the editable drawings and include the JPEG title vignette.
