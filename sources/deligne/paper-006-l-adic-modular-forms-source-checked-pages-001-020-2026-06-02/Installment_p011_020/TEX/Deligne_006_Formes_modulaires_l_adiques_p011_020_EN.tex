\paragraph{(3.13)}
Let $p$ be a prime number, let $n\geq 3$ be an integer prime to $p$, and let $F_{n,p}$ be the functor which associates to each scheme $S$ the set of isomorphism classes of commutative diagrams of $S$-schemes
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
&& \Z/(n)^2 &&\\[-0.2em]
& \alpha\nearrow && \nwarrow\alpha' &\\[-0.2em]
E_n & \longrightarrow & F_n &&\\[-0.2em]
\cap && \cap &&\\[-0.2em]
E & \xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ } & F &&
\end{array}
\tag{3.14}
\]
where $\varphi$ is a $p$-isogeny between elliptic curves and $\alpha$ is an isomorphism. One denotes by $q_1$ and $q_2:F_{n,p}\to M_n$ the morphisms of functors associating to a diagram (3.14) the subdiagrams $(E,E_n,\alpha)$ and $(F,F_n,\alpha')$.

\begin{proposition}[3.15]
The functor $F_{n,p}$ is represented by a scheme $M_{n,p}$, and the morphisms $q_1,q_2:M_{n,p}\to M_n$ are finite.
\end{proposition}

The automorphism $\sigma$ of $F_{n,p}$ sending $\varphi:E\to F$ to ${}^{t}\!\varphi:F\to E$ exchanges $q_1$ and $q_2$; it suffices therefore to consider $q_1$. This morphism identifies $F_{n,p}$ with the functor of subgroups of order $p$ of the universal elliptic curve $E$ over $M_n$, so that, by the theory of Hilbert schemes, $F_{n,p}$ is representable and $M_{n,p}$ is proper over $M_n$. If $s$ is a geometric point of $M_n$, $q_1^{-1}(s)$ is the set of subgroups of order $p$ of $E_s$, and has $p+1$ elements if $\charop(k(s))\ne p$, and only one, the kernel of Frobenius, if $\charop(k(s))=p$. \QED

One can show that $M_{n,p}$ is regular, and that $q_1$ and $q_2$ are finite flat; we do not use this delicate result, but content ourselves here to note that over $\Spec(\Z[1/p])$, each $q_i$ makes $M_{n,p}$ an étale covering of degree $p+1$ of $M_n$.

The morphisms $q_i$ can be inserted into a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
q_1^*E & \xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ } & q_2^*E &&\\[-0.2em]
\swarrow && \searrow &&\\[-0.2em]
E & \xleftarrow{\ u\ } & M_{n,p} & \xrightarrow{\ v\ } & E\\[-0.2em]
\downarrow f_n && \downarrow q_1\quad\downarrow q_2 && \downarrow f_n\\[-0.2em]
M_n && M_n && M_n
\end{array}
\tag{3.16}
\]
where $(\varphi,u,v)$ is part of the universal diagram (3.14).

One denotes by $I_p$ the morphism from $M_n$ to $M_n$ corresponding to the morphism of functors $(E,\alpha)\mapsto (E,\alpha/p)$:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
E&\longrightarrow&E\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
M_n&\xrightarrow{\ I_p\ }&M_n
\end{array}
\qquad
I_p^*(E,\alpha)=(E,\alpha/p).
\tag{3.17}
\]
$I_p^*$ is an automorphism of $\widetilde H^i(M_n^{\an},\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z))$. It is tiresome, but routine, to prove the following.

\begin{proposition}[3.18]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The endomorphism $T_p$ of ${}_nW$ is expressed, with the aid of (3.16), as the composite map
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\widetilde H^1(M_n^{\an},\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Q))
\xrightarrow{\ q_2^*\ }
\widetilde H^1(M_{n,p}^{\an},\Sym^k(R^1v_*\Q))\\
&\xrightarrow{\ \varphi^*\ }
\widetilde H^1(M_{n,p}^{\an},\Sym^k(R^1u_*\Q))
\xrightarrow{\ q_{1*}\ }
\widetilde H^1(M_n^{\an},\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Q)).
\end{aligned}
\]
where $q_{1*}$ is the trace morphism for the covering $q_1$.
\item Similarly, $R_p=p^k I_p^*$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\QED

The distrustful reader could forget the adelic preliminaries and define $T_p$ by (i). When $n=1$ or $2$, one puts ${}_nW=W^{K_n}$, so that
\[
{}_1W={}_nW^{\GL_2(\Z/(n))}.
\]
If $S_{k+2}$ denotes the space of cusp forms, for the group $\SL_2(\Z)$, of weight $k+2$, the Shimura isomorphism (3.11) induces an isomorphism
\[
{}^k_1W_\infty={}^k_1W\otimes\C=S_{k+2}\oplus \overline{S}_{k+2}.
\]
It is tiresome, but routine, to prove the following.

\begin{proposition}[3.19]
The endomorphism $T_p$ of ${}^k_1W_\infty$ is identified, via the Shimura isomorphism, with the direct sum of the Hecke operator on $S_{k+2}$, including the factor $p^{k-1}$, and its conjugate.
\end{proposition}
\QED

\paragraph{(3.20)}
One has canonically
\[
\bigwedge^2 R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell\simeq R^2f_{n*}\Z_\ell\simeq \Z_\ell(-1),
\]
so that $\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell)$ is equipped with a bilinear form, symmetric for $k$ even and alternating for $k$ odd, with values in $\Z_\ell(-k)$. The form induced by tensoring with $\Q_\ell$ is nondegenerate.

If $F$ is an lcc $\Q_\ell$-sheaf over a smooth scheme $X$ of pure dimension $n$ over an algebraically closed field $k$, then Poincaré duality gives
\[
H^i(X,F)^\vee\simeq H_c^{2n-i}(X,\Hom(F,\Q_\ell(n))),
\]
\[
H_c^i(X,F)^\vee\simeq H^{2n-i}(X,\Hom(F,\Q_\ell(n))),
\]
\[
\widetilde H^i(X,F)^\vee\simeq \widetilde H^{2n-i}(X,\Hom(F,\Q_\ell(n))).
\]
Taking $X=\overline M_n$ and $F=\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Q_\ell)$ in these considerations, one defines a nondegenerate bilinear form ${}_n(\ ,\ )$ on ${}^k_nW_\ell$ with values in $\Q_\ell(-k-1)$. This form is symmetric for $k$ odd and alternating for $k$ even. This is the $\ell$-adic analogue of the Peterson inner product. If $n\mid m$, and if the covering $\psi:M_m\to M_n$ is of degree $d$, then one has
\[
{}_m(\psi^*x,\psi^*y)=d\cdot {}_n(x,y).
\]

\section{The Congruence Formula}

In this section we fix integers $k\geq 0$ and $n\geq 3$, and prime numbers $p$ and $\ell$. We suppose that $p$ is prime to $\ell$ and $n$. We denote by $f_n:E\to M_n$ the universal elliptic curve over $M_n$, equipped with $\alpha:E_n\xrightarrow{\sim}\Z/(n)^2$.

Whatever the scheme $Y$, one denotes by $a$ the unique morphism of $Y$ to $\Spec(\Z)$ or, if necessary, to a subscheme of $\Spec(\Z)$. If $Y$ is separated and of finite type over $\Spec(\Z)$, and if $F$ is a $\Z_\ell$- or $\Q_\ell$-sheaf on $Y$, one denotes by $R^ia_*(Y,F)$, respectively $R^ia_!(Y,F)$, respectively $R^i\widetilde a(Y,F)$, the $\Z_\ell$- or $\Q_\ell$-sheaf over $\Spec(\Z)$ that is the $i$th higher direct image of $F$ under $a$, respectively the $i$th direct image with proper supports, respectively $\Imop(R^ia_!(Y,F)\to R^ia_*(Y,F))$. For $m\in\mathbb N$, one puts
\[
Y[1/m]=Y\times\Spec(\Z[1/m]).
\]

\begin{theorem}[4.1, Igusa {\rm [1]}]
The scheme $M_n$ can be compactified to a scheme of curves $M_n^*$ that is projective and smooth over $\Spec(\Z[1/n])$, such that $M_n^*\setminus M_n$ is an étale covering of $\Spec(\Z[1/n])$.
\end{theorem}

The schemes $M_n$ are formally smooth, thus smooth over $\Spec(\Z)$. The modular invariant $j$ of the universal curve over $M_n$ is a morphism of $M_n$ to the affine line $\mathbb A^1$ over $\Spec(\Z[1/n])$. The morphism $j$ is finite, and is an étale covering away from the sections $0$ and $1728$ of $\mathbb A^1$; in fact:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Two elliptic curves over an algebraically closed field with the same $j$-invariant are isomorphic (e.g. [8] 6.3), thus the geometric fibers of $j$ are finite. The schemes $M_n$ and $\mathbb A^1$ being smooth of the same dimension over $\Spec(\Z)$, $j$ is quasi-finite and flat.
\item If $E$ is an elliptic curve over the field of fractions $K$ of a discrete valuation ring $R$, of $j$-invariant in $R$, and whose points of order $n$ are rational over $K$, then $E$ has good reduction. The valuative criterion for properness shows thus that $j$ is proper.
\item If $E$ and $F$ are two elliptic curves over a scheme $S$, of the same $j$-invariant, and if $j$ and $j-1728$ are invertible, then the scheme $\Isom(S;E,F)$ of isomorphisms between $E$ and $F$ is étale over $S$ ([8] 6.3). In the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Isom(M_n\times_{\mathbb A^1}M_n;\operatorname{pr}_1^*E,\operatorname{pr}_2^*E) & \sim & M_n\times \GL_2(\Z/(n))\\
\downarrow u && \downarrow v\\
M_n\times_{\mathbb A^1}M_n&\xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_1}&M_n,
\end{array}
\]
where $j\ne 0,1728$, $u$ and $v$ are étale surjective, thus $\operatorname{pr}_1$ is étale and, by flat descent, $j$ is étale.
\end{enumerate}
The section at infinity of the projective line $\mathbb P^1\supset \mathbb A^1$ over $\Spec(\Z[1/n])$ is a regular divisor whose generic point is of characteristic $0$ in a regular scheme. It follows from a theorem of Abyankhar (see [5]) that along this divisor $j=\infty$, $M_n$ is moderately (tamely?) ramified over $\mathbb P^1$, and that the normalization $M_n^*$ of $\mathbb P^1$ in $M_n$ satisfies (4.1). \QED

It follows from the same theorem that the lcc $\Z_\ell$-sheaves $R^if_{n*}\Z_\ell$ on $M_n[1/\ell]$ are moderately ramified at infinity. Hence, from (4.1) and the specialization theorems for $\ell$-adic cohomology (see [5]), it follows that $R^ia_*(M_n,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))$, $R^ia_!(M_n,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))$, and therefore $R^i\widetilde a(M_n,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))$ are lcc $\Z_\ell$-sheaves over $\Spec(\Z[1/n,1/\ell])$, whose formation is compatible with all base changes.

\begin{proposition}[4.2, Corollary]
The Galois module ${}_nW_\ell$ is the fiber of the lcc $\Q_\ell$-sheaf
\[
R^1\widetilde a(M_n,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))\otimes\Q_\ell
\]
over $\Spec(\Z[1/n,1/\ell])$ at the geometric point $\overline\Q$. It is unramified away from $n$ and $\ell$.
\end{proposition}
\QED

Let the two commutative diagrams over $M_n\otimes\F_p$
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
&&\Z/(n)^2&&\\
&\alpha\nearrow&&\nwarrow\alpha^{(p)}&\\
E_n&\xrightarrow{\ F\ }&E_n^{(p)}&&\\
\cap&&\cap&&\\
E&\xrightarrow{\ F\ }&E^{(p)}&&
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{ccccc}
&&\Z/(n)^2&&\\
&p\alpha^{(p)}\nearrow&&\nwarrow\alpha&\\
E_n^{(p)}&\xrightarrow{\ V\ }&E_n&&\\
\cap&&\cap&&\\
E^{(p)}&\xrightarrow{\ V\ }&E&&
\end{array}
\]
be written more briefly
\[
F:(E,\alpha)\to (E^{(p)},\alpha^{(p)})
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
V:(E^{(p)},p\alpha^{(p)})\to(E,\alpha),
\]
where $F$ is the Frobenius morphism and $V$, its transpose, is the Verschiebung. These diagrams define morphisms $\Phi_1$ and $\Phi_2$ of $M_n\otimes\F_p$ to $M_{n,p}$. These morphisms are finite, considered as sections of $q_1$ and $q_2$, and define a morphism
\[
\Phi=\Phi_1\coprod\Phi_2:M_n\otimes\F_p\coprod M_n\otimes\F_p\to M_{n,p}\otimes\F_p.
\]
Let $\Phi^h$ be the restriction of $\Phi$ to the opens $M_n^h$ and $M_{n,p}^h$ of $M_n\otimes\F_p$ and $M_{n,p}\otimes\F_p$ corresponding to curves of nonzero Hasse invariant $h$.

\begin{proposition}[4.3]
$\Phi^h$ is an isomorphism.
\end{proposition}

Let $\varphi:E_1\to E_2$ be a $p$-isogeny between elliptic curves of invertible Hasse invariant over a scheme $S$ of characteristic $p$. At each geometric point of $S$, either the kernel $\ker(\varphi)$ of $\varphi$ is étale over $S$, or its Cartier dual, isomorphic to $\ker({}^{t}\!\varphi)$, is étale over $S$. The property ``$\ker(\varphi)$ is étale'' is an open property, so that locally on $S$, either $\ker(\varphi)$ is purely infinitesimal, or $\ker({}^{t}\!\varphi)$ is. The only infinitesimal subgroup of order $p$ of $E_1$ or $E_2$ being the kernel of Frobenius, in the first case $\varphi$ is isomorphic to $F:E_1\to E_1^{(p)}$, and in the second case ${}^{t}\!\varphi$ is isomorphic to $F:E_2\to E_2^{(p)}$ and $\varphi$ to $V:E_2^{(p)}\to E_2$. \QED

\begin{proposition}[4.4]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The scheme $M_{n,p}$ is smooth over $\Spec(\Z)$ away from points of characteristic $p$ where $h=0$.
\item The morphisms $q_1$ and $q_2$ induce finite flat morphisms $q'_1$ and $q'_2$ from the normalization $M'_{n,p}$ of $M_{n,p}$ to $M_n$.
\item The morphism $\Phi$ can be factored through a surjective morphism
\[
\Phi':M_n\otimes\F_p\coprod M_n\otimes\F_p\to M'_{n,p}\otimes\F_p.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The automorphism $\sigma$ of (3.15) exchanges $\varphi$ and ${}^{t}\!\varphi$, so that it suffices to prove (i) at the points of characteristic $p$ of $M_{n,p}$ where the kernel of $\varphi$ is infinitesimal: there is no obstruction to infinitesimally lifting an elliptic curve and the infinitesimal part of the kernel of multiplication by $p$.

Where $p=h=0$, the fiber of the finite morphism (3.15) $q_i:M_{n,p}\to M_n$ is reduced to a point, so that the open of smoothness of $M_{n,p}$ is dense in $M_{n,p}$ and $M'_{n,p}$ is everywhere of dimension $2$. The scheme $M_n$ being regular, following EGA $0_{\mathrm{IV}}$ 16.5.1 and 17.3.5(ii), the morphism $q_i:M'_{n,p}\to M_n$ is flat. Finally, (iii) results from the fact that $\Phi$ is finite and $M_n\otimes\F_p$ is a normal curve. \QED

The Hecke endomorphism $T_p$ of ${}_nW_\ell$, such as is made explicit in (3.18), is the tensorization with $\Q_\ell$ of the fiber at the geometric point $\overline\Q$ of $\Spec(\Z[1/n,1/\ell])$ of the endomorphism, again denoted by $T_p$, of $R^1\widetilde a(M_n,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))$ defined by the correspondence
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
q_1'^*E&\xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }&q_2'^*E&&\\
\swarrow&&\searrow&&\\
E&\xleftarrow{\ u\ }&M'_{n,p}&\xrightarrow{\ v\ }&E\\
\downarrow f_n&&\downarrow q'_1\quad\downarrow q'_2&&\downarrow f_n\\
M_n&&M_n&&M_n,
\end{array}
\tag{4.5}
\]
with
\[
T_p=q'_{1*}\,\varphi^*\,q_2'^* \qquad (\text{cf. }3.18).
\]
The endomorphisms $R_p$ and $I_p$ can be interpreted in the same way.

\begin{lemma}[4.6]
Let $X,Y,Z_1$, and $Z_2$ be four schemes that are separated and of finite type over a noetherian scheme $S$, let $F$ be a $\Z_\ell$-sheaf on $X$, let $G$ be a $\Z_\ell$-sheaf on $Y$, and denote by $a$ each of the structural maps from $X,Y,Z_1$ or $Z_2$ to $S$. Suppose we have the following commutative diagram of $S$-schemes and morphisms of sheaves:
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
y_1^*G&\xrightarrow{z_1}&x_1^*F&&y_2^*G\xrightarrow{z_2}x_2^*F\\
&&&&\\[-1em]
Z_1&\xrightarrow{f}&Z_2&&Z_1\xrightarrow{f}Z_2\\[-0.2em]
\downarrow x_1&&\downarrow x_2&&\downarrow y_1\quad\downarrow y_2\\[-0.2em]
X&&X&&Y
\end{array}
\]
Suppose that $f^*z_2=z_1$, that $y_1$ and $y_2$ are proper, that $x_1$ and $x_2$ are finite and flat, and that, for every geometric point $s$ of $Z_2$, the multiplicity of $s$ in its fiber $x_2^{-1}(x_2(s))$ is equal to the sum of the multiplicities in their fibers, for $x_1$, of the geometric points of $Z_1$ in the inverse image of $s$ under $f$. Then the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
R^i\widetilde a(Y,G)&\xrightarrow{y_1^*}&R^i\widetilde a(Z_1,G)&\xrightarrow{z_1}&R^i\widetilde a(Z_1,F)\xrightarrow{x_{1*}}R^i\widetilde a(X,F)\\
\Vert&&\uparrow f^*&&\uparrow f^*\qquad\Vert\\
R^i\widetilde a(Y,G)&\xrightarrow{y_2^*}&R^i\widetilde a(Z_2,G)&\xrightarrow{z_2}&R^i\widetilde a(Z_2,F)\xrightarrow{x_{2*}}R^i\widetilde a(X,F)
\end{array}
\]
commutes.
\end{lemma}

This lemma results from the analogous lemmas for $R^ia_!$ and $R^ia_*$. The commutativity of the first squares is trivial. The last is rewritten
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
R^i\widetilde a(Z_1,x_1^*F)&\xleftarrow{\sim}&R^i\widetilde a(X,x_{1*}x_1^*F)\xrightarrow{\Tr}R^i\widetilde a(X,F)\\
\uparrow&&\uparrow\qquad\Vert\\
R^i\widetilde a(Z_2,x_2^*F)&\xleftarrow{\sim}&R^i\widetilde a(X,x_{2*}x_2^*F)\xrightarrow{\Tr}R^i\widetilde a(X,F),
\end{array}
\]
and one recalls the definition of the trace in verifying that the square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
x_{1*}x_1^*F&\xrightarrow{\Tr}&F\\
\uparrow&&\Vert\\
x_{2*}x_2^*F&\xrightarrow{\Tr}&F
\end{array}
\]
is commutative. \QED

\paragraph{(4.7)}
One denotes by $T_p/\F_p$ the endomorphism induced by $T_p$ on the restriction to $\Spec(\F_p)$ of the lcc $\Z_\ell$-sheaf $R^1\widetilde a(M_n,\Sym^k R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell)$. One has
\[
R^1\widetilde a(M_n,\Sym^k R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell)|\Spec(\F_p)
\xrightarrow{\sim}
R^1\widetilde a(M_n\otimes\F_p,\Sym^k R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell);
\]
the formation of the trace morphism for a finite flat morphism is compatible with change of base, so that $T_p/\F_p$ can be constructed, over the model (3.18), starting from the fiber over $\F_p$ of the correspondence (4.5). Lemma (4.6), applied to the commutative diagrams
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
M_n\otimes\F_p\coprod M_n\otimes\F_p&\xrightarrow{\Phi'}&M'_{n,p}\otimes\F_p\\
\downarrow&&\swarrow q'_1\\
M_n\otimes\F_p&&
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{ccc}
M_n\otimes\F_p\coprod M_n\otimes\F_p&\xrightarrow{\Phi'}&M'_{n,p}\otimes\F_p\\
&&q'_2\searrow\quad\downarrow\\
&&M_n\otimes\F_p,
\end{array}
\]
fournishes then a decomposition of $T_p/\F_p$ into the sum of endomorphisms defined by the two following correspondences:
\[
\begin{array}{cccccc}
&& (E,\alpha)&\xrightarrow{F}&(E^{(p)},\alpha^{(p)})=F^*(E,\alpha)\\[-0.2em]
(a)&& \swarrow && \searrow\\[-0.2em]
& (E,\alpha) && M_n\otimes\F_p && (E,\alpha)\\[-0.2em]
&& \searrow && \swarrow F\\[-0.2em]
&& M_n\otimes\F_p && M_n\otimes\F_p
\end{array}
\]
where $F$ is the absolute Frobenius. One recognizes in this correspondence the geometric Frobenius
\[
\begin{array}{cccccc}
&&x^*(E,\alpha)=(E^{(p)},p\alpha^{(p)})&\xrightarrow{\ V\ }&(E,\alpha)\\[-0.2em]
(b)&& \swarrow && \searrow\\[-0.2em]
& (E,\alpha) && M_n\otimes\F_p && (E,\alpha)\\[-0.2em]
&& \searrow x && \swarrow\\[-0.2em]
&& M_n\otimes\F_p && M_n\otimes\F_p.
\end{array}
\]
The map $x$ is the composite map $I_p^{-1}\circ F$:
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
(E,\alpha)&\longleftarrow&(E,p\alpha)&\longleftarrow&(E^{(p)},p\alpha^{(p)})\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
M_n\otimes\F_p&\xleftarrow{\ I_p^{-1}\ }&M_n\otimes\F_p&\xleftarrow{\ F\ }&M_n\otimes\F_p.
\end{array}
\]
The corresponding endomorphism is thus the composite of
\[
V:
R^1\widetilde a(M_n\otimes\F_p,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))
\xrightarrow{V^*}
R^1\widetilde a(M_n\otimes\F_p,\Sym^k(R^1f^{(p)}_{n*}\Z_\ell))
\xrightarrow{\Tr_F}
R^1\widetilde a(M_n\otimes\F_p,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))
\]
and of
\[
I_p^*=\Tr_{I_p^{-1}}:\quad\text{endomorphism of }R^1\widetilde a(M_n\otimes\F_p,\Sym^k R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell).
\]

\begin{proposition}[4.8]
One has $T_p/\F_p=F+I_p^*V$, and:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $F$ can be identified with the inverse of the arithmetic Frobenius element $\varphi_p$ of the Galois group $\Gal(\overline\F_p/\F_p)$ acting on $\widetilde H^1(M_n\otimes\overline\F_p,\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell))$;
\item $F$ and $V$ are transposes of one another, relative to the scalar product (3.20);
\item $FV=VF=p^{k+1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For the relation (i) between the geometric and arithmetic Frobenius, one is referred to the exposé of C. Houzel (SGA 5.XV). The composite $VF$ is the composite of the homomorphisms obtained from the following maps:
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
E&\xleftarrow{F_E}&E^{(p)}&\xrightarrow{V_E}&E\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
M_n&\xrightarrow{F}&M_n&\xrightarrow{F}&M_n.
\end{array}
\]
Thus
\[
VF=\Tr_F\circ F_E^*\circ V_E^*\circ F^*.
\]
The map $F_E^*V_E^*=(F_EV_E)^*=(p\cdot 1_E)^*$ acts by multiplication by $p^k$ on $\Sym^k R^1f_{n*}\Z_\ell$, so that
\[
VF=p^k\Tr_F\circ F^*=p^k\cdot p=p^{k+1},
\]
because $F:M_n\to M_n$ is of degree $p$.

By transport of structure, $\varphi_p$ respects the scalar product (3.20) with values in $\Q_\ell(-k-1)$, a group on which $\varphi_p$ acts by multiplication by $p^{-k-1}$. One has thus
\[
(Fx,y)=p^{k+1}(\varphi_pFx,\varphi_py)=(x,p^{k+1}F^{-1}y)=(x,Vy).
\]
\QED

The following theorem, synonymous with (4.8), goes back to Eichler.

\begin{theorem}[4.9, The Congruence Formula]
Let $K_{n,\ell}$ be the largest subextension of $\overline\Q$ that is unramified away from $n$ and $\ell$, let $\varphi_p$ be a Frobenius element relative to $p$ in $\Gal(K_{n,\ell}/\Q)$, let $F$ be the endomorphism $\varphi_p^{-1}$ of ${}_nW_\ell$, and let $V$ be the transpose of $F$ relative to the scalar product (3.20). Then
\[
T_p=F+I_p^*V,
\qquad
FV=p^{k+1},
\qquad
1-T_pX+pR_pX^2=(1-FX)(1-I_p^*VX).
\]
\end{theorem}
\QED

\section{Weil implies Ramanujan}

If $p$ is a prime number and $X$ a scheme over $\F_p$, then one denotes by $\overline\F_p$ an algebraic closure of $\F_p$, by $F:X\to X$ the geometric Frobenius endomorphism, and one puts $\overline X=X\otimes\overline\F_p$; $\ell$ always denotes a prime number different from $p$.

By the Weil conjectures one means to claim the following: let $X$ be a projective smooth scheme over $\F_p$ and let $\ell$ be a prime number different from $p$. Then the eigenvalues of the endomorphism $F^*$ of $H^i(\overline X,\Q_\ell)$ are algebraic integers all of whose complex conjugates have absolute value $p^{i/2}$.

With the hypotheses and notations of (4.9), one has, recalling that $(p,n)=1$:

\begin{theorem}[5.1]
If the Weil conjectures are true, then the absolute values of the endomorphism $F$ of ${}^k_nW_\ell$ are algebraic integers, all of whose complex conjugates are of absolute value $p^{k+1/2}$.
\end{theorem}

Assume the Weil conjectures.

\begin{lemma}[5.2, modulo Weil]
Let $X$ be a smooth scheme over $\F_p$, which can be represented as an open in a smooth projective scheme $X^*$. Then the absolute values of the endomorphism $F^*$ of $\widetilde H^i(X,\Q_\ell)$ are algebraic integers of absolute value $p^{i/2}$.
\end{lemma}

The natural map of $H_c^i(X,\Q_\ell)$ to $H^i(X,\Q_\ell)$ can be factored through $H^i(X^*,\Q_\ell)$:
\[
H_c^i(X,\Q_\ell)\to H^i(X^*,\Q_\ell)\to H^i(X,\Q_\ell),
\]
so that, as a $\Gal(\overline\F_p/\F_p)$-module, $\widetilde H^i(X,\Q_\ell)$ is the quotient of a subobject of $H^i(X^*,\Q_\ell)$. \QED

\begin{lemma}[5.3, modulo Weil]
Let $S$ be a smooth scheme over $\F_p$ and $f:A\to S$ an abelian scheme over $S$. Suppose that $A$ can be represented as an open in a projective smooth scheme $A^*$ over $\F_p$. Then the geometric Frobenius $F^*$ of $\widetilde H^i(S,R^jf_*\Q_\ell)$ has eigenvalues that are algebraic integers of absolute value $p^{i+j/2}$.
\end{lemma}

Let $m$ be an integer $>1$, and consider the Leray spectral sequences
\[
E:
E_2^{ij}=H^i(\overline S,R^jf_*\Q_\ell)\Longrightarrow H^{i+j}(\overline A,\Q_\ell),
\qquad
{}_cE:
{}_cE_2^{ij}=H_c^i(\overline S,R^jf_*\Q_\ell)\Longrightarrow H_c^{i+j}(\overline A,\Q_\ell).
\]
The endomorphism of multiplication by $m$, $\psi_m=m\cdot 1_A$, defines endomorphisms of $E$ and ${}_cE$ that fit into a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
{}_cE&\longrightarrow&E\\
\psi_m^*\downarrow&&\psi_m^*\downarrow\\
{}_cE&\longrightarrow&E.
\end{array}
\]
$\psi_m^*$ acts on $R^jf_*\Q_\ell$ by multiplication by $m^j$, so that $\psi_m^*$ acts by multiplication by $m^j$ on the terms ${}_cE_r^{ij}$ and $E_r^{ij}$ of ${}_cE$ and $E$. The maps $d_r$ for $r\ge 2$ commute with $\psi_m^*$, and send $E_r^{ij}$, respectively ${}_cE_r^{ij}$, to $E_r^{i'j'}$, respectively ${}_cE_r^{i'j'}$, with $j\ne j'$. These are nonzero, and $E_2^{ij}$, respectively ${}_cE_2^{ij}$, can be identified with a subspace of $H^{i+j}(\overline A,\Q_\ell)$, respectively $H_c^{i+j}(\overline A,\Q_\ell)$, where $\psi_m^*=m^j$. Consequently, $\widetilde H^i(S,R^jf_*\Q_\ell)$ is identified, as a Galois module, with a subspace of $\widetilde H^{i+j}(A,\Q_\ell)$ where $\psi_m^*=m^j$, and one applies (5.2). The trick used here is due to Lieberman. \QED

Let $f_n:E\to M_n\otimes\F_p$ be the universal elliptic curve over $M_n\otimes\F_p$, and let $f_{n,k}:E_k\to M_n\otimes\F_p$ be its $k$-fold fiber product with itself. The Künneth formula shows that the $\Q_\ell$-sheaf $R^kf_{n,k*}\Q_\ell$ admits as a direct factor the $k$-fold tensor power of $R^1f_{n*}\Q_\ell$; the latter in turn contains as a direct factor the $\Q_\ell$-sheaf $\Sym^k(R^1f_{n*}\Q_\ell)$. Theorem 5.1 results thus from (5.3) and the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}[5.4]
The scheme $E_k$ is an open in a smooth projective scheme $E_k^*$ over $\F_p$.
\end{lemma}

Let $E^*$ be the Néron minimal model of $E$ over $M_n^*\otimes\F_p$ (4.1). The scheme $E^*$ is smooth and projective over $\F_p$. Since $n\ge 3$ and the points of order $n$ of $E$ form a trivial covering of $M_n\otimes\F_p$, the Néron model is semistable, case a or b$_m$ in Néron's classification. In particular, the projection $f_n:E^*\to M_n^*$ has only a finite number of nonsmooth points, and at these points $f_n$ is nondegenerate, presenting an ordinary quadratic singularity.

Let $E_k^{**}$ be the $k$-fold fiber product of $E^*$ over $M_n^*$. To prove (5.4), it suffices to resolve the singularities of $E_k^{**}$ without touching the open $E_k$. We first prove:

\begin{lemma}[5.5]
Let $V$ be the subvariety of affine space over a field $k$, with coordinates $(X_i)_{0\le i\le r}$, $(Y_i)_{0\le i\le r}$, and $(T_i)_{1\le i\le s}$, with equation
\[
X_0Y_0=X_1Y_1=\cdots=X_rY_r.
\]
Let $\mathfrak m$ be the ideal of $\cO_V$ generated by the monomials obtained from the monomial $\prod_{i=0}^r X_i^i$ by a permutation of the coordinates which respects the set of pairs $\{X_i,Y_i\}$, $0\le i\le r$. Then $\mathfrak m=\cO_V$ away from the singular locus of $V$, and the variety $\widetilde V$ obtained from $V$ by blowing up $\mathfrak m$ is smooth over $k$.
\end{lemma}

The singular locus is the place where the four coordinates $X_i,Y_i,X_j,Y_j$ for $i\ne j$ vanish. The open affine of $\widetilde V$ defined by the element $\prod_{i=0}^r X_i^i$ of the ideal $\mathfrak m$ of blowing up is the spectrum of the regular ring
\[
k[Y_0/X_1, X_0/X_1, X_1/X_2,\ldots,X_{r-1}/X_r,X_r,T_1,\ldots,T_s]
\]
(for the proof, note that $X_i/X_{i+1}=Y_{i+1}/Y_i$), and 5.5 results. \QED

One still shows that, locally for the étale topology, the singularities of $E_k^{**}$ are isomorphic to those of $V$ for $r=k-1$, and that this allows the definition on $E_k^{**}$ of an ideal $\mathfrak m$ analogous to the ideal $\mathfrak m$ of (5.5). Blowing up this ideal, one obtains $E_k^*$. \QED

An approximation to the following theorem has been proven by Ihara [2].

\begin{theorem}[5.6]
The Weil conjectures imply the Ramanujan conjecture.
\end{theorem}

We note first that (5.1) is true for $n=1$, because ${}^k_1W_\ell$ is the Galois submodule of ${}^k_mW_\ell$ invariant under $\GL_2(\Z/(m))$. On ${}^k_1W_\ell$, $I_p^*$ induces the identity, and (4.8) reduces to
\[
1-T_pX+p^{k+1}X^2=(1-FX)(1-VX).
\]
The endomorphisms $F$ and $V$ are transposes of each other, so that
\[
\det(1-FX;{}^k_1W_\ell)=\det(1-VX;{}^k_1W_\ell).
\]
The action of $T_p$ on ${}^k_1W_\ell$ is induced by its action on ${}^k_1W$, and is compatible with the decomposition of ${}^k_1W\otimes\C$ into the sum of the space $S_{k+2}$ of cusp forms relative to $\SL_2(\Z)$ of weight $k+2$, and of the complex conjugate space. From the Hermitian property for $T_p$, for the Peterson inner product, and from (3.19), one deduces then that
\[
\det(1-T_pX+p^{k+1}X^2;{}^k_1W_\ell)=\det(1-T_pX+p^{k+1}X^2;S_{k+2})^2,
\]
and
\[
\det(1-T_pX+p^{k+1}X^2;S_{k+2})^2=\det(1-FX;{}^k_1W_\ell)^2,
\]
so that
\[
\det(1-T_pX+p^{k+1}X^2;S_{k+2})=\det(1-FX;{}^k_1W_\ell).
\tag{5.7}
\]
Resume the notations of section 1 and make $k=10$. By virtue of Hecke's theory and of (3.19), (5.7) can be rewritten
\[
H_p(X)=\det(1-FX;{}^{10}_1W_\ell),
\]
and one applies (5.1). \QED

One verifies in the same way that the Weil conjectures imply Petersson's generalization of the Ramanujan conjecture.
