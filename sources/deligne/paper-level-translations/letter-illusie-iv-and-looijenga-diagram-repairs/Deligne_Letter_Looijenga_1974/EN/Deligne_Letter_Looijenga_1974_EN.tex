\section*{1. Coxeter transformations}

\subsection*{1.1}
Let $W$ be a finite Coxeter group, $D$ its Dynkin diagram, $V$ the space of its natural representation, and $R\subset W$ the set of reflections. I shall call a \emph{root} a unit vector orthogonal to a reflecting hyperplane. If $\alpha$ is a root, write
\[
 V^+(\alpha)=\{x\mid (x,\alpha)\geq 0\},\qquad M(\alpha)=\partial V^+(\alpha)
\]
for the corresponding wall, and $z_\alpha\in R$ for the reflection with respect to $M(\alpha)$. Assume that $D$ is irreducible and $D\ne A_1$.

\subsection*{1.2}
Let $c$ be a Coxeter transformation. The following facts are proved in Bourbaki, \emph{Groupes et algèbres de Lie}, Chapter V, §6, no. 2.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item Let $h$ be the order of $c$, and let $P\subset V$ be the largest subspace of $V$ on which $c$ has only the eigenvalues $\exp(\pm2\pi i/h)$. Then $\dim(P)=2$. Orient $P$ so that $c\vert_P$ is a rotation through angle $2\pi/h$.

\item No wall contains $P$; the walls cut on $P$ a system of reflecting hyperplanes of type $I_2(h)$.

\item The walls of $V$ containing the same wall $d$ of $P$ are pairwise orthogonal. Write $s(d)$ for the involution that is the product of the corresponding reflections.

\item Let $C_0$ be a chamber of $P$, let $C$ be the unique chamber of $V$ containing it, let $d_1^+$ and $d_2^+$ be the half-lines bounding $C_0$, taken in the positive direction, and let $d_1,d_2$ be the corresponding lines. Then the walls of $C$ are those containing $d_1$ or $d_2$, and
\[
 c=s(d_2)s(d_1).
\]
Moreover, $s(d_i)$ stabilizes $P$, its restriction to $P$ is the reflection with respect to $d_i$, and $c\vert_P$ is therefore a Coxeter transformation of $P$.
\end{enumerate}

Let $c\in W$ be a Coxeter element, and let $\alpha$ be a root.

\begin{corollaryx}[1.3]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item With the notation of 1.2(iv), the dihedral group $W'$ generated by $s(d_1)$ and $s(d_2)$ is the stabilizer of $P$. It is also the stabilizer of $\{c,c^{-1}\}$. The group $W'$ acts faithfully on $P$, and its restriction to $P$ is the Weyl group of $P$. The centralizer of $c$ is the subgroup $\langle c\rangle$ generated by $c$.

\item There is a chamber $C$ in which $\alpha$ is a simple root, and an ordering $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\ell)$ of the simple roots of $C$, with $\alpha=\alpha_1$, such that
\[
 c=z_{\alpha_1}\cdots z_{\alpha_\ell}.
\]

\item For $C$ as in (ii), the line $d$ obtained as the intersection of the walls $M(\alpha_i)$, $i\ne1$, is the subspace of $V$ fixed by $z_\alpha c$. Thus it does not depend on $C$. The subgroup $W_d$ of $W$ fixing $d$ is a Coxeter group, generated by the $z_{\alpha_i}$ for $i\ne1$; its natural representation is on $V/d$, and $z_\alpha c\in W_d$ is a Coxeter transformation of $W_d$.

\item The vertex of $D$ corresponding to the wall $M(\alpha)$ of $C$, with $C$ as in (ii), is independent of $C$. It is denoted by $\bar\alpha$.

\item $\langle c\rangle$ acts freely on the set of roots. Two roots lie in the same orbit if and only if they have the same image in $D$.

\item Let $C_0$ and $C$ be as in 1.2(iv). For each wall $M$ of $C$, let $\alpha=\alpha(M)$ be the root such that $M=M(\alpha)$ and such that $V^+(\alpha)$ contains, respectively does not contain, $C$, according as $M$ passes through $d_2$, respectively through $d_1$. Then
\[
 \{\alpha(M)\mid M\text{ a wall of }C\}
\]
is a section of the set of $\langle c\rangle$-orbits, and $\bar\alpha(M)$ is the class in $D$ of the wall $M$ of $C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollaryx}

\begin{proofx}
For (i), $W'$ stabilizes $\{c,c^{-1}\}$, since $s(d_i)c s(d_i)^{-1}=c^{-1}$. Conversely, $\{c,c^{-1}\}$ determines $P$; hence the stabilizer of $\{c,c^{-1}\}$ stabilizes $P$. An element $w\in W$ that stabilizes a chamber $C_0$ of $P$ is the identity, because $C_0$ is contained in a unique chamber. Since $W'$ is transitive on the chambers of $P$, it is the full stabilizer of $P$. Finally, $\langle c\rangle$ is the centralizer of $c$ in $W'$, hence also in $W$.

For (ii), take the chamber $C_0$ of $P$ whose wall is $M(\alpha)\cap P$, such that, with the notation of 1.2(iv), $M(\alpha)\cap P=d_2$ and $V^+(\alpha)\supset C_0$. Since $c=s(d_2)s(d_1)$, this choice works.
\[
\coxeterChamberOne
\]

For (iii), $z_\alpha c=z_{\alpha_2}\cdots z_{\alpha_\ell}$; the first assertion follows easily. That $z_\alpha c$ is Coxeter is seen by taking $C$ as in (ii).

For (iv), we check that $\bar\alpha$ depends only on $\alpha$ and $d$. If $C'$ and $C''$ are two chambers in which $\alpha$ is a simple root, and such that the half-line opposite the wall $M(\alpha)$ is $d^+=d\cap V^+(\alpha)$, then there exists $w\in W_d$ sending $C'$ to $C''$; indeed, the chambers containing $d^+$ correspond bijectively to those of $V/d$. Since $w$ preserves $d$, it preserves $M(\alpha)$, and (iv) follows.

Writing $c=s(d_2)s(d_1)$, one sees at once, for $M$ passing through $d_2$, that $\bar\alpha(M)$ is the class of the wall $M$ of $C$. Let $C_0'=s(d_1)C_0$, so $C'=s(d_1)C$; the walls passing through $d_1$ are treated in the same way. It is clear that the $\bar\alpha(M)$ form a section of the $\langle c\rangle$-orbits in the set of roots, and that the action of $\langle c\rangle$ is free. Since $c^{-1}\alpha$ is carried along in the same way, (v) and (vi) follow.
\[
\coxeterChamberTwo
\]
\end{proofx}

\begin{remarkx}[1.4]
No longer assume that $D$ is irreducible or different from $A_1$. Assertions (ii), (iii), and (iv) still hold. The second part of (v) holds after replacing $\langle c\rangle$ by the centralizer of $c$. Write $h(D)$ for the order of this centralizer; then
\[
 h\Bigl(\coprod D_i\Bigr)=\prod h(D_i).
\]
\end{remarkx}

\begin{corollaryx}[1.5]
Let $W$ be a finite Coxeter group and let $c\in W$ be a Coxeter element. Let $\Xi_c$ be the set of sequences of $\ell$ roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\ell$, and let $\Xi'_c$ be the set of sequences of $\ell$ reflections $z_1,\ldots,z_\ell$, where $\ell=\rank W$, such that
\[
 z_{\alpha_1}\cdots z_{\alpha_\ell}=c,
 \qquad z_1\cdots z_\ell=c.
\]
The numbers
\[
 F(D)=\card\Xi_c,
 \qquad F'(D)=\card\Xi'_c
\]
depend only on $D$, and satisfy $F(D)=2^\ell F'(D)$. For irreducible $D$ one has
\[
 F(D)=h(D)\sum_{i\in D}F(D-\{i\}),
\]
hence
\[
 F'(D)=\frac{h(D)}{2}\sum_{i\in D}F'(D-\{i\}).
\]
Indeed, by 1.3(ii),
\[
 F(D)=\sum_{\alpha\;\mathrm{root}}F(D-\{\bar\alpha\}),
\]
and one applies 1.3(v), supplemented by 1.4.
\end{corollaryx}

\begin{remarkx}[1.6]
One has $F(\varnothing)=F'(\varnothing)=1$. If $D=\coprod D_i$ and $D_i$ has rank $\ell_i$, while $\ell=\sum\ell_i$, then
\[
 F(D)=\frac{\ell!}{\prod\ell_i!}\prod_i F(D_i),
\]
and similarly for $F'$.
\end{remarkx}

\begin{remarkx}[1.7]
For fixed $c$, we have defined a map $\alpha\mapsto\bar\alpha$ from the set of roots to $D$. The image $\overline{-\alpha}$ is the transform of $\bar\alpha$ by the opposition involution. Thus, if $D'$ denotes the quotient of $D$ by the opposition involution, $\alpha\mapsto\bar\alpha$ descends to the quotient and defines a map $z\mapsto\bar z:R\to D'$.
\end{remarkx}

\section*{2. Action of the braid group}

Let $W$ be a finite Coxeter group, $D$ its Dynkin diagram, $D'$ the quotient of $D$ by the opposition involution, $\ell$ the rank of $W$, $c$ a Coxeter transformation, and $\Xi'_c$ as in 1.5. Let $B_\ell$ be the braid group on $\ell$ strands, with canonical generators $(w_i)_{1\le i\le \ell-1}$. Make it act on $\Xi'_c$ by
\[
 w_i:(z_1,\ldots,z_\ell)\longmapsto (z_1,\ldots,z_{i-1},z_{i+1},z_{i+1}z_i z_{i+1},z_{i+2},\ldots,z_\ell).
\]
\[
\braidMoveSketch
\]

\begin{theoremx}[2.1]
$B_\ell$ acts transitively on $\Xi'_c$.
\end{theoremx}

\begin{proofx}
Proceed by induction on $\ell$. If $D$ is a disjoint union of diagrams $D_i$, and if the theorem is true for the $D_i$, then it is true for $D$. We are therefore reduced to assuming $D$ irreducible. The cases $D=\varnothing$ and $D=A_1$ are trivial.

\begin{lemmax}[2.1.1]
Let $\sigma'=(z'_1,\ldots,z'_\ell)$ and $\sigma''=(z''_1,\ldots,z''_\ell)$ be in $\Xi'_c$. If $z'_1=z''_1$, then $\sigma'$ and $\sigma''$ lie in the same $B_\ell$-orbit.
\end{lemmax}

Let $d$ be the subspace of $V$ fixed by $z'_1c$. Apply the induction hypothesis to $W_d$ and to $\Xi'_{z'_1c}$, using 1.3(iii).

Say that two roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are equivalent if there exist $\sigma'=(z'_1,\ldots,z'_\ell)$ and $\sigma''=(z''_1,\ldots,z''_\ell)$ in $\Xi'_c$, conjugate under $B_\ell$, such that $z'_1=z_\alpha$ and $z''_1=z_\beta$. It remains to prove that any two roots are equivalent.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item $\alpha$ and $c^{-1}\alpha$ are equivalent. Take $\sigma=(z_\alpha,z_2,\ldots,z_\ell)$ in $\Xi'_c$; then
\[
 w_1\cdots w_{\ell-2}w_{\ell-1}w_{\ell-2}\cdots w_1(\sigma)=(c^{-1}z_\alpha c,\ldots).
\]

\item $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$ are equivalent, since $z_\alpha=z_{-\alpha}$.
\end{enumerate}

By 1.3(v) and 1.7, there is therefore an equivalence relation on $D'$ such that $\alpha\sim\beta$ if and only if $\bar\alpha$ and $\bar\beta$ have equivalent images in $D'$. Since $W$ acts trivially on $D'$, this equivalence does not depend on the choice of $c$. Write $\sim$ also for the inverse-image equivalence on $D$.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em,start=3]
\item If $i,j$ are two unjoined vertices of $D$, then $i\sim j$. Take $c=z_i z_j\cdots$, a product of fundamental reflections. Then
\[
 w_1(z_i,z_j,\ldots)=(z_j,z_i,\ldots),
\]
and $\bar z_i=i$, $\bar z_j=j$.

\item For $i,j$ as below, with $i$ an end vertex, one has $i\sim j$.
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=-.5ex,scale=.7]
\draw (0,0)--(1.2,0)--(2.3,0);
\fill (0,0) circle (2pt) node[below]{$i$};
\fill (1.2,0) circle (2pt) node[below]{$j$};
\fill (2.3,0) circle (1.5pt);
\draw[dotted] (2.45,0)--(3.2,0);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
Take $c=z_jz_i\cdots$, a product of fundamental reflections. Then
\[
 w_1(z_j,z_i,\ldots)=(z_i,z_i z_j z_i,\ldots),
\]
where $z_i z_j z_i$ is the fundamental reflection for the chamber $z_iC$, and $\bar z_j=j$, $\bar z_i=i$.
\end{enumerate}

Parts (c) and (d) give the equivalence of all vertices, and hence the theorem.
\end{proofx}

\section*{3. Calculation of $F'(D)$}

\begin{corollaryx}[3.1]
\[
 F'(A_\ell)=(\ell+1)^{\ell-1}.
\]
In this case, you have in fact proved your conjecture directly. On the other hand, by 2.1, $\Xi'_c$ is a single $B_\ell$-orbit, and one applies your computation of the index.

This is also equivalent to the formula you give for trees: for a product of $\ell$ transpositions in a set with $\ell+1$ elements to be an $(\ell+1)$-cycle, it is necessary and sufficient that the graph obtained by joining the points appearing in these transpositions be a tree. Since there are $\ell!$ $(\ell+1)$-cycles and $\ell!$ total orderings of a given tree, one has
\[
 F'(A_\ell)=\card\{\text{trees with support a given set of }(\ell+1)\text{ elements}\}.
\]
\end{corollaryx}

\subsection*{3.2}
Corollary 1.5 gives
\[
F'(A_\ell)=\frac{\ell+1}{2}
\sum_{\substack{i+j=\ell-1\\ i,j\ge0}}
\frac{(\ell-1)!}{i!j!}\,F'(A_i)F'(A_j),
\]
or
\[
2\ell\frac{F'(A_\ell)}{\ell!}=(\ell+1)
\sum_{i+j=\ell-1}\frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}\frac{F'(A_j)}{j!}.
\]
In terms of the generating series
\[
 a(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}t^{i+1},
\]
this becomes
\[
2t\,\partial_t\left(\frac{a}{t}\right)=\partial_t(a^2),
\]
that is,
\[
 \dd a-a\frac{\dd t}{t}=a\,\dd a,
\]
so
\[
 \frac{\dd a}{a}-\dd a=\frac{\dd t}{t}.
\]
Since $a'(0)=1$, we obtain
\[
 a\,e^{-a}=t.
\]

\begin{corollaryx}[3.3]
\[
 F'(B_\ell)=\ell^\ell.
\]
Here $W$ is the subgroup of $\GL(\ell,\mathbb R)$ preserving a set $\{\pm\varepsilon_i\}$. We have $|W|=2^\ell\ell!$, $h=2\ell$; for example,
\[
 c:\varepsilon_1\mapsto\varepsilon_2\mapsto\cdots\mapsto\varepsilon_\ell\mapsto-\varepsilon_1,
\]
and the number of Coxeter elements is $2^{\ell-1}(\ell-1)!$.

For a product of $\ell$ reflections to be Coxeter, it is necessary and sufficient that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
\item one and only one of them be of type $(\varepsilon_i\mapsto-\varepsilon_i,\;\varepsilon_j\text{ fixed})$;
\item the graph obtained by putting an edge for each reflection joining $i$ and $j$ by a reflection $\varepsilon_i\mapsto\pm\varepsilon_j$ be a tree.
\end{enumerate}
Hence the number of sequences of $\ell$ reflections whose product is Coxeter is
\[
 \ell^{\ell-2}(\ell-1)!\,2^{\ell-1}\ell^2,
\]
and the cardinality of each $\Xi'_c$ is therefore
\[
 \frac{\ell^{\ell-2}(\ell-1)!\,2^{\ell-1}\ell^2}{2^{\ell-1}(\ell-1)!}=\ell^\ell.
\]
\end{corollaryx}

\subsection*{3.4}
Corollary 1.5 gives, with $B_0=\varnothing$ and $B_1=A_1$, for $\ell\ge1$,
\[
 F'(B_\ell)=\ell\sum_{i+j=\ell-1}\frac{(\ell-1)!}{i!j!}\,F'(A_i)F'(B_j),
\]
or
\[
 \frac{F'(B_\ell)}{\ell!}=\sum_{i+j=\ell-1}\frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}\frac{F'(B_j)}{j!}.
\]
In terms of the generating series
\[
 b(t)=\sum_{i=0}^\infty\frac{F'(B_i)}{i!}t^i,
\]
this becomes
\[
 b(t)=a(t)b(t)+1,
\]
that is,
\[
 b(t)=\frac1{1-a(t)}.
\]

\begin{corollaryx}[3.5]
\[
 F'(D_\ell)=2(\ell-1)^\ell\qquad (\ell\ge2).
\]
Let
\[
 d(t)=1+\sum_\ell\frac{F'(D_\ell)}{2\ell!}t^\ell
\]
be the generating series. Corollary 1.5 gives
\[
F'(D_\ell)=(\ell-1)\left[
\sum_{\substack{i+j=\ell-1\\ j\ge2}}
\frac{(\ell-1)!}{i!j!}F'(A_i)F'(D_j)+2F'(A_{\ell-1})\right],
\]
hence
\[
\ell\frac{F'(D_\ell)}{2\ell!}=(\ell-1)\left[
\sum_{\substack{i+j=\ell-1\\ j\ge2}}
\frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}\frac{F'(D_j)}{2j!}
+\frac{F'(A_{\ell-1})}{(\ell-1)!}\right].
\]
Multiplying by $t^{\ell-1}$ and summing gives
\[
 \partial_t d=t\partial_t\left(\frac{ad}{t}\right),
\]
or
\[
\partial_t d=\partial_t d\cdot a+d\cdot t\partial_t\left(\frac{a}{t}\right),\qquad
 t\partial_t\left(\frac{a}{t}\right)=a\partial_t a.
\]
Thus
\[
 \partial_t d(1-a)=d\,a\,\partial_t a,
\]
\[
 \frac{\partial_t d}{d}=\frac{a\partial_t a}{1-a}=\partial_t\{-a-\log(1-a)\},
\]
and
\[
 d=\frac{e^{-a}}{1-a}.
\]
Since $ae^{-a}=t$, this may also be written as
\[
 d=\frac{t}{a(1-a)},\qquad \frac{ad}{t}=b.
\]
Substituting this in the differential equation gives
\[
 \partial_t d=t\partial_t b,
\]
which proves the corollary.
\end{corollaryx}

I have also checked that
\[
 F'(E_6)=2^9 3^4,
 \qquad F'(E_7)=2\cdot 3^{12},
 \qquad F'(E_8)=2\cdot 3^5\cdot 5^7.
\]
Your conjecture is proved.

\section*{4. Complements}

\subsection*{4.1}
The following result sharpens 1.3(ii).

Let $W$ be a finite Coxeter group with irreducible Dynkin diagram $D$. Let $\leq$ be a total order on $D$, and let $\alpha$ be a root. For each chamber $C$ in which $\alpha$ is a simple root, let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_\ell$ be the simple roots associated with $C$, in the order $\leq$, and set
\[
 c(C)=z_\alpha z_{\alpha_1}\cdots \widehat{z_\alpha}\cdots z_{\alpha_\ell},
\]
the product of the $z_{\alpha_i}$ in the order $\leq$, except that $z_\alpha$ is put first.

\begin{propositionx}[4.2]
The map
\[
 C\longmapsto c(C)
\]
from the set of chambers in which $\alpha$ is a simple root to the set of Coxeter transformations is bijective.
\end{propositionx}

Injectivity is proved as in 1.3(iv). With the notation of that proof, let $w\in W_d$ send $C'$ to $C''$. It preserves $\alpha$ and the order of the walls, hence it preserves $c(C')=c(C'')$. Since $w$ centralizes a Coxeter transformation and fixes a root, $w=1$ by 1.3(i) and (v).

It remains to count. There are $|W|/h$ Coxeter transformations, by 1.3(i). If $\ell_\alpha$ is the number of vertices of the Dynkin diagram corresponding to roots conjugate to $\alpha$, then $\alpha$ has $\ell_\alpha h$ conjugates under $W$, by 1.3(v). The number of pairs
\[
(\text{root }\beta\text{ conjugate to }\alpha,
\text{ chamber in which }\beta\text{ is a simple root})
\]
is $|W|\ell_\alpha$; hence the number of chambers in which $\alpha$ is a simple root is $|W|/h$.
