\begin{abstract}
Except for a few corrections added in 2010, this article reproduces notes sent to Nagata in the 1970s, translating into the language of schemes his papers [2] and [3]. The proofs are a ``constructive'' version of Nagata's proofs. Making the proofs constructive made it possible to avoid the use of valuations. A more detailed exposition and additional results are given in Conrad [1]. Unless the contrary is expressly stated, all schemes considered below are assumed noetherian.
\end{abstract}

\section{Recalls on blow-ups}

\subsection*{0.1}
Let \(\mathfrak a\) be an ideal of the ring \(A\). The scheme obtained from \(X=\Spec(A)\) by blowing up \(\mathfrak a\) is the union of the open subschemes \(D(x)\), where \(x\) runs through a system of generators of \(\mathfrak a\), and \(D(x)\) is the spectrum of the subring \(A[\mathfrak a x^{-1}]\) of \(A[x^{-1}]\). Moreover,
\[
D(x)\cap (X-V(\mathfrak a))=\Spec(A[x^{-1}]).
\]
If \(\mathfrak c\) is a second ideal of \(A\), defining a closed subscheme \(V(\mathfrak c)\) of \(X\), the trace on \(D(x)\) of the strict transform of \(V(\mathfrak c)\) is defined by the ideal of \(A[\mathfrak a x^{-1}]\) which is the trace of the ideal of \(A[x^{-1}]\) generated by \(\mathfrak c\).

\begin{lemma}[0.2]
Let \(X\) be a scheme and let \(\mathfrak a\) and \(\mathfrak b\) be two sheaves of ideals on \(X\). Let \(\wh X\) be the blow-up of \(X\) along \(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b\). Then the open subset of \(\wh X\) which is the union of the \(D(x)\) for \(x\in \mathfrak b\) contains the strict transform of \(V(\mathfrak a)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The scheme \(\wh X\) is the union of the \(D(x)\) for \(x\in \mathfrak a\) or \(x\in \mathfrak b\), and, for \(x\in \mathfrak a\), the open subset \(D(x)\) is disjoint from the strict transform of \(V(\mathfrak a)\).
\end{proof}

The lemma 0.3 of the original text was false. The corrected version of the lemma which follows dates from March 1996. It forced a change of notation. The original lemma used the following notation, to which the rest of the text refers: instead of \(F\) and \(G\), one considered their complements \(U\) and \(V\), which, with \(Y' := U\cap p^{-1}(Y)\), formed a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
Y' \ar[r] \ar[d] & Z \ar[d,"p"] \\
U \ar[r] & X,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where \(Y'\) is closed in \(p^{-1}(V)\).

\begin{lemma}[0.3]
Let \(p:Z\to X\) be a morphism, let \(F\supset G\) be closed subschemes of \(X\), and let \(Y\) be a closed subscheme of \(Z\). Suppose that
\[
Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\subset p^{-1}(G)
\]
scheme-theoretically. Blow up \(X\) along \(G\), obtaining \(\wh X\), and let \(\tw p:\wh Z\to \wh X\) be obtained from \(p:Z\to X\) by base change. Then the closure of \(Y-p^{-1}(G)\) in \(\wh Z\) and \(\tw p^{-1}(F-G)\), taken in \(\wh X\), are disjoint closed subschemes of \(\wh Z\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We may suppose that \(Z\) and \(X\) are affine. Let \(\mathfrak G\) be the ideal of \(G\). Cover \(\wh X\) by the open subsets \(D(g)\), for \(g\in \mathfrak G\) (a system of generators of \(\mathfrak G\) suffices), and cover \(X-G\) by
\[
U(g)=D(g)-\text{(inverse image of }G\text{)}.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
X-G \ar[r,hook] & \wh X \\
U(g) \ar[u,hook] \ar[r,hook] & D(g). \ar[u,hook]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
It is enough to see that, for each \(g\), the traces on \(\tw p^{-1}D(g)\) of \(\tw p^{-1}(F-G)\) and of the closure of \(Y-p^{-1}G\) are disjoint. They are respectively the inverse image of the closure of \(F\cap U(g)\) in \(D(g)\), and the closure of \(Y\cap p^{-1}(U(g))\) in \(\tw p^{-1}D(g)\).

Let a tilde denote inverse image, either from \(X\) to \(Z\), or from \(D(g)\) to \(\tw p^{-1}D(g)\). Since \(p^{-1}G\supset Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\), the inverse image \(\tw g\) of \(g\) on \(Z\) can be written
\[
\tw g=y+f,
\]
where \(y\) vanishes on \(Y\) and \(f\) vanishes on \(p^{-1}(F)\). There are functions \(a_i\) on \(Z\) and elements \(f_i\) in the ideal of \(F\) such that
\[
f=\sum_i a_i\,\tw f_i.
\]
Since \(F\supset G\), the \(f_i\) are in the ideal of \(G\), and the functions \(f_i/g\) are regular on \(D(g)\). They vanish on \(F\cap U(g)\), hence on the closure of \(F\cap U(g)\) in \(D(g)\), and therefore
\[
\sum_i a_i\,(f_i/g)^{\sim}
\]
vanishes on \(\tw p^{-1}\cl{F\cap U(g)}\). On \(p^{-1}U(g)\) one has
\[
1=y/g+f/g=y/g+\sum_i a_i(f_i/g)^{\sim},
\]
and the same sum is therefore equal to \(1\) on \(Y\cap p^{-1}U(g)\), hence on its closure in \(\tw p^{-1}D(g)\). This proves the required disjointness.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Suppose instead that one is given closed subschemes \(F\supset G_0\) of \(X\), with \(Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\) contained set-theoretically in \(p^{-1}(G_0)\). Then there exists an \(r\) such that the \(r\)-th infinitesimal neighbourhood \(G\) of \(G_0\) in \(F\) satisfies the hypotheses of the lemma. If \(J\) is the ideal defining \((Y\cap p^{-1}(F))_{\mathrm{red}}\) in \(Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\), it is enough to take \(r\) such that \(J^{r+1}=0\). Thus the lemma permits, by a blow-up which does not touch the complement \(V\) of \(G_0\), the separation of the strict transform of \(F\) from the closure of \(Y-p^{-1}(G_0)\) in \(\wh Z\).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[0.4]
Let
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
X' \ar[r,"p"] \ar[d,"q"'] & X' \ar[d] \\
U \ar[r,hook] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
be a commutative diagram of schemes, with \(U\) a dense open subset of \(X\) and of \(X'\), with \(p\) and \(q\) separated of finite type, and with \(q^{-1}(X)=X'\). Let \(F\) be a closed subscheme of \(X\), and let \(G\) be a closed subscheme of \(X'\) containing \(p^{-1}(F)\). Then there exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset F-\overline{F}\), such that, after base change by the blow-up morphism defined by \(\mathfrak a\), \(\wh X\to X\), and after replacing the new \(X'\) by the closure of \(U\), one has
\[
q^{-1}(F)\subset G.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In Nagata's language, to take the closure of \(F\) in the Zariski--Riemann space of \(X\), it is enough to take the ``intersection'' of the closures of \(F\) in the blow-ups of \(X\) whose centres are contained in \(F-\overline{F}\).

Apply Lemma 0.3 to
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
X'-q^*F \ar[r] & X'-G \\
X-F \ar[u] \ar[r] & X-(F-\overline{F}). \ar[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
One obtains \(\mathfrak a\), and, after the base change \(\wh X\to X\), the new closure of \(F\) is disjoint from the image of the closure of the old \(X'-q^*F\) in \(X'-G\). In particular, after replacing \(\wh X\) by \(U\), this new closure is disjoint from the image of \(X'-G\), which is what was required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[0.5]
Let
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
X_i \ar[r] & X_i \qquad (1\le i\le n) \\
U \ar[u] \ar[r] & X \ar[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
be a diagram of separated \(S\)-schemes of finite type, with \(X_i\) proper over \(S\), and with \(U\) a dense open subscheme of \(X\) and of the \(X_i\). Let \(X^*\) be the closure of \(U\) in the product of the \(X_i\). Finally, let \(F_i\) be a closed subscheme of \(X_i\); suppose that the intersection of the inverse images of the \(F_i\) in \(X^*\) is empty. Then, after replacing the \(X_i\) by blow-ups whose centres lie in \(F_i-\overline{F_i}\), one obtains an analogous situation in which the intersection of the inverse images of the \(\overline{F_i}\) is empty.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(X^{**}\) be a blow-up of \(X^*\), with projections \(p_i:X^{**}\to X_i\), such that in \(X^{**}\) one has
\[
\bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(F_i)=\emptyset;
\]
for example, blow up \(\bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(F_i)\). Apply Lemma 0.4 to the diagrams
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
p_i^{-1}(X_i) \ar[r] & X^{**} \\
U \ar[u] \ar[r] & X_i, \ar[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
and to \(F_i\subset X_i\), \(G_i=p_i^{-1}(F_i)\). This provides the desired blow-ups of the \(X_i\). Indeed, in the new situation, in the closure of \(U\) in \(X^{**}\), one has
\[
\bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(\overline{F_i})\subset \bigcap_i G_i=\emptyset.
\]
Since \(X^{**}\to X^*\) is surjective, this remains true in \(X^*\).
\end{proof}

\section{The theorems}

\begin{definition}[1.1]
Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two \(S\)-schemes, with \(Y\) separated over \(S\). A \emph{quasi-domination} \(f:X\dashrightarrow Y\) is a pair consisting of a dense open subscheme \(U\) of \(X\) and a morphism of \(S\)-schemes \(f:U\to Y\) whose graph is closed in \(X\times_S Y\).

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two \(S\)-schemes, with \(Y\) separated over \(S\), let \(U\) be a quasi-compact open subscheme of \(X\), and let \(f:U\to Y\). We say that \(X\) is quasi-dominant over \(Y\), relative to \(U\) and \(f\), if there exists a quasi-domination of the schematic closure \(\overline U\) of \(U\), with values in \(Y\), which extends \(f\). There is at most one such quasi-domination; its graph is the closure of the graph of \(f\).

A quasi-domination \(f\) is called \emph{proper} or \emph{complete} if the morphism \(f:U\to Y\) is proper.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[1.2]
Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two \(S\)-schemes, and let \(U\subset V\subset X\) be two dense open subschemes of \(X\). Suppose that \(Y\) is separated of finite type over \(S\). Let \(f:U\to Y\) be a quasi-domination of \(V\) in \(Y\). Then there exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-V\), such that the blow-up \(\wh X\) of \(X\) with centre \(V(\mathfrak a)\) quasi-dominates \(Y\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the base change \(X\to S\) one is reduced to the case \(X=S\). Then \(Y\) is a scheme over \(X\), \(f\) is a section of \(Y\) over \(U\), and by hypothesis \(f(U)\) is closed over \(V\).

\emph{Case 1: \(U=V\), \(p:Y\to X\) quasi-affine, and \(X\) affine.} By hypothesis there is a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
Y \ar[r] \ar[dr] & E_X \ar[d] \\
& X,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where \(E_X\) is an affine space over \(X\). If \(\wh X\) quasi-dominates \(E_X\) relative to \(f\), then a fortiori \(\wh X\) quasi-dominates \(Y\). We may therefore suppose \(Y=E_X\). A preliminary blow-up allows us to suppose that \(X-U\) is a divisor \(D\). Locally on \(X\), the section \(f\) has coordinates \((f_i u^{-n})\), where \(u\) is a local equation of \(D\), where the \(f_i\) are regular, and where \(n\) is chosen once and for all sufficiently large. Let \(\mathfrak a\) be the ideal generated by \(u^n\) and the \(f_i\). After blowing up \(V(\mathfrak a)\), the section \(f\) extends to a section of the projective completion of \(E_X\), and we are done.

\emph{Case 2: \(U=V\) and \(p\) quasi-affine.} By Case 1, if \((U_i)_{0\le i\le n}\) is an affine open covering of \(X\), there exists on \(U_i\) an ideal \(\mathfrak a_i\) which works. This ideal admits an extension \(\tw{\mathfrak a_i}\) to \(X\), with \(V(\tw{\mathfrak a_i})\subset X-V\), and it is enough to blow up the product of the \(\tw{\mathfrak a_i}\).

\emph{Case 3: \(p\) quasi-affine.} By Lemma 0.3, applied to
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
f(U) \ar[r] & Y \ar[d,"p"] \\
U \ar[r,hook] & V \ar[r,hook] & X,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
a preliminary blow-up allows us to suppose that
\[
p(f(U))\cap (V-U)=\emptyset.
\]
There is therefore a quasi-compact open subscheme \(X'\) of \(X\) with \(X'\cap V=U\) and \(p(f(U))\subset X'\). By Case 2, there exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) on \(X'\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X'-U\), such that the blow-up \(\wh{X'}\) quasi-dominates \(Y\), that is, \(f(U)\). It remains only to extend \(\mathfrak a\) to an ideal on \(X\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-V\).

\emph{General case.} Let \((Y_i)\) be a finite covering of \(Y\) by affine open subschemes, and put
\[
U_i=f^{-1}(Y_i),\qquad X_i=\overline{U_i},\qquad V_i=X_i\cap V.
\]
Case 3 applies to
\[
Y_i\cap p^{-1}(X_i),\qquad U_i\subset V_i\subset X_i,
\]
and gives an ideal \(\mathfrak a_i\) on \(X_i\). This ideal defines an ideal on \(X\), still denoted \(\mathfrak a_i\), with \(V(\mathfrak a_i)\subset X-V\). Blow up the product of the \(\mathfrak a_i\). The blow-up \(q:\wh X\to X\) thus obtained is such that \(q^{-1}(X_i)\) dominates the blow-up \(\wh X_i\). If \(X'_i=q^{-1}(X_i)\), there exists an open \(U'_i\) of \(X'_i\), with \(U'_i\supset q^{-1}(U_i)\), such that, above \(X_i\), \(f(U_i)\) is a section of \(U'_i\to Y\). One concludes that, after this blow-up,
\[
f(U)=\bigcup_i f_i(U'_i).
\]
For the proof of Theorem 1.2, we may replace \(Y\) by \(f(U)\), and conclude by Case 3 and the following lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[1.3]
The scheme \(f(U)\) is quasi-affine over \(\wh X\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, \(f(U)\) is quasi-finite over \(\wh X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[1.4, a variant of Chow's lemma]
Let \(f:X\to S\) be a separated morphism of finite type. Then for every open subscheme \(U\) which is quasi-projective over \(S\) and dense in \(X\), there exists a diagram of \(S\)-schemes
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
& X' \ar[dl,"q"'] \ar[dr,hook,"j"] & \\
X && \overline X,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where \(q\) is the blow-up morphism defined by an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) satisfying \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-U\), and where \(\overline X\) is projective over \(S\). In particular, \(q\) is projective and surjective and \(q^{-1}(U)\simeq U\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(U^*\) be a projective \(S\)-scheme containing \(U\) as a schematically dense open subscheme. By Theorem 1.2, applied to the injection of \(U\) into \(X\), one may choose \(U^*\) quasi-dominant over \(X\). Thus one has a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
U \ar[r,hook] \ar[d,hook] & V \ar[d,"\varphi"] \\
U^* \ar[r] & X,
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{1.4.1}
\]
and here, since \(U^*\) is proper over \(S\), \(\varphi\) is proper.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[1.5]
Let
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
U \ar[r,hook] \ar[d,hook] & V \ar[d,"\varphi"] \\
Y \ar[r] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
be a diagram of separated \(S\)-schemes of finite type, with \(U\) dense in \(X\), dense in \(Y\), and schematically dense in \(V\), and with \(\varphi\) a quasi-domination of \(Y\) in \(X\). If \(\mathfrak a\) is an ideal of \(X\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-U\), such that the blow-up \(\wh X\) of \(X\) along \(\mathfrak a\) quasi-dominates \(V\), relative to \(U\), and such that \(U\) is schematically dense in \(\wh X\), giving a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
U \ar[r,hook] \ar[d,hook] & W \ar[d,"\psi"] \\
\wh X \ar[r] & V,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \(\psi\) is the blow-up morphism of \(V\) along \(\varphi^*\mathfrak a\);
\item the graph of \(\psi\) is closed in \(\wh X\times_S Y\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\wh V\) be the blow-up of \(V\) along \(\varphi^*\mathfrak a\). By the universal property of \(\wh X\), there is a unique \(X\)-morphism \(\alpha:\wh V\to \wh X\). By the universal property of \(\wh V\), there is a unique \(V\)-morphism \(\beta:W\to \wh V\). The relevant diagram is
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
U \ar[r,hook] \ar[d,hook] & W \ar[r,"\beta"] \ar[d,"\psi"'] & \wh V \ar[d,"\alpha"] \\
V \ar[r,"\varphi"'] & Y & \wh X \ar[ll]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The graph of \(\varphi\) is, trivially, proper over \(V\); the same is true of its inverse image on \(V\times_S\wh X\), and it follows that the graph of \(\psi\) is proper over \(V\), hence that \(\psi\) is proper. Thus \(\beta\) is proper, and its image is schematically dense, because already \(U\) is schematically dense in \(\wh V\). On the other hand, \(\beta\) is locally a section of \(\alpha\) on \(W\) (because \(\beta|_U\) is such a section over \(U\)); hence \(\beta\) is an isomorphism. By hypothesis the graph of \(\psi\) is closed in \(V\times_S\wh X\), and is contained in the inverse image of the graph of \(\varphi\), which is closed in \(Y\times X\). One concludes that \(\Gamma(\psi)\) is closed in \(Y\times\wh X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[1.7]
Let
\[
X_2 \xleftarrow{\ } U \xrightarrow{\ } X_1
\]
be a diagram of separated \(S\)-schemes of finite type, where \(U\) is a dense open subscheme of each \(X_i\). Then there exists \(j:U\hookrightarrow X\), with \(X\) separated of finite type over \(S\) and \(U\) schematically dense in \(X\), and proper quasi-dominations
\[
p_i:X\dashrightarrow X_i.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem 1.2, after blowing up \(X_1\) without touching \(U\), one reduces to the case where \(X_1\) quasi-dominates \(X_2\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
& X_1 \ar[dr] & \\
U \ar[ur,hook] \ar[rr,hook] && X_2.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Apply Lemma 1.5 to this diagram, in order to obtain
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
W \ar[r,"\psi"] & X'_2 \\
U \ar[u,hook] \ar[r,hook] & V \ar[u], \\
& X_1 \ar[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \(\psi\) is the blow-up morphism of an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) of \(V\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset V-U\);
\item the graph of \(\psi\) is closed in \(X_1\times X'_2\);
\item \(X'_2\) is a blow-up of \(X_2\).
\end{enumerate}
Let \(\mathfrak a'\) be an ideal of \(X_1\) extending \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a')\subset X_1-U\), and let \(q:X'_1\to X_1\) be the blow-up of \(X_1\) along \(\mathfrak a'\). Then \(\psi\) induces an isomorphism between \(W\) and \(q^{-1}(V)\), and the graph of this isomorphism is closed in \(X'_1\times X'_2\). Define \(X\) by gluing \(X'_1\) and \(X'_2\) along this isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof shows that one may take for \(p_i:U_i\to X_i\), with \(U_i\subset X\), a blow-up morphism of an ideal outside \(U\).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[1.6]
Let \(f:X\to S\) be separated of finite type. There exists an immersion of \(X\) into an \(S\)-scheme proper over \(S\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma 1.5 to the diagram (1.4.1). There exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) on \(X\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-U\), such that the blow-up \(\widehat X\) quasi-dominates \(V\). We may moreover arrange that \(U\) is schematically dense in \(\widehat X\). By Lemma 1.5, and because \(\varphi\) is proper, \(\widehat X\) is therefore the blow-up of \(V\) along \(\varphi^*\mathfrak a\). If \(\mathfrak a'\) is an ideal of \(U^*\) extending \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a')\subset U^*-U\), we then take
\[
 X'=\widehat X,\qquad \overline X=\widetilde{U^*}.
\]

Let \((U_i)\) be a finite open covering of \(X\) by dense quasi-projective open subschemes. For each \(U_i\), there is, by Corollary 1.4, a commutative diagram of \(S\)-schemes
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
X'_i \ar[r,"q_i"] \ar[d,"j_i"'] & X_i \\
U_i \ar[r,hook] & \overline X_i,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where \(q_i\) is proper, \(\overline X_i\) is proper over \(S\), and \(U_i\) is dense in \(\overline X_i\). Let \(F_i=\overline X_i-U_i\) and \(F'_i=q_i^{-1}(F_i)\). Finally, let \(X^*\) be the closure of \(U\) in the product of the \(\overline X_i\), with projections
\[
p_i:X^*\to \overline X_i.
\]
By Lemma 0.5, a preliminary blow-up of the \(\overline X_i\), not touching \(X'_i\), allows one to suppose that
\[
\bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(F'_i)=\emptyset.
\]
Let \(M_i\) be the \(S\)-scheme, separated over \(S\), obtained by gluing \(X\) and \(\overline X_i-F'_i\) along \(U_i\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\overline X_i-F'_i && X \\
& M_i. \ar[ul] \ar[ur]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Apply Lemma 1.7 to \(X\) and to the \(M_i\), to obtain \(X\hookrightarrow M\), where \(M\) properly quasi-dominates each \(M_i\). It remains to prove that \(M\) is proper over \(S\).

Indeed, one has morphisms
\[
X^*-p_i^{-1}(F'_i)\longrightarrow M_i
\]
which glue on \(U=\bigcap_i U_i\). Replacing \(X^*\) by a blow-up \(X^{**}\), one obtains morphisms
\[
X^{**}-p_i^{-1}(F_i)\longrightarrow M
\]
which glue, and hence a morphism from the schematic closure of \(U\) in \(X^{**}\) to \(M\). The scheme \(M\), as the image of this morphism, is proper over \(S\).
\end{proof}

\begin{comments}
I hope that, by being still more constructive, one can prove Theorem 1.6 for \(f:X\to S\) separated of finite type, with \(S\) quasi-compact and quasi-separated. The rules to follow are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item consider only quasi-compact open subsets and closed subsets defined by ideals of finite type;
\item never take schematic closure (one would leave (a)), but take only a closed subset containing the given part and sufficiently small for the later constructions;
\item do not speak of a dense quasi-projective open subset, cf. (b). Remember that, to construct such open subsets, one could start from an affine covering \((U_i)\), and take
\[
U=\bigcup_i\left(U_i-\bigcup_{j<i}U_j\right).
\]
Translate the arguments in terms of the original \(U_i\), and apply (a) and (b).
\end{enumerate}
On the other hand, I have no idea in the case of algebraic spaces.
\end{comments}
