\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Finiteness of the Extension of $\Q$ Generated by Frobenius Traces, in Finite Characteristic}\par
\vspace{0.5em}
Pierre Deligne\par
\vspace{0.5em}
Dedicated to the memory of I. M. Gelfand
\end{center}

\paragraph{Abstract.}
Let $Z_0$ be a scheme of finite type over $\F_q$ and let $\mathcal F_0$ be a $\Ql$-sheaf on $Z_0$.
We show that there is a finite-type extension $E\subset \Ql$ of $\Q$ such that the local factors of the $L$-function of $\mathcal F_0$ all have coefficients in $E$.
If $Z_0$ is normal and connected and $\mathcal F_0$ is an irreducible $\ell$-adic local system whose determinant has finite order, one may take $E$ to be a finite extension of $\Q$.

\paragraph{Contents.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., start=0]
\item Introduction
\item Irreducibility and extension of the base field
\item The case of curves: quantitative versions
\item The main theorem
\end{enumerate}

\section*{0. Introduction}

\subsection*{0.0}
Fix the following notation:
\begin{align*}
\F_q&: \text{a finite field with $q$ elements, of characteristic $p$;}\\
\F&: \text{an algebraic closure of $\F_q$;}\\
\F_{q^n}&: \text{the extension of degree $n$ of $\F_q$ inside $\F$;}\\
W(\F/\F_{q^n})&: \text{the subgroup $\Z$ of $\Gal(\F/\F_{q^n})=\widehat{\Z}$ generated by the geometric Frobenius $F$}\\
&\qquad \text{(the inverse of $x\mapsto x^{q^n}$);}\notag\\
\ell&: \text{a prime number distinct from $p$;}\\
\Ql&: \text{an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_\ell$.}
\end{align*}
An object over $\F_q$ will be marked by a subscript $0$, and suppressing this subscript will mean extension of the base field from $\F_q$ to $\F$.

For a summary of the formalism of $\Ql$-sheaves and of $\Ql$-Weil sheaves, I refer to [De2] 1.1.1 and 1.1.10.

Let $\overline X_0$ be a smooth projective absolutely irreducible curve over $\F_q$, let $S_0$ be a finite set of closed points of $\overline X_0$, and put $X_0:=\overline X_0-S_0$.
We write $K_0$ for the field of rational functions on $X_0$ and $A$ for its adele ring.

\subsection*{0.1}
Lafforgue [L] provides a natural bijection between
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item isomorphism classes of smooth irreducible $\Ql$-Weil sheaves of rank $r$ on $X_0$, and
\item cuspidal automorphic representations $\pi$ of $\GL(r,A)$, unramified outside $S_0$.
\end{enumerate}
This bijection is characterized by equality, at each closed point $x_0$ of $X_0$, of the local factors of the $L$-functions attached to the objects in (A) and (B).

In (B), the notion of cuspidal automorphic representation is purely algebraic: one may take as coefficient field any algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic zero. We take $k=\Ql$. The topology of $\Ql$ plays no role.

If $Z_0$ is a scheme of finite type over $\F_q$, the action on $\F$ of $W(\F/\F_q)$ induces an action of this Weil group on $Z=Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F$.
A $\Ql$-Weil sheaf $\mathcal F_0$ on $Z_0$ is a $\Ql$-sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $Z$, endowed with an action of $W(\F/\F_q)$ on $(Z,\mathcal F)$ lifting its action on $Z$.
The notion of $\Ql$-sheaf is defined by passage to the limit from that of an etale sheaf of $\mathcal O_\lambda/I$-modules, where $\mathcal O_\lambda$ is the integral closure of $\mathbb Z_\ell$ in a finite extension $E_\lambda\subset\Ql$ of $\mathbb Q_\ell$, and $I$ is a power of the maximal ideal.
Here the topology of $\Ql$ plays a crucial role.
A surprising consequence of the equivalence between (A) and (B) is that, for semisimple smooth sheaves on curves, this role is illusory.

\subsection*{0.2}
One should beware that Lafforgue assumes a choice of an isomorphism $\iota:\mathbb C\to\Ql$, and uses it rather to compare automorphic representations in the classical sense, with coefficient field $\mathbb C$, and $\Ql$-sheaves.
If one uses $\iota$ to replace cuspidal automorphic representations with coefficients in $\mathbb C$ by those with values in $\Ql$, the resulting correspondence depends, when $r$ is even and $q$ is an odd power of $p$, on the square root $\iota(\sqrt q)$ of $q$ in $\Ql$.
Changing $\sqrt q$ to $-\sqrt q$ changes the correspondence by composing it with twisting, see 0.3, by the character
\[
 n\longmapsto (-1)^{n(r-1)}
\]
of $W(\F/\F_q)=\Z$.

Instead of the correspondence used by Lafforgue, we prefer its twist by the character
\[
 n\longmapsto (\sqrt q)^{n(r-1)}
\]
of $W(\F/\F_q)$.
This rationalized correspondence gives a bijection between (A) and (B) independent of $\iota$.

\subsection*{0.3}
For both the objects (A) and the objects (B), twisting by a character
\[
 \chi:W(\F/\F_q)=\Z\longrightarrow \Ql^*
\]
is defined, and these twists correspond.

The set of characters $\chi$ of $W(\F/\F_q)$ is naturally in bijection with the set of isomorphism classes of rank-one $\Ql$-Weil sheaves $\mathcal L_0$ on $\Spec(\F_q)$; for the objects (A), the twist is given by tensor product with the inverse image, under $X_0\to\Spec(\F_q)$, of such a sheaf.

Let $v:A^*\to\Z$ be the homomorphism such that $\|x\|=q^{-v(x)}$.
It factors through the idele class group $K_0^*\backslash A^*$.
In (B), if $\pi$ is a cuspidal automorphic representation of $\GL(r,A)$, viewed as contained in the space of locally constant functions on $\GL(r,K_0)\backslash\GL(r,A)$, the twist $\pi_\chi$ of $\pi$ by $\chi$ is the space of functions
\[
 f(x)\,\chi(v(\det x))
\]
for $f$ in $\pi$.

We will sometimes say ``twist by $b$'' for ``twist by the character $\chi$ of $\Z$ such that $\chi(1)=b$.''

\subsection*{0.4}
The $\Ql$-sheaves form a full abelian subcategory of the abelian category of $\Ql$-Weil sheaves.
On $\Spec(\F_q)$, the isomorphism class of a rank-one $\Ql$-Weil sheaf $\mathcal L_0$ is determined by a character
\[
 \chi:W(\F/\F_q)=\Z\longrightarrow \Ql^* ,
\]
and for $\mathcal L_0$ to be a $\Ql$-sheaf it is necessary and sufficient that $\chi$ extend to a continuous character of $\Gal(\F/\F_q)=\widehat{\Z}$, i.e. that $\chi(1)$ be an $\ell$-adic unit.
This is what forces the use, in 0.1(A), of $\Ql$-Weil sheaves.
If one wanted in (A) to restrict oneself to $\Ql$-sheaves, then in (B) one would have to restrict oneself to cuspidal automorphic representations $\pi$ whose central character $\omega_\pi$ extends to a continuous character of the profinite completion of the idele class group.
This would destroy the purely algebraic character of (B).

The following theorems allow one to pass from $\Ql$-sheaves to $\Ql$-Weil sheaves.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Let $Z_0$ be a connected normal variety of finite type over $\F_q$, and let $\mathcal F_0$ be a smooth irreducible $\Ql$-Weil sheaf of rank $r$ on $Z_0$. Then $\mathcal F_0$ is a $\Ql$-sheaf if and only if
\[
 \det(\mathcal F_0):=\bigwedge^r\mathcal F_0
\]
is one ([De2] 1.3.14).
\item Let $\mathcal L_0$ be a smooth rank-one $\Ql$-Weil sheaf on $Z_0$. Definition: $\mathcal L_0$ is of finite order if there is an $N>0$ such that $\mathcal L_0^{\otimes N}$ is isomorphic to the constant sheaf $\Ql$. There is always an $N>0$ such that $\mathcal L_0^{\otimes N}$ is the inverse image of a Weil sheaf on $\Spec(\F_q)$ ([De2] 1.3.4(i)). The group, for $\otimes$, of isomorphism classes of rank-one Weil sheaves on $\Spec(\F_q)$ is divisible. Every $\mathcal L_0$ is therefore the tensor product of an $\mathcal L'_0$, of finite order, and the inverse image of a Weil sheaf $\mathcal L''_0$ on $\Spec(\F_q)$. For $\mathcal L_0$ to be a $\Ql$-sheaf it is necessary and sufficient that $\mathcal L''_0$ be one.
\item Every $\mathcal F_0$ as in (a) is therefore obtained by twisting from a $\Ql$-sheaf $\mathcal F'_0$ such that $\det(\mathcal F'_0)$ has finite order; and $\mathcal F_0$ is a $\Ql$-sheaf if and only if the twist is tensor product with the inverse image of a rank-one $\Ql$-sheaf on $\Spec(\F_q)$.
\end{enumerate}
In [L], Lafforgue states the correspondence between (A) and (B) by restricting in (A) to smooth irreducible $\Ql$-sheaves whose determinant has finite order, and by imposing in (B) the corresponding restriction on automorphic representations.
The preceding discussion shows that nothing is lost thereby.

\subsection*{0.5. Notation}
Let $\mathcal F_0$ be a $\Ql$-Weil sheaf on $Z_0$ of finite type over $\F_q$, let $x_0$ be a closed point of $Z_0$, and let
\[
 \deg(x_0):=[k(x_0):\F_q]
\]
be its degree.
The choice of $\bar x$ in $Z$ above $x_0$ defines an embedding of $k(x_0)$ into $k(\bar x)=\F$.
The Weil group $W(\F/k(x_0))$ is generated by the geometric Frobenius $F_{x_0}$.
It is of index $\deg(x_0)$ in $W(\F/\F_q)$, fixes $\bar x$, and acts on the fiber $\mathcal F_{\bar x}$ of $\mathcal F_0$ at the geometric point $\bar x$.
In particular $F_{x_0}$ acts on $\mathcal F_{\bar x}$.
The local factor at $x_0$ of the $L$-function of $\mathcal F_0$ is
\begin{equation}
\det\left(1-F_{x_0}t^{\deg(x_0)},\mathcal F_{\bar x}\right)^{-1}.
\tag{0.5.1}
\end{equation}
It does not depend on $\bar x$, only on $x_0$.

Let $n\ge 1$ and let $x$ be an $\F_{q^n}$-point of $Z_0$: $x\in Z_0(\F_{q^n})$.
The point $x$ is an $\F_q$-morphism
\[
 \Spec(\F_{q^n})\longrightarrow Z_0.
\]
Let $x_0$ be the closed point of $Z_0$ which is its image, and let $\bar x$ be the geometric point
\[
 \Spec(\F)\longrightarrow\Spec(\F_{q^n})\xrightarrow{x}Z_0.
\]
We write $F_x$ for the ``geometric Frobenius'' generator of $W(\F/\F_{q^n})$.
We have $W(\F/\F_{q^n})\subset W(\F/k(x_0))$, and
\begin{equation}
F_x=F_{x_0}^{n/\deg(x_0)}.
\tag{0.5.2}
\end{equation}
We will say ``the action of $F_x$ (respectively $F_{x_0}$) on $\mathcal F_0$'' for ``the action of $F_x$ (respectively $F_{x_0}$) on $\mathcal F_{\bar x}$.''
With this abuse of language, the local factor (0.5.1) is written
\[
\det\left(1-F_{x_0}t^{\deg(x_0)},\mathcal F_0\right)^{-1}.
\]
It will be convenient for us to consider, rather than these local factors, the traces $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ for $x$ in some $Z_0(\F_{q^n})$.
The identity of formal series
\[
 \log\det(1-ft,V)=-\sum_{n\ge1}\Tr(f^n,V)\frac{t^n}{n},
\]
for an endomorphism $f$ of $V$, shows that these traces for $x$ above $x_0$ and the local factor at $x_0$ determine one another.

Lafforgue [L] VII 6 proves the following theorem, in which (ii) and (iii) result from the equivalence between (A) and (B) of 0.1.

\paragraph{Theorem 0.6 (Lafforgue).}
Let $X_0/\F_q$ be as in 0.0, and let $\mathcal F_0$ be a smooth irreducible $\Ql$-Weil sheaf of rank $r$ on $X_0$, whose determinant $\det(\mathcal F_0)$ has finite order.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item At every closed point $x_0$ of $X_0$, the eigenvalues of $F_{x_0}$ acting on $\mathcal F_0$ are Weil numbers of weight $0$, i.e. are algebraic numbers of absolute value one at every place not dividing $p$. In particular $\mathcal F_0$ is pure of weight $0$.
\item There exists a finite extension $E\subset\Ql$ of $\Q$ such that, for every closed point $x_0$ of $X_0$, the polynomial
\[
 \det(1-F_{x_0}t,\mathcal F_0)
\]
has coefficients in $E$.
\item For $E$ as in (ii), for every prime number $\ell'\ne p$ and every embedding $\sigma$ of $E$ into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_{\ell'}$, there exists a smooth $\overline{\mathbb Q}_{\ell'}$-Weil sheaf $\mathcal F'_0$ on $X_0$ such that, at every closed point $x_0$ of $X_0$,
\[
 \det(1-F_{x_0}t,\mathcal F'_0)=\sigma\det(1-F_{x_0}t,\mathcal F_0).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The minimal extension $E$ is the field of definition of the automorphic representation $\pi$ corresponding to $\mathcal F_0$, viewed either as an isomorphism class of representations of $\GL(r,A)$ or as a subspace of the space of functions on $\GL(r,K_0)\backslash\GL(r,A)$.
Here one must use the rationalized correspondence of 0.2.

\subsection*{0.7}
Let $Z_0$ be a connected normal scheme of finite type over $\F_q$ and let $\mathcal F_0$ be a smooth irreducible $\Ql$-Weil sheaf on $Z_0$, whose determinant has finite order.
Lafforgue [L] VII 7 shows, by reduction to the case of curves through an argument with plane sections, that assertion (i) of 0.6 remains true for $\mathcal F_0$.
Beware that the reference to Hartshorne which Lafforgue gives for irreducibility of the restriction of $\mathcal F_0$ to a curve $X_0$ which is a plane section of $Z_0$ is not adequate: Hartshorne assumes $Z_0$ projective.
We will explain in 1.5--1.9 how to correct the argument, replacing the reference to Hartshorne by a reference to Jouanolou [J].

\subsection*{0.8}
Let $\mathcal F_0$ and $Z_0$ be as in 0.7.
Using a method developed by Wiesend [W] and Lafforgue's theorem, Drinfeld [Dr] has shown, under the additional hypothesis that $Z_0$ is smooth, that if $\mathcal F_0$ satisfies assertion (ii) of the theorem then $\mathcal F_0$ also satisfies (iii).
Our aim is to show that every $\mathcal F_0$ as in 0.7 satisfies (ii).

\subsection*{0.9}
Let us review the sections of the present article.
In Section 1, we consider smooth semisimple $\Ql$-Weil sheaves on $Z_0$ normal absolutely irreducible of finite type over $\F_q$.
We show how to describe them in terms of smooth $\Ql$-sheaves on schemes $Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^a}$, with determinant of finite order, and whose inverse image on $Z=Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F$ is irreducible.
The decomposition (1.4.1) obtained translates (0.3), together with well-known results on the relation between irreducible representations of a group $G$ and of an extension of $\Z$ by $G$.

In Section 2, we consider a smooth $\Ql$-Weil sheaf $\mathcal F_0$ on a smooth absolutely irreducible curve $X_0$ over $\F_q$.
For simplicity, we assume $X_0$ affine.
The ``complexity'' of $X_0$ will be measured by the integer
\[
 b_1(X):=\dim H^1_c(X,\Ql).
\]
We assume $\mathcal F_0$ algebraic, i.e. that the traces $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$, for $x$ in some $X_0(\F_{q^n})$, are algebraic numbers.
By 0.6(ii), applied to twists of irreducible subquotients of $\mathcal F_0$, we know that these traces generate a finite extension $E$ of $\Q$ in $\Ql$.
Our aim is to make explicit an integer $N$, depending on a ``complexity'' of $(X,\mathcal F)$, such that $E$ is generated by the $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ for $x$ in $X_0(\F_{q^n})$ with $n\le N$.
Put $\log_q^+(a)=\sup(0,\log_q(a))$.
If $\mathcal F$ is tamely ramified, one obtains $N$ of the form
\begin{equation}
O\bigl(\log_q^+(b_1(X))\bigr)+O(1),
\tag{0.9.1}
\end{equation}
where the implicit constants in the $O$ depend on the rank of $\mathcal F_0$.
For arbitrary $\mathcal F_0$, one must replace $b_1(X)$ by
\[
 b_1(X)+\sum_{s\in S}\alpha_s(\mathcal F),
\]
where $\alpha_s(\mathcal F)$ measures the wildness of the ramification of $\mathcal F$ at $s$.

Another way of measuring the wildness of $\mathcal F$, much less economical but convenient in Section 3, is in terms of a connected etale cover $X'$ of $X$ on which the inverse image of $\mathcal F$ is tamely ramified.
We will obtain another $N$, of the form
\begin{equation}
O\bigl(\log_q^+(b_1(X'))\bigr)+O(1).
\tag{0.9.2}
\end{equation}
Here again, the implicit constants in the $O$ depend on the rank of $\mathcal F_0$.

The proofs make essential use of 0.6(iii), in order to reduce the posed problem to the following one.
For $\mathcal F_0$ and $\mathcal G_0$ smooth semisimple Weil sheaves on $X_0$, find $N$ such that if
\[
 \Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_x,\mathcal G_0)
\]
for all $x$ in $X(\F_{q^n})$ with $n\le N$, then $\mathcal F_0$ and $\mathcal G_0$ are isomorphic.

\subsection*{0.10}
The main theorem is proved in Section 3.
After a few devissages, one is reduced to the case in which $\mathcal F_0$ is a smooth $\Ql$-sheaf on a scheme $Z_0$ which is smooth and absolutely irreducible over $\F_q$, and in which $\mathcal F_0$ is algebraic, meaning algebraicity of Frobenius traces.
The point is to show that the extension of $\Q$ generated by Frobenius traces is a finite extension.
This is done by showing that there exists an integer $N$ such that, for every integer $n>N$ and every point $x\in Z_0(\F_{q^n})$, the trace $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ is contained in the extension of $\Q$ generated by the $\Tr(F_{x'},\mathcal F_0)$ with $x'$ in some $Z_0(\F_{q^{n'}})$ and $n'<n$.
To prove this, one passes through $x$ a curve $X_0$ such that $(X_0,\mathcal F_0|_{X_0})$ is not too complex, and one applies to this curve the results of Section 2.
More precisely, one constructs a smooth affine curve $X_0$, a point $\widetilde x\in X_0(\F_{q^n})$, and an $\F_q$-morphism $\varphi:X_0\to Z_0$ sending $\widetilde x$ to $x$.
The construction does not guarantee that the curve $X_0$ is connected.
Replace it by its connected component $X_1$ such that $\widetilde x\in X_1(\F_{q^n})$.
The curve $X_1$ might not be absolutely irreducible over $\F_q$, but there is a bound $D$, depending only on $Z_0$, such that $X_1$ is absolutely irreducible over some $\F_{q^d}$ with $d\le D$.
It is to $X_1/\F_{q^d}$ that the results of Section 2 will be applied.
To simplify the exposition of the strategy, we pretend here, falsely, that $X_0$ is absolutely irreducible over $\F_q$.

Choose a connected etale cover $Z'$ of $Z$ on which $\mathcal F$ becomes tamely ramified.
This is an enormous waste, but is done only once.
Let $X'$ be a connected component of the inverse image of $Z'$ on $X$.
The complexity of $(X_0,\varphi^*\mathcal F_0)$ will be measured by $b_1(X')$.
We will control it by Katz's theorem [K].
Since only curves are involved here, one could instead use Bombieri [B], on which Katz bases himself, and estimate the number of connected components by the product of the degrees of equations of $X'$, but this would only repeat in a simple case Katz's argument.
One obtains a bound for the complexity which is polynomial in $n$.
As soon as $n$ is large enough, the integer $N$ of (0.9.2) satisfies $N<n$, and
\[
 \Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_{\widetilde x},\varphi^*\mathcal F_0)
\]
is in the extension of $\Q$ generated by the
\[
 \Tr(F_{x'},\varphi^*\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_{\varphi(x')},\mathcal F_0)
\]
for $x'\in X_0(\F_{q^{n'}})$ and $n'\le N<n$.

\section*{1. Irreducibility and extensions of the base field}

\subsection*{1.1}
Let $Z_0$ be a normal absolutely irreducible scheme of finite type over $\F_q$, let $Z:=Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F$, and let $x$ be a geometric point of $Z$.
It defines a geometric point, still denoted $x$, of $Z_0$.
The fundamental group of $Z_0$ is an extension
\[
1\to\pi_1(Z,x)\to\pi_1(Z_0,x)\to\Gal(\F/\F_q)\to1.
\]
Taking the fiber product with $W(\F/\F_q)=\Z\subset\Gal(\F/\F_q)=\widehat\Z$, one obtains the Weil group $W(Z_0,x)$:
\[
1\to\pi_1(Z,x)\to W(Z_0,x)\to\Z\to1.
\]
The functor ``fiber at $x$'' is an equivalence from the category of smooth $\Ql$-Weil sheaves on $Z_0$ to that of continuous linear representations of $W(Z_0,x)$ on a finite-dimensional $\Ql$-vector space.

\subsection*{1.2}
If $V$ is an irreducible $\Ql$-representation of $W(Z_0,x)$, its restriction to $\pi_1(Z,x)$ is a sum of pairwise nonisomorphic irreducible representations of $\pi_1(Z,x)$, permuted transitively by $W(Z_0,x)/\pi_1(Z,x)=\Z$.
Let $n$ be the number of summands, and put $Z_1:=Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^n}$; $W(Z_1,x)$ is the inverse image in $W(Z_0,x)$ of $n\Z$.
If $S$ is one of the irreducible summands of the restriction of $V$ to $\pi_1(Z,x)$, then $S$ is a representation of $W(Z_1,x)$ and
\[
 V=\Ind_{W(Z_1,x)}^{W(Z_0,x)}(S).
\]
If $\mathcal V$ (respectively $\mathcal S$) is the $\Ql$-Weil sheaf corresponding to $V$ (respectively $S$), then $\mathcal V$ is the direct image, under $Z_1\to Z_0$, of $\mathcal S$.

\subsection*{1.3}
Now let $V$ be a semisimple representation of $W(Z_0,x)$.
The quotient $\Z$ of $W(Z_0,x)$ permutes the isomorphism classes of simple constituents of the restriction of $V$ to $\pi_1(Z,x)$.
Let $A$ be the set of orbits, and in each orbit $a$ choose a representative $S'_a$; let $n(a)$ be the number of elements of the orbit.
If $Z_a=Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^{n(a)}}$, the representation $S'_a$ extends to a representation $S_a$ of $W(Z_a,x)$, and $S_a$ is unique up to twist by a character of the quotient $\Z$ of $W(Z_a,x)$.
We choose the twist so that the character $\det(S_a)$ of $W(Z_a,x)$ has finite order.

Apply 1.2 to the irreducible constituents of $V$, and collect the occurrences of $S_a$.
One obtains a decomposition
\begin{equation}
 V=\bigoplus_a \Ind_{W(Z_a,x)}^{W(Z_0,x)}(S_a\otimes W_a),
\tag{1.3.1}
\end{equation}
where $W_a$ is a representation of $W(Z_a,x)$ trivial on $\pi_1(Z,x)$.
This description of $V$ is unique, except that one may
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item twist $S_a$ by a finite-order character of $\Z=W(Z_a,x)/\pi_1(Z,x)$ and $W_a$ by the inverse character;
\item replace $S_a$ by its conjugate $S_a^{(i)}$ by an element $i$ of $W(Z_0,x)/W(Z_a,x)=\Z/n(a)$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{1.4}
Passing from the language of group representations to that of sheaves: $S_a$ (respectively $S_a^{(i)}$) corresponds to $\mathcal S_{a,1}$ (respectively $\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)}$) on $Z_a$, $W_a$ to $\mathcal W_a$ on $\Spec(\F_{q^{n(a)}})$, and, if $p_a$ is the projection from $Z_a$ to $Z_0$, and $\pr_a$ the projection from $Z_a$ to $\Spec(\F_{q^{n(a)}})$, (1.3.1) becomes a decomposition of the $\Ql$-Weil sheaf $\mathcal V$ corresponding to $V$:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal V=\bigoplus_{a\in A}p_{a*}\left(\mathcal S_a\otimes\pr_a^*\mathcal W_a\right).
\tag{1.4.1}
\end{equation}
In this decomposition, the $\det(\mathcal S_a)$ have finite order, and the inverse images $\mathcal S_a^{(i)}$ on $Z$ of the $\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)}$ ($a\in A$, $i\in\Z/n(a)$) are irreducible pairwise nonisomorphic $\Ql$-sheaves.

\subsection*{1.5}
Let $\mathcal F_0$ be smooth irreducible of finite-order determinant on $Z_0$ as in 1.1.
By 1.2, the inverse image $\mathcal F$ of $\mathcal F_0$ on $Z:=Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F$ is a direct sum of nonisomorphic smooth irreducible sheaves $\mathcal S^{(i)}$, permuted transitively by $\Z=W(\F/\F_q)$.
Let $n$ be the number of the $\mathcal S^{(i)}$.
In the remainder of this section, we give a proof of the following statement of Lafforgue [L] VII 7, cf. 0.7.

\paragraph{Theorem 1.6 (Lafforgue).}
At every closed point $x_0$ of $Z_0$, the eigenvalues of $F_{x_0}$ acting on $\mathcal F_0$ are Weil numbers of weight $0$.

If $U_0$ is a nonempty open subset of $Z_0$, the restriction of $\mathcal F_0$ to $U_0$ is still irreducible.
Covering $Z_0$ by affine opens, one reduces to the case where, and we will assume that, $Z_0$ is affine.

\paragraph{Lemma 1.7.}
There exists a connected Galois etale cover $Z'$ of $Z$ such that if the inverse image $Y'$ of $Z'$ by a morphism $f:Y\to Z$ is connected, then $\mathcal F$ and $f^*\mathcal F$ have the same monodromy.

Fix a base point $a$ of $Y$, and still denote by $a$ its image in $Z$.
There is a morphism
\[
 \pi_1(Y,a)\longrightarrow\pi_1(Z,a),
\]
and the conclusion ``same monodromy'' means that $\pi_1(Y,a)$ maps onto the image of $\pi_1(Z,a)$ in $\GL(\mathcal F_a)$.
It implies that the $f^*\mathcal S^{(i)}$ remain smooth irreducible and pairwise nonisomorphic.

\paragraph{Proof of 1.7.}
The monodromy group $M$, the image of $\pi_1(Z,a)$ in $\GL(\mathcal F_a)$, is a closed subgroup of a group $\GL(n,\mathcal O_\lambda)$, hence is a compact $\ell$-adic Lie group.
It therefore admits an open normal pro-$\ell$ subgroup $H$, and the intersection $H'$ of the kernels of the homomorphisms $H\to\Z/\ell$ is again an open normal subgroup.
Take $Z'$ to be the cover corresponding to $H'$.
Suppose $Y'$ connected.
This is equivalent to saying that $\pi_1(Y,a)$ maps onto $M/H'$.
Since every closed subgroup of $H$ which maps onto $H/H'$ coincides with $H$, $\pi_1(Y,a)$ maps onto $M$.

\subsection*{1.8}
Choose a closed immersion of $Z_0$ into an affine space $E_0$ over $\F_q$, and choose $Z'$ as in 1.7.
Let $G_0$ be the $\F_q$-scheme parametrizing affine linear subspaces of codimension $\dim(Z_0)-1$ in $E_0$.
Extend the base field from $\F_q$ to $\F$ and apply to the composite morphism
\[
 f:Z'\to Z\hookrightarrow E
\]
the Bertini theorem in the form given by Jouanolou [J], Th. 6.3(iv), p. 67.
One obtains a nonempty open $U$ of the smooth irreducible scheme $G=G_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F$, such that for every $u\in U(\F)$, corresponding to an affine linear subspace $L_u$ of $E$, the inverse image $f^{-1}(L_u)$ of $Z'$ on $Z\cap L_u$ is irreducible.
Note that this irreducibility implies that of $Z\cap L_u$.
The set of $u$ such that $Z\cap L_u$ is not a smooth curve has dimension strictly smaller than that of $U$.
Thus, after shrinking $U$, one may suppose that the $Z\cap L_u$ are smooth curves.
After further shrinking $U$, one may also suppose that $U$ is of the form $U_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F$, for $U_0$ an open subset of $G_0$.

The number of $\F_{q^m}$-points of $U_0$ is, as $m\to\infty$, of the form
\[
 q^{\dim(U)m}\left(1+O(q^{-1/2})\right)
\]
(Lang-Weil, or the trace formula plus the Weil conjecture).
As soon as $m$ is large enough, $U_0(\F_{q^m})$ is therefore nonempty.
This allows one to choose $m$ prime to $n$ such that $U_0(\F_{q^m})$ is nonempty.

For $\mathcal F_0$ on $Z_0$ to satisfy the conclusion of 1.6, it is necessary and sufficient that this conclusion be satisfied by the inverse image of $\mathcal F_0$ on $Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^m}$.
Replacing $\F_q$ by $\F_{q^m}$, $Z_0$ by $Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^m}$, and $\mathcal F_0$ by its inverse image, one is reduced to proving 1.6 under the additional hypothesis that $U_0$ contains an $\F_q$-point $u_0$.
The plane section $Z_0\cap L_{u_0}$ of $Z_0$ is then a smooth absolutely irreducible curve $Y_0$.
Extend the base field to $\F$.
Since $u_0$ is in $U_0$, 1.7 ensures that the restrictions of the $\mathcal S^{(i)}$ to $Y$ are irreducible and pairwise nonisomorphic.
Because $m$ is prime to $n$, they are permuted by $W(\F/\F_q)$: the restriction of $\mathcal F_0$ to $Y_0$ is irreducible.
Apply 0.6(i) to it: for every closed point $y_0$ of $Y_0\subset Z_0$, the eigenvalues of $F_{y_0}$ acting on $\mathcal F_0$ are Weil numbers of weight $0$.
One concludes by the following proposition.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.9.}
Let $\mathcal F_0$ be a smooth $\Ql$-Weil sheaf on a connected scheme $X_0$ over $\F_q$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item there exists a closed point $x_0$ of $X_0$ such that the eigenvalues of $F_{x_0}$ acting on $\mathcal F_0$ are Weil numbers of weight $0$;
\item the same assertion holds at all closed points of $X_0$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Proof, for $X_0$ a smooth connected curve.}
Suppose that $\mathcal F_0$ satisfies (i).
Every irreducible subquotient $\mathcal F'_0$ still satisfies (i), and it suffices to verify (ii) for each $\mathcal F'_0$.
Let $\mathcal F''_0$ be a twist of $\mathcal F'_0$ whose determinant has finite order.
By 0.6(i), $\mathcal F''_0$ satisfies (ii).
Testing at $x_0$, one sees that the twist is by a Weil number of weight $0$, and the validity of (ii) is invariant under such a twist.

\paragraph{Proof, general case.}
For any closed point $x'_0$ of $X_0$, there is a sequence of smooth connected curves $Y_0^j$ and morphisms $f^j:Y_0^j\to X_0$ ($1\le j\le N$) such that $x_0$ is in the image of $Y_0^1$, $x'_0$ is in the image of $Y_0^N$, and the images of $Y_0^j$ and $Y_0^{j+1}$ intersect nontrivially for $1\le j<N$.
If (i) holds at $x_0$, the first part of the proof shows that (ii) holds for the inverse image of $\mathcal F_0$ on $Y_0^1$, hence that (i) holds at every closed point of $X_0$ in the image of $Y_0$.
Similarly, if (i) holds at the closed points in $f(Y_j)$, and hence at at least one closed point of $f(Y_{j+1})$, then (i) holds at the closed points in $f(Y_{j+1})$, and one eventually reaches $x'_0$.

\section*{2. The case of curves: quantitative version}

\subsection*{2.1}
Let $X$ be a smooth connected curve over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, let $\overline X$ be the smooth projective completion of $X$, and let $S$ be the set of points at infinity: $X=\overline X-S$.
Let $\mathcal F$ be a smooth $\Ql$-sheaf of rank $r$ on $X$.
Put
\[
 \chi_c(X,\mathcal F):=\sum (-1)^i\dim H^i_c(X,\mathcal F).
\]
This Euler-Poincare characteristic is given by the Grothendieck-Ogg-Shafarevich formula
\begin{equation}
 \chi_c(X,\mathcal F)=r\chi_c(X)-\sum_{s\in S}\Sw_s(\mathcal F).
\tag{2.1.1}
\end{equation}
If the curve $\overline X$ has genus $g$, then
\begin{equation}
 \chi_c(X)=2-2g-|S|.
\tag{2.1.2}
\end{equation}
The term $\Sw_s(\mathcal F)$ is the Swan conductor of $\mathcal F$ at $s$.

Let $K_s$ be the fraction field of the henselization of $X$ at $s$.
The reader who prefers may replace ``henselization'' by ``completion.''
This does not change $\Gal(\overline K_s/K_s)$, for $\overline K_s$ an algebraic closure of $K_s$.
The $\Ql$-sheaves on $\Spec(K_s)$ are identified with continuous $\Ql$-linear representations $V$ of $\Gal(\overline K_s/K_s)$.
The conductor $\Sw_s(\mathcal F)$ is the Swan conductor $\Sw(V)$ of the linear representation $V$ of $\Gal(\overline K_s/K_s)$ corresponding to the inverse image of $\mathcal F$ on $\Spec(K_s)$.

The conductor $\Sw(V)$ is additive in $V$, for short exact sequences.
Let $\Gal(\overline K_s/K_s)^\beta$ denote the ramification subgroups of $\Gal(\overline K_s/K_s)$ in upper numbering.
Define $\alpha(V)$ as the infimum of the $\beta>0$ such that $\Gal(\overline K_s/K_s)^\beta$ acts trivially on $V$.
If the representation $V$ is irreducible, then
\begin{equation}
 \Sw(V)=\dim(V)\,\alpha(V)\qquad (V\text{ irreducible})
\tag{2.1.3}
\end{equation}
(a consequence of Serre [S] 19.1 and of the definition of the Herbrand functions relating the lower numbering to the upper numbering).
If $V$ comes from $\mathcal F$ on $X$, we put $\alpha_s(\mathcal F):=\alpha(V)$.

It follows from (2.1.3) by additivity that
\begin{equation}
 \Sw(V)\le \dim(V)\,\alpha(V).
\tag{2.1.4}
\end{equation}
Let $P$ be the pro-$p$ Sylow subgroup of $\Gal(\overline K_s/K_s)$.
Since $V$ is $\ell$-adic, it acts on $V$ through a finite quotient $\overline P$.
Both $\Sw(V)$ and $\alpha(V)$ vanish if and only if $V$ is tame, i.e. if $P$ acts trivially.
Otherwise, $\alpha(V)$ is the largest rational number such that $\overline P^{\alpha(V)}$ is nontrivial.
The importance for us of the invariant $\alpha(V)$ comes from the observation that
\begin{equation}
 \alpha(V\otimes W)\le \sup(\alpha(V),\alpha(W)).
\tag{2.1.5}
\end{equation}

Poincare duality places $H^2_c(X,\mathcal F)$ and $H^0(X,\mathcal F^\vee(1))$ in perfect duality.
In particular, $H^2_c(X,\mathcal F)$ has rank at most $r$, and has rank $r$ if $\mathcal F$ is constant.
If the curve $X$ is affine, then $H^0_c(X,\mathcal F)=0$, and (2.1.1), (2.1.4) give
\begin{equation}
 \dim H^1_c(X,\mathcal F)\le r\left(b_1(X)+\sum \alpha_s(\mathcal F)\right)\qquad (X\text{ affine}),
\tag{2.1.6}
\end{equation}
where we have put
\begin{equation}
 b_1(X)=\dim H^1_c(X,\Ql)=2g+|S|-1\qquad (X\text{ affine}).
\tag{2.1.7}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{2.2}
Let $X_0=\overline X_0-S_0$ be a curve over $\F_q$ as in 0.0, and let $\mathcal F_0$ be a smooth $\Ql$-Weil sheaf of rank $r$ on $X_0$.
Assume $\mathcal F_0$ algebraic, in the sense of 0.9.
We do not assume $\mathcal F_0$ irreducible.
Let $E$ be a Galois extension of $\Q$ in $\Ql$ containing the $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ for $x$ in any $X_0(\F_{q^n})$, $n\ge1$.
By 0.6, for every $\sigma\in\Gal(E/\Q)$, there is a smooth semisimple $\Ql$-Weil sheaf $\sigma(\mathcal F_0)$ such that, for every $x$ in $X_0(\F_{q^n})$,
\[
 \Tr(F_x,\sigma(\mathcal F_0))=\sigma(\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)).
\]
We write $\sigma(\mathcal F)$ for the inverse image of $\sigma(\mathcal F_0)$ on $X$.
This notation is reasonable because $\sigma(\mathcal F)$, which is defined up to isomorphism, depends only on $\mathcal F$; this follows from (1.4.1) and a compatibility between ``$\sigma$'' and twisting.
For each $s\in S$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \Sw_s(\sigma\mathcal F)=\Sw_s(\mathcal F),\qquad
 \alpha_s(\sigma\mathcal F)=\alpha_s(\mathcal F).
\tag{2.2.1}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Sketch of proof.}
One may suppose $\mathcal F_0$ irreducible.
If $\mathcal F_0$ corresponds to an automorphic representation $\pi$, by the rationalized correspondence, then $\pi$ is defined over $E$ and $\sigma\mathcal F_0$ corresponds to $\sigma\pi$.
At each $s\in S_0$, $\mathcal F_0$ defines a representation of the local Weil group.
By the local correspondence, its Frobenius-semisimplification ([De1] 8.6) corresponds to the local component $\pi_s$ of $\pi$.
Note that isomorphism classes of continuous $\Ql$-linear $F$-semisimple representations of the local Weil group are in bijection with isomorphism classes of $\Ql$-linear $F$-semisimple representations, loc. cit., of the Weil-Deligne group.
The latter are purely algebraic objects.
If $\pi_s$ is defined over $E$, then the corresponding representation will be also, and conjugation by $\sigma$ is compatible with the rationalized correspondence.
In particular, $\mathcal F_0$ and $\sigma(\mathcal F_0)$ give rise to representations of the wild inertia group at $s$ having the same kernel, and one is reduced to (2.1.3).

For our main result, the following more elementary lemma suffices.

\paragraph{Lemma 2.3.}
If $\mathcal F$ is tamely ramified, then $\sigma(\mathcal F)$ is also tamely ramified.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The image under $\sigma$ of the $L$-function of $\mathcal F_0$,
\[
 L(X_0,\mathcal F_0,t)=\prod_{x\in |X_0|}\det\left(1-F_{x_0}t^{\deg(x_0)},\mathcal F_0\right)^{-1},
\]
is the $L$-function of $\sigma(\mathcal F_0)$.
This $L$-function is a rational function, and by Grothendieck's cohomological interpretation of $L$-functions the order of its pole at $t=\infty$ is $-\chi_c(\mathcal F)$.
Thus $\chi_c(\mathcal F)=\chi_c(\sigma\mathcal F)$.
Since $\mathcal F$ is tamely ramified, the $\Sw_s(\mathcal F)$ are zero.
Comparing (2.1.1) for $\mathcal F$ and $\sigma\mathcal F$, one sees that the sum of the $\Sw_s(\sigma\mathcal F)$, hence each of them, is zero, i.e. that $\sigma(\mathcal F)$ is tamely ramified.

\subsection*{2.4}
Suppose $X_0$ affine, and let $\mathcal F_0,\mathcal G_0$ be two smooth semisimple Weil sheaves of rank $r$ on $X_0$.
Write $\log_q^+$ for the function $\sup(0,\log_q)$ and $[\ ]$ for the integer-part function.
Put
\begin{align}
 \alpha_s(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)&:=\alpha_s(\mathcal F\oplus\mathcal G)=\sup(\alpha_s(\mathcal F),\alpha_s(\mathcal G)),\tag{2.4.1}\\
 N_0&=2\log_q^+\left(2r^2\left(b_1(X)+\sum_{s\in S}\alpha_s(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)\right)\right),\tag{2.4.2}\\
 N&=[N_0]+2r.\tag{2.4.3}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Proposition 2.5.}
If for every integer $n\le N$ and every $x\in X(\F_{q^n})$ one has
\[
 \Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_x,\mathcal G_0),
\]
then $\mathcal F_0$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal G_0$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Apply (1.3), (1.4) to $\mathcal F_0\oplus\mathcal G_0$.
One obtains decompositions (1.4.1)
\begin{align}
 \mathcal F_0&=\bigoplus_{a\in A}p_{a*}\left(\mathcal S_{a,1}\otimes\pr_a^*\mathcal W_a\right),\tag{2.5.1}\\
 \mathcal G_0&=\bigoplus_{a\in A}p_{a*}\left(\mathcal S_{a,1}\otimes\pr_a^*\mathcal W'_a\right).
\end{align}
In (2.5.1), to each $a\in A$ is attached an integer $n(a)\ge1$; $\mathcal S_{a,1}$ is a smooth $\Ql$-Weil sheaf on $X_a:=X_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^{n(a)}}$; $\mathcal W_a$ and $\mathcal W'_a$ are $\Ql$-Weil sheaves on $\Spec(\F_{q^{n(a)}})$; and $p_a$ and $\pr_a$ are the projections from $X_a$ to $X_0$ and to $\Spec(\F_{q^{n(a)}})$.
Let $\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)}$ denote the image of $\mathcal S_{a,1}$ by the $i$-th power of the Frobenius $F\in\Gal(\F_{q^{n(a)}}/\F_q)$, and omit the subscript $1$ to indicate the inverse image from $X_a$ to $X$.
By (1.4), the $\mathcal S_{a,1}$, $\mathcal W_a$, and $\mathcal W'_a$ in (2.5.1) satisfy
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\det(\mathcal S_{a,1})$ has finite order;
\item the $\mathcal S_a^{(i)}$ ($a\in A$, $i\in\Z/n(a)$) are smooth irreducible $\Ql$-sheaves on $X$, pairwise nonisomorphic;
\item for each $a$, $\mathcal W_a$ or $\mathcal W'_a$ is nonzero.
\end{enumerate}
Since $\mathcal S_a^{(i)}$ is a direct factor of $\mathcal F$ or $\mathcal G$, one has at every point $s\in S$
\[
 \alpha_s(\mathcal S_a^{(i)})\le \sup(\alpha_s(\mathcal F),\alpha_s(\mathcal G)).
\]
Let $A(n)$ denote the set of $a$ in $A$ such that $n(a)\mid n$.

\paragraph{Lemma 2.6.}
If $n>N_0$ (2.4.2), the functions on $X_0(\F_{q^n})$
\[
 t_{a,i}:x\longmapsto \Tr(F_x,\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)})\qquad (a\in A(n),\ i\in\Z/n(a))
\]
are linearly independent.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The functions $t_{a,i}$ take values in a number field $E\subset\Ql$.
Embed $E$ into $\mathbb C$ to treat them as complex-valued functions.
The idea of the proof is to show that they are nearly orthogonal in $L^2(X_0(\F_{q^n}))$.

By 0.6(i), $\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)}$ is pure of weight $0$.
The complex conjugate function of $t_{a,i}$ is therefore
\[
 x\longmapsto\Tr(F_x,(\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)})^\vee),
\]
and the scalar product $\langle t_{b,j},t_{a,i}\rangle=\sum \overline{t_{b,j}(x)}t_{a,i}(x)$ is
\[
 \langle t_{b,j},t_{a,i}\rangle=
 \sum \Tr(F_x,\Hom(\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)},\mathcal S_{b,1}^{(j)})).
\]
By the trace formula, this sum is the trace of the Frobenius $F\in W(\F/\F_{q^n})$:
\[
 \langle t_{b,j},t_{a,i}\rangle=
 \sum (-1)^k\Tr\left(F,H^k_c\left(X,\Hom(\mathcal S_a^{(i)},\mathcal S_b^{(j)})\right)\right).
\]
Since $X$ is affine, $H^0_c$ is zero.
The ``dominant'' term is given by $H^2_c$: it is $q^n$ if $(a,i)=(b,j)$ and zero otherwise.
The promised almost orthogonality comes from the fact that the term $k=1$ is $O(q^{n/2})$ for large $n$.
More precisely, since $\Hom(\mathcal S_{a,1}^{(i)},\mathcal S_{b,1}^{(j)})$ is pure of weight $0$, the eigenvalues of $F$ on its $H^1_c$ have absolute value $q^{n/2}$ or $1$, and the term $k=1$ is bounded in absolute value by $q^{n/2}$ times
\[
 \dim H^1_c\le \dim\mathcal S_b^{(j)}\cdot \dim\mathcal S_a^{(i)}\cdot\left(b_1(X)+\sum\alpha_s(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)\right).
\]
Suppose that there is a linear dependence relation $\sum \lambda_{b,j}t_{b,j}=0$.
Choose $a,i$ such that $|\lambda_{a,i}|$ is maximal among the $|\lambda_{b,j}|$.
Dividing by $\lambda_{a,i}$, one may suppose $\lambda_{a,i}=1$ and $|\lambda_{b,j}|\le1$ for $b\in A(n)$ and $j\in\Z/n(b)$.
Then
\[
0=\left\langle\sum \lambda_{b,j}t_{b,j},t_{a,i}\right\rangle
=\sum_{b,j}\lambda_{b,j}\langle t_{b,j},t_{a,i}\rangle
=q^n+\text{remainder},
\]
where the absolute value of the remainder is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{b,j}|\lambda_{b,j}|\left|\Tr\left(F,H^1_c(X,\Hom(\mathcal S_a^{(i)},\mathcal S_b^{(j)}))\right)\right|
 \le q^{n/2}\,2r^2\left(b_1(X)+\sum\alpha_s(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)\right).
\]
The hypothesis on $n$ ensures $|\text{remainder}|<q^n$: contradiction.

\paragraph{Corollary 2.7.}
Let $F$ denote the Frobenius of $W(\F/\F_{q^n})$.
If $n>N_0$ and $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_x,\mathcal G_0)$ for $x\in X_0(\F_{q^n})$, then
\[
 \Tr(F,\mathcal W_a)=\Tr(F,\mathcal W'_a)
\]
for each $a\in A(n)$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The decompositions (2.5.1) give the identity between trace functions
\[
 \sum t_{a,i}\Tr(F,\mathcal W_a)=\sum t_{a,i}\Tr(F,\mathcal W'_a),
\]
and one applies 2.6.

\subsection*{2.8. End of the proof of 2.5}
It remains to show that, for each $a$, and for $F$ the geometric Frobenius generator of $W(\F/\F_{q^{n(a)}})$, $F$ has the same multiset of eigenvalues on $\mathcal W_a$ and $\mathcal W'_a$.
By 2.7, if $n$ is divisible by $n(a)$ and
\[
 [N_0]+1\le n\le N=[N_0]+2r,
\]
then
\[
 \Tr(F^{n/n(a)},\mathcal W_a)=\Tr(F^{n/n(a)},\mathcal W'_a).
\]
There are at least $[2r/a]$ such values of $n$; $\mathcal W_a$ and $\mathcal W'_a$ are of dimension at most $[r/a]$; it remains to apply the following lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma 2.9.}
Let $\alpha_i$ be $k$ distinct nonzero numbers.
If the $\lambda_i$ satisfy, for a suitable $m$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum\lambda_i\alpha_i^r=0\qquad\text{for }m\le r<m+k,
\tag{2.9.1}
\end{equation}
then all the $\lambda_i$ are zero.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Equation (2.9.1) can be rewritten
\[
 \sum(\lambda_i\alpha_i^m)\alpha_i^s=0\qquad\text{for }0\le s<k,
\]
and the Vandermonde determinant $\det(\alpha_i^s)$ is nonzero.

In the proposition which follows, $X_0$ is assumed affine, and $\mathcal F_0$ is a smooth Weil sheaf of rank $r$ on $X_0$.
Assume $\mathcal F_0$ algebraic in the sense of 0.9.
Put
\[
 N_0=2\log_q^+\left(2r^2\left(b_1(X)+\sum\alpha_s(\mathcal F)\right)\right),\qquad
 N=[N_0]+2r.
\]

\paragraph{Proposition 2.10.}
Let $E_0$ be the extension of $\Q$ in $\Ql$ generated by the $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ for $x$ in $X_0(\F_{q^n})$ with $n\le N$.
Then, for every $n$ and every $x\in X_0(\F_{q^n})$, $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ is in $E_0$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
After semisimplifying $\mathcal F_0$, one reduces to the case where $\mathcal F_0$ is semisimple.
Let $E$ be as in 2.2, a sufficiently large Galois extension of $\Q$ in $\Ql$.
We must show that for $\sigma\in\Gal(E/E_0)$, the $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ are fixed by $\sigma$.
This is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 \Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_x,\sigma(\mathcal F_0)).
\tag{2.10.1}
\end{equation}
By hypothesis, (2.10.1) is true if $x$ is in some $X(\F_{q^n})$ with $n\le N$.
By 2.5 and by the fact that $\alpha_s(\mathcal F_0)=\alpha_s(\sigma(\mathcal F_0))$, $\mathcal F_0$ is isomorphic to $\sigma(\mathcal F_0)$.
The assertion follows.

\subsection*{2.11. Variants}
Keep the hypotheses and notation of 2.5.
Let $q:X'\to X$ be a connected etale cover of $X$ on which the inverse images of $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal G$ are tamely ramified.
For every $\Ql$-sheaf $\mathcal H$ on $X$ whose inverse image on $X'$ is tamely ramified, the morphism
\[
 q:H^*(X,\mathcal H)\to H^*(X',q^*\mathcal H)
\]
is injective, because its composite with $\Tr_q$ is multiplication by the degree of the cover.
Thus
\[
 \dim H^1_c(X,\mathcal H)\le \dim H^1_c(X',\mathcal H)\le \rank(\mathcal H)b_1(X').
\]
If one repeats the arguments proving 2.5 using this estimate rather than (2.1.6), one obtains the following.

\paragraph{Variant 2.12.}
Put $N'_0:=2\log_q^+(2r^2b_1(X'))$ and $N':=[N'_0]+2r$.
If for every integer $n\le N'$ and every $x\in X_0(\F_{q^n})$ one has
\[
 \Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_x,\mathcal G_0),
\]
then $\mathcal F_0$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal G_0$.

Similarly, if $\mathcal F_0$ is as in 2.10 and its inverse image by $q:X'\to X$ is tamely ramified, then $\sigma(\mathcal F_0)$ has the same property: after a finite extension of the base field $\F_q$, one may suppose that $q:X'\to X$ comes from $q_0:X'_0\to X_0$ and apply 2.3.
Repeating similarly the arguments which prove 2.10, one obtains the following.

\paragraph{Variant 2.13.}
Let $E_0$ be the extension of $\Q$ in $\Ql$ generated by the $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ for $x$ in $X_0(\F_{q^n})$ with $n\le N'$, where $N'$ is as in 2.12.
Then, for every $n$ and every $x\in X_0(\F_{q^n})$, $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ is in $E_0$.

\paragraph{Remark 2.14.}
The hypothesis that $\mathcal F_0$ is algebraic ensures that $E_0$ is a finite extension of $\Q$.
It is not required in order to prove 2.13.
If $E_0\subset E$ are extensions of $\Q$ in $\Ql$, and if $P$ is the set of $\sigma:E\to\Ql$ extending the inclusion of $E_0$ in $\Ql$, then
\[
 E_0=\{e\in E\mid \sigma(e)=e\text{ for every }\sigma\in\Sigma\},
\]
and this observation can replace the Galois argument.

\section*{3. Main theorem}

\paragraph{Theorem 3.1.}
Let $Z_0$ be a scheme of finite type over $\F_q$.
If $\mathcal F_0$ is an algebraic $\Ql$-Weil sheaf (0.9) on $Z_0$, there exists a finite extension $E\subset\Ql$ of $\Q$ such that for every $n$ and every $x\in Z_0(\F_{q^n})$, $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ lies in $E$.

The fact that all these traces lie in $E$ is equivalent to saying that, at every closed point $x_0$ of $Z_0$, the local factor of the $L$-function of $\mathcal F_0$ has coefficients in $E$; see 0.5.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.2.}
Theorem 3.1 is implied by the special case in which one assumes that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $Z_0$ is affine, smooth, irreducible, and is endowed with an etale morphism $\varphi:Z_0\to\mathbb E^k_0$ to an affine space over $\F_q$;
\item $\mathcal F_0$ is smooth, irreducible, and $\det(\mathcal F_0)$ has finite order.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The scheme $Z_0$ of 3.1 admits a partition into irreducible locally closed parts $F_i$ satisfying (i) and such that $\mathcal F_0|_{F_i}$ is smooth.
It suffices to treat separately each $(F_i,\mathcal F_0|_{F_i})$.
One can then treat separately each irreducible subquotient of $\mathcal F_0|_{F_i}$, and twist it so that it satisfies (ii).

\paragraph{Lemma 3.3.}
There exists a connected etale cover $q:Z'\to Z$ such that the monodromy group of $q^*\mathcal F$ is a pro-$\ell$ group.

What matters for us is that the restriction of $q^*\mathcal F$ to a curve drawn on $Z'$ is tamely ramified at infinity.

\paragraph{Proof.}
There exists a finite extension $E_\lambda$ of $\mathbb Q_\ell$ in $\Ql$, with ring of integers $\mathcal O_\lambda$, and a torsion-free smooth $\mathcal O_\lambda$-sheaf $\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ such that
\[
 \mathcal F\simeq \widetilde{\mathcal F}\otimes_{\mathcal O_\lambda}\Ql.
\]
The reduction of $\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ modulo the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_\lambda$ of $\mathcal O_\lambda$ is a locally constant etale sheaf, and it suffices to take for $Z'$ a cover of $Z$ on which it becomes constant.
If $\mathcal F$ has rank $r$, one can take for $Z'$ a connected component of the cover
\[
 \Isom\left(\widetilde{\mathcal F}/\mathfrak m_\lambda\widetilde{\mathcal F},(\mathcal O_\lambda/\mathfrak m_\lambda)^r\right).
\]
On the cover $Z'$, the monodromy of each $\widetilde{\mathcal F}/\mathfrak m_\lambda^n\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ is an $\ell$-group.

\paragraph{Proposition 3.4.}
If the integer $n$ is sufficiently large, then for every $x\in Z_0(\F_{q^n})$ the trace $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ is contained in the extension of $\Q$ in $\Ql$ generated by the traces $\Tr(F_y,\mathcal F_0)$ with $y\in Z_0(\F_{q^m})$ and $m<n$.

If $N$ is such that the integers $n>N$ are ``sufficiently large,'' it follows by induction that every trace $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ is contained in the extension of $\Q$ generated by the $\Tr(F_y,\mathcal F_0)$ with $y$ in some $Z_0(\F_{q^m})$ with $m\le N$.
This extension is finite, and therefore 3.4 implies 3.1.

Let $x\in Z_0(\F_{q^n})$, and try to prove the conclusion of 3.4 for $x$.
We will succeed for $n$ large enough.

Choose a generator of $\F_{q^n}$ over $\F_q$.
This choice defines an $\F_{q^n}$-point $x'$ of the affine line $\mathbb E^1_0$ over $\F_q$, whose $\Gal(\F_{q^n}/\F_q)$-conjugates are all distinct.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.5.}
If $y$ is an $\F_{q^n}$-point of $\mathbb E^1_0$, there exists an $\F_q$-morphism $P:\mathbb E^1_0\to\mathbb E^1_0$, i.e. a polynomial $P\in\F_q[T]$, which sends $x'$ to $y$ and has degree $\le n-1$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The $\sigma x'$, for $\sigma\in\Gal(\F_{q^n}/\F_q)$, are $n$ distinct points of the affine line.
There is therefore a unique polynomial $P\in\F_{q^n}[T]$, of degree $\le n-1$, which for each $\sigma$ takes at $\sigma x'$ the value $\sigma y$.
Its uniqueness ensures its Galois invariance, hence that it lies in $\F_q[T]$.

\paragraph{Corollary 3.6.}
There exists an $\F_q$-morphism $P:\mathbb E^1_0\to\mathbb E^k_0$, whose coordinates are polynomials of degree $\le n-1$, which sends $x'\in\mathbb E^1_0(\F_{q^n})$ to $\varphi(x)\in\mathbb E^k_0(\F_{q^n})$.

This is checked by applying 3.5 to each coordinate of $\varphi(x)$.

\subsection*{3.7}
Fix $P$ as in 3.6.
Let $X''_0$ be the fiber product of $Z_0$ and $\mathbb E^1_0$ over $\mathbb E^k_0$.
Since $\varphi(x)=P(x')$, there exists $\bar x\in X''_0(\F_{q^n})$ mapping to $x$ and $x'$.
Let $X_0$ denote the connected component of $X''_0$ containing $\bar x$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
X_0 \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[dr,"\widetilde P"'] & X''_0 \arrow[r,"\varphi''"] \arrow[d] & \mathbb E^1_0 \arrow[d,"P"] & \bar x\in X_0(\F_{q^n}) \arrow[d]\\
& Z_0 \arrow[r,"\varphi"] & \mathbb E^k_0 & x\in Z_0(\F_{q^n}) .
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{3.7.1}
\end{equation}
The morphism $\varphi''$ is etale, of degree over the generic point of $\mathbb E^1_0$ at most equal to the degree $D$ of $\varphi$.
Let $k_0$ be the field of constants of the curve $X_0$ over $\F_q$.
The degree $d$ of $k_0$ over $\F_q$ is $\le D$, because $X_0$ is of degree $\le D$ over $\mathbb E^1_0$, and $d$ divides $n$, because $X_0$ has a point over $\F_{q^n}$.
Extend the base field from $\F_q$ to $\F_{q^d}$, and let $X_1$ (respectively $Z_1$) be the connected component of $X_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^d}$ (respectively of $Z_0\otimes_{\F_q}\F_{q^d}$) which contains $\bar x$ (respectively $x$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
\bar x:\Spec(\F_{q^n}) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,equal] & X_1 \arrow[r,"\sim"] \arrow[d] & X_0 \arrow[d,"\widetilde P"]\\
x:\Spec(\F_{q^n}) \arrow[r] & Z_1 \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & Z_0 \arrow[d]\\
& \Spec(\F_{q^d}) \arrow[r] & \Spec(\F_q) .
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{3.7.2}
\end{equation}
The curve $X_1$ over $\F_{q^d}$ is smooth and absolutely irreducible.
Finally extend the base field from $\F_{q^d}$ to $\F$, to obtain $X$ and $Z$, and let $X'$ be a connected component of the inverse image $X''$ of the etale cover $Z'$ of $Z$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
X' \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d] & X'' \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & X\\
Z' \arrow[r] & Z . &
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{3.7.3}
\end{equation}

\subsection*{3.8. Proof of 3.4}
The inverse image of $\mathcal F$ on $X'$ is tamely ramified.
We propose to apply 2.13 to the curve $X_1$ over $\F_{q^d}$, to $X'\to X$, and to the inverse image $\mathcal F_1$ of $\mathcal F_0$ on $X_1$.
Let us estimate $b_1(X')$.

The composite $\F$-morphism
\[
 Z'\to Z\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathbb E^k
\]
is affine.
It therefore factors through a closed immersion $Z'\hookrightarrow\mathbb E^{k+k'}$, and there is a family of $A'$ equations of degree $\le B$ defining $Z'$ in $\mathbb E^{k+k'}$.

The graph $\Gamma_P$ of $P:\mathbb E^1\to\mathbb E^k$ is defined in $\mathbb E^{1+k}$ by $k$ equations of degree $\le n-1$.
The fiber product, calculated over $\F$, of $Z'\to\mathbb E^k$ and $P:\mathbb E^1\to\mathbb E^k$ is the intersection, in $\mathbb E^{1+k+k'}$, of the inverse images of $Z'\subset\mathbb E^{k+k'}$ and of $\Gamma_P\subset\mathbb E^{1+k}$.
It is therefore defined, in $\mathbb E^{1+k+k'}$, by $A:=A'+k$ equations of degree $\le\sup(B,n-1)$.
By Katz [K], this implies an upper bound for the sum of the Betti numbers of this fiber product, and a fortiori of $b_1(X')$, since $X'$ is a connected component of the fiber product.

For $n>B$, one has $A$ equations of degree $\le n-1$ in an affine space of dimension $k+k'+1$, and Katz gives
\begin{equation}
 b_1(X')\le 6\cdot 2^A(An+3)^{k+k'+1}.
\tag{3.8.1}
\end{equation}
Put, as in 2.12,
\[
 N'_0=2\log_{q^d}^+(2r^2b_1(X'))\qquad\text{and}\qquad
 N'=[N'_0]+2r.
\]
The bound (3.8.1) is polynomial in $n$, and $d$ is bounded by $D$.
As soon as $n$ is large enough, one therefore has
\[
 \frac nd>N'.
\]
Assume $n$ large enough for this inequality to hold.

By (3.7.2), one has $\Tr(F_{\bar x},\mathcal F_1)=\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$.
By 2.13, this trace is contained in the field generated by the $\Tr(F_y,\mathcal F_1)$ for $y$ in $X_1(\F_{q^m})$ with $d\mid m$ and with $m/d$, the degree of $\F_{q^m}$ over $\F_{q^d}$, at most $N'$.
Again by (3.7.2), these traces are also the $\Tr(F_y,\widetilde P^*\mathcal F_0)$ for $y$ in $X_0(\F_{q^m})$ with $m/d\le N'$.
We have
\[
 \Tr(F_y,\widetilde P^*\mathcal F_0)=\Tr(F_{\widetilde P(y)},\mathcal F_0),
\]
and $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ is in the field generated by the $\Tr(F_y,\mathcal F_0)$ with $y\in Z_0(\F_{q^m})$ and
\[
 \frac md\le N'<\frac nd,
\]
and hence $m<n$.
This proves 3.4.

\paragraph{Remark 3.9.}
If one does not assume $\mathcal F_0$ algebraic, it remains true that the extension of $\Q$ in $\Ql$ generated by the $\Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0)$ for $x$ in any $Z_0(\F_{q^n})$ is an extension of finite type.
One may deduce this by twisting from the algebraic case, or repeat the preceding arguments while applying 2.14.

\paragraph{Remark 3.10.}
Except in the proof of 3.3, our arguments have used the sheaf $\mathcal F_0$ only through its inverse images on smooth curves.
Theorem 3.1 therefore remains valid if, instead of $\mathcal F_0$, one is given ``trace'' functions $t_n$ on the $X_0(\F_{q^n})$, with values in $\Ql$, having the following two properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*.]
\item For every smooth curve $X_0$ and every morphism $\varphi:X_0\to Z_0$, there is a smooth $\Ql$-sheaf $\mathcal F_0[\varphi]$ on $X_0$ such that, for every $n$ and every $x\in X_0(\F_{q^n})$,
\[
 \Tr(F_x,\mathcal F_0[\varphi])=t_n(\varphi(x)).
\]
\item There exists a connected etale cover $q:Z'\to Z$ such that for every $\varphi:X_0\to Z_0$ as in a., if $X'$ denotes the etale cover of $X$ obtained as the inverse image of $Z'$, then the inverse image of $\mathcal F_0[\varphi]$ on $X'$ is tamely ramified at infinity.
\end{enumerate}
Hypothesis b. replaces 3.3.
The conclusion is that the values of the $t_n$ generate a finite-type extension of $\Q$; a finite extension if the values of the $t_n$ are algebraic.
