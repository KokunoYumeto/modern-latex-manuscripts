\section*{1. Generically Smooth Cohen-Macaulay Supercurves}

In this section I propose an analogue, in algebraic supergeometry, of the notion of a geometrically reduced curve.

The accepted notion of a smooth supercurve $X/S$ is the following: a superscheme $X$ over the superscheme $S$, smooth of relative dimension $(1,1)$, equipped with
\[
  \cD\subset T_{X/S}
\]
locally a direct factor of dimension $(0,1)$, and ``as non-integrable as possible.'' For such a system $(X,\cD)$, one identifies $T_{X/S}/\cD$ with $\cD^{\otimes 2}$ by
\[
  D_1\otimes D_2\longmapsto \frac12[D_1,D_2].
\]
Dualizing $\cD\subset T_{X/S}$ gives
\begin{equation}\tag{1.1}
  \Omega^1_{X/S}\longrightarrow \cD^\vee,
\end{equation}
and the isomorphism $T_{X/S}/\cD\simeq \cD^{\otimes 2}$ dualizes to
\[
  0\longrightarrow \cD^{\vee\otimes 2}
    \longrightarrow \Omega^1_{X/S}
    \longrightarrow \cD^\vee\longrightarrow 0,
\]
whence an isomorphism
\[
  \Ber(\Omega^1_{X/S})=\cD^\vee.
\]
Thus one obtains
\begin{equation}\tag{1.2}
  \delta:\Omega^1_{X/S}\longrightarrow \omega_{X/S}:=\Ber(\Omega^1_{X/S}),
\end{equation}
that is, a derivation
\[
  \delta:\cO_X\longrightarrow\omega_{X/S}.
\]

My choice of signs may be ambiguous. For $X$ with local coordinates $(z,\theta)$, with
\[
  D=\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z,
\]
I want
\[
  \delta:f\longmapsto \bigl((\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z)f\bigr)[dz/d\theta],
\]
where $[dz/d\theta]$ is the basis of $\Ber(\Omega^1_{X/S})$ defined by the basis $(dz,d\theta)$ of $\Omega^1_{X/S}$.

Conversely, $\delta$ determines $\cD$: $\cD$ is the orthogonal of $\Ker(\delta)$, or the multiples of $\delta$ in the basis of $\Ber(\Omega^1_{X/S})$. Notice that, for an epimorphism $\delta:\Omega^1_{X/S}\to\omega_X$, it is not enough that $\Ker(\delta)^{\perp}$ be as non-integrable as possible for $\delta$ to come from a supercurve structure: the normalization must be correct,
\[
  \delta f=(Df)\ell,
  \qquad \ell=[D^2/D]^{-1}.
\]

In local coordinates $(z,\theta)$, for
\[
  D:=\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z
\]
(an odd derivation), one has:

\paragraph{Lemma 1.1.}
For
\[
  \delta=(aD+bD^2)[dz/d\theta]
\]
to define a supercurve structure, it is necessary and sufficient that $a$ be invertible and that
\[
  [(aD+bD^2)^2/(aD+bD^2)]=[D^2/D],
\]
i.e.
\begin{equation}\tag{1.1.1}
  \Ber\begin{pmatrix}
    a^2+bD^2b+aDb & b\\
    -(aDa+bD^2a) & a
  \end{pmatrix}=1,
\end{equation}
where
\[
  \Ber\begin{pmatrix}a&\beta\\ \gamma&d\end{pmatrix}
   =(ad-\beta\gamma)d^{-2}.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
One has
\[
  (aD+bD^2)^2=(aDa+bD^2a)D+(a^2+bD^2b+aDb)D^2.
\]
The case that we shall use most is the following: $\delta=D\ell$ is a supercurve structure, $S_0\hookrightarrow S$ is defined by an ideal of square zero, and
\[
  D_1=D+a_1D+b_1D^2
\]
coincides with $D$ over $S_0$. The Berezinian then reduces to a simple expression.

\paragraph{Corollary 1.2.}
Under the hypotheses just stated, the conditions of 1.1 are equivalent to
\begin{equation}\tag{1.2.1}
  a_1+Db_1=0.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Write
\[
  (2a_1+Db_1)-a_1=0.
\]
Of course, in the above, $a$ and $a_1$ are even, while $b$ and $b_1$ are odd.

\paragraph{Remark 1.3.}
(i) For $X/S$ smooth of relative dimension $(1,1)$, let $\ell$ be a basis of $\omega_X$ and let
\[
  \delta=D\ell:\cO\longrightarrow\omega
\]
be a derivation. The condition that $D$ generate a local direct factor of dimension $(1,0)$ in $T_{X/S}$ is equivalent to the surjectivity of
\[
  \delta:\Omega^1_{X/S}\longrightarrow\omega_{X/S}.
\]
For $S$ an ordinary even scheme, it is also equivalent to the corresponding condition for the morphism induced by $\delta$,
\[
  \Theta_X^-\longrightarrow\omega_X^-.
\]

(ii) For a surjective
\[
  \delta=D\ell:\Omega^1_{X/S}\longrightarrow\omega_{X/S},
\]
if on a schematically dense open set $\delta$ is a supercurve structure, then it is one everywhere: in the absence of a reference supercurve structure, the Berezinian in (1.1.1) has an invertible denominator, and since $\Ber=1$ on a schematically dense open set, the identity holds everywhere.

\paragraph{Definition 1.4.}
A supercurve over $S$ is a superscheme $X/S$, flat and relatively Cohen-Macaulay, which on a fibrewise dense open set is smooth of relative dimension $(1,1)$, equipped with a derivation
\[
  \delta:\cO_X\longrightarrow\omega_{X/S},
\]
where $\omega_{X/S}$ is the dualizing sheaf, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item on each geometric fibre, $\delta$ induces an isomorphism
\[
  \cO_{X_s}^-\xrightarrow{\sim}\omega_{X_s/s}^-;
\]
\item on the smooth locus, $\delta$ is a smooth supercurve structure over $S$.
\end{enumerate}
By 1.3(ii), condition (ii) may be replaced by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*${}'$)]
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\item on an open set smooth over $S$ and schematically dense, $\delta$ defines a smooth supercurve structure over $S$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{1.5.}
For $S$ an ordinary scheme, let us spell out this definition in terms of the scheme
\[
  C:=(X,\cO_X^+)
\]
over $S$. The hypotheses of flatness, Cohen-Macaulayness, and fibrewise generically smoothness give
\begin{equation}\tag{1.5.1}
  C/S\text{ is a flat curve with geometrically reduced fibres.}
\end{equation}
The coherent sheaf
\[
  \cL:=\cO_X^-
\]
is flat over $S$, relatively Cohen-Macaulay, locally free of rank $1$ on a fibrewise dense open set, and
\[
  \cO_X^-\cdot\cO_X^-=0.
\]
For such a superscheme
\[
  X=(C,\cO_C\oplus\cL),
\]
the dualizing sheaf $\omega_X$ is computed as follows. We have
\[
  p:X\longrightarrow C,
\]
and
\[
  \omega_{X/S}=p^!\omega_{C/S}=\Hom_{\cO_C}(\cO_X,\omega_C).
\]
The relative Cohen-Macaulay hypothesis ensures the vanishing of the higher $\operatorname{Ext}$'s and says that $\omega_{X/S}$ is flat over $S$ and commutes with arbitrary base change. One has
\[
  \omega_{X/S}^+=\omega_C,
  \qquad
  \omega_{X/S}^-=\Hom_{\cO_C}(\cL,\omega_C).
\]
Moreover
\[
  \cO_X^-\omega_{X/S}^+=0,
\]
and
\[
  \cO_X^-\otimes\omega_{X/S}^-\to\omega_{X/S}^+
\]
is the evaluation map
\[
  \cL\otimes\Hom(\cL,\omega_C)\to\omega_C.
\]

In the smooth case, in local coordinates $(z,\theta)$, $\omega_X$ has basis $[dz/d\theta]$, and $\omega_X^-$ is identified with $\omega_C$ by
\[
  \theta[dz/d\theta]\longmapsto dz.
\]
A derivation
\[
  \delta:\cO_X\longrightarrow\omega_X
\]
is defined by
\[
  \delta^+:\cO_C\longrightarrow\omega_C \quad\text{(a derivation)},
\]
and
\[
  \delta^-:\cL\longrightarrow\Hom(\cL,\omega_C)
  \quad\text{($\cO_C$-linear)}.
\]
The derivation conditions give the usual rules when $x$ or $y$ is even. For $x$ and $y$ odd, one should express the symmetry of the bilinear form
\[
  \cL\otimes\cL\longrightarrow\omega_C
\]
defined by $\delta^-$. Since $\cL$ is generically of rank $1$, this is automatic. Axiom 1.4(i) says that $\delta^-$ is an isomorphism. Axiom 1.4(ii) says that, on the smooth locus,
\[
  \delta^+:\cO_C\longrightarrow\omega_C
\]
is
\[
  d:\cO_C\longrightarrow\Omega_C^1=\omega_C.
\]
Indeed, this condition is satisfied by a smooth supercurve in standard local coordinates,
\[
  \theta\,dz=\theta[dz/d\theta]\sim dz,
\]
and all $\delta$ satisfying the condition are locally isomorphic for the étale topology. Conversely, in local coordinates, if
\[
  \delta f=(u(z)\partial_\theta+\theta v(z)\partial_z)f\,[dz/d\theta],
\]
then
\[
  (u(z)\partial_\theta+\theta v(z)\partial_z)^2=u(z)v(z)\partial_z,
\]
and the condition
\[
  [u(z)v(z)\partial_z/(u(z)\partial_\theta+\theta v(z)\partial_z)]=[\partial_z/\partial_\theta]
\]
gives $v=1$, as desired.

Almost: on a schematically dense open set, one has $\delta^+=d$; hence this is true everywhere. Thus, over an ordinary scheme, giving a supercurve is equivalent to giving $C,\cL$ as in (1.5.1), together with:
\begin{equation}\tag{1.5.2}
  \text{a bilinear, automatically symmetric, form }
  \cL\otimes\cL\longrightarrow\omega_C
\end{equation}
which induces an isomorphism
\[
  \cL\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom(\cL,\omega_C).
\]
As explained, one then has
\begin{equation}\tag{1.5.3}
  X=(C,\cO_C\oplus\cL),\qquad
  \omega_X=\omega_C\oplus\cL,
\end{equation}
with the module structure defined by (1.5.2), and
\[
  \delta=(d,\operatorname{id}).
\]

In characteristic $2$, a family of smooth supercurves is not necessarily locally, even formally, trivial. For this reason I shall henceforth assume that $2$ is invertible.

So that everything up to this point could work over $\mathbf Z$, I used the definition of a ``commutative super-ring'' in which $a^2=0$ for odd $a$, and I took care to use $D^2$ rather than $[D,D]$ for odd derivations.

\paragraph{1.6.}
The following description of $\omega_{X/S}$ will be useful. Let
\[
  j:U\hookrightarrow X
\]
be a fibrewise dense smooth open subset. For every section $s$ of $j_*\omega_{U/S}$ and every connected component $F$ of $X-U$, finite over $S$, a residue
\[
  \Res_F(s)\in\cO_S
\]
is defined. The sheaf $\omega_{X/S}$ is the subsheaf of $j_*\omega_{U/S}$ consisting of those sections $s$ such that, locally for the étale topology on $S$ and for every such $F$, and every $f$ in $\cO_X$, one has
\[
  \Res_F(fs)=0.
\]
Over nilpotent $S$, $\Res_F$ is a sum of residues along the branches passing through $F$ and, for each branch $B\hookrightarrow X$ closed in $X$, is invariant under birational morphisms $B_1\to B$. This permits one to compute $\omega_X$ in Serre's manner.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.7.}
Let $X_0$ be a supercurve over a field $k$. An infinitesimal deformation $X$ of $X_0$ that is trivial as a deformation of the underlying superscheme is trivial as a deformation of supercurve.

\paragraph{Proof.}
It is enough to treat first-order deformations, by a Schlessinger-type argument. The case reduces to an even deformation and an odd deformation.

\subparagraph{a. Even deformation.}
We use the point of view of 1.5. Suppose that $X_0$ is defined by $C$, $\cL$, and an isomorphism
\[
  \cL\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom(\cL,\omega_C).
\]
A deformation with the superscheme held fixed can be viewed as a deformation of this isomorphism. Such a deformation is of the form $\varphi_0\circ a$, where $a$ is an infinitesimal automorphism of $\cL$. Such an infinitesimal automorphism $a=1+a_1$ has square root $1+\frac12a_1$, and $\varphi$ is conjugate to $\varphi_0$ by $(1+a_1)^{-1/2}$. It suffices to check this on the smooth locus, where $a$ is multiplication by an element of $\cO_{C_0[t]/(t^2)}$ congruent to $1$ modulo $t$. Conjugating $\varphi$ does not change the isomorphism class of the deformation of supercurve, so the deformation is trivial.

\subparagraph{b. Odd deformations.}
A deformation of $(X_0,\delta_0)$, trivial as a deformation of superscheme, is of the form $(X,\delta)$, with $X$ obtained from $X_0$ by extension of scalars and
\[
  \delta=\delta_0+\varepsilon D,
\]
where $\delta_0:\Omega^1_X\to\omega_X$ is obtained from the original $\delta_0$ by extension of scalars, and where $D$ is an odd derivation
\[
  \cO_{X_0}\longrightarrow\omega_{X_0}.
\]
We must prove that, if $\delta$ satisfies 1.4(ii), then $\delta$ is conjugate to $\delta_0$ by an automorphism of $X$ trivial on $X_0$. Such an automorphism is of the form
\[
  f\longmapsto f+\varepsilon Ef,
\]
where $E$ is an odd derivation of $\cO_{X_0}$ into $\cO_{X_0}$.

From the point of view of 1.5, an odd derivation
\[
  D:\cO_{X_0}\longrightarrow\omega_{X_0}
\]
is given by its components
\[
\begin{cases}
D_0:\cO_C\longrightarrow\cL, & \text{a derivation},\\
D_1:\cL\longrightarrow\omega_C, & D_1(a\ell)=-D_0(a)\ell+aD_1(\ell),
\end{cases}
\]
where the underlined term comes from the product $\cL\otimes\cL\to\omega_C$.

For $\delta_0+\varepsilon D$ satisfying 1.4(ii), $D_1$ is determined by $D_0$. It is enough to verify this on the smooth locus. There, in local coordinates,
\[
  Df=(u(z)\partial_z+v(z)\theta\partial_\theta)f\,[dz/d\theta],
\]
and
\[
  D_0:f\longmapsto u(z)\partial_z f\,[dz/d\theta]:\Theta_{X_0}^+\to\omega_{X_0}^-.
\]
Writing
\[
  D=\theta v(\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z)+u\partial_z,
\]
and applying 1.2, one sees that 1.4(ii) means
\[
  v=\partial_z u.
\]
Thus $D_0$ determines $D$.

An odd derivation $E$ of $\cO_X$ is described, from the point of view of 1.5, by its components
\[
\begin{cases}
E_0:\cO_C\longrightarrow\cL, & \text{a derivation},\\
E_1:\cL\longrightarrow\cO_C, & \text{$\cO_C$-linear}.
\end{cases}
\]
We take for $E$ the derivation with components $(D_0,0)$. To verify that the infinitesimal automorphism $\operatorname{id}+\varepsilon E$ defined by $E$ conjugates $\delta_0$ to $\delta$, it is enough to check this on the smooth locus, and it is enough to verify that the component $0$ of $L_E(\delta_0)$ is $D_0$. In local coordinates one has
\[
  E=u(z)\theta\partial_z,
  \qquad
  \delta_0=(\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z)[dz/d\theta].
\]
Thus
\[
  L_E(\delta_0)= [E,\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z][dz/d\theta]
    +(\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z)L_E([dz/d\theta]),
\]
and
\[
  [E,\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z]
   =[u(z)\theta\partial_z,\partial_\theta+\theta\partial_z]
   =u(z)\partial_z.
\]
The second term contributes to component $1$ of $L_E(\delta_0)$. We have recovered the required $D_0$.
