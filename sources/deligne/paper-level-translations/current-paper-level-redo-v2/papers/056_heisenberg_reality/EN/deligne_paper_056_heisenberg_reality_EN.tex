\section*{1}
Let $H$ be the complex Heisenberg group, an extension of $\C^2$ by $\C^*$. It has coordinates $(x,y,\lambda)$, the group law being
\begin{equation}\tag{1.1}
(x,y,\lambda)(x',y',\lambda')=(x+x',y+y',\lambda\lambda'\exp(\pi i(xy'-x'y))).
\end{equation}
The involution
\begin{equation}\tag{1.2}
\tau:(x,y,\lambda)\longmapsto (-x,-y,\lambda)
\end{equation}
is an automorphism of $H$.

The coordinate system $x,y,\lambda:H\to \C^2\times\C^*$ exhibits the section $\C^2\times\{1\}$ of the projection of $H$ onto $\C^2$. On this section, $\tau(g)=g^{-1}$. Each line $D\subset \C^2$ has, by means of this section, a lift to a one-parameter subgroup of $H$, again denoted by $D$. The commutator $(u,v)=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}$ of two elements $u=(u_1,u_2)$ and $v=(v_1,v_2)$ of $\C^2$ is $\exp(2\pi i\langle u,v\rangle)\in\C^*$, with
\begin{equation}\tag{1.3}
\langle u,v\rangle=u_1v_2-u_2v_1 .
\end{equation}

Let $H_\R$ be the real form defined by $x,y\in\R$ and $|\lambda|=1$. The group $\SL(2,\C)$ acts on $H$, and $\SL(2,\R)$ acts on $H_\R$, which as a set is identified with $\R^2\times U^1$.

It is known, by the Stone-von Neumann theorem~\cite{vonNeumann}, that $H_\R$ has a unique irreducible unitary representation $V$ for which $\lambda\in U^1$ acts by multiplication by $\lambda$. In the Schr\"odinger model of $V$, one has $V=L^2(\R)$, and the action of $H_\R$ is
\begin{equation}\tag{1.4}
\begin{aligned}
(x,0,1):&\quad f(z)\longmapsto f(z+x),\\
(0,y,1):&\quad f(z)\longmapsto e^{2\pi i yz}f(z).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
From the uniqueness of $V$ one deduces a projective unitary action of $\SL(2,\R)$ on $V$, compatible with the action of $\SL(2,\R)$ on $H_\R$ (A. Weil~\cite{Weil}, D. Shale~\cite{Shale}).

For the action of $H_\R$ on $V=L^2(\R)$, the $C^\infty$ vectors, i.e. those $v\in V$ for which $h\mapsto hv$ is $C^\infty$ in $h\in H_\R$, form the Schwartz space $\mathscr S$ of $C^\infty$ rapidly decreasing functions on $\R$. The holomorphic vectors, i.e. those $v\in V$ for which $h\mapsto hv$ extends to a holomorphic map from $H$ to $V$, are the $L^2$ functions on $\R$ that extend to holomorphic functions on $\C$ such that, in every horizontal strip $|\operatorname{Im}z|<A_1$, one has, for all $A_2\in\R$,
\[
|f(z)|\le O\bigl(\exp(-A_2|z|)\bigr).
\]
The complex group $H$ acts on the space $V^{\hol}$ of holomorphic vectors by the formulae (1.4).

For the holomorphic action of $H$ on $V^{\hol}$, one does not have the same uniqueness as for the unitary action of $H_\R$ on $V$. If one did, the projective action of $\SL(2,\R)$ on $V$ would extend to a projective action of $\SL(2,\C)$ on $V^{\hol}$; since $\SL(2,\C)$ is simply connected, this action would lift to an honest action of $\SL(2,\C)$, and the projective action of $\SL(2,\R)$ would lift to an honest action. This is not the case: only the double cover of $\SL(2,\R)$ acts. Our purpose is to make explicit what happens.

\section*{2}
Let $S=\mathbb P^1(\C)$ be the space of lines of $\C^2$. For $D\in S$, lifted in $H$ as in no.~1, let $W_D$ be the space of holomorphic functions $\varphi$ on $H$ satisfying
\[
\varphi(\lambda d h)=\lambda\varphi(h)
\]
for $\lambda\in\C^*$ and $d\in D$, endowed with the action of $H$ given by $g:\varphi(h)\mapsto\varphi(hg)$. For $E\ne D$, restriction to $E$ is a bijection
\begin{equation}\tag{2.1}
r_E:W_D\xrightarrow{\sim}\cO(E),
\end{equation}
where $\cO(E)$ denotes the space of holomorphic functions on $E$.

If $R$ is a holomorphic representation of $H$, on which each $\lambda\in\C^*$ acts by $\lambda$, and if $\omega$ is a $D$-invariant linear form on $R$, then
\begin{equation}\tag{2.2}
w\longmapsto \omega(hw)
\end{equation}
is a morphism from $R$ to $W_D$.

\section*{3. Example}
Let $V$, as in nos.~1 and 2, be the irreducible unitary representation of $H_\R$, let $\mathscr S\subset V$ be the space of its $C^\infty$ vectors, and let $\mathscr S'\supset V$ be the dual of the space of $C^\infty$ vectors of the dual representation; in the Schr\"odinger model, this is the space of tempered distributions.

Let $D_t\in S$ be the line generated by $(-t,1)$. If $\operatorname{Im}t>0$, the linear form, written in the Schr\"odinger model,
\[
\omega_t:f\longmapsto {1\over\sqrt{t/i}}\int_\R f(x)\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\dd x
\]
is defined on $V$, and even on $\mathscr S'$. The form $\omega_t$ is a holomorphic vector of the dual of $V$, fixed by $D_t$. In terms of the infinitesimal generator $-t\partial_x+2\pi ix$ of $D_t$, the invariance is written
\[
\int_\R [(-t\partial_x+2\pi ix)f(x)]\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\dd x
=\int_\R f(x)(t\partial_x+2\pi ix)\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\dd x=0.
\]

By (2.2), $\omega_t$ defines
\[
\Omega_t:V^{\hol}\longrightarrow W_{D_t},
\]
which extends to $V$ and even to $\mathscr S'$. Let $E$ be the $x$-axis. The map $r_E\Omega_t:V\to\cO(E)$ sends $f\in V$ to
\[
f(x,t)=r_E\Omega_t(f)\qquad (\operatorname{Im}t>0),
\]
a solution of the heat equation
\[
\left(\partial_t-{1\over4\pi i}\partial_x^2\right)f(x,t)=0.
\]
For real $t$, the form $\omega_t$ remains defined on $\mathscr S\subset V$. For $t=0$ it is $f\mapsto f(0)$, and for $t=\infty$ it is $f\mapsto \int f(x)\dd x$. The homomorphism $\Omega_t$ remains defined on $V^{\hol}$. For $t=0$, $r_E\Omega_0$ is, in the Schr\"odinger model, the identity.

Assume $\operatorname{Im}t>0$. Let $\overline D_t$ be the complex conjugate of $D_t$. In the affine line $S-\{\overline D_t\}$, the ``half-plane'' $\{D_u\mid \operatorname{Im}u>0\}$ is a disk with center $D_t$. The map $r_{\overline D_t}\circ\Omega_t:V\to W_{D_t}\to \cO(\overline D_t)$ gives the holomorphic model of $V$: the action of $H$ is given by
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\in D_t:&\quad f(x)\longmapsto f(x+d),\\
e\in \overline D_t:&\quad f(x)\longmapsto \exp(-2\pi i\langle e,x\rangle)f(x),
\end{aligned}
\]
and the Hilbert structure for which $(f,f)$ is the integral of
\[
f(d)\overline{f(d)}\exp(2\pi i\langle d,\overline d\rangle)
\]
is invariant under $H_\R$; moreover $r_{\overline D_t}\circ\Omega_t$ identifies $V$ with the space of $f$ of finite norm. For $d=(-tz,z)$, the factor $\exp(2\pi i\langle d,\overline d\rangle)$, the commutator of $d$ and $\overline d$, is
\[
\exp(2\pi i\langle d,\overline d\rangle)=\exp(2\pi i(t-\overline t)|z|^2).
\]

\section*{4}
Fix $E$. The isomorphisms $r_E$ of (2.1) make it possible to regard the $W_D$ for $D\ne E$ as a family of representations of $H$ operating in the fixed space $\cO(E)$, with the action of $H$ depending holomorphically on $D$. Let us make explicit the dependence on $E$ of $r_E$. Choose coordinates as in no.~1, with $E$ the $x$-axis. Let $E'$ be generated by $(1,u)$ and $D$ by $(-t,1)$. We use $x$ as coordinate on both $E$ and $E'$. Then
\[
(x',ux',1)=(-tux',ux',1)(x,0,1)(0,0,\lambda)
\]
for $(1+tu)x'=x$ and $\lambda=\exp(\pi iuxx')$. Hence $r_{E'}r_E^{-1}$ sends $f_E\in\cO(E)$ to $f_{E'}\in\cO(E')$, with $f_{E'}(x')=\lambda f_E(x)$:
\begin{equation}\tag{4.1}
f_{E'}\!\left({x\over 1+tu}\right)=\exp\!\left({\pi iu\over 1+tu}x^2\right)f_E(x).
\end{equation}
This formula is holomorphic in $t$: the $W_D$ form a holomorphic vector bundle $\cW$ of infinite dimension over $S$, on which $H$ acts.

To understand (4.1), one may observe that the point of $E$ with coordinate $x$ has the same image in $\C^2/D$ as the point of $E'$ with coordinate $x/(1+tu)$.

From (4.1) one deduces that the following growth condition on $v\in W_D$ is independent of the choices of $E\ne D$ and of the coordinate $z$ on $E$:
\begin{equation}\tag{4.2}
\text{for } f=r_E(v),\quad \exists A\quad |f(z)|\le O\bigl(\exp(A|z|^2)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Let $W_D^0\subset W_D$ be defined by this condition. The $W_D^0$ form a holomorphic sub-bundle $\cW^0$ of the bundle $\cW$ in holomorphic representations of $H$.

\section*{5. Proposition}
Let $T>0$ and consider the following conditions on an entire function $f$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)$_T$]
\item There exists a holomorphic function $g(z,t)$, for $z\in\C$ and $|t|<T$, satisfying the heat equation $(\partial_t-\partial_z^2)g=0$, with initial condition $g(z,0)=f(z)$.
\item $|f(z)|\le O(\exp(|z|^2/4T))$.
\item $\displaystyle \int f(z)\overline{f(z)}\exp(-|z|^2/2T)|\dd z\wedge \dd\overline z|<\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
If $T<T_1$, each of $(i)_{T_1}$, $(ii)_{T_1}$, $(iii)_{T_1}$ implies each of $(i)_T$, $(ii)_T$, $(iii)_T$.

\emph{Proof.} $(i)_{T_1}\Rightarrow(ii)_T$. The heat equation remains satisfied by the derivatives of $g$, and therefore
\[
\partial_z^{2n}g=\partial_t^n g,
\qquad
\partial_z^{2n+1}g=\partial_t^n(\partial_z g).
\]
Since $g(0,t)$ is holomorphic for $|t|<T_1$, if $T<T_2<T_1$, then at $(0,0)$
\[
|\partial_t^n g/n!|\le O(1/T_2^n),
\qquad
|\partial_z^{2n}g|\le O(n!/T_2^n),
\]
and there is an analogous estimate for $\partial_z^{2n+1}g$. The Taylor series of $f$ then gives
\[
|f(z)|\le O\left(\sum {n!\over(2n)!}{1\over T_2^n}|z|^{2n}\right).
\]
One has
\[
{1\over2n+1}2^{2n}\le{(2n)!\over(n!)^2}\le 2^{2n},
\]
hence
\[
|f(z)|\le O\left(\sum 2^{-2n}{1\over T^n}{|z|^{2n}\over n!}\right),
\]
and this sum is $\exp(|z|^2/4T)$.

$(ii)_{T_1}\Rightarrow(i)_T$. It suffices to take $g$ given by the kernel of the heat equation:
\[
g(z,t)=\int_{\sqrt t\,\R} f(z+x){1\over2\sqrt{\pi t}}\exp(-x^2/4t)\dd x.
\]
Changing $\sqrt t$ to $-\sqrt t$ changes the orientation of the cycle $\sqrt t\,\R$ and the sign of the integrand, so that the integral does not change. Condition (ii) ensures convergence for $|t|<T$.

The relation between (ii) and (iii) is clear: $(ii)_{T_1}\Rightarrow(iii)_T$, and $(iii)_{T_1}\Rightarrow(ii)_T$ is verified by writing $f(z)$ as the mean of its values on a disk with center $z$ and radius $1/|z|$, and estimating that mean by Cauchy--Schwarz.

\section*{6}
In homage to the Stone-von Neumann theorem, the bundle $\cW$ over $S$ is, projectively, equipped with a holomorphic connection for which the action of $H$ is horizontal.

Let us specify ``projectively.'' On the projective line $S$ there is the ample invertible sheaf $\cO(1)$. Let $\cO(-1)$ be its dual. A square root $\cO(-\half)$ of $\cO(-1)$ is an invertible sheaf $L$ endowed with an isomorphism $L^{\otimes2}\simeq\cO(-1)$. Locally such a square root exists and, locally, two square roots are isomorphic; but the isomorphism is not unique, with ambiguity $\pm1$. This ambiguity obstructs the global existence of $\cO(-\half)$. It is not on $\cW$, but on
\[
\cW(-\half):=\cW\otimes\cO(-\half)
\]
that there is a connection:
\begin{equation}\tag{6.1}
\nabla:\cW(-\half)\longrightarrow \cW(-\half)\otimes\Omega^1.
\end{equation}

Note that if $\cO(-\half)'$ and $\cO(-\half)''$ are two square roots of $\cO(-1)$, and if $\nabla$ is a connection on $\cW\otimes\cO(-\half)'$, then its transport to $\cW\otimes\cO(-\half)''$ by an isomorphism $a:\cO(-\half)'\xrightarrow{\sim}\cO(-\half)''$ of square roots of $\cO(-1)$ does not depend on the choice of $a$, which can be changed locally only by a constant $\pm1$. Thus the notion of a ``connection on $\cW(-\half)$'' is well defined independently of the global existence, or the choice, of $\cO(-\half)$.

Let $D\in S$ and let $t$ be a tangent vector at $D$. For a local section $s$ of $\cW(-\half)$, the problem is to define $\nabla_t s$ in the fiber of $\cW(-\half)$ at $D$. We proceed as follows: after making an auxiliary choice $u$, one defines $\nabla^u_t s$ for $s$ a local section of $\cW$ or of $\cO(-\half)$. This derivation $\nabla^u_t$ depends on $u$, with dependence in $u$ of the form
\[
\nabla^{u'}_t(s)=\nabla^u_t(s)+a s(D),
\]
where $a$ takes opposite values for $\cW$ and for $\cO(-\half)$. Hence, for $s=s_1s_2$, with $s_1$ a local section of $\cW$ and $s_2$ a local section of $\cO(-\half)$,
\[
\nabla_t(s):=\nabla^u_t(s_1)s_2(D)+s_1(D)\nabla^u_t(s_2)
\]
is independent of the choice of $u$.

Let $D\in S$ be a line of $\C^2$. A tangent vector $t$ at $D\in S$ is identified with a linear map $t:D\to\C^2/D$, i.e. with an element of $(\C^2/D)^{\otimes2}$: to $u'\otimes v'\in(\C^2/D)^{\otimes2}$ one associates $t(d)=\langle u,d\rangle v'$, where $u$ is any representative of $u'$. The auxiliary datum will be a lift $\tilde t:D\to\C^2$ of $t$, or equivalently a lift of $t$ to $\C^2/D\otimes\C^2$. We write it $\tilde t=u'\otimes u$, with $u\in\C^2$ mapping to $u'$ in $\C^2/D$.

The tangent space at the origin of $H$ is $\mathfrak h=\C^2\times\C$. For $z$ in this tangent space, let $\delta_z$ be the corresponding right-invariant vector field. One has
\[
[\delta_x,\delta_y]=-\delta_{[x,y]},
\]
where on the right appears the bracket in the Lie algebra of $H$. For $x,y\in\C^2$, computing $[x,y]$ as an infinitesimal commutator, one deduces
\begin{equation}\tag{6.2}
[\delta_x,\delta_y]=-2\pi i\langle x,y\rangle\delta_{(0,0,1)}.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal U_1$ be the quotient of the enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra of right-invariant vector fields by the relation $\delta_{(0,0,1)}=1$. In $\mathcal U_1$, (6.2) implies that, for $d\in D$,
\begin{equation}\tag{6.3}
\left[-{\delta_u^2\over4\pi i},\delta_d\right]
={1\over4\pi i}\,2\cdot 2\pi i\langle u,d\rangle\delta_u
=\langle u,d\rangle\delta_u=\delta_{t(d)}.
\end{equation}

Let us compute to first order around $D$. For $\varepsilon^2=0$ and $\varphi(g)$ in $W_D$, so $\delta_d\varphi=0$, one has
\[
\delta_{d+\varepsilon t(d)}\left[\varphi(g)+\varepsilon{-\delta_u^2\over4\pi i}\varphi(g)\right]
=\varepsilon\left[\delta_{t(d)}\varphi(g)+\delta_d{-\delta_u^2\over4\pi i}\varphi(g)\right]=0,
\]
because $\delta_d(-\delta_u^2/4\pi i)\varphi=-[-\delta_u^2/4\pi i,\delta_d]\varphi$, by (6.3). To first order around $D$, the function of $g$ and $D'$ defined by
\[
\varphi(g,D+\varepsilon t)=\varphi(g)+\varepsilon(-\delta_u^2/4\pi i)\varphi(g)
\]
is therefore a section of the bundle $\cW$. If $\varphi(g,D')$ is a local section of $\cW$, this permits one to define the derivative $\nabla^u_t\varphi$ in $\cW_D$ by
\begin{equation}\tag{6.5}
\nabla^u_t\varphi=\delta_t\varphi(g,D')+(\delta_u^2/4\pi i)\varphi(g,D).
\end{equation}

Let us compute the dependence of (6.5) on $u$. If $u$ is replaced by $u+x$ with $x\in D$, then in $\mathcal U_1$
\begin{equation}\tag{6.6}
\delta_{u+x}^2=(\delta_u+\delta_x)^2=\delta_u^2+2\delta_u\delta_x+[\delta_x,\delta_u]
=\delta_u^2+2\delta_u\delta_x+2\pi i\langle u,x\rangle.
\end{equation}
For $\varphi\in W_D$, $\delta_x\varphi=0$, and (6.6) becomes
\begin{equation}\tag{6.7}
\delta_{u+x}^2\varphi=\delta_u^2\varphi+2\pi i\langle u,x\rangle\varphi,
\end{equation}
whence
\begin{equation}\tag{6.8}
{1\over4\pi i}\delta_{u+x}^2\varphi={1\over4\pi i}\delta_u^2\varphi+{1\over2}\langle u,x\rangle\varphi.
\end{equation}

The choice of $u$ also makes it possible to define the derivative $\nabla^u_t$ with respect to $t$ of a local section of $\cO(1)$. A section of $\cO(1)$ on $U\subset S$ is a homogeneous function of degree $1$ on the inverse image of $U$ in $\C^2$, and one differentiates with respect to $\langle u,d\rangle u$ to obtain a homogeneous function of degree $1$ on $D$. When $u$ is changed into $u+x$,
\[
\nabla^{u+x}_t(s)=\langle u+x,d\rangle\delta_{u+x}s
=\nabla^u_t(s)+\langle u,x\rangle s.
\]
This derivation induces one on $\cO(-\half)$, with
\[
\nabla^u_t(s^{-1/2})=-{1\over2}\nabla^u_t(s)\,s^{-3/2},
\]
and, for the dependence on $u$, $s$ now being a local section of $\cO(-\half)$,
\begin{equation}\tag{6.9}
\nabla^{u+x}_t(s)=\nabla^u_t(s)-{1\over2}\langle u,x\rangle s.
\end{equation}
The dependences on $u$ in (6.8) and (6.9) cancel to provide a derivation $\nabla_t$ for $\cW(-\half)$. Since the connection $\nabla$ is defined in terms of right-invariant vector fields on $H$, it commutes with the action of $H$.

Let us spell out the connection of no.~6 in coordinates. Choose coordinates as in no.~1, and let $E$ be the $x$-axis. On $S$, $\cO(1)$ has a section $s$ with a simple zero at $E$. It is unique up to a factor and trivializes $\cO(1)$ on $S-E$. Thus there is a square root $\cO(-\half)$ on $S-E$, trivialized by a square root $s^{-1/2}$ of $s^{-1}$. By such a trivialization, the desired connection is transported to a connection on the bundle $\cW$. We calculate it.

Let $D_t$ be the line generated by $(-t,1)$. By $r_E$ of (2.1), a local section of $\cW$ is identified with a function $f(z,t)$ holomorphic in $z$ and $t$. We compute its derivative with respect to $t$. In no.~6, one may take
\[
\tilde t:D_t\longrightarrow\C^2:(-tz,z)\longmapsto(-1,0)z;
\quad\text{that is } d\longmapsto-\langle(1,0),d\rangle(1,0).
\]
The corresponding derivation of $\cO(1)$ annihilates $s$, so that simply
\begin{equation}\tag{7.1}
\nabla_t f=(\partial_t-\partial_z^2/4\pi i)f,
\end{equation}
and the horizontal local sections are the solutions of the heat equation
\begin{equation}\tag{7.2}
(\partial_t-\partial_z^2/4\pi i)f=0.
\end{equation}

From no.~5 one deduces that through $s\in W_D^0(-\half)$ there passes a horizontal local section of $\cW(-\half)$. It is automatically a section of $\cW^0(-\half)$. It exists in a neighborhood of $D$ that is larger the slower the growth of $s$. Note that in infinite dimension a connection does not necessarily define a local trivialization, even if through each point there passes a local horizontal section.

The connection $\nabla$ on $\cW^0(-\half)$ defines a connection on the corresponding bundle of projective spaces
\[
\mathbb P(\cW^0)=\mathbb P(\cW^0(-\half)).
\]
The horizontal local sections are the images of the nonzero horizontal sections of $\cW^0(-\half)$. Although the sphere $S$ is simply connected, one has the following.

\section*{8. Surprise}
The projective-space bundle $\mathbb P(\cW^0)$ on $S$ has no global horizontal section.

The argument of no.~1 would have made it possible to predict this: apply it to the projective representation of $\SL(2,\C)$ given by the action of $\SL(2,\C)$ on the projective space of global horizontal sections of $\mathbb P(\cW^0)$.

\emph{First proof.} Divide $S$ into two hemispheres $S_1$ and $S_2$, and choose $\cO(-\half)_i$ on $S_i$. On $S_1\cap S_2$ one has locally an isomorphism $\alpha:\cO(-\half)_1\xrightarrow{\sim}\cO(-\half)_2$, defined up to sign, but $\alpha$ can be defined globally only on the double cover of the circle $S_1\cap S_2$: the monodromy is $-1$. If there existed global horizontal projective sections of $\mathbb P(\cW^0)$, there would exist horizontal sections $f_1,f_2$ of $\cW^0(-\half)$ on $S_1$ and $S_2$ which, on $S_1\cap S_2$, would coincide up to sign. On $S_1\cap S_2$ this would make it possible to normalize $\alpha:\cO(-\half)_1\to\cO(-\half)_2$ by $\alpha(f_1)=f_2$, contradicting the nonexistence of $\alpha$ on $S_1\cap S_2$.

\emph{Second proof.} Choose coordinates as in no.~1. Let $E$ be the $x$-axis and $D$ the $y$-axis. As in no.~2, identify $W_D$ with the space of entire functions $f(z)$. Let $f\ne0$ in $W_D$. By the proposition, for there to exist on $S-E$ a horizontal section of $\mathbb P(\cW^0)$ passing through $f$, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ satisfy the growth condition
\begin{equation}\tag{8.1}
\forall\varepsilon>0\quad |f(z)|\le O(\exp(\varepsilon |z|^2)).
\end{equation}
If $E'$ is another line, then by (4.1) the existence of a horizontal section on $S-E'$ passing through $f$ is expressed by a growth condition
\begin{equation}\tag{8.2}
\forall\varepsilon>0\quad |f(z)\exp(az^2)|\le O(\exp(\varepsilon |z|^2))
\end{equation}
with suitable $a\ne0$.

Put $g(z)=f(z)\exp(az^2/2)$. By (8.1) and (8.2), $|g(z)|$ is bounded by a multiple both of $|\exp(\varepsilon |z|^2+az^2/2)|$ and of $|\exp(\varepsilon |z|^2-az^2/2)|$; hence in all directions except those for which $az^2$ is purely imaginary, $g$ decreases exponentially quadratically. In all directions, it has uniformly at most exponential-quadratic growth. By Phragm\'en--Lindel\"of, $g$ decreases exponentially quadratically in all directions, hence is zero, and therefore $f=0$.

\section*{9}
In the coordinates of no.~1, let $D_t$ be the line generated by $(-t,1)$. Let $d=(-tz,z)$. The commutator of $d$ and $\overline d$ is
\[
\langle d,\overline d\rangle=\exp(-2\pi i(t-\overline t)|z|^2)\in\C^*;
\]
the commutator is real positive, and
\[
\langle d,\overline d\rangle>1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Im}(t)>0.
\]
The lines $D$ such that, for $d$ generating $D$, one has $\langle d,\overline d\rangle>1$, therefore form a disk in $S$, called the disk attached to the real structure $H_\R$.

The real structures $L\subset\C^2$ for which $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ is real on $L$ form a homogeneous space under $\SL(2,\C)$, with $\SL(2,\R)$ as the stabilizer of $\R^2$. Each defines a real structure $H_L=L\cdot U^1$ on $H$, hence a complex conjugation $\sigma_L$ with fixed points $LU^1$.

The disks in $S$ likewise form a homogeneous space under $\SL(2,\C)$, the stabilizer of the one associated to $H_\R$ being $\SL(2,\R)$. The correspondence
\[
L\longmapsto X(L):=\{D\mid \langle d,\sigma_L(d)\rangle>1\text{ for }d\text{ a generator of }D\}
\]
is therefore a bijection from the space of the real structures under consideration to the space of disks in $S$.

Passing from a disk to the complementary disk corresponds, on real structures, to $L\mapsto iL$.

\section*{10}
The calculations of no.~3 admit the following interpretation. Let $X$ be an open disk of $S$, corresponding to a real structure $L$, whence real forms $H_L$ of $H$ and $\SL(L)$ of $\SL(2,\C)$. Then $\Gamma(X,\cW^0(-\half)^{\nabla=0})$ has an $H_L$-invariant pre-Hilbert structure; its completion $\Gamma(X,\cW^0(-\half)^{\nabla=0})^\wedge$ is the irreducible unitary representation of $H_L$. One has
\[
\Gamma(X,\cW^0(-\half)^{\nabla=0})
\subset
\Gamma(X,\cW^0(-\half)^{\nabla=0})^\wedge
\subset
\Gamma(X,\cW(-\half)^{\nabla=0}).
\]
The double cover $\SL(L)^\sim$ of $\SL(L)$ acts on $\cO(-\half)|_X$ compatibly with its action on $\cO(-1)$; hence $\SL(L)^\sim$ acts on $\Gamma(X,\cW^0(-\half)^{\nabla=0})$. This is the action that normalizes the action of $H_L$ and induces the natural action of $\SL(L)$ on $H_L$.

The subrepresentation $\Gamma(X,\cW^0(-\half)^{\nabla=0})$ may be interpreted as the space of real analytic vectors for the action of $\SL(L)^\sim$.

This description shows that the projective representation of $\SL(L)$ extends to the monoid of $g\in\SL(2,\C)$ such that $gX\supset X$.

\section*{11}
Everything preceding generalizes from $\SL(2,\C)$ to $\Sp(2n,\C)$: replace $\C^2$ by $\C^{2n}$, endowed with a nondegenerate alternating form $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ real on $\R^{2n}$, replace $H$ by $\C^{2n}\times\C^*$ with group law
\[
(x,\lambda)(y,\mu)=(x+y,\lambda\mu\exp(\pi i\langle x,y\rangle)),
\]
and replace $S$ by the space of Lagrangian, i.e. maximal isotropic, subspaces $D$ of $\C^{2n}$. The disk attached to a real structure becomes the Siegel space of those $D$ for which $|\langle d,\overline d\rangle|>1$ for $d\ne0$ in $D$. The invertible sheaf $\cO(-\half)$ must be replaced by $\det(D)^{1/2}$.

\section*{12. Remark}
Beside representations of $H$ in which $\C^*$ acts by multiplication by $\lambda$, consider likewise representations in which the action is by $\lambda^{-1}$. One defines, as in no.~2, a holomorphic bundle $\cW_-^0$ of such representations over $S$, and on $\cW_-^0(-\half)$ one again has a connection, given by a heat equation.

On a connected simply connected open subset $U$ of $S$, consider $W=\cW^0(-\half)$, its connection, and the action of $H$. In $\Gamma(U,W)^{\nabla=0}$, for each $x\in U$ there is a linear form fixed by the corresponding line, defined up to a factor: $f\mapsto f(e)$ for $f\in \cW_x^0$. For each $x\notin U$, there exists a vector $v(x)$ fixed by $D_x$; in the Schr\"odinger model, and for $x$ corresponding to $E=$ the $x$-axis, it is the constant $1$. It is unique up to a factor.

Fix the real structure $H_\R$, whence a real structure $S_\R\subset S$ (a circle), and a decomposition of $S-S_\R$ into two disks $U_1,U_2$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The complex conjugate of $\Gamma(U_1,W)^{\nabla=0}$ is $\Gamma(U_2,W_-)^{\nabla=0}$.
\item The Hermitian form on $\Gamma(U_1,W)^{\nabla=0}$, invariant under $H_\R$, is reinterpreted as a bilinear pairing between $\Gamma(U_1,W)^{\nabla=0}$ and $\Gamma(U_2,W_-)^{\nabla=0}$, invariant under $H$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Question.} Let $C$ be a Jordan curve in $S$, separating $S-C$ into two open sets $U_1$ and $U_2$. Does there exist a unique separating $H$-invariant bilinear form pairing $\Gamma(U_1,W)^{\nabla=0}$ and $\Gamma(U_2,W_-)^{\nabla=0}$, induced by a perfect duality between $\Gamma(U_1,W)^{\nabla=0}$ and $\Gamma(U_2,W_-)^{\nabla=0}$?
