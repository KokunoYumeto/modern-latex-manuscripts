\section*{3. First-Order Deformations}

\paragraph{3.1.}
For the moment, let us forget $\delta$ and compute the versal deformation, to first order, of the singularity 2.2, I or II. The computation is painful, but standard. For an arbitrary superscheme with an isolated singularity, one must:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] write
\[
  X=\Spec(A),\qquad A=k[X,\Theta]/\langle f_i\rangle ;
\]
\item[(b)] find a system of syzygies $S_k$,
\[
  \sum_i a_i(X,\Theta)f_i=0,
\]
generating all of them modulo the trivial syzygies
\[
  f_i f_j-f_j f_i=0\qquad\text{(even, or even/odd, case)},
\]
\[
  f_i f_j+f_j f_i=0\qquad\text{($f_i$ and $f_j$ odd)};
\]
\item[(c)] compute the cohomology of the complex of free $A$-modules
\[
  \bigoplus_i\left(A\frac{\partial}{\partial X_i}\oplus
  A\frac{\partial}{\partial\Theta_i}\right)
  \xrightarrow{\ df/\partial X\ }
  \bigoplus_i A\frac{\partial}{\partial f_i}
  \xrightarrow{\ \text{coefficients}\ }
  \bigoplus_k A\frac{\partial}{\partial S_k}.
\]
The $H^1$ parametrizes the deformations.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Case I.}
The coordinates are $(z_1,z_2,\theta)$, the equation is
\[
  z_1z_2=0,
\]
and there is no syzygy. The universal first-order deformation is
\[
  z_1z_2=t+\tau\theta
  \qquad\bigl(\text{over }k[t,\tau]/(t^2,t\tau)\bigr).
\]
Case II is another matter.

\paragraph{Case II.}
We take the coordinates
\[
  z_1,\ z_2,\ \theta_1,\ \theta_2
\]
and the equations
\[
  z_1z_2=z_1\theta_2=\theta_1z_2=\theta_1\theta_2=0.
\]
The syzygies are
\[
\begin{cases}
\theta_1(z_1z_2)=z_1(\theta_1z_2),\\
\theta_2(z_1z_2)=z_2(z_1\theta_2),\\
z_1(\theta_1\theta_2)=\theta_1(z_1\theta_2),\\
z_2(\theta_1\theta_2)=-\theta_2(\theta_1z_2).
\end{cases}
\]
One has to consider a system of four equations, one per syzygy, in four unknowns, one per equation, in the ring $A$, expressing that these unknowns satisfy the syzygies. The task is to compute the space of solutions modulo the trivial solutions, obtained by differentiating the equations. I denote the unknowns by
\[
  ++,\quad +-,\quad -+,\quad --.
\]

The equations corresponding to the syzygies have coefficients
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
& ++ & +- & -+ & --\\ \hline
1& \theta_1&0&-z_1&0\\
2& \theta_2&-z_2&0&0\\
3&0&-\theta_1&0&z_1\\
4&0&0&\theta_2&z_2
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
& \partial_{z_1}&\partial_{z_2}&\partial_{\theta_1}&\partial_{\theta_2}\\ \hline
++& z_2&z_1&0&0\\
+-& \theta_2&0&0&z_1\\
-+&0&\theta_1&z_2&0\\
--&0&0&\theta_2&-\theta_1
\end{array}
\]
\[
\text{each row, one syzygy},\qquad
\text{each column, one trivial solution}.
\]

Let $(a,b,c,d)$ be a solution. Modifying it by the last two trivial solutions, one may suppose that $d$ is even. The equations
\[
  z_1d=\theta_1 b,
  \qquad
  z_2d=-\theta_2 c
\]
then give $d=0$. The linear combinations with $d=0$ of the trivial solutions are the linear combinations of the first, the second, the third multiplied by $\theta_2$, and the fourth multiplied by $\theta_1$. This reduces us to the system
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
& ++&+-&-+&--=0\\ \hline
1&\theta_1&0&-z_1&0\\
2&\theta_2&-z_2&0&0\\
3&0&-\theta_1&0&0\\
4&0&0&\theta_2&0
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
&1&2&3\theta_2&4\theta_1\\ \hline
++&z_2&z_1&0&0\\
+-&\theta_2&0&0&\theta_1z_1\\
-+&0&\theta_1&\theta_2z_2&0\\
--&0&0&0&0
\end{array}
\]

Let $(a,b,c)$ be a solution, with $d=0$. The equation
\[
  \theta_1 a=z_1c
\]
imposes that, modulo $(\theta_2,z_2)$, the pair $(a,c)$ is a multiple of the second trivial solution. In particular
\[
  a\in (z_1,\theta_1,z_2,\theta_2)=(z_1,\theta_1)\oplus(z_2,\theta_2).
\]
Similarly for $b$ and $c$:
\[
  a=a_1+a_2,
  \qquad b=b_1+b_2,
  \qquad c=c_1+c_2.
\]
Modulo the first two trivial solutions, one obtains
\[
  a=0,\qquad b_2=0,\qquad c_1=0.
\]
By the last equations,
\[
  a_1\in(\theta_1),\quad a_2\in(\theta_2),\quad b_2=c_1=0,
\]
\[
  a_1\in(\theta_1),\quad a_2\in(\theta_2),\quad b_1\in(\theta_1),\quad c_1=0,
  \qquad b_2=0,\quad c_2\in(\theta_2).
\]
Finally, modulo the $\theta$-multiples of the first trivial solutions, every solution is reduced to one of the following:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
& a&b&c&d\\ \hline
1&\theta_1&0&0&0\\
2&\theta_2&0&0&0\\
3&0&\theta_1&0&0\\
4&0&0&\theta_2&0
\end{array}
\]
Thus the versal first-order deformation is given by the parameters
\[
  t_1,t_2,\tau_1,\tau_2,
\]
the coordinates
\[
  z_1,z_2,\theta_1,\theta_2,
\]
and the equations
\[
\begin{cases}
 z_1z_2+\tau_1\theta_1+\tau_2\theta_2=0,\\
 z_1\theta_2+t_1\theta_1=0,\\
 z_2\theta_1+t_2\theta_2=0,\\
 \theta_1\theta_2=0.
\end{cases}
\]
