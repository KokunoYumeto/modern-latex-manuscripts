Dear Luc,

Pencils give the conjecture from my letter to Katz in the general case.

Let:
\[
\begin{array}{rll}
X & \text{be smooth and projective} & \F\ \text{a smooth sheaf of rank }1\text{ on }U=X-D,\\[0.3em]
D & \text{a smooth divisor} & \text{ramified along }D,\ \text{locally defined by}\\[0.3em]
D'\subset D & \text{a smooth divisor in }D & \psi\colon \Fp\longrightarrow \Qell^{\ast}\ \text{and a covering.}
\end{array}
\]
of Artin--Schreier type, ramifying as follows: for \(x=0\) a local equation for \(D\), and \(x=y=0\) for \(D'\),
\[
T^p-T=\frac{y}{x^d},\qquad \text{with }(d,p)=1.
\]
Let \(\F'\) be the virtual sheaf
\[
\F'=j_!\F-j_!\Qell+d_!\bigl(\Qell\bigr)_{D-D'}.
\]
Then
\[
\chi(\F')=0.
\]

Proof by induction. Take a general Lefschetz pencil.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(\chi'\) does not change after passing to the blow-up associated with the pencil \([\)using the vanishing of \(\chi'\) for the intersection of \(X\) with the axis: this is known by induction\(]\).

\item \(\chi\) of the general hyperplane section is \(0\) \([\)by induction\(]\).

\item An accident occurs only when the section becomes tangent to \(X\), \(D\), or \(D'\). Elsewhere one has in fact a product situation \([\)locally upstairs, \((X,\F)\) is the product of the section by the parameter of the section\(]\).

\item For a general pencil of high degree, each of these accidents contributes:
\[
\chi=\alpha\bigl(\#\,\tg\text{ to }X\bigr)
+\beta\bigl(\#\,\tg\text{ to }D\bigr)
+\gamma\bigl(\#\,\tg\text{ to }D'\bigr).
\]
Now vary the degree of the pencil.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Lemma.} Let \(X\subset\PP\) be smooth and projective. The class of \(X\) in the \(n\)-th Segre embedding \((\)by \(\OO(n)\)\()\) is a polynomial in \(n\), of degree equal to \(\dim(X)\).

An easy calculation shows that the coefficient of \(n^{\dim X}\) is
\[
\deg_X \OO(1)=\bigl[c_1\OO(1)\bigr]^{\dim X}.
\]
(relativized to pencils, this extends from \(X\) to a hyperplane section\ldots)

It follows that, varying the degree,
\[
\chi=\alpha\bigl(\mathrm{pol}\ d^\circ\,\dim X\bigr)
+\beta\bigl(\mathrm{pol}\ d^\circ(\dim X-1)\bigr)
+\gamma\bigl(\mathrm{pol}\ d^\circ(\dim X-2)\bigr).
\]

Hence, if \(\dim X\geq 3\), one has \(\alpha=\beta=\gamma=0\) and \(\chi=0\).

The case \(\dim X\leq 2\) having already been treated, this completes the proof.

\vspace{0.8em}

Best regards,

\hspace{2em}P. Deligne
