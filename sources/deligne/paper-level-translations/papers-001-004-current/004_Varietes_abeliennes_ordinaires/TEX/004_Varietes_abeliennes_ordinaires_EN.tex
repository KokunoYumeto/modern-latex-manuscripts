\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Ordinary Abelian Varieties over a Finite Field}\\[0.8em]
{\large P. Deligne}
\end{center}
\vspace{1em}

We give a down-to-earth description of the category of ordinary abelian varieties over a finite field $\F_q$. The result was inspired by Ihara [2, Chap. V]; see also [3].

\section*{1. Notation}
Let $p$ be a prime number, let $\F_p=\Z/(p)$, let $\overline{\F}_p$ be an algebraic closure of $\F_p$, and, for each power $q$ of $p$, let $\F_q$ be the subfield of $\overline{\F}_p$ with $q$ elements. For every algebraic extension $k$ of $\F_p$, let $W_0(k)$ be the henselian discrete valuation ring, essentially of finite type over $\Z$, absolutely unramified, with residue field $k$. Let $W(k)$ be the ring of Witt vectors of $k$, i.e. the completion of $W_0(k)$; put $W=W(\overline{\F}_p)$, and fix an embedding $\varphi:W\hookrightarrow\C$.

Let $\Z(1)$ denote the subgroup $2\pi i\Z$ of $\C$. The exponential map gives an isomorphism
\[
 \Z(1)\otimes\Z_\ell \simeq \Z_\ell(1)(\C)=\varprojlim_m \mu_{\ell^m}(\C).
\]
If $A$ is an abelian variety, $A^*$ denotes its dual. For every field $k$, $\bar k$ denotes an algebraic closure.

\section*{2. Ordinary abelian varieties}
Let $A$ be an abelian variety of dimension $g$ over a field $k$ of characteristic $p$. Recall that $A$ is ordinary if the following equivalent conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
\item $A$ has $p^g$ points of order dividing $p$ with values in $\bar k$;
\item the Hasse--Witt matrix
\[
 F^*:H^1(A^{(p)},\calO_{A^{(p)}})\longrightarrow H^1(A,\calO_A)
\]
is invertible;
\item the identity component of the group scheme $A_p$, the kernel of multiplication by $p$, is of multiplicative type, hence geometrically isomorphic to a power of $\mu_p$.
\end{enumerate}
If $k=\F_q$, if $F$ is the Frobenius endomorphism of $A$, and if $P_A(F;x)$ is its characteristic polynomial, these conditions are also equivalent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*),start=4]
\item at least half of the roots of $P_A(F;x)$ in $\Q_p$ are $p$-adic units; equivalently, if $n=\dim A$, then the reduction of $P_A(F;x)$ modulo $p$ is not divisible by $x^{n+1}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{3. The canonical lift}
Let $A$ be an ordinary abelian variety over $\overline{\F}_p$. We denote by $\widetilde A$ the Serre--Tate canonical lift of $A$ to $W$. It depends functorially on $A$ and is characterized by the fact that the $p$-divisible group $T_p(\widetilde A)$ over $W$ attached to $\widetilde A$ is the product of the $p$-divisible groups lifting, respectively, the identity component and the largest étale quotient of $T_p(A)$.

The canonical lift is also the unique lift of $A$ to which every endomorphism of $A$ lifts. Let $T(A)$ be the integral homology of the complex abelian variety $\widetilde A_\C$ obtained from $\widetilde A$ by extending scalars from $W$ to $\C$ through $\varphi$:
\[
 T(A)=H_1(\widetilde A_\C,\Z).
\]
The canonical lift descends uniquely to $W_0(\overline{\F}_p)$, so $\widetilde A_\C$ depends only on $A$ and on the restriction of $\varphi$ to $W_0(\overline{\F}_p)$. The free $\Z$-module $T(A)$ has rank $2\dim A$ and is functorial in $A$. If $\ell\ne p$, there is a functorial identification
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p004-en-31}
 T(A)\otimes\Z_\ell=T_\ell(A).
\end{equation}

The canonical lift of the dual abelian variety $A^*$ is the dual of $\widetilde A$. Thus $(\widetilde A_\C)^*=\widetilde{A^*}_\C$, and $T(A)$ and $T(A^*)$ are in perfect duality with values in $\Z(1)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p004-en-32}
 T(A)\otimes T(A^*)\longrightarrow \Z(1).
\end{equation}
The use of $\Z(1)$, rather than $\Z$, is essential for a theory invariant under complex conjugation. The pairings \eqref{eq:p004-en-32} are compatible, via \eqref{eq:p004-en-31}, with the pairings
\[
 T_\ell(A)\otimes T_\ell(A^*)\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(1).
\]
A morphism $\xi:A\to A^*$ defines a polarization of $A$ if and only if $\xi_\C:\widetilde A_\C\to\widetilde{A^*}_\C$ defines a polarization of $\widetilde A_\C$.

Put
\[
 T'_p(A)=\Hom(\Q_p/\Z_p,A(\overline{\F}_p)),
\qquad
 T''_p(A)=\Hom_{\Z_p}(T'_p(A^*),\Z(1)\otimes\Z_p).
\]
These $\Z_p$-modules are covariant functors of $A$. By definition of the canonical lift, the $p$-divisible group $T_p(\widetilde A)$ is the sum of the constant pro-étale group $T'_p(A)$ and the Cartier dual of $T'_p(A^*)$. For every morphism $u:A\to B$, the induced morphism $T_p(\widetilde A)\to T_p(\widetilde B)$ identifies with the sum of $u|T'_p(A):T'_p(A)\to T'_p(B)$ and the Cartier transpose of ${}^t u|T'_p(B^*):T'_p(B^*)\to T'_p(A^*)$. Over $\C$ there is a canonical isomorphism $\Z(1)/p^n\Z(1)\simeq\mu_{p^n}$, hence a functorial decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p004-en-pdec}
 T_{(p)}(A):=T(A)\otimes\Z_p=T'_p(A)\oplus T''_p(A).
\end{equation}

\section*{4. Finite subgroups}
If $\psi:X\to Y$ is an isogeny of complex abelian varieties, the exact homotopy sequence reduces to a short exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow H_1(X)\longrightarrow H_1(Y)\longrightarrow \Ker(\psi)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus quotient abelian varieties of $X$ by finite subgroups, and the finite subgroups themselves, are in bijection with the overlattices of $H_1(X)\otimes\Q$ containing $H_1(X)$.

Let $A$ be ordinary over $\overline{\F}_p$. If $n$ is prime to $p$, the finite group subschemes of order $n$ of $A$, of $\widetilde A$, and of $\widetilde A_\C$ correspond bijectively; they also correspond to overlattices $R$ of $T(A)$ such that $[R:T(A)]=n$.

Put
\[
 V'_p(A)=T'_p(A)\otimes\Q_p,
\qquad
 V''_p(A)=T''_p(A)\otimes\Q_p.
\]
Finite group subschemes of $A$ of order $p^k$ are products of an étale subgroup and an infinitesimal subgroup. Étale subgroups of order $p^k$ correspond to those subgroups of order $p^k$ of $\widetilde A_\C$ for which the corresponding overlattice $R$ of $T(A)$ is contained in $T_{(p)}(A)+V'_p(A)$. By duality, infinitesimal subgroups correspond to overlattices $R$ of $T(A)$ which are $p$-isogenous to $T(A)$, i.e. for which $[R:T(A)]$ is a power of $p$, and are contained in $T_{(p)}(A)+V''_p(A)$.

Thus finite subgroups of $A$, or quotient abelian varieties of $A$, correspond bijectively to overlattices $R$ of $T(A)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p004-en-41}
 R\otimes\Z_p=(R\otimes\Z_p\cap V'_p)+(R\otimes\Z_p\cap V''_p).
\end{equation}

\section*{5. The quotient by the infinitesimal part}
In particular, $A^{(p)}$, the quotient of ordinary $A$ by its largest infinitesimal subgroup killed by $p$, is defined by the overlattice $T(A)^{(p)}$ of $T(A)$, $p$-isogenous to $T(A)$, such that
\[
 T(A)^{(p)}\otimes\Z_p=T'_p(A)+\frac{1}{p}T''_p(A).
\]

\section*{6. Frobenius}
Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $\F_q$, and let $F:x\mapsto x^q$ be its Frobenius endomorphism. Recall that $A$ is uniquely determined by the pair $(\overline A,F)$ obtained from $(A,F)$ after extending scalars from $\F_q$ to $\overline{\F}_q$. The endomorphism $F$ of $\overline A$ factors through the relative Frobenius $Fr^{(q)}:\overline A\to\overline A^{(q)}$ and an isomorphism $F':\overline A^{(q)}\to\overline A$.

If $A$ is ordinary, we write $T(A)$ for the $\Z$-module $T(\overline A)$ endowed with the endomorphism $F$ induced by Frobenius. By the preceding discussion and \eqref{eq:p004-en-pdec}, the lattices $T(A)$ and $F(T(A))$ are $p$-isogenous and
\begin{align}
 F(T'_p(A))&=T'_p(A),\label{eq:p004-en-61}\\
 F(T''_p(A))&=qT''_p(A).\label{eq:p004-en-62}
\end{align}

\section*{7. The theorem}
\begin{theorem}
The functor
\[
 A\longmapsto (T(A),F)
\]
is an equivalence between the category of ordinary abelian varieties over $\F_q$ and the category of finite-rank free $\Z$-modules $T$ endowed with an endomorphism $F$ satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $F$ is semisimple, and its eigenvalues have complex absolute value $q^{1/2}$;
\item at least half of the roots in $\Q_p$ of the characteristic polynomial of $F$ are $p$-adic units; equivalently, if $T$ has rank $d$, the reduction of $P_T(F;x)$ modulo $p$ is not divisible by $x^{[d/2]+1}$;
\item there exists an endomorphism $V$ of $T$ such that $FV=q$.
\end{enumerate}
If (a) holds, then (b) and (c) are equivalent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),start=4]
\item $T\otimes\Z_p$ has an $F$-stable decomposition into two $\Z_p$-submodules $T'_p$ and $T''_p$ of the same dimension, such that $F|T'_p$ is invertible and $F|T''_p$ is divisible by $q$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(A) We first show that (a), (b), and (c) imply (d). If $\alpha$ is a complex eigenvalue of $F$, then $q/\alpha$ is another eigenvalue with the same multiplicity, and $\alpha(q/\alpha)=q$. Apart from possible values $\pm q^{1/2}$, the eigenvalues of $F$ in $\C$, and therefore in $\overline{\Q}_p$, fall into pairs $\alpha$ and $q/\alpha$. The two roots in such a pair cannot both be $p$-adic units. By (b), $\pm q^{1/2}$ is not an eigenvalue; half of the eigenvalues in $\Q_p$ are units, say $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{d/2}$, and the other half are $\beta_i=q/\alpha_i$. Let $T_{(p)}=T\otimes\Z_p$, $V_p=T\otimes\Q_p$, let $V'_p$ be the kernel of $\prod(F-\alpha_i)$, and let $V''_p$ be the kernel of $\prod(F-\beta_i)$. Then $V_p=V'_p\oplus V''_p$. If $T'_p$ is the projection of $T_{(p)}$ onto $V'_p$ and $T''_p=T_{(p)}\cap V''_p$, then $T_{(p)}=T'_p\oplus T''_p$, giving (d).

(B) Full faithfulness. For abelian varieties $A$ and $B$ over $\F_q$, let
\[
 \psi:\Hom(A,B)\longrightarrow \Hom_F(T(A),T(B))
\]
be the natural map. Tate's theorem, together with \eqref{eq:p004-en-31}, shows that
\[
 \psi_\ell:\Hom(A,B)\otimes\Z_\ell\longrightarrow \Hom_F(T(A),T(B))\otimes\Z_\ell
\]
is an isomorphism for $(\ell,p)=1$. Hence $\psi\otimes\Q$ is an isomorphism. Since $\Hom(A,B)$ is torsion-free, $\psi$ is injective. If $u:A\to B$ is such that $T(u)$ is divisible by $n$, then $u_\C:\widetilde A_\C\to\widetilde B_\C$ is divisible by $n$, and so is the lift $\widetilde u:\widetilde A\to\widetilde B$ at the generic point of $W$. The kernel of multiplication by $n$ is flat over $W$, so $\widetilde u$ vanishes on that kernel; therefore $\widetilde u$ and $u$ are divisible by $n$. Thus $\psi$ is bijective.

(C) Necessity. For $(T(A),F)$, condition (a) follows from Weil. Condition (d), and hence (b) and (c), follows from \eqref{eq:p004-en-61} and \eqref{eq:p004-en-62}.

(D) Isogenies. Let $(T_0,F)$ satisfy (a) and (d), and let $T$ be an $F$-stable lattice in $T_0\otimes\Q$ satisfying (d). Suppose $(T_0,F)$ comes from an abelian variety $A$ over $\F_q$. After multiplying $T$ by a rational number, we may assume $T\supset T_0$. Condition (d) implies \eqref{eq:p004-en-41}; $T$ therefore defines a subgroup $H$ of $A$, defined over $\F_q$, and $(T,F)=T(A/H)$.

(E) Essential surjectivity. Passing to isogeny classes, the functor $T$ sends ordinary abelian varieties over $\F_q$ to finite-dimensional $\Q$-vector spaces with an automorphism $F$ satisfying (a) and (b). By (D), it remains to prove essential surjectivity on simple objects. For every simple object $(V,F)$ in the image category, Honda's theorem gives an abelian variety $A$ over $\F_q$ whose Frobenius characteristic polynomial is a power of that of $F$. The criterion in Section 2 shows that $A$ is ordinary. Moreover $T(A)\otimes\Q$ is a sum of copies of $(V,F)$; by (B), the isogeny class of $A$ is a sum of copies of an abelian variety $B$ with $T(B)\otimes\Q=(V,F)$.
\end{proof}

\section*{8. The complex structure}
Let $(T,F)$ satisfy the hypotheses of the theorem, let $2g=\rank T$, and let $A$ be the corresponding abelian variety over $\F_q$. For the complex abelian variety $\widetilde A_\C$ obtained from the canonical lift,
\[
 T=H_1(\widetilde A_\C,\Z).
\]
Thus $T\otimes\R$ is identified with the Lie algebra of $\widetilde A_\C$ and has a complex structure. According to J.-P. Serre, this complex structure can be reconstructed from $T$, $F$, and the restriction of $\varphi$ as follows.

\begin{proposition}
The complex structure on $T\otimes\R$ defined above is characterized by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
\item the endomorphism $F$ is $\C$-linear;
\item if $v$ is the valuation of the algebraic closure $\overline{\Q}$ of $\Q$ in $\C$ extending the valuation of $W_0(\overline{\F}_p)$, then the valuations of the $g$ eigenvalues of this endomorphism are strictly positive.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Condition (I) is clear. Condition (II) follows from the fact that the action of $F$ on the Lie algebra of $\widetilde A$ is congruent to zero modulo $p$. Uniqueness of the complex structure satisfying (I) and (II) follows easily from condition (b) for $(T,F)$.
