Dear Serre,

Your letter gives me an occasion to try to collect my thoughts. I shall begin by listing some obstacles to grasping the relation between automorphic forms and motives.

\textbf{A.} In order to have a correspondence with reasonable functoriality between representations of \(G(F)\) (\(F\) local non-archimedean) and representations of the Weil--Deligne group \(W'(\Fbar/F)\), Langlands is led to use the correspondence that I call ``unitary'' in Antwerp II (forms \(\ldots\), \S3, p. Del42--45). Since functoriality is crucial here, against my inclination, I rally to it. The problem already appears for \(\GL(2)\).

Example: if \(\pi\) is an irreducible admissible representation of \(\GL(2,F)\), its central character \(\omega_\pi\) is a character of \(F^\ast\), i.e. of \(W(\Fbar/F)\) by class field theory. The dual \(G^\vee\) of \(\GL(2,F)\) is \(\GL(2,\mathbb C)\) [\(\mathbb C\), since one is considering complex representations of \(\GL(2,F)\)], and one wants \(\pi\) to correspond to a conjugacy class of representations
\[
\sigma:W'(\Fbar/F)\longrightarrow \GL(2,\mathbb C).
\]
One wants a relation between \(\omega_\pi\) and \(\det(\sigma)\), of the form
\[
\omega_\pi=\det(\sigma)\,\normchar^k.
\]
One also wants to be able to consider a group \(G\) isomorphic to \(\GL(2,F)\), but for which one has not fixed which was the ``obvious'' two-dimensional representation: \(V\) or \(V^\vee\)? In other words, one wants compatibility with the outer automorphism \(\tau\) of \(\GL(2,F)\) (which likewise induces one on the dual \(G^\vee\)): if \(\pi\) corresponds to \(\sigma\), then \(\pi\circ\tau\) (isomorphic to \(\pi^\vee\)) must correspond to \(\tau\circ\sigma\) (isomorphic to \(\sigma^\vee\)). Since \(\omega_\pi\) and \(\det(\sigma)\) are thereby replaced by their inverses, this is possible only if \(k=0\).

If \(\chi_1\) and \(\chi_2\) are characters of \(F^\ast\), the representation \(\chi_1\oplus\chi_2\) of \(W'(\Fbar/F)\) must, for
\[
\chi_1\chi_2^{-1}\ne \normchar^{\pm1},
\]
correspond to an induced representation, independent of the order in which \(\chi_1\) and \(\chi_2\) are taken, and with central character \(\chi_1\chi_2\). These constraints are satisfied by the unitary induction of the character \((\chi_1,\chi_2)\) of a split torus. Unitary: twist by \(\delta^{1/2}\).

The drawback is that, although the notion of irreducible admissible representation \(\pi\) is purely algebraic (one can replace \(\mathbb C\) by any algebraically closed \(K\) of characteristic \(0\), \(G^\vee\) becoming a group over \(K\)), the correspondence is not: it uses a square root of \(q\) (the number of elements of the residue field). An analyst like Langlands believes that \(q\) has one square root better than the other. I do not. This forces me to say that there is not one correspondence between the \(\pi\)'s and the \(\sigma\)'s, but two. In order to name one, one must have chosen a character of \(F^\ast\) which is a square root of \(\normchar\) (with values in \(K\) when \(\pi\) is \(/K\)). I shall suppose the chosen square root is unramified, but this is probably not necessary. In the case of \(\GL(n)\), if one replaces one choice of \(\normchar^{1/2}\) by another, the ratio of the two is an \(\varepsilon\) of order \(2\), and if \(\pi\) corresponds to \(\sigma\) for one choice, then
\[
\pi\cdot\varepsilon(\det)^{(n-1)/2}
\]
corresponds to \(\sigma\) for the other. Notice that the central character has not changed.

In the global case, likewise, I would want a correspondence between motives and certain automorphic representations only for \(F\) endowed with a character of the idele class group which is a square root of \(\normchar\).

Example: the trivial representation of \(\GL(2,F)\) corresponds to the representation
\[
\normchar^{1/2}\oplus\normchar^{-1/2}
\]
of \(W'(\Fbar/F)\).

Example: a classical automorphic form of weight \(2k\) for \(\GL(2,\mathbb Z)\) is a function of a lattice \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb C\) satisfying, among other things,
\[
f(\lambda\Lambda)=\lambda^{-2k}f(\Lambda).
\]
The group here is \(\GL_{\mathbb R}(\mathbb C)\), and the central character is \(\normchar^{2k}\) (or \(\normchar^{-2k}\), depending on conventions). The determinant of the corresponding \(\ell\)-adic representation ``is'' \(\normchar^{2k-1}\). This shows that the customary correspondence between modular forms and motives is not compatible with the outer automorphism of \(\GL(2)\). If one has endowed \(\mathbb Q\) with the positive square root of \(\normchar\) on \(\mathbb A^\ast\), I am forced to say that it is
\[
\vol(\mathbb C/\Lambda)^{1/2}f
\]
that one wants to correspond to a motive. I do not see how one could hope to say anything sensible for a unitary group if the correspondence for \(\GL(n)\) were not compatible with outer automorphisms.

Question of sign: the \(\normchar^{\pm2k}\) is tied to the following nightmare: if \(T\) is a torus, and one lets \(T(\A)\) act on \(T(\A)/T(\mathbb Q)\) by left translations, then on functions on \(T(\mathbb Z)/T(\mathbb Q)\) by transport of structure, one has
\[
[t\ast f](u)=f(t^{-1}u).
\]
A character \(\chi\) defines the automorphic representation \(\mathbb C\chi\); the action of \(T(\A)\) on it is by \(\chi^{-1}\).

Complement. For \(G\) a reductive group over a field \(k\), there is a canonical morphism
\[
G(k)\longrightarrow H^1(\Gal(\bar k/k),\mu_2)=k^\ast/k^{\ast2},
\]
defined as follows. It factors through \(G^{\mathrm{ad}}\), is compatible with products, and for \(R_{k'/k}(G')\) is deduced, via \(N:k'^\ast\to k^\ast\), from its analogue for \(G'(k')\). This reduces to the case where \(G\) is absolutely simple adjoint. Let \(G_{\mathrm{sc}}\) be the corresponding simply connected group, and \(Z\) its center. The desired morphism is deduced from
\[
\gamma:Z\longrightarrow \mu_2,
\]
to be defined. The question is geometric, and it is enough to define this \(\gamma\) for \(G\) split. Choose a torus \(T_{\mathrm{sc}}\subset G_{\mathrm{sc}}\) and a Borel \(B\) containing it, and take for \(\gamma\) the restriction of the character
\[
\delta_B^{1/2}=\prod \omega_i
\]
of \(T_{\mathrm{sc}}\) to \(Z\). The square of this character factors through the image of \(T_{\mathrm{sc}}\) in \(G\); its restriction to \(Z\) therefore takes values in \(\mu_2\). Changing \(B\) changes \(\delta_B^{1/2}\) by a character of the torus of \(G\), and \(\gamma\) is therefore well-defined [another argument: two \(B\)'s are conjugate, whereas \(Z\) is central].

For arbitrary \(G\), this in fact defines a central extension of \(G^{\mathrm{ad}}\) by \(\mu_2\), whose pull-back to \(G\) one may take:
\[
1\longrightarrow \mu_2\longrightarrow G_1\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow 1.
\]

For the group \(G^\vee\), this corresponds to a canonical central element \(c\) of order dividing \(2\). For \(G\) adjoint (absolutely simple, to simplify), \(G^\vee\) is simply connected, and the element in question is
\[
\prod_{\alpha>0}\alpha^\vee(-1).
\]
If \(E\) is an irreducible self-dual representation of \(G^\vee\), \(c\) acts by \(\pm1\) according as \(E\) is orthogonal or symplectic (Bourb. Lie VIII \S7 prop.12, p. 152). In fact there is a slightly more precise property than Bourb. loc. cit. which characterizes \(c\). Pin \(G^\vee\), and let \(P=\mathbb Z/2\ltimes G^\vee\) be the semidirect product for the action of \(\mathbb Z/2\) which respects the pinning and acts on the Dynkin diagram by the opposition involution. Every irreducible representation \(E\) of \(P\) is self-dual, \(c\) is central in \(P\), and \(c\) acts on \(E\) by \(\pm1\) according as \(E\) is orthogonal or symplectic.

In other words, if one regards the representations of \(P\) as super vector spaces, by means of the action of \(c\), and modifies the commutativity of the tensor product accordingly, all representations of \(P\) admit an invariant (super)symmetric bilinear form.

This extends to any reductive \(G\) and gives \(c\) central of order dividing \(2\) in \(G^\vee\). These characters/elements must, as for \(\GL(n)\), control the effect of changing \(\normchar^{1/2}\).

\textbf{B.} If \(f\) is a classical automorphic form of weight \(2k\), for instance on \(\GL(2,\mathbb Z)\), and \(E\) is the subfield of \(\mathbb C\) generated by its coefficients, what corresponds to it is a motive \(M\) with coefficients in \(E\), or more concretely a compatible system of \(E_\lambda\)-adic representations of dimension \(2\). A motive with coefficients in \(E\) is a motive \(M\) endowed with \(E\to\End(M)\), and there is a coefficient-extension functor \(M\mapsto E'\otimes_E M\) for \(E\subset E'\). Passing to the limit, one defines the category of motives with coefficients in an algebraic extension \(\mathcal E\) of \(\mathbb Q\) by
\[
(\mathcal M\ \mathrm{coeff}\ \mathcal E)=\varinjlim_E \ind(\mathcal M\ \mathrm{coeff}\ E\subset\mathcal E).
\]
I would like to restrict myself, to begin with, to having a correspondence between certain automorphic representations and motivic objects, relative to motives with coefficients in the algebraic closure \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) of \(\mathbb Q\) in \(\mathbb C\). The field \(\mathbb C\) appears because one considers automorphic representations contained in the space of complex-valued functions on \(G(\A)/G(F)\).

In the case of function fields, one may instead consider functions with values in an algebraically closed field \(K\) of characteristic \(0\), and one should consider motives with coefficients in the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q\) in \(K\). The story should be invariant under \(\Aut(K)\) (which acts on this algebraic closure as well as on \(\normchar^{1/2}\)).

In the number-field case, one might hope that the automorphic representations having to do with motives are those to which one can attach Hodge numbers, but this is an inaccessible hope: since one does not understand why there exist Maass forms with a Laplacian eigenvalue other than the good one, \(\lambda_0\), corresponding to \(L\)-functions of real quadratic fields, how could one show that for \(\Delta=\lambda_0\) they are all motivic?

One must not hope that every motivated automorphic representation ``with coefficients in \(E\)'' corresponds to a motivic object with coefficients in \(E\). If \(F'/F\) is Galois with quaternion group, the two-dimensional representation of \(\Gal(F'/F)\) gives an automorphic representation ``with coefficients in \(\mathbb Q\)'', but there is no corresponding motive with coefficients in \(\mathbb Q\) (only with coefficients in \(L\) if \(L\) splits the quaternions). Similarly, conjecturally, for abelian surfaces with quaternion algebra of endomorphisms.

\textbf{C.} How is one to understand the semidirect product
\[
\LG=\Gal(F'/F)\ltimes G^\vee
\]
introduced by Langlands? In the local non-archimedean case, what plays the role of \(\sigma\) in the preceding explanations is
\[
\varphi:W'(\Fbar/F)\longrightarrow \LG
\]
compatible with the projections to \(\Gal(F'/F)\), and taken up to \(G^\vee\)-conjugacy. If I allow myself to replace \(G^\vee\), a complex group, by \(G^\vee(E_\lambda)\), a group over an extension \(E_\lambda\) of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), \(W'\) by Gal, and \(\varphi\) by a continuous homomorphism (which restores the unipotent part of \(W'\)), what one considers is
\[
H^1(\Gal(\Fbar/F),G^\vee(E_\lambda)),
\]
in whose definition enters the action of \(\Gal(\Fbar/F)\) on \(G^\vee\). What corresponds to \(\pi\) is an isomorphism class of \(\Gal(\Fbar/F)\)-equivariant \(G^\vee(E_\lambda)\)-torsors (the equivariance also affecting \(G^\vee(E_\lambda)\)). When things are said this way, the motivic analogue becomes clear.

To spell it out: \(\varphi:\Gal(\Fbar/F)\to \LG(E_\lambda)\) versus \(H^1\): let \(P\) be the trivial \(G^\vee(E_\lambda)\). If \(\Gamma\) is a group of automorphisms of \(G^\vee(E_\lambda)\), the group of automorphisms of the system (torsor \(P\) under \(G^\vee(E_\lambda)\)) inducing on \(G^\vee(E_\lambda)\) an element of \(\Gamma\) is the semidirect product
\[
\Gamma\ltimes G(E_\lambda);
\]
\(\gamma\) acts by \((\gamma,\gamma)\), \(g\in G(E_\lambda)\) acts by (left translation by \(g\), Id). By this isomorphism, \(\varphi\) gives an equivariant torsor. If one changes the trivialization of the torsor, \(\varphi\) changes by a \(G(E_\lambda)\)-inner automorphism.

All this leads me to the following suggestion. Let \(F\) be a global field and \(G\) reductive over \(F\). Suppose given a square root \(\normchar^{1/2}\) of the character \(\normchar\) of the idele class group. If \(F\) is a number field, \(\normchar^{1/2}\) has values in \(\mathbb R\). If \(F\) is a function field and one defines automorphic forms as functions with values in an algebraically closed \(K\) of characteristic \(0\), then \(\normchar^{1/2}\) has values in \(K\).

Regard the dual \(G^\vee\) of \(G\) as a local system, on \(\Spec(F)_{\mathrm{et}}\), of reductive groups over \(\mathbb Q\). Concretely, such an object is the datum, for each sufficiently large étale extension \(F'\) of \(F\), of a reductive group over \(\mathbb Q\), \(G^\vee(F')\), functorial in \(F'\), and in such a way that for \(F''\to F'\), the morphism \(G^\vee(F')\to G^\vee(F'')\) is an isomorphism; definition of \(G^\vee\): say that \(F'/F\) is large enough if \(F'\) splits \(G\). Starting from \(G\otimes_F F'\), one defines its dual \(G^\vee\) (split and pinned) as usual. In fact I shall only have to use the corresponding local system \(G^\vee_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}\) of reductive groups over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\), where \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) is the algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q\) in \(\mathbb C\) (respectively \(K\) for a function field). It is not necessary, in order to have \(G^\vee(F')\), that \(F'\) split \(F\): it suffices that \(\Gal(\Fbar/F')\) act trivially on the Dynkin diagram and that \(F'\) split the connected center.

Given this, what should correspond to certain automorphic representations \(\pi\) is an isomorphism class of motivic \(G^\vee_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}\)-torsors \(\sigma\), i.e. of objects of the following type: for \(F'/F\) large enough that \(G^\vee(F')\) be defined, a \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\)-linear tensor functor
\[
\Rep(G^\vee_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(F'))\longrightarrow
(\text{motives with coefficients in }\overline{\mathbb Q}\text{ over }F'),
\]
functorial in \(F'\).

Changing the choice of \(\normchar^{1/2}\). Suppose that \(\normchar^{1/2}\) is replaced by \(\varepsilon\normchar^{1/2}\), where \(\varepsilon\) is a character of order \(2\) of the idele class group. We constructed in (A., Complement) an extension
\[
1\longrightarrow\mu_2\longrightarrow\widetilde G\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow1.
\]
Push it out by \(\mu_2\hookrightarrow\mathbb G_m\):
\[
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
1&\longrightarrow&\mu_2&\longrightarrow&\widetilde G&\longrightarrow&G&\longrightarrow&1\\
&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\Vert\\
1&\longrightarrow&\mathbb G_m&\longrightarrow&G_1&\longrightarrow&G&\longrightarrow&1\\
&&\downarrow^{z^2}&&\downarrow\\
&&\mathbb G_m&=&\mathbb G_m.
\end{array}
\]
Using this diagram, one defines a character, again denoted \(\varepsilon\), of \(G(\mathbb A)\), trivial on \(G(F)\): lift \(g\) to \(g_1\in G_1(\mathbb A)\), project to \(\mathbb G_m(\mathbb A)\), and apply \(\varepsilon\). If, for \(\normchar^{1/2}\), \(\pi\) corresponds to \(\sigma\), then for \(\normchar^{1/2}\varepsilon\), \(\pi\varepsilon\) must correspond to \(\sigma\).

Changing the choice of \(\normchar^{1/2}\) (bis). We also constructed a canonical element \(c\) of \(G^\vee\), of order dividing \(2\). The character \(\varepsilon\) gives a rank \(1\) Artin motive \(L_\varepsilon\) over \(F\), endowed with \(L_\varepsilon^{\otimes2}\simeq1\). The element \(c\) makes it possible to twist \(\sigma\) by \(L_\varepsilon\): if \(\sigma\) associates the motive \(\sigma(E)\) to the representation \(E\) on which \(c\) acts by \((-1)^n\), then \(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon\) associates to \(E\) the motive \(\sigma(E)\otimes L_\varepsilon\). If, for \(\normchar^{1/2}\), \(\pi\) corresponds to \(\sigma_\pi\), then \(\pi\) must still correspond to a motivic torsor when \(\normchar^{1/2}\) is replaced by \(\normchar^{1/2}\varepsilon\): it must correspond to \(\sigma\otimes\varepsilon\).

These two rules should of course be compatible.

\textit{Loose ends}: (i) For \(G\) reductive over \(F\), split over \(F'/F\), it is perhaps more natural to consider
\[
[G(\mathbb A_{F'})/G(F')]^{\Gal(F'/F)}=:G(\mathbb A/F)
\]
than \(G(\mathbb A)/G(F)\). In the function-field case, this is required if one wants the points of an algebraic stack. For tori, the character group of \(T(\mathbb A/F)\) seems more pleasant than that of \(T(\mathbb A)/T(F)\).

(ii) What should one do if \(\pi\) appears in \(L(G(\mathbb A)/G(F))\) with multiplicity \(>1\)? Can one hope that the \(\pi\)-isotypic component is canonically a direct sum of representations isomorphic to \(\pi\) (and, in the function-field case, each of these summands containing functions corresponding to \(\ell\)-adic sheaves)? And that each summand gives rise to a \(\sigma\)?

(iii) How should one characterize \(\sigma\) if its isomorphism class is not uniquely determined by the corresponding local isomorphism classes? If such an indeterminacy is responsible for a multiplicity \(>1\) as in (ii), how is one to relate \(\pi\)'s in \(L(G(\mathbb A)/G(F))\) to \(\sigma\)'s?

Example: \(\GL(2)\). Here, to \(\pi\) and to \(\normchar^{1/2}\), one can associate \(\pi|\det|^{1/2}\), and this associated automorphic representation is the same for \((\pi,\normchar^{1/2})\) and for \((\pi\varepsilon,\normchar^{1/2}\varepsilon)\) (\(\varepsilon\) of order \(2\)). This makes it natural to attach a \(\sigma\) to \(\pi|\det|^{1/2}\) rather than to \((\pi,\normchar^{1/2})\). This is the usual practice, although it is not compatible with duality. The same thing can be done for \(\GL(2n)\).

I had intended to spell out other examples, but they are delaying this letter too much. They will be for next time.
