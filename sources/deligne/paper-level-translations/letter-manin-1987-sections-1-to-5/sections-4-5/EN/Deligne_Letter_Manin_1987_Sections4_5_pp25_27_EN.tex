\section*{4. Infinitesimal automorphisms: proof of 2.6(i)}

A stable supercurve has no non-trivial infinitesimal automorphism.

Locally, on the smooth locus, the sheaf \(\cA\) of infinitesimal automorphisms projects isomorphically onto \(T_X/\cD\), where \(\cD\) denotes the structural distribution. For
\[
  X=(C,\OO_C\oplus \om_C^{1/2}),
  \qquad
  \cA=\omega_C^{-1}\oplus \omega_C^{1/2}\otimes\omega_C^{-1}.
\]

The first task, near a singularity, is to compute which meromorphic sections of \(\cA\) are infinitesimal automorphisms. One has to send \(\TT\) into \(\TT\).

\paragraph{Case I.}
Here \(\TT_X^{-}\) is a square root of \(\omega_C\). Let \(D\) be as in 3.1, I, where it was denoted \(D_0\). An infinitesimal automorphism is written as
\[
  aD+bD^2
  \qquad\text{with}\qquad
  (-1)^{\deg a}2a+Db=0,
\]
or equivalently
\[
  a=\mp\frac12(-1)^{\deg b}Db.
\]
In order for it to send \(\TT\) into \(\TT\), one needs:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{even derivation}:& b\text{ even},\quad b\in\OO^{+},\\[1mm]
\text{odd derivation}:& b\text{ odd},\quad b\in\OO^{-}.
\end{array}
\]
Thus, in a neighbourhood of a component \(C\),
\[
  \boxed{\;\cA=\omega_C^{-1}\oplus \omega_C^{1/2}\otimes\omega_C^{-1}\; }.
\]
\footnote{On the smooth locus, \([D,X+aD]\bmod \cD=DX+(-1)^{|a|}a\,\partial_z\); thus there is a unique \(a\) that brings the bracket back into \(\cD\).}

\paragraph{Case II.}
Similarly, take \(D\) as in 3.1, II, and write the derivations as
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{even}:&\displaystyle \frac12Db\cdot D+bD^2\qquad (b\text{ even}),\\[3mm]
\text{odd}:&\displaystyle -\frac12Db\cdot D+bD^2\qquad (b\text{ odd}).
\end{array}
\]
The condition for \(b\), a rational section of \(\OO\), to give rise to a derivation \(\TT\to\TT\), is
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{even}:& b\in\OO^{+},\quad b\text{ vanishes at }0,\\[1mm]
\text{odd}:& b\in\OO^{-},\quad b\text{ vanishes at }0.
\end{array}
\]
In terms of the branches \(B_i\), one has
\[
  \cA=\cA_1\oplus\cA_2,
  \qquad
  \cA_i=T_{B_i}(-0)\oplus T_{B_i}(-0)\otimes\omega_{B_i}^{1/2}.
\]

An automorphism \(\sim\id\), even or odd, therefore gives, on the normalization of a component \(A\) of \(C\), a non-zero section of a line bundle of degree
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{even}:&
(2-2g)-\dfrac12\bigl(\#\text{ points of type I}\bigr)-\bigl(\#\text{ points of type II}\bigr),\\[3mm]
\text{odd}:&
(1-2g)-\dfrac12\bigl(\#\text{ points of type I}\bigr)-\bigl(\#\text{ points of type II}\bigr).
\end{array}
\]
Under the stability hypothesis, these degrees are strictly negative. Hence there is no non-trivial infinitesimal automorphism.

\section*{5. Valuative criterion of properness}

Here the only parameter spaces one has to consider are traits \((S,\eta,s)\), with \(S=\Spec(V)\), and one can therefore use the point of view of 1.5. By Deligne--Mumford, the ordinary stable curve \(C_\eta\) degenerates uniquely into a stable curve \(C\) over \(S\), after ramifying \(S\). What remains, after a further ramification if necessary, is to make \(L_\eta\) and the pairing with values in \(\omega_{C/S}\) degenerate.

Away from the singular points, one can extend \(L_\eta\) to an invertible sheaf \(L\). Correcting \(L\) by a divisor concentrated on the special fibre, and after passing to a double cover of \(S\), one may assume that
\[
  L^{\otimes2}\longrightarrow \omega_{C/S}
\]
is an isomorphism away from the singular points. After ramification, the unique extension of this \(L\) will do.

In an étale neighbourhood of each singular point, the square roots of \(\omega_X\) on the complement of the singular point are classified by
\[
  H^1(\mu_2)=H^1(\mathbf Z/2).
\]
Choose a section \(s\). In \(L\), over \(X-s\), the \(\sqrt{z}\)'s form a double cover. The variety of vanishing cycles has an \(H^1(\mathbf Z/2)\simeq\mathbf Z/2\). After ramification over the base \(S\) and étale localization at the singular point, there are therefore two possible cases. They are covered by cases I and II of 2.3.
