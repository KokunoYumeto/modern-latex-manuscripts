
\section*{(1.9) Local monodromy of mixed sheaves}

This paragraph will not be used later. It generalizes (1.8.7) to divisors with normal crossings.

\paragraph{(1.9.1).}
Let $X$ be smooth over $\Fq$, let $D$ be a normal-crossing divisor on $X$, the transverse union of smooth divisors $D_i$ indexed by $I$, and let $E=\bigcap_{i\in I}D_i$. Assume, for the construction of (1.7.8), that each $D_i$ is defined by a global equation $t_i=0$. Let $\mathcal F$ be a lisse sheaf on $X-D$, tamely ramified along $D$, and suppose that $\mathcal F$ has a finite increasing filtration $W$ by lisse subsheaves such that $\Gr^W_i\mathcal F$ is pointwise $\iota$-pure of weight $i$. Let $L$ be the set of primes different from $p$, and form $\mathcal F[E]$ as in (1.7.8). It is lisse on $E$, endowed with an action of $\mathbb Z_L(1)^I$; this action is quasi-unipotent, hence gives commuting nilpotent operators
\[
  N_i:\mathcal F[E](1)\longrightarrow \mathcal F[E].
\]
For a sheaf $\mathcal N$, two finite increasing filtrations $W'$ and $W''$, and a finite family of such nilpotent endomorphisms $N_i$, write
\[
  L(W',W'',(N_i)_{i\in A})
\]
for the condition that the $N_i$ stabilize $W'$, satisfy $N_iW'_a(1)\subset W'_{a-2}$, and that, for all rational $c_i>0$, the endomorphism $N=\sum c_iN_i$ induce isomorphisms
\[
  N^b:(\Gr^{W''}_{a+b}\Gr^{W'}_a\mathcal N)(b)
  \xrightarrow{\sim}\Gr^{W''}_{a-b}\Gr^{W'}_a\mathcal N.
\]
By (1.6.13), for fixed $W'$ and $N_i$ there is at most one $W''$ satisfying this condition.

\paragraph{Theorem (1.9.2).}
With the hypotheses and notation of (1.9.1), there exists a unique system of finite increasing filtrations $W(J)$, indexed by subsets $J\subset I$, by lisse subsheaves of $\mathcal F[E]$ stable under $\mathbb Z_L(1)^I$, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item $W(\varnothing)$ is the filtration induced from $W$ by functoriality;
\item for $J\subset K$, the condition
\[
  L(W(J),W(K),(N_k)_{k\in K-J})
\]
is satisfied.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover $\Gr^{W(J)}_a\mathcal F[E]$ is pointwise $\iota$-pure of weight $a$.

\paragraph{Lemma (1.9.3).}
Let $(c_k)_{k\in I}$ be positive integers. The sheaf $\mathcal F[E]$ has a unique finite increasing filtration $W(I)$ such that $N=\sum c_kN_k$ satisfies $L(W,W(I),N)$, and $\Gr^{W(I)}_a\mathcal F[E]$ is pointwise $\iota$-pure of weight $a$.

The assertion may be checked fibrewise. After a finite extension of the base field, choose local coordinates $(t_i,u_j)$ at a rational point of $E$. Restrict $\mathcal F$ to the curve given parametrically by $u_j=0$ and $t_i=t^{c_i}$. On the strict localization of this curve the geometric generic fibre identifies with the fibre of $\mathcal F[E]$, and the logarithm of local monodromy is precisely $N=\sum c_iN_i$. The lemma follows from (1.8.5).

\paragraph{(1.9.4).}
The filtration $W(I)$ of (1.9.3) is independent of the positive integers $c_k$, because it is characterized as the weight filtration. For $J\subset I$, apply the preceding construction to $X_J$ and to the traces of the divisors $D_j$ on $D_J'$; this defines $W(J)$ on $\mathcal F[D_J']$. Applying the construction again after restriction to $D_J$ and using the transitivity isomorphism (1.7.9.1) shows that, on $\mathcal F[E]$, the filtrations $W(J)$ satisfy the conditions of Theorem (1.9.2). The uniqueness is the uniqueness of the relative monodromy filtration in (1.6.13).

\paragraph{Variant (1.9.5).}
Let $S$ be of finite type over $\mathbb Z$, let $f:X\to S$ be smooth, let $D\subset X$ be a relative normal-crossing divisor, and let $\mathcal F$ be lisse on $X-D$, tamely ramified along $D$. The analogue of (1.9.2) holds fibre by fibre, by reduction to the finite-field case.

\paragraph{Remark (1.9.6).}
The expected parallelism with variations of Hodge structure suggests the same picture on $(D^*)^I$. Cattani and Kaplan proved the analogue of (1.9.2), in the pure case, for variations of Hodge structure on $(D^*)^I$; in their context the coefficients $c_i$ in the condition $L(W',W'',(N_i))$ may even be taken to be real positive numbers.

\section*{(1.10) Non-archimedean absolute values}

The conventions of (0.7) are in force. Let $X_0$ be a smooth absolutely irreducible curve over $\Fq$, let $j:U_0\hookrightarrow X_0$ be an open with finite complement $S_0$, and let $\mathcal F_0$ be lisse on $U_0$. In (1.8) the trace formula and local monodromy were used to study the archimedean absolute values of Frobenius eigenvalues at the boundary. Here one allows a different absolute value.

Choose either an isomorphism $\iota:\overline{\QQ}_\ell\simeq\CC$, or a prime $\ell'$, an algebraic closure $\overline{\QQ}_{\ell'}$, and an isomorphism $\iota:\overline{\QQ}_\ell\simeq\overline{\QQ}_{\ell'}$. In the non-archimedean case the absolute value is normalized by $|\ell'|=\ell'^{-1}$. The same argument as in (1.8.1), with this absolute value in place of the complex absolute value, gives the corresponding boundary estimates.

\paragraph{The basic estimate.}
If the eigenvalues of Frobenius on $\mathcal F_0$ at almost all points of $U_0$ satisfy a fixed upper bound for the chosen absolute value, then the eigenvalues at points of $S_0$ satisfy the same upper bound. The tensor-power argument again removes the harmless $+2$ that appears at the first stage of the trace-formula estimate.

\paragraph{Unit case.}
If the eigenvalues of Frobenius on $\mathcal F_0$ at almost all points of $U_0$ are $\ell'$-adic units, then the eigenvalues at the boundary are again $\ell'$-adic units. After shrinking $U_0$, the hypothesis ``almost all'' may be replaced by ``all points of $U_0$'' without changing the conclusion.

\paragraph{(1.10.6).}
For $\ell'=p$, it is useful to speak in terms of valuations. For a power $N$ of $p$, let $v_N$ be the valuation of $\overline{\QQ}_p$ normalized by $v_N(N)=1$. If $(\alpha_i)_{i\in I}$ is a finite family, its Newton polygon relative to $N$ is the continuous piecewise-linear function $n$ on $[0,|I|]$ characterized by $n(0)=0$ and by slopes the $v_N(\alpha_i)$ arranged in increasing order. Equivalently, $n(j)$ is the least valuation of a product of $j$ distinct $\alpha_i$.

For $x\in |X_0|$, the Newton polygon of $\mathcal F_0$ at $x$, relative to $\iota$, is the Newton polygon relative to $N(x)$ of the image under $\iota$ of the eigenvalues of $F_x$ on $\mathcal F_0$.

\paragraph{Corollary (1.10.7).}
Assume that, for almost all $x\in |U_0|$, the Newton polygon of $\mathcal F_0$ at $x$ is a fixed polygon $n$. Then, for $s\in |S_0|$, the Newton polygon $n_s$ at $s$ lies above $n$ and has the same endpoints:
\[
  n_s\geq n.
\]
The inequality is just the estimate applied to exterior powers $\bigwedge^j\mathcal F_0$; equality of endpoints follows from the rank-one case, since the valuation of the determinant is independent of $x$.

\paragraph{(1.10.8).}
Sharper statements are obtained by taking account of the monodromy filtration. For example:

\paragraph{Corollary (1.10.9).}
Let $\gamma\leq\delta$, and suppose that, for almost all $x\in |U_0|$, every Frobenius eigenvalue $\alpha$ on $\mathcal F_0$ satisfies
\[
  \gamma\leq v_{N(x)}(\iota\alpha)\leq \delta.
\]
If $n=\lfloor\delta-\gamma\rfloor$, then the logarithm $N$ of the unipotent part of the local monodromy at every $s\in |S_0|$ satisfies
\[
  N^{n+1}=0.
\]
Indeed, if $m$ is minimal with $N^{m+1}=0$, local monodromy gives eigenvalues related by multiplication by $N(s)^m$; the preceding estimate bounds $m$ by $n$.

\section*{(1.11) Specialization of monodromy}

\paragraph{Proposition (1.11.1).}
Let $S$ be irreducible, let $f:X\to S$ be smooth of pure relative dimension one with geometrically connected fibres, let $g$ be a section of $f$, and let $\mathcal F$ be a lisse $\mathbb Z_\ell$-sheaf on $X$. For every geometric point $\bar s$ of $S$, let $M_{\bar s}$ be the image of
\[
  \pi_1(X_{\bar s},g(\bar s))
\]
in
\[
  \Aut((g^*\mathcal F/\ell^n g^*\mathcal F)_{\bar s}).
\]
Then there exists a non-empty open $U\subset S$ such that, for every $n$, these groups form the fibres of a lisse subsheaf $M^n$ of $\Aut(g^*\mathcal F/\ell^n g^*\mathcal F)$ on $U$.

Let $I_n$ be the finite etale cover of $X$ which is the sheaf of isomorphisms from $\mathcal F/\ell^n\mathcal F$ to $f^*g^*(\mathcal F/\ell^n\mathcal F)$. The identity gives a section over $g$. After shrinking $S$, one constructs simultaneously finite etale covers $X_n\subset I_n$, containing this section and stable under $M^n$, such that fibre by fibre $X_n$ is the Galois cover trivializing $\mathcal F/\ell^n\mathcal F$. The groups $M^n$ are then $g^*(X_n/X)$.

The proof uses two facts: automorphism groups modulo $\ell$ are finite, and the kernel of reduction from automorphisms modulo $\ell^{n+1}$ to automorphisms modulo $\ell^n$ is an $\ell$-group. Standard specialization results for prime-to-$p$ fundamental groups then make the construction locally constant after shrinking.

\paragraph{Remark (1.11.2).}
For the prime-to-$L$ fundamental groups $\pi_1^{(L)}(X_{\bar s},g(\bar s))$ to form a local system on $S$, it suffices, after shrinking, that $X$ admit a compactification $\overline X$ proper and smooth over $S$, with complement finite etale over $S$. It is enough that such a compactification exist after a finite radicial surjective base change, since the etale topology does not see such a change.

\paragraph{Remark (1.11.3).}
Under such compactifications, tame ramification groups at boundary points also specialize. If $\bar s_1$ specializes to $\bar s_2$, and a boundary point specializes accordingly, the image of the inertia group in the monodromy group specializes to a conjugate subgroup. In the tamely ramified case this is expressed by conjugacy classes of morphisms
\[
  \mathbb Z_{(L)}(1)\longrightarrow M_{\bar s},
\]
and these classes specialize compatibly.

\paragraph{(1.11.4). Proof of (1.3.1).}
With the notation of (1.3.1), it is enough to prove the assertion after a finite extension of the field of constants. One must show that the largest quotient of
\[
  H_1(X,\mathbb Z_{(p')})
\]
on which Frobenius acts trivially is finite. Choose a dense quasi-projective open $U_0\subset X_0$ and embed it into projective space. Let $V$ be a sufficiently general linear section of dimension one. Bertini gives surjectivity
\[
  \pi_1(V)\longrightarrow \pi_1(U)\longrightarrow \pi_1(X),
\]
and hence $H_1(V,\mathbb Z_{(p')})\to H_1(X,\mathbb Z_{(p')})$ is surjective. By the specialization statement above, the prime-to-$p$ fundamental group of a sufficiently general section is the same as that of a section defined over a finite extension of $\Fq$. Thus the assertion is reduced to the curve case, already proved in (1.3.2).

\paragraph{Remark (1.11.5).}
Proposition (1.11.1) remains valid for arbitrary morphisms of finite type: localization on $S$ and Mayer--Vietoris reduce one to flat morphisms with normal fibres; restriction to opens reduces to the smooth quasi-projective case; the hyperplane-section argument then reduces to relative dimension one.
