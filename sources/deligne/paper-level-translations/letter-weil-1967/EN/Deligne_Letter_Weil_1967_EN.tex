\begin{center}
{\large Letter to André Weil}\\
Bures-sur-Yvette, July 1, 1969
\end{center}

Dear Professor Weil,

Here is the determination, in the case of a function field, of the way in which the constant of the functional equation we had discussed varies. I enclose a copy of a letter from Langlands and some calculations related to it. I shall use only the assertion (L.L.) below.

\section*{1. \(\lambda\)-adic representations, after Serre and Grothendieck}

Let \(K\) be a function field in one variable with field of constants \(\Fq\), let \(X\) be the set of its places, let \(E\) be a number field, let \(\lambda\) be a place of \(E\) not dividing \(q\), and let \(E_\lambda\) be the corresponding local field, with ring of integers \(\cO_\lambda\) and residue field \(k_\lambda=\cO_\lambda/\lambda\).

A \(\lambda\)-adic representation of \(K\) is a continuous representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on a finite-dimensional vector space over \(E_\lambda\), such that there is a finite subset \(S\) of \(X\) outside which \(\sigma\) is unramified.

If \(v\in X\), we denote by \(\varphi_v\) a corresponding Frobenius substitution, by \(F_v=\varphi_v^{-1}\) a geometric Frobenius, by \(I_v\) the inertia group of the local Galois group at \(v\), and by \(\sigma_v\) the local representation defined by \(\sigma\). We put
\[
Z_v(\sigma,T)=\det(1-F_vT,\sigma_v^{I_v})^{-1}
\]
(the action of Frobenius on the inertia invariants), and
\[
Z(\sigma,T)=\prod_v Z_v(\sigma,T)\in E_\lambda[[T]].
\]

\emph{Example.} A rank-one \(\lambda\)-adic representation of \(K\) is the same thing as a homomorphism from the idèle class group of \(K\) to \(\cO_\lambda^\ast\), continuous for the discrete topology on \(\cO_\lambda^\ast\). We denote by \(\ell(n)\) the representation corresponding to \(x\mapsto |x|^{-n}\), \(n\in\Z\), and we put
\[
\sigma(n)=\sigma\otimes\ell(n).
\]

A theorem of Grothendieck says that if \(\sigma\) is a \(\lambda\)-adic representation of \(K\), then
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(Z(\sigma,T)\) is a rational function in \(E_\lambda(T)\);
\item it satisfies a functional equation
\[
Z(\sigma^\vee(1),T)=\eps(\sigma,T)Z(\sigma,T),
\]
where \(\eps(\sigma,T)\) is a monomial. I shall write
\[
\eps(\sigma,T)=q^{(1-g)\dim\sigma}\,\eps_0(\sigma,T),\qquad
\eps_0(\sigma)=\eps_0(\sigma,1);
\]
this quantity is a \(\lambda\)-adic unit;
\item if \(\sigma\) also denotes the \(\lambda\)-adic sheaf defined by \(\sigma\), then
\[
\eps(\sigma,T)=\det(-FT,H^\ast(\sigma))
=\prod_i\det(-FT,H^i(\sigma))^{(-1)^i}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

If \(S\) is a finite subset of \(X\), put
\[
Z_S(\sigma,T)=\prod_{v\notin S} Z_v(\sigma,T)
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eps_S(\sigma,T)
&=\eps(\sigma,T)\prod_{v\in S}
\det(-F_vT,\sigma_v^{I_v})^{-1}  \\
&=\text{the monomial asymptotic to }Z_S(\sigma,T)\text{ as }T\to\infty \\
&=\det(-FT,H^\ast(\sigma_S)),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\sigma_S\) is the \(\lambda\)-adic sheaf \(\sigma\), restricted to \(X-S\) and extended by \(0\) at the \(v\in S\). We also put
\[
\eps_{0,S}(\sigma,T)=q^{(g-1)\dim\sigma}\,\eps_S(\sigma,T).
\]

The conductors are defined by
\[
m_v(\sigma_S)=
\begin{cases}
\dim(\sigma)-\dim(\sigma_v^{I_v})
+\dim\Hom_{I_v}(\Sw_v,\sigma_v), & v\notin S,\\[4pt]
\dim(\sigma)+\dim\Hom_{I_v}(\Sw_v,\sigma_v), & v\in S,
\end{cases}
\]
where, for example,
\[
\dim\Hom_{I_v}(\Sw_v,\sigma_v)
=
\dim\Hom_{I_v}(\Sw_v,\sigma_v \bmod \lambda).
\]
Here \(\Sw_v\) is the Swan representation of a sufficiently large quotient of \(I_v\). We omit \(S\) from the notation when \(S=\varnothing\).

Now let \(L\) be a nonempty set of prime places of \(E\), and let \(S\) be a finite subset of \(X\). A compatible system of \(\lambda\)-adic representations \((\lambda\in L)\), with exceptional set \(S\), is the datum, for each \(\lambda\in L\), of a \(\lambda\)-adic representation \(\sigma_\lambda\) of \(K\), satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item each \(\sigma_\lambda\) is unramified outside \(S\);
\item for \(v\notin S\), \(Z_v(\sigma_\lambda,T)\) lies in \(E(T)\) and is independent of \(\lambda\in L\).
\end{enumerate}
We then put
\[
Z_v(\sigma,T)=Z_v(\sigma_\lambda,T)\quad(v\notin S),\qquad
Z_S(\sigma,T)=\prod_{v\notin S}Z_v(\sigma,T),
\]
and
\[
\eps_S(\sigma,T)=\eps_S(\sigma_\lambda,T).
\]
These quantities do not depend on \(\lambda\); indeed, \(\eps_S\) can be defined in terms of \(Z_S\).

\section*{2. (L.L.)}

We shall admit the following statement. Let \(\sigma\) and \(\rho\) be two complex representations of a finite quotient of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\). Assume that their conductors are disjoint. With the preceding notation, one then has
\[
\tag{L.L.}
\begin{aligned}
\eps_0(\sigma\otimes\rho,T)
&=\eps_0(\sigma,T)^{\dim\rho}\,
  \eps_0(\rho,T)^{\dim\sigma}  \\
&\quad\times
\prod_{v\in X}\det\sigma(F_v)^{-m_v(\rho)}
\prod_{v\in X}\det\rho(F_v)^{-m_v(\sigma)} .
\end{aligned}
\]

\section*{3. Variation of the constants}

\emph{Theorem.} Assume that \(L\) is infinite, and let \(\sigma\) and \(\rho\) be two compatible systems of \(\lambda\)-adic representations \((\lambda\in L)\), with disjoint exceptional sets. Then, for every \(\lambda\in L\),
\[
\tag{*}
\begin{aligned}
\eps_0(\sigma_\lambda\otimes\rho_\lambda,T)
&=\eps_0(\sigma_\lambda,T)^{\dim\rho}
  \eps_0(\rho_\lambda,T)^{\dim\sigma} \\
&\quad\times
\prod_{v\in X}\det\sigma_\lambda(F_v)^{-m_v(\rho_\lambda)}
\prod_{v\in X}\det\rho_\lambda(F_v)^{-m_v(\sigma_\lambda)},
\end{aligned}
\]
that is, the formula (L.L.) remains valid.

Let \(S\) and \(T\) be the disjoint exceptional sets for \(\sigma\) and \(\rho\).

\emph{Lemma 1.} For fixed \(\lambda\), formula \((*)\) is equivalent to
\[
\tag{\(*_{S,T}\)}
\begin{aligned}
\eps_{0,S\cup T}(\sigma_\lambda\otimes\rho_\lambda,T)
&=\eps_{0,S}(\sigma_\lambda,T)^{\dim\rho}
  \eps_{0,T}(\rho_\lambda,T)^{\dim\sigma} \\
&\quad\times
\prod_{v\in T}\det\sigma_\lambda(F_v)^{-m_v(\rho_\lambda)}
\prod_{v\in S}\det\rho_\lambda(F_v)^{-m_v(\sigma_\lambda)} .
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(A\) (respectively \(B\)) is the ratio of the two sides of \((*)\) (respectively of \((*)_{S,T}\)), the quotient \(A/B\) appears as a product of local terms; we shall show that all of them are \(1\). If \(v\in S\), one has to verify
\[
\det(-F_vT,\sigma_v^{I_v}\otimes\rho_v)
=
\det(-F_vT,\sigma_v^{I_v})^{\dim\rho}
\det\rho(F_v)^{\dim\sigma_v^{I_v}},
\]
which is immediate.

If \(\sigma\) is a representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on a finite-dimensional vector space over \(k_\lambda\), define \(Z(\sigma,T)\) and \(Z_S(\sigma,T)\) in \(k_\lambda[[T]]\) by the same formulae as above. By reduction modulo \(\lambda\), it is trivial that if \(\sigma\) is a representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on a finite free \(\cO_\lambda\)-module, with conductor contained in \(S\), and if \(\sigma/\lambda\) is the \(k_\lambda\)-representation obtained from it by reduction modulo \(\lambda\), then:

\emph{Lemma 2.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(Z_S(\sigma/\lambda,T)\) is a rational function; one denotes by
\[
q^{(g-1)\dim\sigma}\eps_{0,S}(\sigma/\lambda,T)
\]
the monomial asymptotic to \(Z(\sigma/\lambda,T)\) as \(T\to\infty\).
\item If \(\sigma\) and \(\rho\) are as above, with conductors contained in disjoint finite subsets \(S\) and \(T\) of \(X\), the following two conditions are equivalent:
\[
\tag{a}
\begin{aligned}
\eps_{0,S,T}(\sigma\otimes\rho,T)
&=\eps_{0,S}(\sigma,T)^{\dim\rho}
  \eps_{0,T}(\rho,T)^{\dim\sigma}  \\
&\quad\times
\prod \det\sigma(F_v)^{-m_v(\rho)}
\prod \det\rho(F_v)^{-m_v(\sigma)}
\quad(\bmod\ \lambda),
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\tag{b}
\begin{aligned}
\eps_{0,S,T}((\sigma/\lambda)\otimes(\rho/\lambda),T)
&=\eps_{0,S}(\sigma/\lambda,T)^{\dim\rho}
  \eps_{0,T}(\rho/\lambda,T)^{\dim\sigma}  \\
&\quad\times
\prod \det(\sigma/\lambda)(F_v)^{-m_v(\rho)}
\prod \det(\rho/\lambda)(F_v)^{-m_v(\sigma)}
\quad\text{in }k_\lambda .
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{enumerate}

We shall deduce from (L.L.) that:

\emph{Corollary 3.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If \(\sigma\) is a representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on a finite-dimensional \(k_\lambda\)-vector space, the functions \(Z_S(\sigma,T)\in k_\lambda[[T]]\) are rational.
\item If \(\sigma\) and \(\rho\) are two such representations, with conductors contained in disjoint finite sets \(S\) and \(T\), formula (a) is always valid.
\end{enumerate}

To prove (i), note that, after enlarging \(E\) and \(k_\lambda\) if necessary, Brauer's theorem says that \(\sigma\) lifts virtually to a representation of \(G\) on a finite-dimensional vector space over \(E\); one then applies 2.(i) to this representation. Assertion (ii) is proved similarly, from (L.L.) and Lemma 1. It follows that assertion 2.(ii)(a) is always true, and hence that \((*)_{S,T}\) is true modulo \(\lambda\).

The same technique of reduction to the case of ordinary \(L\)-series, or another method, shows that
\[
\deg_T\bigl(\eps_{0,S}(\sigma_\lambda,T)\bigr)
=
\dim(\sigma)(2-2g)-\sum_v m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)
\]
(the Ogg--Shafarevich--Grothendieck formula). The first member of this formula is independent of \(\lambda\in L\), so that
\[
\sum_v m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)
\]
is independent of \(\lambda\in L\). There is therefore an infinite subset \(L_1\) of \(L\) such that the integers \(m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)\) and \(m_v(\overline{\rho}_\lambda,T)\) are independent of \(\lambda\in L_1\). The two sides of \((*)_{S,T}\) are then independent of \(\lambda\in L_1\); since they are equal modulo \(\lambda\) for infinitely many \(\lambda\), they are equal for \(\lambda\in L_1\). To complete the proof, it remains only to verify:

\emph{Lemma 4.} Assume that \(L\) is infinite, and let \(\sigma\) be a compatible system of \(\lambda\)-adic representations, with exceptional set \(S\). Then the integers
\[
m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)
\]
are independent of \(\lambda\in L_1\).

We apply the preceding results to \(\sigma\) and to a finite-order character \(\chi\) of the idèle class group of \(K\), with values in a finite extension of \(E\), and with conductor \(T\) disjoint from \(S\). We already know which integers \(m_v\) -- namely the \(m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)\) for \(\lambda\in L_1\) -- satisfy
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eps_{0,S\cup T}(\sigma\otimes\chi,T)
&=\eps_{0,S}(\sigma,T)\,
  \eps_{0,T}(\chi,T)^{\dim\sigma} \\
&\quad\times
\prod_{v\in T}\det\sigma(F_v)^{-m_v(\chi)}
\prod_{v\in S}\chi(F_v)^{-m_v}.
\end{aligned}
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eps_{0,S\cup T}(\sigma\otimes\chi,T)
&=\eps_{0,S}(\sigma,T)\,
  \eps_{0,T}(\chi,T)^{\dim\sigma} \\
&\quad\times
\prod_{v\in T}\det\sigma(F_v)^{-m_v(\chi)}
\prod_{v\in S}\chi(F_v)^{-m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)}
\quad(\bmod\ \lambda).
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\prod_{v\in S}\chi(F_v)^{m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)-m_v}
=1\quad(\bmod\ \lambda).
\]
Since this is true for every \(\chi\), it follows that
\[
m_v(\overline{\sigma}_\lambda,S)=m_v,
\]
which completes the proof.

Yours sincerely,

\hfill P. Deligne
