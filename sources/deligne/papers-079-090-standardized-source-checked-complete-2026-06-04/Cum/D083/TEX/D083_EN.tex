\textbf{Abstract.} Except for a few corrections added in 2010, the article reproduces notes, sent to Nagata in the 1970s, which were a translation in the language of schemes of his articles [2], [3]. The proofs are a ``constructive'' version of his proofs. Making the proofs constructive allowed us to proceed without using valuations. A more detailed exposition and additional results are in Conrad [1]. Unless otherwise mentioned, all the schemes considered are supposed to be Noetherian.

\section*{0. Reminders on blow-ups}

\heading{0.1.}
If \(\mathfrak a\) is an ideal of the ring \(A\), the scheme obtained from \(X=\Spec(A)\) by blowing up \(\mathfrak a\) is the union of the open sets \(D(x)\), where \(x\) runs through a system of generators of \(\mathfrak a\), and \(D(x)\) is the spectrum of the subring \(A[\mathfrak a x^{-1}]\) of \(A[x^{-1}]\). Moreover,
\[
D(x)\cap (X-V(\mathfrak a))=\Spec(A[x^{-1}]).
\]
If \(\mathfrak c\) is a second ideal of \(A\), defining a subscheme \(V(\mathfrak c)\) of \(X\), the trace on \(D(x)\) of the proper transform of \(V(\mathfrak c)\) is defined by the ideal of \(A[\mathfrak a x^{-1}]\) obtained as the trace of the ideal of \(A[x^{-1}]\) generated by \(\mathfrak c\).

\begin{lemma*}[0.2]
Let \(X\) be a scheme, let \(\mathfrak a\) and \(\mathfrak b\) be two sheaves of ideals on \(X\), and let \(\widetilde X\) be the blow-up of \(X\) along \(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak b\). Then the open subset of \(\widetilde X\) which is the union of the \(D(x)\), for \(x\in\mathfrak b\), contains the proper transform of \(V(\mathfrak a)\).
\end{lemma*}
Indeed, \(\widetilde X\) is the union of the \(D(x)\), for \(x\in\mathfrak a\) or \(x\in\mathfrak b\), and, for \(x\in\mathfrak a\), \(D(x)\) is disjoint from the proper transform of \(V(\mathfrak a)\).

Lemma 0.3 of the original text was false. The corrected version below dates from March 1996. It brought about a change of notation. The original lemma used the following notation, to which the rest of the text refers: rather than \(F\) and \(G\), one considered their complements \(U\) and \(V\), which, with \(Y':=U\cap p^{-1}(Y)\), formed a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.7em,row sep=1.5em]
Y' \arrow[rr] \arrow[d] && Z \arrow[d,"p"] \\
U \arrow[r,hook] & V \arrow[r,hook] & X .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where \(Y'\) is closed in \(p^{-1}(V)\).

\begin{lemma*}[0.3]
Let \(p:Z\to X\), let \(F\supset G\) be closed subschemes of \(X\), and let \(Y\) be a subscheme of \(Z\). Assume that
\[
Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\subset p^{-1}(G)
\]
scheme-theoretically. Blow up \(X\) along \(G\), obtaining \(\widetilde X\), and let \(\widetilde p:\widetilde Z\to\widetilde X\) be deduced from \(p:Z\to X\) by base change. Then the closure \(\overline{Y-p^{-1}(G)}\) in \(\widetilde Z\) of \(Y-p^{-1}(G)\), and \(\widetilde p^{-1}(\overline{F-G})\) in \(\widetilde X\), are disjoint closed subsets of \(\widetilde Z\).
\end{lemma*}

We may assume that \(Z\) and \(X\) are affine. Let \(\mathfrak G\) be the ideal of \(G\). Cover \(\widetilde X\) by the opens \(D(g)\), for \(g\) in \(\mathfrak G\) (a system of generators of \(\mathfrak G\) suffices), and cover \(X-G\) by
\[
U(g)=D(g)-\text{(inverse image of }G\text{)}.
\]
Thus
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=4em,row sep=1.5em]
X-G \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,equal] & \widetilde X \arrow[d,equal] \\
\displaystyle\bigcup U(g) \arrow[r,hook] & \displaystyle\bigcup D(g).
\end{tikzcd}
\]
It is enough to see that, for each \(g\), the traces on \(\widetilde p^{-1}D(g)\) of \(\widetilde p^{-1}(\overline{F-G})\) and of \(\overline{Y-p^{-1}G}\) are disjoint. They are respectively the inverse image of the closure of \(F\cap U(g)\) in \(D(g)\), and the closure of \(Y\cap p^{-1}(U(g))\) in \(\widetilde p^{-1}D(g)\).

Let \(\widetilde{\ }\) denote an inverse image from \(X\) to \(Z\), or from \(D(g)\) to \(\widetilde p^{-1}D(g)\). Since \(p^{-1}G\supset Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\), the inverse image \(\widetilde g\) of \(g\) on \(Z\) can be written \(\widetilde g=y+f\), with \(y\) zero on \(Y\) and \(f\) zero on \(p^{-1}(F)\). There are functions \(a_i\) on \(Z\) and elements \(f_i\) in the ideal of \(F\) such that \(f=\sum a_i\widetilde f_i\). Since \(F\supset G\), the \(f_i\) lie in the ideal of \(G\) and the \(f_i/g\) are functions on \(D(g)\). They vanish on \(F\cap U(g)\), hence on \(\overline{F\cap U(g)}\) in \(D(g)\), and \(\sum a_i(f_i/g)^{\sim}\) is zero on \(\widetilde p^{-1}(\overline{F\cap U(g)})\). On \(p^{-1}U(g)\), one has
\[
1=y/g+f/g=y/g+\sum a_i(f_i/g)^{\sim},
\]
and the same sum is therefore equal to \(1\) on \(Y\cap p^{-1}U(g)\), hence on its closure in \(\widetilde p^{-1}D(g)\). This proves the required disjointness.

\heading{Remark.}
If one is instead given closed subsets \(F\supset G_0\) of \(X\), with \(G_0\) such that \(Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\) is set-theoretically contained in \(p^{-1}(G_0)\), then there is an \(r\) such that the \(r\)-th infinitesimal neighbourhood \(G\) of \(G_0\) in \(F\) satisfies the hypotheses of the lemma. If \(\mathfrak J\) is the ideal defining \((Y\cap p^{-1}(F))_{\red}\) in \(Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\), it is enough to take \(r\) such that \(\mathfrak J^{r+1}=0\). Thus the lemma makes it possible, by a blow-up which does not touch the complement \(V\) of \(G_0\), to ``separate'' the proper transform of \(F\) from \(\overline{Y-p^{-1}(G_0)}\) in \(\widetilde Z\).

\begin{lemma*}[0.4]
Let there be a commutative diagram of schemes
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.6em]
& X' \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,"p"'] & \overline{X}' \arrow[d,"q"] \\
U \arrow[ur] \arrow[r,hook] & X \arrow[r,hook] & \overline X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(U\) a dense open subset of \(\overline X\) and \(\overline X'\), with \(p\) and \(q\) separated of finite type, and with \(q^{-1}(X)=X'\). Let \(F\) be a closed subset of \(X\), and let \(G\) be a closed subset of \(\overline X'\) containing \(p^{-1}(F)\). Then there exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset\overline F-F\), such that, after base change by the blow-up morphism defined by \(\mathfrak a:\widetilde X\to\overline X\), and after replacing the new \(\overline X'\) by the closure of \(U\), one has
\[
q^{-1}(\overline F)\subset G.
\]
\end{lemma*}
In Nagata's language, to take the closure of \(F\) in the Zariski--Riemann space of \(X\) (``bubble space''), it is enough to take the ``intersection'' of the closures of \(F\) in the blow-ups of \(X\) with centre contained in \(\overline F-F\).

Apply Lemma 0.3 to
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.7em,row sep=1.6em]
\overline X'-q^*\overline F \arrow[rr,hook] \arrow[d] && \overline X'-G \arrow[d] \\
\overline X-\overline F \arrow[r,hook] & \overline X-(\overline F-F) \arrow[r,hook] & \overline X .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
One obtains \(\mathfrak a\), and, after the base change \(\widetilde X\to\overline X\), the new closure \(\overline F\) is disjoint from the image of the closure of the old \(\overline X'-q^*\overline F\) in \(\overline X'-G\); in particular, after replacing \(\widetilde X\) by \(\overline U\), this new closure is disjoint from the image of \(\overline X'-G\), as required.

\begin{lemma*}[0.5]
Let there be a diagram of separated finite type \(S\)-schemes
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.6em]
& X_i \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d] & \overline X_i \qquad (1\le i\le n) \\
U \arrow[ur] \arrow[r,hook] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(\overline X_i\) proper over \(S\), and \(U\) dense open in \(X\) and \(\overline X_i\). Let \(X^*\) be the closure of \(U\) in the product of the \(\overline X_i\). Finally, let \(F_i\) be a closed subset of \(X_i\); assume that the intersection of the inverse images of the \(F_i\) in \(X^*\) is empty. Then, after replacing \(\overline X_i\) by a blow-up with centre in \(\overline F_i-F_i\), one can obtain an analogous situation in which the intersection of the inverse images of the \(\overline F_i\) is empty.
\end{lemma*}

Let \(X^{**}\) be a blow-up of \(X^*\), \(X^{**}\xrightarrow{p_i}\overline X_i\), such that in \(X^{**}\) one has
\[
\bigcap_i \overline{p_i^{-1}(F_i)}=\varnothing
\]
(blow up \(\bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(F_i)\)). Apply Lemma 0.4 to the diagrams
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em,row sep=1.6em]
& p_i^{-1}(X_i) \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d] & X^{**} \arrow[d] \\
U \arrow[ur] \arrow[r,hook] & X_i \arrow[r,hook] & \overline X_i
\end{tikzcd}
\]
and to \(F_i\subset X_i\), \(G_i=p_i^{-1}(F_i)\). This gives the desired blow-up of the \(\overline X_i\). Indeed, in the new situation, in the closure of \(U\) in \(X^{**}\), one has
\[
\bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(\overline F_i)\subset \bigcap_i G_i=\varnothing.
\]
Since \(X^{**}\to X^*\) is surjective, this remains true in \(X^*\).

\section*{1. The theorems}

\begin{definition*}[1.1]
Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two \(S\)-schemes, with \(Y\) separated over \(S\). A quasi-domination \(f:X\to Y\) is a pair consisting of a dense open subset \(U\) of \(X\) and a morphism of \(S\)-schemes \(f:U\to Y\) whose graph is closed in \(X\times_S Y\).
\end{definition*}

Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two \(S\)-schemes, with \(Y\) separated over \(S\), let \(U\) be a (quasi-compact) open subset of \(X\), and let \(f:U\to Y\). We say that \(X\) is quasi-dominant (relative to \(U\) and \(f\)) over \(Y\) if there exists a quasi-domination of the schematic closure \(\overline U\) of \(U\), with values in \(Y\), extending \(f\). There is at most one such quasi-domination, whose graph is the closure of the graph of \(f\). A quasi-domination \(f\) is said to be proper (or complete) if the morphism \(f:U\to Y\) is proper.

\begin{theorem*}[1.2, {\normalfont [2, Theorem 3.2]}]
Let \(X\) and \(Y\) be two \(S\)-schemes and let \(U\subset V\subset X\) be two dense open subsets of \(X\). Assume that \(Y\) is separated and of finite type over \(S\). Let \(f:U\to Y\) be a quasi-domination of \(V\) into \(Y\). There exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-V\), such that the blow-up \(\widetilde X\) of \(X\) along \(V(\mathfrak a)\) quasi-dominates \(Y\).
\end{theorem*}

By the base change \(X\to S\), we are reduced to assuming \(X=S\). Then \(Y\) is a scheme over \(X\), \(f\) is a section of \(Y\) above \(U\), and, by hypothesis, \(\overline{f(U)}\) is closed above \(V\).

\textit{Case 1.} \(U=V\), \(p:Y\to X\) quasi-affine, \(X\) affine. By hypothesis there is a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.6em]
Y \arrow[rr] \arrow[dr] && E_X \arrow[dl] \\
& X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(E_X\) an affine-space type over \(X\). If \(\widetilde X\) quasi-dominates \(E_X\) (relative to \(f\)), then a fortiori \(X\) quasi-dominates \(Y\). We may therefore assume that \(Y=E_X\). A preliminary blow-up allows one to assume that \(X-U\) is a divisor \(D\). Locally on \(X\), \(f\) then has coordinates \((f_i u^{-n})\), where \(u\) is a local equation of \(D\), where \(f_i\) is regular, and where \(n\) is chosen sufficiently large once and for all. Let \(\mathfrak a\) be the ideal generated by \(u^n\) and the \(f_i\). After blowing up \(V(\mathfrak a)\), \(f\) extends to a section of the completed projective space of \(E_X\), and this proves the case.

\textit{Case 2.} \(U=V\), \(p\) quasi-affine. By Case 1, if \((U_i)_{0\le i\le n}\) is an affine covering of \(X\), then on \(U_i\) there exists a suitable ideal \(\mathfrak a_i\). This ideal has an extension \(\widetilde{\mathfrak a}_i\) on \(X\), with \(V(\widetilde{\mathfrak a}_i)\subset X-V\), and it is enough to blow up the product of the \(\widetilde{\mathfrak a}_i\).

\textit{Case 3.} \(p\) quasi-affine. By Lemma 0.3 applied to
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em,row sep=1.6em]
\overline{f(U)} \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d] & Y \arrow[d,"p"] \\
U \arrow[r,hook] & V \arrow[r,hook] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
a preliminary blow-up allows us to assume that \(p(\overline{f(U)})\cap (V-U)=\varnothing\). There is therefore a quasi-compact open \(X'\) of \(X\) with \(X'\cap V=U\) and \(p(\overline{f(U)})\subset X'\). By Case 2, there exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) on \(X'\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X'-U\), such that the blow-up \(\widetilde X'\) quasi-dominates \(Y\), that is, \(\overline{f(U)}\). It remains to extend \(\mathfrak a\) to an ideal on \(X\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-V\).

\textit{General case.} Let \((Y_i)\) be a finite covering of \(Y\) by affine opens, and put
\[
U_i=f^{-1}(Y_i),\qquad X_i=\overline{U_i},\qquad V_i=X_i\cap V.
\]
Case 3 applies to
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.6em]
&& Y_i\cap p^{-1}(X_i) \arrow[d] \\
U_i \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[urr] & V_i \arrow[r,hook] & X_i .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
and gives an ideal \(\mathfrak a_i\) on \(X_i\); this ideal defines an ideal \(\mathfrak a_i\) on \(X\), still with \(V(\mathfrak a_i)\subset X-V\). Blow up the product of the \(\mathfrak a_i\). The resulting blow-up \(q:\widetilde X\to X\) is such that \(q^{-1}(X_i)\) dominates the blow-up \(\widetilde X_i\). If \(X'_i=q^{-1}(X_i)\), there is therefore an open subset \(U'_i\) of \(X_i\), with \(U'_i\supset q^{-1}(U_i)\), such that, over \(X_i\), \(\overline{f(U_i)}\) is a section of \(f:U'_i\to Y\). It follows that, after blowing up,
\[
\overline{f(U)}=\bigcup_i f_i(U'_i).
\]
To prove Theorem 1.2, we may replace \(Y\) by \(\overline{f(U)}\), and conclude by Case 3 and the following lemma.

\begin{lemma*}[1.3]
\(\overline{f(U)}\) is quasi-affine over \(\widetilde X\).
\end{lemma*}
Indeed, \(\overline{f(U)}\) is quasi-finite over \(\widetilde X\).

\begin{corollary*}[1.4, a variant of Chow's lemma]
Let \(f:X\to S\) be a separated morphism of finite type. Then for every open \(U\), quasi-projective over \(S\) and dense in \(X\), there exists a diagram of \(S\)-schemes
\[
X \xleftarrow{q} X' \xrightarrow{j} \overline X
\]
where \(q\) is a blow-up morphism defined by an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) satisfying \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X'-U\), and where \(\overline X\) is projective over \(S\).
\end{corollary*}
In particular, \(q\) is projective and surjective and \(q^{-1}(U)\simeq U\).

Let \(U^*\) be a projective \(S\)-scheme containing \(U\) as a schematically dense open. By Theorem 1.2 applied to the inclusion of \(U\) in \(X\), one can choose \(U^*\) quasi-dominant over \(X\): there is a diagram
\[
\tag{1.4.1}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.5em]
U \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,equal] & V \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,"\varphi"] & U^* \\
U \arrow[r,hook] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
and here, since \(U^*\) is proper over \(S\), \(\varphi\) is proper.

\begin{lemma*}[1.5]
Let there be a diagram of separated finite type \(S\)-schemes
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.5em]
U \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,equal] & V \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,"\varphi"] & Y \\
U \arrow[r,hook] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(U\) dense in \(X\), dense in \(Y\), and schematically dense in \(V\), and with \(\varphi\) a quasi-domination of \(Y\) in \(X\). If \(\mathfrak a\) is an ideal of \(X\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-U\), such that the blow-up \(\widetilde X\) of \(X\) along \(\mathfrak a\) quasi-dominates \(V\) (relative to \(U\)), and if \(U\) is schematically dense in \(\widetilde X\), whence a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.5em]
U \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[dr] & W \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,"\psi"] & \widetilde X \\
& V
\end{tikzcd}
\]
then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \(\psi\) is the blow-up morphism of \(V\) along \(\varphi^*\mathfrak a\);
\item the graph of \(\psi\) is closed in \(\widetilde X\times_S Y\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma*}

Let \(\widetilde V\) be the blow-up of \(V\) along \(\varphi^*\mathfrak a\). By the universal property of \(\widetilde X\), there is a unique \(X\)-morphism from \(\widetilde V\) to \(\widetilde X\), call it \(\alpha\). By the universal property of \(\widetilde V\), there is a unique \(V\)-morphism from \(W\) to \(\widetilde V\), call it \(\beta\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.6em,row sep=1.6em]
& V \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\varphi"] & Y\\
U \arrow[ur] \arrow[r] \arrow[ddr] & W \arrow[r] \arrow[u,"\psi"] \arrow[d,"\beta"'] & \widetilde X\\
& \widetilde V \arrow[ur,"\alpha"']\\
& X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
By Theorem 1.2, by blowing up \(X_1\) without touching \(U\), we are reduced to the case in which \(X_1\) quasi-dominates \(X_2\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.5em]
& V \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[dd] & X_1 \\
U \arrow[ur] \arrow[dr] & {\phantom{X}} & {\phantom{X}} \\
& X_2
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Apply Lemma 1.5 to this diagram, in order to obtain
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.5em]
& W \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[dd,"\psi"] & X'_2 \\
U \arrow[ur] \arrow[dr] & {\phantom{X}} & {\phantom{X}} \\
& V \arrow[r,hook] & X_1
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where \(\psi\) is the blow-up morphism of an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) of \(V\) with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset V-U\), the graph of \(\psi\) is closed in \(X_1\times X'_2\), and \(X'_2\) is a blow-up of \(X_2\).

Let \(\mathfrak a'\) be an ideal of \(X_1\) extending \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a')\subset X_1-U\), and let \(q:X'_1\to X_1\) be the blow-up of \(X_1\) along \(\mathfrak a\). Then \(\psi\) induces an isomorphism between \(W\) and \(q^{-1}(V)\), and the graph of this isomorphism is closed in \(X'_1\times X'_2\). Define \(X\) by gluing \(X'_1\) and \(X'_2\) along this isomorphism.

\heading{Remark.}
The proof shows that one may take \(p_i:U_i\to X_i\) (\(U_i\subset X\)) to be a blow-up morphism of an ideal away from \(U\).

\begin{theorem*}[1.6, {\normalfont [2, Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.3]}]
Let \(f:X\to S\) be separated of finite type. There exists an immersion of \(X\) into an \(S\)-scheme proper over \(S\).
\end{theorem*}

The graph of \(\varphi\) is (trivially) proper over \(V\); the same is true of its inverse image on \(V\times_S\widetilde X\), and it follows that the graph of \(\psi\) is proper over \(V\), i.e. that \(\psi\) is proper. Thus \(\beta\) is proper, and has schematically dense image, since already \(U\) is schematically dense in \(\widetilde V\). On the other hand, \(\beta\) is a local section of \(\alpha\) on \(W\) (because \(\beta|_U\) is one on \(U\)), hence \(\beta\) is an isomorphism.

By hypothesis, the graph of \(\psi\) is closed in \(V\times_S\widetilde X\), and is contained in the inverse image of the graph of \(\varphi\), which is closed in \(Y\times X\); hence \(\Gamma(\psi)\) is closed in \(Y\times\widetilde X\).

Apply Lemma 1.5 to (1.4.1). There exists an ideal \(\mathfrak a\) on \(X\), with \(V(\mathfrak a)\subset X-U\), such that the blow-up \(\widetilde X\) quasi-dominates \(V\). One can moreover arrange that \(U\) be schematically dense in \(\widetilde X\). By Lemma 1.5, and because \(\varphi\) is proper, \(\widetilde X\) is therefore the blow-up of \(V\) along \(\varphi^*\mathfrak a\). If \(\mathfrak a'\) is an ideal of \(U^*\) extending \(\mathfrak a\), with \(V(\mathfrak a')\subset U^*-U\), one then takes
\[
X'=\widetilde X,
\qquad
\overline X=\widetilde{U^*}.
\]

\begin{lemma*}[1.7, {\normalfont [2, Lemma 4.2]}]
Let there be a diagram of separated finite type \(S\)-schemes
\[
X_2\xleftarrow{} U \xrightarrow{} X_1
\]
with \(U\) a dense open subset of the \(X_i\). There exist \(j:U\to X\), with \(X\) separated of finite type over \(S\) and \(U\) schematically dense in \(X\), and proper quasi-dominations \(p_i:X\to X_i\).
\end{lemma*}

Let \(U_i\) be a finite open covering of \(X\) by dense quasi-projective opens. For each \(U_i\), there exists, see Corollary 1.4, a commutative diagram of \(S\)-schemes
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.4em,row sep=1.5em]
& X'_i \arrow[r,"j_i"] \arrow[d,"q_i"'] & \overline X_i \\
U_i \arrow[ur] \arrow[r] & X_i
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(q_i\) proper, \(\overline X_i\) proper over \(S\), and \(U_i\) dense in \(\overline X_i\). Put \(F_i=\overline X_i-U_i\) and \(F'_i=q_i^{-1}(F_i)\). Finally, let \(X^*\) be the closure of \(U\) in the product of the \(\overline X_i\):
\[
p_i:X^*\longrightarrow\overline X_i.
\]
By Lemma 0.5, a preliminary blow-up of \(\overline X_i\), not touching \(X'_i\), allows us to assume that
\[
\bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(F'_i)=\varnothing.
\]
Let \(M_i\) be the separated \(S\)-scheme obtained by gluing \(X\) and \(\overline X_i-F'_i\) along \(U_i\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em,row sep=1.4em]
& \overline X_i-F'_i \arrow[d] \\
X \arrow[r,hook] & M_i .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Apply Lemma 1.7 to \(X\) and the \(M_i\), to obtain \(X\to M\), where \(M\) properly quasi-dominates each of the \(M_i\). It remains to prove that \(M\) is proper over \(S\). Indeed, one has maps
\[
X^*-p_i^{-1}(F'_i)\longrightarrow M_i
\]
which glue over \(U=\bigcap_iU_i\). Replacing \(X^*\) by a blow-up \(X^{**}\), one obtains maps
\[
X^{**}-p_i^{-1}(F'_i)\longrightarrow M
\]
which glue; this gives a map from the schematic closure of \(U\) in \(X^{**}\) to \(M\). The scheme \(M\), being the image of this map, is proper over \(S\).

\section*{Comments}

I hope that, by being still more constructive, one could prove Theorem 1.6 for \(f:X\to S\) separated of finite type, with \(S\) quasi-compact and quasi-separated. The rules to follow are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item consider only quasi-compact opens and closed subsets defined by ideals of finite type;
\item never take schematic closures (one would leave (a)), but only take a closed subset containing the given part and sufficiently small for the later constructions;
\item do not speak of dense quasi-projective opens (cf. (b)). Remember that, to construct such opens, one could start from an affine covering \(U_i\), and take
\[
U=\bigcup_i\left(U_i-\bigcup_{j<i}U_j\right).
\]
Translate the arguments in terms of the original \(U_i\), and apply (a) + (b).
\end{enumerate}
On the other hand, I have no idea in the case of algebraic spaces.
