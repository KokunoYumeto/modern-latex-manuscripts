We now fix a class \(\mathcal E\) of open embeddings \(U\to X\) in \((QP/G)\). We require the following stability properties.

\begin{definition*}[Conditions 34]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item If \(U\to U'\to X\) are open embeddings and \(U\to X\) is in \(\mathcal E\), then \(U'\to X\) is in \(\mathcal E\).
\item If \(U\to X\) is an open embedding in \(\mathcal E\), and if \(f:X'\to X\) is étale, inducing an isomorphism from \(f^{-1}(Z)\) to \(Z\), where \(Z\) is the complement of \(U\) in \(X\), then \(f^{-1}(U)\to X'\) is in \(\mathcal E\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition*}

The classes \(\mathcal E\) that will be used are the following: the open embeddings \(U\to X\) with \(X\) smooth; the open embeddings \(U\to X\) with \(X\) smooth and the action of \(G\) free on \(X-U\), equivalently \(X\) is the union of \(U\) and the open subset on which the action of \(G\) is free; and, when working in \((Sch/S)\), all open embeddings.

\begin{definition*}[7]
A morphism \(f:F\to G\) is \(\mathcal E\)-solid if it is a composite
\[
F=F_0\longrightarrow F_1\longrightarrow \cdots\longrightarrow F_n=G
\]
where each \(F_i\to F_{i+1}\) is deduced by push-out from some \(h_U\to h_X\), with \(U\subset X\) in \(\mathcal E\). A sheaf \(F\) is solid if \(\emptyset\to F\) is \(\mathcal E\)-solid. In the pointed context, a pointed sheaf is pointed \(\mathcal E\)-solid if the morphism \(\pt\to F\) is \(\mathcal E\)-solid.
\end{definition*}

\begin{example*}[1]
For \(U\) open in \(X\), let \(h_X/h_U\) be the sheaf \(h_X\) with the subsheaf \(h_U\) contracted to the base point. If \(U\to X\) is in \(\mathcal E\), then \(\pt\to h_X/h_U\) is solid: it is the push-out of \(h_U\to h_X\) by \(h_U\to\pt\). Thom spaces are of this form: from a vector bundle \(E\) on \(T\), one contracts, in the total space of \(E\), the complement of the zero section to a point.
\end{example*}

The class of solid morphisms is the smallest class, closed under compositions and push-outs, which contains all \(h_U\to h_X\), \(U\subset X\) in \(\mathcal E\). By Proposition 33, solid morphisms are open.

For a monomorphism \(F\to G\) of sheaves, define \(G/F\) to be the pointed sheaf obtained by contracting \(F\) to a point. Thus one has a cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.6em,row sep=1.6em]
F \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & G \arrow[d]\\
\pt \arrow[r] & G/F .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
By transitivity of push-outs, any cocartesian diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.6em,row sep=1.6em]
F \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & G \arrow[d]\\
F' \arrow[r] & G'
\end{tikzcd}
\]
induces an isomorphism \(G/F\simeq G'/F'\).

\begin{proposition*}[35]
A morphism of sheaves \(f:F\to G\) is \(\mathcal E\)-solid if and only if it is a composite
\[
F=F_0\longrightarrow F_1\longrightarrow \cdots\longrightarrow F_n=G
\]
of monomorphisms such that each \(F_{i+1}/F_i\) is isomorphic to some \(h_X/h_U\), with \(U\subset X\) in \(\mathcal E\).
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
If a morphism \(F\to G\) is deduced by push-out from \(U\to X\), then \(G/F\) is isomorphic to \(h_X/h_U\). This gives the ``only if'' direction.

Conversely, suppose first that we have a cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
F \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & G \arrow[d]\\
\pt \arrow[r] & h_X/h_U
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{35.1}
\]
and that the natural map \(h_X\to h_X/h_U\) lifts to \(G\). Then \(F\to G\) is obtained by push-out from \(h_U\to h_X\). Indeed, (35.1) is cartesian as well as cocartesian, so that we have a cartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
h_U \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_X \arrow[d]\\
F \arrow[r] & G .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
If \(G'\) is obtained from \(F\) by push-out from \(h_U\to h_X\),
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.3em,row sep=1.6em]
h_U \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_X \arrow[d] \arrow[r,equal] & h_X \arrow[d]\\
F \arrow[r] & G' \arrow[r] & G ,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
then \(G'/F\simeq G/F\), and it follows that \(G'\simeq G\).

It is enough, more generally, to have an étale map \(v:\widetilde X\to X\), inducing an isomorphism from \(\widetilde X-v^{-1}(U)\) to \(X-U\), together with a lifting of \(h_{\widetilde X}\to h_X/h_U\) to \(G\). If \(\widetilde U=v^{-1}(U)\), then \(h_{\widetilde X}/h_{\widetilde U}\to h_X/h_U\) is an isomorphism. This is checked most directly on the fibre functors defined by \(G\)-local henselian schemes: a morphism \(T\to X\) which does not map to \(U\) lifts uniquely to \(\widetilde X\). Under these assumptions \(F\to G\) is a push-out of \(h_{\widetilde U}\to h_{\widetilde X}\). By the second stability property of \(\mathcal E\), the open embedding \(\widetilde U\to \widetilde X\) is in \(\mathcal E\).

We reduce the general case to this one. Suppose \(f:F\to G\) is a monomorphism with \(G/F\simeq h_X/h_U\), \(U\to X\) in \(\mathcal E\). The cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
F \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & G \arrow[d]\\
\pt \arrow[r] & G/F
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{35.2}
\]
gives an epimorphism \(\pt\amalg G\to h_X/h_U\). The natural section of \(h_X/h_U\) on \(X\) can therefore be lifted locally either to \(\pt\) or to \(G\). Thus, for a filtration
\[
\emptyset=C_{n+1}\subset C_n\subset\cdots\subset C_1\subset C_0=X
\]
by closed equivariant subschemes, there are étale maps \(T_i\to X\), with equivariant sections over \(C_i-C_{i+1}\), and liftings of \(h_{T_i}\to h_X/h_U\) to \(\pt\) or to \(G\). We may start with \(X_0=U\), where the lifting is to \(\pt\), and then shrink the \(T_i\) so that the succeeding liftings are to \(G\) and induce isomorphisms over the strata.

Since \(F\to G\) is a monomorphism, (35.2) is also cartesian. Pulling back the composite
\[
\pt\longrightarrow h_{X_1}/h_U\longrightarrow h_{X_2}/h_U\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow h_X/h_U
\]
gives a factorization of \(F\to G\) in which each quotient is of the form \(h_{T_i}/h_{T_i'}\), and the maps \(h_{T_i}\to h_X/h_U\) lift to the intermediate sheaves. Hence each step is a push-out of an open embedding in \(\mathcal E\), and solidity follows.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{remark*}[1]
Equivalently, \(F\to G\) is \(\mathcal E\)-solid if and only if the pointed sheaf \(G/F\) is an iterated extension of sheaves \(h_X/h_U\), with \(U\to X\) in \(\mathcal E\): there are morphisms
\[
\pt=E_0\to E_1\to\cdots\to E_n=G/F
\]
with each \(E_{i+1}/E_i\) of the form \(h_X/h_U\).
\end{remark*}

\begin{proposition*}[36]
If \(f:F\to G\) is open and \(G\) is \(\mathcal E\)-solid, then \(f\) is \(\mathcal E\)-solid.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
We say ``solid'' for \(\mathcal E\)-solid. Let \((*)\) be the property of a sheaf \(G\) that every open \(F\to G\) is solid. If \(G\) is solid, it lies at the end of a chain
\[
\emptyset=G_0\to G_1\to\cdots\to G_n=G
\]
in which each \(G_i\to G_{i+1}\) is a push-out of some \(h_U\to h_X\), \(U\subset X\) in \(\mathcal E\). We prove by induction on \(n\) that \(G_i\) satisfies \((*)\).

For \(n=0\), \(G_1=h_X\) is representable and \(\emptyset\to X\) is in \(\mathcal E\). If \(F\to h_X\) is open, it is \(h_U\to h_X\) for \(U\) open in \(X\), hence solid by Condition 34(1).

It remains to check the induction step. In a cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
h_U \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_X \arrow[d]\\
G' \arrow[r] & G
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{36.1}
\]
the map \(h_U\to h_X\) is a monomorphism, and the square is therefore cartesian as well as cocartesian. Let \(F\to G\) be open. Pull back (36.1) by \(F\to G\). We obtain a cartesian and cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
h_V \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_Y \arrow[d]\\
F' \arrow[r] & F
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{36.2}
\]
where \(Y\) is open in \(X\) and \(V=U\cap Y\). The diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.6em,row sep=1.55em]
F' \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & F \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_Y/h_V \arrow[d]\\
G' \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & G \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_X/h_U \arrow[d]\\
G'/F' \arrow[r] & G/F \arrow[r] & h_X/(h_U\cup h_Y)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
expresses \(G/F\) as an extension of \(h_X/h_{U\cup Y}\) by \(G'/F'\). By the induction hypothesis applied to \(G'\), and by Remark 1, it follows that \(F\to G\) is solid. Here \(U\cup Y\to X\) lies in \(\mathcal E\).
\end{proofdel}

\begin{proposition*}[37]
The pull-back of a solid morphism by an open morphism is solid. In particular, if \(F\to G\) is open and \(G\) is solid, then \(F\) is solid.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
Since the pull-back of an open morphism is open, it suffices to check the assertion for a solid morphism obtained as a push-out of \(h_U\subset h_X\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
h_U \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_X \arrow[d]\\
G' \arrow[r] & G .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
After pulling back by an open morphism \(F\to G\), we obtain a cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
h_{U'} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_{X'} \arrow[d]\\
F' \arrow[r] & F
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(U'\) open in \(U\) and \(X'\) open in \(X\). This shows that \(F'\to F\) is solid.
\end{proofdel}

Suppose now that two classes \(\mathcal E\) and \(\mathcal E'\) of open embeddings satisfying Conditions 34 are given. Define \(\mathcal E\times\mathcal E'\) to be the smallest class, stable under Conditions 34, containing the open embeddings
\[
(U\times X')\cup(X\times U')\subset X\times X',
\]
for \(U\subset X\) in \(\mathcal E\) and \(U'\subset X'\) in \(\mathcal E'\).

\begin{example*}[2]
If \(\mathcal E\) is the class of all open embeddings and \(\mathcal E'\) is the class of those \(U'\subset X'\) for which \(G\) acts freely outside \(U'\), then \(\mathcal E\times\mathcal E'=\mathcal E'\).
\end{example*}

\begin{proposition*}[38]
If the pointed sheaves \(F\) and \(F'\) are respectively \(\mathcal E\)- and \(\mathcal E'\)-solid, then \(F\wedge F'\) is \(\mathcal E\times\mathcal E'\)-solid.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
By assumption, \(F\) is an iterated extension, in the sense of Remark 1, of pointed sheaves \(h_{X_i}/h_{U_i}\), \(U_i\subset X_i\) in \(\mathcal E\). Similarly \(F'\) is built from \(h_{X'_j}/h_{U'_j}\), \(U'_j\subset X'_j\) in \(\mathcal E'\). The smash product \(F\wedge F'\) is then an iterated extension, for instance in lexicographic order, of
\[
(h_{X_i}/h_{U_i})\wedge(h_{X'_j}/h_{U'_j})
\simeq
h_{X_i\times X'_j}\big/
h_{(U_i\times X'_j)\cup(X_i\times U'_j)}.
\]
Hence it is \(\mathcal E\times\mathcal E'\)-solid.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{definition*}[8]
A morphism is called ind-solid relative to \(\mathcal E\) if it is a filtered colimit of \(\mathcal E\)-solid morphisms.
\end{definition*}

\heading{Exercise 39.}
Take \(G\) to be the trivial group. A section on \(T\) of a push-out
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
h_U \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_X \arrow[d]\\
F \arrow[r] & G
\end{tikzcd}
\]
can be described as follows. For an open subset \(V\) of \(T\) and a section \(s\) of \(F\) on \(V\), consider on the small Nisnevich site \(T_{\Nis}\) the presheaf \(a(V,s)\) which sends \(Y\to T\) to the set of morphisms \(f:Y\to X\) such that \(f^{-1}(U)=Y^{-1}(V)\) and such that the restriction is compatible with \(s\). A section of \(G\) on \(T\) is given by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item an open subset \(V\) of \(T\);
\item a section \(s\) of \(F\) on \(V\);
\item a section of \(i_*(a(V,s)_{\Nis})\) on \(T-V\), where \(i:T-V\to T\) is the closed embedding.
\end{enumerate}

\heading{Exercise 40.}
In the notation of Exercise 39, if \(F\) is a sheaf for the étale topology, so is \(G\). For fixed \((V,s)\), the datum in (3) is an étale sheaf. This follows from the usual criterion for a Nisnevich sheaf to be étale: for \(x\in T\) and for a finite separable extension \(K/k(x)\), after extension of the residue field from the henselization \(\mathcal O^h_{T,x}\), the corresponding functor of étale neighbourhoods must be an étale sheaf on \(\Spec k(x)\).

\heading{Exercise 41.}
It follows from Exercises 39 and 40 that if \(f:F\to G\) is ind-solid, and if \(F\) is étale, then \(G\) is étale. In particular, solid sheaves, as well as pointed solid sheaves, are étale sheaves.

The same formalism of open and solid morphisms holds in the site of all schemes of finite type over \(S\), with the étale topology.
\end{remark*}

\subsection*{4.2 A criterion for preservation of local equivalences}

We work with pointed sheaves on \(QP/G\). The aim of this section is to prove the following result.

\begin{theorem*}[6]
Let \(a\) be a functor from pointed sheaves to pointed sets. Suppose that \(a\) commutes with all colimits, and that for every open embedding \(U\to X\), the map
\[
a((h_U)_+)\longrightarrow a((h_X)_+)
\]
is a monomorphism. If \(f:F_\bullet\to G_\bullet\) is a local equivalence and the terms \(F_n\) and \(G_n\) are pointed ind-solid, then \(a(f)\) is a weak equivalence.
\end{theorem*}

Suppose that
\[
Q=\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em,row sep=1.6em,baseline=(current bounding box.center)]
B \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & Y \arrow[d]\\
A \arrow[r] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is an upper distinguished square. Adding a base point, we obtain \(Q_+\). The morphism \(K_Q\to X_+\) is a local equivalence. Let us check that \(a(K_Q)\to a(X_+)\) is a weak equivalence. Since \(a\) commutes with coproducts, this morphism is obtained from the commutative square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.6em,row sep=1.8em]
a((h_B)_+) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & a((h_Y)_+) \arrow[d]\\
a((h_A)_+) \arrow[r] & a((h_X)_+)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
by applying the same construction as in (23.3). The square is cocartesian because \(Q\) is. The top horizontal map is a monomorphism, hence the square is homotopy cocartesian, and the claim follows. Since \(a\) commutes with colimits, this special case implies the following.

\begin{lemma*}[15]
If \(f\) is in the \(\bar\Delta\)-closure of the morphisms \((K_Q)_+\to X_+\) as above, then \(a(f)\) is a weak equivalence.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proposition*}[42]
Proof of Theorem 6.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
For any pointed sheaf \(F\), the morphism \(S(F)\to F\) is a local equivalence: for every connected \(X\in QP/G\), \(S(F)(X)\to F(X)\) is a weak equivalence by Construction 29. Hence, for a local equivalence \(f:F\to G\), the square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
S(F) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & S(G) \arrow[d]\\
F \arrow[r] & G
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is a commutative square of local equivalences. By Lemmas 15 and 12, \(a(S(f))\) is a weak equivalence. It remains to prove that \(a(S(F))\to a(F)\) is a weak equivalence, and similarly for \(G\). Since \(a\) and \(S\) commute with filtered colimits, ind-solid reduces to solid, and by the inductive definition of solidity it suffices to prove the following lemma.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{lemma*}[16]
Let \(U\to X\) be an open embedding. Suppose that in a cartesian square of pointed sheaves
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
(h_U)_+ \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & (h_X)_+ \arrow[d]\\
F \arrow[r] & G
\end{tikzcd}
\]
the sheaf \(F\) is such that \(a(S(F))\to a(F)\) is a weak equivalence. Then the same holds for \(G\).
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proofdel}
Consider the cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
S((h_U)_+) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & S((h_X)_+) \arrow[d]\\
S(F) \arrow[r] & S(G).
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The top morphism is a monomorphism. It follows that this square is pointwise homotopy cocartesian, and \(S\to S(G)\) is a local equivalence. The functor \(i^*i_*\) of Construction 29 sends a monomorphism to a coprojection of the form \(F\to F\amalg\coprod(h_{U_\alpha})_+\). Consequently each \(S_n\) is of the form \((\coprod h_{U_\alpha})_+\), and by Lemmas 15 and 12 the map \(a(S)\to a(S(G))\) is a weak equivalence. It remains to show that \(a(S)\to a(G)\) is a weak equivalence.

Apply \(a\) to the morphism from the square just displayed to the original cartesian square. By Construction 29, both
\[
S((h_U)_+)\to(h_U)_+,\qquad S((h_X)_+)\to(h_X)_+
\]
are homotopy equivalences and remain so after applying \(a\). We assumed \(a(S(F))\to a(F)\) to be a weak equivalence. Since \(a\) sends both squares to cocartesian squares with top morphisms monomorphisms, it follows that \(a(S)\to a(G)\) is a weak equivalence. Hence so is \(a(S(G))\to a(G)\).
\end{proofdel}

\section*{5 Two functors}

\subsection*{5.1 The functor \(X\mapsto X/G\)}

There is a natural morphism of sites
\[
p:(QP/G)_{\Nis}\longrightarrow (QP)_{\Nis}
\]
given by the functor
\[
p^*:\ QP\longrightarrow QP/G,\qquad X\longmapsto (X\hbox{ with the trivial }G\hbox{-action}).
\]
Indeed \(p^*\) commutes with finite limits and sends covering families to covering families. In particular it is continuous: if \(F\) is a sheaf on \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\), the presheaf
\[
X\longmapsto F(X\hbox{ with trivial }G\hbox{-action})
\]
is a sheaf on \((QP)_{\Nis}\). The functor \(p^*\) has a left adjoint
\[
q:\ QP/G\longrightarrow QP,\qquad X\longmapsto X/G .
\]
As \(p^*\) is continuous, the functor \(q\) is cocontinuous, and the inverse-image functor \(p^*\) from sheaves on \((QP)_{\Nis}\) to sheaves on \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\) is
\[
F\longmapsto \bigl(\hbox{sheaf associated to }X\longmapsto F(X/G)\bigr).
\]

\begin{proposition*}[43]
The cocontinuous functor \(q:X\mapsto X/G\) is also continuous. Equivalently, if \(F\) is a sheaf on \(QP\), then the presheaf \(X\mapsto F(X/G)\) on \(QP/G\) is a sheaf.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
By Lemma 2 it is enough to test the sheaf property of \(X\mapsto F(X/G)\) for a covering of \(X\) obtained by pull-back from a Nisnevich covering \(U_i\to X/G\). Passage to quotients commutes with flat base change. Taking \(X/G\) as base, this identifies \(X\times_{X/G}U_i\to U_i\) with the quotient, by \(G\), equal to \(U_i\). The same holds for multiple intersections. Thus the sheaf property for the covering of \(X\) by the \(X\times_{X/G}U_i\) is reduced to the sheaf property of \(F\) for the covering \((U_i)\) of \(X/G\).
\end{proofdel}

The functor \(q:X\mapsto X/G\) gives rise to a pair of adjoint functors between the categories of presheaves on \(QP/G\) and \(QP\). Since \(q\) is continuous, it induces a similar pair on sheaves. We write this pair as
\[
(p_\#,p^*),
\]
so that one has a sequence of adjunctions \((p_\#,p^*,p_*)\). If \(F\) on \(QP/G\) is representable, \(F=h_X\), then \(p_\#F=h_{X/G}\). In particular, \(p_\#\) sends the final sheaf, or point, on \(QP/G\) to the final sheaf on \(QP\), and \((p_\#,p^*)\) is an adjoint pair for pointed sheaves as well. It is clear that \(p_\#\) sends solid sheaves to solid sheaves. We also have the following.

\begin{proposition*}[44]
Let \(F\) be a pointed sheaf which is solid with respect to open embeddings \(U\subset X\) of smooth schemes such that the action of \(G\) on \(X\) is free outside \(U\). Then \(p_\#(F)\) is solid with respect to open embeddings of smooth schemes.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
If \(X'\) is the open subset of \(X\) where the action of \(G\) is free, then \(U\cup X'\to X\) is in the same class, and, putting \(U'=U\cap X'\), a push-out of \(U\to X\) is also a push-out of \(U'\to X'\). Thus we may suppose that the action is free everywhere. Since \(p_\#\) is a left adjoint, it preserves colimits and in particular push-outs. It sends \(h_U\) to \(h_{U/G}\). Hence the assertion is reduced to the following lemma.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{lemma*}[17]
If \(G\) acts freely on \(U\), smooth over \(S\), then \(U/G\) is smooth.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proofdel}
If \(G\) is finite étale, for instance \(S_n\), the case most relevant here, this is clear, since \(U\to U/G\) is étale. In general, the assumption that \(G\) acts freely on \(U\) implies that \(U\) is a \(G\)-torsor over \(U/G\). In particular \(U\to U/G\) is faithfully flat. Since \(U\) is flat over \(S\), this forces \(U/G\) to be flat over \(S\). To check smoothness of \(U/G\) over \(S\), it suffices to check it geometric fibre by geometric fibre. For a geometric point \(\bar s\) of \(S\), smoothness of \((U/G)_{\bar s}\) amounts to regularity. Since \(U_{\bar s}\) is smooth over \(\bar s\), hence regular, and \(U_{\bar s}\to(U/G)_{\bar s}\) is faithfully flat, this is [4], an application of Serre's cohomological criterion for regularity.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{proposition*}[45]
The functor \(p_\#\) respects local, respectively \(\Aone\)-, equivalences between termwise ind-solid simplicial sheaves.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
Let \(f:F\to F'\) be a local equivalence between termwise ind-solid simplicial sheaves on \(QP/G\). To verify that \(p_\#(f)\) is a local equivalence, it is enough to check that for every \(X\in QP\) and \(x\in X\) the map
\[
p_\#(F)(\Spec\mathcal O^h_{X,x})\longrightarrow
p_\#(F')(\Spec\mathcal O^h_{X,x})
\]
is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets. Since \(p_\#\) is a left adjoint, the functor
\[
F\longmapsto p_\#(F)(\Spec\mathcal O^h_{X,x})
\tag{45.1}
\]
commutes with colimits. For an open embedding \(U\to X\) in \(QP/G\), the map \(U/G\to X/G\) is again an open embedding, and Theorem 6 applies to (45.1).

Let \(f:F\to F'\) be an \(\Aone\)-equivalence. Consider the square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
S(F) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & S(F') \arrow[d]\\
F \arrow[r] & F' .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
By the first part of the proposition, \(p_\#\) takes the vertical arrows to local equivalences. Since \(p_\#\) commutes with colimits, Lemma 13 implies that \(p_\#(S(f))\) is in the \(\bar\Delta\)-closure of the class containing the \(p_\#((K_Q)_+\to X_+)\), for \(Q\) upper distinguished, and the \(p_\#((X\times\Aone)_+\to X_+)\), for \(X\in QP/G\). By Theorem 4 it remains to prove that morphisms of these two types are \(\Aone\)-equivalences. For the first type this follows from the first half of the proposition. For the second type, it follows from the fact that
\[
p_\#((X\times\Aone)_+)\longrightarrow p_\#(X_+)
\]
and
\[
p_\#(X_+)\times\Aone\longrightarrow p_\#((X\times\Aone)_+)
\]
are mutually inverse \(\Aone\)-homotopy equivalences.
\end{proofdel}

Define
\[
Lp_\#:\Ho_{A^1}(QP/G)\longrightarrow \Ho_{A^1}(QP)
\]
(and similarly in the pointed setting) by
\[
Lp_\#(F):=p_\#(S(F)),
\]
where \(S(F)\) is Construction 29. Proposition 45 shows that \(Lp_\#\) is well-defined, and that, for a termwise ind-solid \(F\), one has \(Lp_\#(F)\simeq p_\#(F)\).

\subsection*{5.2 The functor \(X\mapsto X^T\)}

As in Sect. 3.1, we fix \(G\) and \(S\). We also fix \(T\in QP/G\), finite and flat over \(S\).

For a presheaf \(F\) on \(QP/G\), define \(F^T\) to be the internal Hom object \(\underline{\Hom}(h_T,F)\). Its value on \(U\) is
\[
F^T(U)=F(U\times_S T).
\]
If \(F\) is a sheaf, so is \(F^T\).

\begin{example*}[6]
Take \(G\) and \(T\) deduced from the finite group \(S_n\) acting on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) by permutations. If \(F\) is represented by \(X\), with trivial \(S_n\)-action, then \(F^T\) is represented by \(X^n\), on which \(S_n\) acts by permutation of the factors.
\end{example*}

\begin{remark*}[1]
If \(F\) is representable, and represented by \(X\) smooth over \(S\), then \(F^T\) is representable, and represented by a smooth \(G\)-scheme. More precisely, if \(F\) is represented by \(X\in QP/G\), consider the contravariant functor on \(Sch/S\) of morphisms of schemes from \(T\) to \(X\),
\[
U\longmapsto \Hom_S(T\times_S U,\; X\times_S U).
\]
This functor is represented by a scheme \(Y\), quasi-projective over \(S\) and smooth if \(X\) is smooth. The scheme \(Y\) carries the evident action of \(G\), and \((Y,G)\) represents \(F^T\). Indeed, by attaching to a morphism \(T\to X\) its graph, one maps the functor into the Hilbert functor of finite subschemes of \(T\times_S X\) of the same rank as \(T\). This Hilbert functor is represented by a quasi-projective Hilbert scheme. The condition that a finite flat subscheme be the graph of a morphism from \(T\) to \(X\) is an open condition; it gives the required open subscheme \(Y\). If \(X\) is smooth, smoothness of \(Y\) follows from the infinitesimal lifting criterion. The \(G\)-action is the evident one. For \(Z\in QP/G\) one has the chain of identifications
\[
\Hom_{QP/G}(Z,Y)
\simeq \Hom_S(Z,\Hom(T,X))
\simeq \Hom_S(Z\times_S T,X)
\simeq F^T(Z).
\]
\end{remark*}

Let \(\mathcal E\) be a class of open embeddings in \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\). We will simply say ``solid'' for \(\mathcal E\)-solid.

\begin{theorem*}[7]
If \(F\) is a solid sheaf on \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\), then \(F^T\) is solid.
\end{theorem*}

If \(A\to F\) is open, that is, relatively representable by open equivariant embeddings, there is a natural sequence of sheaves between \(A^T\) and \(F^T\). In the situation of Example 6, and for \(h_U\to h_X\), these are represented by the open equivariant subschemes of \(X^n\) consisting of the \(n\)-tuples \((x_1,\ldots,x_n)\) for which at least \(i\) of the \(x_j\) lie in \(U\).

Formally, a section of \(F^T\) over \(Z\) is a section \(s\) of \(F\) over \(T\times_S Z\). Let \(U(s)\) be the equivariant open subscheme of \(T\times_S Z\) on which \(s\) lies in \(A\). The sheaf \((F,A)^T_i\) is the subsheaf of \(F^T\) consisting of those \(s\) such that all fibres of \(U(s)\to Z\) have degree at least \(i\). The condition that the degree be at least \(i\) is open; hence the inclusion \((F,A)^T_i\subset F^T\) is open. For \(i=0\), this sheaf is \(F^T\); for \(i\) large, it is \(A^T\).

\begin{lemma*}[18]
Suppose that \(A\to F\) is deduced by push-out from an open map \(B\to C\), so that
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
B \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & C \arrow[d]\\
A \arrow[r] & F
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{45.2}
\]
is cocartesian. Then, for each \(i\), the cartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.2em,row sep=1.8em]
\text{fiber product} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
(A\amalg C,A)^T_i \arrow[d]\\
(F,A)^T_{i+1} \arrow[r] &
(F,A)^T_i
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{45.3}
\]
is cocartesian as well.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proofdel}
The site \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\) has enough points. As a consequence of Lemma 2, for each \(X\in QP/G\) and each \(x\in X/G\), the functor
\[
F\longmapsto \varinjlim F(X\times_{X/G}U)
\]
where \(U\) ranges over the Nisnevich neighbourhoods of \(x\) in \(X/G\), is a point. Such points are represented by \(G\)-local henselian schemes \(Z\), finite disjoint unions of local henselian schemes essentially of finite type over \(S\), with \(Z/G\) local.

We show that (45.3) becomes cocartesian after applying any such fibre functor \(F\mapsto F(Z)\). Fix \(s\in (F,A)^T_i(Z)\), viewed as a section of \(F\) over \(T\times_S Z\), and let \(U\subset T\times_S Z\) be the open subset on which \(s\) lies in \(A\). The assumption means that the degree of the fibre \(U_z\) is at least \(i\), independently of the chosen closed point \(z\) of \(Z\). It remains to show that, if \(s\) is not in \((F,A)^T_{i+1}(Z)\), so that this degree is exactly \(i\), then \(s\) has a unique lifting to \((A\amalg C,A)^T_i(Z)\).

The scheme \(T\times_S Z\) is a disjoint union of \(G\)-local henselian schemes. On each component on which \(s\) does not lie in \(A\), cocartesianness of (45.2) gives a unique lift to \(C\). On the components on which \(s\) lies in \(A\), the lift is the given section in \(A\). This produces a section of \(A\amalg C\) over \(T\times_S Z\) lifting \(s\), and it is unique by the same componentwise argument.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{proofnamed}{Proof of Theorem 7}
Since \(F\) is solid, it lies at the end of a sequence
\[
\emptyset=F_0\to F_1\to\cdots\to F_n=F
\]
where each \(F_i\to F_{i+1}\) is a push-out of an open embedding in \(QP/G\). We prove by induction on \(i\) that for every \(Z\), \((F_i\amalg h_Z)^T\) is solid. For \(i=0\), \(F_0\) is representable, and so is \((F_0\amalg h_Z)^T\) by the representability statement above. For the induction step, one applies the following lemma to \(F_i\amalg h_Z\to F_{i+1}\amalg h_Z\).
\end{proofnamed}

\begin{lemma*}[19]
Let
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.8em]
h_U \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & h_X \arrow[d]\\
F \arrow[r] & G
\end{tikzcd}
\]
be a cocartesian square with \(U\) open in \(X\). Suppose that, for every \(Z\), \((F\amalg h_Z)^T\) is solid. Then \(F^T\to G^T\) is solid.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proofdel}
Since \(F\to G\) is open by Proposition 33, the \((G,F)^T_i\) are defined. It suffices to prove that, for each \(i\), the open morphism
\[
(G,F)^T_{i+1}\longrightarrow (G,F)^T_i
\]
is solid. By Lemma 18, this morphism sits in a cartesian and cocartesian square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.0em,row sep=1.8em]
\text{fiber product} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
(F\amalg h_X,F)^T_i \arrow[d]\\
(G,F)^T_{i+1} \arrow[r] &
(G,F)^T_i .
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{45.4}
\]
By assumption \((F\amalg h_X)^T\) is solid; hence \((F\amalg h_X,F)^T_i\) is solid as well, by Proposition 37 applied to the open morphism into \((F\amalg h_X)^T\). The right vertical arrow in (45.4) is open, hence solid by Proposition 36, and the lower horizontal arrow is solid as the push-out of a solid map.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{example*}[7]
It is not always true that, if \(f:A\to F\) is a solid morphism, then \(f^T\) is solid. Take \(G\) trivial and \(T\) two points, so that \(F^T=F^2\). For every sheaf \(F\), the inclusion \(F\to F\amalg\pt\) is solid. Applying \((-)^T\), one obtains
\[
F^2\longrightarrow F^2\amalg F\amalg F\amalg \pt .
\]
Pulling back by the natural map from \(F\) to one of the two summands \(F\), which is an open map, we see that if \(f^T\) were solid, then \(F\) would be solid.
\end{example*}

\begin{corollary*}[46]
If \(f:F\to G\) is open and \(G\) is solid, then
\[
f^T:F^T\longrightarrow G^T
\]
is solid. In particular, if \(G\) is pointed solid, then \(G^T\) is pointed solid.
\end{corollary*}

\begin{proofdel}
The morphism \(f^T\) is open, and one applies Theorem 7 and Proposition 36.
\end{proofdel}

We now define, for pointed sheaves on \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\), a smash variant of the construction \(F\mapsto F^T\). If the marked point \(\pt\to F\) is open, it is
\[
F^{\wedge T}:=F^T/(F,\pt)^T_1,
\]
that is, \(F^T\) with \((F,\pt)^T_1\) contracted to the new base point. This definition can be repeated for any pointed sheaf if, for every monomorphism \(A\to F\), \((F,A)^T_1\) is defined as the following subsheaf of \(F^T\): a section \(s\) of \(F^T(X)=F(X\times T)\) is in \((F,A)^T_1\) if, for every non-empty \(X'\to X\), there is a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=1.7em]
X'' \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & X\times T \arrow[d]\\
X' \arrow[r] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(X''\) non-empty and \(s\) lying in \(A\) on \(X''\).

\begin{example*}[8]
Under the assumptions of Example 6, if \(U\) is open in \(X\) with complement \(Z\), and if \(F=h_X/h_U\), then \(F^{\wedge T}\) is \(h_{X^n}/h_{X^n-Z^n}\), where \(X^n\) has its natural \(S_n\)-action. In particular, if \(F\) is the Thom space of a vector bundle \(E\) on \(Y\), then \(F^{\wedge T}\) is the Thom space of the \(S_n\)-equivariant vector bundle \(\bigoplus_1^n E\) on \(Y^n\).
\end{example*}

\begin{proposition*}[47]
If a pointed sheaf \(F\) is pointed solid, that is, if \(\pt\to F\) is solid, then \(F^{\wedge T}\) is pointed solid.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
Since \(F^T\) is solid, the open morphism \((F,\pt)^T_1\to F^T\) is solid by Proposition 36, and \(\pt\to F^{\wedge T}\) is obtained from it by push-out.
\end{proofdel}

The definition of \(F^{\wedge T}\) immediately implies the following.

\begin{lemma*}[20]
Let \(Z\) be a \(G\)-local henselian scheme. Then
\[
F^{\wedge T}(Z)=\bigwedge_i F((T\times Z)_i),
\]
where the \((T\times Z)_i\) are the \(G\)-local henselian components of \(T\times Z\).
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proposition*}[48]
The functor \(F\mapsto F^{\wedge T}\) respects local and \(\Aone\)-equivalences.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
Let \(f:F\to G\) be a local equivalence. To check that \(F^{\wedge T}\to G^{\wedge T}\) is a local equivalence, it is enough to test on any \(G\)-local henselian \(Z\). By Lemma 20, the map becomes the smash product of the maps
\[
F((T\times Z)_i)\longrightarrow G((T\times Z)_i),
\]
and hence is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets.

Let \(f\) be an \(\Aone\)-equivalence. By the first part it is enough to show that
\[
S(f)^{\wedge T}: S(F)^{\wedge T}\longrightarrow S(G)^{\wedge T}
\]
is an \(\Aone\)-equivalence. We use the characterization of \(\Aone\)-equivalences in Theorem 5. Since \(F\mapsto F^{\wedge T}\) commutes with filtered colimits and preserves local equivalences, it suffices to check that it takes \(\Aone\)-homotopy equivalences to \(\Aone\)-homotopy equivalences. This follows from the natural map
\[
F^{\wedge T}\wedge(\Aone)_+\longrightarrow (F\wedge(\Aone)_+)^{\wedge T}.
\]
\end{proofdel}
