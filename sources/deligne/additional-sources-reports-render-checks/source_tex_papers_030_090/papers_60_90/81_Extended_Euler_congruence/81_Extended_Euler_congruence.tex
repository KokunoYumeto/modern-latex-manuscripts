\paragraph{Abstract.}
The matricial Euler congruence $\Tr(A^{p^n}) \equiv \Tr(A^{p^{n-1}})$ modulo $p^n$, previously announced in Arnold, Japanese J.\ Math.\ 1(1), 1--24, 2006 for $A \in M_N(\mathbb{Z})$, is given a proof based on extending it to the ring of Witt vectors of length $n$.

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Euler congruence $\cdot$ Witt vector

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{Mathematics Subject Classification (2000)} 11A07 $\cdot$ 05B20 $\cdot$ 13K05

\medskip
The matricial Euler congruence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:1}
\Tr\bigl(A^{p^n}\bigr) \equiv \Tr\bigl(A^{p^{n-1}}\bigr) \pmod{p^n},
\end{equation}
which was announced in \cite{Arnold2006} for $A \in M_N(\mathbb{Z})$, can be extended as follows. We replace $\mathbb{Z}$ with $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ and consider $\mathbb{Z}/p^n\mathbb{Z}$ as being $W_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$ (the ring of Witt vectors of length $n$). As geometers, we dislike considering the case of the field $\mathbb{F}_p$ alone: the main part of the following proof depends on the discovery of an extension to the case of $W_n(R)$, where $R$ is of characteristic $p$.

\begin{proposition}
Let $R$ be an $\mathbb{F}_p$ algebra (commutative with unity). The endomorphism $F \colon x \mapsto x^p$ of $R$ defines by functoriality an endomorphism $F$ of $W_n(R)$. For $A$ in $M_N(W_n(R))$, the equality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:2}
\Tr\bigl(A^{p^n}\bigr) = F\Bigl(\Tr\bigl(A^{p^{n-1}}\bigr)\Bigr)
\end{equation}
holds in $W_n(R)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to consider the universal case, where $R$ is the ring of the polynomials of $N^2 n$ variables $X_{ij}^{(k)}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $A_{ij}$ is the Witt vector whose components are $X_{ij}^{(k)}$. We must prove a polynomial identity in these variables. It suffices to prove it in $W_n(R[1/D])$, where $D$ is the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of $\bigl(X_{ij}^{(0)}\bigr)$. If $k$ is an algebraically closed field containing $R[1/D]$, it suffices to prove \eqref{eq:2} in $W_n(k)$: we may and shall assume that $R = k$ is algebraically closed and that the image of $A$ in $M_N(k)$ is a matrix with distinct eigenvalues.

To simplify, we may also suppose that the image of $A$ in $M_N(k)$ is invertible. In this case, the matrix $A$ is conjugate to a diagonal matrix. It remains to prove that for $a_i \in W_n(k)^*$ ($1 \leq i \leq n$), we have
\[
\sum_i a_i^{p^n} = F\Bigl(\sum_i a_i^{p^{n-1}}\Bigr) = \sum_i F\bigl(a_i^{p^{n-1}}\bigr).
\]
As $F$ is an endomorphism, the task is thus reduced to the case $N = 1$.

It remains to show that for $a \in W_n(k)$, the element $a^{p^{n-1}}$ is a multiplicative representative (i.e., a Witt vector of the form $(\lambda, 0, 0, \dots, 0)$). Indeed, $F$ acts on a multiplicative representative by raising it to the $p$-th power.

Writing $a = \alpha u$, where $\alpha$ is a multiplicative representative and $u \equiv 1$ in $k$, we must prove that $u^{p^{n-1}} = 1$.

The ring $W(k)$ of the Witt vectors is a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field $k$ and maximal ideal $p$, and $W_n(k)$ is its quotient by $p^n$. We have
\[
u \equiv 1 \pmod{p},
\]
whence $u^{p^a} \equiv 1 \pmod{p^{a+1}}$ follows inductively, as can be seen from the development of $(1 + p^{a-1}x)^p$.

And, finally, \eqref{eq:1} follows from \eqref{eq:2} because $F$ is the identity of $\mathbb{F}_p$.
\end{proof}
