{\Large\bfseries THE HODGE CONJECTURE}\\[0.5em]
{\large PIERRE DELIGNE}
\end{center}

\section{Statement}

We recall that a pseudo complex structure on a $C^{\infty}$-manifold $X$ of dimension $2N$ is a $\mathbf{C}$-module structure on the tangent bundle $TX$. Such a module structure induces an action of the group $\mathbf{C}^{*}$ on $TX$, with $\lambda \in \mathbf{C}^{*}$ acting by multiplication by $\lambda$. By transport of structures, the group $\mathbf{C}^{*}$ acts also on each exterior power $\wedge^{n} TX$, as well as on the complexified dual $\Omega^{n} := \Hom(\wedge^{n} TX, \mathbf{C})$. For $p+q=n$, a $(p,q)$-form is a section of $\Omega^{n}$ on which $\lambda \in \mathbf{C}^{*}$ acts by multiplication by $\lambda^{-p}\bar{\lambda}^{-q}$. From now on, we assume $X$ complex analytic. A $(p,q)$-form is then a form which, in local holomorphic coordinates, can be written as
\begin{equation*}
\sum a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p},j_{1}\ldots j_{q}} \, dz_{i_{1}} \wedge \cdots \wedge dz_{i_{p}} \wedge d\bar{z}_{j_{1}} \wedge \cdots \wedge d\bar{z}_{j_{q}},
\end{equation*}
and the decomposition $\Omega^{n} = \oplus \Omega^{p,q}$ induces a decomposition $d = d' + d''$ of the exterior differential, with $d'$ (resp.~$d''$) of degree $(1,0)$ (resp.~$(0,1)$).

If $X$ is compact and admits a K\"ahler metric, for instance if $X$ is a projective non-singular algebraic variety, this action of $\mathbf{C}^{*}$ on forms induces an action on cohomology. More precisely, $H^{n}(X,\mathbf{C})$ is the space of closed $n$-forms modulo exact forms, and if we define $H^{p,q}$ to be the space of closed $(p,q)$-forms modulo the $d'd''$ of $(p-1,q-1)$-forms, the natural map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hodge-decomp}
\bigoplus_{p+q=n} H^{p,q} \longrightarrow H^{n}(X,\mathbf{C})
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism. If we choose a K\"ahler structure on $X$, one can give the following interpretation to the decomposition \eqref{eq:hodge-decomp} of $H^{n}(X,\mathbf{C})$: the action of $\mathbf{C}^{*}$ on forms commutes with the Laplace operator, hence induces an action of $\mathbf{C}^{*}$ on the space $\mathcal{H}^{n}$ of harmonic $n$-forms. We have $\mathcal{H}^{n} \xrightarrow{\sim} H^{n}(X,\mathbf{C})$ and $H^{p,q}$ identifies with the space of harmonic $(p,q)$-forms.

When $X$ moves in a holomorphic family, the Hodge filtration $F^{p} := \bigoplus_{a \geq p} H^{a,n-a}$ of $H^{n}(X,\mathbf{C})$ is better behaved than the Hodge decomposition. Locally on the parameter space $T$, $H^{n}(X_{t},\mathbf{C})$ is independent of $t \in T$ and the Hodge filtration can be viewed as a variable filtration $F(t)$ on a fixed vector space. It varies holomorphically with $t$, and obeys Griffiths transversality: at first order around $t_{0} \in T$, $F^{p}(t)$ remains in $F^{p-1}(t_{0})$.

So far, we have computed cohomology using $C^{\infty}$ forms. We could as well have used forms with generalized functions coefficients, that is, currents. The resulting groups $H^{n}(X,\mathbf{C})$ and $H^{p,q}$ are the same. If $Z$ is a closed analytic subspace of $X$, of complex codimension $p$, $Z$ is an integral cycle and, by Poincar\'e duality, defines a class $\cl(Z)$ in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{Z})$. The integration current on $Z$ is a closed $(p,p)$-form with generalized function coefficients, representing the image of $\cl(Z)$ in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{C})$.

The class $\cl(Z)$ in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{Z})$ is hence of type $(p,p)$, in the sense that its image in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{C})$ is. Rational $(p,p)$-classes are called Hodge classes. They form the group
\begin{equation*}
H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{Q}) \cap H^{p,p}(X) = H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{Q}) \cap F^{p} \subset H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{C}).
\end{equation*}

In \cite{hodge1952}, Hodge posed the
\begin{conjecture}[Hodge Conjecture]
On a projective non-singular algebraic variety over $\mathbf{C}$, any Hodge class is a rational linear combination of classes $\cl(Z)$ of algebraic cycles.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Remarks}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item By Chow's theorem, on a complex projective variety, algebraic cycles are the same as closed analytic subspaces.

\item On a projective non-singular variety $X$ over $\mathbf{C}$, the group of integral linear combinations of classes $\cl(Z)$ of algebraic cycles coincides with the group of integral linear combinations of products of Chern classes of algebraic (equivalently by GAGA: analytic) vector bundles. To express $\cl(Z)$ in terms of Chern classes, one resolves the structural sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{Z}$ by a finite complex of vector bundles. That Chern classes are algebraic cycles holds, basically, because vector bundles have plenty of meromorphic sections.

\item A particular case of (ii) is that the integral linear combinations of classes of divisors (= codimension $1$ cycles) are simply the first Chern classes of line bundles. If $Z^{+} - Z^{-}$ is the divisor of a meromorphic section of $\mathcal{L}$, $c_{1}(\mathcal{L}) = \cl(Z^{+}) - \cl(Z^{-})$. This is the starting point of the proof given by Kodaira and Spencer \cite{kodaira-spencer1953} of the Hodge conjecture for $H^{2}$: a class $c \in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z})$ of type $(1,1)$ has image $0$ in the quotient $H^{0,2} = H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O})$ of $H^{2}(X,\mathbf{C})$, and the long exact sequence of cohomology defined by the exponential exact sequence
\begin{equation*}
0 \longrightarrow \mathbf{Z} \longrightarrow \mathcal{O} \xrightarrow{\;\exp(2\pi i\,\cdot\;)\;} \mathcal{O}^{*} \longrightarrow 0
\end{equation*}
shows that $c$ is the first Chern class of a line bundle.

\item The relation between algebraic cycles and algebraic vector bundles is also the basis of the Atiyah and Hirzebruch theorem \cite{atiyah-hirzebruch1962} that the Hodge conjecture cannot hold integrally. In the Atiyah--Hirzebruch spectral sequence from cohomology to topological $K$-theory,
\begin{equation*}
E_{2}^{pq} = H^{p}(X, K^{q}(\text{Pt})) \Longrightarrow K^{p+q}(X);
\end{equation*}
the resulting filtration of $K^{n}(X)$ is by the
\begin{equation*}
F^{p}K^{n}(X) = \Ker(K^{n}(X) \to K^{n}((p-1)\text{-skeleton, in any triangulation})).
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, a class $c$ is in $F^{p}$ if for some topological subspace $Y$ of codimension $p$, $c$ is the image of a class $\tilde{c}$ with support in $Y$. If $Z$ is an algebraic cycle of codimension $p$, a resolution of $\mathcal{O}_{Z}$ defines a $K$-theory class with support in $Z$: $c_{Z} \in K^{0}(X, X-Z)$. Its image in $F^{p}K^{0}(X)$ agrees with the class of $Z$ in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{Z})$. The latter hence is in the kernel of the successive differentials $d_{r}$ of the spectral sequence.

No counterexample is known to the statement that integral $(p,p)$ classes killed by all $d_{r}$ are integral linear combinations of classes $\cl(Z)$. One has no idea of which classes should be effective, that is, of the form $\cl(Z)$, rather than a difference of such.

On a Stein manifold $X$, any topological complex vector bundle can be given a holomorphic structure and, at least for $X$ of the homotopy type of a finite CW complex, it follows that any class in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{Z})$ in the kernel of all $d_{r}$ is a $\mathbf{Z}$-linear combination of classes of analytic cycles.

\item The assumption in the Hodge conjecture that $X$ be algebraic cannot be weakened to $X$ being merely K\"ahler. See Zucker's appendix to \cite{zucker1977} for counterexamples where $X$ is a complex torus.

\item When Hodge formulated his conjecture, he had not realized it could hold only rationally (i.e.~after tensoring with $\mathbf{Q}$). He also proposed a further conjecture, characterizing the subspace of $H^{n}(X,\mathbf{Z})$ spanned by the images of cohomology classes with support in a suitable closed analytic subspace of complex codimension $k$. Grothendieck observed that this further conjecture is trivially false, and gave a corrected version of it in \cite{grothendieck1969}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The Intermediate Jacobian}

The cohomology class of an algebraic cycle $Z$ of codimension $p$ has a natural lift to a group $J^{p}(X)$, extension of the group of classes of type $(p,p)$ in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{Z})$ by the intermediate jacobian
\begin{equation*}
J^{p}(X)_{0} := H^{2p-1}(X,\mathbf{Z}) \backslash H^{2p-1}(X,\mathbf{C}) / F^{p}.
\end{equation*}
This expresses that the class can be given an integral description (in singular cohomology), as well as an analytic one, as a closed $(p,p)$ current, giving a hypercohomology class in $\mathbb{H}^{2p}$ of the subcomplex $F^{p}\Omega_{\text{hol}}^{\bullet} := (0 \to \cdots \to 0 \to \Omega_{\text{hol}}^{p} \to \cdots)$ of the holomorphic de~Rham complex, with an understanding at the cocycle level of why the two agree in $H^{2p}(X,\mathbf{C})$. `Understanding' means a cochain in a complex computing $H^{*}(X,\mathbf{C})$, whose coboundary is the difference between cocycles coming from the integral, resp.~analytic, constructions. Indeed, $J^{p}(X)$ is the hypercohomology $\mathbb{H}^{2p}$ of the homotopy kernel of the difference map $\mathbf{Z} \oplus F^{p}\Omega_{\text{hol}}^{\bullet} \to \Omega^{\bullet}$.

In general, using that all algebraic cycles on $X$ fit in a denumerable number of algebraic families, one checks that the subgroup $A^{p}(X)$ of $J^{p}(X)$ generated by algebraic cycles is the extension of a denumerable group by its connected component $A^{0}_{p}(X)$, and that for some sub-Hodge structure $H_{\text{alg}}$ of type $\{(p-1,p), (p,p-1)\}$ of $H^{2p-1}(X)$, $A^{0}_{p}(X)$ is $H_{\text{alg},\mathbf{Z}} \backslash H_{\text{alg},\mathbf{C}} / F^{p}$. `Sub-Hodge structure' means the subgroup of the integral lattice whose complexification is the sum of its intersections with the $H^{a,b}$. The Hodge conjecture (applied to the product of $X$ and a suitable abelian variety) predicts that $H_{\text{alg}}$ is the largest sub-Hodge structure of $H^{2p-1}(X)$ of type $\{(p-1,p),(p,p-1)\}$.

No conjecture is available to predict what subgroup of $J^{p}(X)$ the group $A^{p}(X)$ is. Cases are known where $A^{p}(X)/A^{0}_{p}(X)$ is of infinite rank. See, for instance, the paper \cite{voisin2000} and the references it contains. This has made generally inapplicable the methods introduced by Griffiths (see, for instance, Zucker \cite{zucker1977}) to prove the Hodge conjecture by induction on the dimension of $X$, using a Lefschetz pencil of hyperplane sections of $X$. Indeed, the method requires not just the Hodge conjecture for the hyperplane sections $H$, but that all of $J^{p}(H)$ comes from algebraic cycles.

\section{Detecting Hodge Classes}

Let $(X_{s})_{s \in S}$ be an algebraic family of projective non-singular algebraic varieties: the fibers of a projective and smooth map $f : X \to S$. We assume it is defined over the algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$ of $\mathbf{Q}$ in $\mathbf{C}$. No algorithm is known to decide whether a given integral cohomology class of a typical fiber $X_{0}$ is somewhere on $S$ of type $(p,p)$. The Hodge conjecture implies that the locus where this happens is a denumerable union of algebraic subvarieties of $S$ (known: see \cite{cattani-deligne-kaplan1995}), and is defined over $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$ (unknown).

The Hodge conjecture is not known even in the following nice examples.

\begin{example}
For $X$ of complex dimension $N$, the diagonal $\Delta$ of $X \times X$ is an algebraic cycle of codimension $N$. The Hodge decomposition being compatible with K\"unneth, the K\"unneth components $\cl(\Delta)_{a,b} \in H^{a}(X) \otimes H^{b}(X) \subset H^{2N}(X \times X)$ ($a+b=2N$) of $\cl(\Delta)$ are Hodge classes.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
If $\eta \in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Z})$ is the cohomology class of a hyperplane section of $X$, the iterated cup product $\eta^{p} : H^{N-p}(X,\mathbf{C}) \to H^{N+p}(X,\mathbf{C})$ is an isomorphism (hard Lefschetz theorem, proved by Hodge. See \cite[IV.6]{weil1958}). Let $z \in H^{N-p}(X,\mathbf{C}) \otimes H^{N-p}(X,\mathbf{C}) \subset H^{2N-2p}(X \times X)$ be the class such that the inverse isomorphism $(\eta^{p})^{-1}$ is $c \mapsto \pr_{1!}(z \cup \pr_{2}^{*} c)$. The class $z$ is Hodge.
\end{example}

\section{Motives}

Algebraic varieties admit a panoply of cohomology theories, related over $\mathbf{C}$ by comparison isomorphisms. Resulting structures on $H^{*}(X,\mathbf{Z})$ should be viewed as analogous to the Hodge structure. Examples: If $X$ is defined over a subfield $K$ of $\mathbf{C}$, with algebraic closure $\bar{K}$ in $\mathbf{C}$, $\Gal(\bar{K}/K)$ acts on $H^{*}(X,\mathbf{Z}) \otimes \mathbf{Z}_{\ell}$ and $H^{*}(X,\mathbf{C}) = H^{*}(X,\mathbf{Z}) \otimes \mathbf{C}$ has a natural $K$-structure $H_{\text{DR}}^{*}(X \text{ over } K)$, compatible with the Hodge filtration. Those cohomology theories give rise to conjectures parallel to the Hodge conjecture, determining the linear span of classes of algebraic cycles. Example: the Tate conjecture \cite{tate1965}. Those conjectures are open even for $H^{2}$.

Grothendieck's theory of motives aims at understanding the parallelism between those cohomology theories. Progress is blocked by a lack of methods to construct interesting algebraic cycles. If the cycles of Examples 1 and 2 of \S 4 were algebraic, Grothendieck's motives over $\mathbf{C}$ would form a semi-simple abelian category with a tensor product, and be the category of representations of some pro-reductive group-scheme. If the algebraicity of those cycles is assumed, the full Hodge conjecture is equivalent to a natural functor from the category of motives to the category of Hodge structures being fully faithful.

\section{Substitutes and Weakened Forms}

In despair, efforts have been made to find substitutes for the Hodge conjecture. On abelian varieties, Hodge classes at least share many properties of cohomology classes of algebraic cycles: they are ``absolutely Hodge'' \cite{deligne-brylinski1980}, even ``motivated'' \cite{andre1996}. This suffices for some applications --- for instance, the proof of algebraic relations among periods and quasi periods of abelian varieties predicted by the Hodge conjecture \cite{deligne-brylinski1980}, but does not allow for reduction modulo $p$. The following corollaries of the Hodge conjecture would be particularly interesting. Let $A$ be an abelian variety over the algebraic closure $\mathbf{F}$ of a finite field $\mathbf{F}_{q}$. Lift $A$ in two different ways to characteristic $0$, to complex abelian varieties $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ defined over $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$. Pick Hodge classes $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ on $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$, of complementary dimension. Interpreting $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ as $\ell$-adic cohomology classes, one can define the intersection number $\kappa$ of the reduction of $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ over $\mathbf{F}$. Is $\kappa$ a rational number? If $z_{1}$ and $z_{2}$ were $\cl(Z_{1})$ and $\cl(Z_{2})$, $Z_{1}$ and $Z_{2}$ could be chosen to be defined over $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$ and $\kappa$ would be the intersection number of the reductions of $Z_{1}$ and $Z_{2}$. Same question for the intersection number of the reduction of $z_{1}$ over $\mathbf{F}$ with the class of an algebraic cycle on $A$.
