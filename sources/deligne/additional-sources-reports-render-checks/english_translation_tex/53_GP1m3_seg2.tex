\subsection*{8.7}

(b) A $M_\B$-torseur $P_\B \subset P_{\DR,\C} = P_{\DR,\R} \otimes \C$, stable by the conjugaison complex of $P_{\DR,\R} \otimes \C$.

A trivialisation of $F^0(P_{\DR,\R})$ identifie $P_\B$ a point of $M_\B \backslash M_\DR \otimes \C$, fixe by the conjugaison complex, and
\[

We shall denote $\Lie U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}$ (resp.~$\Lie U_\DR^{\mathrm{impair}}$) the product of the $\Lie U_\DR^n$ for $n$ pair (resp.~impair) and $U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}$ the under-group of $U_\DR$ of algebra of Lie $\Lie U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}$; c'is the under-group fixe of $\Int \tau(-1)|U_\DR$.

\begin{proposition}[8.10]\label{prop:8.10}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There exists $a \in G_\DR(\C)$ such that for every $M$ in $T$, one ait
\begin{equation*}\tag{8.10.1}
M_\B = a(M_\DR) \subset M_\DR \otimes \C.
\end{equation*}
\item One can trouver $a$ verifiant (8.10.1) of the form
\begin{equation*}\tag{8.10.2}
a = a_0 \tau(2\pi i) \quad a_0 \in U_\DR(\R).
\end{equation*}
A tel $a$ is unique a multiplication a line pres by a element of $U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}(\Q)$.

\item Ecrivons $a_0 = a_- a_+$ with $a_- = \exp(N)$, $N \in \Lie U_\DR^{\mathrm{impair}} \otimes \R$ and $a_+ \in U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}(\R)$. By (ii), $a_-$ is unique. the involution $F_\infty$ of all $M_\B$ in $T$ is given by
\begin{equation*}\tag{8.10.3}
F_\infty = a\bar{a}^{-1} = a_0 \tau(-1) a_0^{-1} = a_- \tau(-1) a_-^{-1} \tau(-1)^{-1} = \exp(-2N).
\end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $\omega_\B$ (resp.~$\omega_\DR$) the functor fiber $M \\mapsto M_\B$ (resp.~$M \\mapsto M_\DR$). This are of the functors fibers on $\Q$. of after [Sa], the functors fibers of $T$ on $\C$ are isomorphes. There exists therefore a isomorphism of functors fibers on $\C$ of $\omega_\B$ with $\omega_\DR$. A tel isomorphism $a$ satisfies (8.10.1).

(ii) one has $\Gm(\C) = \C^*$ and $\tau: \C^* \to G_\DR(\C)$. The group $G_\DR(\C)$ agit on the set of the isomorphisms of functors fibers on $\C$. the set of these isomorphisms is a torseur under $U_\DR(\C)$: $\Gm$ agit trivialement car the graduation 8.8.1 of $M_\DR$ is the action of $\tau(\Gm)$. There exists therefore $a$ of the form (8.10.2).

Let $a = a_0 \tau(2\pi i)$ and $a' = a'_0 \tau(2\pi i)$ deux tels isomorphisms. one has $a' = a u$ with $u \in U_\DR(\C)$, and $a_0 \tau(2\pi i) u = a_0 u \tau(2\pi i) = a'_0 \tau(2\pi i)$ car $\tau(2\pi i)$ centralise $U_\DR$. Therefore $a'_0 = a_0 u$. For that $a'_0 \in U_\DR(\R)$, it is necessary and sufficient that $\bar{u} = u$, i.e. $u \in U_\DR(\R)$.

For $a$ of the form (8.10.2), one has $\bar{a} = a_0 \tau(-2\pi i) = a \tau(-4\pi i) = a_0 \tau(-1) a_0^{-1} \cdot a$, of or $a \bar{a}^{-1} = a_0 \tau(-1) a_0^{-1}$. C'is (8.10.3).

Let $a_0$ and $a'_0 = a_0 g$ ($g \in U_\DR(\Q)$) deux choix verifiant (8.10.2). one has $a_0 \tau(-1) a_0^{-1} = a'_0 \tau(-1) (a'_0)^{-1}$, therefore $g$ commute a $\tau(-1)$. The centralisateur of $\tau(-1)$ in $U_\DR$ is $U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}$, of or $g \in U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}(\Q)$. Reciproquement, si $g \in U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}(\Q)$, $a'_0 = a_0 g$ satisfies (8.10.2) and defines the same $F_\infty$.

(iii) Ecrivons $a_0 = a_- a_+$. Then $a_0 \tau(-1) a_0^{-1} = a_- \tau(-1) a_-^{-1}$ car $a_+$ commute a $\tau(-1)$. As $a_- = \exp(N)$ with $N$ impair, one has $\tau(-1) a_-^{-1} \tau(-1)^{-1} = \exp(-N)$ and $a_- \tau(-1) a_-^{-1} \tau(-1)^{-1} = \exp(2N) = \exp(-2N)$. This proves (8.10.3).

the unicite of $a_-$ resulte of the unicite of $a_0$ a multiplication pres by $U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}(\Q)$: si $a_0 g = a'_-$ with $g \in U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}(\Q)$ and $a_-, a'_-$ as en (iii), then $a_- a_+ g = a'_- a'_+$ with $a_+, a'_+ \in U_\DR^{\mathrm{pair}}$, therefore $a_- = a'_-$.
\end{proof}
