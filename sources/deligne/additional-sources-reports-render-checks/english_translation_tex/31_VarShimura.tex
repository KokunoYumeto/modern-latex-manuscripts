\subsection{Moduli spaces of Hodge structures}

\subsubsection{Recall}

Recall that a \textit{Hodge structure} on a space vector reel $V$ is a bigrading $V_{\CC} = \bigoplus V^{pq}$ of the complexifie of $V$, such that $V^{pq}$ let the complex conjugue of $V^{qp}$.

Definissons a action $h$ of $\CC^*$ on $V_{\CC}$ by the formule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.1.1}
h(z)\cdot v = z^{-p}\bar{z}^{-q}v \quad \text{pour } v \in V^{pq}.
\end{equation}
The $h(z)$ commutent a the conjugaison complex of $V_{\CC}$, therefore se deduisent by extension of scalars of a action, again notee $h$, of $\CC^*$ on $V$. Regardons $\CC$ as a extension of $\RR$, and let $S$ son group multiplicatif, considered as algebraic group reel (autrement dit, $S = \Res_{\CC/\RR}\Gm_m$ (restriction of scalars a the Weil)); one has $S(\RR) = \CC^*$, and $h$ is a action of the algebraic group $S$. One checks that this construction defines a equivalence of categories: (vector spaces reels equipped with a Hodge structure) $\to$ (vector spaces reels equipped with a action of the algebraic group reel $S$).

\`A the inclusion $\RR^* \subset \CC^*$ correspond a inclusion of algebraic groups reels $\Gm_m \subset S$. We shall denote $w_h$ (or simplement $w$) the restriction of $h$ a $\Gm_m$, and the appellerons the \textit{weight}: $w:\Gm_m \to \GL(V)$. One dit that $V$ is homogene of weight $n$ si $V^{pq} = 0$ for $p+q \neq n$, and $w(\lambda)$ is the homothetie of rapport $\lambda^n$.

We shall denote $\mu$ (or simplement $\mu$) the action of $\Gm_{m,\CC}$ on $V_{\CC}$ defined by $\mu(z)v = z^{-p}v$ for $v \in V^{pq}$. C'is a compose $\Gm_{m,\CC} \to S_{\CC} \xrightarrow{h_{\CC}} \GL(V_{\CC})$.

The \textit{Hodge filtration} $F_h$ (or simplement $F$) is defined by $F_h^p = \bigoplus_{r \ge p} V^{r,s}$. One dit that $V$ is of type $E \subset \ZZ \times \ZZ$ si $V^{pq} = 0$ for $(p,q) \notin E$.

More generally, si $A$ is a under-ring of $\RR$ such that $A \otimes \QQ$ Let field (en pratique, $A = \ZZ$, $\QQ$ or $\RR$), a $A$-\textit{Hodge structure} is a $A$-module of type finite $V$, equipped with a Hodge structure on $V \otimes_A \RR$.

\subsubsection{}

A \textit{variation of Hodge structure} on a variety analytic complex $S$ consiste en

(a) a systeme local $V$ of vector spaces reels;

(b) en each point of $S$, a Hodge structure on the fiber of $V$ en $s$, variant continument with $S$.

One exige that the Hodge filtration varie holomorphiquement with $s$, and satisfies the axiome dit of \textit{transversalite}: the derivee of a section of $F^p$ is in $F^{p-1}$.

It will be souvent given a systeme local $V_{\ZZ}$ of $\ZZ$-modules of type finite, such that $V = V_{\ZZ} \otimes \RR$. One parlera then of variation of $\ZZ$-Hodge structure. Similarly for $\ZZ$ remplace by a ring $A$ as en 1.1.1.

\begin{remark}
Regardons $S$ as being a variety reelle, of which the space tangent en each point is equipped with a structure complex, i.e.~of a Hodge structure of type $\{(-1,0),(0,-1)\}$. the integrabilite of the structure presque complex of $S$ s'exprime en disant that the crochet of champs of vecteurs is compatible a the Hodge filtration of the complexifie of the fiber tangent: $[T^{0,1}, T^{0,1}] \subset T^{0,1}$. Similarly, the axiome of the variations of Hodge structure exprime that the derivation (fiber tangent) $\otimes$ (sections $C^{\infty}$ of $V$) $\to$ (sections $C^{\infty}$ of $V$) (or plutot the complexifie of this map) is compatible to the Hodge filtrations: $\nabla_D F^p \subset F^p$ for $D$ in $T^{0,1}$ (holomorphie) and $\nabla_D F^p \subset F^{p-1}$ for $D$ arbitraire (transversalite).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Principe}

\textbf{Principe 1.1.9.} En geometrie algebraic, each fois that apparait a Hodge structure dependant of parametres complexes, c'is a variation of Hodge structure on the space of the parametres. the example fondamental is 1.1.2, with parametres: si $f:X \to S$ is a morphism proper and smooth, of fibers $X_s$ kahleriennes, the $H^n(X_s,\ZZ)$ forment a systeme local on $S$, and the Hodge filtration on the complexifie $H^n(X_s,\CC)$ varie holomorphiquement with $s$ and satisfies the axiome of transversalite.

\begin{proof}

The verification detaillee is laissee to the reader.
\end{proof}
