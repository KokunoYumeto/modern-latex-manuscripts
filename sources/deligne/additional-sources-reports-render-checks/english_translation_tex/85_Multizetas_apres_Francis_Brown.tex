\section*{0. Introduction}

Soient $k \geq 0$ et $s = (s_1,\ldots,s_k)$ une suite de $k$ entiers $\geq 1$. Le nombre multiz\^eta $\zeta(s)$ est la somme it\'er\'ee (l'appellation est expliqu\'ee en 1.1)
@@MATH0@@
(somme sur les suites strictement d\'ecroissantes de $k$ entiers $> 0$). Pour $k = 0$, cette d\'efinition donne $\zeta(s) = 1$. Pour $k \geq 1$, la somme (0.1) converge si et seulement si $s_1 > 2$. Nous ne consid\'ererons que les $\zeta(s)$ convergents, i.e. tels que (0.1) converge.

Le produit de deux nombres multiz\^etas est une combinaison lin\'eaire \`a coefficients entiers de nombres multiz\^etas. Voir 3.6. Il revient donc au m\^eme de d\'eterminer les relations polynomiales \`a coefficients rationnels entre les nombres multiz\^etas, ou de d\'eterminer les relations $\Q$-lin\'eaires entre eux.

Pour $k = 1$, les $\zeta(s)$ convergents sont les valeurs en les entiers $n > 2$ de la fonction $\zeta$ de Riemann. Euler a montr\'e que pour $n$ pair, $\zeta(n)$ est un multiple rationnel de $\pi^n$ ([3]). Calculant $\zeta(3)$ avec 10 chiffres significatifs, il a aussi v\'erifi\'e que $\zeta(3)$ n'\'etait pas le produit de $\pi^3$ par un nombre rationnel de petit d\'enominateur. \`A la suite d'une correspondance avec Goldbach, Euler a \'etudi\'e dans [4], pour $k = 2$, la variante des sommes (0.1) dans laquelle on somme sur $n_1 > n_2$:
\[
\zeta^{\text{Euler}}(s_1,s_2) = \zeta(s_1,s_2) + \zeta(s_1+s_2).
\]
It proves a serie of relations $\Q$-lineaires between nombres multizetas, parmi lesquelles $\zeta(2,1) = \zeta(3)$. A of ses motivations etait the espoir of obtenir of the informations on the values of $\zeta$ en the integers impairs $> 3$ (see the fin of sa lettre a Goldbach of the 5 janvier 1743).

The probleme of determiner all the relations $\Q$-lineaires between nombres multizetas a deux aspects:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A)] \`A quelles relations faut-it s'attendre? The prouver.
\item[(B)] Prouver that it n'y en a not of other.
\end{itemize}

the evidence disponible, tant numerique that theorique, suggere that every relation $\Q$-lineaire between the $\zeta(s)$ is sum of relations isobares, i.e. between $\zeta(s)$ similarly weight $\sum s_i$. Si one se limite a ne considerer that the relations isobares, one ne sait rien on the probleme (B). We ne parlerons that of (A).

F. Brown a defined a class of relations $\Q$-lineaires between the $\zeta(s)$, the relations \emph{motiviques}. More precisely, it defines a $\Q$-algebra graduee $M$, of the elements homogenes $\zeta^m(s)$ of $M$, of degree the weight $\sum s_i$ of $s$, and a homomorphisme $\mathrm{real} : M \to \R$ such that $\mathrm{real}(\zeta^m(s)) = \zeta(s)$. the algebra $M$ is en tant that space vector generated by the $\zeta^m(s)$. The relations motiviques between $\zeta(s)$ are celles which proviennent of relations between the $\zeta^m(s)$.

A variante of the conjecture of the periods of Grothendieck implique that every relation $\Q$-lineaire between the $\zeta(s)$ is motivic. This prediction a ete verifiee for beaucoup of the relations connues. Mise en garde: si certain proofs of relations between the $\zeta(s)$ are faciles a relever en a proof of the same relations between the $\zeta^m(s)$, c'is loin of be toujours the cas.

\begin{theorem}[F. Brown {\cite{Brown}}]\label{thm:0.2}
The $\zeta^m(s_1,\ldots,s_k)$ for lesquels each $s_i$ vaut $2$ or $3$ forment a base of $M$ on $\Q$.
\end{theorem}

Each $\zeta(s)$ can therefore be exprime uniquement, in such a way motivic, as combinaison lineaire a coefficients rationnels of $\zeta(s_1,\ldots,s_k)$ with $k \geq 0$ and each $s_i \in \{2,3\}$.

Malheureusement, the proof of Brown ne fournit not a algorithme, of the less not a algorithme utilisable, for trouver quelle is this combinaison lineaire. Of the theorem \ref{thm:0.2}, F. Brown deduces that the category of the motives of Tate mixtes on $\Z$ is generated by the fundamental group of $\mathbf{P}^1 - \{0,1,\infty\}$. See \ref{thm:7.18}.

Certain of nos notations different of celles of [1]. The correspondence between the notations is involutive: $\zeta(s_1,\ldots,s_k)$ devient $\zeta(s_k,\ldots,s_1)$, the composition of the chemins $\alpha\beta$ devient $\beta\alpha$, a mono\^me en the $e_t$ (resp.~a sequence of $0$ and $1$) is a remplacer by the same, lu of line a gauche, and $e_1$ is a remplacer by $-e_1$.

\section{Formule integral}\label{sec:1}

\subsection{}\label{ssec:1.1}

Let $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_N$ of the $1$-forms on the intervalle $[0,1]$. the integral iteree $\int_0^1 \omega_1,\ldots,\omega_N$ is the integral, on the simplexe
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.1}
\sigma_N := \{(t_1,\ldots,t_N) \mid 1 > t_1 > \cdots > t_N > 0\},
\end{equation}
contenu in the cube $[0,1]^N$, of $\pr_1^*\omega_1 \wedge \pr_2^*\omega_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge \pr_N^*\omega_N$. Let $S$ the operation which a $1$-form $\omega$ attached the function $S[\omega](t) := \int_0^t \omega$. the integral iteree $\int_0^1 \omega_1,\ldots,\omega_N$ can be construite as suit: appliquer $S$ a $\omega_N$, multiplier the function obtained by $\omega_{N-1}$, appliquer $S$, multiplier by $\omega_{N-2}$, \ldots, a the fin, evaluer en $1$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.2}
\int_0^1 \omega_1,\ldots,\omega_N = S[\omega_1 \cdot S[\omega_2 \cdots S[\omega_N]\cdots]](1).
\end{equation}
C'is this construction which explique the appellation ``integral iteree''. A sum such that (0.1) admet a description analogue, en terme of the operateur which a function $f(n)$ ($n > 1$) attached the function $\sum_{m=1}^{n-1} f(m)$; this explique the appellation of ``sum iteree'' datum a (0.1).

Calculons ainsi the integral iteree
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.3}
\int_0^1 \underbrace{\frac{dt}{t},\ldots,\frac{dt}{t}}_{(s_1-1)\text{ fois}}, \frac{dt}{1-t}, \underbrace{\frac{dt}{t},\ldots,\frac{dt}{t}}_{(s_2-1)\text{ fois}}, \frac{dt}{1-t}, \ldots, \underbrace{\frac{dt}{t},\ldots,\frac{dt}{t}}_{(s_k-1)\text{ fois}}, \frac{dt}{1-t}.
\end{equation}
one has $\frac{dt}{1-t} = \sum_{n>0} t^n\,dt$. Appliquant $S$ terme a terme, one obtains $\sum_{n>1} \frac{t^n}{n}$. Multipliant by $\frac{dt}{t}$ and appliquant $S$, one obtains $\sum_{n>1} \frac{t^n}{n^2}$. Iterant $(s-1)$ fois, one obtains $\sum_{n>1} \frac{t^n}{n^{s_k}}$. Multipliant by $\frac{dt}{1-t} = \sum_{m>0} t^m\,dt$ and appliquant $S$, one obtains
\[
S\Bigl[\sum_{m>0,n>1} \frac{t^{m+n}}{n^{s_k}}\Bigr] = \sum_{m>0,n>1} \frac{t^{m+n}}{(m+n)n^{s_k}} = \sum_{m>n>0} \frac{t^m}{m\,n^{s_k}}.
\]
Continuant ainsi, one obtains finalement the

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1.2}
the integral iteree (1.1.3) vaut $\zeta(s_1,\ldots,s_k)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{}\label{ssec:1.3}

Then that the notion of sum infinie is etrangere a the geometrie algebraic, the etude of integrals of quantites algebraic en is a of the sources. C'is grace a the proposition \ref{prop:1.2} that the geometrie algebraic, more precisely the theorie of the mixed Tate motives, is utile a the etude of the nombres multizetas.

\section{Fundamental group pro-unipotent and cohomology}\label{sec:2}

\subsection{}\label{ssec:2.1}

We shall have need of a variante $Z^0$ of the serie central descendante $Z$ of a group $\Gamma$. For each $i \geq 1$, $\Gamma/Z^i$ is a group nilpotent. the set $T$ of ses elements of twisting is therefore a under-group. One defines $Z^i_0$ as being the inverse image of $T$ in $\Gamma$. Ci-dessous, we supposerons toujours $\Gamma$ of generation finite. This hypothese implique that the $Z^i_0/Z^{i+1}_0$ are of the groups abeliens libres of type finite.

Supposons all of abord $\Gamma$ nilpotent without twisting, i.e. that there exists $A$ such that $Z^A_0 = 0$. For each $i$ ($1 \leq i < A$), choisissons a base of $Z^i_0/Z^{i+1}_0$ and relevons-the in $Z^i_0$. Let $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_M$ the elements of $\Gamma$ obtained, ranges in a ordre quelconque, and let $w(j)$ the integer $i$ such that $\gamma_j$ releve a element of a base of $Z^i_0/Z^{i+1}_0$. One verifies by induction on $A$ that the map
\[
\Z^M \to \Gamma : n \mapsto \gamma_1^{n_1} \cdots \gamma_M^{n_M}
\]
is bijective. In a tel ``systeme of coordonnees'', the loi of group of $\Gamma$ is given by of the polynomes a coefficients rationnels and a values entieres on the integers. More precisely, si the coordonnee of indice $j$ is vue as being of weight $w(j)$, the $j$-ieme coordonnee of $m \circ n$ is a polynome of weight $\leq w(j)$ en the $m_k$ and $n_l$.

The same polynomes definissent a algebraic group unipotent on $\Q$, the enveloppe unipotente $\Gamma^{\mathrm{un}}$ of $\Gamma$. The group $\Gamma^{\mathrm{un}}(\Q)$ of ses points rationnels is $\Q^N$, equipped with the loi of group given by the same formules that celle of $\Gamma$. The group $\Gamma^{\mathrm{un}}(\Q)$ is the completion of Malcev $\Gamma \otimes \Q$ of $\Gamma$.

Let $\Q[\Gamma]$ the algebra of group of $\Gamma$, and $I$ son ideal of augmentation. Quillen a given a description elegante of the algebra affine $\mathcal{O}(\Gamma^{\mathrm{un}})$ of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{un}}$: c'is the filtered colimit of the duaux of the $\Q[\Gamma]/I^N$. More precisely, the inclusion of $\Gamma$ in $\Q[\Gamma]$ identifie the dual of $\Q[\Gamma]$ a the space of the functions $\Gamma \to \Q$, and by this identification the dual of $\Q[\Gamma]/I^{N+1}$ devient the space of the polynomes en the $n_j$ of weight $\leq N$.

For $\Gamma$ of generation finite quelconque, this which precede s'applique to the $\Gamma/Z^i_0$, and one defines $\Gamma^{\mathrm{un}}$ as being the scheme en group projective limit of the $(\Gamma/Z^i_0)^{\mathrm{un}}$. the algebra affine of $\Gamma^{\mathrm{un}}$ is again the filtered colimit of the duaux of the $\Q[\Gamma]/I^N$.

\subsection{}\label{ssec:2.2}

Let $E$ a space topologique raisonnable, by example a algebraic variety complex. One assumed $E$ connected. A chemin $\gamma$ of $a$ a $b$: $[0,1] \to E$ fournit $\gamma^N: [0,1]^N \to E^N$ and, by restriction to the simplexe $\sigma_N$ of (\ref{eq:1.1.1}), a chaine singular of $E^N$ which is a cycle modulo the reunion of the $b = x_1$, $x_1 = x_2$, \ldots, $x_N = a$. The class of homology of this cycle ne depend that of the class of homotopie of $\gamma$.

Faisons $a = b$. \'Elaborant the construction precedente (cf.~[2] \S\,3), one obtains a isomorphism of $\Q[\pi_1(E,a)]/I^{N+1}$ with a group of homology relative of $E^N$ and, of son dual $(\Q[\pi_1(E,a)]/I^{N+1})^\vee$ with a group of cohomology relative of $E^N$. Of the, by passage a the filtered colimit, a description cohomologique of the algebra affine of $\pi_1(E,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$.

Ne supposons more that $a = b$. Let $\pi_1(E;b,a)$ the set of the classes of homotopie of chemins of $a$ a $b$. The group $\pi_1(E,a)$ agit a line, by composition of the chemins, on $\pi_1(E;b,a)$. This action fait of $\pi_1(E;b,a)$ a space principal homogene, we preferons dire \emph{torseur}, under $\pi_1(E,a)$. One denotes $\pi_1(E;b,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$ the $\pi_1(E,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$-torseur which s'en deduces en poussant by $\pi_1(E,a) \to \pi_1(E,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$. Let $\Q[\pi_1(E;b,a)]$ the space of the combinaisons lineaires formelles of elements of $\pi_1(E;b,a)$. C'is a $\Q[\pi_1(E,a)]$-module a line, free of rank a. One sets
\[
\Q[\pi_1(E;b,a)]/I^N := \Q[\pi_1(E;b,a)]/\Q[\pi_1(E;b,a)]I^N.
\]
The construction which precede fournit again a description cohomologique of the $(\Q[\pi_1(E;b,a)]/I^N)^\vee$, and of the algebra affine of $\pi_1(E;b,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$ which en is the filtered colimit.

\subsection{}\label{ssec:2.3}

Let $X$ a algebraic variety defined on a under-field $K$ of $\C$. The cohomology of Betti $H_{\B}^*(X)$ of $X$ is the cohomology rational of the space $X(\C)$ of the points complexes of $X$, equipped with sa topologie classique. It is equipped with structures which refletent the structure algebraic of $X$. In particular: a mixed Hodge structure. One dispose also of the de Rham cohomology algebraic $H_{\dR}^*(X)$. Si $X$ is not singular, $H_{\dR}^*(X)$ is the hypercohomologie of the de Rham complex of the sheaves of forms differentielles algebraic. C'is a $K$-space vector, one dispose of a isomorphism of comparaison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.1}
\comp_{\B,\dR} : H_{\dR}^i(X) \otimes_K \C \xrightarrow{\sim} H_{\B}^i(X) \otimes_{\Q} \C,
\end{equation}
and the filtrations $W$ and $F$ of the mixed Hodge structure of $H_{\B}^i(X)$ proviennent of filtrations of $H_{\dR}^i(X)$. The coefficients of the matrice of the isomorphism (\ref{eq:2.3.1}), in a base of $H_{\dR}^i(X)$ and a of $H_{\B}^i(X)$, portent the nom of \emph{periods}.

Grace a \ref{ssec:2.2}, all this s'applique to the algebras affine of the schemes en group $\pi_1(X,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$ or of leurs spaces homogenes $\pi_1(X;b,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$. One ecrira $\pi_1(X,a)_{\B}$ for $\pi_1(X,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$, $\pi_1(X,a)_{\dR}$ for sa variante of Rham, and similarly for the $\pi_1(X;b,a)$. One mettra $\B$/$\dR$ en indice quand the states vaut also bien en Betti that en of Rham.

\subsection{Example}\label{ssec:2.4}

Prenons $K = \Q$ and let $X$ the group multiplicatif $\Gm$, of group of points complexes $\C^*$. Ici, the fundamental group, commutative, is independant of the point-base and s'identifie to the group of homology $H_1$. En of Rham, the dual $H_{\dR}^1$ of $H_1^{\dR}$ is $\Q \cdot \frac{dz}{z}$. En Betti, $H_1(\C^*,\Z)$ is $\Z$, generated by a tour positive autour of $0$. Si one ne veut not parler of ``tour positive'', i.e.~choisir which is $i$ and which is $-i$, it vaut mieux dire that $H_1 = \pi_1 = 2\pi i\Z$, with `$\ell \in 2\pi i\Z$' corresponding to the lacet $\exp(\ell t)$ ($0 \leq t \leq 1$), on lequel the integral of $\frac{dz}{z}$ vaut $\ell$.

The $H_{\B}^1(\Gm) = H^1(\C^*,\Q)$ and $H_{\dR}^1(\Gm)$ are the realisations of Betti and of Rham of the Tate motive $\Q(1)$ (see \S\,5). one has $\Q(1)_{\dR} = \Q$ ($\frac{dz}{z} \in H_{\dR}^1(\Gm)$), and $\Q(1)_{\B}$, inclus in $\Q(1)_{\dR} \otimes \C$ by $\comp_{\dR,\B}$, is $2\pi i\,\Q$; $\Q(1)_{\dR}$ is purement of type of Hodge $(-1,-1)$.

The algebraic group $\pi_1(\Gm,1)_{\dR}$ is the group additif $\Ga$, of group of points rationnels $\Q(1)_{\dR} = \Q$, and of algebra affine $\Q[T]$ (more precisely: $\mathrm{Sym}^*(H_{\dR}^1(\Gm))$). The algebraic group $\pi_1(\Gm,1)_{\B}$ is the group additif of group of points rationnels $2\pi i\,\Q$. Algebra affine: $\mathrm{Sym}^*(H_{\B}^1(\Gm))$. C'is the algebra of the functions $f$ on $2\pi i\Z$, a values rationnelles, and such that si one identifie $2\pi i\Z$ a $\Z$ by $n \mapsto 2\pi i n$, the conditions equivalentes suivantes let verifiees for $N$ sufficiently grand: (a) $f$ is a polynome of degree $< N$; (b) notant $\Delta$ the operateur $g \mapsto (g(n+1)-g(n))$, one has $\Delta^N f = 0$; (c) the form lineaire on the algebra of group $\Q[\Z]$ defined by $f$ is zero on $I^N$.

By abus of langage, we ecrirons parfois $\Q(1)_{\B/\dR}$ for these algebraic groups.

\section{The cas of $\mathbf{P}^1 - \{0,1,\infty\}$}\label{sec:3}

\subsection{}\label{ssec:3.1}

Si $X$ is the complement in $\mathbf{P}^1$ of a set finite $T$ of points rationnels, the variety $X$ is defined on $\Q$ and the Hodge structures mixte of \ref{ssec:2.2}, \ref{ssec:2.3} are of Hodge-Tate: $\Gr_n^W$ is zero for $n$ impair, and for $n = 2m$ is purement of type of Hodge $(m,m)$. For chacun of the groups $M$ consideres en \ref{ssec:2.2}, let $M_{\dR}$ sa variante of Rham (\ref{ssec:2.3}). After renumerotation, the filtrations by the weight $W$ and of Hodge $F$ of $M_{\dR}$ are opposees. They definissent the graduation of $M_{\dR}$ for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.1}
W_{-2n}(M_{\dR}) = \bigoplus_{m>n} M_{\dR}^m, \qquad F^{-p}(M_{\dR}) = \bigoplus_{m \leq p} M_{\dR}^m.
\end{equation}
The convention (\ref{eq:3.1.1}) assure that $\Q(1)_{\dR}$ is of degree a. It is parfois commode of graduer by the oppose of the degree, the \emph{codegre}, and of poser $(M_{\dR})_m := (M_{\dR})^{-m}$.

Cas particulier: the algebra affine of $\pi_1(X;b,a)_{\dR}$ is graduee. It is a degrees $\leq 0$ and reduite a $\Q$ en degree $0$. The morphism of augmentation $\mathcal{O}(\pi_1(X;b,a)_{\dR}) \to \Q$ is a point $1_{b,a}$ of $\pi_1(X;b,a)_{\dR}$. En $\dR$, one dispose ainsi of a chemin canonical $1_{b,a}$ of a point-base a other: for a certain scheme en group pro-unipotent $\Pi$, each $\pi_1(X;b,a)_{\dR}$ s'identifie a copie ${}_b\Pi_a$ of $\Pi$, and the composition of the chemins is the loi of group.

The group $\Pi$ admet the description suivante. Let $L$ the algebra of Lie on $\Q$ generated by of the elements $e_t$ ($t \in T$) soumis a the seule relation $\sum e_t = 0$. One the munit of the graduation for which each $e_t$ is homogene of degree $1$. The serie central descendante $Z$ is the filtration by the degrees $> i$. Let $\hat{L}$ the complete of $L$ for this filtration, and $\exp(\hat{L}/Z^i)$ the algebraic group unipotent of algebra of Lie $\hat{L}/Z^i$. one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.2}
\Pi := \varprojlim \exp(\hat{L}/Z^i).
\end{equation}

Other description: the algebra enveloppante $A$ of $L$ is the algebra associative generated by of the elements $e_t$ ($t \in T$) soumis a the seule relation $\sum e_t = 0$. It is graduee, each $e_t$ being homogene of degree a, and the power $N$-ieme of the ideal of augmentation $I$ is the part of $A$ of degree $> N$. The complete $\hat{A} = \varprojlim A/I^N$ of $A$ is equipped with a coproduit
\[
\Delta : \hat{A} \to \hat{A} \otimes \hat{A} : e_t \mapsto e_t \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes e_t,
\]
$\hat{L}$ is the algebra of Lie of the elements primitifs, and for every $\Q$-algebra $R$, si one sets $\hat{A} \otimes R := \varprojlim_N (A/I^N) \otimes_{\Q} R$, the group $\Pi(R)$ of the $R$-points of $\Pi$ is the group of the elements groupaux of $(\hat{A} \otimes R)^*$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.3}
\Pi(R) = \{g \in (\hat{A} \otimes R)^* \mid \Delta g = g \otimes g\}.
\end{equation}
the algebra affine $\mathcal{O}(\Pi)$ of $\Pi$ (equipped with the topologie discrete) and the algebra enveloppante completee $\hat{A}$ (equipped with sa topologie of projective limit) are duales the a of the other.

The dual topologique of $\hat{A}$ s'identifie to the dual gradue $\bigoplus (A_n)^\vee$ of $A$, and this given the graduation (a degrees $\leq 0$) of $\mathcal{O}(\Pi)$.

One notera $\omega$ the $1$-form logarithmique on $X$ with values in $\hat{L}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.4}
\omega := \sum_{t \in T} e_t \frac{dz}{z-t}.
\end{equation}
En each $t \in T$, it a the residu $e_t$.

Assume that $\infty \in T$, and posons $T_0 := T - \{\infty\}$. one has $A = \Q\langle e_t \rangle_{t \in T_0}$ and $\hat{A} = \Q\langle\langle e_t \rangle\rangle_{t \in T_0}$ (series formelles associatives en the $e_t$). For every mot $w = t_1 \cdots t_k$ en the alphabet $T_0$, notons $c(w)$ the form lineaire on $\hat{A}$ coefficient of the mono\^me $e_{t_1} \cdots e_{t_k}$.

One denotes again $c(w)$ the restriction of $c(w)$ a $\Pi \subset \hat{A}^*$, and for a combinaison lineaire formelle of mots, one sets $c(\sum \lambda_w w) = \sum \lambda_w c(w)$. The $c(w)$ forment a base vectorielle of $\mathcal{O}(\Pi)$. The product in $\mathcal{O}(\Pi)$ correspond to the product of melange of the mots:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.5}
c(w')c(w'') = c(w' \shuffle w'')
\end{equation}
or, for $w' = t_1 \cdots t_k$ and $w'' = t_{k+1} \cdots t_{k+\ell}$, $w' \shuffle w''$ is the sum of the $t_{\sigma^{-1}(1)} \cdots t_{\sigma^{-1}(k+\ell)}$ for $\sigma$ parcourant the set $S_{k,\ell}$ of the permutations of $\{1,\ldots,k+\ell\}$ croissantes on $\{1,\ldots,k\}$ and on $\{k+1,\ldots,k+\ell\}$ (shuffle product).

\subsection{}\label{ssec:3.2}

A chemin $\gamma$ of $a$ a $b$ defines a point rational of $\pi_1(X;b,a)_{\B} = \pi_1(X(\C);b,a)^{\mathrm{un}}$. Let $\comp_{\dR,\B}(\gamma)$ the image of this point rational by the isomorphism of comparaison

Quels that let the integers $a, b \geq 0$, there exists of the nombres rationnels $\alpha_{a,b,r}$ ($1 \leq r \leq a+b+1$), uniques, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.13.1}
\zeta^m(2^{\{b\}} 3 2^{\{a\}}) = \sum_{r=1}^{a+b+1} \alpha_{a,b,r} \, \zeta^m(2r+1) \zeta^m(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}}).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\noindent \textit{Proof} -- Regardons, by (\ref{eq:7.9.1}), $\zeta^m(2^{\{b\}} 3 2^{\{a\}})$ as a function on $IU_{\dR} \times D$. Developpons-the selon the powers of a coordonnee on $D$: $\zeta^m(2^{\{b\}} 3 2^{\{a\}}) = \sum u_{2r+1} \pi_2^m{}^{\{a+b+1-r\}}$, with $u_{2r+1}$ of codegre $2r+1$ on $IU_{\dR}$. of after \ref{prop:6.6} and \ref{ssec:7.9}, a translate a gauche of $u_{2r+1}$ ne differe of $u_{2r+1}$ that by the addition of a constant. C'is therefore a homomorphisme of $IU_{\dR}$ in the group additif. Being of weight $2r+1$, c'is of after \ref{prop:7.12} a multiple rational of $\zeta^m(2r+1)$ and \ref{cor:7.13} resulte of \ref{lem:7.11}.

\begin{theorem}[D. Zagier {\cite{Zagier}}]\label{thm:7.14}
Posons $A_{a,b}^r = \binom{2r}{2a+2}$ and $B_{a,b}^r = (1-2^{-2r}) \binom{2r}{2b+1}$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.14.1}
\zeta(2^{\{b\}} 3 2^{\{a\}}) = 2 \sum_{r=1}^{a+b+1} (-1)^r (A_{a,b}^r - B_{a,b}^r) \zeta(2r+1) \zeta(2^{\{a+b+1-r\}}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This theorem suggere that the $\alpha_{a,b,r}$ of (\ref{eq:7.13.1}) are the $2(-1)^r (A_{a,b}^r - B_{a,b}^r)$. Comment the prouver en partant of \ref{thm:7.14} will be explique to the \S\,\ref{sec:8}.

\subsection{}\label{ssec:7.15}

the algebra affine $\mathcal{O}(U_{\dR})$ of $U_{\dR}$ is en duality, to the sens gradue, with the algebra enveloppante of $\mathrm{Lie}^{\gr}(U_{\dR})$. This algebra enveloppante is a algebra associative free en of the generateurs of degrees the integers impairs $\geq 3$. The serie of Poincare of $\mathcal{O}(U_{\dR})$, graduee by the codegre, is therefore
\[
P(\mathcal{O}(U_{\dR}),t) = \prod_{n>1 \text{ impair}} (1 - t^n)^{-1} = \prod_{n>1} (1 - t^{2n+1})^{-1} = \left(1 - \frac{t^3}{1-t^2}\right)^{-1} = \frac{1-t^2}{1-t^2-t^3}.
\]
Si the algebra affine of the group additif $\Ga$, of coordonnee $\lambda$, is graduee en donnant a $\lambda$ the codegre $2$, one has $P(\mathcal{O}(\Ga),t) = (1-t^2)^{-1}$ and
\[
P(\mathcal{O}(IU_{\dR} \times D),t) = (1-t^2-t^3)^{-1}.
\]
the algebra affine $\mathcal{O}(IU_{\dR})$ is a under-algebra graduee of $\mathcal{O}(U_{\dR})$. By \ref{ssec:7.9}, one has therefore

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:7.16}
The serie of Poincare of $\mathcal{O}(M_{\dR})$, graduee by the codegre, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.16.1}
P(\mathcal{O}(M_{\dR}),t) \leq (1-t^2-t^3)^{-1} \text{ (inegalite terme a terme),}
\end{equation}
with equality if and only if the morphism $I: U_{\dR} \to IU_{\dR}$ is a isomorphism, i.e. si $U_{\dR}$ agit fidelement on $\pi_1(X;\vec{1},\vec{0})_{\dR}$.
\end{proposition}

Comparant with \ref{cor:6.10}, one obtains the

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:7.17}
(i) $U_{\dR}$ agit fidelement on ${}_{\vec{1}}\Pi_{\vec{0}}$;

(ii) The morphism of $\Q$-vector spaces $H(2,3) \to \mathcal{O}(M_{\dR})$ is a isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

the algebra affine of $\pi_1(X;\vec{1},\vec{0})_{\mot}$ contient en under-quotient a $\Q(-1)$. It follows therefore of (i) that the action of $G_{\dR}$ on the algebra affine of $\pi_1(X;\vec{1},\vec{0})_{\dR}$ is faithful. The theorie of the Tannakian categories given that en consequence of (i), one has the

\begin{corollary}\label{thm:7.18}
the algebra affine $\mathcal{O}(\pi_1(X;\vec{1},\vec{0})_{\mot})$, which is a Ind-object of $\MT(\Z)$, generated the Tannakian category $\MT(\Z)$.
\end{corollary}

More precisely, all Tate motive mixte a bonne reduction on $\Spec(\Z)$ se deduces of under-objects in $\MT(\Z)$ of $\mathcal{O}(\pi_1(X;\vec{1},\vec{0})_{\mot})$ by the operations of tensor products, direct sums, duaux and under-quotients.

the assertion (ii) of \ref{thm:7.17} equivaut a dire that the $\zeta^m(s)$ with $s_i \in \{2,3\}$ forment a base of $\mathcal{O}(M_{\dR})$.

\subsection{}\label{ssec:8.1}

The scheme en group $V_{\dR} = (\Pi, \circ)$ agit on ${}_{\vec{1}}\Pi_{\vec{0}}$ (\ref{ssec:7.1}), and therefore on son algebra affine $\mathcal{O}({}_{\vec{1}}\Pi_{\vec{0}})$. This action is the action of $(\Pi, \circ)$ on $\mathcal{O}(\Pi)$ by translations a gauche (\ref{eq:7.2.3}): $x$ transforme $f$ en $y \mapsto f(x^{-1} \circ y)$. These actions are data by (\ref{eq:7.2.1}) (\ref{eq:7.2.2}).

Derivant these formules, one obtains the action of $\mathrm{Lie}(V_{\dR}) = (\mathrm{Lie}\,\Pi, \{,\})$ on $\mathcal{O}({}_{\vec{1}}\Pi_{\vec{0}})$, and dualement the coaction of the coalgebre of Lie. Celle-ci is $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$, for $\mathfrak{m}$ the ideal of the functions nulles a the element neutre $1$, and the coaction is the projection on $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 \otimes \mathcal{O}(\Pi)$ of $f(x) \mapsto f(x^{-1} \circ y) - f(y)$ in $\mathfrak{m} \otimes \mathcal{O}(\Pi) \subset \mathcal{O}(\Pi \times \Pi)$.

Goncharov a given a formule commode for this coaction (see [5] theorem 1.2). For $w$ a mot of the alphabet $\{0,1\}$, considerons the under-mots $cId$ of $1w0$, with $I$ not vide: one considers all the decompositions $w = w_0 I w_{00}$, with $I$ not vide, and $c,d$ are the lettres mitoyennes a $I$ in $1w0$. For each decomposition, posons $w_{rI} := w_0 w_{00}$. Formule for the coaction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:8.1.1}
c(w) \mapsto -\sum_{c,d,I} p_{cd}(I) \otimes c(w_{rI}) \quad \text{ou}
\end{equation}
$p_{cd}(I)$ is the projection in $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ of the element suivant of $\mathfrak{m} \subset \mathcal{O}(\Pi)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:8.1.2}
p_{cd}(I) = \text{projection de } \begin{cases} 0 & \text{si } c = d \\ c(I) & \text{si } c = 1, d = 0 \\ \text{l'antipode } c(I)^* & \text{si } c = 0, d = 1, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
or, si $I = a_1 \cdots a_k$, the antipode is $(-1)^k c(a_k \cdots a_1)$.
