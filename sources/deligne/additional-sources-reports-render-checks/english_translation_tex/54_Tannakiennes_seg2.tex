\begin{theorem}[7.1]\label{thm:7.1}

\end{equation}

A \emph{extension of Picard-Vessiot} of $K$, for $(X, \nabla)$, is a field differentiel $(L, \partial)$ extension of $(K, \partial)$ such that

\item[(c)] the field of the constants of $L$ is $k$.

Let $(e^i)_{1 \leq i \leq n}$ a base of $X$ and $\nabla e^i = \sum_j c^i_j e^j$. Let $M$ a extension differentielle of $K$. For that $\xi = \sum y_i \otimes e^i \in X \otimes_K M$ let horizontal, it faut and suffit that the $y_i$ satisfy the systeme
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.8.1}
\partial y_i = -\sum_j y_j c^j_i.
\end{equation}
Plutot that of a extension of Picard-Vessiot for $X$, one parle classiquement of a extension of Picard-Vessiot for a systeme (9.8.1): a extension differentielle of $L$, generated by of the $y_{\alpha i}$ formant $n$ solutions lineairement independentes of (9.8.1) and similarly field of constants that $K$.

More classiquement again, one considers of the equations differentielles lineaires of the $n$\textsuperscript{eme} ordre en a indeterminee $y$. One passe of the a systeme by the astuce habituelle: passer to the systeme satisfies by $(y, \partial y, \partial^2 y, \ldots, \partial^{n-1}y)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[9.9]
More generally, let $K$ a field of caracteristique 0, $\mathcal{D}$ a space vector on $K$ and $[,]$ a crochet of Lie on $\mathcal{D}$. One ne the assumed not $K$-lineaire, but one assumed that there exists $\delta : \mathcal{D} \to \Der(K, K)$ (lineaire and morphism of Lie) verifiant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.9.1}
[D_1, \lambda D_2] - \lambda[D_1, D_2] = \delta(D_1)(\lambda) D_2.
\end{equation}
The field of the constants $k$ is the intersection of the noyaux of the $\delta(D)$ ($D \in \mathcal{D}$).

A \emph{connexion integrable} on a $K$-space vector $V$ is a action of Lie $\nabla$ of $\mathcal{D}$ on $V$ verifiant
\[
\nabla_D(\lambda v) = \delta(D)(\lambda) \cdot v + \lambda \nabla_D v.
\]
The $K$-vector spaces of dimension finite a connexion integrable $(V, \nabla)$ formant a Tannakian category on $k$.

A \emph{extension differentielle} of $(K, \mathcal{D})$ is $(L, \mathcal{D}')$ with $L$ extension of $K$, equipped with $L \otimes_K \mathcal{D} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{D}'$ compatible to the actions $\delta$ of $\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{D}'$ on $K$ and $L$.

A \emph{extension of Picard-Vessiot} of $(K, \mathcal{D})$, for $(V, \nabla)$, is a extension differentielle $(L, \mathcal{D}')$ of $(K, \mathcal{D})$, similarly field of constants, such that $L \otimes_K V$ let generated by ses vecteurs horizontaux (annules by the $\nabla_D$) and that $L$ let generated by the coordonnees, in a base of $V$ on $K$, of the vecteurs horizontaux of $V \otimes_K L$. As en 9.6, the extensions of Picard-Vessiot correspondent to the functors fibers of $\langle V \rangle^\otimes$ on $k$. In particular, si $k$ is algebriquement clos, they exist and are uniques a isomorphism pres.

\textit{Example:} one prend for $K$ a field to the derivees partielles: equipped with $d$ derivations $\partial_i$ which commutent. One prend for $\mathcal{D}$ the $K$-space vector of base the $\partial_i$, for $\delta : \partial_i \mapsto \partial_i$ and one defines $[,]$ by (9.9.1) and $[\partial_i, \partial_j] = 0$.
\end{remark}
