\section*{O. INTRODUCTION}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{O. Introduction}

Let $X_0/k$ a curve elliptique ordinaire. of after the theorie of Serre--Tate [14, V], the variety modulaire formelle $\mathcal{M}$ of the deformations of $X_0$ on the $W$-algebras artiniennes local of field residuel $k$ is isomorphic to the group multiplicatif formel $\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m/W$. More precisely, the group $p$-divisible $G = \bigcup_n \Ker(p^n : X_0 \to X_0)$ is isomorphic a $(\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p) \times \widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m$, and the choix of a isomorphism $\alpha$ between the part étale of $G_0$ and $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ permet of identifier canonically, for every $W$-algebra $R$ artinienne local of field residuel $k$, the set of the liftings of $X_0$ on $R$ to the group $\Ext^1((\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p)_R, (\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m)_R)$, a son tour isomorphic canonically to the group of the unites of $R$ congrues a 1 modulo the ideal maximal: one obtains therefore ainsi a isomorphism (ne dependant that of $\alpha$) between $\mathcal{M}$ and $(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m)_W$, and in particular the deformation universelle $X$ of $X_0$ on $\mathcal{M}$ defines a ``coordonnee canonical'' $q$ on $\mathcal{M}$, such that $\mathcal{M} \simeq \Spf W[[q-1]]$. Of this parametre $q$, which decrit the group $p$-divisible $G$ associated a $X$ as extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p/\mathbb{Z}_p$ by $\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m$, one can donner a other interpretation, en termes of the module $\mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/\mathcal{M})$, equipped with sa structure of $F$-cristal and of sa Hodge filtration $H^0(X,\Omega^1_{X/\mathcal{M}}) \subset \mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/\mathcal{M})$. The datum of a isomorphism as ci-dessus fournit indeed by duality a isomorphism $\alpha'$ between the group formel $\widehat{X}/\mathcal{M}$ associated a $X$ and the group formel $(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m)_{\mathcal{M}}$, of or a form $b \in H^0(X,\Omega^1_{X/\mathcal{M}})$, corresponding by $\alpha'$ a the form invariante on $(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m)_{\mathcal{M}}$. Let $a \in \mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/\mathcal{M})$ such that $\nabla a = 0$ and $(a,b)$ Let base symplectique of $\mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/\mathcal{M})$, i.e.\ $\langle a,b \rangle = 1$, $\langle a,a \rangle = 0$. Si
\[
\nabla : \mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/\mathcal{M}) \longrightarrow \Omega^1_{\mathcal{M}/W} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/\mathcal{M})
\]
designe the connexion of Gauss--Manin, one has necessarily
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.1}
\nabla b = \xi \otimes a\,,
\end{equation}
with $\xi \in \Omega^1_{\mathcal{M}/W}$. One can montrer (see Appendice and Expose suivant, by N.\ Katz) that the one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.2}
\xi = d\log q\,,
\end{equation}
or $q$ is the parametre defined more haut, which se trouve therefore coincider with celui defined independamment by Dwork ([8], [6]) a the aide of (0.2). The base $(a,b)$ ci-dessus jouit moreover of properties remarquables vis-a-vis of the operation of Frobenius $F$ on $\mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/\mathcal{M})$: si the one choisit as endomorphism of $\mathcal{M}$ relevant the Frobenius of $\mathcal{M} \otimes k$ celui given by $q \mapsto q^p$, then $F$ s'exprime by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.3}
Fa = a\,, \quad Fb = pb\,.
\end{equation}

The constructions precedentes se generalisent to the abelian varieties ordinaires [8], see also [9], [11] for of the maps arithmetiques in the cas of the curves elliptiques.

the object of the expose is of montrer that one can developper a theorie analogue for the surfaces K3 ordinaires, of the less si $p \neq 2$. Let $X_0/k$ a surface K3 ordinaire, i.e.\ such that the Frobenius of $H^2(X_0, \mathcal{O})$ let not zero, and let $S$ the variety modulaire formelle of the deformations of $X_0$ on the $W$-algebras artiniennes local of field residuel $k$ (IV 1.2). Si $p \neq 2$, one proves that $S$ is equipped canonically of a structure of group formel, isomorphic a $\widehat{\mathbb{G}}_m^{20}/W$; in particular, the deformation universelle $X$ of $X_0$ on $S$ defines of the ``coordonnees canonical'' $q_i$ ($1 \leq i \leq 20$) such that $S \simeq \Spf W[[q_1-1, \ldots, q_{20}-1]]$, formant a base of the characters of $S$.

One shows moreover that, si $L_0$ is a sheaf inversible not trivial on $X_0$, the hypersurface $\Sigma(L_0)$ of $S$ such that $L_0$ se prolonge a $X \times_S \Sigma(L_0)$ (IV 1.5) is the kernel of a character of $S$, defined in such a way presque tautologique by the class of Chern cristalline of $L_0$.

En the absence of a theorie of Serre--Tate for the liftings of the surfaces K3, c'is en termes of the structure of $F$-cristal of $\mathcal{H}_{dR}(X/S)$ that the one defines the structure of group formel of $S$. En fait, one can englober the cas of the abelian varieties and celui of the surfaces K3 in a same theorem of structure for a certain class of $F$-cristaux ordinaires. the etude of these cristaux fait the object of the n$^\circ$\ 1. The maps geometriques are data to the n$^\circ$\ 2. En this which concerne the theorem relative to the hypersurfaces $\Sigma(L_0)$, the ingredient essential is the utilisation of the class of Chern cristalline for controler the obstruction to the prolongement.

\section{Parametres canonical of the F-cristaux of Hodge ordinaires}

\subsection{Rappels of definitions}

\subsubsection{}

For the definitions and properties generales of the $F$-cristaux, see Katz [8], [10]. One fixe for every the sequence of the n$^\circ$\ 1 of the rings of series formelles
\[
A_0 = k[[t_1, \ldots, t_n]]\,, \qquad A = W[[t_1, \ldots, t_n]]\,,
\]
or $(t_1, \ldots, t_n)$ is a sequence finite of indeterminees (for $n = 0$, one convient that $A_0 = k$, $A = W$).

A cristal (under-entendu, en modules libres of type finite) on $A_0$ is by definition a $A$-module free of type finite $H$, equipped with a connexion
\[
\nabla : H \longrightarrow \Omega^1_{A/W} \otimes H
\]
integrable and $p$-adiquement topologiquement nilpotente. That $\nabla$ let integrable signifie that, si the one sets $D_i = d/dt_i$, one has
\[
\nabla(D_i)\nabla(D_j) = \nabla(D_j)\nabla(D_i)
\]
quels that let $i,j$. That permet of faire operer on $H$ the operateurs PD-differentiels on $A$, i.e.\ the polynomes en the $D_i$ with coefficients in $A$, by the formule
\[
\nabla\Bigl(\sum_m a_m D^m\Bigr) = \sum_m a_m (\nabla(D))^m\,,
\]
with the notations condensees habituelles $D^m = D_1^{m_1} \cdots D_n^{m_n}$, etc. Quant a the nilpotence topologique of $\nabla$, it s'exprime by the fait that, for every $i$, $\nabla(D_i)^m$ tend toward zero for the topologie $p$-adique of $\End_W(H)$ quand $m$ tend toward $+\infty$. of after Berthelot [1, II 4], the datum of a connexion $\nabla$ as ci-dessus equivaut a the datum, for every couple of $W$-homomorphismes $(f,g)$ of $A$ in a $W$-algebra $B$ local, noetherienne and complete, such that $f$ and $g$ let congrus modulo a ideal $I$ of $B$ equipped with powers divisees compatible to the powers divisees standard of $p$, of a isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\chi(f,g) : f^*H \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} g^*H\,,\tag{$*$}
\end{equation}
these isomorphisms being assujettis a verify certain conditions of transitivite explicitees in [8, 1.2], i.e.\ $\chi(g,h)\chi(f,g) = \chi(f,h)$, $\chi(fk,gk) = k^*\chi(f,g)$, $\chi(\id,\id) = \id$. The formule suivante decrit concretement this dictionnaire in the sens which we interesse:

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:1.1.2}
With the notations ci-dessus, designons by $f^* : H \to f^*H$ the fleche of adjonction, or $f^*H = f^* \otimes_f H$ by abus. the homomorphisme $A$-lineaire compose
\[
\chi(f,g)f^* : H \xrightarrow{\;f^*\;} f^*H \xrightarrow{\;\chi(f,g)\;} g^*H
\]
is given by the formule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.2.1}
\chi(f,g)f^*(x) = \sum_m (f(t)-g(t))^{[m]} g^*(\nabla(D)^m x)\,,
\end{equation}
the sum portant on all the multi-indices $m = (m_1, \ldots, m_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$, with the notations condensees
\[
(f(t)-g(t))^{[m]} = (f(t_1)-g(t_1))^{[m_1]} \cdots (f(t_n)-g(t_n))^{[m_n]}
\]
(the crochet designant a power divisee in $I$), and
\[
\nabla(D)^m = \nabla(D_1)^{m_1} \cdots \nabla(D_n)^{m_n}
\]
\emph{(N.B.\ the serie to the second membre of (1.1.2.1) is convergente grace a the nilpotence topologique of $\nabla$).}
\end{lemma}

This states is essentiellement contenu in [1, II 4.3]. It is enough to the verify after reduction mod $p^r$ for every $r$. We shall denote again by abus $(f,g) : A \to^2 B$ the $W_r$-homomorphismes deduits of ceux donnes by reduction mod $p^r$. Let $D$ the enveloppe a powers divisees of the ideal of augmentation $J$ of $A \otimes_{W_r} A$ ($= W_r[[t_1, \ldots, t_n, u_1, \ldots, u_n]]$), notons $d_0$ (resp.\ $d_1$): $A \to D$ the homomorphisme given by $a \mapsto a \otimes 1$ (resp.\ $1 \otimes a$), definissant the structure of $A$-module ``a gauche'' (resp.\ ``a line'') of $D$. As $J$, generated by the $t_i - u_i$, is regular, it follows of [1, I 4.5.1] that $D$ s'identifie for the structure gauche a the algebra a powers divisees $A\langle \xi_1, \ldots, \xi_n \rangle$, or $\xi_i = d_1t_i - d_0t_i =$ image of $u_i - t_i$ in $D$. By the property universelle of the enveloppes a powers divisees, and of the fait that $B$ is complet, there exists a PD-morphism $s : D \to B$ such that $sd_0 = g$, $sd_1 = f$. of other part, of after [1, II 4.3.8], the connexion defines a PD-stratification

A \emph{$F$-cristal} on $A_0$ is by definition a cristal $(H,\nabla)$ on $A_0$, equipped, for each lifting $\sigma : A \to A$ of the Frobenius of $A_0$ compatible a the endomorphism of Frobenius $\sigma$ of $W$, of a homomorphisme horizontal

\subsection{F-cristaux unites}

\subsubsection{}

One dit that a $F$-cristal $(H,\nabla)$ on $A_0$ is a \emph{$F$-cristal unite} si, for a lifting $\sigma : A \to A$ of the Frobenius of $A_0$, $F(\sigma)$ is a isomorphism. of after (1.1.3.1), it revient to the same of dire that $F(\sigma)$ is a isomorphism for every lifting $\sigma$.

All $\mathbb{Z}_p$-module free of type finite $M$ fournit a $F$-cristal unite on $A_0$, namely $(H = M \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} A,\ \nabla = 1 \otimes d,\ F(\sigma) = 1 \otimes \sigma)$. En fait, all $F$-cristal unite on $A_0$ is of this type:

\begin{proposition}[{cf.\ [8, \S 3]}]\label{prop:1.2.2}
Let $H$ a $F$-cristal unite on $A_0$. There exists a base $(e_i)$ $(1 \leq i \leq r)$ of $H$ on $A$ such that $\nabla e_i = 0$ and $F(\sigma)\sigma^*e_i = e_i$ for every lifting $\sigma$ of the Frobenius of $A_0$.
\end{proposition}

Autrement dit, the base $(e_i)$ defines a isomorphism of the $F$-cristal trivial $\mathbb{Z}_p^r \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_p} A$ defined ci-dessus on $H$.

Before of demontrer \ref{prop:1.2.2}, faisons of abord deux remarks.

\textbf{a)} Si $x \in H$ is such that, for a lifting $\sigma$, $F(\sigma)\sigma^*x = x$, then $\nabla x = 0$. Indeed, as $\sigma$ releve Frobenius, one has $(\sigma^*\omega)_i \in p\Omega^1_{A/W}$, therefore $p$ divise $\nabla x = \nabla F(\sigma)\sigma^*x$ of after (1.1.3.3). The same raisonnement shows, by recurrence, that $\nabla x$ is divisible by $p^n$ for every $n$, therefore that $\nabla x = 0$.

\textbf{b)} Si $x \in H$ is such that $F(\sigma)\sigma^*x = x$ for a lifting $\sigma$, then $F(\sigma)\sigma^*x = x$ for every lifting $\sigma$. Compte tenu of a), that resulte indeed of the loi of variation of $F(\sigma)$ (1.1.3.4).

It is enough therefore of trouver a base $(e_i)$ of $H$ on $A$ such that $F(\sigma)\sigma^*e_i = e_i$ for a lifting $\sigma$ choisi, by example celui given by $t_j \mapsto t_j^p$ ($1 \leq j \leq n$). Let $s : A \to W$ the augmentation given by $s(t_i) = 0$. As $s\sigma = \sigma$, $F(\sigma)\sigma^*$ induces on $s^*H$ a automorphism $\sigma$-lineaire $\Phi$. The field $k$ being algebriquement clos, there exists a base of $s^*H$ fixe by $\Phi$ (partir of a base fixe by $\Phi$ mod $p$, which exists grace to the lemma of Lang, and the modifier of proche en proche for the rendre fixe by $\Phi$ mod $p^n$ for every $n$). Autrement dit, there exists a base $(f_i)$ $(1 \leq i \leq r)$ of $H$ such that, si the one sets $F(\sigma)\sigma^* = F$, one ait
\[
Ff = (1+M)f\,,
\]
with $M \in M_r(A)$ such that $M \equiv 0 \pmod{(t)}$ ($= (t_1, \ldots, t_n)A$). It is enough to montrer that one can remplacer $f$ by $e = (1+N)f$, with $N \in M_r(A)$ such that $N \equiv 0 \pmod{(t)}$, of maniere a satisfaire a $Fe = e$. Explicitant, one trouve the equation
\begin{equation}\tag{$*$}
N = M + \sigma(N) + \sigma(N)M\,.
\end{equation}
Or the map $N \mapsto M + \sigma(N) + \sigma(N)M$ is contractante for the topologie $(t)$-adique, car $\sigma((t)) \subset (t)^p$, therefore $(*)$ possede a solution unique, this which acheve the proof of \ref{prop:1.2.2}.

\subsubsection{}

We shall say that a $F$-cristal $(H,\nabla)$ on $A_0$ is \emph{topologiquement nilpotent} si, for a lifting $\sigma$ of the Frobenius of $A_0$, $F(\sigma)\sigma^*$ is $p$-adiquement topologiquement nilpotent; this condition ne depend not of the choix of $\sigma$, as the shows (1.1.3.4) (or more exactly the analogue of (1.1.3.4) for of the iteres of $F(\sigma)\sigma^*$, $F(\tau)\tau^*$). It n'is not vrai that all $F$-cristal on $A_0$ let extension of a $F$-cristal topologiquement nilpotent by a $F$-cristal unite. More precisely, one has the critere suivant, cas particulier of a theorem of Katz:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1.2.4}
Let $H$ a $F$-cristal on $A_0$. Notons $F_0$ the endomorphism of $H_0 = H \otimes_A A_0$ induces by $F(\sigma)\sigma^*$, or $\sigma$ releve the Frobenius $F_{A_0}$ of $A_0$ ($F_0$ is $F_{A_0}$-lineaire, and independant of $\sigma$).

The conditions suivantes are equivalentes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The rangs stables of $(H_0, F_0)$ to the point closed and en a point geometriquement algebriquement clos localise to the point generique of $\Spec(A_0)$ are egaux.
\item There exists a extension of $F$-cristaux on $A_0$
\[
0 \longrightarrow U \longrightarrow H \longrightarrow E \longrightarrow 0\,,
\]
or $U$ is a $F$-cristal unite and $E$ a $F$-cristal topologiquement nilpotent.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Recall this that one entend by ``rank stable'': si $L$ is a space vector of dimension finite over a field parfait of car.\ $p$, equipped with a endomorphism $p$-lineaire $\varphi$, $L$ se decompose canonically en $L = L^{\mathrm{ss}} \oplus L^{\mathrm{nilp}}$, or $L^{\mathrm{ss}} = \bigcap \Im \varphi^n$, $L^{\mathrm{nilp}} = \bigcup \Ker \varphi^n$; the dimension of $L^{\mathrm{ss}}$ is by definition the \emph{rank stable} of $(L,\varphi)$.

Bien that it s'agisse of a cas particulier of [10, 2.4.2], indiquons rapidement a proof of \ref{prop:1.2.4}\footnote{figure in of the notes of Katz anterieures a (loc.\ cit.), not publiees.}, the situation envisagee ici being nettement more simple that celle of (loc.\ cit.). It is clear that (ii) implique (i). Inversement, posons $\overline{H}_0 = H_0 \otimes_{A_0} k = H \otimes_A k$. Relevons in $H_0$ a base of $\overline{H}_0$ adaptee a the decomposition of $\overline{H}_0$ under $F_0 \otimes k$, $\overline{H}_0 = \overline{H}_0^{\mathrm{ss}} \oplus \overline{H}_0^{\mathrm{nilp}}$: $F_0$ is then given by a matrice $\begin{psmallmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{psmallmatrix}$ with $b \equiv 0 \pmod{(t)}$, and $a$ inversible of rank $r = \dim \overline{H}_0^{\mathrm{ss}}$. In a new base of $H_0$, given by a matrice of passage $P = \begin{psmallmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{psmallmatrix}$, the matrice of $F_0$ is $\begin{psmallmatrix} a' & c' \\ b' & d' \end{psmallmatrix} = P^{-1}\begin{psmallmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{psmallmatrix}\sigma(P)$, and the one can determiner $x \equiv 0 \pmod{(t)}$ of maniere that $b' = 0$: indeed, one obtains for $x$ the equation $x = f(x)$, or $f(x) = ba^{-1} + x\sigma(c)a^{-1} + d\sigma(x)a^{-1}$, and this equation possede a solution unique car $f$ is a map contractante for the topologie $(t)$-adique. In this new base, the matrice of $F_0$ prend therefore the form $\begin{psmallmatrix} a' & c' \\ 0 & d' \end{psmallmatrix}$, with $a'$ inversible of rank $r$. the hypothese (i) entraine then that the endomorphism $d'$ is nilpotent, car it en is ainsi of $d'$ localise en a extension algebriquement close of the field of the fractions of $A_0$.

Choisissons a lifting $\sigma$ of $F_{A_0}$. In a base of $H$ relevant the base of $H_0$ choisie ci-dessus, the matrice of $F = F(\sigma)\sigma^*$ s'ecrit (with of the notations changees) $\begin{psmallmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{psmallmatrix}$, or $b \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, and $d$ topologiquement nilpotent. Appliquant a new the methode of the point fixe (with this fois a map contractante for the topologie $p$-adique), one sees that one can trouver a new base $(e_1, \ldots, e_r, e_{r+1}, \ldots, e_{r+s})$ of $H$, given by a matrice of passage $\begin{psmallmatrix} 1 & x \\ 0 & 1 \end{psmallmatrix}$, with $x \equiv 0 \pmod{p}$, of maniere that in this new base, the matrice of $F$ s'ecrive $\begin{psmallmatrix} a' & c' \\ 0 & d' \end{psmallmatrix}$, with $a'$ inversible (of rank $r$) and $d'$ topologiquement nilpotent. The under-$F$-module $U = \bigoplus_{1 \leq i \leq r} Ae_i$ is egal a $\bigcap_{n \geq 0} \Im F^n$, therefore (grace a (1.1.3.2)) horizontal, i.e.\ such that $\nabla U \subset \Omega^1_{A/W} \otimes U$. of after (1.1.3.1), $U$ is therefore a under-$F$-cristal unite of $H$, and by construction the $F$-cristal $H/U$ is topologiquement nilpotent, this which acheve the proof.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:1.2.5}
Under the hypotheses of \ref{prop:1.2.4}, the $F$-cristal $U$ is caracterise, en termes of $H$, by $U = \bigcap_{n \geq 0} \Im F^n$ (or $F = F(\sigma)\sigma^*$). We shall say that $U$ is the \emph{under-$F$-cristal unite} of $H$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{F-cristaux ordinaires}

\subsubsection{}

Let $H$ a $F$-cristal on $A_0$, $H_0 = H \otimes_A A_0$. Let $\sigma$ a lifting $\sigma : A \to A$ of the Frobenius $F_{A_0}$, posons $H_0^{(p)} = F_{A_0}^*H_0 \otimes_{A_0} A_0$, and, for $i \in \mathbb{Z}$,
\begin{align}
M_iH_0^{(p)} &= \{x \in H_0^{(p)} \mid \text{il existe } y \in \sigma^*H \text{ relevant } x \text{ et tel que } F(\sigma)y \in p^iH\}\,, \label{eq:1.3.1.1} \\
\Fil^iH_0 &= H_0 \cap M_iH_0^{(p)} \label{eq:1.3.1.2}
\end{align}
(or $H_0$ is considered as under-module of $F_{A_0}^*H_0^{(p)}$ by the fleche of adjonction),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.1.3}
\Fil_iH_0 = \{x \in H_0 \mid \text{il existe } y \in H \text{ relevant } x \text{ et tel que } p^iy \in \Im F(\sigma)\}\,.\end{equation}

of after (1.1.3.1), the under-modules $\Fil^iH_0$ (resp.\ $\Fil_iH_0$) ne dependent not of the choix of $\sigma$; they forment a filtration decroissante (resp.\ croissante) of $H_0$, that one appelle \emph{Hodge filtration} (resp.\ \emph{filtration conjuguee}), cf.\ [15, 1.9], or this filtration is notre $F^{\,i}$ (resp.\ $F_{\mathrm{con}}^{\,-i}$). One can montrer [15, 2.2] that the one has also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.1.4}
\Fil_iH_0 = \{x \in H_0 \mid \text{il existe } y \in H \text{ relevant } x \text{ et tel que } F(\sigma)\sigma^*y \in p^iH\}\,.\end{equation}

The terminologie provient of the theorem of Mazur--Ogus [3, 8.26] affirmant that si $X_0/A_0$ is a scheme (or scheme formel) proper and smooth such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the cohomology cristalline $H_{\crys}^*(X_0/A)$ Let $A$-module free,
\item the spectral sequence $E_1^{i,j} = H^j(X_0, \Omega_{X_0/A_0}^i) \Rightarrow H_{\crys}^*(X_0/A_0)$ degenere en $E_1$ and $E_1$ let free on $A_0$ and of formation compatible to the changement of base,
\end{enumerate}
then si the one sets $H = H_{\crys}^n(X_0/A)$ (therefore $H_0 = H_{\crys}^n(X_0/A_0)$) ($n$ integer fixe) the filtration $\Fil_iH_0$ (resp.\ $\Fil^iH_0$) coincide with the filtration canonical on the aboutissement of the spectral sequence ci-dessus (resp.\ the spectral sequence conjuguee $H^i(X_0, \mathcal{H}^j(\Omega_{X_0/A_0}^\bullet))$).

Recall [15, 1.6] that the filtrations (1.3.1.2) and (1.3.1.3) are finite, separees and exhaustives, and that for $n$ given the conditions $\Fil^{n+1}H_0 = 0$ and $\Fil_nH_0 = H_0$ are equivalentes: one dit then that $H_0$ is of niveau $\leq n$.

One notera
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.1.5}
\gr^iH_0 \quad \text{(resp.\ } \gr_iH_0\text{)}
\end{equation}
the gradue associated a the filtration $\Fil^iH_0$ (resp.\ $\Fil_iH_0$). Si $\gr_iH_0$ is free on $A_0$, it en is similarly of $\gr^iH_0$, the formation of $\gr_iH_0$ and $\gr^iH$ commute to the changement of base, and, for every $i$, $\gr_iH_0$ and $\gr^iH_0$ are canonically isomorphes, in particular have same rank $h_i$, that one appellera $i$-eme \emph{nombre of Hodge} of $H$ [15, 1.12, 2.3].

Recall of other part [15, 1.6, 2.6] that the filtration conjuguee is horizontale for the connexion $\nabla$ induite on $H_0$ (and of gradue associated of $p$-courbure zero), tandis that the Hodge filtration satisfies the condition of transversalite of Griffiths
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.1.6}
\nabla_0\Fil^iH_0 \subset \Omega^1_{A_0/k} \otimes \Fil^{i-1}H_0\,.
\end{equation}

The notion of $F$-cristal ordinaire a ete degagee by Mazur [12] and Ogus, a which is due the caracterisation suivante [15, 3.1.3]:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1.3.2}
Let $H$ a $F$-cristal on $A_0$ such that $\gr_\bullet H_0$ let free on $A_0$. The conditions suivantes are equivalentes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the polygones of Newton and of Hodge of the $F$-cristal $e_0^*H$ induces by $H$ to the point closed $e_0 : A \to k$ (1.1.4) coincident;
\item[(i$'$)] for every point $s_0$ of $\Spec(A_0)$ with values in a extension algebriquement close $k'$ of $k$, the polygones of Newton and of Hodge of the $F$-cristal $s_0^*(H)$ (1.1.4) coincident;
\item the Hodge filtrations and conjuguee of $H_0$ are opposees, i.e.\ one has, for every $i$, $H_0 = \Fil_iH_0 \oplus \Fil^{i+1}H_0$;
\item there exists a unique filtration of $H$ by of the under-$F$-cristaux
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.2.1}
0 \subset U_0 \subset U_1 \subset \cdots \subset U_i \subset U_{i+1} \subset \cdots
\end{equation}
such that $U_i$ releve $\Fil_iH_0$, and that $U_i/U_{i-1}$ let of the form $V_i(-i)$, or $V_i$ is a $F$-cristal unite and $(-i)$ designe the twisting a the Tate consistant a remplacer $F$ by $p^iF$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:1.3.3}
One dit that a $F$-cristal $H$ on $A_0$ is \emph{ordinaire} si $\gr_\bullet H_0$ is free and $H$ satisfies the conditions equivalentes of \ref{prop:1.3.2}.
\end{definition}

Demonstrons \ref{prop:1.3.2}. the implication (i$'$) $\Rightarrow$ (i) is trivial, and it is clear that (iii) entraine (i$'$). Prouvons (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii). Supposons $H$ of niveau $\leq n$, and the conclusion etablie for the $F$-cristaux of niveau $\leq n-1$. Notons $F$ the endomorphism $p$-lineaire of $H_0$ induces by $F(\sigma)$ for a lifting $\sigma$ ($F$ ne depend not of this choix). By definition (1.3.1), one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.3.1}
\Fil_0H_0 = \Im F : H_0^{(p)} \to H_0\,, \qquad \Fil^1H_0 = \Ker F : H_0 \to H_0\,.\end{equation}

the hypothese (i) entraine of abord that the rank stable of $e_0^*H_0$ is $h_0 = \rg \Fil_0H_0 = \rg \Fil^0H_0$ (coincidence of the parties of pente 0 of the polygones of Newton and of Hodge of $e_0^*H$). Let $s_0$ a point of $\Spec(A_0)$ with values in a extension algebriquement close of the field of the fractions of $A_0$. The proof of \ref{prop:1.2.4} shows that the one has
\[
\rg\nolimits.\st(s_0^*H_0) \geq \rg\nolimits.\st(e_0^*H_0)
\]
($\rg\nolimits.\st =$ rank stable), but as one has also $\rg\nolimits.\st(s_0^*H_0) \leq h_0$ of after (1.3.3.1), one deduces
\[
\rg\nolimits.\st(e_0^*H_0) = \rg\nolimits.\st(s_0^*H_0) = h_0\,.
\]
It follows of a part that the one has
\[
\Fil_0(s_0^*H_0) = (s_0^*H_0)^{\mathrm{ss}}\,, \qquad \Fil^1(s_0^*H_0) = (s_0^*H_0)^{\mathrm{nilp}}\,,
\]
therefore $s_0^*\Fil_0H_0 \cap s_0^*\Fil^1H_0 = 0$, therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.3.2}
H_0 = \Fil_0H_0 \oplus \Fil^1H_0\,.\end{equation}

of other part, of after \ref{prop:1.2.4}, one has a exact sequence of $F$-cristaux on $A_0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.3.3}
0 \longrightarrow U \longrightarrow H \longrightarrow E \longrightarrow 0\,,
\end{equation}
or $U$ is the under-$F$-cristal unite of $H$, and $E$ a $F$-cristal topologiquement nilpotent. As by definition $\rg(U_0) = \rg\nolimits.\st(e_0^*H_0) = h_0$, $U_0$ releve $\Fil_0H_0$, therefore the projection $H \to E$ induces a isomorphism $\Fil^1H_0 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} E_0$, this which signifie that $F(\sigma) : \sigma^*E \to E$ is divisible by $p$, therefore that $E = E'(-1)$, or $E'$ is a $F$-cristal of niveau $\leq n-1$. It is clear that $e_0^*E$, therefore also $e_0^*E'$, a same polygones of Newton and of Hodge, therefore by the hypothese of recurrence appliquee a $E'$, one en conclut that the Hodge filtrations and conjuguees of $E_0 = E \otimes_A A_0$ are opposees. One aura therefore proves (ii) si the one checks that the projection $H \to E$ induces of the isomorphisms (for $i \geq 0$)
\[
\Fil^iH_0/\Fil_0H_0 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \Fil^iE_0\,, \qquad \Fil_{i+1}H_0 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \Fil_{i+1}E_0\,.\]
Or it is clear that these fleches are injectives, and leur surjectivite resulte aisement of this that $U$ is a $F$-cristal unite.

Prouvons maintenant (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii). One assumed a new $H$ of niveau $\leq n$, and the conclusion etablie for the $F$-cristaux of niveau $\leq n-1$. the unicite of (1.3.2.1) is clear, compte tenu of the hypothese of recurrence, car $U_0$ is necessarily the under-$F$-cristal unite of $H$. For the existence, notons that one has (1.3.3.2) by hypothese. of after (1.3.3.1), one deduces
\[
e_0^*\Fil_0H_0 = (e_0^*H_0)^{\mathrm{ss}}\,, \qquad s_0^*\Fil_0H_0 = (s_0^*H_0)^{\mathrm{ss}}
\]
(car the decomposition en part semisimple and part nilpotente is unique), therefore the rangs stables of $e_0^*H_0$ and $s_0^*H_0$ are egaux. Grace a \ref{prop:1.2.4}, one obtains therefore again a exact sequence (1.3.3.3). one has vu ci-dessus that the Hodge filtrations and conjuguees of $E_0$ se deduisent of celles of $H_0$ by passage to the quotient: they are therefore opposees. As $E = E'(-1)$, with $E'$ of niveau $\leq n-1$, the hypothese of recurrence entraine the existence of a filtration of $E$ by of the under-$F$-cristaux
\[
0 = W_0 \subset W_1 \subset \cdots \subset W_i \subset W_{i+1} \subset \cdots
\]
such that $W_i$ releve $\Fil_iE_0$, and $W_i/W_{i-1}$ let of the form $T_i(-i)$, with $T_i$ a $F$-cristal unite. For $i \geq 1$, notons $U_i$ the inverse image of $W_i$ in $H$. The $U_i$, for $i \geq 1$, and $U_0$ forment a filtration (1.3.2.1), which satisfait visiblement to the conditions enoncees en (iii). This acheve the proof of \ref{prop:1.3.2}.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:1.3.4}
Si $A_0 = k$, the filtration (1.3.2.1) admet a scindage unique
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.4.1}
U_i = \bigoplus_{j \leq i} V_j(-j)\,,
\end{equation}
or $V_j$ is a $F$-cristal unite. C'is a cas particulier of the theorem of Katz [10, 1.6.1], but it is easy of verify this point directement: the unicite is clear, car, for $j < i$, $\Hom(V_i(-i), V_j(-j)) = 0$, quant a the existence of the scindage, it s'etablit aisement by recurrence on the niveau, a the aide of a argument of point fixe analogue a ceux utilises in the proof of \ref{prop:1.2.4}.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{}

Let $H$ a $F$-cristal on $A_0$. One appellera \emph{Hodge filtration} on $H$ a filtration finite decroissante by of the under-$A$-modules libres
\[
\Fil^0H = H \supset \Fil^1H \supset \cdots \supset \Fil^iH \supset \Fil^{i+1}H \supset \cdots
\]
verifiant the deux conditions suivantes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item for every $i$, $\Fil^iH$ releve $\Fil^iH_0$,
\item (``transversalite'') for every $i$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.5.1}
\nabla\Fil^iH \subset \Omega^1_{A/W} \otimes \Fil^{i-1}H\,.\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

One appellera \emph{$F$-cristal of Hodge} on $A_0$ a $F$-cristal on $A_0$ equipped with a Hodge filtration.

A example standard is fourni, in the situation geometric envisagee en 1.3.1, by the datum of a lifting of $X_0/A_0$ en a scheme formel proper and smooth $X/A$ such that the spectral sequence of Hodge of $X/A$, $E_1^{i,j} = H^j(X, \Omega_{X/A}^i) \Rightarrow H_{dR}^*(X/A)$ ($= H_{\crys}^*(X_0/A)$), degenere en $E_1$, with a terme $E_1$ free on $A$. The Hodge filtration of $H_{dR}^*(X/A)$ aboutissement of this spectral sequence, satisfies the conditions (i) and (ii) ci-dessus.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1.3.6}
Let $(H, \Fil^\bullet H)$ a $F$-cristal of Hodge on $A_0$. Si $H$ is ordinaire (\ref{def:1.3.3}), the filtrations $U_i$ (1.3.2.1) and $\Fil^\bullet H$ are opposees, i.e.\ one has, for every $i$, $H = U_i \oplus \Fil^{i+1}H$, of or a decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.6.1}
H = \bigoplus_i H^i\,, \qquad H^i = U_i \cap \Fil^iH\,,
\end{equation}
with $H^i$ of rank $h_i$ (1.3.1.5).
\end{proposition}

C'is a consequence immediate of \ref{prop:1.3.2} and 1.3.5 (i) (the condition (ii) ne sert not).

In the example ci-dessus, supposons the $F$-cristal $H = H_{dR}^n(X/A)$ ordinaire. For each $r \geq 1$, the spectral sequence conjuguee
\[
(*)_{r}\quad E_2^{i,j} = H^i(X \otimes W_r, \mathcal{H}^j(\Omega_{X \otimes W_r/A \otimes W_r}^\bullet)) \Longrightarrow H_{dR}^*(X \otimes W_r/A \otimes W_r)
\]
defines a filtration $(U_{i,r})$ on $H_{dR}^*(X \otimes W_r/A \otimes W_r)$. One can esperer that the projective limit of the spectral sequences $(*)_r$ is a spectral sequence, of which the filtration aboutissement on $H$ is the limite of the $(U_{i,r})$ and coincide with the filtration $(U_i)$.

\subsection{F-cristaux of Hodge ordinaires of niveau $\leq 1$}

\subsubsection{}

Let $H$ a $F$-cristal of Hodge ordinaire of niveau $\leq 1$ on $A_0$. of after \ref{prop:1.3.2}, one has therefore a extension of $F$-cristaux
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.1.1}
0 \longrightarrow U \longrightarrow H \longrightarrow V(-1) \longrightarrow 0\,,
\end{equation}
or $U$ and $V$ are of the $F$-cristaux unites, and $U$ releve $\Fil_0H_0$.

One notera $r$ (resp.\ $s$) the rank of $U$ (resp.\ $V$). of other part, $H$ is equipped of the under-module free $\Fil^1H$, which releve $\Fil^1H_0$, therefore satisfies, of after (1.3.1.4),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.1.2}
F(\sigma)\sigma^*\Fil^1H \subset pH
\end{equation}
for every lifting $\sigma$ of Frobenius. Moreover, of after \ref{prop:1.3.6}, $H$, en tant that $A$-module, se decompose en direct sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.1.3}
H = U \oplus \Fil^1H\,.\end{equation}
In particular, $\Fil^1H$ is of rank $s$, and se projette isomorphiquement on $V$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:1.4.2}
With the notations of \emph{1.4.1}:

\emph{(i)} There exists a base $a = (a_i)_{1 \leq i \leq r}$ of the $A$-module $U$ and $b = (b_i)_{1 \leq i \leq s}$ of the $A$-module $\Fil^1H$ verifiant the conditions
\begin{align}
\nabla a_i &= 0\,, && 1 \leq i \leq r\,, \label{eq:1.4.2.1} \\
\nabla b_i &= \sum_{1 \leq j \leq r} \eta_{ij} \otimes a_j\,, && \eta_{ij} \in \Omega^1_{A/W}\,, \quad 1 \leq i \leq s\,, \label{eq:1.4.2.2} \\
F(\sigma)\sigma^*a_i &= a_i\,, && 1 \leq i \leq r\,, \label{eq:1.4.2.3} \\
F(\sigma)\sigma^*b_i &= pb_i + p\sum_{1 \leq j \leq r} u_{ij}(\sigma)a_j\,, && u_{ij}(\sigma) \in A\,, \quad 1 \leq i \leq s\,, \label{eq:1.4.2.4}
\end{align}
for every lifting $\sigma$ of Frobenius. Moreover, the forms $\eta_{ij}$ are fermees, and satisfy, for every lifting $\sigma$ of Frobenius,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2.5}
\sigma^*\eta_{ij} = p\eta_{ij} + pdu_{ij}(\sigma)\,.\end{equation}

\emph{(ii)} The bases $a$ and $b$ being choisies as en (i), there exists a unique family of series formelles $\tau_{ij} \in K[[t]]$ (or $K$ is the field of the fractions of $W$) such that, quels that let $i$ and $j$,
\begin{align}
d\tau_{ij} &= \eta_{ij}\,, \label{eq:1.4.2.6} \\
\sigma^*\tau_{ij} - p\tau_{ij} - pu_{ij}(\sigma) &= 0 \label{eq:1.4.2.7}
\end{align}
for every lifting $\sigma$ of Frobenius. one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2.8}
\tau_{ij}(0) \in pW\,.\end{equation}
Moreover, si $p \neq 2$, the series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2.9}
q_{ij} = \exp(\tau_{ij})\end{equation}
sony defined, appartiennent a $A$, and satisfy $q_{ij}(0) \equiv 1 \pmod{pW}$.
\end{theorem}

Prouvons (i). Vu the structure of the $F$-cristaux unites (\ref{prop:1.2.2}), there exists a base $a = (a_i)_{1 \leq i \leq r}$ of $U$ verifiant (1.4.2.1) and (1.4.2.3), and a base $b = (b_i)_{1 \leq i \leq s}$ of $\Fil^1H$ such that, si $b_i'$ designe the image of $b_i$ in $V(-1)$, one ait $\nabla b_i' = 0$ and $F(\sigma)\sigma^*b_i' = pb_i'$ for every $i$ and all lifting $\sigma$ of Frobenius. By sequence $\nabla b_i$ and $F(\sigma)\sigma^*b_i$ s'ecrivent under the forms (1.4.2.2) and (1.4.2.4). the integrabilite of $\nabla$ entraine, by map of $\nabla$ a (1.4.2.2), that $d\eta_{ij} = 0$ quels that let $i$ and $j$. of other part, appliquant $\nabla$ a (1.4.2.4), and utilisant the horizontalite of $F$ (1.1.3.3), one obtains the relations (1.4.2.5).

Prouvons (ii). The forms $\eta_{ij}$ being fermees, there exists, en vertu of the lemma of Poincare, of the series $\tau_{ij} \in K[[t]]$, determinees a constant pres, such that the one ait (1.4.2.6). The relation (1.4.2.5) entraine then that
\[
\sigma^*\tau_{ij} - p\tau_{ij} - pu_{ij}(\sigma) \in K\,,
\]
therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2.10}
\sigma^*\tau_{ij} - p\tau_{ij} - pu_{ij}(\sigma) = (\sigma^*\tau_{ij})(0) - p\tau_{ij}(0) - pu_{ij}(\sigma)(0)\,.\end{equation}
Notons $x \mapsto x^\sigma$ the automorphism of Frobenius of $K$. Fixons all of abord the lifting $\sigma$ of Frobenius such that $\sigma(t_i) = t_i^p$, and normalisons $\tau_{ij}$ by the condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2.11}
(\sigma^*\tau_{ij})(0) - p\tau_{ij}(0) - pu_{ij}(\sigma)(0) = 0\end{equation}
which s'ecrit again (car $(\sigma^*\tau_{ij})(0) = \tau_{ij}(0)^\sigma$)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2.12}
\tau_{ij}(0) = \sum_{n \geq 1} V^nu_{ij}(\sigma)(0)\end{equation}
(or $Vx = pF^{-1}x = px^\sigma$). In particular, one has (1.4.2.8).

Montrons that, si $\sigma$ is a lifting quelconque of Frobenius, the condition (1.4.2.7), with $\sigma$ remplace by $\sigma$, is satisfaite. Compte tenu of (1.4.2.10) (with $\sigma$ remplace by $\sigma$), it revient to the same of verify that the one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2.13}

\begin{lemma}[{[5], Lemma 1}]\label{lem:1.4.3}
Notons $\sigma$ the automorphism $\sigma$-lineaire of $K[[t]]$ (or $t = (t_1, \ldots, t_n)$ and $K$ is the field of the fractions of $W$) given by $t_i \mapsto t_i^p$. Let $f \in K[[t]]$ such that $f(0) = 1$. The conditions suivantes are equivalentes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $f \in W[[t]]$,
\item $(\sigma f)/f^p = 1 + pu$, with $u \in W[[t]]$, $u(0) = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

the implication (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) is immediate. Recall the proof of (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i). Ecrivons $f = \sum_i a_it^i$ ($a_0 = 1$), and supposons proves that $a_i \in W$ for $|i| < r$ (or $|i| = i_1 + \cdots + i_n$). Notant $(\ )_{<j}$ the part of degree total $< j$, one calcule
\[
((\sum_{|i| \geq 0} a_it^i)^p)_{<r} = ((\sum_{|i| \leq r-1} a_it^i)^p)_{<r} + p\sum_{|i|=r} a_it^i \pmod{pW[[t]]}\,,
\]
then, posant $(\sigma f)/f^p = 1 + p\sum_i d_it^i$ ($d_i \in W$),
\[
(\sum_{|i| \geq 0} a_i^\sigma t^{pi})(1 + p\sum_{|i| \geq 1} d_it^i) = ((\sum_{|i| \leq r-1} a_it^i)^p)_{<r} \pmod{pW[[t]]}\,,
\]
\[
(1 + p\sum_{|i| \geq 1} d_it^i)(\sum_{|i| \leq r-1} a_i^\sigma t^{pi})_{<r} \equiv (\sum_{|i| \leq r-1} a_i^pt^{pi})_{<r} \pmod{pW[[t]]}\,.\]

Comparant the deux expressions obtained for $(f^p)_{<r}$, one trouve
\[
p\sum_{|i|=r} a_it^i \in pW[[t]]\,,
\]
therefore $a_i \in W$ for every $i$ such that $|i| = r$, this which proves (ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i).

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:1.4.4}
Let $g \in K[[t]]$ such that $g(0) = 0$. The conditions suivantes are equivalentes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\exp(g) \in W[[t]]$,
\item $\sigma^*g - pg \in pW[[t]]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

Indeed, posons $f = \exp(g)$. As, for $n > 1$, $p^n/n!$ (and a fortiori $p^n/n$) appartiennent a $pW$, the condition (ii) equivaut a dire that $\sigma f/f^p = 1 + pu$, with $u \in W[[t]]$, $u(0) = 0$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:1.4.5}

\end{corollary}

Indeed, let $h = g - g(0)$. As $p \neq 2$ and that $g(0) \in pW$, $\exp(g(0))$ is defined, therefore also $\exp(g) = \exp(g(0))\exp(h)$. Posons $g(0) = a$ ($a \in W$). one has
