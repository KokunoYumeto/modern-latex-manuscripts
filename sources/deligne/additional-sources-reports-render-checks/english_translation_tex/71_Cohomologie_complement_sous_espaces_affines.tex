\section*{0.\ Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{0.\ Introduction}

Let $X$ the complement in a affine space reel $V$ of a set locally finite $E \subset \{W \subsetneq V \mid W \text{ sous-espace affine de } V\}$ of under-spaces affins proper of $V$. The \emph{plats} of $E$ are $V$ and the intersections not vides of elements of $E$:
In [GM] (part III), Goresky and MacPherson appliquent the theorie of Morse a function ``distance a $v_0 \in V$'' for (a) decomposer the homology of $X$ en factors indexes by the plats and (b) donner a description combinatoire of each factor. To the \S\,1, we retrouvons these results by of the techniques faisceautiques. They mettent en evidence of what depend the decomposition of [GM], and s'appliquent also a the determination of the cohomology $\ell$-adique of the complement of a family finite of under-affine spaces in a affine space over a field algebriquement clos.

By duality, these results en homology se transposent en cohomology. For $E$ finite, one has a decomposition indexee by the plats
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.1}
H^*_c(X) \cong \bigoplus_A h^*(A).
\tag{0.1}
\end{equation}
Let $\coor(A)$ the $\mathbb{Z}$-module free of rank $1$, of bases $\pm e$ the coorientations of $A$ in $V$; and mettons-the en degree $\codim(A)$. one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.2}
\coor(A) \otimes H^*(V;A) \cong H^{*+\codim(A)}_c(V;A).
\tag{0.2}
\end{equation}
The direct factor $h^*(A)$ is the tensor product of $\coor(A)$ by a group ne dependant that of the combinatoire of the arrangement, i.e., that of the set ordonne by inclusion. The decomposition \eqref{eq:0.1} depend of the choix for each plat $A$ of a point
\[
x_A \in A^0 := A \setminus \bigcup\{\text{plats plus petits}\}.
\]
Seule importe the composante connected of $A^0$ or se trouve $x_A$. In particular, si the hypothese of connexite (CONN) suivante is verifiee, the decomposition \eqref{eq:0.1} ne depend of aucun choix.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{(CONN)} \quad Si a plat $B$ is contenu in a plat $A$, and $B \subset A$ or $\dim(A) - \dim(B) \geq 2$:
\end{quote}

To the \S\,4.2, under the hypothese (CONN), we calculons the cup product of $H^*_c(X)$; en terme of the decomposition \eqref{eq:0.1}. The point essential is that the composante $h^*(A) \otimes h^*(B) \to h^*(C)$ of the cup product ne can be not zero that for $C = A \cap B$; the intersection being transverse. We the montrons en interpretant the cup product as the product externe suivi of a restriction a the diagonale $X \to X \times X$; and en traitant this restriction as a cas particulier of a morphism of restriction $H^*(X) \to H^*(X')$; for $X' = V' \cap X$ the trace of $X$ on a under-affine space.

Under the hypothese (CONN), it follows of this calcul that the algebra of cohomology $H^*_c(X)$ ne depend a isomorphism unique pres that of the data (a) a (d) suivantes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the set $\mathcal{C}$ of the plats, ordonne by inclusion;
\item the function $A \mapsto \codim(A)$;
\item for each plat $A$, the $\mathbb{Z}$-module free of rank $1$, $\coor(A)$;
\item for $C = \inf(A,B)$ and $\codim(C) = \codim(A) + \codim(B)$ (i.e., for $C = A \pitchfork B$, a intersection transverse), the isomorphism natural of $\coor(A) \otimes \coor(B)$ with $\coor(C)$.
\end{enumerate}

M. of Longueville and C.~Schultz have independemment determine the algebra of cohomology integer of the complement of a arrangement central verifiant (CONN) [dS]. See also [FZ].

\textbf{Remerciements.} Notre interet a ete attire on these questions by the preprint [Y] of Yuzvinsky. In the cas of the arrangements complexes, Yuzvinsky determine a isomorphism pres, a the aide of the results of Morgan [M], the algebra of cohomology rational of the complement of a arrangement.

We prendrons the notation of stratification to the sens faible suivant: a stratification of a space topologique $T$, indexee by a set ordonne finite $I$, is a decomposition indexee by $I$ of $T$ en parties $S_i$ not vides disjointes $S_i$; the \emph{strates}, such that for every $i$ in $I$ the reunion $T_i := \bigcup_{j \leq i} S_j$ let closed. The $T_i$ are the strates fermees. Noter that $i \leq j$ if and only if $T_i \subset T_j$; and that the $T_i$ determinent the $S_i$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.1}
S_i = T_i \setminus \bigcup_{j<i} T_j.
\tag{1.1.1}
\end{equation}

Let $\tau$ the map of $T$ in $I$ valant $i$ on $S_i$. Si $I$ is equipped with the topologie for which the adherence of a point $i$ is the intervalle $[-\infty,i] := \{j \mid j \leq i\}$, dire that the $T_i$ are fermes equivaut a dire that the map $\tau$ is continue.

One notera again $I$ the category of objects the elements of $I$, with a fleche of $i$ a $j$ for $i \leq j$.

Each point $i$ of the space topologique $I$ a more petit neighborhood: the intervalle $[i,+\infty] := \{j \mid i \leq j\}$. For a sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $I$, the fiber $\mathcal{F}_i$ of $\mathcal{F}$ en $i$ coincide therefore with $\mathcal{F}([i,+\infty])$. For $i \leq j$, the restriction of $\mathcal{F}([i,+\infty])$ a $[j,+\infty]$ is a morphism of $\mathcal{F}_i$ in $\mathcal{F}_j$; and this construction identifie sheaves of groups abeliens on $I$ and functors $\mathcal{F}$ of $I$ in $(\mathrm{Ab})$.

Si $I$ a more petit element $0$, the functor $\Gamma_0(I;\mathcal{F}) = \mathcal{F}_0$ is exact and the $H^i$ superieurs are nuls. Si $I$ a more grand element $1$, all sheaf constant is flasque and ses $H^i$ superieurs are therefore nuls: the space $I$ is acyclique, i.e., sa cohomology a coefficients constants is celle of a point.

\textit{Other Proof.} Calculant the cohomology by the resolution flasque canonical, one checks that the cohomology a coefficients constants of $I$ coincide with celle of the space classifiant $|I|$ of the category $I$. In particular, it is the same for $I$ and for $I$ equipped with the ordre oppose, and one has deja vu that all $I$ ayant a more petit element is acyclique.

\begin{lemma}[Lemma of Devissage 1.3]\label{lem:1.3}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ a class of sheaves on $I$. Si $\mathcal{C}$ contient the sheaf $0$, contient all sheaf constant on a closed $[-\infty,i]$ prolonge by $0$ on $I$ and si for every exact sequence courte $0 \to \mathcal{F}_1 \to \mathcal{F}_2 \to \mathcal{F}_3 \to 0$ of sheaves on $I$, si deux of the $\mathcal{F}_i$ are in $\mathcal{C}$, the troisieme the is also, then $\mathcal{C}$ contient all the sheaves on $I$.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textit{Proof.} One procede by recurrence on the nombre of elements of $I$. For $i: J \hookrightarrow I$ a closed of $I$ distinct of $I$, appliquant the hypothese of recurrence a the class of the sheaves on $J$ such that $i_!$ let in $\mathcal{C}$, one sees that all sheaves of support $\subset I$ is in $\mathcal{C}$. Si $I$ is vide, the lemma is clear. Otherwise, let $x$ a element maximal of $I$ and $j$ the inclusion of $\{x\}$ in $I$. Prenant for $\mathcal{F}_1$ (resp.~$\mathcal{F}_2$, $\mathcal{F}_3$) a sheaf constant on $\{x\}$ (resp.~$[-\infty,x]$, $[-\infty,x[$ prolonge by zero), one sees that for every group abelien $M$, $j_!M$ is in $\mathcal{C}$. For $\mathcal{F}$ on $I$, one conclut en observant that the support of $\mathcal{F} = j_!j^*\mathcal{F}$ is contenu in the closed $I \setminus \{x\}$.

\begin{proposition}[1.4]\label{prop:1.4}
Si the strates fermees are acycliques, then, for every sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $I$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.1}
H^*(I;\mathcal{F}) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^*(T;\tau^*\mathcal{F}).
\tag{1.4.1}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textit{Proof.} Si $\mathcal{F}$ is a sheaf constant on a closed $[-\infty,i]$, prolonge by zero on $I$, $\tau^*\mathcal{F}$ is constant on $T_i$, prolonge by zero on $T$, and (\ref{eq:1.4.1}) resulte of this that $[-\infty,i]$ and $T_i$ are acycliques.

The cas general en resulte by devissage: for $0 \to \mathcal{F}_1 \to \mathcal{F}_2 \to \mathcal{F}_3 \to 0$ a exact sequence courte of sheaves on $I$; the deux membres of (\ref{eq:1.4.1}) donnent lieu a exact sequence longue of cohomology, and si (\ref{eq:1.4.1}) is a isomorphism for deux of the $\mathcal{F}_i$, it the is therefore also for the troisieme. One conclut by \ref{lem:1.3}.

\begin{remark}[1.5]\label{rem:1.5}
Si $|I|$ is the space classifiant of the category $I$, one defines a map continue of $|I|$ in $I$ en envoyant the interieur of the simplexe $[i_0;\ldots;i_n]$ ($i_0 < i_1 < \cdots < i_n$) on $i_n$. By this map, the inverse image of $[-\infty,i]$ is $|[{-\infty},i]|$, a cone of sommet $i$. the hypothese of \ref{prop:1.4} is therefore verifiee and prenant for $\mathcal{F}$ a sheaf constant, one retrouve that $I$ and $|I|$ have same cohomology.
\end{remark}

\tag{1.6.2}

Choisissons in each strate $S_i$ a point $x_i$. For every sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $I$, one dispose of a inverse image en cohomology a support

\tag{1.6.3}
\end{equation}
Noter that $U_i := \bigcup_{i \leq j} S_j$ is a open of $S$, and that $S_i$ is closed in $U_i$. One dispose of a cup-product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.6.4}

\tag{1.6.4}
\end{equation}
In the cas of $\mathcal{F} = \mathbb{Z}$, $H^*_{\{x_i\}}(S_i;\mathbb{Z})$ is of twisting, and $H^*_{S_i}(U_i;\tau^*\mathcal{F})$ is without twisting on $H^*_{S_i}(U_i;\mathbb{Z})$. The cup-product is simplement the composition. The compose

\end{equation}

Composant (\ref{eq:1.6.3}) and (\ref{eq:1.6.5}), one obtains of the morphisms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.6.6}
H^*_{\{i\}}([i,+\infty];\mathcal{F}) \otimes \ori_i^{-1} \longrightarrow H^*_c(U_i;\tau^*\mathcal{F})
\tag{1.6.6}
\end{equation}
which, composes with $H^*_c(U_i;\tau^*\mathcal{F}) \to H^*_c(T;\tau^*\mathcal{F})$, donnent
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.6.7}

\tag{1.6.7}
\end{equation}

\end{theorem}

\subsection{1.10. Variantes: Homology and cohomology of $S_1$}\label{subsec:1.10}

Si $V$ is a variety orientee purement of dimension $n$, the cap-product with the class fondamentale is a isomorphism of the cohomology with compact support of $V$ with the homology of $V$:
\[H^i_c(V;M) \xrightarrow{\sim} H_{n-i}(V;M).\]

The result obtained en cohomology with compact support or, this which revient ici to the same, en homology, fournissent by duality (between homology and cohomology, or of Poincare between cohomology and cohomology with compact support) of the results analogues en cohomology.

For the cohomology with coefficients in a field $k$, one has simplement $H_i = \Hom_k(H^i,k)$ (resp.~$H^i_c = \Hom_k(H^{n-i},k)$), and these vector spaces are in notre cas of dimension finite. \begin{example}[1.11]\label{ex:1.11}
Let $V$ denoter a affine space reel and $\mathcal{A}$ a set finite of under-affine spaces $A \neq \emptyset, V$ of $V$, such that si the intersection of deux elements of $\mathcal{A}$ is not vide, this intersection is in $\mathcal{A}$. One sets
\[\mathcal{C} := \mathcal{A} \cup \{V\} \quad \text{et} \quad X := V \setminus \bigcup_{A \in \mathcal{A}} A.\]
We appellerons $\mathcal{C}$ (resp.~$\mathring{\mathcal{C}}$) the set of the plats (resp.~plats proper) of the \emph{arrangement}. The plats are of the varieties acycliques. One denotes $\tau': V \to \mathcal{C}$ the map corresponding. One notera parfois $A^0$ the strate corresponding to the plat $A$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.11.1}
A^0 := \tau'^{-1}(A) = A \setminus \bigcup\{\text{plats plus petits}\}.
\tag{1.11.1}
\end{equation}
(cf.~(\ref{eq:1.1.1})).

The plats are of the varieties acycliques. Appliquons \ref{cor:1.8}: the choix in each strate $A^0$ of a point $x_A$ defines a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.11.2}
H^*_c(X;M) \cong \bigoplus_A h^*_c(A) \qquad (\text{somme sur les plats}),
\tag{1.11.2}
\end{equation}
with $h^*_c(A) = H^*_{\{A\}}([A,V];j_{V!}M) \otimes \ori(A)$, $j_V$ being the inclusion of $\{V\}$ in $\mathcal{C}$.

In [GM], Goresky and MacPherson obtiennent a telle decomposition of the homology of $X$ en appliquant the theorie of Morse a function ``distance a $v_0 \in V$''. The structure Euclidienne of $V$ and the point $v_0$ are choisis generiquement, to the sens that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item for each plat $A$, the point critique on $A$ of the function distance $d(v_0,\bullet)$, i.e., the pied $x_A$ of the perpendiculaire abaissee of $v_0$ on $A$, is in $A^0$;
\item the values critiques $d(v_0,x_A)$ are distinctes.

for the $\ori(A_1) \otimes \ori(A_2) \xrightarrow{\sim} \ori(A_1 \times A_2)$ and for $X_1 \times X_2$. The diagram
\begin{tikzcd}
R\Gamma_{\{A_1\}}([A_1,V_1];j_{V_1!}M) \otimes \ori(A_1) \otimes^L R\Gamma_{\{A_2\}}([A_2,V_2];j_{V_2!}M) \otimes \ori(A_2) \arrow[r] \arrow[d, "\sim"'] & R\Gamma_c(X_1;M) \otimes^L R\Gamma_c(X_2;M) \arrow[d, "\sim"] \\\
R\Gamma_{\{A_1 \times A_2\}}([A_1 \times A_2,V];j_{V!}M) \otimes \ori(A_1 \times A_2) \arrow[r] & R\Gamma_c(X;M)
\end{tikzcd}
is commutative. The decomposition (\ref{eq:4.1.2}) of $R\Gamma_c(X;M)$ is therefore simplement the tensor product derive of the decompositions (\ref{eq:4.1.2}) of the factors $R\Gamma_c(X_i;M)$ ($i=1,2$). Passons to the dual, note $D(\bullet) := \mathrm{RHom}_M(\bullet,M)$. Under of the hypotheses of finitude verifiees ici, c'is in the derived category a operation involutive commutant to the tensor product, and the duality of Poincare dit that $R\Gamma(X_i;M)$ is the dual of $R\Gamma_c(X_i;M)$; tordu by $\ori(V_i)$:
\[R\Gamma(X_i;M) = D(R\Gamma_c(X_i;M)) \otimes \ori(V_i).\]
Similarly for $X$.

the isomorphism (\ref{eq:4.1.2}) se dualise therefore en a isomorphism
\[\bigoplus_A R\Gamma(X_i;M) \xrightarrow{\sim} D(R\Gamma_{\{A\}}([A,V_i];j_{V_i!}M)) \otimes \coor(A)\]
and, by compatibility between formule of Kunneth and duality of Poincare, the diagram
\begin{tikzcd}
R\Gamma(X_1;M) \otimes^L R\Gamma(X_2;M) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & D(R\Gamma_{\{A_1\}}([A_1,V_1];j_{V_1!}M)) \otimes \coor(A_1) \otimes^L D(R\Gamma_{\{A_2\}}([A_2,V_2];j_{V_2!}M)) \otimes \coor(A_2) \arrow[d] \\\
R\Gamma(X;M) \arrow[r] & D(R\Gamma_{\{A_1 \times A_2\}}([A_1 \times A_2,V];j_{V!}M)) \otimes \coor(A_1 \times A_2)
\end{tikzcd}
is commutative. the isomorphism en seconde colonne, is the tensor product of a isomorphism $\coor(A_1 \times A_2) = \coor(A_1) \otimes \coor(A_2)$ with the isomorphism deduces by duality of (\ref{eq:4.1.3}).

\subsection{4.2}\label{subsec:4.2}

Let maintenant $(V,\mathcal{A})$ as en \ref{ex:1.11} and supposons verifiee the hypothese of connexite (CONN) of the introduction. One appliquera \ref{subsec:4.1} a $V \times V$. The cup-product en cohomology is the compose of the product externe deduces of the isomorphism etudie en \ref{subsec:4.1},
\[R\Gamma(X;M) \otimes^L R\Gamma(X;M) \xrightarrow{\sim} R\Gamma(X \times X;M)\]
and of the restriction a the diagonale
\[\Delta^*: H^*(X \times X;M) \longrightarrow H^*(X;M).\]
Celle-ci is the restriction to the under-space diagonal $V$ of $V \times V$. C'is a cas particulier of (\ref{eq:3.5.1}).

of after (\ref{eq:4.1.4}), the product externe envoie $h^*_c(A_1) \otimes h^*_c(A_2)$ in $h^*_c(A_1 \times A_2)$. That $A_1 \times A_2$ coupe transversalement the diagonale equivaut a this that $A_1$ and $A_2$ se coupent transversalement in $V$. of after \ref{subsec:3.5}, the seules composantes not nulles of the cup-product are therefore the composantes

h^*(A) \otimes h^*(B) \longrightarrow h^*(C)
\tag{4.2.1}
\end{equation}

In this case, (\ref{eq:4.2.1}) is deduces of the dual of the isomorphism (\ref{eq:4.1.3}) and of the dual of

for $\varphi: [A,V] \times [B,V] \to [C,V]$ the intersection.
