\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

Let $M$ a variety differentiable. One s'interesse to the deformations of the operateur
``product'' of deux functions en a product associatif but not necessarily
commutative. More precisely, one s'interesse to the deformations $*_{t}$ dependant of a
parametre formel $t$, of the form
\[
f *_{t} g = \sum c_{n}(f,g)\, t^{n}
\]
with $c_{n}$ $\R$-bilineaire and differentiel en $f$ and $g$. For a panorama of the theorie of these
``star-products'', we renvoyons a [10]. that one deforme the product usuel signifie that
$c_{0}(f, g) = fg$. one has
\[
f *_{t} g - g *_{t} f = \{f,g\}t + O(t^{2})
\]
with $\{f, g\} = c_{1}(f, g) - c_{1}(g, f)$ and $\{\, , \,\}$ is a crochet of Poisson.

Of Wilde and Lecomte [1], [2] and Fedosov [4] have shows that si $M$ is a variety
symplectique, there exists a deformation $*_{t}$ of the product donnant lieu to the crochet of
Poisson symplectique. Disons that deux deformations $*_{t}$ and $*'_{t}$ are isomorphes si
one passe of the a the other by $f \mapsto \sum c_{n}(f)t^{n}$ with $c_{0}(f) = f$ and $c_{n}$ $\R$-lineaire and
differentiel en $f$. The construction of Fedosov shows that the classes of isomorphie of
deformations $*_{t}$, donnant lieu to the crochet of Poisson symplectique, are classifiees
by the sequences $(R_{n})_{n > 0}$ of classes in $H^{2}(M)$. The methode of Wilde and Lecomte
permet a classification analogue.

Notre but is of comparer the methodes utilisees, the classifications correspon-
dantes, and of expliciter the effet of a changement of parametre formel
\[
t \mapsto \sum_{n \geq 1} a_{n} t^{n} \quad (a_{1} \text{ inversible}).
\]


\section{Deformations}

\subsection{}

Let $M$ a variety differentiable (toujours assumed paracompacte) and
$A = C^{\infty}(M)$ the algebra of the functions $C^{\infty}$ on $M$. The geometrie algebraic suggere
a other description of the notion of ``deformation $*_{t}$ of the product'' consideree in
the introduction. It s'agit of considerer a $\R[[t]]$-algebra $A_{t}$, equipped with a isomorphism
$A \iso A_{t}/tA_{t}$, and verifiant the conditions (1.1.1) and (1.1.2) ci-dessous.

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{1.1.1.} $A_{t}$ is plat on $\R[[t]]$ and is separe and complet for the topologie $t$-adique:
\[
A_{t} \iso \varprojlim A_{t}/t^{n}A_{t}.
\]
Si one choisit a lifting $\R$-lineaire of $A$ in $A_{t}$: a section $a \mapsto \tilde{a}$ of the
projection $A_{t} \to A_{t}/tA_{t} = A$, the condition (1.1.1) equivaut a this that
\[
\sum a_{n} t^{n} \longmapsto \sum \tilde{a}_{n} t^{n} : A[[t]] \longrightarrow A_{t}
\]
is a bijection. The product of $A_{t}$ is determine by sa restriction a the image $\tilde{A} \subset A_{t}$
of $A$ by $\sim$:
\[
\tilde{a} \cdot \tilde{b} = \sum \widetilde{c_{n}(a,b)}\, t^{n}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent \textbf{1.1.2.} There exists a lifting $a \mapsto \tilde{a}$ for lequel the $c_{n}(a, b)$ are differentiels en $a$
and $b$.

\medskip
For a tel lifting, the $c_{n}(a, b)$ definissent a product $*_{t}$ to the sens of
the introduction. Si one change the lifting en
\begin{equation}
a \longmapsto \sum \tilde{c}_{n}(a) t^{n}, \tag{1.1.3}
\end{equation}
with $c_{0}(a) = a$ and $c_{n}$ $\R$-lineaire and differentiel en $a$, the condition (1.1.2) is again
verifiee.

\begin{lemma}
One obtains ainsi all the liftings verifiant \emph{(1.1.2)}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Utilisons the lifting given to the depart for identifier $A_{t}$ and $A[[t]]$. Let $G$
the group of the automorphismes $\R[[t]]$-lineaires $g$ of $A[[t]]$ which are the identity modulo
$t$. They are determines by leur restriction a $A$, of the form $a \mapsto \sum c_{n}(a)t^{n}$ with
$c_{0}(a) = a$. The transforme by $g$ of the lifting identique $\id$ of $A$ in $A[[t]]$ is
$a \mapsto \sum c_{n}(a)t^{n}$, and the liftings forment a space principal homogene under $G$.
the ecriture, en terme of the lifting $g(\id)$, of the product $*$, co\"incide with celle, en terme
of the lifting identique, of the product $g^{-1}(*): x, y \mapsto g^{-1}(g x * g y)$.

Let $G_{\text{diff}}$ the under-group of $G$ form of the $g$ for lesquels $c_{n}(a)$ is differentiel en
$a$. It s'agit of see that si a product $*$ is given by of the $c_{n}(a, b)$ differentiels en $a$ and
$b$ and that $g^{-1}(*)$ a the same property, then $g$ is in $G_{\text{diff}}$. Prouvons by recurrence
on $n$ that for a tel $g$, given by $a \mapsto \sum c_{i}(a)t^{i}$, the $c_{n}(a)$ are differentiels en
$a$. Supposant the $c_{i}(a)$ differentiels en $a$ for $i < n$, it s'agit of the prouver for
$i = n > 0$. one has $g = g_{1} \circ h$, with $g_{1}$ given by $a \mapsto \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} c_{i}(a)t^{i}$ in $G_{\text{diff}}$ and $h$ of the form $a \mapsto a + c_{n}(a)t^{n} + \cdots$ and $h^{-1}$ transforme the product $*' := g_{1}^{-1}(*)$, given by of the $c'_{n}(a, b)$ differentiel en $a$ and $b$, en $g^{-1}(*)$. Mod $t^{n+1}$, one has
\begin{align*}
h^{-1}(h a *' h b) &\equiv h^{-1}((a + t^{n}c_{n}(a)) *' (b + t^{n}c_{n}(b))) \\
&\equiv h^{-1}(a *' b + t^{n}(c_{n}(a)b + a c_{n}(b))) \\
&\equiv a *' b + t^{n}(c_{n}(a)b + a c_{n}(b) - c_{n}(ab))
\end{align*}
of sorte that $c_{n}(a)b + a c_{n}(b) - c_{n}(ab)$ is differentiel en $a$ and $b$. The or
$a \mapsto c_{n}(a)b - c_{n}(ab)$ is differentiel en $a$ of ordre $\leq N$ for every $b$,
$a \mapsto c_{n}(a)$ is differentiel en $a$ of ordre $\leq N+1$. This termine the proof of 1.1.4.
\end{proof}

Changer the lifting change the product $*_{t}$ corresponding en a product iso-

equipped with $A_{t}/tA_{t} \iso A$, verifiant (1.1.1) (1.1.2).

For $a, b \in A$, and $\tilde{a}, \tilde{b}$ in $A_{t}$ relevant $a$ and $b$, $[\tilde{a}, \tilde{b}]$ mod $t^{2}$ ne depend that

\subsection{}
Let $M$ a variety symplectique. Anterieurement to the travaux of Wilde,
Lecomte and Fedosov, one savait deja ([8], [9]) that si $H^{3}(M,\R) = 0$, there exists a
deformation $A_{t}$ of $A = C^{\infty}(M)$ to the sens 1.1, donnant lieu to the crochet of Poisson symplectique. Moreover, si $H^{2}(M, \R) = 0$, it is unique a isomorphism pres. The methodes of Wilde, Lecomte and Fedosov donnent the existence without hypothese on the cohomology. the unicite a isomorphism pres n'a not lieu en general: si $A_{t}^{1}$ and $A_{t}^{2}$ are deux deformations, it n'exists not toujours of isomorphism of deformations of $A_{t}^{1}$ with $A_{t}^{2}$ (on $M$ all integer).

Recall the principe of the proof of [8], [9]. For every algebra $A$, the complex of Hochschild $C^{*}(A,A)$, which calcule the $\Ext^{*}_{A \otimes A^{0}}(A,A)$, a for cohomology the algebra exterieure on the space of the derivations of $A$ (Koszul). For $A = C^{\infty}(M)$, that se precise as suit. Let $C^{*}_{\text{diff}}(A,A)$ the under-complex form of the cochaines data by of the operateurs differentiels en each variable. the inclusion of $C^{*}_{\text{diff}}$ in $C^{*}$ is a quasi-isomorphism. The group of cohomology $H^{p}(C^{*}_{\text{diff}}(A,A))$ is the space of the champs of $p$-vecteurs, and the crochet of $C^{*}_{\text{diff}}(A,A)[1]$ induces en cohomology the crochet of Schouten. A crochet of Poisson is a champ of $2$-vecteurs verifiant $[\gamma,\gamma] = 0$. It induces a differentielle $\ad_{\gamma}$ on $H^{*}(C^{*}_{\text{diff}}(A,A)[1])$. Point essential: si $\gamma$ is not degenere, and that one utilise the structure symplectique corresponding for identifier $p$-vecteurs and $p$-forms, $\ad_{\gamma}$ devient the differentielle exterieure.

\begin{theorem}

corresponding a the structure symplectique, is trivial: isomorphic a $C^{\infty}(M)[[t]]$ with

\subsection{}
We travaillerons en cohomology of \v{C}ech, on a space paracompact $X$.
The paracompacite garantit that for every cover open $\mathcal{U} = (U_{i})_{i \in I}$, being
given $n > 1$ and $(a_{m})$ for each $i_{0}, \ldots, i_{n} \in I$, a cover open $(U_{i_{0}, \ldots, i_{n}})_{\alpha}$ of $U_{i_{0}} \cap \cdots \cap U_{i_{n}}$, there exists a cover open $\mathcal{V} = (V_{j})_{j \in J}$ and $g: J \to I$ such that $V_{j} \subset U_{g(j)}$
and that each $V_{j_{0}, \ldots, j_{n}}$ let contenu in a $(U_{g(j_{0}), \ldots, g(j_{n})})_{\alpha}$.

This usage of the paracompacite n'is not serieux. S'it is seul en cause, the cas
general (a topos quelconque) can se traiter similarly en cohomology hyper\v{C}echiste
(cf.~SGA4$\tfrac{1}{2}$ V 7). Seules the notations are more lourdes.

The paracompacite garantit also that the sheaves mous have of bonnes properties of acyclicite.

A open $U$ of a space paracompact $X$ n'is not necessarily paracom-
pact, and the restriction a $U$ of a sheaf mou n'is not necessarily molle. This difficulte disparait for $X$ metrisable ([7] II 3.4.2). In the cas general, the bon critere of localisation is [7] II 3.4.1: let $\mathcal{U}_{i}$ a cover open of a space
paracompact $X$ and $\mathcal{F}$ a sheaf on $X$. For that $\mathcal{F}$ let mou, it is necessary and sufficient
that for every $U$ in $\mathcal{U}$, the restriction of $\mathcal{F}$ a $U$ satisfies
\[
\text{Toute section de } \mathcal{F} \text{ sur } F \subset U \text{ ferm\'e dans } X \text{ se prolonge \`a } U. \tag{A.1.1}
\]

We shall say that a gerbe $\mathcal{G}$ on a space paracompact $X$ a \emph{lien mou} si
for every object $G$ of $\mathcal{G}$ on a open $U$, the sheaf on $U$ of the automorphismes of
$G$ on $U$ satisfies (A.1.1). Condition equivalent: for every object $G$ of $\mathcal{G}$ on a closed
$F$ of $X$, the sheaf on $F$ of the automorphismes of $G$ is mou.

\begin{equation}
\delta: H^{1}(X, C) \longrightarrow H^{2}(X, A). \tag{A.2.2}
\end{equation}

For $Q$ a $C$-torseur, notons $\cl(Q)$ the cobord of the class of $Q$ in $H^{1}(X,C)$. C'is the obstruction a relever $Q$ en a $B$-torseur (i.e.\ the class in $H^{2}(X, A)$ of the $A$-gerbe of the liftings of $Q$).

The sheaf $C$ agit on the exact sequence (A.2.1). Tordons-the by $Q$. the action of $C$
on $A$ being trivial, the sequence tordue s'ecrit
\begin{equation}
0 \longrightarrow A \longrightarrow B^{Q} \longrightarrow C^{Q} \longrightarrow 0. \tag{A.2.1$_{Q}$}
\end{equation}
The sheaf $C^{Q}$ is the sheaf of the automorphismes of the torseur $Q$, which en devient a $(C, C^{Q})$-bitorseur. Si $P$ is a $C^{Q}$-torseur, the compose $P \circ Q = P \times Q/C^{Q}$ is a
$C$-torseur. one has
\begin{equation}
\cl(P \circ Q) = \cl(P) + \cl(Q). \tag{A.2.3}
\end{equation}
The proof is the same that en 2.4.

\begin{proposition}
Si $B$ is mou on $X$ paracompact, the cobord (A.2.2) is bijective.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}
$H^{1}(X, B) = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Si $P$ is a $B$-torseur, the sheaf of the sections of $P$ is mou: it is locally
isomorphic a $B$ and one applique the critere local of mollesse (A.1). In particular, $P$
admet a section global: all $B$-torseur is trivial.
\end{proof}

\noindent \textsc{Injectivite.} Si $P$ and $Q$ are deux $C$-torseurs similarly class, the class in $H^{2}(A)$
of the $C^{Q}$-torseur $P \circ Q^{-1}$, egale a $\cl(P) - \cl(Q)$ by (A.2.3), is trivial. The exact sequence (A.2.1)$_{Q}$ fournit a exact sequence of sets pointes
\[
H^{1}(B^{Q}) \longrightarrow H^{1}(C^{Q}) \longrightarrow H^{2}(A)
\]
and $H^{1}(B^{Q}) = 0$ by A.4, car of after the critere local of mollesse (A.1), the sheaf $B^{Q}$, locally isomorphic a $B$, is mou. The $C^{Q}$-torseur $P \circ Q^{-1}$ is therefore trivial: $P$
is isomorphic a $Q$.

Similarly that the injectivite of (A.2.2) resulte of (A.4), the surjectivite resultera
of the lemma suivant.

\begin{lemma}
On a space paracompact $X$, a gerbe $\mathcal{G}$ of lien mou admet a object global.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(F_{i})_{i \in I}$ a cover closed locally finite of $X$, such that on each
$F_{i}$, $\mathcal{G}$ admette a object $G_{i}$. Si $\mathcal{G}$ admet a object $G$ on a closed $F$, this object
is unique a isomorphism (not unique) pres: resulte of $H^{1}(F, \Aut(G)) = 0$.
Let $\mathcal{E}$ the set of the systemes form of a part $J$ of $I$, and of isomorphisms
$c_{jk}: G_{k} \iso G_{j}$ ($j,k \in J$) on $F_{j} \cap F_{k}$, verifiant the condition of cocycle. Ordonne by
prolongement, the set $\mathcal{E}$ is inductif, therefore admet a element maximal $(J, c)$.
The $G_{j}$ se recollent en $G_{F}$ on the reunion $F$ of the $F_{j}$ ($j \in J$). For every $i \notin J$, $G_{i}$ and $G_{F}$ are isomorphes on $F \cap F_{i}$, and the choix of a isomorphism permet of prolonger $(J, c)$ en $(J \cup \{i\}, c)$. By maximalite, $J = I$ and $G_{F}$ is the object cherche.
\end{proof}

\noindent \textsc{Surjectivite.} Let $c \in H^{2}(A)$ corresponding a gerbe $\mathcal{G}$, of which the objects
local have for sheaf of automorphismes $A$. Poussons by $A \to B$ for obtenir
a gerbe $\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}$ of lien $B$. Si $c_{ijk}$ is a cocycle of \v{C}ech representant $c$, it admet the
description suivante that the reader can prendre for definition: a object $G$ of $\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}$ on
$U$ is the datum of $B$-torseurs $G_{i}$ on the $U \cap U_{i}$ and of isomorphisms $c_{ij}: G_{j} \iso G_{i}$
on $U_{i} \cap U_{j}$, verifiant the condition of cocycle tordue
\[
c_{jk} c_{ij} c_{ik}^{-1} = c_{ijk}.
\]
By A.5, the gerbe $\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}$ a object global $G$. Poussons the torseurs $G_{i}$ by $B \to C$.
The torseurs obtained se recollent en a $C$-torseur, with the image in $C$ of the
$c_{ij}$ as datum of gluing, and one laisse to the reader the soin of verify that $\cl = c$.

Let $\mathcal{H}$ a gerbe, $\mathcal{F}$ a sheaf abelien and supposons given, for each
object local $Z$ of $\mathcal{H}$, a central extension
\begin{equation}
0 \longrightarrow \mathcal{F} \longrightarrow G_{Z} \longrightarrow \Aut(Z) \longrightarrow 0, \tag{A.6.1}
\end{equation}
fonctorielle en $Z$ and compatible a the localisation. One assumed that the action by foncto-

(this action interieure se factorise by $G_{Z}/\mathcal{F}$).

\noindent \textsc{Example 1.} Let $\mathcal{L}$ a lien, $\mathcal{H}$ the gerbe of the realisations of $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ the centre of $\mathcal{L}$.
For each realisation $Z$ of $\mathcal{L}$, the sheaf (in $\mathcal{H}$) of the automorphismes of $Z$ is
celui of the automorphismes interieurs, of or a central extension
\[

\]

\noindent \textsc{Example 2.} Let $(M, \omega)$ a variety symplectique, $\mathcal{H}$ the gerbe of the deformations of $C^{\infty}$ on $\R[[t]]$ donnant lieu to the crochet of Poisson symplectique and $\mathcal{F}$ the sheaf constant $\R[[t]]$. For $A_{t}$ in $\mathcal{H}$, one has $\Aut(A_{t}) = \exp(A_{t}/\R[[t]])$, of or a central extension
\[
0 \longrightarrow \R[[t]] \longrightarrow \exp(A_{t}) \longrightarrow \exp(A_{t}/\R[[t]]) \longrightarrow 0.
\]

We we proposons of define a class $\Obs_{\mathcal{H}} \in H^{3}(\mathcal{F})$. Let $\mathcal{U} = (U_{i})_{i \in I}$
a cover open of $X$. For $\mathcal{U}$ sufficiently fin, $\mathcal{H}$ admet a object $Z_{i}$ on each $U_{i}$. Choisissons $Z_{i}$ and posons $G_{i} := G_{Z_{i}}$. After passage a cover more fin (cf.~A.1), $Z_{i}$ and $Z_{j}$ are isomorphes on $U_{ij}$. Let $P_{ij}$ the bitorseur of the isomorphisms $Z_{i} \iso Z_{j}$: a $(\Aut(Z_{i}), \Aut(Z_{j}))$-bitorseur on $U_{ij}$. Choisissons a $(G_{i}, G_{j})$-bitorseur $B_{ij}$, equipped with a morphism of bitorseurs $g: B_{ij} \to P_{ij}$ such that, for $s$ a section local of $P_{ij}$, the isomorphism corresponding $G_{i} \iso G_{j}$: $g \mapsto s(g)$ such that $sg = s(g)s$ let deduces of $g(s)$ by fonctorialite. Deux tels $(B_{ij}, g)$ are locally isomorphes, with for sheaf of automorphismes $\mathcal{F}$. After passage a cover more fin (cf.~A.1), $B_{ij} \circ B_{jk}$, equipped with sa projection on $P_{ij} \circ P_{jk} = P_{ik}$, is therefore isomorphic a $B_{ik}$ on $U_{ijk}$. Choisissons $c_{ijk}: B_{jk} \circ B_{ij} \iso B_{ik}$. On $U_{ijkl}$, the $c_{ijk}$ fournissent deux isomorphisms $B_{il} \iso B_{ij} \circ B_{jk} \circ B_{kl}$.

The cochaine with values in $\mathcal{F}$
\[
d_{ijkl} = (c_{ijl} c_{jkl})^{-1} (c_{ijk} c_{ikl})
\]
is a cocycle, and changer the $B_{ij}$ modifie $d$ by a cobord.

\noindent \textsc{Definition.} $\Obs_{\mathcal{H}}$ is the class of the cocycle $d$.
