Let $\cD$ a triangulated category equipped with a $t$-structure [1, \S\,1.3.1] $\cC$ son coeur (loc.~cit.). We shall use the notations $\cD^{\leqslant a}$, $\cD^{\geqslant b}$, $\tau_{[a,b]}$, $H^i$ of [1, \S\,1.3]. Let $K \mapsto K(1)$ a autoequivalence of $\cD$, sending triangles distingues en triangles distingues and respectant the $t$-structure. One denotes $K \mapsto K(i)$ the itere $i$-eme ($i \in \mathbb{Z}$) of this autoequivalence. One appellera ``twisting'' the functors $K \mapsto K(i)$.

One assumed the objects of $\cD$ a cohomology bornee: all object is in a $\cD^{\leqslant a}$ and in a $\cD^{\geqslant b}$. Si this hypothese n'etait not remplie, it y aurait lieu of supposer the objects consideres by the sequence a cohomology bornee.

Let $K$ a object of $\cD$, equipped with a morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.1}
\eta \colon K \to K(1)[2].
\end{equation}
One notera again $\eta$ the morphisms deduits of $\eta$ by twisting $(i)$, decalage and troncation $\tau_{[a,b]}$, and one notera $\eta^i$ a compose of $i$ tels morphisms. By example, one denotes again $\eta$ the morphisms in the abelian category $\cC$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.2}
\eta \colon H^i K \to H^{i+2} K(1)
\end{equation}
deduits of \eqref{eq:0.1}.

Assume that $\eta$ satisfies

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{(L.V.)} For every $i \geqslant 0$, the $i$\textsuperscript{eme} itere of $\eta$, $\eta^i \colon H^{-i}(K) \to H^i(K)(i)$ is a isomorphism.
\smallskip

The arguments of [2], rappeles to the section 1, montrent then that $K$ is isomorphic a the sum of the $H^i(K)$, chacun place en son degree cohomologique:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.3}
K \simeq \bigoplus H^i(K)[-i].
\end{equation}

In [2], j'annonce the existence of a isomorphism \eqref{eq:0.3} canonical. The but of this note is of en define a, and same plusieurs.

\section{Rappel of the proof of [2]}

For $i \geqslant 0$, the part primitive $P^{-i}$ of $H^{-i}(K)$ is the kernel of $\eta^{i+1} \colon H^{-i}(K) \to H^{i+2}(K)(i+1)$. It follows of (L.V.) that the morphisms \eqref{eq:0.2} iteres induisent a decomposition en direct sum:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1}
\sum_{j \geqslant 0} \eta^j \colon \bigoplus_{j \geqslant 0} P^{-i-2j}(-j) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^{-i}(K).
\end{equation}

For $H^i(K)$, one deduces a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.2}
\sum_{j \geqslant 0} \eta^{i+j} \colon \bigoplus_{j \geqslant 0} P^{-i-2j}(-i-j) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^i(K).
\end{equation}

These isomorphisms se rassemblent en
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3}
\sum \eta^j \colon \bigoplus_{i,j} P^{-i}(-j) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^*(K),
\end{equation}
the sum being on the $i$, $j$ with $0 \leqslant j \leqslant i$. In \eqref{eq:1.3}, $P^{-i}(-j)$ is direct factor of $H^{2j-i}(K)$.

Recall that a functor cohomologique $F$ of $\cD$ in a abelian category $\cM$ is a functor additif such that for every triangle distingue $K \to L \to M \to K[1]$, the sequence $F(K) \to F(L) \to F(M)$ let exact [6, \S\,3.1]. One sets $F^i(K) = F(K[i])$. Faisant tourner the triangle distingue $K \to L \to M \to K[1]$, one sees that a functor cohomologique $F$ given lieu a exact sequence longue
\[
\cdots \to F^i(K) \to F^i(L) \to F^i(M) \to F^{i+1}(K) \to F^{i+1}(L) \to \cdots.
\]

For $K$ in $\cD$, the tronques $\tau_{[a,b]}K$ of $K$ donnent lieu a of the triangles distingues
\[
\to \tau_{[a,b]}K \to \tau_{[a,c]}K \to \tau_{[b+1,c]}K \to .
\]
The systeme of sequences exact longues correspondantes is a ``object spectral'' to the sens of J.~L.~Verdier and fournit a spectral sequence
\[
E_2^{pq} = F^p H^q K \Rightarrow F^{p+q} K.
\]
See the appendice. Let ${}^i\!E_2^{pq}$ the spectral sequence analogue for $K(i)$. A morphism \eqref{eq:0.1} fournit of the morphisms of spectral sequences
\[
{}^i\!E_2^{pq} \to {}^{i+1}\!E_2^{p,q+2}.
\]

Montrons that (L.V.) force the degenerescence of these spectral sequences. Procedant by recurrence on $r \geqslant 2$, assume that the $d_s$ are nuls for $2 \leqslant s < r$ and prouvons that $d_r = 0$. For $q \leqslant 0$, let ${}^i\!P^{pq}$ the direct factor $F^p P^q(i)$ of ${}^i\!E_2^{pq} = {}^i\!E_r^{pq}$.

By \eqref{eq:1.1} and \eqref{eq:1.2}, it is enough to verify that $d_r$ is zero on the ${}^i\!P^{pq}$. By definition, ${}^i\!P^{pq}$ is annule by $\eta^{q+1}$. The morphism $d_r$ envoie ${}^i\!E_r^{pq}$ in ${}^i\!E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}$, on lequel $\eta^{q+1}$ is injective. The nullite of $d_r$ en resulte.

Appliquant the degenerescence of \eqref{eq:1.3} a functor cohomologique $\Hom(X,K)$, for $X$ in $\cC$, one trouve that all morphism $X \to H^i K$ se releve en a morphism of degree $i$ of $X$ in $K$. In particular, for $X = H^i K$, the map identique of $H^i K$ se releve en a morphism of degree $i$ of $H^i K$ in $K$. The sum of these morphisms
\[
\bigoplus H^i K[-i] \xrightarrow{\sim} K
\]
is a isomorphism en cohomology, therefore a isomorphism.

\subsection*{3.4}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:3.5}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.5.1}

\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Note: that for $d=1$, \eqref{eq:3.5.1} signifie that $\eta^{(1)} = 0$.

\begin{proof}
By recurrence on $n \geqslant -1$, we shall montrer that the conditions \eqref{eq:3.5.1} for $-n < d \leqslant 1$ determinent $\varphi$ uniquement modulo composition with a endomorphism $1+w$ of the sum of the $H^i(K)[-i]$, with $w$ of degree $\leqslant -n-2$. Noter that, with the notations of 3.2, the composante homogene $\psi^{(d)}$ of degree $d$ of $w$ is in $F_0^d$ for $F$ the functor $\Ext^{-d}$.

For $n=-1$, the hypothese is vide and the conclusion exprime that $\varphi$, assumed be the identity on the cohomology, is defined modulo composition with $1+\psi$, $\psi$ of degree $\leqslant -1$.

Prouvons the assertion for $n \geqslant 0$, en the supposant vraie for $n-1$. Changer $\varphi$ en $\varphi \circ (1+\psi)$ conjugue the $1$-morphism $\eta$ (plutot, $\varphi^{-1}\eta\varphi$) of the sum of the $H^iK[-i]$ en $(1+\psi)^{-1}\eta(1+\psi)$. For $\psi$ of degree $\leqslant -n-1$, puisque $\eta$ is of degree $\leqslant 2$, one has
\begin{align*}
(1+\psi)^{-1}\eta(1+\psi) &\equiv \eta + [\eta,\psi] \\
&\equiv \eta + e(\psi^{(-n-1)})
\end{align*}
modulo degree $\leqslant -n$. By \ref{lem:3.3}, a unique choix of $\psi^{(-n-1)}$ fournit a new $\eta$ verifiant \eqref{eq:3.5.1} for $d > -n$, as promis.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:3.6}

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Identifions the sum of the $H^i[-i]$ a $K$ by $\varphi$. By the caracterisation \ref{prop:2.4} of $f_i$, it is enough to verify that for $s > i$, the restriction a $P^{-i}[i]$ of the $s$-morphism $\eta^s$ is of degree $< s+i$. one has a decomposition en composantes homogenes
\[
\eta = \eta^{(2)} + \sum_{a \leqslant 0} \eta^{(a)}.
\]
Posons $e = \eta^{(2)}$. By \ref{prop:3.5}, one has
\[
(\operatorname{ad} e)^{-a+1}(\eta^{(a)}) = 0.
\]
Developpons $\eta^s$, and reordonnant the factors by map iteree of the regle
\[
ex = xe + \operatorname{ad} e(x).
\]
One trouve that $\eta^s$ is sum of termes of the types suivant:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $e^s$,
\item $(\operatorname{ad} e)^{r_k}(\eta^{(a_k)}) \cdots (\operatorname{ad} e)^{r_1}(\eta^{(a_1)}) e^{r_0}$ with $\sum_0^k r_k < s$.
\end{enumerate}
Si $s > i$, $e^s$ s'annule on $P^{-i}[i]$. Similarly, the termes (b) with $r_0 > i$ s'annulent on $P^{-i}[i]$. The factor $(\operatorname{ad} e)^{r_k}(\eta^{(a_k)})$ is of degree $2r_k + a_k$, and zero si $r_k > -a_k$. S'it is not zero, it is of degree
\[
2r_k + a_k = r_k + (r_k + a_k) \leqslant r_k.
\]
Each terme (b) not zero on $P^{-i}[i]$ is therefore of degree $\leqslant 2r_0 + \sum_1^k r_k = r_0 + \sum_0^k r_i < i + s$, as requis.
\end{proof}

\section*{Appendice. Objects spectraux, of after J.~L.~Verdier}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendice}

J'ai appris the formalism expose in this appendice of J.~L.~Verdier, which disait s'be inspire of the traitement of the spectral sequences in Cartan-Eilenberg (vol.~1, pp.~318--319).

\subsection*{A.1}
Let $\cD$ a triangulated category. A \emph{object spectral} in $\cD$ is the datum of
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item a family $X_{pq}$ of objects of $\cD$, indexee by the paires of integers $p \leqslant q$;
\item for $p' \leqslant p$, $q' \leqslant q$, a morphism $X_{pq} \to X_{p'q'}$;
\item for $p \leqslant q \leqslant r$, a morphism degree a, appele cobord, $X_{qr} \to X_{pq}$.
\end{enumerate}
One exige that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the morphisms (2) definissent a contravariant functor of the set ordonne of the paires $(p,q)$ with $p \leqslant q$ in $\cD$;
\item for $p \leqslant q \leqslant r$, $p' \leqslant q' \leqslant r'$ and $p' \leqslant p$, $q' \leqslant q$, $r' \leqslant r$, the diagram of morphisms (2) and (3) is commutative;
\item for $p \leqslant q \leqslant r$, the triangle
\[
X_{qr} \to X_{pr} \to X_{pq} \xrightarrow{+1} X_{qr}
\]
of morphisms (2) and (3) is distingue.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{example}
For $\cD$ the derived category of a abelian category $\cA$ and $(K,F)$ a complex filtre, the $X_{pq} = F^p(K)/F^q(K)$ forment a object spectral.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
A object spectral $X$ will be dit of amplitude in $[a,b]$ si the morphisms (1): $X_{pq} \to X_{p'q'}$ are of the isomorphisms for $p' \leqslant p \leqslant a$ and $q' = q$, ainsi that for $b < q' \leqslant q$ and $p' = p$. It revient to the same of imposer the nullite of the $X_{p,p+1}$ for $p \notin [a,b]$. A object spectral of amplitude $[0,n]$ with $n=1$ (resp.~$2$) s'identifie a triangle distingue (resp.~a diagram of the octaedre), cf.~[1, \S\S\,1.1.6, 1.1.7, 1.1.14].
\end{remark}

\subsection*{A.2}
Let $\cA$ a abelian category, equipped with a ``functor of translation'' $X \mapsto X[1]$ (a autoequivalence). One denotes $X \mapsto X[n]$ the itere $n$-eme of the functor of translation and one appelle morphism of degree $n$ of $X$ in $Y$ a morphism of $X$ in $Y[n]$. A sequence $X \xrightarrow{\alpha} Y \xrightarrow{\beta} Z$, with $\alpha$ (resp.~$\beta$) of degree $n$ (resp.~$m$) is dite exact si the sequence $X \to Y[n] \to Z[n+m]$ the is.

A \emph{object spectral} of $\cA$ is the datum $(1')$, $(2')$, $(3')$ of objects and of morphisms of $\cA$, as en A.1, verifiant the conditions $(a')$, $(b')$ similarly formulation that A.1 (a), (b), and the condition

\[
\cdots \to X_{qr} \to X_{pr} \to X_{pq} \xrightarrow{\partial} X_{qr} \to X_{pr} \to \cdots

of morphisms $(2')$ and $(3')$ is exact.
\end{enumerate}

Let $X$ a object spectral of $\cA$. of after $(b')$ the morphism $(2')$: $X_{p,r} \to X_{p,r}$ is the identity. of after $(c')$ for $p=q$, one has $X_{pp} = 0$.
