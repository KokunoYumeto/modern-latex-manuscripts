\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

Let $X$ a scheme proper and smooth over a field $k$. The de Rham cohomology of
$X/k$, $H^*_{\dR}(X/k) := H^*(X, \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k})$, is the aboutissement of the spectral sequence of Hodge
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.1}
E_1^{ij} = H^j(X, \Omega^i_{X/k}) \Rightarrow H^{i+j}_{\dR}(X/k).
\end{equation}

One sait that, si $k$ is of caracteristique zero, (\ref{eq:0.1}) degenere en $E_1$: for $X$
projective, that resulte of the theorie of Hodge, and the cas proper se ramene to the cas
projective by the lemma of Chow and the resolution of the singularities (cf.~[5, 5.5]).

The premiere proof of this fait n'utilisant not the theorie of Hodge a ete
given by Faltings [8], as map of son theorem of existence of a
decomposition of Hodge-Tate for the étale cohomology $p$-adique of the varieties
proper and smooth on the field local of inegale caracteristique.

Si $k$ is of caracteristique $p > 0$, it can arriver that (\ref{eq:0.1}) ne degenere not en $E_1$ (cf.
Mumford [22] and 2.5 (i)). Toutefois, Kato a shows recemment [14] that, for $k$
parfait of caracteristique $p > 0$ and $X$ projective and smooth on $k$, si the one assumed that $X$ is
of dimension $< p$ and se releve on the ring $W(k)$ of the vecteurs of Witt of $k$, then this
phenomene ``pathologique'' ne se product not. Fontaine and Messing [10] have
etendu this result to the cas proper, and deduces, by a argument standard, the
degenerescence of (\ref{eq:0.1}) en caracteristique zero. This seconde proof
utilise of the techniques cristallines.

We donnons ici a proof elementaire of a result more precise that
celui of Kato and Fontaine-Messing, and which is the suivant. Supposons $k$ parfait, of
caracteristique $p > 0$, and let $X$ a $k$-scheme smooth (of dimension quelconque, and not
necessarily proper). Let $X'$ deduces of $X$ by the extension of scalars $k \to k$,
$\lambda \mapsto \lambda^p$, and $F: X \to X'$ the Frobenius relative (1.1). We prouvons that each
lifting smooth $\tilde{X}$ of $X$ on the ring $W_2(k)$ of the vecteurs of Witt of longueur 2
determine a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.2}
\varphi_{\tilde{X}}: \bigoplus_{i < p} \Omega^i_{X'/k}[-i] \xrightarrow{\sim} \tau_{<p} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}
\end{equation}
in the derived category $D(X', \cO)$. It follows, by a comptage of dimensions,
that si $X$ admet a lifting smooth $\tilde{X}$ on $W_2(k)$, and is moreover assumed proper and of dimension $<p$, the spectral sequence (\ref{eq:0.1}) degenere en $E_1$.

Voici the principe of the construction of $\varphi = \varphi_{\tilde{X}}$. Let $\sigma: W_2(k) \xrightarrow{\sim} W_2(k)$ the
lifting $(\lambda_0, \lambda_1) \mapsto (\lambda_0^p, \lambda_1^p)$ of the automorphism $\lambda \mapsto \lambda^p$ of $k$ and $\tilde{X}'$ deduces of $\tilde{X}$ by
the extension of scalars $\sigma$. Si $F$ se releve en $\tilde{F}: \tilde{X} \to \tilde{X}'$, the homomorphisme
$\tilde{F}^*: \Omega^1_{\tilde{X}'/W_2(k)} \to \tilde{F}_* \Omega^1_{\tilde{X}/W_2(k)}$ fournit, after division by $p$, a morphism of complexes
\[
f: \Omega^1_{X'/k}[-1] \to F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}
\]
induisant on $\mathscr{H}^1$ the isomorphism of Cartier $C^{-1}$ (1.2) (this idee, which remonte a
the article of Mazur [20], a deja ete tres largement exploitee). Si $\tilde{F}_1$ and $\tilde{F}_2$ are deux
liftings, leur ``difference'' is a homomorphisme of $\Omega^1_{X'/k}$ in $F_* \cO_X$. It
fournit a homotopie between the fleches $f_1$ and $f_2$ associees a $\tilde{F}_1$ and $\tilde{F}_2$. These
homotopies satisfy a condition of transitivite. This permet of globaliser the
construction a the aide of a cover of $X$ by of the ouverts or $F$ se releve. One obtains ainsi the composante $\varphi^1$ of $\varphi$. One defines the composante $\varphi^0$ as the fleche
induite by $F^*$, and one deduces the $\varphi^i$ of $\varphi^0$ and $\varphi^1$ grace a the structure multiplicative of the
de Rham complex (c'is ici that intervient the restriction $i < p$).

This construction is expliquee to the n$^\circ$ 2, after a bref rappel, to the n$^\circ$ 1, on the
definition of the Frobenius relative and the isomorphism of Cartier. We donnons
egalement, to the n$^\circ$ 2, the argument ``standard'' permettant of deduire of (\ref{eq:0.2}) the
degenerescence of (\ref{eq:0.1}) en characteristic zero. For $X$ of dimension $<p$,
relevable on $W_2(k)$, (\ref{eq:0.2}) fournit a decomposition in $D(X', \cO)$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:0.3}
\bigoplus_i \Omega^i_{X'/k}[-i] \xrightarrow{\sim} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/k}.
\end{equation}
We montrons that one has again a telle decomposition for $X$ of dimension $p$.
Enfin, we deduisons of (\ref{eq:0.2}) a theorem of annulation of type Kodaira en
caracteristique $p$: si $X$ is a $k$-scheme projective and smooth, equidimensionnel,
relevable on $W_2(k)$, and si $L$ is a sheaf inversible ample on $X$, then
\[
H^j(X, \Omega^i_{X/k} \otimes L^{-1}) = 0 \quad \text{pour} \quad i + j < \inf(p, \dim X).
\]
This result and sa proof are dus a Michel Raynaud. The theorem classique
of Kodaira-Akizuki-Nakano en caracteristique zero en decoule by the argument
habituel.

the obstruction a the existence of a decomposition of type (\ref{eq:0.2}) and the dependance
of $\varphi_{\tilde{X}}$ with respect to $\tilde{X}$ are etudiees to the n$^\circ$ 3, or the construction of $\varphi$ is reprise on
a base $S$ of caracteristique $p > 0$. We montrons in particular the result suivant:
si $S$ admet a lifting $\tilde{S}$ plat on $\mathbf{Z}/p^2$, si $X$ is a $S$-scheme smooth and $X'$ is son
inverse image by the Frobenius absolute of $S$, then $X'$ admet a lifting smooth on
$\tilde{S}$ si and only s'there exists a fleche $\Omega^1_{X'/S}[-1] \to F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ in $D(X', \cO)$ induisant
the isomorphism of Cartier $C^{-1}$ on $\mathscr{H}^1$. A map a the degenerescence of the

One defines dualement $\tau_{\ge n} L$, a quotient of $L$ with $H^i \tau_{\ge n} L = H^i L$ (resp.~$0$) si $i \ge n$
(resp.~$i < n$).

place en degree $0$; $M[n]$ is then the complex reduit a $M$ place en degree $-n$.

\section{Decomposition of the de Rham complex and maps\\(cas of a field parfait)}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:2.1}
Let $k$ a field parfait of caracteristique $p>0$, $S = \Spec(k)$,
$\tilde{S} = \Spec(W_2(k))$ \textup{(1.3)}, and let $X$ a $S$-scheme smooth. A all $\tilde{S}$-scheme smooth $\tilde{X}$ relevant $X$
is associated canonically a isomorphism
\[
\varphi_{\tilde{X}}: \bigoplus_{i < p} \Omega^i_{X'/S}[-i] \xrightarrow{\sim} \tau_{<p} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}
\]
of $D(X')$, such that $\mathscr{H}^i \varphi_{\tilde{X}} = C^{-1}$ \textup{(1.2)} for $i < p$.
\end{theorem}

The proof va se faire en quatre etapes.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:2.3}
With the notations of \ref{thm:2.1}, let $X$ a $k$-scheme smooth of dimension $\le p$, relevable on $W_2(k)$. Then the complex $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ is isomorphic, in $D(X')$, a complex a differentielle zero.
\end{corollary}

The conclusion signifie again that $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ is isomorphic, in $D(X')$, a the sum of ses $\mathscr{H}^i[-i]$, or that there exists, in $D(X')$, a isomorphism
\[

\]
induisant $C^{-1}$ on $\mathscr{H}^i$ (cf.~3.1 ci-after).

\begin{proof}[Prouvons \ref{cor:2.3}]
One se ramene a supposer $X$ connected. Si $\dim X < p$, it is enough to appliquer \ref{thm:2.1}. Supposons therefore that $\dim X = p$. On $X$, the sheaves locally libres $\Omega^i_{X/S}$ and $\Omega^{p-i}_{X/S}$ are en duality of Serre: the product $\alpha \wedge \beta$ is a duality parfaite, with values in $\Omega^p_{X/S}$, between $\Omega^i_{X/S}$ and $\Omega^{p-i}_{X/S}$. The morphism $F = F_{X/S}$ being finite and plat, it follows, by the duality of Grothendieck, that $F_* \Omega^i_{X/S}$ and $F_* \Omega^{p-i}_{X/S}$ are again en duality (with values in $\Omega^p_{X'/S}$), given by the accouplement $(\alpha, \beta) \mapsto C(\alpha \wedge \beta)$, or the one designe ici by $C: F_* \Omega^p_{X/S} \to \Omega^p_{X'/S}$ the compose of $F_* \Omega^p_{X/S} \to \mathscr{H}^p F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ and of the inverse of $C^{-1}$ en degree $p$ (1.2): the point is that $C$ n'is other that the morphism trace. It is of ailleurs easy of verify directement that this accouplement is parfait. The transpose, for this duality, of the differentielle $d$ of $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ is again $d$ (a signe dependant of the conventions pres). This exprime that
\[
C(d\alpha \wedge \beta) \pm C(\alpha \wedge d\beta) = C(d(\alpha \wedge \beta)) = 0.
\]

of after \ref{thm:2.1}, $\tau_{<p} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ is isomorphic, in $D(X')$, a the sum of ses $\mathscr{H}^i[-i]$. By duality, $\tau_{\ge 1} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ a the same property. One dispose of a triangle distingue
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
\tau_{<p} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S} \to F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S} \to \mathscr{H}^p[-p] \xrightarrow{e}.
\tag{$*$}
\end{equation}
Puisque $\tau_{<p} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ is sum of ses $\mathscr{H}^i[-i]$, the conclusion of \ref{cor:2.3} equivaut a the nullite of
\[
e: \mathscr{H}^p[-p] \to \Bigl(\bigoplus_{i < p} \mathscr{H}^i[-i]\Bigr)[1].
\]
Let $e_i$ ($0 \le i \le p-1$) the composantes of $e$:
\[
e_i \in \Hom(\mathscr{H}^p[-p], \mathscr{H}^i[-i+1]) = H^{p-i+1}(X', \mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{H}^p, \mathscr{H}^i)).
\]
The triangle ($*$) s'envoie in the triangle
\[
\tau_{[1,p-1]} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S} \to \tau_{\ge 1} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S} \to \mathscr{H}^p[-p] \to,
\]
or $\tau_{[1,p-1]} = \tau_{\ge 1} \tau_{<p} = \tau_{<p} \tau_{\ge 1}$. As $\tau_{\ge 1} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}$ is sum of ses $\mathscr{H}^i[-i]$, one has therefore $e_i = 0$ for $i \neq 0$. For $i = 0$, the class $e_i$ vit in $H^{p+1}(X', \ldots)$, and this group is zero car $\dim X = p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:2.9}
Let $X$ a $k$-scheme smooth, $M$ a sheaf inversible on $X$ and $b$ a integer. Assume that $\tau_{<b} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X$ let isomorphic, in $D(X')$, a complex a differentielle zero, and that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star3}
H^j(X, \Omega^i_X \otimes M^{\otimes p}) = 0 \quad \text{pour} \quad i + j < b.
\end{equation}
Then one has
\[
H^j(X, \Omega^i_X \otimes M) = 0 \quad \text{pour} \quad i + j < b.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $M'$ on $X'$ deduces of $M$ by changement of base. one has $F^* M' = M^{\otimes p}$, of or, by the formule of projection,
\[
H^j(X, M^{\otimes p} \otimes_{\cO_X} \Omega^i_X) = H^j(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} F_* \Omega^i_X)
\]
for every $(i, j)$. As $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X$ is a complex of $\cO_{X'}$-modules (a differentielle $\cO_{X'}$-lineaire), one can considerer the tensor product $M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X$, and one has a spectral sequence
\[
E_1^{ij} = H^j(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} F_* \Omega^i_X) \Rightarrow H^{i+j}(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X).
\]
the hypothese (\ref{eq:star3}) implique therefore that $H^n(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X) = 0$ for $n < b$. But, for $n < b$, one has
\[
H^n(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X) = H^n(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} \tau_{<b} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X).
\]
Or one dispose, by hypothese, of a isomorphism of $D(X')$
\[
\bigoplus_{i < b} \Omega^i_{X'}[-i] \xrightarrow{\sim} \tau_{<b} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X.
\]
By sequence, for $n < b$, one has
\[
0 = H^n(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X) = \bigoplus_i H^{n-i}(X', M' \otimes_{\cO_{X'}} \Omega^i_{X'}).
\]
Puisque $(X', M')$ se deduces of $(X, M)$ by the changement of base $F_S: S \to S$, one has also
\[
H^{n-i}(X, M \otimes_{\cO_X} \Omega^i_X) = 0
\]
for $n < b$ and all $i$.
\end{proof}

Prouvons (2.8.2). of after \ref{thm:2.1}, for $b \le p$, $\tau_{<b} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_X$ is isomorphic, in $D(X')$, a complex a differentielle zero. \section{Gerbes of liftings and of scindages, and decomposition of the de Rham complex (cas general)}

\subsection{}

Let $\mathcal{A}$ a abelian category. We shall say that a object $K$ of $D^b(\mathcal{A})$ is \emph{decomposable} si $K$ is isomorphic (in $D^b(\mathcal{A})$) a complex a differentielle zero.

For that $K$ let decomposable, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists, in $D(\mathcal{A})$, of the morphisms $f_i: H^i K[-i] \to K$ such that $H^i(f_i)$ let the map identique of $H^i K$ (this condition is necessary, car verifiee by $\bigoplus H^i K[-i]$, and sufficient, car the $f_i$ induisent a isomorphism $\bigoplus H^i K[-i] \xrightarrow{\sim} K$). Si $K$ is decomposable, one appelle \emph{decomposition} of $K$ a morphism $f = \sum f_i: \bigoplus H^i K[-i] \to K$ such that $H^i f$ let the map identique of $H^i K$.

Let $K$ a complex decomposable such that $K^i = 0$ for $i \neq 0, 1$. A decomposition of $K$ is entierement determinee by the datum of $f_1: H^1 K[-1] \to K$ such that $H^1 f_1$ let the identity ($f_0$ is given by the injection $H^0 K \hookrightarrow K^0$). The triangle distingue
\[
H^0 K \to K \to H^1 K[-1] \to
\]
fournit the exact sequence
\[
0 \to \Hom(H^1 K[-1], H^0 K) \to \Hom(H^1 K[-1], K) \xrightarrow{a} \Hom(H^1 K, H^1 K),
\]
which shows that the set of the decompositions of $K$ is a affine space under $\Hom(H^1 K[-1], H^0 K) = \Ext^1(H^1 K, H^0 K)$ (inverse image by $a$ of $\id$).

\subsection{}

Let $(T, \cO)$ a site annele and $K$ a complex of $\cO$-modules on $T$ with $K^i = 0$ for $i \neq 0, 1$. One assumed that $\mathscr{H}^1 K$ is locally free of rank finite; the projection of $K^1$ on $\mathscr{H}^1 K$ admet therefore locally a section. One assumed en outre that the projection of $K^0$ on $\Im(d)$ admet also locally a section.

Let $\mathrm{sc}'(K)$ the category fibree suivante on $T$: a object on $U$ is a scindage (on $U$) $s: \mathscr{H}^1 K \to K^1$ of the projection of $K^1$ on $\mathscr{H}^1 K$; a fleche of $s'$ in $s''$ is a homomorphisme $h: \mathscr{H}^1 K \to K^0$ (on $U$) such that $s'' = s' + dh$. Si $s'$ and $s''$ are deux objects on $U$, the morphisms of $s'$ in $s''$ forment a sheaf on $U$; $\mathrm{sc}'(K)$ is a prechamp (en groupo\"ides). Moreover, the hypotheses faites on $K$ impliquent that: a) all $U$ admet a cover $R$ such that the category fiber $\mathrm{sc}'(K)(R)$ let not vide; b) deux objects quelconques of $\mathrm{sc}'(K)$ are locally isomorphes. The champ associated to the prechamp $\mathrm{sc}'(K)$ is therefore a gerbe (Giraud [12, III \S\,2]), the \emph{gerbe of the scindages} of $K$, notee $\mathrm{sc}(K)$.

For every object $s$ of $\mathrm{sc}(K)$, $\mathscr{H}om(s,s)$ is the sheaf abelien $\mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{H}^1 K, \mathscr{H}^0 K)$. The gerbe $\mathrm{sc}(K)$ admet a object global\footnote{See 3.3 (b) for a description of the objects globaux.} if and only if sa class [12, IV 3.1, 3.5]
\[

\]
is zero. Si tel is the cas, the set of the classes a isomorphism pres of objects globaux of $\mathrm{sc}(K)$ is a affine space under $H^1(T, \mathscr{H}om(\mathscr{H}^1 K, \mathscr{H}^0 K)) = \Ext^1(\mathscr{H}^1 K, \mathscr{H}^0 K)$ (si $s$ and $t$ are deux objects globaux, leur ``difference'' $t - s$ is the class of the torseur of the isomorphisms local of $s$ on $t$, cf.~[12, III 2.2.6]).

Notons moreover
\[
e(K) \in \Ext^2(\mathscr{H}^1 K, \mathscr{H}^0 K)
\]
the class defined by the morphism of degree 1 of the triangle distingue
\[
\mathscr{H}^0 K \to K \to \mathscr{H}^1 K[-1] \xrightarrow{+1}.
\]

one has $e(K) = 0$ if and only if $K$ is decomposable (3.1). one has vu of other part that, si $K$ is decomposable, the set of the decompositions of $K$ is a affine space under $\Ext^1(\mathscr{H}^1 K, \mathscr{H}^0 K)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:3.3}
With the notations precedentes:

\textup{(a)} one has $\mathrm{cl}\,\mathrm{sc}(K) = -e(K)$.

\textup{(b)} There exists a bijection affine canonical $\alpha$, defined ci-dessous, of the set of the classes a isomorphism pres of objects globaux of $\mathrm{sc}(K)$ on the set of the decompositions of $K$, induisant the identity on the group of the translations $\Ext^1(\mathscr{H}^1 K, \mathscr{H}^0 K)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:4.1.2}

By composition with (\ref{eq:4.2.1.1}) tensorise with $\cO(-A')$ one obtains therefore a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.2.1.3}
C^{-1}: \Omega^i_{X'/S}(A', B') \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathscr{H}^i F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(A, B).
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:4.2.2}
\textup{(a)} The definition (\ref{eq:4.2.1.2}) garde a sens on a base $S$ quelconque. For $X$ the line affine on $S$, of coordonnee $t$, $D$ the diviseur $(t)$, one has
\[
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D) = \Bigl(\cO_S[t] \to \cO_S[t] \frac{dt}{t}\Bigr), \quad
\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)(-D) = (t\cO_S[t] \to \cO_S[t] dt).
\]

Let $a, b, c$ of the integers $\ge 0$, $n = a + b + c$, and for $1 \le i \le n$, $E_i$ the line affine on $S$, of coordonee $t_i$, $D_i$ the diviseur $(t_i)$. Let $X = E_1 \times_S \cdots \times_S E_n$ and $D = A + B$ the diviseur defined by $A = \sum_{1 \le i \le a} pr_i^{-1}(D_i)$, $B = \sum_{a+1 \le i \le a+b} pr_i^{-1}(D_i)$. Then $\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(A, B)$ is tensor product externe of the $\Omega^{\bullet}_{E_i/S}(\log D_i)(-D_i)$ for $1 \le i \le a$, of the $\Omega^{\bullet}_{E_i/S}(\log D_i)$ for $a+1 \le i \le a+b$, and of the $\Omega^{\bullet}_{E_i/S}$ for $i > a+b$.

\textup{(b)} Let $(X, D)$ as en 4.2.1, with $X$ of dimension relative $n$ on $S$. The complex $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)$ is auto-dual, for a duality with values in $\Omega^n_{X'/S}(\log D')$. of other part, the dual of Serre of $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(A, B)$ (duality with values in $\Omega^n_{X'/S}$) is $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(B, A)$.

\textup{(c)} Let $S$ a scheme, $X$ a $S$-scheme smooth, and $D$ a diviseur a croisements normaux relative sum of diviseurs smooth distincts $D_i$ ($1 \le i \le r$). Then the sequence
\[
0 \to \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)(-D) \to \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S} \to \bigoplus_i \Omega^{\bullet}_{D_i/S} \to \bigoplus_{i<j} \Omega^{\bullet}_{D_i \cap D_j/S} \to \cdots
\]
is exact (verification local, a the aide of a decomposition a the Kunneth, as en (a)). The quotient $\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}/\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)(-D)$ is the de Rham complex ``a the Sullivan'' of $D$: forms differentielles on the $D_i$ which se recollent. For $S = \Spec \mathbf{C}$, it fournit a resolution of the sheaf constant $\mathbf{C}$ on the space analytic associated a $D$; the complex $\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/\mathbf{C}}(\log D)(-D)$ calcule therefore $j_! \mathbf{C}$, or $j: X - D \hookrightarrow D$ is the inclusion. One sait of other part that $\Omega^{\bullet}_{X/\mathbf{C}}(\log D)$ calcule $Rj_* \mathbf{C}$ (cf.~[6, 3.1.8]). More generally, si $X$ is a variety analytic complex, and $A, B, D = A + B$ of the diviseurs a croisements normaux on $X$,
\[
@@MATH31@@
\]
one checks that the one has
\[
\Omega^{\bullet}_X(A, B) = Rj_{A!} Rj_{B*} \mathbf{C} = Rj_{B*} Rj_{A!} \mathbf{C},
\]
with $\Omega^{\bullet}_X(A, B)$ defined as en (\ref{eq:4.2.1.2}).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{}

The enonces \ref{thm:2.1}, \ref{cor:2.3}, \ref{cor:2.4}, \ref{thm:3.5}, \ref{cor:3.6}, \ref{cor:3.7}, \ref{rem:3.8} have of the variantes ``a poles logarithmiques''. One defines again a equivalence of the gerbe $\Rel(X', D', \tilde{S})$ of the liftings of $(X', D')$ on $\tilde{S}$ with the gerbe of the scindages of $\tau_{\le 1} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)$. One ne considered that of the liftings $(\tilde{X}', \tilde{D}')$ or $\tilde{D}'$ is again a diviseur a croisements normaux relative, the sheaf $\Theta_{X'/S}$ is remplace by the dual $\Theta_{X'/S}(-\log D')$ of $\Omega^{1}_{X'/S}(\log D')$, and, for a choix (local) of a lifting $(\tilde{X}, \tilde{D})$ of $(X, D)$, one ne considered that the liftings $\tilde{F}$ such that $\tilde{F}^{-1}(\tilde{D}') = p\tilde{D}$. Moreover, all lifting global of $(X', D')$ fournit a decomposition canonical of $\tau_{<p} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)$ and a decomposition (not canonical) of $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)$ si $X$ is of dimension relative $\le p$ and si $H^{p+1}(X', (\Omega^p_{X'/S}(\log D'))^\vee) = 0$ (this which is the cas by example si $S$ is affine and $X$ proper on $S$) (autodualite of $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)$, \ref{rem:4.2.2} (b)).

Si $D = A + B$ as en 4.2.1, a telle decomposition fournit a decomposition analogue for $F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(A, B)$ (quasi-isomorphic a $(F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D))(-A')$).

Let $f: X \to S$ proper and smooth, $D = A + B$ as en 4.2.1, $b$ a integer, and assume that $\tau_{\le b} F_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(\log D)$ let decomposable. Then one verifies as en \ref{cor:4.1.4} that, for $i + j \le b$, the $R^j f_* \Omega^i_{X/S}(A, B)$ are locally libres of type finite, of formation compatible a all changement of base, and that the spectral sequence
\[
E_1^{ij} = R^j f_* \Omega^i_{X/S}(A, B) \Rightarrow R^{i+j} f_* \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/S}(A, B)
\]
satisfies $E_1^{ij} = E_\infty^{ij}$ for $i + j \le b$.

By a argument similaire a the proof of \ref{cor:2.7}, one deduces:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:4.2.4}
Let $X$ a scheme proper and smooth over a field $K$ of characteristic zero and $D = A + B$ a diviseur a croisements normaux on $X$. Then the spectral sequence of Hodge
\[
E_1^{ij} = H^j(X, \Omega^i_{X/K}(A, B)) \Rightarrow H^{i+j}(X, \Omega^{\bullet}_{X/K}(A, B))
\]
degenere en $E_1$.
\end{corollary}

The cas particulier $A = \emptyset$ fournit a new proof of the result of degenerescence [6, 3.2.13 (ii)].
