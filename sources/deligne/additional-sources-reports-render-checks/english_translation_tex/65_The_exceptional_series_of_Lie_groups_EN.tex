\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} Numerology of exceptional Lie groups and a conjectural explanation.

\bigskip

Let $G_0$ be the split adjoint group of one of the following types:
\[
A_1,\ A_2,\ G_2,\ D_4,\ F_4,\ E_6,\ E_7,\ E_8.
\]
Fix a pinning of $G_0$. Let $\Gamma$ denote the group of automorphisms of $G_0$. For $\Gamma$ the group of automorphisms of the Dynkin diagram, identified with the group of automorphisms of $G_0$ preserving the pinning, this is a semidirect product $\Gamma \ltimes G_0$. The group $G$ is an algebraic group over $\Q$.

It would be enough for our purposes to consider the complex Lie group $G(\C)$, or even a compact form of $G(\C)$. The possibility, for our purpose, of including $D_4$ in the list was discovered by Cohen and de Man.

Let $T$ be the maximal torus of $G$ supplied by the pinning, and let $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ be the canonical bilinear form on $\Lie(T)^\vee$: the inverse form to the restriction to $\Lie(T)$ of the Killing form $\Tr(\ad x\,\ad y)$. Let $\alpha$ be the highest root and set $k=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle$. The values of $k$ are
\[
1/2,\quad 1/3,\quad 1/4,\quad 1/6,\quad 1/9,\quad 1/12,\quad 1/18,\quad 1/30.
\]
Finally let $h$ be the Coxeter number, equal either to $k$ or to $-(1/6)-k$. Put $a:=1/h$. To this list we also add the trivial group, with $a=5/6$ or $a=-1$.

Let $\frg$ be the Lie algebra of $G$, let $U$ be its enveloping algebra, and let $C\in U$ be the Casimir element corresponding to the Killing form. If $V$ is a representation of $G$, we will call the action of $C$ the Casimir endomorphism of $V$. If $V$ is irreducible, this is multiplication by a scalar, called the Casimir of $V$. Recall that the Casimir of the adjoint representation is $1$.

\textbf{Our observation is that, for $(G,a)$ as above, $G$ has virtual representations}
$X_i$ ($0\leq i\leq 4$), $Y_i$ ($0\leq i\leq 3$), $Y_i^*$ ($0\leq i\leq 3$), $A$, $C$, and $C^*$
\textbf{with the following properties (A)--(G).}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(A)} Each of these representations is either irreducible, zero, or the negative of an irreducible representation. One has $X_0=Y_0=Y_0^*=1$, the trivial representation, and $X_1=Y_1=Y_1^*=\frg$, the adjoint representation.

\medskip
For each representation $R$ of the symmetric group $S_n$, there is a functor
\[
V\longmapsto [R](V):=\Hom_{S_n}(R,V^{\otimes n}).
\]
If $V$ is a representation of a group, the class of $[R](V)$ in the Grothendieck group of representations is a $\lambda$-polynomial, that is, a polynomial in the $\wedge^iV$, in the class of $V$. If $R$ is irreducible and corresponds to a partition $p$ of $n$, we write $[p]_V$ for the class $[R](V)$. For example,
\[
[(n)]_V=\Sym^n V,\quad [(1,\ldots,1)]_V=\wedge^n V,\quad [(2,1)]_V=V\otimes\wedge^2V-\wedge^3V,
\]
\[
[(3)]_V=V^3-2V\cdot\wedge^2V+\wedge^3V.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(B)} In the $\lambda$-ring of representations of $G$, one has
\begin{align*}
\text{(B1)}\quad &[(2)]_{\frg}=\Sym^2\frg=1+Y_2+Y_2^*,\\
\text{(B2)}\quad &[(1,1)]_{\frg}=\wedge^2\frg=\frg+X_2,\\
\text{(B3)}\quad &[(3)]_{\frg}=\Sym^3\frg=\frg+X_2+A+Y_3+Y_3^*,\\
\text{(B4)}\quad &[(2,1)]_{\frg}=2\frg+X_2+Y_2+Y_2^*+A+C+C^*,\\
\text{(B5)}\quad &[(1,1,1)]_{\frg}=\wedge^3\frg=1+X_2+Y_2+Y_2^*+X_3,\\
\text{(B6)}\quad &Y_2\otimes\frg=\frg+X_2+Y_2+A+Y_3+C,\\
\text{(B7)}\quad &Y_2^*\otimes\frg=\frg+X_2+Y_2^*+A+Y_3^*+C^*.
\end{align*}
These formulae are permuted by the involution $*$, provided that $A$ and the $X_i$---in particular $\frg$---are declared fixed by $*$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(C)} As a consequence of \textbf{(B)}, in the $\lambda$-ring of representations of $G$ one has
\begin{align*}
\text{(C1)}\quad &X_2=\wedge^2\frg-\frg,\\
\text{(C2)}\quad &X_3=\wedge^3\frg-\frg\cdot\wedge^2\frg+\Sym^2\frg+\frg-1.
\end{align*}
Moreover,
\[
X_4=\wedge^4\frg-\frg\cdot\wedge^3\frg+\Sym^2\frg\cdot\wedge^2\frg-\wedge^2\frg-(\frg+A)\cdot\frg+\frg.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(D)} The Casimir has the following values on these representations:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccccc}
 & $X_n$ & $Y_n$ & $Y_n^*$ & $A$ & $C$ & $C^*$ \\
\hline
$n$ & $n+(n^2-n)a$ & & $n+(n^2-n)a^*$ & $8/3$ & $3+3a$ & $3+3a^*$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
When the representation in question is zero, this assertion is to be interpreted as empty.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(E)} The dimensions of these representations are given by the following formulae, where $\lambda:=-6a$:
\begin{align*}
\dim X_1&=-2(\lambda+5)(\lambda-6)/\lambda(\lambda-1),\\
\dim X_2&=5(\lambda+3)(\lambda+5)(\lambda-4)(\lambda-6)/\lambda^2(\lambda-1)^2,\\
\dim X_3&=-10(\lambda+2)(\lambda+4)(\lambda+5)(\lambda-3)(\lambda-5)(\lambda-6)/\lambda^3(\lambda-1)^3,\\
\dim X_4&=5(\lambda+3)(\lambda+4)(\lambda+5)(\lambda+3)(2\lambda-4)(\lambda-5)(\lambda-6)(2\lambda-5)/\lambda^4(\lambda-1)^4,\\
\dim Y_2&=-90(\lambda+5)(\lambda-4)/\lambda^2(\lambda-1)(2\lambda-1),\\
\dim Y_3&=-10(\lambda+5)(5\lambda-6)(\lambda-4)(\lambda-5)(\lambda-6)/\lambda^3(\lambda-1)^2(2\lambda-1)(3\lambda-1),\\
\dim A&=-27(\lambda+3)(\lambda+4)(\lambda+5)(\lambda-4)(\lambda-5)(\lambda-6)/\lambda^2(\lambda-1)^2(3\lambda-1)(3\lambda-2),\\
\dim C&=640(\lambda+3)(\lambda+5)(\lambda-3)(\lambda-5)/\lambda^3(\lambda-1)(2\lambda-1)(3\lambda-2).
\end{align*}
For $Y_n^*$ and $C^*$, the dimension is given by the same formula as for $Y_n$ and $C$, but with $\lambda$ replaced by $\lambda^*:=1-\lambda=-6a^*$. Note that the formulae for the $X_i$ and for $A$ are invariant under $\lambda\mapsto\lambda^*$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(F)} For each of the equalities (B1)--(B7), (C1), and (C2), the formulae (E) express the dimension of the left-hand side, respectively of the right-hand side, as the value at $\lambda$ of a rational function $R_1$, respectively $R_2$. One has $R_1=R_2$ as rational functions.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{(G)} For each of the equalities (B1)--(B7), (C1), and (C2), the formulae (D), (E), and (1), (2) express the trace of the Casimir on the left-hand side, respectively on the right-hand side, as the value at $\lambda$ of a rational function $R_1$, respectively $R_2$. One has $R_1=R_2$.

\bigskip
If $R$ is a representation of $S_n$ and $V$ is a representation of a semisimple group, the trace of the Casimir endomorphism of $[R](V)$ is obtained from the trace of the Casimir endomorphism of $V$ by the following formula. If $\chi$ is the character of $R$, $n(\sigma)$ is the number of cycles of a permutation $\sigma\in S_n$, and $m(\sigma)$ is the sum of the squares of the cycle lengths of $\sigma$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:casimir-trace}
\Tr(C,[R](V))=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\chi(\sigma)\,m(\sigma)\,(\dim V)^{n(\sigma)-2}\,\Tr(C,V).
\end{equation}
For two representations $V$ and $W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:casimir-tensor}
\Tr(C,V\otimes W)=\Tr(C,V)\dim(W)+\dim(V)\Tr(C,W).
\end{equation}

I do not know a satisfactory proof of the preceding assertions. The assertions (B), (C), and (D) are obtained by the brute force of the LiE program. That the dimensions and Casimirs of $\frg$, $Y_2$, and $Y_2^*$ have the form (D), (E) for the appropriate $a$ follows in Vogel [2], \S 6.9, from the fact that the only invariant elements of $\Sym^4(\frg)$ are multiples of the square of the invariant element of $\Sym^2(\frg)$. The dimension of the $X_i$ follows from that of $\frg$ by (C). If $n=k$, then $Y_n$ is the representation of dominant weight $n\alpha$, with $\alpha$ the highest root, and (D) for $Y_n$ can be deduced from this. The assertion (D) for $X_n$ can be deduced from (C): on the right-hand side, the trace of the Casimir endomorphism is computed by (1).

Assume (D) and the dimension formulae for $Y_2$, $Y_2^*$, and the $X_i$. If one expresses equality of dimensions and of Casimir traces for both sides of (B3), (B4), (B6), and (B7), one obtains a system of linear equations in $\dim Y_3$, $\dim Y_3^*$, $\dim A$, $\dim C$, and $\dim C^*$, whose right-hand coefficients are rational functions of $\lambda$. This gives an expression for these dimensions as rational functions of $\lambda$. We used this method to find a polynomial in $\lambda$ which is a multiple of the denominator, and to estimate the degree of the numerator; the numerator was then obtained by interpolation from the known values corresponding to the pairs $(G,a)$. That the resulting rational functions factor as in (E) is a miracle. The identities $R_1=R_2$ of (F), (G) follow simply from the fact that, for a suitable polynomial $P$ in $\lambda$, $R_1P$ and $R_2P$ are two polynomials in $\lambda$ which are equal at a number of values of $\lambda$ strictly greater than their degree.

\section*{The conjecture}

I conjecture that there exists a semisimple abelian $\Q(t)$-linear category $\mathcal C_t$, equipped with a tensor product $\otimes$ and associativity, commutativity, and unit constraints in the sense of Saavedra [1], Chapter 1, having the following properties.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(i)] The groups $\Hom(X,Y)$ are finite-dimensional over $\Q(t)$.
\item[(ii)] Every object has a dual: $\mathcal C_t$ is rigid. Recall that this makes it possible to define, for every object $X$, its dimension $\dim(X)\in \End(1)=\Q(t)$ as the composite $1\to X^\vee\otimes X\to 1$ of the evaluation and coevaluation morphisms.
\item[(iii)] The tensor category $\mathcal C_t$ is of adjoint type in the following sense. There is an object $\frg$, equipped with a Lie bracket $[\ ,\ ]:\frg\otimes\frg\to\frg$, acting functorially on each object $X$ by an action $\rho_X:\frg\otimes X\to X$. The action is compatible with tensor products: $\rho_{X\otimes Y}=\rho_X\otimes 1+1\otimes\rho_Y$. For $X=\frg$, $\rho_X$ is $[\ ,\ ]$. The action $\rho_X$ transposes to a coaction ${}^t\!\rho_X:X\to\frg^\vee\otimes X$. For every $X$, $X^\frg:=\Ker({}^t\!\rho_X)$ is the largest subobject of $X$ which is a sum of copies of $\mathbf 1$. Finally, every object is a subquotient of a sum of objects $\frg^{\otimes n}$.
\item[(iv)] $\frg$ is a simple object, and the Killing form is nondegenerate.
\item[(v)] There are simple objects $X_i$ ($0\leq i\leq 4$), $Y_i$ ($0\leq i\leq 3$), $Y_i^*$ ($0\leq i\leq 3$), $A$, $C$, and $C^*$ satisfying (A)--(E), with $a=t$.
\item[(vi)] Every simple object $S$ is absolutely simple, i.e. $\End(S)=\Q(t)$, and is isomorphic to its dual; more precisely, it admits a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form $S\otimes S\to\mathbf 1$. The dimension $\dim(S)\in\Q(t)$ of a simple object factors into linear factors.
\item[(vii)] The category $\mathcal C_t$ has a $\otimes$-autoequivalence $*$, semilinear with respect to $t\mapsto -(1/6)-t$, sending $A$ and the $X_i$ to themselves, $Y_i$ to $Y_i^*$, and $C$ to $C^*$; the square of $*$ is isomorphic to the identity.
\item[(viii)] In a sense to be made precise, the category of representations of $G$ is a specialization of $\mathcal C_t$ at $t=a$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{A model}

Here is a model for this conjecture. Let $\mathcal B_n^-[t]$ be the following rigid $\Z[t]$-linear tensor category.

Objects: finite sets.

$\Hom(X,Y)$: let $\mathcal C$ be the set of isomorphism classes of compact one-dimensional manifolds with boundary, whose boundary is $X\amalg Y$; these are bordisms from $X$ to $Y$. The $\Z[t]$-module $\Hom(X,Y)$ is the quotient of the free $\Z[t]$-module $\Z[t]^{(\mathcal C)}$ with basis $[c]$, $c\in\mathcal C$, by the relations
\[
[c\cup\text{a circle}]=t[c].
\]

Composition: composition of bordisms.

$\otimes$: disjoint union.

For $\lambda\in\Q$, let $\mathcal B_\lambda$ be the rigid $\Q$-linear tensor category obtained from $\mathcal B_n^-[t]$ by scalar extension $\Z[t]\to\Q$, $t\mapsto\lambda$. A morphism $f:X\to Y$ of $\mathcal B_\lambda$ will be called negligible if $\Tr(gf)=0$ for every $g:Y\to X$. The negligible morphisms form an ideal $I_\lambda$, and if $f$ is negligible then so is $f\otimes g$. Thus the quotient category $\mathcal B_\lambda/I_\lambda$ is still rigid tensor. Let $(\mathcal B_\lambda/I_\lambda)^{\mathrm{kar}}$ be its Karoubian envelope, obtained by formally adjoining the direct factors corresponding to idempotent endomorphisms.

If $\lambda$ is an integer $n\geq 0$, this Karoubian envelope is just the category $\Rep(O(n))$ of representations of the orthogonal group $O(n)$. More precisely, let $V$ be a vector space over $\Q$ equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form $B$. Then there is a tensor functor, unique up to unique isomorphism, from $\mathcal B_n^-$ to $\Rep(O(V))$, sending the object $\{1\}$ to $V$ and sending the bordism
\begin{center}
\begin{picture}(80,40)
\put(10,20){\circle{20}}
\put(20,20){\line(1,0){40}}
\put(60,20){\circle{20}}
\end{picture}
\end{center}
from $\{1,2\}$ to $\emptyset$ to $B:V\otimes V\to\mathbf 1$. This tensor functor induces an equivalence of $(\mathcal B_n/I_n)^{\mathrm{kar}}$ with $\Rep(O(V))$. This is a translation of Weyl's invariant theory for $O(V)$ in $V^{\otimes N}$; see [3], Chapter V, \S 5.

If $\lambda$ is an even integer $-2p\leq 0$, the Karoubian envelope $(\mathcal B_\lambda/I_\lambda)^{\mathrm{kar}}$ is a variant of the category $\Rep(\Sp(2p))$ of representations of the symplectic group $\Sp(2p)$: the central element $-1\in\Sp(2p)$ defines a $\Z/(2)$-grading of each representation $V$ of $\Sp(2p)$, and the commutativity constraint is defined by the Koszul sign rule. Equivalently, in the category of super-representations of $OSp(0|2p)$, one considers the subcategory of subquotients of sums of $V^{\otimes N}$, with $V$ the obvious super-representation.

Similarly, if one considers finite oriented sets $S$, that is, sets equipped with $\varepsilon:S\to\{\pm1\}$, and oriented bordisms, one obtains a rigid $\Z[t]$-linear tensor category $\mathcal A_n^+[t]$. For $\lambda\in\Q$, let $\mathcal A_\lambda$ be the rigid $\Q$-linear category obtained from $\mathcal A_n^+[t]$ by the scalar extension $\Z[t]\to\Q$, $t\mapsto\lambda$. Define the ideal $I_\lambda$ of negligible morphisms in $\mathcal A_\lambda$ as before. If $\lambda$ is an integer $n\geq0$, $(\mathcal A_\lambda/I_\lambda)^{\mathrm{kar}}$ is just $\Rep(\GL(n))$. If $\lambda$ is an integer $-n\leq0$, one obtains the variant of $\Rep(\GL(n))$ defined by $-1\in\GL(n)$: in the category of super-representations of $\GL(0|n)$, one takes the subcategory of subquotients of sums of $V^{\otimes N}\otimes V^{\vee\otimes M}$, with $V$ the evident super-representation.

In both cases, after extending scalars from $\Z[t]$ to $\Q(t)$, the Karoubian envelope of the category $\mathcal A_t$ or $\mathcal B_t$ obtained in this way is a semisimple rigid tensor category. Its simple objects $S$ are absolutely simple, and their dimensions $\dim(S)\in\Q(t)$ are polynomials in $t$ that are products of linear factors.

\section*{The formulae}

Here are the formulae.

Let $V_\lambda^-$ be the object ``one point'' of $\mathcal B_\lambda^-$, and let $V$ be its image in $\mathcal B_\lambda^{-\mathrm{kar}}$, the ``evident representation''. To study the subcategory of $\mathcal B_\lambda^{-\mathrm{kar}}$ formed by the subquotients of sums of $V^{\otimes i}$, $i\leq N$, one method is to use polynomial continuation from the category $\Rep(O(n))$ for $n$ large. The irreducible objects of $\mathcal B_\lambda^{-\mathrm{kar}}$ then correspond to decreasing sequences of integers $\ell_i\geq0$, with $\ell_i=0$ for $i$ large enough. The corresponding representation of $O(n)$, for $n$ large, has dominant weight $\sum_i(\ell_i-\ell_{i+1})\omega_i$ in the notation of the Bourbaki tables. Weyl's dimension formula gives, by continuation, the dimension of this irreducible object. Let $h((\ell_i))$, or simply $h$, be the integer such that the representation of the symmetric group defined by the partition $(\ell_i)$ of $\sum\ell_i$ has dimension $(\sum\ell_i)!/h$. If $\ell_i=0$ for $i>a$, then
\[
h=\prod_{i=1}^a(\ell_i+a-i)!\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq a}((\ell_i-i)-(\ell_j-j)).
\]

Let $a(n)$, respectively $b(n)$, be the number of pairs $i,j$, $1\leq i\leq j$, such that $n=(\ell_i-i)+(\ell_j-j)$, respectively $n=-i-j$. With this notation,
\[
\dim V((\ell_i))=(1/h)\prod(t+n)^{a(n)-b(n)}.
\]
Formally, this is
\[
(1/h)\frac{\prod(t+\ell_i-i+\ell_j-j)}{\prod(t-i-j)}.
\]
It is a polynomial of degree $\sum\ell_i$.

The object $\frg:=\wedge^2V$ plays the role of the adjoint representation: it has a Lie bracket, and acts functorially and compatibly with tensor products on every object. The bilinear form $\Tr(\rho(x)\rho(y),V)$ on $\frg$ is nondegenerate. On $V((\ell_i))$, the corresponding Casimir is
\[
\sum_i\ell_i(\ell_i-2i)+t\sum_i\ell_i,
\]
as one checks by continuation from the case of $O(n)$ for $n$ large.

Let $V_\lambda^+$ be the object ``one positively oriented point'' ($\varepsilon=1$) of $\mathcal A_\lambda^+$, and let $V$ be its image in $\mathcal A_\lambda^{+\mathrm{kar}}$, the ``evident representation''. To study the subcategory of $\mathcal A_\lambda^{+\mathrm{kar}}$ formed by the subquotients of sums of $V^{\otimes i}\otimes V^{\vee\otimes j}$, $i,j\leq N$, one method is to use polynomial continuation from the category $\Rep(\GL(n))$ for $n$ large. The irreducible objects of $\mathcal A_\lambda^+$ then correspond to pairs of decreasing sequences of positive integers $((\ell_i),(m_i))$, with $\ell_i$ and $m_i$ zero for $i$ sufficiently large. If the character group of the maximal torus of diagonal matrices in $\GL(n)$ is identified with $\Z^n$ in the usual way, then the corresponding representation of $\GL(n)$, for $n$ large, has dominant weight
\[
(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ldots,0,\ldots,0,-m_2,-m_1).
\]
The object $\frg:=V\otimes V^\vee$ plays the role of the adjoint representation, and the bilinear form $\Tr(\rho(x)\rho(y),V)$ is nondegenerate.

Let $c(n)$, respectively $d(n)$, be the number of pairs $i,j$, $1\leq i,j$, such that $n=\ell_i-i+m_j-j+1$, respectively $n=-i-j+1$. By continuation of Weyl's formula,
\[
\dim V((\ell_i),(m_i))=(1/h((\ell_i))\cdot h((m_i)))\prod(t+n)^{c(n)-d(n)}.
\]
Formally, this is
\[
(1/h((\ell_i))\cdot h((m_i)))\frac{\prod(t+\ell_i-i+m_j-j+1)}{\prod(t-i-j+1)}.
\]
It is a polynomial in $t$ of degree $\sum\ell_i+\sum m_i$. The Casimir relative to $\Tr(\rho(x)\rho(y),V)$ is
\[
\sum_i\ell_i(\ell_i-2i+1)+\sum_i m_i(m_i-2i+1)+t\Bigl(\sum_i\ell_i+\sum_i m_i\Bigr).
\]
