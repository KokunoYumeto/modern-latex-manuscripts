\Large\textbf{Real Mixed Hodge Structures}
\end{center}

\begin{flushright}
\textsc{Pierre Deligne}
\end{flushright}

\vspace{0.5cm}

Real mixed Hodge structures (\S2) form a rigid abelian $\otimes$-category, and the functor ``underlying real vector space'' is a fiber functor. The functor $\Gr^W$ is another. We propose to compute what the general theory of Tannakian categories gives in this particular case.

\section{Three opposed filtrations}

\subsection{}

This section is a variation on Section 1.2 of P. Deligne, \textit{Th\'eor\`eme de Hodge II}, Publ. Math. IHES \textbf{40}, pp. 5--58. Let $\A$ be an abelian category, and let $A$ be an object of $\A$ equipped with three opposed filtrations $W$, $F$, $\bar F$. Here ``filtration'' means ``finite filtration'' (loc. cit. 1.1.4); the filtration $W$ is assumed increasing, and the filtrations $F$, $\bar F$ decreasing. The opposition condition is written
\[
\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\bar F}^q\Gr_n^W A=0\quad\text{for }n\ne p+q
\]
(loc. cit. 1.2.7, 1.2.13).

For every bigraded object $B=\bigoplus B^{p,q}$, we shall write $W$, $F_W$, and $\bar F_W$ for the three opposed filtrations
\begin{align*}
W_n&=\bigoplus_{p+q\le n}B^{p,q},\\
F_W^i&=\bigoplus_{p\ge i}B^{p,q},\\
\bar F_W^i&=\bigoplus_{q\ge i}B^{p,q}.
\end{align*}
With this notation, to say that the three filtrations $W$, $F$, and $\bar F$ of $A$ are opposed is equivalent (loc. cit. 1.2.6) to saying that each $\Gr_n^W A$ admits a unique decomposition
\[
\Gr_n^W A=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}A_W^{p,q}
\]
such that the filtrations of $\Gr_n^W A$ induced by $F$ and $\bar F$ are given by
\begin{align*}
F^i\Gr_n^W A&=\bigoplus_{\substack{p+q=n\\ p\ge i}}A_W^{p,q},\\
\bar F^i\Gr_n^W A&=\bigoplus_{\substack{p+q=n\\ q\ge i}}A_W^{p,q}.
\end{align*}
In other words, $F$ and $\bar F$ induce on $\Gr_n^W A$ the filtrations $F_W$ and $\bar F_W$.

Set
\[
A_F^{p,q}:=(W_{p+q}\cap F^p)\cap\Bigl((W_{p+q}\cap \bar F^q)+\sum_{i>0}(W_{p+q-i}\cap \bar F^{q+1-i})\Bigr)
\]
and, interchanging the roles of $F$ and $\bar F$ and of $p$ and $q$,
\[
A_{\bar F}^{p,q}:=(W_{p+q}\cap \bar F^q)\cap\Bigl((W_{p+q}\cap F^p)+\sum_{i>0}(W_{p+q-i}\cap F^{p+1-i})\Bigr).
\]

For $p+q=n$, the projection of $W_n$ onto $\Gr_n^W A$ induces an isomorphism from $A_F^{p,q}$ to $A_W^{p,q}$: loc. cit. 1.2.8, restricted to the special case 1.2.11, where $A_F^{p,q}$ is denoted $A_0^{p,q}$; note that in the formula defining $A_F^{p,q}$, one may replace the sum over $i>0$ by the same sum over $i\ge2$. It follows (loc. cit.) that the $A_F^{p,q}$, respectively the $A_{\bar F}^{p,q}$, form a bigrading of $A$, and that the sum $a_F$, respectively $a_{\bar F}$, of the isomorphisms
\[
A_F^{p,q}\xrightarrow{\sim}A_W^{p,q},\qquad A_{\bar F}^{p,q}\xrightarrow{\sim}A_W^{p,q}
\]
is a bifiltered isomorphism from $(A;W,F)$ to $(\Gr^W\!A;W,F_W)$, respectively from $(A;W,\bar F)$ to $(\Gr^W\!A;W,\bar F_W)$.

\begin{lemma}
The automorphism $d=a_{\bar F}a_F^{-1}$ of $\Gr^W\!A$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.1}
(d-1)(A_W^{p,q})\subset \bigoplus_{\substack{r<p\\ s<q}}A_W^{r,s}.\tag{1.1.1}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

Passing to the associated graded, the isomorphisms $a_F$ and $a_{\bar F}$ induce the identity of $\Gr^W\!A$. Hence $\Gr^W(d)=1$.

For each subset $I$ of $\Z^2$, and for $B$ a bigraded object, set $B_I=\bigoplus_{(p,q)\in I}B^{p,q}$. Set
\begin{align*}
I(p,q)&=\{(r,s)\mid r+s\le p+q\text{ and }((r,s)=(p,q)\text{ or }r<p)\},\\
J(p,q)&=\{(r,s)\mid r+s\le p+q\text{ and }((r,s)=(p,q)\text{ or }s<q)\},\\
K(p,q)&=\{(r,s)\mid r+s\le p+q\text{ and }r\ge p\}.
\end{align*}

In the definition of $A_F^{p,q}$ as an intersection of two subobjects of $A$, the first subobject is $A_F^{I(p,q)}$ and the second is $A_{\bar F}^{J(p,q)}$. This follows from the fact that $a_F$, respectively $a_{\bar F}$, is a bifiltered isomorphism. Thus $A_F^{p,q}\subset A_F^{I(p,q)}$, whence
\[
dA_F^{p,q}=a_{\bar F}a_F^{-1}A_F^{p,q}\subset A_{\bar F}^{I(p,q)},
\]
and $dA_F^{p,q}\subset A_{\bar F}^{J(p,q)}$. Since $\Gr^W(d)=1$, one has equality $dA_F^{I(p,q)}=A_{\bar F}^{I(p,q)}$. Interchanging $F$ and $\bar F$, one similarly obtains $d^{-1}A_{\bar F}^{J(p,q)}=A_F^{J(p,q)}$, hence $dA_F^{J(p,q)}=A_{\bar F}^{J(p,q)}$, and
\[
dA_W^{p,q}=dA_W^{I(p,q)}\cap dA_W^{J(p,q)}=A_W^{I(p,q)\cap J(p,q)}=A_W^{K(p,q)}=\bigoplus_{\substack{r<p\\ s<q}}A_W^{r,s}.
\]

We conclude by using $\Gr^W(d)=1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1.2}
The functor $A\mapsto \Gr^W\!A$ is an equivalence from the category of objects of $\A$ equipped with three opposed filtrations $W$, $F$, $\bar F$, to the category of objects of $\A$ equipped with a finite bigrading and an automorphism $d$ satisfying \eqref{eq:1.1.1}.
\end{proposition}

The isomorphism $a_F:A\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gr^W\!A$ respects the filtration $W$, carries $F$ to $F_W$, and carries $\bar F$ to $d^{-1}(F_W)$: one has $a_{\bar F}=d^{-1}a_F$. Thus the functor $\Gr^W$ has a left inverse, namely the functor
\[
s:(B=\bigoplus B^{p,q},d)\longmapsto (B,W,F_W,d^{-1}(F_W)),
\]
equipped with the functorial isomorphism $a_F:A\xrightarrow{\sim}s\Gr^W\!A$.

Conversely, start from a bigraded object $B$ equipped with an automorphism $d$, and define $W$, $F$, and $\bar F$ as above. These filtrations are opposed as soon as $\Gr^W(d)=1$. For these filtrations,
\[
B_F^{p,q}=B^{K(p,q)}\cap d^{-1}(B^{I(p,q)}).
\]
If $d$ satisfies \eqref{eq:1.1.1}, then $dB^{p,q}\subset B^{I(p,q)}$ and $d^{-1}B^{p,q}\subset B^{J(p,q)}$. Since $(B^{p,q})$ and $(B_F^{p,q})$ are bigradings of $B$, one has equality $B_F^{p,q}=B^{p,q}$. The same argument, applied to $d^{-1}$ and to the bigrading $\bar F^{p,q}$, shows that $d^{-1}B^{\bar F^{p,q}}=B^{p,q}$. It follows that the evident isomorphism from $B$ to $\Gr^W\!B$ is an isomorphism of functors $\mathrm{Id}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gr^W\circ s$.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:1.3}
If $2$ is invertible, $d$ has a unique square root $d^{1/2}$ which still satisfies \eqref{eq:1.1.1}. Set
\[
a:=d^{1/2}a_F=d^{-1/2}a_{\bar F}.
\]
Then $a_{\bar F}=d^{1/2}a$ and $a_F=d^{-1/2}a$, so that $a$ carries $F$, respectively $\bar F$, to the filtration $d^{1/2}(F_W)$, respectively $d^{-1/2}(\bar F_W)$, of $\Gr^W\!A$. The isomorphism $a$ is the same for $A$ equipped with $W,F,\bar F$ and for $A$ equipped with $W,\bar F,F$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{}

These constructions are compatible with tensor product. If $\otimes:\A_1\times\A_2\to\A$ is an exact biadditive functor, we extend it to filtered objects by setting
\[
F^n(A_1\otimes A_2)=\sum_{a+b=n}F^a(A_1)\otimes F^b(A_2)\subset A_1\otimes A_2.
\]
The associated graded functor is compatible with $\otimes$, and for bifiltered objects the isomorphism
\[
\Gr^{W_1\otimes W_2}(A_1\otimes A_2)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gr^{W_1}(A_1)\otimes\Gr^{W_2}(A_2)
\]
carries the filtration induced by $F_1\otimes F_2$ to the tensor product of the filtrations induced by $F_1$ and $F_2$.

For vector spaces over a field, this can be deduced from the fact that a bifiltration always underlies a bigrading. It follows that if $W_i$, $F_i$, and $\bar F_i$ are three opposed filtrations on $A_i$ for $i=1,2$, then the filtrations $W_1\otimes W_2$, $F_1\otimes F_2$, and $\bar F_1\otimes\bar F_2$ on $A_1\otimes A_2$ are again opposed.

The formation of the bigradings $A_F^{p,q}$ and $A_{\bar F}^{p,q}$ of $A$, and $A_W^{p,q}$ of $\Gr^W(A)$, as well as the isomorphisms $a_F,a_{\bar F}:A\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gr^W(A)$ and the automorphism $d$ of $\Gr^W(A)$, is compatible with $\otimes$.

\subsection{}

Let $k$ be a commutative field, let $\A$ be the category of finite-dimensional vector spaces over $k$, and let $\mathscr C$ be the category of vector spaces equipped with three opposed filtrations $W$, $F$, and $\bar F$. This is a rigid abelian $\otimes$-category with $\End(1)=k$. It has the fiber functor $\omega_0$, the underlying vector space functor, and hence is neutral Tannakian. We also have the fiber functor $\Gr^W=:\omega_W$. The isomorphisms $a_F$ and $a_{\bar F}$ are isomorphisms of fiber functors $\omega_0\xrightarrow{\sim}\omega_W$. Put $G=\Aut(\omega_W)$. The functorial automorphism $d$ defines a $k$-point of the pro-algebraic group $G$.

The functorial bigrading of $\Gr^W(V)$ by the $V_W^{p,q}$ corresponds to a homomorphism
\[
\alpha:\Gm\times\Gm\longrightarrow G.
\]
It is a section of the projection
\[
\pr:G\to\Gm\times\Gm
\]
corresponding to the inclusion in $\mathscr C$ of the category of bigraded vector spaces, via
\[
B\longmapsto (B,W,F_W,\bar F_W).
\]
I wish to normalize $\alpha$ so that $\alpha(\lambda,\mu)v=\lambda^p\mu^{-q}v$ for $v\in V_W^{p,q}$.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:1.2}, $G$ is also the algebraic group of automorphisms of the underlying-vector-space fiber functor on the category of bigraded vector spaces equipped with an automorphism $d$ satisfying \eqref{eq:1.1.1}. We are going to ``compute'' it when $k$ has characteristic $0$.

Let $\mathcal L$ be the Lie algebra over $k$ freely generated by elements $D^{i,j}$ for $i,j<0$, and equip it with the unique bigrading for which $D^{i,j}$ has bidegree $(i,j)$. The $W_n(\mathcal L)$ are ideals of $\mathcal L$, and the quotients $\mathcal L/W_n(\mathcal L)$ are nilpotent Lie algebras. We shall write $U$ for the pro-algebraic group which is the projective limit of the unipotent algebraic groups $\exp(\mathcal L/W_n(\mathcal L))$ with Lie algebras $\mathcal L/W_n(\mathcal L)$. Let $\Gm\times\Gm$ act on $\mathcal L$ so that $(\lambda,\mu)$ acts on an element $D$ of degree $(i,j)$ by multiplication by $\lambda^i\mu^j$. This action respects the filtration $W$ and therefore gives an action of $\Gm\times\Gm$ on $U$.

\begin{construction}\label{constr:1.6}
The group $G$ is the semidirect product $(\Gm\times\Gm)\ltimes U$.
\end{construction}

To make the semidirect product act on a finite-dimensional vector space $V$ is the same as to make $\Gm\times\Gm$ and $U$ act, subject to a compatibility condition relating the two actions. The action of $\Gm\times\Gm$ corresponds to a bigrading, namely the one for which $(\lambda,\mu)v^{p,q}=\lambda^p\mu^{-q}v^{p,q}$. The action of $U$ corresponds to an action of $\mathcal L$, zero on some $W_n$ and nilpotent, i.e. to the data of endomorphisms $D^{i,j}$, for $i,j<0$, such that every composite of $D^{i,j}$ of sufficiently large absolute weight is zero. Compatibility says that $D^{i,j}$ has bidegree $(i,j)$, and this property implies the required vanishing just stated. The data of the $D^{i,j}$ is equivalent to the data of $D:=\sum D^{i,j}$. Altogether, the action of the semidirect product on $V$ is identified with the data of a bigrading and of an endomorphism $D$ of $V$ satisfying
\[
DV^{p,q}\subset \bigoplus_{i<0,\,j<0}V^{p+i,q+j}.
\]
Putting $d=\exp(D)$, this is again identified with the data of a bigrading and an automorphism $d$ satisfying \eqref{eq:1.1.1}. This construction is compatible with tensor products, giving the desired isomorphism
\[
(\Gm\times\Gm)\ltimes U\xrightarrow{\sim}G\xrightarrow{\sim}\Aut(\omega_W).
\]
This isomorphism is of course compatible with $\alpha$ and $\pr$ above.

\section{Mixed Hodge structures}

Recall that a real mixed Hodge structure is a finite-dimensional real vector space equipped with an increasing filtration $W$, whose complexification $V_\C$ is equipped with a decreasing filtration $F$. One requires that on $V_\C$ the filtration $W_\C$ obtained from $W$ by extension of scalars, often denoted simply by $W$, together with $F$ and its complex conjugate $\bar F$, form a system of three opposed filtrations.

It is equivalent to give a real vector space or to give a complex vector space equipped with a real structure, i.e. with complex conjugation, an antilinear involution. Under this dictionary, to give a real mixed Hodge structure is equivalently to give a complex vector space equipped with three opposed filtrations $W$, $F$, and $\bar F$, and with a complex conjugation which respects $W$ and interchanges $F$ and $\bar F$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:1.2}, the functor $\Gr^W$ induces an equivalence from the category of these objects to the category of bigraded complex vector spaces $V=\bigoplus V^{p,q}$ equipped with an automorphism $d$ satisfying \eqref{eq:1.1.1} and a complex conjugation $c$ which interchanges $V^{p,q}$ and $V^{q,p}$, and for which the complex conjugate $\bar d$ of $d$ is $d^{-1}$:
\[
\bar d=cdc^{-1}=d^{-1}.
\]

The category $\mathscr H$ of real mixed Hodge structures is a rigid abelian $\otimes$-category, with $\End(1)=\R$. It has the fiber functor $\omega_0$, the underlying vector space functor, and hence is neutral Tannakian. We also have the fiber functor $\Gr^W=:\omega_W$. With the notation of Remark~\ref{rem:1.3}, the functorial morphism
\[
a:V_\C\longrightarrow \Gr^W(V_\C)
\]
respects the real structures of both sides because it depends symmetrically on $F$ and $\bar F$. It induces an isomorphism of fiber functors, again denoted
\[
a:\omega_0\xrightarrow{\sim}\omega_W.
\]

With the notation of Section 1, take $k=\C$, and let $G_2$ be the real form of $G$ defined by the following complex conjugation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item on $\Gm\times\Gm$, it is $(\lambda,\mu)\mapsto(\bar\mu,\bar\lambda)$;
\item on $U$, it is the one compatible with the complex conjugation of $\mathcal L$ given by
\[
D^{i,j}\mapsto -D^{j,i}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

As in 1.1, to make $G_2$ act on a real vector space $V$ is equivalent to giving:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item a bigrading $V_\C=\bigoplus V^{p,q}$ of $V_\C$, such that $V^{p,q}$ and $V^{q,p}$ are complex conjugates of one another, and
\item an automorphism $d$ of $V_\C$, such that $\bar d=d^{-1}$ and
\[
(d-1)V^{p,q}\subset\bigoplus_{\substack{i<p\\ j<q}}V^{i,j}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Hence:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:2.1}
The fiber functor $\omega_W$ induces an equivalence from $\mathscr H$ to the category of representations of $G_2$: one has $G_2=\Aut(\omega_W)$.
\end{proposition}

The inverse functor $\Phi$ is obtained as follows. From a representation of $G_2$ on $V$, one obtains a bigrading of $V_\C$ and an automorphism $d$. To this data one attaches the real vector space $V$ equipped with the filtration $W$ whose complexification is the filtration $W$ associated with the bigrading of $V_\C$, and with the filtration
\[
F:=d^{-1/2}(F_W)
\]
of its complexification. The isomorphism $\Phi\circ\Gr^W\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Id}$ is the functorial isomorphism $a$.
