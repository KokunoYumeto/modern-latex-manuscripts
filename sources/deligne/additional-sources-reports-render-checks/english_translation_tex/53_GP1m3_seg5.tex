\section*{16. $P^1$ less trois points: a quotient of the $\pi_1$ motivic (sequence)}

\subsection*{16.11}

For every point base $b$ of $X$, the inclusion of $X$ in $\Gm$ induces
$\pi_1(X,b)^\mot \to \pi_1(\Gm,b)^\mot = \ZZ(1)$.
Notons $U(b)$ the quotient suivant of $\pi_1(X,b)^\mot$ (equipped with the structure
integer quotient):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.11.0}
U(b) := \pi_1(X,b)^\mot / \Ker(\pi_1(X,b)^\mot \to \ZZ(1))',
\end{equation}
or $'$ indique the passage to the group derive. Let $U_1(b)$ the group
$\Ker(\pi_1(X,b)^\mot \to \ZZ(1))^{\mathrm{ab}}$. one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.11.1}
1 \to U_1(b) \to U(b) \to \ZZ(1) \to 1.
\end{equation}
By abus of notation, one notera $U_1(b)(N)$ the image of $U_1(b)$ in
$U(b)(N)$: one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.11.2}
1 \to U_1(b)(N) \to U(b)(N) \to \ZZ(1) \to 1.
\end{equation}
the action by automorphismes interieurs of $U(b)$ on $U_1(b)$ se factorise
by $\ZZ(1)$. Si $b'$ is a other point base, $U(b')$ se deduces of $U(b)$
en tordant by the $U(b)$-torseur of the $U(b)$-chemins of $b$ a $b'$, and
$U_1(b')$ se deduces therefore of $U_1(b)$ en tordant by the $\ZZ(1)$-torseur
image: the torseur of the chemins, in $\Gm$, of $b$ a $b'$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.11.3}
U_1(b') = U_1(b) \text{ tordu par } P_{b',b} \text{ calcul\'e dans } \Gm.
\end{equation}

Lorsque the point base $b$ is a vecteur tangent en $0$, one dispose of the
monodromy en $0$: $\mu_0 : \ZZ(1) \to U(b)$ which scinde
\eqref{eq:16.11.1}. Lorsque $b$ is a vecteur tangent en $1$, one dispose of
$\mu_1 : \ZZ(1) \to U_1(b)$.

Prenons for point base the vecteur tangent $1$ en $0$ and ecrivons
simplement $U$ and $U_1$ for $U(b)$ and $U_1(b)$. one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.11.4}
\mu_0 : \ZZ(1) \to U : U = \ZZ(1) \ltimes U_1.
\end{equation}

For $b'$ a vecteur tangent en $1$, the torseur $P_{b',(0,1)}$, calcule
in $\Gm$, is trivial. By \eqref{eq:16.11.3}, one has $U_1(b') = U_1$,
of or
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.11.5}
\mu_1 : \ZZ(1) \to U_1.
\end{equation}

A reformulation of this definition of $\mu_1$ we will be utile.
Appliquant 13.15 a \eqref{eq:16.11.1}, one trouve that for $t$ a vecteur
tangent en $1$, the torseur of the $U_1$-chemins of $(0,1)$ a $t$ is defined.
Notons the $Q$. The morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.11.6}
Q \times \ZZ(1) \to U_1 : (q,\alpha) \mapsto q^{-1}\mu_t(\alpha)q
\end{equation}
envoie $Q_\ZZ \times \ZZ(1)_\Betti$ in $U_{1\ZZ}$. Parce that $U_1$ is
commutative, $q^{-1}\mu_t(\alpha)q$ is independant of $q$ and
\eqref{eq:16.11.6} se factorise by a morphism of $\ZZ(1)$ in $U_1$:
c'is the morphism $\mu_t$ of \eqref{eq:16.11.5}.

\subsection*{16.12}

On the product $\prod_1^N \QQ(n)$, $\QQ(1)$ agit (action of algebra of Lie
abelienne) by the $\QQ(1) \otimes \QQ(n) \to \QQ(n+1)$:
\[
(a,b) \longmapsto ab
\]
with $a \in \QQ(1)$, $b \in \QQ(n)$, $ab \in \QQ(n+1)$. Exponentiant this
action nilpotente, one obtains a action of the group motivic $\QQ(1)$ on
the product of the $\QQ(n)$ ($1 \leq n \leq N$). One can passer a the projective limit on $N$. We shall have a considerer the group product
semi-direct defined by this action
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.12.1}
\QQ(1) \ltimes \prod_{n \geq 1} \QQ(n)
\end{equation}
and the morphisms $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ suivant of $\QQ(1)$ in this product
semi-direct: $\mu_0$ is the isomorphism identique with the prime factor,
and $\mu_1$ the inclusion of $\QQ(1)$ in the product of the $\QQ(n)$.

the algebra of Lie of \eqref{eq:16.12.1} is the product semi-direct
analogue, for the action of Lie of $\QQ(1)$ on the product of the $\QQ(n)$.

\begin{proposition}[16.13]\label{prop:16.13}
With the notations of \ref{eq:16.11.1}, the group $U \otimes \QQ$ equipped with
$\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ is isomorphic a \eqref{eq:16.12.1}, equipped with $\mu_0$ and
$\mu_1$. the isomorphism is unique.
\end{proposition}

\noindent \textit{Mise en garde.} 16.13 decrit the group motivic
$U \otimes \QQ$, but not sa structure integer. For celle-ci, see 16.16.

\noindent \textsc{Proof.} It is enough to prouver the analogue of 16.13 for
the algebras of Lie of $U$ and of the product semi-direct
\eqref{eq:16.12.1}. Let again $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ the morphisms
defined by $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$ of $\QQ(1)$ in $\Lie U$. Let
$\nu_n := (\ad\mu_0)^{n-1}(\mu_1) : \QQ(n) \to \Lie U$ ($n \geq 1$). The
morphism $\mu_0$ and the $\nu_n$ definissent for each $N$ a morphism
of algebras of Lie
\begin{equation}\label{eq:16.13.1}
\QQ(1) \ltimes \prod_1^N \QQ(n) \to \Lie U^{(N)}.
\end{equation}

En de Rham realization, $\Lie U_{\dR}$ is generated by
$e_0 := \mu_0(1)$, $e_1 := \mu_1(1)$ and is the more grand quotient of
$\mathrm{Lib}(e_0,e_1)^{(N)} = \Lie \pi_1(X,(0,1))_{\dR}^{(N)}$ in lequel
the ideal generated by $e_1$ let commutative. This quotient a for base
$e_0$ and the $(\ad e_0)^{n-1}(e_1)$ ($1 \leq n \leq N$), and the
$(\ad e_0)^{n-1}(e_1)$ commutent between eux. This description shows that
\eqref{eq:16.13.1} is a isomorphism en de Rham realization.
C'is therefore a isomorphism. C'is of ailleurs the unique isomorphism which
respecte $\mu_0$ and $\mu_1$. Passant a the limite on $N$, one obtains
16.13. \hfill $\bullet$
