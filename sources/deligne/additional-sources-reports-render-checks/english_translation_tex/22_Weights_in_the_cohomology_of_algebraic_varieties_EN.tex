\section*{}

1. Let $X$ be a complex algebraic variety, that is, a separated scheme of finite type over $\mathbf C$. We again write $X$ for the usual topological space $X(\mathbf C)$ underlying it. In this lecture I describe a remarkable filtration of the rational cohomology groups of $X$, the \textit{weight filtration}, and give a collection of results about its properties. Its definition will be given in \S 12. For proofs, I refer to the works cited in the bibliography, where the theorems are often proved in more general settings. Working over $\mathbf Q$ allows one to have at the same time Hodge theory, actions of Galois groups, and resolution of singularities.

The weight filtration is a finite increasing filtration. I denote it by $W$. It is also defined in relative situations and in cohomology with proper supports. It depends not only on the topological space $X$, but also on the way in which that space is realized as an algebraic variety. It is compatible with Künneth isomorphisms: under the isomorphism
\[
H^*(X\times Y)=H^*(X)\otimes H^*(Y)
\]
one has
\[
W_j\bigl(H^*(X\times Y)\bigr)=\sum_{k+l=j}W_k(H^*(X))\otimes W_l(H^*(Y)).
\]
It is functorial for algebraic morphisms. More precisely, if $f:X\to Y$ is algebraic, then
\[
f^*:H^*(Y)\to H^*(X)
\]
is strictly compatible with the weight filtrations of $H^*(Y)$ and $H^*(X)$.\footnote{If a class $x\in H^*(X)$ lies in the image of $f^*$, then it has filtration $\le i$ if and only if it is the image of a class of filtration $\le i$.} More generally, every natural map is strictly compatible with the weight filtrations.

The weight filtration is a discrete invariant: it is invariant under deformation of the algebraic structure of $X$. More precisely, one has the following theorem.

\begin{theorem}
Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism. For $t\in S$, let $X_t=f^{-1}(t)$. If the sheaf $R^if_*\mathbf Q$ is locally constant, that is, a local system, then there is a weight filtration $W$ of $R^if_*\mathbf Q$ by local subsystems such that the maps
\[
r_t:(R^if_*\mathbf Q)_t\to H^i(X_t)
\]
are strictly compatible with the weight filtrations, and in particular such that $r_t$ is an isomorphism and $W$ induces the weight filtration of $H^i(X_t)$.
\end{theorem}

3. Roughly speaking, the weight filtration expresses how the cohomology of $X$ may be built from the cohomology of nonsingular projective varieties. Here are several examples.

\begin{example}[3.1]
If $X$ is proper, equivalently compact for example projective, and smooth, equivalently nonsingular, then $H^i(X)$ is pure of weight $i$:
\[
\Gr_j^W H^i(X)=0\quad(j\ne i).
\]
Equivalently, $W_{i-1}H^i(X)=0$ and $W_iH^i(X)=H^i(X)$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[3.2]
Let $X$ be smooth, and let $X$ be open in a nonsingular compactification $\overline X$ with complement a divisor with normal crossings. If the irreducible components of $\overline X-X$ are denoted $Y_\alpha$, and if $Y_I=\bigcap_{\alpha\in I}Y_\alpha$, then the graded pieces of $H^n(X)$ are controlled by the cohomology of the $Y_I$. In particular the possible weights of $H^n(X)$ lie between $n$ and $2n$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[3.3]
If $X$ is proper, possibly singular, then the possible weights of $H^n(X)$ are at most $n$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[3.4]
If $X$ is arbitrary, then the possible weights of $H^n(X)$ lie between $0$ and $2n$.
\end{example}

4. Let $H$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbf Q$, provided with a finite increasing filtration $W$. A \textit{grading} of $W$ is a direct-sum decomposition
\[
H=\bigoplus H_i
\]
such that
\[
W_jH=\bigoplus_{i\le j}H_i.
\]
The filtration $W$ is always gradable, but noncanonically. In the situations considered above, it is often useful to know more: the grading can sometimes be chosen to respect additional algebraic structures.

5. Suppose, for example, that $H$ is the cohomology of a differential graded algebra $\mathscr M$, or of a minimal model in rational homotopy theory. One seeks a grading compatible not only with the vector spaces $H^i$, but also with products and differentials. In favorable cases, one obtains a grading of the whole model whose induced grading on cohomology is the weight grading. Such a grading imposes severe restrictions on the rational homotopy type.

6. Let $X$ be smooth. Then $H^n(X)$ is controlled by the part of the square $[0,n]\times[0,n]$ above the second diagonal, in the following sense. The mixed Hodge structure on $H^n(X)$ has nonzero Hodge numbers only for pairs $(p,q)$ in a region dictated by smoothness. In particular the weights range from $n$ to $2n$.

7. More explicitly, for the cohomology groups considered below, the following regions $\mathscr C$ in the square $[0,n]\times[0,n]$ control the possible Hodge numbers and weights.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(7.1)] If $X$ is proper, $H^n(X)$ is controlled by
\[
\{(p,q)\in[0,n]\times[0,n]\mid p+q\le n\}.
\]
\item[(7.2)] If $X$ is smooth, $H^n(X)$ is controlled by
\[
\{(p,q)\in[0,n]\times[0,n]\mid p+q\ge n\}.
\]
\item[(7.3)] If $N=\dim X\le n$, the square $[0,n]\times[0,n]$ may be replaced by the square $[n-N,N]\times[n-N,N]$.
\end{itemize}

8. Variants.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(8.1)] The group with proper supports $H_c^n(X)$ is controlled by the part of the square $[0,n]\times[0,n]$ below the second diagonal. For $N=\dim X\le n$, this square may again be replaced by $[n-N,N]\times[n-N,N]$. In particular $H_c^{2N}(X)$ is pure of weight $2N$.

\item[(8.2)] The ``duality'' between the proper and smooth cases follows from Poincare duality. If $X$ is smooth and purely of dimension $N$, then
\[
H^n(X)=\Hom(H_c^{2N-n}(X),H_c^{2N}(X))=(H_c^{2N-n}(X)^\vee)(-N).
\]
This argument extends the results stated for smooth $X$ to the case where $X$ is a rational homology manifold. For such an $X$, the image of $H_c^n(X)$ in $H^n(X)$ is pure of weight $n$.

\item[(8.3)] If $X$ is assumed only normal, it remains true that $H^1(X)$ is controlled by
\[
\{(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}.
\]
\end{itemize}

9. Let $X$ be a connected variety, let $x\in X$, and let $\Gamma^{(n)}$ be the largest torsion-free nilpotent quotient of length $n$ of $\pi_1(X,x)$. It is known that sufficiently small finite-index subgroups $H$ of $\Gamma^{(n)}$ have the following property.
\begin{itemize}
\item[($*$)] There exists a nilpotent Lie algebra of length $n$, $(\mathscr G,[\,])$, with $\mathscr G$ a free $\mathbf Z$-module, such that the Campbell--Hausdorff formula
\[
x\circ y=x+y+[x,y]/2+[x,[x,y]]/12+[y,[y,x]]/12+\cdots
\]
turns $\mathscr G$ into a group isomorphic to $H$.
\end{itemize}
Moreover, the Lie algebra
\[
\mathscr G^{(n)}=\mathscr G\otimes\mathbf Q
\]
depends only on $\Gamma^{(n)}$, not on $H$.

\begin{theorem}[Morgan]
If $X$ is normal, then $\mathscr G^{(n)}$ admits a grading $W$, in negative degrees, such that $\mathscr G^{(n)}$ is generated by elements of degrees $-1$ and $-2$, subject only to relations of degrees $-2$, $-3$, and $-4$, together with the vanishing of $n$-fold iterated commutators.
\end{theorem}

It is more convenient to work with the projective system $\mathscr G^{(\infty)}$ of the $\mathscr G^{(n)}$. Defining
\[
H^i(\mathscr G^{(\infty)})=\varinjlim H^i(\mathscr G^{(n)}),
\]
one has
\[
H^1(\mathscr G^{(\infty)})=H^1(X),
\qquad
H^2(\mathscr G^{(\infty)})\subset H^2(X).
\]
Furthermore, a grading $E$ of $\mathscr M$ as in \S 5 defines compatible gradings of $\mathscr G^{(\infty)}$, of its cohomology, and of the cohomology of $X$. By (8.3), respectively (7.1), $H^1(X)$ has weights $1$ and $2$, and $H^2(X)$ has weights at most $4$. Hence $H^1(\mathscr G^{(\infty)})$, the dual of the group $\mathscr G^{(\infty)}/[\mathscr G^{(\infty)},\mathscr G^{(\infty)}]$ of generators of $\mathscr G^{(\infty)}$, has weights $1$ and $2$, while $H^2(\mathscr G^{(\infty)})$, the dual of the group of relations among the generators, has weights at most $4$. This proves the theorem.

11. For a proper smooth variety, the weight filtration reduces to very little (3.1). The fact that it can be graded in the sense of \S 4 implies the vanishing of all Massey products. The fact that it can be graded in the sense of \S 5 even implies that the rational homotopy type of $X$ can be read off from its cohomology algebra; see \cite{DGMS}.

12. Hodge theory. The cohomology group $H^i(X)$ of every complex algebraic variety is endowed with a mixed Hodge structure $(W,F)$. Here $W$ is a filtration of $H^i(X)$ and $F$ is a filtration of
\[
H^i(X)\otimes\mathbf C=H^i(X,\mathbf C),
\]
with $W$ and $F$ satisfying suitable axioms; see \cite{D1}. The weight filtration is, by definition, this filtration $W$.

13. $l$-adic theory. An algebraic variety $X$ is defined by finitely many polynomial equations. The coefficients of these equations generate a subring $R$ of $\mathbf Q$ of finite type, with fraction field $K$, and $X$ is obtained by extension of scalars to $\mathbf C$ from a scheme over $K$, indeed over $R$. The statements below become true after replacing $R$ by $R[1/f]$, with $f\in R$ sufficiently divisible.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(13.1)] Let $l$ be a prime number. The group $H^*(X)\otimes\mathbf Q_l$ identifies with the $l$-adic cohomology group $H^*(X,\mathbf Q_l)$. The latter is defined in a purely algebraic way, and therefore, by transport of structure, carries an action of $\Aut(\mathbf C/K)$.

\item[(13.2)] Let $\overline K$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ inside $\mathbf C$. The action factors through $\Gal(\overline K/K)$. It is unramified over $R[1/l]$, that is, it factors through the Galois group of the largest extension $K_{\mathrm{nr}}\subset\overline K$ of $K$ unramified over $R[1/l]$.

\item[(13.3)] Let $\mathfrak m$ be a maximal ideal of $R[1/l]$, and put $N(\mathfrak m)=\#R/\mathfrak m$, so that $R/\mathfrak m$ is a finite field. To $\mathfrak m$ corresponds a conjugacy class of Frobenius substitutions $\varphi_{\mathfrak m}\in\Gal(K_{\mathrm{nr}}/K)$, whose inverses are the geometric Frobenius elements
\[
F_{\mathfrak m}=\varphi_{\mathfrak m}^{-1}.
\]
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}
For $f$ sufficiently divisible, the following hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] For every $l$ and every $\mathfrak m$, the eigenvalues $\alpha$ of $F_{\mathfrak m}$, considered in a suitable finite extension of $\mathbf Q_l$, are algebraic integers. For each $\alpha$, there is an integer $w(\alpha)$ such that all complex conjugates of $\alpha$ have absolute value
\[
N(\mathfrak m)^{w(\alpha)/2}.
\]

\item[(ii)] Choose a geometric Frobenius $F_{\mathfrak m}$, and let ${}_{\mathfrak m}W_j$ be the sum of the generalized eigenspaces corresponding to eigenvalues $\alpha$ of $F_{\mathfrak m}$ such that $w(\alpha)=j$. The filtration by the ${}_{\mathfrak m}W_j$ is independent of $\mathfrak m$ and of the choice of $F_{\mathfrak m}$; it is rational and its trace on $H^*(X)$ is independent of $l$. It is the weight filtration.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\textsc{Principle of the proof.} One expresses the cohomology of $X$, by means of a spectral sequence, in terms of the cohomology of proper smooth varieties, as in \cite{D1}. The spectral sequence converges to a filtration $W$ of $H^*(X)$, which is by definition the weight filtration in Hodge theory. This spectral sequence has an $l$-adic analogue, so the $l$-adic extension $W_l$ of $W$ is stable under a Galois group. The Weil conjecture implies that the eigenvalues of $F_{\mathfrak m}$ on $\Gr_j^{W_l}$ are algebraic integers with complex absolute values $N(\mathfrak m)^{j/2}$, and the theorem follows.

15. In the cases considered in (7.1), (7.2), and (7.3), the region $\mathscr C$ described there controls not only the weights but also the Hodge numbers and the divisibility of the eigenvalues of the $F_{\mathfrak m}$. More precisely, in (7.1), (7.2), and (7.3), a region $\mathscr C$ was assigned to a group $H$, and:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] the nonzero Hodge numbers $h^{p,q}$ of the Hodge structures $\Gr_j^W(H)$ satisfy $(p,q)\in\mathscr C$;
\item[(b)] useful only in case (7.3), if the pairs $(p,q)\in\mathscr C$ with $p+q=j$ satisfy $p,q\ge k$, then, again for $f$ sufficiently divisible, the eigenvalues of $F_{\mathfrak m}$ with complex absolute value $N(\mathfrak m)^{j/2}$ are divisible by $N(\mathfrak m)^k$.
\end{itemize}
One hopes that this is a special case of a general principle; see \cite{M}.

16. The following result is clear from the viewpoint of Hodge theory.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(16.1)] For $j$ odd, $\dim\Gr_j^W H^n(X)$ is even.
\end{itemize}
By contrast, I do not yet know how to prove \S\S 2 and 5 except by the $l$-adic method. For \S 5, the method of \S 14 gives an $l$-adic grading; a trick of Sullivan then implies the existence of rational gradings of the required type.

The functoriality properties of the weight filtration are evident from the $l$-adic point of view, because Galois commutes with everything that can be defined. It is nevertheless useful to prove them from the Hodge-theoretic point of view, in order to obtain analogous properties for the filtration $F$. Morgan has obtained many results in this direction, enough to prove a slightly weaker form of \S 10 by Hodge theory.

17. As \S 5 suggests, a weight filtration also exists on the homotopy groups, after tensoring with $\mathbf Q$, under hypotheses of simple connectedness.

It also exists on groups of vanishing cycles (cf. \cite{St}), and I hope that these filtrations will help us understand them better.
