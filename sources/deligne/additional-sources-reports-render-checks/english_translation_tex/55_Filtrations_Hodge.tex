L'analogie est basée sur la conjecture de Grothendieck (étendue aux motifs mixtes) comme quoi si la réalisation de de Rham d'un motif $M$ vérifie $M_{\mathrm{DR}} = \mathrm{F}^\mu M_{\mathrm{DR}}$, alors $M$ est produit tensoriel du motif de Tate $\mathbf{Q}(-\mu)$ par un motif effectif, et qu'en conséquence, pour une réduction modulo $p$ de $M$ sur $\mathbf{F}_q$ les valeurs propres de Frobenius sont divisibles par $q^\mu$. \[

\begin{proposition}[3.5]
With the notations precedentes

\emph{(i)} $\mathrm{H}^*(G(r,s), \Omega^p(-a)) = 0$ for $0 < a < l - p$.

\emph{(ii)} The same conclusion vaut for $0 < a \leqq l - p$, to the exceptions suivantes pres: $\mathrm{H}^*(G(3,3), \Omega^3(-2))$, $\mathrm{H}^*(G(2,r), \Omega^{r/2}(1+r/2))$ for $r$ pair, and similarly for $G(r, 2) \simeq G(2, r)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
We shall use the critere (3.4.1). Let $R$, $\bar{R}$ as en 3.4 with $l(R) = p$. Posons $x = \inf(\bar{R}) - 1$, $y = \sup(R)$. one has $[1, x] \subset R$, $x+1 \in \bar{R}$ therefore $(R+a) \cap \bar{R} \neq \varnothing$ si $0 < a \leqq x$; one has $[y+1, r+s] \subset \bar{R}$, $y \in R$ therefore $(R+a) \cap \bar{R} \neq \varnothing$ si $0 < a \leqq r+s-y$.

Supposons therefore that $a > x$ and $a > r+s-y$. one has then
\begin{align*}
\inf((R+a) \cup \bar{R}) &= \inf \bar{R} = x+1 \\
\sup((R+a) \cup \bar{R}) &= \sup(R+a) = y+a
\end{align*}
and $(R+a) \cup \bar{R} \subset [x+1, y+a]$ a to the more $y-x+a$ elements.

The paires $(i, j)$ with $j = x+1$, $i \in R - [1, x]$, ainsi that the paires $(i, j)$ with $i = y$, $j \in \bar{R} - [y+1, r+s]$, satisfy $i \in R$, $j \in \bar{R}$, $j < i$. The paire $(y, x)$ is comptee deux fois. one has therefore
\[
l(R) \geqq (r-x) + (s-(r+s-y)) - 1 = y - x - 1.
\]
There is equality if and only if $R$ is of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.5.1}
R = [1, x] \cup [x+2, r] \cup \{y\},\ R \neq [1, r].
\tag{3.5.1}
\end{equation}

Si the condition (i) is remplie, one has therefore
\[
0 < a < (r+s-1) - (y-x-1) = (r+s) - (y-x)
\]
and
\[
\#((R+a) \cup \bar{R}) < (y-x) + [(r+s) - (y-x)] = r+s,
\]
of or $(R+a) \cap \bar{R} \neq \varnothing$ as promis.

Prouvons (ii). Si the a of the inegalites $a \leqq l-p$, $l(R) \geqq y-x-1$ is stricte, the argument ci-dessus continue a s'appliquer. One can supposer, and one assumed, that $a = (r+s) - (y-x)$ and $l(R) = y-x-1$. In particular, $R$ a the form (3.5.1). Si $x = 0$, one has $a \leqq r+s-y$. Si $y = r+s$, one has $a \leqq x$. These cas have deja ete traites: one can supposer and one assumed that $x \neq 0$, $y \neq r+s$. one has $1 \leqq x \leqq r-1$, $r+1 \leqq y \leqq r+s-1$.

Assume that $(R+a) \cap \bar{R} = \varnothing$ and montrons that this conduit to the cas of exception of (ii). The translation $z \mapsto z+a$ envoie $y$ on $(r+s)+x$ and si $i \in [1, r+s]$, let $i+a \in [1, r+s]$, let $i+a-(r+s) \in [1, x] \subset R$. Let $qR$ and $q\bar{R}$ the images of $R$ and $\bar{R}$ in $\mathbf{Z}/(r+s)$. Puisque $(R+a) \cap \bar{R} = \varnothing$, one has therefore $qR + a \subset q\bar{R}$ of or $qR + a = q\bar{R}$ and $q\bar{R} + a = qR$.

Appelons ``composantes connected'' of $R$ (resp.\ $qR$) the more grands intervalles (resp.\ images of intervalles) contenus in $R$ (resp.\ $qR$). The composantes connected of $qR$ are the images of celles of $R$. By (3.5.1), $R$ a 2 or 3 composantes connected. The translation $z \mapsto z+a$ permute the composantes connected of $qR$, and envoie celle of $y$ in the suivante, celle of $x$. The complement $q\bar{R}$ a the same nombre of composantes connected, and they are permutees similarly.

Si $R$ a 3 composantes, the composante of $y$ is reduite a $y$ and each composante a 1 element. Similarly, $x$ is a composante of $\bar{R}$ and each composante of $\bar{R}$ a 1 element: one has $r = s = 3$, $R = \{1, 3, 5\}$ and $a = 2$. C'is the prime cas of exception. one has $l(R) = 3$.

Assume that $R$ a deux composantes connected. Deux cas: $x = r-1$ and $y = r+1$. Si $x = r-1$, the composante of $y$ is reduite a $y$ and each composante a 1 element. one has $R = \{1, y\}$ and the translation $z \mapsto z+a$ permute $1$ and $y$ in $\mathbf{Z}/(r+s)$: one has $r = 2$, $s$ pair,
\[
R = \Bigl\{1, 2 + \frac{s}{2}\Bigr\}.
\]
Si $y = r+1$, the composante of $x$ in $\bar{R}$ is reduite a $x$, and similarly $r$ is pair, $s = 2$,
\[
R = \biggl[1, \frac{r}{2}\biggr] \cup \biggl[\frac{r}{2} + 2, r+1\biggr].
\]
C'is the deuxieme cas of exception.
\end{proof}
