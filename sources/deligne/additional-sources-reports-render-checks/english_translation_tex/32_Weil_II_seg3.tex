\section{Pinceaux of Lefschetz}

In this section, sauf to the n$^\circ$~5, we travaillerons to the-dessus of a field algebriquement clos $k$, of caracteristique $p$. Let $\ell$ a nombre prime distinct of $p$. The groups of cohomology consideres will be with coefficients in $\Zl$, $\Ql$ or $\bbZ/m$, for $m$ a power of $\ell$.

\subsection{The Lefschetz theorem difficult}

In this numero, we supposerons choisi a isomorphism, on $k$, between $\Zl$ and $\Zl(1)$. This we dispensera of ecrire the twists a the Tate.

\begin{theorem}[4.1.1]
Let $X$ a projective variety and smooth on $k$, $\scrL$ a sheaf inversible ample on $X$ and $\eta = c_1(\scrL) \in H^2(X, \Ql)$. One assumed $X$ purement of dimension $n$. Then, for each $i \geq 0$, the cup product by $\eta^i$: $\eta^i \smile : H^{n-i}(X, \Ql) \to H^{n+i}(X, \Ql)$ is a isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

Prouvons the theorem by recurrence on $n$. The cas $n=0$ is trivial, and one assumed that $n \geq 1$. Puisque $c_1(\scrL^{\otimes m}) = m c_1(\scrL)$, the assertion of the theorem for $\scrL$ equivaut a the same assertion for ses powers tensorielles. This we permet of supposer $\scrL$ tres ample. Let $Y$ a section hyperplane smooth of $X$ (in the prolongement projective defined by $\scrL$). We shall all of abord deduire of the hypothese of recurrence that the theorem, for $X$ and $\scrL$, equivaut to the

\begin{lemma}[4.1.2]\ \textup{(cf.~SGA 7 XVIII (5.2.4)).} --- The form of intersection on $H^{n-1}(Y)$ is not degeneree on the image of $H^{n-1}(X)$.
\end{lemma}

For $i=0$, the theorem is trivial. For $i \geq 1$, one interprete the cup-product by $\eta = cl(Y)$ as the compose of the morphism of restriction $H^*(X) \to H^*(Y)$, and of son transpose by duality of Poincare $H^*(Y) \to H^{*+2}(X)$ (SGA $4\frac{1}{2}$), for factoriser $\eta^i \smile : H^{n-i}(X) \to H^{n+i}(X)$ en:
\[
\eta^i \smile : H^{n-i}(X) \to H^{(n-1)-(i-1)}(Y) \xrightarrow{\eta^{i-1} \smile} H^{(n-1)+(i-1)}(Y) \to H^{n+i}(X).
\]
For $i \geq 2$, the fleches extremes are of the isomorphisms, by the Lefschetz theorem faible rappele ci-dessous, and the fleche mediane is a isomorphism of after the hypothese of recurrence, of or the theorem. For $i=1$, the cas crucial, Lefschetz faible dit only that $H^{n-1}(X)$ s'injecte in $H^{n-1}(Y)$. Son transpose $H^{n-1}(Y) \to H^{n+1}(X)$ is therefore surjective, and for that the compose let bijective, it is necessary and sufficient that the states of the lemma let vrai. Remplacant $\scrL$ by a of ses powers tensorielles, one can supposer that, in the embedding projective defined by $\scrL$, $X$ admet a pinceau of Lefschetz of sections hyperplanes (SGA 7 XVII (2.5)). Prenons $Y$ in a tel pinceau $(Y_t)_{t \in \bbP^1}$: $Y = Y_{t_0}$. We shall prouver (4.1.2), for $Y$, en interpretant the image of $H^{n-1}(X)$ in $H^{n-1}(Y)$ en terme of monodromy.

Let $S$ the set of the values of $t$ for lesquelles $Y_t$ is singular. The $H^i(Y_t)$ ($t \in \bbP^1 - S$) are then the fibers of a $\Ql$-sheaf smooth on $\bbP^1 - S$, and $\pi_1(\bbP^1 - S, t_0)$ agit on $H^i(Y)$. one has

\begin{proposition}[4.1.3]
the image of $H^{n-1}(X)$ in $H^{n-1}(Y)$ is the under-space $H^{n-1}(Y)^{\pi_1}$ of the invariants by the monodromy.
\end{proposition}

This result is proves in SGA 7 XVIII (5.6.10) (under the hypothese that $Y$ satisfies the theorem --- notre hypothese of recurrence). On $\bbC$, and en cohomology integer, a proof direct is datum in SGA 7 XIX (4) (y conjuguer (4.3.1) and the formule of Picard-Lefschetz). To the n$^\circ$~(4.3), we transposerons this derniere to the cas of the $\Zl$-cohomology, on $k$ quelconque.

We savons moreover (3.4.13) that $H^{n-1}(Y)$ is a representation completement reductible of $\pi_1(\bbP^1 - S, t_0)$ and it ne reste that a appliquer the lemma algebraic suivant.

\begin{lemma}[4.1.4]
Let $V$ a representation lineaire completement reductible of a group $\pi$, equipped with a form bilineaire $\Phi$, invariante by $\pi$ and not degeneree. Then, the restriction of $\Phi$ a $V^\pi$ is not degeneree.
\end{lemma}

By complete reductibilite, one has $V = V^\pi \oplus W$, for a under-representation $W$ ne contenant not the representation trivial en quotient. The under-space $W$ is therefore $\Phi$-orthogonal a $V^\pi$: $\Phi = (\Phi|V^\pi) \oplus (\Phi|W)$, and $\Phi|V^\pi$ is not degeneree.

\begin{corollary}[4.1.5]
The nombres of Betti of indice impair of a projective variety not singular $X$ are pairs.
\end{corollary}

One se ramene a supposer the variety connected, purement of dimension $n$. Si $\eta = c_1(\scrL)$, with $\scrL$ ample, the form $\Tr(\eta^i xy)$ on $H^{n-i}(X)$ is not degeneree, of after (4.1.1) and the duality of Poincare. Si $n-i$ is impair, it is alternee, of sorte that $b_{n-i} = b_{n+i}$ is pair.

This result devrait valoir without hypothese of projectivite, but je ne sais not the prouver en caracteristiche $\neq 0$.

\subsubsection*{\textup{(4.1.6)} Lefschetz faible.}

Let $X$ and $Y$ as ci-dessus, a that pres that one assumed $X$ purement of dimension $n+1$ (therefore $Y$ purement of dimension $n$). The result clef is celui which given the dimension cohomologique of the varieties affine (SGA 4 XIV (3.2)). It given, en $\bbZ/m$-cohomology, that:
\[
H^i_c(X - Y) = 0 \quad \text{pour} \quad i > n+1.
\]
Puisque $X - Y$ is smooth, one has also, by duality of Poincare:
\[
H^i(X - Y) = 0 \quad \text{pour} \quad i < n.
\]
The exact sequence longue of cohomology relative:
\[
\to H^i_c(X - Y) \to H^i(X) \to H^i(Y) \to
\]
permet of en deduire that:
\[
H^i(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^i(Y) \quad \text{pour} \quad i < n = \dim(Y) \quad \text{et que} \quad H^n(X) \hookrightarrow H^n(Y).
\]
The morphisms of Gysin, transposes by duality of Poincare of the morphisms of restriction, are therefore such that:
\[
H^i(Y) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^{i+2}(X) \quad \text{pour} \quad i > n \quad \text{et} \quad H^n(Y) \twoheadrightarrow H^{n+2}(X).
\]
By passage a the limite, these results restent vrais en $\Zl$- and $\Ql$-cohomology. En $\Zl$-cohomology, the formule of the coefficients universels:
\[
H^i_c(X - Y, \bbZ/\ell) = H^i_c(X - Y, \Zl) \otimes \bbZ/\ell \oplus \Tor^{\bbZ}_1(H^{i+1}_c(X - Y, \Zl), \bbZ/\ell)
\]
fournit the renseignement supplementaire that $H^{n+1}_c(X - Y, \Zl)$ is without twisting. This implique that $H^n(Y, \Zl) / H^n(X, \Zl)$ is without twisting.

\subsection{Pinceaux of Lefschetz}

In (1.3), (1.4), je resumais the results essentiels of the theorie of the pinceaux of Lefschetz, en excluant the cas or $p=2$ and or the sections hyperplanes are of dimension paire. The but of this numero is of reparer en part this omission and, this faisant, of completer SGA 7 XVIII: in the cas sauvage, the theorie vaut for of the pinceaux of Lefschetz generaux, and not only for ceux which are generiques.

\subsubsection*{\textup{(4.2.1)} Theorie local.}

Let $f : X \to S$ as en (1.4.2), of dimension relative $n$. One sets $n = 2n'$ or $2n'+1$. Let $\delta \in H^n(X_{\overline{\eta}})(n')$ the vanishing cycle. It is bien defined to the signe pres. In the cas or $n$ is pair and or $p=2$, exclu in (1.4.3), the group of inertie $I$ admet plusieurs characters of ordre $2$. For the a of eux, dependant of the singularity quadratique ordinaire of $f$, the local monodromy is again given by the formule of loc.~cit. All these results valent also bien en $\bbZ/m$-, en $\Zl$- or en $\Ql$-cohomology. The proof, in the cas of the $\bbZ/m$-cohomology, of which decoulent the other, is in SGA 7. Notons the

\begin{corollary}[4.2.2]
the image of $H^n(X_s)$ in $H^n(X_{\overline{\eta}})$ is the orthogonal of $\delta$. En $\Zl$- or $\Ql$-cohomology, c'is also the under-space of the invariants by the group of inertie $I$.
\end{corollary}

The seconde assertion resulte of this that the expression $(x, \delta)\delta$ which apparait in the formule of Picard-Lefschetz donnant the action of $I$ is zero if and only if $(x, \delta) = 0$: c'is clear si $\delta$ n'is not of twisting, and si $\delta$ is of twisting, $(x, \delta)$ is identiquement zero.

\subsubsection*{\textup{(4.2.3)} Theorie global.}

One reprend the notations of (1.5.6); in particular $X \subset \bbP$ is a projective variety not singular connected and of dimension $n+1$, $(X_t)_{t \in D}$ is a pinceau of Lefschetz of sections hyperplanes of $X$, $S$ is the set of the values exceptionnelles $t \in D$ for lesquelles the section hyperplane $X_t$ is singular, and $j : U \to D$ the inclusion of the open complementaire. One sets $n = 2n'$ or $2n'+1$. Identifions $D$ a line of the projective space dual $\check{\bbP}$, and notons $X^\vee \subset \check{\bbP}$ the image of $X$ by the correspondence ``the hyperplan $H$ is tangent a $X$ en $x$''. A pinceau of Lefschetz is dit \emph{transverse} si $D$ is transverse a $X^\vee$ (in particular, $D \cap X^\vee = \emptyset$ si $X^\vee$ n'is not a hypersurface). In the cas of exception, a pinceau of Lefschetz n'is not automatiquement transverse.

Recall (cf.~SGA 1 V (5.7)) that a \emph{chemin} between deux points geometriques $\overline{a}$ and $\overline{b}$ of a scheme $S$ is a isomorphism between the functors ``fiber en $\overline{a}$'' and ``fiber en $\overline{b}$'', of the category of the coverings etale of $S$ in (Ens). For every chemin $c$, trace on $U$, of $u$ a point generique geometric $\overline{\eta}_t$ of $D_{(t)}$, one has $H^n(X_u, \Zl) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^n(X_{\overline{\eta}_t}, \Zl)$, of or a vanishing cycle $\pm \delta_c$ in $H^n(X_u, \Zl)(n')$.

\begin{definition}[4.2.4]
\ \begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] the \emph{set of the vanishing cycles} is the set of these cycles $\pm \delta_c$ for $c$ and $t \in S$ variables.
\item[(ii)] The \emph{part evanescente} of $H^n(X_u)$ is the under-space generated by the $\lambda \delta$ ($\lambda \in \Zl(-n')$, $\delta$ evanescent).
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(4.2.5)}}

Puisque the projective line $D$ is simply connected, the fundamental group $\pi_1(U, u)$ is topologiquement generated by the conjugues of the groups of inertie $I_t$ for $t \in S$. The \emph{group of monodromy}, image of $\pi_1(U, u)$ in $\GL(H^n(X_u, \Zl))$, is therefore topologiquement generated by the images of the conjugues of the $I_t$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[$n$ impair:] by the transvections $x \mapsto x + \lambda(x\delta)\delta$, $\lambda \in \Zl(1)$, the quotient $\ell$-primaire of the group of inertie modere;
\item[$n$ pair:] by the reflexions $x \mapsto x - (-1)^{n'}(x\delta)\delta$ (one has $(\delta, \delta) = (-1)^{n'} \cdot 2$).
\end{itemize}

It devrait resulter of a theorie --- not ecrite --- of the vanishing cycles for a base of dimension $> 1$ that, for a pinceau of Lefschetz transverse, the vanishing cycles pris to the signe pres $\pm \delta$ are all conjugues under the group of monodromy and that the geometrie of the situation (in particular, the group of monodromy) ne depend not of the pinceau of Lefschetz transverse choisi. We we contenterons of results more faibles (cf.~SGA 7 XVIII (6)):

\begin{proposition}[4.2.6]
For a pinceau of Lefschetz generique, the vanishing cycles, pris to the signe pres, are all conjugues under the group of monodromy.
\end{proposition}

Si $X^\vee$ n'is not a hypersurface of $\check{\bbP}$, then, for a pinceau general, one has $S = \emptyset$. the assertion is trivial in this case. Supposons therefore that $X^\vee$ Let hypersurface. Puisque $X$ is assumed irreducible, $X^\vee$ is irreducible.

Let $\eta$ the point generique of the grassmannienne of the lines in $\check{\bbP}$, $\overline{\eta}$ a point generique geometric, $D_\eta$ the projective line on $k(\eta)$ corresponding, and $D_{\overline{\eta}}$ celle which s'en deduces by extension of scalars a $k(\overline{\eta})$. On $D_{\overline{\eta}}$, the lieu critique $S_{\overline{\eta}}$ is the intersection of $D_{\overline{\eta}}$ with $X^\vee$. The scheme $S_\eta$ s'envoie on the point generique of $X^\vee$, with a fiber geometric connected. The scheme $S_\eta$ is therefore \emph{connected}.

Posons $U_\eta = D_\eta - S_\eta$ and $U_{\overline{\eta}} = D_{\overline{\eta}} - S_{\overline{\eta}}$. For $u \in U_{\overline{\eta}}$, considerons the fundamental groups:
\[
\pi_1(U_{\overline{\eta}}, u) \to \pi_1(U_\eta, u) \to \pi_1(\check{\bbP} - X^\vee, u).
\]
The theorem of Bertini assure that the map composee is surjective. Moreover, the action of $\pi_1(U_\eta, u)$ on the cohomology of the section hyperplane $X_u$ corresponding a $u$ se factorise by $\pi_1(\check{\bbP} - X^\vee, u)$. For prouver the conjugaison of the vanishing cycles under $\pi_1(U_{\overline{\eta}}, u)$ it is enough therefore of prouver a conjugaison under $\pi_1(U_\eta, u)$. This group s'envoie on $\Gal(\overline{\eta}/\eta)$, and the conjugaison resulte by transport of structure of this that $\Gal(\overline{\eta}/\eta)$ agit transitivement on $S_{\overline{\eta}}$ (connexite of $S_\eta$).

For $p=2$, $n$ pair, one assumed that the pinceau of Lefschetz $(X_t)_{t \in D}$ is sufficiently general ($=$ of axe in a open suitable of the grassmannienne). Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $n$ impair: the homomorphismes of $\Zl(1)$ in the group of monodromy, donnes by the formules of Picard-Lefschetz $x \mapsto x + \lambda(x\delta)\delta$, for $\delta$ a vanishing cycle, are conjugues between eux;
\item[(ii)] $n$ pair: the reflexions $x \mapsto x - (-1)^{n'}(x\delta)\delta$, for $\delta$ a vanishing cycle, are conjugues between they.
\end{itemize}
\end{corollary}

For a pinceau generique, this corollary resulte of (4.2.6) and of the formules of Picard-Lefschetz donnant the action of the groups of inertie local. One passe of the to the cas general grace a (1.11).

\begin{corollary}[4.2.8]
Under the hypotheses of (4.2.7) the vanishing cycles pris to the signe pres are all conjugues in $H^n(X_u, \Ql)$.
\end{corollary}

Si the one neglige the twisting, $\pm \delta$ is indeed determine, to the signe pres, by the transformation of Picard-Lefschetz corresponding.

\section{Map to the Ql-type of homotopie}

In this section, we travaillons on $\bbC$. Let $X$ a algebraic variety complex smooth. The but is of comparer the type of homotopie rational of $X$ with celui defined by the cohomology $\ell$-adique.

\subsection{The Z\{t\}-de Rham complex, of after Grothendieck and Miller}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.1.1)}}

Let $A$ a ring commutative. One denotes $\Zt$ the localise of the ring of polynomes $\bbZ[t]$ en the part multiplicative formee of the $P(t)$ of terme constant inversible (the $P(t)$ such that $P(0) \in \bbZ^*$):
\[
\Zt = \bbZ\{t\} = \bbZ[t]_{(t)} = \bbZ[t, (1+t\bbZ[t])^{-1}].
\]
More intrinsequement, $\Zt$ is the complete of $\bbZ[t]$ en the ideal $(t)$. one has:
\[
\Zt = \varprojlim \bbZ[t]/(t^n).
\]
Let $\Omega$ the $A$-algebra differentielle graduee quotient of the algebra of Rham of $A$ by the ideal generated by the $da$ ($a \in A$) and leurs differentielles. One sets:
\[
\scrA = A \otimes \Zt \quad \text{et} \quad \scrB = A \otimes \Omega
\]
and the one munit $\scrB$ of the differentielle $d(a \otimes \omega) = 1 \otimes a\omega$ (c'is a cas particulier of the construction of the tensor product of algebras differentielles graduees). The de Rham complex rational of $A$ is then:
\[
\At := \scrA \otimes \scrB
\]
equipped with the differentielle tensor product.

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.1.2)}}

For a scheme $X$ on $\bbC$, one sets:
\[
\At(X) = \Gamma(X, \scrA_X^\bullet) = \Gamma(X, \scrA_X \otimes \Omega_X^\bullet)
\]
This complex, fonctoriel en $X$, defines on the schemes smooth on $\bbC$ a complex of sheaves for the topologie usuelle. The sheaves of cohomology $\scrH^i(\At(X))$ are the sheaves of de Rham cohomology.

\begin{theorem}[5.1.3]
For $X$ smooth on $\bbC$, one has $H^*(\At(X)) \xrightarrow{\sim} H^*(X, \bbQ)$.
\end{theorem}

The proof utilise the theorem of Grothendieck, which compare the de Rham cohomology algebraic with the de Rham cohomology holomorphic, and celle-ci with the cohomology singular, via the lemma of Poincare. One utilise also the fait that the de Rham cohomology rational is isomorphic a the cohomology rational ordinaire, for the smooth varieties.

\subsection{The Ql-type of homotopie}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.2.1)}}

Let $X$ a algebraic variety over a field $k$, and let $\ell$ a nombre prime distinct of the caracteristique of $k$. One can define a type of homotopie $\ell$-adique $T_\ell(X)$ by analogie with the type of homotopie rational. More precisely, for $k = \bbC$, the type of homotopie rational of $X(\bbC)$ is determine by the datum of:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] the fundamental group $\pi_1$;
\item[(ii)] the groups of homotopie superieurs;
\item[(iii)] the operations of $\pi_1$ on the $\pi_i$.
\end{itemize}

One can reconstruire the type of homotopie rational a partir of the cohomology rational, en utilisant the theorie of Sullivan. The type of homotopie rational is determine by the $A_\infty$-algebra of cohomology (or the algebra differentielle graduee minimale).

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.2.2)}}

Let $X$ a algebraic variety over a field finite $\Fqt$, and $\overline{X} = X \otimes_{\Fqt} \overline{\Fqt}$. The Frobenius geometric $F$ agit on the cohomology $\ell$-adique $H^i(\overline{X}, \Qlb)$. one has:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] The values proper of $F$ on $H^i(\overline{X}, \Qlb)$ are of the nombres algebraic;
\item[(ii)] For every value proper $\alpha$, there exists a integer $w$ such that for every place finite $\lambda$ ne divisant not $p$, $|\alpha_\lambda| = q^{w/2}$, or $q$ is the cardinal of $\Fqt$.
\end{itemize}

This is a consequence direct of the theorem fondamental (3.3.1) on the purete of the perverse sheaves.

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.2.3)}}

En utilisant the theorie of Sullivan and the cohomology $\ell$-adique, one can define the $Q_\ell$-type of homotopie of a variety over a field algebriquement clos of caracteristique $p$, and etudier son comportement under specialisation.

\begin{theorem}[5.2.4]
Let $f : X \to S$ a morphism proper and smooth, with $S$ the spectre of a ring of valuation discrete strictement henselien of field residuel of caracteristique $p$. Then, the $Q_\ell$-type of homotopie of the fiber generique geometric is isomorphic a celui of the fiber speciale geometric.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Graduations by the weight}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.3.1)}}

Let $X$ a algebraic variety on $\bbC$. The cohomology rational $H^*(X, \bbQ)$ is equipped with a weight filtration $W$, defined by:
\[
W_n H^i(X, \bbQ) = \text{image de } H^i(\overline{X}, \bbQ) \to H^i(X, \bbQ)
\]
for a compactification smooth $\overline{X}$ of $X$. The quotients successifs $\Gr^W_n H^i(X, \bbQ)$ are purs of weight $n$.

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.3.2)}}

One can transposer this construction a the cohomology $\ell$-adique, en utilisant the theorie of the sheaves mixtes. The weight filtration on $H^*(X, \Qlb)$ is then compatible with the mixed Hodge structures.

\begin{proposition}[5.3.3]
For $X$ smooth on $\bbC$, the weight filtration on $H^i(X, \Qlb)$ coincide with celle defined by the mixed Hodge structure on $H^i(X, \bbQ)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(5.3.4)}}

These results permettent of comparer the type of homotopie rational and the $Q_\ell$-type of homotopie, and of etendre the theorie of the weight to the contexte $\ell$-adique.

\begin{definition}

Let $\eta$ the point generique of $S_{(s)}$, $\overline{s}$ a point geometric to the-dessus of $s$ and $\overline{\eta}$ a point geometric generique of the localise strict $S_{(\overline{s})}$. The \emph{sheaves of vanishing cycles} $R^i \Psi(\scrF')$ are of the sheaves on $X_{\overline{s}}$, equipped with a action continue of $\Gal(\overline{\eta}/\eta)$, to the-dessus of son action on $X_{\overline{s}}$ (via $\Gal(\overline{s}/s)$). The restriction of this action to the group of inertie is automatiquement quasi unipotente.

Definissons, for a sheaf $\scrG$, equipped with a telle action $\rho$ of $\Gal(\overline{\eta}/\eta)$, this that signifie be \emph{mixte}:
\begin{itemize}
\item[$a)$] A sheaf on $X_{\overline{s}}$ equipped with a action continue of $\Gal(\overline{s}/s)$ s'identifie a sheaf on $X_s$ (SGA7 XIII (1.1.3)). Si the action $\rho$ se factorise by $\Gal(\overline{s}/s)$, one dit that $(\scrG, \rho)$ is mixte si the sheaf corresponding on $X_s$ the is.
\item[$b)$] Si $(\scrG, \rho)$ admet a filtration finite $F$ such that $\Gr_F(\rho)$ se factorise by $\Gal(\overline{s}/s)$ (cas unipotent), one dit that $(\scrG, \rho)$ is mixte si $\Gr_F(\scrG)$ the is, to the sens $a)$.
\item[$c)$] the hypothese $b)$ devient vraie after avoir remplace $\Gal(\overline{\eta}/\eta)$ by a under-group of indice finite (quasi-unipotence). Geometriquement, this revient a remplacer $S_{(s)}$ by a trait finite on $S_{(s)}$. One dit that $(\scrG, \rho)$ is mixte s'it the devient --- to the sens $b)$ --- after a tel changement of trait.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}
With the notations ci-dessus, si $\scrF$ is mixte, the sheaves of vanishing cycles are egalement mixtes.
\end{theorem}

Grace to the theorem of monodromy, quitte a remplacer $S_{(s)}$ by a covering finite suitable, one can supposer that the group of inertie $I$ agit in such a way unipotente, via son quotient $\Zl(1)$. One can then appliquer the constructions of the numeros (1.6.1), (1.6.14). The formules (1.6.14) montrent that it is enough to verify that the sheaves $(R^i \Psi \scrF')^I$ are mixtes. Let $u$ the inclusion of $X_s$ in $X$, and $v$ celle of the open complementaire. Procedant as en (3.6.1), one sees that these sheaves are quotients of the sheaves $u^* R^i v_*(v^* \scrF)$, and one applique (6.1.11).

\subsection{Complexes purs}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(6.2.1)}}

For every scheme $X$ of type finite on $\Fp$: $a : X \to \Spec(\Fp)$, we posons:
\[
K_X = Ra^! \Qlb.
\]
C'is the \emph{dualizing complex}.

\textbf{Example:} si $X$ is smooth purement of dimension $n$, $K_X$ is reduit to the sheaf $\Qlb(n)$ place en degree $-2n$: $K_X = \Qlb(n)[2n]$.

We denote $D$ the contravariant functor ``dual a value in the dualizing complex'':
\[
D : D_c^b(X) \to D_c^b(X) : K \mapsto R\Hom(K, K_X).
\]
This functor is involutif. For every morphism $f : X \to Y$, it echange the functors $Rf^!$ and $f^*$, ainsi that the functors $Rf_!$ and $Rf_*$. one has also:
\[
D(K \otimes L) = R\Hom(K, DL).
\]

\begin{definition}
A complex $K$ is \emph{mixte of weight $\leq n$} si for every $i$, the sheaf $\scrH^i K$ is mixte of weight ponctuels $\leq i + n$.
\end{definition}

With this definition, the theorem fondamental (3.3.1) fournit the

\begin{proposition}
Let $f : X \to Y$ a morphism of schemes of type finite on $\Fp$. Si $K \in \Ob D_c^b(X)$ is mixte of weight $\leq n$, then $Rf_! K$ is mixte of weight $\leq n$.
\end{proposition}

Let indeed the spectral sequence:
\[
E_1^{pq} = R^p f_! \scrH^q K \Rightarrow \scrH^{p+q} Rf_! K.
\]
of after (3.3.1), the terme $E_1^{pq}$ is mixte of weight ponctuels $\leq p + (q + n)$. the aboutissement $\scrH^{p+q} Rf_! K$ is therefore mixte of weight ponctuels $\leq (p+q) + n$.

\begin{definition}
A complex $K$ is \emph{pur of weight $n$} s'it is mixte of weight $\leq n$, and that $DK$ is mixte of weight $\leq -n$. A sheaf $\scrF$ is \emph{pur of weight $n$} si the complex reduit a $\scrF$ en degree $0$ the is.
\end{definition}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(6.2.5)}}

\textbf{Examples.} --- $a)$ For every $N$, a complex $K$ is mixte of weight $\leq n$ if and only if $K(N)[2N]$ the is. In the definition (6.2.4) of the purete, one can therefore remplacer the dualizing complex $K_X$ by a complex locally of the form $K_X(N)[2N]$, and $DK$ by $DK(N)[2N]$.

$b)$ In particular, for $X$ smooth, one can remplacer the condition on $DK$ by: ``$R\Hom(K, \Qlb)$ is mixte of weight $\leq -n$''. Si the sheaves of cohomology of $K \in \Ob D_c^b(X)$ are smooth, the complex $K$ is pur of weight $n$ if and only if each $\scrH^i K$ is ponctuellement pur of weight $i + n$.

\subsubsection*{\textup{(6.2.6)}}

\begin{proposition}
Si $f : X \to Y$ is proper, and that $K \in \Ob D_c^b(X)$ is pur, then $Rf_* K$ is pur of the same weight.
\end{proposition}

One applique (6.2.3) a $K$ and a $DK$, compte tenu of this that $DRf_* K = Rf_! DK$ puisque $f$ is proper.

\begin{proposition}
For every scheme $X$ of type finite on $\bbZ[1/\ell]$:
\[
a : X \to \Spec(\bbZ[1/\ell]),
\]
posons $K'_X = Ra^! \Qlb$. one has again a functor of passage to the dual $D' K = R\Hom(K, K'_X)$, and a definition of the purete parallele a (6.2.4). Si $X$ is of type finite on $\Fp$, one has $K'_X = K_X(-1)[-2]$: the functors $D$ and $D'$ se deduisent the a of the other by decalage and twist and, of after (6.2.5) $a)$, the definition ci-dessus of ``pur of weight $n$'' generalise (6.2.4). The proposition (6.2.6) reste vraie.
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection*{\textup{(6.2.8)}}

Let $k$ a field algebriquement clos, $S$ the spectre of the henselise of $k[T]$ en $(T)$, $s$ the point closed of $S$, $\eta$ the point generique and $\overline{\eta}$ a point generique geometric. Let $f : X \to S$ a morphism proper.

A complex $K \in \Ob D_c^b(X)$ is dit \emph{potentiellement pur} si $(X/S, K)$ provient of a situation arithmetic of the type suivant:
\begin{itemize}
\item[$a)$] $\Spec(k)$ is a point geometric generique of a scheme integre $A_0$, of type finite on $\bbZ[1/\ell]$;
\item[$b)$] $S$ provient by extension of scalars a $k$, and henselisation, of a curve smooth $S_0$ on $A_0$ equipped with a section;
\item[$c)$] $f$ provient of a morphism proper $f_0 : X_0 \to S_0$;
\item[$d)$] $K$ provient of a complex pur $K_0 \in \Ob D_c^b(X_0)$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[6.2.9]\ \textup{(theorem local of the cycles invariants, for the complexes purs).}
With the notations ci-dessus, let $K$ a complex potentiellement pur, on $X$. Then, the morphism of specialisation:
\[
sp^* : H^*(X_s, K) \to H^*(X_{\overline{\eta}}, K)^{\Gal(\overline{\eta}/\eta)}
\]
is surjective.
\end{theorem}

In the fin of this numero, nos arguments will be geometriques, sauf a reference a (6.2.9) and a reference a (3.4).

Let therefore $k$ a field algebriquement clos. For every scheme $X$ of type finite on $k$: $a : X \to \Spec(k)$ one defines as en (6.2.1) $K_X = Ra^! \Qlb$ and $D(K) = R\Hom(K, K_X)$. one has the same formalism that en (6.2.1).

\subsubsection*{\textup{(6.2.11)}}

The proof is parallele a celle of (4.1.6). One verifies of abord the Lefschetz theorem faible $H_c^i(X - Y, K) = 0$ for $i \leq n$. By duality, this nullite se ramene a celle of $H^{-i}(X - Y, DK)$ for $-i \geq -n$, that one has prise for hypothese. the assertion (i) en resulte, by the exact sequence longue of cohomology.

For $Y$ a section hyperplane sufficiently generale, the restriction of $K$ a $Y$ satisfies the hypothese, with $n$ remplace by $n-1$. That resulte of the theorem of acyclicite generique SGA $4\frac{1}{2}$ [Th.~finitude] for $Z/\check{\bbP}$ and $q^* K$: one has $D(K|Y) = ((DK)|Y)(-1)[-2]$. This permet of appliquer (4.3.7) as en (4.3.8), and of conclure as en (4.3.9).

\begin{corollary}[6.2.12]\ \textup{(theorem global of the cycles invariants, for the complexes purs).}
Under the hypotheses of (6.2.11), si $K$ is potentiellement pur, then, for $Y_u$ a section hyperplane generale of $X$, $H^n(X, K)$ s'identifie to the under-group $H^n(Y_u, K)^{\pi_1(U, u)}$ of the invariants of the monodromy in $H^n(Y_u, K)$.
\end{corollary}

It is enough to the verify for a section hyperplane sufficiently generale. For a pinceau $D$ sufficiently general, $i^* q^* K$ on $\widetilde{X}$ is potentiellement pur (one has $D i^* q^* K = i^* q^* DK$). One can therefore appliquer (6.2.9) en each point of $D - V$, and one trouve that:

\]
of after (6.2.11), $H^n(X, K)$ s'envoie therefore on $H^n(Y_u, K)^{\pi_1(V, u)} \supset H^n(Y_u, K)^{\pi_1(U, u)}$.

\begin{theorem}
Let $X \subset \bbP$ a scheme projective, $\eta$ the premiere class of Chern of $\scrO(1)$ and $K \in \Ob D_c^b(X)$, a complex potentiellement pur. One identifie $\Zl$ a $\Zl(1)$, on $k$, for pouvoir regarder $\eta$ as being in $H^2(X, \Zl)$.

Let $n$ a integer, and supposons $K$ and $DK[-2n]$ satisfy the condition suivante: for every $i$, the dimension of the support of the sheaf $\scrH^i$ is $\leq n - i$ (one sets by convention $\dim \emptyset = -\infty$).

Then, for every $i \geq 0$, the cup-product itere:
\[
\eta^i : H^{n-i}(X, K) \to H^{n+i}(X, K)
\]
is a isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

The proof is parallele a celle of (4.1.1). It procede by recurrence on the dimension of $X$.

Si $X$ is of dimension $0$, and that $\scrH^i K \neq 0$, the hypothese given $0 \leq n - i$ and $0 \leq n - (-i + 2n) = i - n$, let $i = n$. The group $H^i K$ is therefore zero for $i \neq n$, and the assertion en resulte.

Si $Y$ is a section hyperplane sufficiently generale of $X$, the restriction of $K$ a $Y$ satisfies again the hypotheses, with $n$ remplace by $n-1$. This permet as en (4.1.1) of ne considerer that the map:
\[
\eta : H^{n-1}(X, K) \to H^{n-1}(Y, K) \to H^{n+1}(X, K).
\]
The seconde fleche is transposee of the morphism of restriction:
\[
H^{n-1}(X, DK[-2n]) \to H^{n-1}(Y, DK[-2n]).
\]
It s'agit therefore of verify that the duality parfaite between $H^{n-1}(Y, K)$ and $H^{n-1}(Y, DK[-2n])$ (or $DK[-2n] = (D(K|Y))[-2(n-1)]$) induces a duality parfaite between the parties fixes ((6.2.12)). That se satisfies by (4.1.4), the representation of $\pi_1(U, u)$ on $H^*(Y_u, K)$ being semisimple ((6.2.6) and (3.4.1) (iii)).
