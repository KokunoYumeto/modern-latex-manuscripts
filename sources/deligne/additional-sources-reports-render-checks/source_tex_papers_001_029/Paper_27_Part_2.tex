\begin{theorem}[9.16]
If $u \in G^F$ is a regular unipotent element, then, for any $F$-stable maximal torus $T$, we have $Q_{T,G}(u) = 1$.
\end{theorem}

We must prove that for any $w \in \mathbf{W}$, $\Tr(u, H_c^*(X(w))) = 1$. We know that $\Tr(u, H_c^*(X(w)))$ is an integer, and that
\[
\sum_{w \in \mathbf{W}} \Tr(u, H_c^*(X(w))) = |\,\mathbf{W}\,|
\]
(see (7.14) and its proof). Hence it suffices to prove the

\begin{lemma}[9.17]
Under the assumptions of (9.16), $\Tr(u, H_c^*(X(w))) > 0$, for any $w \in \mathbf{W}$.
\end{lemma}

Take $w = s_1 \cdots s_n$ as in (9.10) and let $\bar{X}(s_1, \cdots, s_n)$ be the corresponding compactification of $X(w)$. For $P \subset [1, n]$ we write $\bar{D}_P$ for the intersection, in $\bar{X}(s_1, \cdots, s_n)$, of the divisors $\bar{D}_i$ ($i \in P$) (9.11). For $P = \varnothing$, $\bar{D}_P$ is $\bar{X}(s_1, \cdots, s_n)$. The additivity property of cohomology with compact support shows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.17.1}
\Tr(u, H_c^*(X(w))) = \sum_{P \subset [1,n]} (-1)^{|P|} \Tr(u, H^*(\bar{D}_P))\,.
\end{equation}

Let $b$ be the unique fixed point of $u$ in $X$. Then $a = (b, \cdots, b)$ is the unique fixed point of $u$ in $\bar{X}(s_1, \cdots, s_n)$; it is contained in each $\bar{D}_P$. The variety $\bar{D}_P$ being smooth and compact, $\Tr(u, H^*(\bar{D}_P))$ is just the multiplicity of the unique fixed point $a$ of $u$ acting on $\bar{D}_P$; to compute it, we may replace $\bar{D}_P$ by its completion $\hat{D}_P$ at $a$. Let $(x_i)$ be a formal coordinate system for $\bar{X}(s_1, \cdots, s_n)$ at $a$, such that $x_i = 0$ is an equation for $\bar{D}_i$. In terms of these coordinates, $u$ maps the point with coordinates $x_1, \cdots, x_n$ into the point with coordinates $P_1(x_1, \cdots, x_n), \cdots, P_n(x_1, \cdots, x_n)$, for some formal power series $P_i$. Since the divisor $\bar{D}_i$ is preserved by $u$, $P_i$ is divisible by $x_i$. The jacobian matrix of $u$ at $(0, \cdots, 0)$ is hence diagonal; $u$ being of order a power of $p$, it is the identity, and we have
\[
P_i = x_i(1 + Q_i)\,.
\]
with $Q_i(0, \cdots, 0) = 0$. The fixed point scheme of $u$ is given by the equations $x_i Q_i = 0$.

Let $E_i$ be the divisor $Q_i = 0$. It is non-empty. The formula \eqref{eq:9.17.1} can be rewritten
\begin{align*}
\Tr(u, H_c^*(X(w)))
&= \sum_{P \subset [1,n]} (-1)^{|P|} \prod_{i \in P} \hat{D}_i \prod_{j \notin P} (\hat{D}_j + E_j) \\
&= \sum_{P \subset Q \subset [1,n]} (-1)^{|P|} \prod_{i \in Q} \hat{D}_i \prod_{j \notin Q} E_j \\
&= \sum_{Q \subset [1,n]} \Bigl(\sum_{P \subset Q} (-1)^{|P|}\Bigr) \prod_{i \in Q} \hat{D}_i \prod_{j \notin Q} E_j \\
&= \prod_j E_j > 0\,,
\end{align*}
(the product of $n$ divisors stands for the multiplicity of their intersection).

\begin{proposition}[9.18]
Let $s \in G^F$ be a semisimple element. Let $\nu$ be the class function on $G^F$ defined by:
\[
\nu(g) = \begin{cases}
\displaystyle |Z^0Z^0(s)^F|\, q^{l(Z^0(s))} \frac{|Z(s)^F|}{|Z^0(s)^F|}\,, & \text{if $g \in G^F$ is regular with semisimple}\\[1em]
& \text{part conjugate in $G^F$ to $s$}\\[0.5em]
\quad\quad 0\,, & \text{otherwise\,.}
\end{cases}
\]
Then $\nu$ is the character of
\[
\frac{1}{|Z^0(s)^F|} \sum_{\substack{T \\ s \in T}} |T^F| \sum_{\theta \in (T^F)^{\sim}} \theta(s)^{-1} R_T^\theta\,.
\]
\end{proposition}

(Here $Z^0Z^0(s)$ is the connected centre of the connected centralizer of $s$, and $l(Z^0(s))$ is the semisimple rank of $Z^0(s)$.) The proof is similar to that of (7.5); it uses (9.16) and (4.2) instead of (7.2).

For any $\rho \in \mathscr{R}(G^F)$ we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:9.18.1}
\langle \rho, \nu \rangle = \frac{|Z^0Z^0(s)^F|}{|Z^0(s)^F|} q^{l(Z^0(s))} \sum_{u \in A(s)} \rho(su)
\end{equation}
where $A(s)$ is the set of regular unipotent elements in $Z^0(s)^F$.

\begin{corollary}[9.19]
For any $\rho \in \mathscr{R}(G^F)$, the average value of the character of $\rho$ on the regular elements in $G^F$ with semisimple part $s$, equals
\[
\frac{1}{|A(s)|} \sum_{u \in A(s)} \Tr(su, \rho) = \frac{1}{|Z^0(s)^F|} \sum_{\substack{T \\ s \in T}} |T^F| \sum_{\theta \in (T^F)^{\sim}} \theta(s) \langle \rho, R_T^\theta \rangle\,.
\]
\end{corollary}

This follows from (9.18), \eqref{eq:9.18.1} and the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}[9.20]
The number of regular unipotent elements in $G^F$ equals $|G^F|/|Z^{0F}|\, q^l$.
\end{lemma}

(Here $Z^0$ is the connected centre of $G$ and $l$ is the semisimple rank of $G$.)

To count regular unipotents in $G^F$, we observe that any such element is contained in a unique Borel subgroup and then count the regular unipotents in $G^F$ contained in a fixed $F$-stable Borel subgroup.

\section{A decomposition of the Gelfand-Graev representation}

\subsection{}
Let $(G^F)^{\sim}$ be the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible representations of $G^F$ (over $\bar{\mathbf{Q}}_l$). For any $\rho \in (G^F)^{\sim}$, there exists an $F$-stable maximal torus $T$ and $\theta \in (T^F)^{\sim}$ such that $\langle \rho, R_T^\theta \rangle \neq 0$ (7.7); moreover the geometric conjugacy class $[\theta]$ of $(T, \theta)$ (see 5.5), is uniquely determined by $\rho$ (6.3). We thus get a well-defined surjective map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.1.1}
(G^F)^{\sim} \longrightarrow \mathfrak{S}
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak{S}$ is the set of all geometric conjugacy classes of pairs $(T, \theta)$.

\subsection{}
In the rest of this chapter we shall assume that the centre $Z$ of $G$ is connected. Let $T^*$, $B^*$ be as in (1.8). Let $U^*$ be the unipotent radical of $B^*$ and let $U_*^*$ be the subgroup of $U^*$ generated by the root subgroups corresponding to non-simple roots. The quotient $U^*/U_*^*$ is commutative and is a direct product over the simple roots $\alpha$: $\prod_\alpha U_\alpha^*$, with $U_\alpha^*$ one-dimensional. Let $I$ be the set of orbits of $F$ on the simple roots. For any $i \in I$, let $U_i^* = \prod_{\alpha \in i} U_\alpha^*$; then $U^*/U_*^* = \prod_{i \in I} U_i^*$. This decomposition is $F$-stable, hence we have also $U^{*F}/U_*^{*F} = \prod_{i \in I} U_i^{*F}$.

We consider the Gelfand-Graev representation $\Gamma_G = \Ind_{U^{*F}}^{G^F}(\chi)$ where $\chi$ is any character of $U^{*F}$ which is trivial on $U_*^{*F}$ and defines a non-trivial character of $U_i^{*F}$ for all $i \in I$. All such $\chi$ are conjugate under $T^{*F}$, since $Z$ is connected, hence the $G^F$-module $\Gamma_G$ is well-defined up to isomorphism.

Let $\Delta_G$ be the class function on $G^F$ which equals $|Z^F|\, q^l$ on any regular unipotent element in $G^F$ and vanishes on all other elements. ($l$ is the semisimple rank of $G$.) The following result shows that this is the character of a virtual representation of $G^F$ (which will be also denoted by $\Delta_G$):

\begin{proposition}[10.3]
For any subset $J \subset I$, let $P(J) \supset B^*$ be the parabolic subgroup generated by $B^*$ and by the root subgroups corresponding to minus the simple roots in $F$-orbits in $J$. Let $L(J)$ be $P(J)$ modulo its unipotent radical. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.3.1}
\Delta_G = \sum_{J \subset I} (-1)^{|J|} \Ind_{P(J)^F}^{G^F}(\Gamma_{L(J)})\,.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

(Note that the centre of $L(J)$ is connected, since that of $G$ is, hence $\Gamma_{L(J)}$ is well-defined.)

\begin{remark}[10.4]
The following identity is a formal consequence of \eqref{eq:10.3.1}:
\[
\Gamma_G = \sum_{J \subset I} (-1)^{|J|} \Ind_{P(J)^F}^{G^F}(\Delta_{L(J)})\,.
\]
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of 10.3}
For any subset $J \subset I$, let $\mathcal{C}(J)$ be the set of characters $\chi$ of $U^{*F}$ with $\chi \mid U_*^{*F} = 1$ and such that $\chi$ induces a non-trivial character of $U_i^{*F}$ ($i \in I$) if and only if $i \in J$. It is easy to see that, for any $\chi \in \mathcal{C}(J)$, we have
\[
\Ind_{P(J)^F}^{G^F}(\Gamma_{L(J)}) = \Ind_{U^{*F}}^{G^F}(\chi)\,.
\]

Let $u_0 \in U^{*F}$ be a fixed regular unipotent element; in other words, the image of $u_0$ in $U_i^{*F}$ is non-zero for any $i \in I$. It follows that
\[
\sum_{\chi \in \mathcal{C}(J)} \chi(u_0) = (-1)^{|J|}\,.
\]

Hence \eqref{eq:10.3.1} is equivalent to:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.5.1}
\Delta_G = \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal{C}} \chi(u_0) \Ind_{U^{*F}}^{G^F}(\chi)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C} = \bigcup_{J \subset I} \mathcal{C}(J)$. The character of the right hand side of \eqref{eq:10.5.1} vanishes at non-unipotent elements. Its value at $u \in U^{*F}$ is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.5.2}
\begin{aligned}
&|U^{*F}|^{-1} \sum_{\substack{g \in G^F \\ g^{-1}ug \in U^{*F}}} \sum_{\chi \in \mathcal{C}} \chi(g^{-1}ugu_0) \\
&\qquad = q^l\, |U^{*F}|^{-1}\, \#\{g \in G^F \mid g^{-1}ug \in U^{*F},\, g^{-1}ugu_0 \in U_*^{*F}\}\,.\\
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This is zero unless $u \in U^{*F}$ is regular unipotent; we now assume that $u \in U^{*F}$ is regular unipotent. For any $g \in G^F$ with $g^{-1}ug \in U^{*F}$, we have $u \in B^* \cap gB^*g^{-1}$; but $B^*$ is the only Borel subgroup containing $u$, hence $g \in B^{*F}$. If $g = tu'$, $t \in T^{*F}$, $u' \in U^{*F}$, the relation $g^{-1}ugu_0 \in U_*^{*F}$ determines $t$ uniquely modulo $Z^F$ and leaves $u'$ arbitrary. Thus the expression \eqref{eq:10.5.2} becomes
\[
q^l\, |U^{*F}|^{-1}\, |Z^F|\, |U^{*F}| = |Z^F|\, q^l\,,
\]
and (10.3) is proved.

We shall now prove the following

\begin{lemma}[10.6]
Let $M$ be a virtual representation of $G^F$ such that
\[
\langle M, M \rangle = |Z^F|\, q^l \quad \text{and} \quad \langle M, R_T^\theta \rangle = \varepsilon_T^\theta = \pm 1
\]
for any $F$-stable maximal torus $T \subset G$ and any $\theta \in T^{\vee F}$. Then
\[
M = \sum_{x \in \mathfrak{S}} \sigma_x M_x
\]
where $\sigma_x = \pm 1$ and $M_x$ are distinct irreducible representations of $G^F$ such that
\[
M_x = \sigma_x \sum_{\substack{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F \\ [\theta] = x}} \frac{\varepsilon_T^\theta}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} R_T^\theta\,.
\]
\end{lemma}

Let $\{M\}$ be the set of all $\rho \in (G^F)^{\sim}$ such that $\langle \rho, M \rangle \neq 0$; $\{M\}$ has at most $|Z^F|\, q^l$ elements. By our assumption and by 5.7(i), the map $\{M\} \to \mathfrak{S}$ (the restriction of \eqref{eq:10.1.1}) is surjective. Since $|\mathfrak{S}| = |Z^F|\, q^l$ (5.7), it is bijective and $\{M\}$ has exactly $|Z^F|\, q^l$ elements. Let $M_x \in \{M\}$ be the element corresponding to $x \in \mathfrak{S}$ under this bijection; we must have $M = \sum_{x \in \mathfrak{S}} \sigma_x M_x$, $\sigma_x = \pm 1$, since $\langle M, M \rangle = |\{M\}|$. If $(T, \theta)$ is such that $[\theta] = x$, we have
\[
\langle M, R_T^\theta \rangle = \sigma_x \langle M_x, R_T^\theta \rangle = \varepsilon_T^\theta\,;
\]
from the orthogonality of the $R_T^\theta$'s (6.8) we have
\[
M_x = \sigma_x \sum_{\substack{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F \\ [\theta] = x}} \frac{\varepsilon_T^\theta}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} R_T^\theta + M_x'
\]
where $M_x'$ is orthogonal to all the $R_T^\theta$'s. It remains to prove that $M_x' = 0$. Since $M_x$ is irreducible, this would follow if we prove that $\langle M_x - M_x', M_x - M_x' \rangle = 1$; we certainly have $\langle M_x - M_x', M_x - M_x' \rangle \le 1$, hence it is sufficient to prove that
\[
\sum_{x \in \mathfrak{S}} \langle M_x - M_x', M_x - M_x' \rangle = |Z^F|\, q^l
\]
or, in other words, that
\[
\sum_{\substack{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F \\ [\theta] = x}} \frac{1}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} = |Z^F|\, q^l\,.
\]
By (6.8), this is equivalent to the identity
\[
\sum_{T \bmod G^F} \frac{1}{|W(T)^F|}\, |T^F| = |Z^F|\, q^l\,,
\]
which is the same as
\[
\frac{1}{|\mathbf{W}|} \sum_{w \in \mathbf{W}} \det(q - w\tau) = q^l
\]
where $w$ is regarded as an automorphism of the lattice $X_0 = X(T/Z)$ and $\tau$ is defined by $F^* = q \cdot \tau^{-1}$. The last identity (compare Steinberg [15, p.~91]) can be written as
\[
\sum_{0 \le i \le l} (-1)^i q^{l-i} \frac{1}{|\mathbf{W}|} \sum_{w \in \mathbf{W}} \Tr(w\tau, \Lambda^i(X_0)) = q^l
\]
and follows from the fact that $\Lambda^i(X_0)^{\mathbf{W}} = 0$ for $i > 0$ (5.8).

\begin{theorem}[10.7]
We recall that $Z$ is connected. Let $x \in \mathfrak{S}$, and $x'$ be a representative of the corresponding semisimple conjugacy class in the dual group (5.24). We define $\delta_x$ to be the $\mathbf{F}_q$-rank of the centralizer of $x'$.

\emph{(i)} The formulae
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.7.1}
\rho_x = \sum_{\substack{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F \\ [\theta] = x}} \frac{(-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)}}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} R_T^\theta
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.7.2}
\rho_x' = (-1)^{\sigma(G) - \delta_x} \sum_{\substack{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F \\ [\theta] = x}} \frac{1}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} R_T^\theta
\end{equation}
define irreducible representations of $G^F$. The $|Z^F|\, q^l$ elements $\rho_x \in (G^F)^{\sim}$, $x \in \mathfrak{S}$ (resp. $\rho_x' \in (G^F)^{\sim}$, $x \in \mathfrak{S}$) are distinct. The maps $x \mapsto \rho_x$, $x \mapsto \rho_x'$ are two cross-sections of the map \eqref{eq:10.1.1}.

\emph{(ii)} One has
\begin{align}
\label{eq:10.7.3} \Gamma_G &= \sum_{x \in \mathfrak{S}} \rho_x = \sum_{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F} \frac{(-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)}}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} R_T^\theta\,,\\
\label{eq:10.7.4} \Delta_G &= \sum_{x \in \mathfrak{S}} (-1)^{\sigma(G) - \delta_x} \rho_x' = \sum_{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F} \frac{1}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} R_T^\theta\,.\\
\end{align}

\emph{(iii)} Assume that all the roots have the same length. Fix $(T, \theta)$ in the geometric conjugacy class $x$, let $S$ be the connected centralizer of $(T, \theta)$ (5.19) and let $\theta_S$ be the corresponding character of $S^F$. We have
\begin{align}
\label{eq:10.7.5} \rho_x \otimes \St_G &= \Ind_{S^F}^{G^F}(\theta_S \otimes \St_S \otimes \St_S)\,,\\
\label{eq:10.7.6} \rho_x' \otimes \St_G &= \Ind_{S^F}^{G^F}(\theta_S \otimes \St_S)\,.\\
\end{align}

\emph{(iv)} Let $S^*$ be the centralizer of $x'$. We still have
\begin{align}
\label{eq:10.7.7} \dim \rho_x &= \frac{|G^F|/\St_G(e)}{|S^{*F}|/\St_{S^*}(e)} \cdot \St_{S^*}(e)\,,\\
\label{eq:10.7.8} \dim \rho_x' &= \frac{|G^F|/\St_G(e)}{|S^{*F}|/\St_{S^*}(e)}\,.\\
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

If we tensor the right hand side of \eqref{eq:10.7.1} by $\St_G$ we find (by 7.3):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.7.9}
\sum_{\substack{(T, \theta) \bmod G^F \\ [\theta] = x}} \frac{1}{\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle} \Ind_{T^F}^{G^F}(\theta)\,.\end{equation}

Let us first assume that all roots have the same length. By 5.14(ii), the $G^F$-conjugacy classes of pairs $(T, \theta)$, $T \subset G$, with $[\theta] = x$ are in one-to-one correspondence with $S^F$-conjugacy classes of pairs $(T', \theta_S \mid T')$, with $T'$ an $F$-stable maximal torus in $S$. Moreover, by 5.14(i) and (6.8), we have $\langle R_T^\theta, R_T^\theta \rangle = |W_S(T')^F|$. Thus the expression \eqref{eq:10.7.9} equals
\begin{align*}
&\sum_{\substack{T' \subset S \\ \bmod S^F}} \frac{1}{|W_S(T')^F|} \Ind_{T'^F}^{G^F}(\theta_S \mid T') = \sum_{\substack{T' \subset S \\ \bmod S^F}} \frac{1}{|W_S(T')^F|} \Ind_{S^F}^{G^F}\bigl(\Ind_{T'^F}^{S^F}(1) \otimes \theta_S\bigr) \\
&\qquad = \Ind_{S^F}^{G^F}\biggl(\sum_{\substack{T' \subset S \\ \bmod S^F}} \frac{1}{|W_S(T')^F|} \Ind_{T'^F}^{S^F}(1) \otimes \theta_S\biggr) \\
&\qquad = \Ind_{S^F}^{G^F}(\theta_S \otimes \St_S \otimes \St_S)\,, \quad \text{by 7.14}\,.
\end{align*}

Similarly, by tensoring the right hand side of \eqref{eq:10.7.2} by $\St_G$, we find
\[
\Ind_{S^F}^{G^F}\biggl(\sum_{\substack{T' \subset S \\ \bmod S^F}} \frac{(-1)^{\sigma(S) - \sigma(T')}}{|W_S(T')^F|} \Ind_{T'^F}^{S^F}(1) \otimes \theta_S\biggr) = \Ind_{S^F}^{G^F}(\theta_S \otimes \St_S)\,, \quad \text{by 7.14}\,.
\]

In general, the $G$-conjugacy classes of pairs $(T, \theta)$, with $[\theta] = x$, are in one-to-one correspondence with the $G^{*F}$-conjugacy classes of pairs $(T', \theta')$, with $\theta'$ conjugate to $x'$ (5.24), i.e., with the $S^{*F}$-conjugacy classes of pairs $(T', x')$, ($T' \subset S^*$). The dimension of \eqref{eq:10.7.9} is that of
\[
\sum_{\substack{T' \subset S^*}} \frac{1}{|W_{S^*}(T')^F|} \Ind_{T'^F}^{G^F}(1)
\]
(because $|G^{*F}| = |G^F|$), while that of $(10.7.2) \otimes \St_G$ is
\[
\sum_{\substack{T' \subset S^*}} \frac{1}{|W_{S^*}(T')^F|} \Ind_{T'^F}^{G^F}(1)
\]
and the same proof as above gives \eqref{eq:10.7.7}, \eqref{eq:10.7.8}.

We now show that (10.6) can be applied with $M = \Gamma_G$ or $\Delta_G$. We have
\begin{align*}
\langle \Delta_G, \Delta_G \rangle &= \frac{1}{|G^F|} (|Z^F|\, q^l)^2 \, \#\{\text{regular unipotents in } G^F\} \\
&= \frac{1}{|G^F|} (|Z^F|\, q^l)^2 \frac{|G^F|}{|Z^F|\, q^l} = |Z^F|\, q^l \quad \text{(by 9.20)}\,.
\end{align*}
The analogous identity $\langle \Gamma_G, \Gamma_G \rangle = |Z^F|\, q^l$ follows from R.~Steinberg ([14]).

Now, for any $(T, \theta)$, $\langle \Delta_G, R_T^\theta \rangle$ equals the average value of the character of $R_T^\theta$ on the regular unipotents in $G^F$ hence equals $1$, by (9.16).

We now prove that $\langle \Gamma_G, R_T^\theta \rangle = (-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)}$ for any $(T, \theta)$. Using a result of Rodier (cf.\ T.~A.~Springer, Caract\`eres de groupes de Chevalley finis, S\'em.\ Bourbaki 429, F\'ev.\ 1973) and the induction formula 8.2, we are reduced to the case where $T$ is not contained in any proper $F$-stable parabolic subgroup of $G$. Using \eqref{eq:10.3.1} and the already known fact that $\langle \Delta_G, R_T^\theta \rangle = 1$, we see that it is enough to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:10.7.10}
\langle \Ind_{P(J)^F}^{G^F}(\Gamma_{L(J)}), R_T^\theta \rangle = 0\,, \qquad J \neq I\,.
\end{equation}
(Note that in this case $(-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)} = (-1)^{|I|}$.) Assuming that the theorem is already proved for groups of dimension strictly smaller than that of $G$ and, in particular, for $L(J)$, we see from \eqref{eq:10.7.5} and (8.2) that $\Ind_{P(J)^F}^{G^F}(\Gamma_{L(J)})$ is a linear combination of terms of the form $R_{T'}^{\theta'}$, with $T' \subset P(J)$, $FT' = T'$ so that \eqref{eq:10.7.10} follows from the orthogonality formula (6.8).

Now \eqref{eq:10.7.1}, \eqref{eq:10.7.2}, \eqref{eq:10.7.5}, \eqref{eq:10.7.6} follow directly from (10.6) applied to $M = \Gamma_G$ or $\Delta_G$, except for an indeterminacy of sign. To remove this indeterminacy of sign, we only have to check that the right hand sides of \eqref{eq:10.7.1} and \eqref{eq:10.7.2} have strictly positive dimension; but they are both of the form $|G^F|/|S^{*F}|$ up to a power of $q$, by the first part of the proof. The theorem is proved.

\begin{corollary}[10.8]
For any $\rho \in (G^F)^{\sim}$, the average value of the character of $\rho$ on the regular unipotents in $G^F$ equals $0$, $1$, or $-1$. This value is $\pm 1$ if and only if $\rho = \rho_x'$ for some $x \in \mathfrak{S}$.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, this average value is just $\langle \rho, \Delta_G \rangle$.

\begin{remark}[10.9]
If we assume that $p$ is a good prime for $G$, then all regular unipotents in $G^F$ fall in a single $G^F$-conjugacy class so that the character of $\rho$ at any regular unipotent in $G^F$ equals $0$, $1$, or $-1$. This was first proved in the article by J.~A.~Green, G.~I.~Lehrer, and G.~Lusztig (On the degrees of certain group characters, to appear in The Quarterly Journal of Mathematics).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[10.10]
Let $x \in \mathfrak{S}$. Let $(T, \theta)$ be a maximally split pair in $x$ (see (5.17); $(T, \theta)$ is uniquely defined up to $G^F$-conjugacy by (5.18)). Then the virtual representation $(-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)} R_T^\theta$ of $G^F$ can be represented by an actual $G^F$-module, in which $\rho_x$ and $\rho_x'$ occur with multiplicity $1$.
\end{proposition}

By (5.23), there exists an $F$-stable parabolic subgroup $P \subset G$ such that $T$ is contained in some $F$-stable Levi subgroup $L$ of $P$ and $(T, \theta)$ is non-singular in $L$. $L$ has connected centre, hence, by 5.20, $(T, \theta)$ is non-degenerate in $L$. Let $R_{T,L}^\theta$ be the virtual representation corresponding to $(T, \theta)$ with respect to $L$ (see 1.20). By 7.4,
\[
(-1)^{\sigma(L) - \sigma(T)} R_{T,L}^\theta = (-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)} R_{T,L}^\theta
\]
is irreducible. By 8.2,
\[
(-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)} R_T^\theta = \Ind_{P^F}^{G^F}\bigl((-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)} R_{T,L}^\theta\bigr)\,,
\]
hence it can be realized as an actual $G^F$-module.

It remains to observe that for any $(T, \theta)$ in $x$ (not necessarily maximally split) we have (by \eqref{eq:10.7.1}, \eqref{eq:10.7.2}, and (6.8)):
\begin{align*}
\langle \rho_x, R_T^\theta \rangle &= (-1)^{\sigma(G) - \sigma(T)}\,,\\
\langle \rho_x', R_T^\theta \rangle &= (-1)^{\sigma(G) - \delta_x}\,.\\
\end{align*}

\section{Suzuki and Ree groups}

Our methods apply also to the Suzuki and Ree groups ${}^2B_2(q)$, ${}^2G_2(q)$, ${}^2F_4(q)$ (with $q$ an odd power of $\sqrt{2\vphantom{3}}$, resp.\ $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{2\vphantom{3}}$).

These groups are the fixed point set of an exceptional isogeny $F' \colon G \to G$, with $G$ respectively of type $B_2$, $G_2$ and $F_4$, and $F'^2$ being the Frobenius endomorphism relative to a rational structure over the field with $q^2$ elements (R.~Steinberg [15]). For the sake of induction, one should more generally consider groups of the form $G^{F'}$, with $G$ reductive and $F'^2$ the Frobenius endomorphism relative to a rational structure over $\mathbf{F}_{q^2}$ ($q$ an odd power of $\sqrt{2\vphantom{3}}$ or $\sqrt{3}$). Besides the Suzuki and Ree groups, this includes for instance groups of the form $G = G_1 \times G_1$, with $F'(x, y) = (Fy, x)$; for such a group, $G^{F'} = G_1^F$.

All definitions and results in Chapter 1 can be given with $F$ replaced by $F'$ throughout; in particular, for any $F'$-stable maximal torus $T$ in $G$ and any Borel subgroup $B$ containing $T$, the subscheme $X_{T \subset B}$ of $X_G$ and the $G^{F'}$-equivariant $T^{F'}$-torsor $\tilde{X}_{T \subset B}$ over $X_{T \subset B}$ are well-defined (1.17). Thus for any $\theta \in (T^{F'})^{\sim}$, the virtual representation $R_{T \subset B}^\theta$ of $G^{F'}$ is well-defined (1.20). For $G = G_1 \times G_1$, $F'(x, y) = (Fy, x)$, this coincides with the similar representation of $G_1^F = G^{F'}$.

All the results in Chapters 4, 5, 9 (except (9.18), (9.19)), the disjointness Theorem 6.2 (with its Corollary 6.3), the results (7.13) and (8.2) continue to be true. On the other hand, the proofs of the orthogonality relations (6.8), (6.9), and of the dimension formula (7.1) require the assumption $|T^{F'}| > 1$. If $q > 2$ this is automatically satisfied: $|T^{F'}|$ is of the form $\prod (q - \rho_i)$, where $|\rho_i| = 1$, hence $|T^{F'}| > \prod (q - 1)$. Thus, if we exclude the case where $q$ equals $\sqrt{2\vphantom{3}}$ or $\sqrt{3}$, all the results in Chapters 6, 7, 8, 10 hold, as well as (9.18), (9.19). They can all be deduced from the orthogonality relations (6.8), (6.9). Even in the case $q = \sqrt{2\vphantom{3}}$ or $\sqrt{3}$, we can prove them for ${}^2B_2$ and ${}^2G_2$, but we cannot handle ${}^2F_4(\sqrt{2\vphantom{3}})$.

\textsc{Added in Proof} (2$^{\text{nd}}$ Dec.\ 1975). We can now handle ${}^2F_4(\sqrt{2\vphantom{3}})$, too; this will be considered by one of us, in a future article.
