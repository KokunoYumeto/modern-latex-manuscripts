Nevertheless, as soon as the weights are nonzero, constraints appear on the homotopy type of \(X\) (cf. §10). The following rules help locate the weights.

\NumPara{7.} For a cohomology group \(H\) of a given type, each rule will consist in describing a subset \(\region\) of \(\Z\times\Z\). The weights are controlled by \(\region\) in the following sense: if \(\Gr_n^W(H)\ne0\), then there exists \((p,q)\in\region\) with \(p+q=n\). This manner of speaking, which may seem artificial here, is not so: information other than the possible weights is controlled by the same region \(\region\); cf. §15.

\NumPara{(7.1)} \(H^n(X)\) is controlled by the square \([0,n]\times[0,n]\).

\NumPara{(7.2)} If \(X\) is proper, \(H^n(X)\) is controlled by the part of this square below the second diagonal, namely \(\{(p,q)\in[0,n]\times[0,n]\mid p+q\le n\}\). If \(X\) is smooth, \(H^n(X)\) is controlled by the part of this square above that diagonal, namely \(\{(p,q)\in[0,n]\times[0,n]\mid p+q\ge n\}\).

\NumPara{(7.3)} If \(N=\dim X\le n\), the square \([0,n]\times[0,n]\) may be replaced by the square \([n-N,N]\times[n-N,N]\).

\NumPara{8. Variants.} \textbf{(8.1)} The compactly supported cohomology group \(H_c^n(X)\) is controlled by the part of the square \([0,n]\times[0,n]\) below the second diagonal. If \(N=\dim X\le n\), this square may again be replaced by \([n-N,N]\times[n-N,N]\). In particular, \(H_c^{2N}(X)\) is pure of weight \(2N\).

\NumPara{(8.2)} The “duality” between the proper and smooth cases follows from Poincaré duality: for \(X\) smooth and purely of dimension \(N\),
\[
H^n(X)=\Hom\bigl(H_c^{2N-n}(X),H_c^{2N}(X)\bigr)=\bigl(H_c^{2N-n}(X)^\vee\bigr)(-N).
\]
This argument extends the results given for smooth \(X\) to the case in which \(X\) is a “rational homology manifold.” For such an \(X\), the image of \(H_c^n(X)\) in \(H^n(X)\) is pure of weight \(n\).

\NumPara{(8.3)} If \(X\) is only assumed normal, it remains true that \(H^1(X)\) is controlled by \(\{(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}\).

\NumPara{(*)} There exists a nilpotent Lie algebra of length \(n\), \((\LieH,[\ ,\ ])\), with \(\LieH\) a free

Moreover, the Lie algebra \(\LieL^{(n)}=\LieH\otimes\Q\) depends only on \(\Pi^{(n)}\), not on \(H\).

\TheoremPara{10. THEOREM (MORGAN).} If \(X\) is normal, \(\LieL^{(n)}\) admits a grading \(\Wg\) in degrees \(<0\), such that \(\LieL^{(n)}\) is generated by elements of degrees \(-1\) and \(-2\), subject only to relations of degrees \(-2\), \(-3\), and \(-4\), together with the vanishing of \(n\)-fold iterated commutators.\EndTheorem

It is more convenient to work with the inverse system \(\LieL^{(\infty)}\) of the \(\LieL^{(n)}\). Defining \(H^i(\LieL^{(\infty)})=\varinjlim H^i(\LieL^{(n)})\), one has
\[
H^1(\LieL^{(\infty)})=H^1(X),\qquad H^2(\LieL^{(\infty)})\subset H^2(X).
\]
Moreover, a grading \(\Wg\) of \(\Mmin\) as in §5 defines compatible gradings of \(\LieL^{(\infty)}\), its cohomology, and that of \(X\). By (8.3) (respectively, (7.1)), \(H^1(X)\) has weights 1 and 2 and \(H^2(X)\) has weights \(\le4\). Consequently, \(H^1(\LieL^{(\infty)})\) (the dual of the group \(\LieL^{(\infty)}/[\LieL^{(\infty)},\LieL^{(\infty)}]\) of generators of \(\LieL^{(\infty)}\)) has weights 1 and 2, and \(H^2(\LieL^{(\infty)})\), the dual of the group of relations among generators, has weights \(\le4\). This proves the theorem.

\NumPara{11.} For a proper smooth variety, the weight filtration reduces to very little (3.1). Its gradability in the sense of §4 implies the vanishing of all Massey products. Its gradability in the sense of §5 even implies that the rational homotopy type of \(X\) can be read off from its cohomology algebra---see [3].

\NumPara{12. Hodge theory.} The cohomology group \(H^i(X)\) of every complex algebraic variety is endowed with a mixed Hodge structure \((W,F)\) (\(W\) is a filtration on \(H^i(X)\), and \(F\) is a filtration on \(H^i(X)\otimes\C=H^i(X,\C)\), with \(W\) and \(F\) satisfying suitable axioms---see [1]). The weight filtration is, by definition, the filtration \(W\).

\NumPara{13. \(l\)-adic theory.} An algebraic variety \(X\) is defined by finitely many polynomial equations. The coefficients of these equations generate a finitely generated subring \(R\) of \(\C\), with fraction field \(K\), and \(X\) is obtained by extension of scalars to \(\C\) from a scheme over \(K\), or even over \(R\). The following statements become true after replacing \(R\) by \(R[1/f]\), with \(f\in R\) sufficiently divisible.

\NumPara{(13.1)} Let \(l\) be a prime number. The group \(H^i(X)\otimes\Q_l\) is identified with the \(l\)-adic cohomology group \(H^i(X,\Q_l)\). The latter is defined in a purely algebraic manner and therefore carries, by transport of structure, an action of \(\Aut(\C/K)\).

\NumPara{(13.2)} Let \(\overline K\) be the algebraic closure of \(K\) in \(\C\). The action factors through \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\). It is unramified over \(R[1/l]\), i.e., it factors through the Galois group of the largest extension \(K_{\mathrm{nr}}^{(l)}\subset\overline K\) of \(K\) unramified over \(R[1/l]\).

\NumPara{(13.3)} Let \(\mathfrak m\) be a maximal ideal of \(R[1/l]\), and let \(N(\mathfrak m)=\#R/\mathfrak m\) (\(R/\mathfrak m\) is a finite field). To \(\mathfrak m\) there corresponds a conjugacy class of Frobenius substitutions \(\varphi_{\mathfrak m}\in\Gal(K_{\mathrm{nr}}/K)\), whose inverses are the geometric Frobenius substitutions \(F_{\mathfrak m}=\varphi_{\mathfrak m}^{-1}\).

\TheoremPara{14. THEOREM.} For \(f\) sufficiently divisible, one has\EndTheorem

\TheoremPara{(i)} For every \(l\) and \(\mathfrak m\), the eigenvalues \(\alpha\) of \(F_{\mathfrak m}\) (in a suitable finite extension of \(\Q_l\)) are algebraic integers. For each \(\alpha\), there is an integer \(w(\alpha)\) such that all complex conjugates of \(\alpha\) have absolute value \(N(\mathfrak m)^{w(\alpha)/2}\).

\NumPara{(ii)} Choose a geometric Frobenius \(F_{\mathfrak m}\), and let \({}_{\mathfrak m}W_j\) be the sum of the generalized eigenspaces corresponding to eigenvalues \(\alpha\) of \(F_{\mathfrak m}\) such that \(w(\alpha)=j\). The spectral sequence abuts to a filtration \(W\) on \(H^*(X)\), which is by definition the weight filtration of Hodge theory. This spectral sequence has an \(l\)-adic analogue; the \(l\)-adification \(W_l\) of \(W\) is therefore stable under a Galois group. The Weil conjecture implies that the eigenvalues of \(F_{\mathfrak m}\) on \(\Gr_j^{W_l}\) are algebraic integers whose complex absolute values are \(N(\mathfrak m)^{j/2}\), and the theorem follows.

\NumPara{15.} In the cases considered in (7.1), (7.2), and (7.3), the region \(\region\) described there controls not only the weights, but also the Hodge numbers and the divisibility of the eigenvalues of the \(F_{\mathfrak m}\): in (7.1), (7.2), and (7.3), a region \(\region\) has been assigned to a group \(H\), and

\NumPara{(a)} The nonzero Hodge numbers \(h^{pq}\) of the Hodge structures \(\Gr_j^W(H)\) satisfy \((p,q)\in\region\).

\NumPara{(b)} (Useful only in case (7.3).) If the \((p,q)\in\region\) with \(p+q=j\) satisfy \(p,q\ge k\), then (still for \(f\) sufficiently divisible) the eigenvalues of \(F_{\mathfrak m}\) with complex absolute value \(N(\mathfrak m)^{j/2}\) are divisible by \(N(\mathfrak m)^k\).

One hopes that this is a special case of a general principle; cf. [4].

\NumPara{16.} The following result is clear from the viewpoint of Hodge theory.

\NumPara{(16.1)} For odd \(j\), \(\dim\Gr_j^W H^i(X)\) is even.

On the other hand, up to now I know how to prove §§ 2 and 5 only by \(l\)-adic methods. For §5, the method of §14 yields an \(l\)-adic grading; a device of Sullivan then gives the existence of rational gradings of the desired kind.

The functoriality properties of the weight filtration are evident from the \(l\)-adic viewpoint (because Galois commutes with everything that can be defined). It is nevertheless useful to prove them from the viewpoint of Hodge theory in order to obtain analogous properties for the filtration \(F\). Morgan has obtained numerous results in this direction---enough to prove by Hodge theory a result slightly weaker than §10.

\NumPara{17.} As §5 suggests, a weight filtration also exists on homotopy groups (tensored with \(\Q\)) under simple-connectivity hypotheses.

It also exists on groups of vanishing cycles (cf. [6]), and I hope that these filtrations will help us to understand the latter better.
