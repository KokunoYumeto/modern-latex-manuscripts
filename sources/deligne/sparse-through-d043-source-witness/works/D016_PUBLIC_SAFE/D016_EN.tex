\section*{Introduction}

The essential results of this article are the following.

\paragraph{(A)} We give a simple proof of Langlands' theorem [9], already proved up to sign by Dwork [6], which makes it possible to write the constant in the functional equations of Artin \(L\)-functions, or of Weil \(L\)-functions [19], as a product of local constants.

The theorem to be proved is local, and Langlands' proof had the elegance of being purely local. Essentially, the problem is to prove a multiplicative identity between somewhat generalized Gauss sums whenever one has a \(\Z\)-linear relation between characters of local Galois groups induced by characters of one-dimensional representations of subgroups. Langlands constructed a generating set of such relations, and reduced the identities to be proved to identities between Gauss sums, which he proved using Stickelberger's theorem.

The proof presented here is very close to the proof [5] of the Hasse--Davenport identity (cf. also Weil [18]). It uses a global argument (4.12), and the fact that the local constants to be defined are essentially trivial at infinity, in the unramified case, and in certain very ramified cases (4.14).

\paragraph{(B)} Let \(K\) be a function field and
\[
\rho:\Gal(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow \GL(n,\Q_\ell)
\]
an \(\ell\)-adic representation of the Galois group of \(K\), unramified almost everywhere. Grothendieck showed that the Euler product over the places of \(K\), with \(p^{-s}\) replaced by an indeterminate \(t\),
\[
Z(\rho,t)=\prod_v\det\bigl(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},(\Q_\ell^n)^{\rho(I_v)}\bigr)^{-1}
\in\Q_\ell[[t]],
\]
is a rational function, and that a functional equation relates \(Z(\rho,t)\) to
\[
Z^\vee(\rho,t):
\qquad
Z^\vee(\rho,p^{-1}t^{-1})=\varepsilon\, Z(\rho,t),
\]
where the constant \(\varepsilon\) is a monomial \(at^b\). This theory is summarized in §10. Result (A) suggests a formula expressing \(\varepsilon\) as a product of local constants. We prove that this formula is true when \(\rho\) belongs to an infinite compatible system of \(\ell\)-adic representations (9.3 and 9.9). According to Weil [16] and Jacquet--Langlands [8], this formula, applied to the compatible system of \(\ell\)-adic representations defined by an elliptic curve over \(K\), makes it possible to attach to every elliptic curve over \(K\) a modular form for \(\GL(2,K)\).

It would be very interesting to know whether the formula obtained remains valid for an arbitrary \(\ell\)-adic representation.

Here is the content of the various sections, and their interdependence.

The beginning of §1 gathers a few results on induced representations of finite groups which will be useful to us.

The end of §1, from 1.11 to the end, is not used in the rest of the article. For a solvable group \(G\), it describes a set \(R\) of \(\Z\)-linear relations between the characters of \(G\) induced by one-dimensional characters of subgroups, and proves that every such relation is a linear combination of elements of \(R\). The result and its proof are implicitly contained in [9].

Sections 2 and 3 serve mainly to fix notation. In §2 we recall the structure of the Galois group of a local field [10], define the Weil groups, a variant of Galois groups, and give a few statements from class field theory. The reader should note that throughout this article the isomorphism of local class field theory sends a uniformizer to the inverse of the Frobenius substitution; the reason is given in 3.6. Section 3 recalls Tate's thesis and Artin [1].

Section 4 contains the proof of result (A) above. The formula sheet in §5 gives a list of fundamental properties of the local constants constructed in §4, and a few identities between Gauss sums that follow from them.

Sections 6, 7, and 8, the latter depending only on §2, are preliminaries to the proof of (B), given in §9. In the statements of §§3 and 4, we were very careful to use only rational formulas, involving neither passage to a limit nor square roots, even of a positive integer. We harvest the benefit of this in §§6 and 7. In §6, for \(K\) a non-archimedean local field and \(V\) a modular representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\) over a field \(A\) of characteristic \(\ell\ne p\), we define, by transport of structure for \(\ell=0\) and by reduction modulo \(\ell\) for \(\ell\ne0\), a local constant
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\in A^*.
\]
In §7, for \(K\) global of characteristic \(p\) and \(V\) a modular representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\), we prove, by reduction modulo \(\ell\), a functional equation for the corresponding \(L\)-function \(L\in\Lambda(t)\).

In §8 we give the isomorphism classes of \(\ell\)-adic representations of the Weil group of a local field a purely algebraic description, independent of the topology of \(\Q_\ell\). This is used to define compatibility of \(\ell\)-adic and \(\ell'\)-adic representations. The notion introduced is finer than Serre's [12].

Section 9 gives the proof of (B) (9.3 and 9.9). The idea is that, to prove an identity, it is enough to prove it modulo \(\ell\) for infinitely many \(\ell\). We also give, for function fields, a partial answer to a question of Serre by showing (9.8) that two \(\ell\)- and \(\ell'\)-adic representations giving the same characteristic polynomial of Frobenius, assumed to lie in \(\Q[t]\), at almost all places satisfy compatibility at the residual places.

Section 10 summarizes Grothendieck's theory of \(L\)-functions (cf. [7] and SGA 5), which is used crucially in §9. In 10.11 I try to explain the point of his method. The odd results 10.13 follow immediately from what precedes. I do not know their meaning.

\section*{\S1. Virtual representations of a finite group}

\paragraph{1.1.} Let \(K\) be a commutative field. The most important case for us will be that in which \(K\) is algebraically closed of characteristic \(0\), for example \(K=\C\). Let \(G\) be a group. We shall write \(R_K(G)\) for the Grothendieck group of the category of finite-dimensional \(K[G]\)-modules over \(K\). It is identified with the free \(\Z\)-module with basis the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible \(K\)-linear representations of \(G\). It is a commutative ring with unit, for tensor product over \(K\), and even a \(\lambda\)-ring in the sense of Grothendieck. Its elements are the virtual representations of \(G\) over \(K\).

To define a homomorphism \(f\) from \(R_K(G)\) to a commutative group \(X\), it is enough:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item to define \(f(V)\) for \(V\) a representation of \(G\) over \(K\);
\item to check that for every exact sequence of representations
\[
0\longrightarrow V'\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow V''\longrightarrow0,
\]
one has \(f(V)=f(V')+f(V'')\).
\end{enumerate}
Applying this rule, one defines the dimension of a virtual representation
\[
\dim:R_K(G)\longrightarrow\Z,
\]
and its determinant
\[
\det:R_K(G)\longrightarrow\Hom(G,K^*).
\]

If \(H\) is a subgroup of finite index in \(G\), define induction
\[
\Ind_H^G:R_K(H)\longrightarrow R_K(G)
\]
as corresponding to extension of scalars
\[
V\longmapsto K[G]\otimes_{K[H]}V.
\]
This is an exact functor, and therefore passes to virtual representations. For \(f:G\to H\), define restriction
\[
\Res_G^H:R_K(H)\longrightarrow R_K(G)
\]
as restriction of scalars from \(K[H]\) to \(K[G]\). For \(f\) surjective one sometimes speaks rather of inflation.

For every group \(G\), write \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\) for the largest abelian quotient of \(G\). A \(K\)-quasi-character \(\chi\) of \(G\) is a homomorphism
\[
\chi:G\longrightarrow K^*;
\]
we identify it with the \(K\)-quasi-character of \(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\) through which it factors, and denote by \([\chi]\) the corresponding one-dimensional representation. For \(G\) finite, we shall use the words character and quasi-character interchangeably. The word character will never mean the character of a representation of dimension \(>1\).

The following proposition is due to P. Gallagher [20].

\paragraph{Proposition 1.2.} Let \(G\) be a group, \(H\) a subgroup of finite index, and
\[
t:G^{\mathrm{ab}}\longrightarrow H^{\mathrm{ab}}
\]
the transfer. For \(\rho\) a virtual representation of dimension \(0\) of \(H\), and \(x\in G^{\mathrm{ab}}\), one has
\[
\det(\Ind_H^G(\rho))(x)=\det(\rho)(t(x)).
\]
We shall prove, in only slightly greater generality, that if \(\varepsilon\) is the determinant of the permutation representation of \(G\) on \(G/H\),
\[
\varepsilon:G\longrightarrow \mathfrak S_{G/H}\longrightarrow \{\pm1\},
\]
then, for \(\rho\) of arbitrary dimension,
\[
\det(\Ind_H^G(\rho))(x)=\varepsilon^{\dim\rho}\det(\rho)(t(x)).
\]

This is fairly evident in topological terms. Let \(BG\) be the classifying space of \(G\), and let \(B_H\) be that of \(H\), regarded as a covering of \(B_G\). Representations of \(G\) are identified with local systems of finite-dimensional \(K\)-vector spaces on \(B_G\), and \(\Ind_H^G\) with the functor \(f_*\). The transfer \(H^1(B_G,X)\to H^1(B_H,X)\), for \(X=K^*\), corresponds to the norm \(f_!\). If \(\rho\in H^1(B_G,K^*)\) is the class of a rank-one local system of \(K\)-vector spaces, then \(f_!(\rho)\) is the class of \(N(L)\), the local system on \(B_G\) which, locally on \(B_G\), is the tensor product of the inverse images of \(L\) by the \([G:H]\) disjoint sections.

Put
\[
\det V=\bigwedge^{\dim V}V
\]
for a local system \(V\), and let \(\varepsilon\) be the local system \(\det f_*K\). Proposition 1.2 follows from the existence of a canonical isomorphism, for \(V\) a local system on \(B_H\),
\[
\det f_*V\simeq \varepsilon^{\otimes\dim V}\otimes N(\det V).
\]
The construction of this isomorphism is local on \(B_G\), and reduces to the following construction.

\paragraph{Construction 1.3.} For \((V_i)_{i\in I}\) a finite family of \(K\)-vector spaces of dimension \(d\), and \(\varepsilon=\det(K^I)\), one has canonically
\[
\det\Bigl(\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_i\Bigr)\simeq
\varepsilon^{\otimes d}\otimes\bigotimes_{i\in I}\det(V_i).
\]
For \(i_1,\ldots,i_N\) the sequence of elements of \(I\), the map is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(e_{i_1,1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_1,d})\wedge\cdots\wedge
(e_{i_N,1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_N,d}) \\
&\quad\longmapsto
(i_1\wedge\cdots\wedge i_N)^{\otimes d}\otimes
(e_{i_1,1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_1,d})\otimes\cdots\otimes
(e_{i_N,1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_N,d}).
\end{aligned}
\]

\paragraph{Parenthesis 1.4.} Instead of a classifying space \(BG\), one could more intrinsically have used, in the preceding argument, Grothendieck's classifying topos \(B_G\) (SGA 4, IV, 2.4, p. 315, for \(E=(\mathrm{Ens})\)).

Assume \(G\) finite. We shall say that \(K\) is large enough if it contains all roots of unity whose orders divide the l.c.m. of the orders of elements of \(G\). A group \(H\) will be called \(p\)-elementary if it is the product of a \(p\)-group and a cyclic group; elementary if it is \(p\)-elementary for some prime \(p\). We shall need the following variant of Brauer's theorem on induced characters, obtained by omitting everywhere ``of dimension zero.''

\paragraph{Proposition 1.5.} If \(K\) is large enough, every virtual representation of dimension \(0\) of \(G\) is a sum of virtual representations
\[
\Ind_H^G(\chi),
\]
where \(\chi\) is virtual of dimension \(0\), \(H\) is elementary, and \(\chi\) is a linear combination of one-dimensional representations of \(H\).

By Brauer's theorem applied to the trivial representation \(1_G\) of \(G\), there is a decomposition
\[
1_G=\sum_H \Ind_H^G(z_H),
\qquad H\text{ elementary}.
\]
For \(y\) a virtual representation of dimension \(0\) of \(G\), one then has
\[
y=\sum_H y\cdot \Ind_H^G(z_H)
  =\sum_H\Ind_H^G(\Res_H^G(y)\cdot z_H),
\]
with \(\dim(\Res_H^G(y)\cdot z_H)=0\). This reduces us to the case where \(G\) is elementary.

By linearity, one may suppose that \(y\) is of the form
\[
\rho-\dim(\rho)\,1_G,
\]
with \(\rho\) irreducible, hence, since \(G\) is nilpotent, induced by a character \(\chi\) of a subgroup \(H\) of \(G\) containing the center \(Z\). Then
\[
\rho-\dim(\rho)\,1_G
=
\Ind_H^G(\chi-1_H)+\bigl(\Ind_H^G(1_H)-[G:H]1_G\bigr).
\]
The first term is of the desired type, and the second is the inflation of a virtual representation of dimension \(0\) of \(G/Z\). One concludes by induction on the order of \(G\), noting that \(|G/Z|<|G|\) if \(G\ne\{e\}\).

\paragraph{Scholium 1.6.} A homomorphism
\[
f:R_K(G)\longrightarrow X
\]
is known once its value on \(1_G\) and on the
\[
\Ind_H^G(\chi-1_H),
\]
for \(H\) elementary and \(\chi\) a character of \(H\), are known.

\paragraph{1.7.} Let \(A\) be a discrete valuation ring of unequal characteristic \(p\), with fraction field \(K\) and residue field \(k=A/\mathfrak m\). Recall the definition of the decomposition homomorphism ([11], III, 2.2)
\[
d:R_K(G)\longrightarrow R_k(G):
\]
for \(V\) a representation of \(G\) over \(K\), and \(E\) a \(G\)-invariant lattice,
\[
d([V])=[E\otimes_Ak].
\]




\paragraph{Proposition 1.8.} Assume that \(K\) is sufficiently large. The kernel of the decomposition homomorphism \(d\) is then generated by the elements
\[
\Ind_H^G(\chi')-\Ind_H^G(\chi'')
\]
where \(H\) is an elementary subgroup and \(\chi',\chi''\in\Hom(H,K^*)\) are congruent modulo \(\mathfrak m\).

Since the ring homomorphism \(d\) commutes with induction, the argument of 1.5 reduces us to the case where \(G\) is elementary, hence the product of a \(p\)-group \(P\) and a group \(H\) of order prime to \(p\). Since \(K\) and \(k\) are sufficiently large, one has
\[
R(G)=R(P)\otimes R(H),
\]
and similarly over \(k\). For a group of order prime to \(p\), \(d\) is bijective. Applying Brauer's theorem to \(H\), it is enough to prove 1.8 for a \(p\)-group. This case is trivial, since every character \(\chi\in\Hom(P,K^*)\) of a \(p\)-group is congruent to \(1\) modulo \(\mathfrak m\).

\paragraph{1.9.} For the rest of this subsection, assume that \(K=\C\), consider only finite groups, and abbreviate \(R_\C(G)\) to \(R(G)\). For \(G\) commutative, write \(G^*\) for the group of characters of \(G\).

Let \(R^+(G)\) be the free \(\Z\)-module with basis the set of \(G\)-conjugacy classes of pairs \((H,\chi)\), where \(H\) is a subgroup of \(G\) and \(\chi\) a character of \(H\). Define a multiplication in \(R^+(G)\) by
\[
\tag{1.9.1}
(A,\alpha)\,(B,\beta)=
\sum_{(x,y)\in G\backslash(G/A\times G/B)}
\bigl(A^x\cap B^y,\,
(\alpha^x|_{A^x\cap B^y})(\beta^y|_{A^x\cap B^y})\bigr),
\]
where \((x,y)\) runs through representatives of the orbits of \(G\) in \(G/A\times G/B\), \(A^x=xAx^{-1}\), and \(\alpha^x\) is the character \(a\mapsto \alpha(x^{-1}ax)\) of \(A^x\). With this multiplication, \(R^+(G)\) is a commutative ring with unit \((G,1)\), and one defines a ring homomorphism
\[
\tag{1.9.2}
b:R^+(G)\longrightarrow R(G),\qquad (H,\chi)\longmapsto \Ind_H^G(\chi).
\]
This homomorphism is surjective by Brauer's theorem. For \(\chi\) a character of \(G\), multiplication by \((G,\chi)\) is denoted \(\cdot\chi\) and is called twisting by \(\chi\):
\[
\tag{1.9.3}
(H,\varphi)\cdot\chi=(H,\varphi\cdot\chi|_H).
\]
For \(G'\subset G\), define induction
\[
\tag{1.9.4}
\Ind_{G'}^G:R^+(G')\longrightarrow R^+(G),\qquad (H,\chi)\longmapsto(H,\chi).
\]
For a morphism \(u:G\to G'\), define restriction by
\[
\tag{1.9.5}
\Res_u(H,\chi)=
\sum_{s\in u(G)\backslash G'/H}
\bigl(u^{-1}(sHs^{-1}),\,\chi^s\circ u\bigr),
\]
where \(s\) runs over a set of representatives of the double classes. There are commutative diagrams
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.0em,row sep=2.0em]
R^+(G') \arrow[r,"\Ind"] \arrow[d,"b"'] & R^+(G) \arrow[d,"b"]\\
R(G') \arrow[r,"\Ind"'] & R(G)
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.0em,row sep=2.0em]
R^+(G') \arrow[r,"\Res"] \arrow[d,"b"'] & R^+(G) \arrow[d,"b"]\\
R(G') \arrow[r,"\Res"'] & R(G),
\end{tikzcd}
\]
for the second, see [11], p. 75. For \(R\in R^+(G)\) one has
\[
\tag{1.9.6}
R\cdot(H,\chi)=\Ind_H^G\bigl(\Res_H^G(R)\cdot\chi\bigr).
\]
When \(u\) is surjective, one speaks of inflation.

\paragraph{Notation 1.9.8.} For every additive function of representations \(F\), write the same symbol for the function it defines on \(R(G)\), and for \(F\circ b\). Thus
\[
\dim(v)=\dim(b(v)),\quad\ldots
\]

\paragraph{Variants 1.10.} (i) Let \(R^+_0(G)\) be the subgroup of \(R^+(G)\) generated by the \((H,\chi)-(H,1)\). As \((H,\chi)\) runs through the canonical basis of \(R^+(G)\), except for the \((H,1)\), these elements form a basis of \(R^+_0(G)\). One checks that \(R^+_0(G)\) is an ideal of \(R^+(G)\). Let \(R^0(G)\subset R(G)\) be the set of virtual representations of dimension \(0\) of \(G\). By 1.5, the natural map, induced by (1.9.2), from \(R^+_0(G)\) to \(R^0(G)\) is surjective.

(ii) For a topological group \(G\), finite or not, \(R^+(G)\) and \(R^+_0(G)\) denote the analogous groups obtained by restricting to closed subgroups \(H\) of finite index and continuous quasi-characters. The functorialities of 1.9 generalize mutatis mutandis.

The rest of this subsection will not be used below. We give results drawn from Langlands [9].

\paragraph{Lemma 1.11.} Suppose that \(G=H\cdot C\), where \(C\) is commutative and normal, and let \(\chi\) be a character of \(H\). For every \(\mu\in C^*\), let \(G_\mu\subset G\) be the stabilizer of \(\mu\) under conjugation. If
\[
\chi|_{H\cap C}=\mu|_{H\cap C},
\]
write \(\{\chi,\mu\}\) for the character of \(G_\mu=(H\cap G_\mu)\cdot C\) which extends \(\chi|_{H\cap G_\mu}\) and \(\mu\). Then
\[
\Ind_H^G(\chi)=
\sum_{\substack{\mu\in C^*/G\\ \chi|_{H\cap C}=\mu|_{H\cap C}}}
\Ind_{G_\mu}^G\{\chi,\mu\}.
\]
The verification is left to the reader.

\paragraph{1.12.} An element \(R\) of \(\Ker(R^+(G)\to R(G))\) will be said to be of type I, II, or III if there exists a quotient \(A\) of a subgroup \(B\) of \(G\) such that \(R\) is obtained from an element \(S\) of one of the following respective types in \(\Ker(R^+(A)\to R(A))\), by inflation from \(A\) to \(B\), twisting by a character of \(B\), and induction from \(B\) to \(G\). Let \(\ell\) be a prime number.

\emph{I.} \(A\simeq\Z/\ell\Z\), and
\[
S=(e,1)-\sum_{\chi\in A^*}(A,\chi).
\]

\emph{II.} \(A\) is a central extension of \((\Z/\ell\Z)^2\) by an abelian group \(Z\). Let \(H_i\), \(i=1,2\), be the inverse images in \(A\) of the first and second factors \(\Z/\ell\Z\), and let \(\chi_i\) be a character of \(H_i\). Assume that \(\chi_1|_Z=\chi_2|_Z\), and that this character of \(Z\) is nontrivial on a commutator.
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.2em,row sep=2.7em]
Z \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d,hook] & H_1 \arrow[d,hook]\\
H_2 \arrow[r,hook] & A
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The relation \(S\) is
\[
S=(H_1,\chi_1)-(H_2,\chi_2).
\]

\emph{III.} \(A\) is a semidirect product \(H\cdot C\), \(\chi\) is a character of \(H\), and the normal subgroup \(C\) is minimal among the nontrivial commutative normal subgroups of \(A\). For every character \(\mu\) of \(C\), \(A_\mu\) is the stabilizer of \(\mu\), and \(\{\chi,\mu\}\) is the character of \(A_\mu\) extending \(\chi\) and \(\mu\). The relation \(S\) expresses
\[
\Ind_H^A(\chi)=
\sum_{\mu\in C^*/A}
\Ind_{A_\mu}^A(\{\chi,\mu\}).
\]
where \(\mu\) runs over a set of representatives for the orbits of \(A\) in \(C^*\).

These relations are special cases of 1.11: for I, \(H=\{e\}\) and \(C=A\); for II, \(H=H_1\) and \(C=H_2\); for III, \(H\) and \(C\) are as indicated.

Every relation obtained by induction, inflation, or twisting from a relation of type I, respectively II or III, is again of the same type.

\paragraph{Theorem 1.13 (Langlands [9], §18).} If \(G\) is nilpotent, the kernel of
\[
b:R^+(G)\longrightarrow R(G)
\]
is generated, as a \(\Z\)-module, by the relations of type I and II.

\paragraph{Lemma 1.13.1.} If \(G\) is commutative, \(\Ker(b)\) is generated by the relations of type I.

One has to show that, for every subgroup \(H\) of \(G\) and every \(\chi\in H^*\), the relation
\[
(H,\chi)-\sum_{\psi\in (G/H)^*}(G,\tilde\chi\psi)
\]
is a consequence of relations of type I. These are the following: for \(H'\subset H''\subset G\), with \([H'':H']\) prime, and \(\chi\) a character of \(H'\), always induced by a character of \(G\),
\[
\Ind_{H'}^G(\chi)-\sum_{\chi''|_{H'}=\chi}\Ind_{H''}^G(\chi'')=0.
\]
The general relation follows by taking a Jordan--Hölder series of \(G/H\).

\paragraph{Lemma 1.13.2.} Let \(C\) be a commutative normal subgroup of \(G\), let \(T\) be a set of representatives of \(C^*/G\), let \(\mu\in T\), and let \(G_\mu\) be the stabilizer of \(\mu\). Let \((H_i)\) be a family of subgroups of \(G\) containing \(C\), and let \(\chi_i\) be a character of \(H_i\), with \(\chi_i|_C\in T\). If the relation
\[
\sum_i n_i\,\Ind_{H_i}^G(\chi_i)=0
\]
holds in \(R(G)\), then
\[
\sum_{\chi_i|_C=\mu}
n_i\,\Ind_{H_i}^{G_\mu}(\chi_i)=0
\]
holds in \(R(G_\mu)\). The verification is left to the reader.

Let \(Z\) be the center of \(G\), and prove 1.13 by induction on the order of \(G/Z\). By 1.13.1, we may suppose \(G\) noncommutative. Let \(C\) be a commutative normal subgroup containing \(Z\), with \([C:Z]=\ell\) prime, and with central image in \(G/Z\).

Consider a relation
\[
\tag{1}
\sum_i n_i\,\Ind_{H_i}^G(\chi_i)=0.
\]
It is a consequence of relations induced by
\[
\sum_{\chi_i|_{H_i\cap Z}=\chi}\Ind_{H_i}^G(\chi_i)=\Ind_{H_iZ}^G(\chi)
\]
and of a relation (2) analogous to (1), with \(H_i\supset Z\). The displayed relations are linear combinations of relations of type I; this follows from 1.13.1 applied to \(H_iZ/\Ker(\chi_i)\).

The relation (2) is a consequence of relations induced from 1.11 for \(HC,H,C,\chi\), with \(H\supset Z\),
\[
\tag{b}
\Ind_H^{HC}(\chi)=
\sum_{\substack{\mu\in C^*/H\\ \chi|_{H\cap C}=\mu|_{H\cap C}}}
\Ind_{G_\mu}^{HC}\{\chi,\mu\},
\]
and of a relation (3) analogous to (1), this time with \(H_i\supset C\). Apply 1.13.2 to (3), to express it as a sum of relations induced by relations
\[
\sum_i n_i\,\Ind_{H_i}^{G_\mu}(\chi_i)=0.
\]
The image of \(C\) in \(G/\Ker(\mu)\) is central, and the induction hypothesis therefore applies to \(G_\mu\); the resulting relations are of the desired type.

It remains to prove that the relations (b) are of the desired type. If \(HC\ne G\), this follows from the induction hypothesis applied to \(HC\). If \(HC=G\), then \(H\) is normal, because it contains \(Z\) and \(C\) is central modulo \(Z\). Let \(A\) be the intersection of the kernels of the conjugates of \(\chi\). The relation (b) is the inflation of an analogous relation for \(G/A\). If the induction hypothesis applies to \(G/A\), we are done. Otherwise we are in the same situation as before, with \(A=\{e\}\), and we conclude by the following lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma 1.13.3.} With the preceding notation, if \(A=\{e\}\), the relation (b) is of type II.

The group \(H\) is commutative, since characters separate its elements. The group \(Z\) is cyclic, since one character separates its elements. For \(c\in C\) and \(h\in H\), put
\[
u(c,h)=chc^{-1}h^{-1}.
\]
This is an element of \(Z\), since \(C\) is central modulo \(Z\). It depends biadditively on \(c\) and \(h\), and defines
\[
u:C/Z\otimes H/Z\longrightarrow Z.
\]
Let \(c\) generate \(C/Z\). Since \(Z\) is the center and \(G\ne Z\), the map \(u(c,\ ):H/Z\to Z\) is injective, with nontrivial cyclic image killed by \(\ell\). Hence \(|C/Z|=|H/Z|=\ell\), and \(G\) is a central extension of \(C/Z\times H/Z\) by \(Z\). The character \(\chi\) is distinct from at least one of its conjugates, otherwise \(H\) would be central; it is therefore nontrivial on a commutator, and we are in situation II.

\paragraph{Theorem 1.14 (Langlands [9]).} If \(G\) is solvable, the kernel of
\[
b:R^+(G)\longrightarrow R(G)
\]
is generated by the relations of type I, II, and III.

\paragraph{Lemma 1.14.1.} If \(G\) is solvable, the \(\Z\)-submodule \(N(G)\) of \(R^+(G)\) generated by the relations of type I, II, and III is an ideal.

We prove 1.14 and 1.14.1 by simultaneous induction on the order of \(G\).

(A) Theorem 1.14 for the proper subgroups of \(G\) implies 1.14.1 for \(G\). Let \(R\in R^+(G)\) be of type I, II, or III, let \(H\) be a subgroup, and let \(\chi\) be a character of \(H\). We prove that
\[
R\cdot(H,\chi)\in N(G).
\]
If \(H=G\), this follows from the stability of \(N(G)\) under twisting by a character of \(G\). If \(H\ne G\), then by (1.9.6)
\[
R\cdot(H,\chi)=\Ind_H^G(\Res_H^G(R)\cdot\chi).
\]
By the hypothesis, \(\Res_H^G(R)\in N(H)\), and twisting followed by induction gives an element of \(N(G)\).

(B) Lemma 1.14.1 for \(G\), together with Theorem 1.14 for groups of smaller order, implies 1.14 for \(G\). Let \(Z\) be the center of \(G\). By 1.13, we may and shall suppose that \(G\) is not nilpotent. There are two cases.

(B1) \(Z\ne\{e\}\). By Brauer's theorem in \(G/Z\), there exist nilpotent subgroups \(H_i\supset Z\) of \(G\), and characters \(\chi_i\) of \(H_i/Z\), such that
\[
\sum_i n_i\,\Ind_{H_i/Z}^{G/Z}(\chi_i)=1_{G/Z}.
\]
Since this relation lifts to a relation \(S\) in \(R^+(G)\), and since the induction hypothesis applies to \(G/Z\), one has \(S-(G,1)\in N(G)\). For \(R\in\Ker(b)\), one has \(R\cdot S\in N(G)\) by 1.14.1, while (1.9.6) reduces the terms to restrictions to the \(H_i\), where the induction hypothesis applies. This proves the assertion.

(B2) \(Z=\{e\}\). Let \(C\) be a nontrivial minimal commutative normal subgroup and, for \(\mu\in C^*\), let \(G_\mu\) be its stabilizer. Proceed as in the proof of 1.13. One finds that every relation \(R\in\Ker(b)\) is a linear combination of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item relations induced from 1.11 for \(HC,H,C,\chi\), with \(H\subset G\), \(\chi\) a character of \(H\), and \(H\not\supset C\);
\item relations induced from identities in \(G_\mu\).
\end{enumerate}
Since \(Z=\{e\}\), one has \(|G_\mu/\Ker(\mu)|<|G|\), and the induction hypothesis applies to the latter relations. Consider a relation of type (a). If \(HC\ne G\), apply the induction hypothesis. If \(HC=G\), then \(H\cap C\) is normal, since it is normal in both \(H\) and \(C\). By minimality of \(C\), \(H\cap C=\{e\}\), and the relation is of type III.

\Ker(R^+_0(G)\longrightarrow R^0(G))
\]
for suitable groups \(G\). I have not had the courage to try.

\section*{2. Weil groups}

\paragraph{2.1. Roots of unity.} Let \(K\) be a separably closed field of characteristic exponent \(p\). For every integer \(n\), write \(\mu_n(1)(K)\), or simply \(\mu_n(1)\), for the group of \(n\)-th roots of unity in \(K\). For \(n\mid m\), there is a transition map
\[
\mu_m(1)\longrightarrow\mu_n(1),\qquad x\longmapsto x^{m/n};
\]
put
\[
\widehat\Z(1)=\varprojlim_n \mu_n(1).
\]
For a prime number \(\ell\), similarly put
\[
\Z_\ell(1)=\varprojlim_r\mu_{\ell^r}(1).
\]
The evident maps \(\widehat\Z(1)\to\Z_\ell(1)\) induce an isomorphism
\[
\widehat\Z(1)\iso\prod_{\ell\ne p}\Z_\ell(1).
\]
For every \(\widehat\Z\)-module \(V\), put \(V(1)=V\otimes_{\widehat\Z}\widehat\Z(1)\). For a \(\Z_\ell\)-module \(V\), one has
\[
V(1)\simeq V\otimes_{\Z_\ell}\Z_\ell(1).
\]
For \(K=\C\), the group \(\mu_n(1)\) is canonically isomorphic to the group of roots of unity in \(\C\). For \(x\in\mu_n(1)\) and \(\xi\in\Z/n\Z\), the image of \(\xi\otimes x\) in \(\Q/\Z(1)\) is \(\xi x\), giving the usual identifications.

For \(\ell\ne p\), \(\Z_\ell(1)\) is a free rank-one \(\Z_\ell\)-module. For \(i\in\Z\), write \(\Z_\ell(i)\) for its \(i\)-th tensor power, and for \(i<0\) for the dual of \(\Z_\ell(-i)\). For every \(\Z_\ell\)-module \(V\), put again \(V(i)=V\otimes_{\Z_\ell}\Z_\ell(i)\).

\paragraph{2.2. Notation.} Let \(K\) be a local field. We write \(\|\ \|\) for the normalized absolute value of \(K\). If \(dx\) is a Haar measure, then
\[
\int f(ax)\,dx=\|a\|^{-1}\int f(x)\,dx,
\]
i.e. \(d(ax)=\|a\|\,dx\).




For \(s\in\C\), write \(\omega_s\) for the quasi-character
\[
x\longmapsto \|x\|^s
\]
from \(K^*\) to \(\C^*\).

If \(K\) is nonarchimedean, i.e. \(K\ne\R,\C\), write \(\mathcal O\) for the valuation ring of the valuation \(v\) of \(K\), \(\pi\) for a uniformizer, \(k\) for the residue field \(\mathcal O/(\pi)\), \(p\) for the characteristic of \(k\), and put
\[
d=[k:\F_p],\qquad q=p^d=\#k.
\]
Thus
\[
\|x\|=q^{-v(x)}.
\]

\paragraph{2.2.2.} Suppose \(K\) is nonarchimedean. Let \(\overline K\) be a separable closure of \(K\), write \(\overline{\mathcal O}\) for the integral closure of \(\mathcal O\) in \(\overline K\), let \(\overline k\) be the residue field of \(\overline{\mathcal O}\), an algebraic closure of \(k\), and let \(v\) be the valuation of \(\overline K\), with values in \(\Q\), extending that of \(K\).

There is a three-step filtration of the Galois group
\[
\Gal(\overline K/K)\supset I\supset P
\]
whose successive quotients are
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\Gal(\overline K/K)/I&\iso&\Gal(\overline k/k)\iso\widehat\Z,\\[0.35em]
I/P&\iso&\widehat\Z(1)(\overline k),\\[0.35em]
P&:&\text{a pro-\(p\)-group.}
\end{array}
\]
It is obtained as follows; for proofs we refer to [10].

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item By transport of structure, \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) acts on \(\overline{\mathcal O}\) and on \(\overline k\). The inertia group \(I\) is the kernel of the reduction map
\[
v':\Gal(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow \Gal(\overline k/k).
\]
The quotient \(\Gal(\overline k/k)\) is canonically isomorphic to \(\widehat\Z\), with topological generator the Frobenius substitution
\[
\varphi:x\longmapsto x^q.
\]

\item The wild inertia group \(P\) is the unique pro-\(p\)-Sylow subgroup of \(I\). The equivariant morphism
\[
t:I\longrightarrow \widehat\Z(1)(\overline k),
\]
with kernel \(P\), is characterized by the congruence, modulo the maximal ideal of \(\overline{\mathcal O}\),
\[
\frac{\sigma(x)}{x}=t(\sigma)\,v(x)
\qquad(\sigma\in I,\ x\in\overline K).
\]
For a prime \(\ell\ne p\), write \(t_\ell\) for the composite
\[
I\longrightarrow\widehat\Z(1)(\overline k)\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(1)(\overline k).
\]
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2.2.3.} Let \(k\) be a finite field with \(q\) elements, and let \(\overline k\) be its algebraic closure. Let \(W(\overline k/k)\) be the subgroup of \(\Gal(\overline k/k)\simeq\widehat\Z\) generated by
\[
\varphi:x\longmapsto x^q.
\]
There is a canonical isomorphism
\[
W(\overline k/k)\simeq\Z
\]
with generator \(\varphi\). The geometric Frobenius \(F\in W(\overline k/k)\) is, by definition, \(\varphi^{-1}\).

\paragraph{2.2.4.} Let \(K,\overline K\) be as in 2.2.2. The Weil group \(W(\overline K/K)\), or absolute Weil group of \(K\), is the subgroup of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) consisting of the \(\sigma\) such that \(v'(\sigma)\) is an integral power of Frobenius \(\varphi\). It is endowed with the topology inducing the natural topology on \(I\), and for which \(I\) is open. The group \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) is the completion of \(W(\overline K/K)\) for the topology of open subgroups of finite index; if \(L\) is a finite extension of \(K\) in \(\overline K\), the corresponding subgroup of \(W(\overline K/K)\) is identified with \(W(\overline K/L)\). We shall sometimes call geometric Frobenius elements those elements of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) or \(W(\overline K/K)\) whose image is \(F\).

\paragraph{2.2.5.} Suppose \(K\) is archimedean, with algebraic closure \(\overline K\). Define \(W(\overline K/K)\) as the following extension of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) by \(\overline K^*\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item \(K\simeq\R\): \(W(\overline K/K)\) is generated by \(\overline K^*\) and an element \(F\), with
\[
F^2=-1,\qquad FzF^{-1}=\overline z\quad(z\in\overline K^*).
\]
\item \(K\simeq\C\): \(W(\overline K/K)=\overline K^*\).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2.3.} Local class field theory provides an isomorphism between \(W(\overline K/K)^{\ab}\) and \(K^*\).

Assume \(K\) nonarchimedean. For a reason explained in 3.6, we normalize this isomorphism, i.e. choose it or its inverse, so that geometric Frobenius elements correspond to uniformizers:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.7em,row sep=1.9em]
P \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d] & I \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[d] &
W(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
W(\overline k/k)\simeq\Z \arrow[d,"{-1}"]\\
1+(\pi) \arrow[r,hook] & \mathcal O^* \arrow[r,hook] &
K^* \arrow[r,"v"] & \Z .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
A representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\) is unramified, respectively tamely ramified, if it is trivial on \(I\), respectively on \(P\). Similarly, a quasi-character \(\chi\) of \(K^*\) is unramified, respectively tamely ramified, if it is trivial on \(\mathcal O^*\), respectively on \(1+(\pi)\). For \(\ell\) prime to \(p\), the action of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on \(\Z_\ell(1)\) is unramified, described by the quasi-character
\[
\omega_1=\|x\|
\]
of \(K^*\).

For \(K\) complex, the isomorphism of local class field theory is the evident one on 2.2.5; for \(K\) real, it is the one which makes 2.3.1 and 2.3.2 below true.

Let \(L\subset\overline K\) be a finite extension of \(K\), so that \(W(\overline K/L)\subset W(\overline K/K)\). The following diagrams, where \(N\) is the norm and \(t\) the transfer, are commutative ([10], XIII, §4):
\[
\tag{2.3.1}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.3em,row sep=2.2em]
W(\overline K/L) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
W(\overline K/L)^{\ab} \arrow[r,"\sim"] \arrow[d] &
L^* \arrow[d,"N_{L/K}"]\\
W(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] &
W(\overline K/K)^{\ab} \arrow[r,"\sim"] &
K^* ,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\[
\tag{2.3.2}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.5em,row sep=2.3em]
W(\overline K/K)^{\ab} \arrow[r,"\sim"] \arrow[d,"t"'] &
K^* \arrow[d]\\
W(\overline K/L)^{\ab} \arrow[r,"\sim"] &
L^* .
\end{tikzcd}
\]

\paragraph{2.4.} Let \(K\) be a function field in one variable over \(\F_p\), and let \(k\) be its field of constants, the algebraic closure of \(\F_p\) in \(K\). Let \(\overline K\) be a separable closure of \(K\), let \(\overline k\) be the algebraic closure of \(k\) in \(\overline K\), and let \(\Gal^0(\overline K/K)\) be the kernel of the restriction map from \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) to \(\Gal(\overline k/k)\). The absolute global Weil group \(W(\overline K/K)\) is, by analogy with 2.2.4, defined by the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.55em,row sep=2.1em]
0 \arrow[r] & \Gal^0(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,equal] &
W(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,hook] &
W(\overline k/k)=\Z \arrow[r] \arrow[d,hook] & 0\\
0 \arrow[r] & \Gal^0(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] &
\Gal(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] &
\Gal(\overline k/k)=\widehat\Z \arrow[r] & 0 .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Let \(v\) be a place of \(K\), and suppose a place of \(\overline K\) above \(v\) has been chosen. There is then a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.55em,row sep=2.1em]
0 \arrow[r] & I_v \arrow[r] \arrow[d,hook] &
W(\overline K_v/K_v) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,hook] &
W(\overline k_v/k_v)=\Z \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"{[k_v:k]}"] & 0\\
0 \arrow[r] & \Gal^0(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] &
W(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] &
W(\overline k/k)=\Z \arrow[r] & 0 .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Global class field theory provides an isomorphism
\[
W(\overline K/K)^{\ab}\iso\A_K^*/K^*
\]
where \(\A_K^*/K^*\) is the idèle-class group, making the diagrams
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.0em,row sep=2.0em]
W(\overline K_v/K_v)^{\ab} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
W(\overline K/K)^{\ab} \arrow[d]\\
K_v^* \arrow[r] & \A_K^*/K^*
\end{tikzcd}
\]
commutative.

\paragraph{2.5.} For the definition of global Weil groups in the case of number fields, we refer to Weil [19] or to [2], XIV. We shall use them only incidentally.

\section*{3. Reminders on \(L\)-functions}

\paragraph{3.1.} Let \(K\) be a local field and use again the notation of 2.2.1. We denote by \(dx\) a Haar measure on \(K\), by \(d^*x\) a Haar measure on \(K^*\), and by \(\psi\) a nontrivial additive character of \(K\).

\paragraph{3.2.} For a quasi-character, i.e. a continuous homomorphism,
\[
\chi:K^*\longrightarrow\C^*,
\]
define \(L(\chi)\in\C\cup\{\infty\}\) as follows.

\[
\tag{3.2.1}
K\simeq\R.
\]
Put
\[
\Gamma_\R(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),
\]
and, for \(x\) the embedding of \(K\) in \(\C\) and \(N=0\) or \(1\),
\[
L(x^{-N}\omega_s)=\Gamma_\R(s).
\]

\[
\tag{3.2.2}
K\simeq\C.
\]
Put
\[
\Gamma_\C(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s),
\]
and, for \(z\) an embedding of \(K\) in \(\C\) and \(N\ge0\),
\[
L(z^{-N}\omega_s)=\Gamma_\C(s).
\]

\[
\tag{3.2.3}
K\text{ nonarchimedean.}
\]
Put
\[
L(\chi)=1\quad(\chi\text{ ramified}),
\qquad
L(\chi)=\frac1{1-\chi(\pi)}\quad(\chi\text{ unramified}).
\]

\paragraph{3.3.} Given \(\psi\) and \(dx\), put, for \(f\) a \(C^\infty\) function of compact support on \(K\),
\[
\widehat f(y)=\int_K f(x)\psi(xy)\,dx.
\]
Define \(\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)\in\C^*\) by Tate's local functional equation, independent of \(f\),
\[
\tag{3.3.1}
\frac{\displaystyle\int_{K^*}\widehat f(x)\,\omega_1\chi^{-1}(x)\,d^*x}
     {L(\omega_1\chi^{-1})}
=
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)\,
\frac{\displaystyle\int_{K^*}f(x)\chi(x)\,d^*x}
     {L(\chi)}.
\]
The same Haar measure \(d^*x\) is used on both sides. Both sides are defined by analytic continuation in \(\chi\) in the families \(\chi\omega_s\), \(s\in\C\). In these families, \(\varepsilon\) is of the form \(Ae^{Bs}\).

One has
\[
\tag{3.3.2}
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,a\,dx)=a\,\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx),
\]
\[
\tag{3.3.3}
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi(ax),dx)=\chi(a)\|a\|^{-1}\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx).
\]

\paragraph{3.4.} For \(K\) nonarchimedean, put:
\[
n(\psi)=\text{the largest integer }n\text{ such that }\psi|_{\pi^{-n}\mathcal O}=1;
\]
\[
a(\chi)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\chi\text{ unramified},\\
\text{the least }m\text{ such that }\chi|_{1+(\pi)^m}=1,&\chi\text{ ramified};
\end{cases}
\]
\[
sw(\chi)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\chi\text{ unramified or tamely ramified},\\
a(\chi)-1,&\chi\text{ ramified};
\end{cases}
\]
and let \(\gamma\) be an element of \(K^*\) of valuation
\[
v(\gamma)=n(\psi)+sw(\chi)+1.
\]
The local constant \(\varepsilon\) is given by (3.3.2), (3.3.3), and the following formulas.

\[
\tag{3.4.1}
K\simeq\R.
\]
Let \(x\) be the embedding of \(K\) in \(\C\), and \(N=0\) or \(1\). For \(\psi=\exp(2\pi ix)\) and \(dx\) the Lebesgue measure,
\[
\varepsilon(x^{-N}\omega_s,\psi,dx)=i^N.
\]

\[
\tag{3.4.2}
K\simeq\C.
\]
Let \(z\) be an embedding of \(K\) in \(\C\), and \(N\ge0\). For
\[
\psi=\exp(2\pi i\,\Tr_{\C/\R}(z))
\]
and
\[
dx=|dz\wedge d\overline z|=2\,da\,db\quad(z=a+bi),
\]
one has
\[
\varepsilon(z^{-N}\omega_s,\psi,dx)=i^N.
\]

\paragraph{3.4.3.} \(K\) nonarchimedean. If \(\chi\) is unramified,
\[
\int_{\mathcal O}dx=1,\qquad n(\psi)=0,
\]
then
\[
\tag{3.4.3.1}
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)=1.
\]
For \(\chi\) ramified, one has
\[
\tag{3.4.3.2}
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)=
\int_{K^*}\chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx
=
\sum_n\int_{v(x)=n}\chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx
=
\int_{\gamma^{-1}\mathcal O^*}\chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx.
\]

We shall see that it is natural to unify these two formulas as follows. If \(\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)\) is defined by
\[
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)=\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)
\quad(\chi\text{ ramified}),
\]
and
\[
\tag{3.4.3.3}
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)=\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)\,\chi(\pi)^{-1}
\quad(\chi\text{ unramified}),
\]
then
\[
\tag{3.4.3.4}
\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)=
\int_{\gamma^{-1}\mathcal O^*}\chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx.
\]
From (3.4.3.3) and (3.4.3.4), one deduces that, for \(\omega\) unramified,
\[
\tag{3.4.3.5}
\varepsilon_0(\chi\omega,\psi,dx)
=
\omega(\pi)^{n(\psi)+sw(\chi)+1}\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx),
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon(\chi\omega,\psi,dx)
=
\omega(\pi)^{n(\psi)+a(\chi)}\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx).
\]

\paragraph{3.5.} Suppose \(K\) nonarchimedean, and resume the notation of 2.2.1. Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional vector space over a field \(E\) of characteristic \(0\), or over \(\C\). The representations
\[
\rho:W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow\GL(V)
\]
will always be assumed continuous: an open subgroup of \(I\) acts trivially. For \(V\) the space of a representation and \(V^I\) the subspace of inertia invariants, put
\[
\tag{3.5.1}
Z(V;t)=\det(1-Ft^d,V^I)^{-1},
\]
\[
\tag{3.5.2}
L(V)=Z(V;1)\qquad(E\in E^*\cup\{\infty\}).
\]
Let
\[
\omega^t:W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow E((t))^*
\]
be the unramified character such that \(\omega^t(F)=t^d\). In an evident sense, \(\omega_s=\omega^{p^{-s}}\). One has
\[
\tag{3.5.3}
Z(V,t)=L(V\otimes\omega^t).
\]

\paragraph{3.6.} It is so that (3.2.3) and (3.5.2) are compatible that we normalized the local class field theory isomorphism so that geometric Frobenius elements correspond to uniformizers. Both (3.2.3) and (3.5.2) are very natural: 3.2.3 is forced from the analytic point of view (cf. 3.3.1), and 3.5.2 follows the tradition of geometers; it is geometric Frobenius which tends to act on the cohomology of algebraic varieties with algebraic integers as eigenvalues.

\paragraph{3.7.} The irreducible continuous complex representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\) are, for \(K\) complex, the quasi-characters. For \(K\) real, besides the quasi-characters of \(K^*\), they are the representations induced by quasi-characters \(\chi\) of \(\overline K^*\) such that \(\chi\ne\chi\circ\sigma\), where \(\sigma\) is complex conjugation of \(\overline K\).

For any complex representation \(V\), expressed in the Grothendieck group of representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\) as a sum of simple representations, define \(L(V)\) as follows:
\[
\tag{3.7.1}
K\simeq\R,\quad
V=\sum_i[\chi_i]+\sum_j\Ind_{\overline K^*}^{W}(\chi_j),
\]
\[
L(V)=\prod_i L_K(\chi_i)\prod_j L_{\overline K}(\chi_j).
\]
\[
\tag{3.7.2}
K\simeq\C,\quad V=\sum_i[\chi_i],
\qquad
L(V)=\prod_iL(\chi_i).
\]

\paragraph{Proposition 3.8.} Let \(K\) be a local field and \(\overline K\) an algebraic closure of \(K\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow V'\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow V''\longrightarrow0
\]
of complex representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\), one has
\[
\tag{3.8.1}
L(V)=L(V')L(V'').
\]
\item Let \(L\) be a finite separable extension of \(K\) in \(\overline K\), and let \(V_L\) be a complex representation of \(W(\overline K/L)\). Let \(V_K\) be the representation induced to \(W(\overline K/K)\). Then
\[
\tag{3.8.2}
L(V_K)=L(V_L).
\]
\end{enumerate}

Assertion (i) is trivial. In the archimedean case, assertion (ii) follows from the duplication formula
\[
\Gamma_\C(s)=\Gamma_\R(s)\Gamma_\R(s+1),
\]
corresponding to
\[
\Ind([\omega_s])=[\omega_s]+[\chi^{-1}\omega_{s+1}].
\]

We prove (ii) for \(K\) nonarchimedean. Let \(k\) and \(\ell\) be the residue fields of \(K\) and \(L\). Then
\[
V_K^{I_K}=
\left(\Ind_{W(\overline K/L)}^{W(\overline K/K)}V_L\right)^{I_K}
\simeq
\Ind_{W(\overline\ell/\ell)}^{W(\overline k/k)}(V_L^{I_L})
\]
as \(W(\overline k/k)\)-representations, both sides being identified with
\[
\Hom_{W(\overline K/L)}(W(\overline k/k),V_L),
\]
the covariant functions from \(W(\overline k/k)\) to \(V_L\), \(W(\overline K/L)\) acting on \(W(\overline k/k)\) by translation; cf. the commutative diagram




\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.0em,row sep=2.0em]
W(\overline K/L)\arrow[r]\arrow[d]&W(\overline\ell/\ell)\arrow[d]\\
W(\overline K/K)\arrow[r]&W(\overline k/k).
\end{tikzcd}
\]
This reduces (3.8.2) to the following well-known lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.9.} Let \(F\) be an endomorphism of \(V\), let \(n\) be an integer, and let \(F_n\) be the endomorphism of
\[
V_n=V\otimes\C^n
\]
such that, for a basis \(e_0,\ldots,e_{n-1}\) of \(\C^n\),
\[
F_n(v\otimes e_i)=v\otimes e_{i+1}\quad(0\le i<n-1),
\qquad
F_n(v\otimes e_{n-1})=F(v)\otimes e_0.
\]
Then
\[
\det(1-F_nt)=\det(1-Ft^n).
\]
By the principle of continuation of algebraic identities, one may suppose \(F\) diagonal in a suitable basis of \(V\). One is immediately reduced to the case \(\dim(V)=1\), where \(F\) is multiplication by a number \(a^n\), \(a\ne0\). In the basis \(f_i=a^{-i}(v\otimes e_i)\), one has \(F_n(f_i)=af_{i+1}\), for \(i\in\Z/n\Z\). The eigenvectors of \(F_n\) are the
\[
\sum_i\chi(i)f_i
\]
where \(\chi\) runs through the characters of \(\Z/n\Z\), and the determinant identity follows.

\paragraph{3.10.} Let \(K\) be a global field, an \(\mathfrak A\)-field in the sense of [15]. For every place \(v\) of \(K\), write \(K_v\) for the local field which is the completion of \(K\) at \(v\). The objects defined above for local fields will be denoted, for \(K_v\), with a subscript \(v\). Let \(\mathbb A\) be the adele ring of \(K\), let \(\|\ \|\) be the absolute value satisfying
\[
d(ax)=\|a\|\,dx
\]
for a Haar measure \(dx\) on \(\mathbb A\), and let \(\omega_s\) be the quasi-character \(\|x\|^s\) of \(\mathbb A^*\). If \(x_v\) is the component of \(x\in\mathbb A\) in \(K_v\), then
\[
\|x\|=\prod_v\|x_v\|_v.
\]
Let \(dx\) denote the unique Haar measure on \(\mathbb A\) such that
\[
\int_{\mathbb A/K}dx=1
\]
(the Tamagawa measure), let \(d^*x\) be a Haar measure on \(\mathbb A^*\), and let \(\psi\) be a character trivial on \(\mathbb A/K\), with local components \(\psi_v\). For \(f\) a \(C^\infty\) function with compact support on \(\mathbb A\), put
\[
\widehat f(y)=\int_{\mathbb A}f(x)\psi(xy)\,dx.
\]

\paragraph{3.11.} Tate's global functional equation [14] says, for \(\chi\) a quasi-character of \(\mathbb A^*/K^*\),
\[
\tag{3.11.1}
\int_{\mathbb A^*}\widehat f(x)\chi'(x)\,d^*x
=
\int_{\mathbb A^*}f(x)\chi(x)\,d^*x,
\qquad
\chi'=\omega_1\chi^{-1},
\]
the integrals being defined by analytic continuation in the families \(\chi\omega_s\). Put
\[
\tag{3.11.2}
L(\chi)=\prod_vL(\chi_v)
\]
by analytic continuation, and, for \(dx=\otimes_vdx_v\),
\[
\tag{3.11.3}
\varepsilon(\chi)=\prod_v\varepsilon(\chi_v,\psi_v,dx_v).
\]
The product (3.11.3) is independent of the choice of \(\psi\) and of the decomposition of \(dx\), as follows from (3.3.2) and (3.3.3). If, for almost all \(v\),
\[
\int_{\mathcal O_v}dx_v=1,
\]
then almost all its factors are equal to \(1\). Formulas (3.3.1) and (3.11.1) give the global functional equation of Hecke \(L\)-functions:
\[
\tag{3.11.4}
\varepsilon(\chi)L(\chi')=L(\chi).
\]

\paragraph{3.12.} The results stated below are proved in [1] and [19]. Let \(K\) be a global field, \(\overline K\) an algebraic closure of \(K\), and \(V\) a complex representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\), always assumed continuous: the action of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) factors through a finite Galois group \(\Gal(K'/K)\). For every place \(v\) of \(K\), choose a place of \(\overline K\) above it, and let \(V_v\) be the representation obtained from \(V\) by restriction to the subgroup
\[
W(\overline K_v/K_v)
\]
of the decomposition group \(\Gal(\overline K_v/K_v)\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item For \(\chi\) a quasi-character of \(\mathbb A^*/K^*\), with local components \(\chi_v\), the infinite product
\[
L(V,\chi)=\prod_vL(V_v\otimes\chi_v)
\]
can be defined by analytic continuation in \(\chi\) in the families \(\chi\omega_s\). It converges for \(\Re(s)\) large and extends to a meromorphic function of \(s\).

\item Let \(V^*\) denote the dual of \(V\). One has
\[
L(V,\chi)=\varepsilon(V,\chi)\,L(V^*,\chi'),
\qquad \chi'=\omega_1\chi^{-1},
\]
with \(\varepsilon(V,\chi)\in\C^*\), of the form \(Ae^{Bs}\) as \(\chi\) varies in the families \(\chi\omega_s\).

\item Let \(L\) be a finite extension of \(K\) in \(\overline K\), let \(N_{L/K}\) be the norm, let \(V_L\) be a representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/L)\), and put
\[
V_K=\Ind_{\Gal(\overline K/L)}^{\Gal(\overline K/K)}(V_L).
\]
For \(\chi\) a quasi-character of \(\mathbb A_K^*/K^*\), one has
\[
L(V_K,\chi)=L(V_L,\chi\circ N_{L/K}),
\]
and similarly
\[
\varepsilon(V_K,\chi)=\varepsilon(V_L,\chi\circ N_{L/K}).
\]
\item One also has
\[
\varepsilon(V'\oplus V'',\chi)=\varepsilon(V',\chi)\varepsilon(V'',\chi),
\]
which makes it possible to define \(\varepsilon(V,\chi)\) when \(V\) is only a virtual representation.
\end{enumerate}
The assertions (A) and (B) are proved by reduction to the case \(\dim V=1\), via Brauer's theorem. Assertion (C) follows from (B) and from the compatibility of norm and transfer recalled above.

\section*{4. Existence of local constants}

\paragraph{Theorem 4.1.} There exists a unique function \(\varepsilon\), satisfying conditions (1) to (4) below, which associates a number
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\in\C^*
\]
to each isomorphism class of sextuples \((K,\overline K,\psi,dx,V,\rho)\), consisting of a local field \(K\), an algebraic closure \(\overline K\) of \(K\), a nontrivial additive character \(\psi:K\to\C^*\), a Haar measure \(dx\) on \(K\), a finite-dimensional complex vector space \(V\), and a representation
\[
\rho:W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow\GL(V).
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item For every exact sequence of representations
\[
0\to V'\to V\to V''\to0,
\]
one has
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)=\varepsilon(V',\psi,dx)\varepsilon(V'',\psi,dx).
\]
This condition shows that \(\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\) depends only on the class of \(V\) in the Grothendieck group of representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\), and allows \(\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\) to be interpreted when \(V\) is only a virtual representation.

\item
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,a\,dx)=a^{\dim V}\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx).
\]
In particular, for virtual \(V\) of dimension \(0\), \(\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\) is independent of \(dx\); it is denoted \(\varepsilon(V,\psi)\).

\item If \(L\) is a finite separable extension of \(K\) in \(\overline K\), and \(V_K\) is the virtual representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\) induced by a virtual representation of dimension zero \(V_L\) of \(W(\overline K/L)\), then
\[
\varepsilon(V_K,\psi)=\varepsilon(V_L,\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K}).
\]

\item If \(\dim V=1\), and \(\rho\) is defined by a quasi-character \(\chi\) of \(K^*\), then
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)=\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx).
\]
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{4.2.} Let
\[
v=\sum_{i,j}n_{ij}(H_i,\chi_{ij})\in R^+_0(W(\overline K/K))
\]
(1.10). Assume that the \(H_i\) are pairwise nonconjugate. Let \(K_i\) be the extension of \(K\) defined by \(H_i\), and again denote by \(\chi_{ij}\) the quasi-character of \(K_i^*\) defined by \(\chi_{ij}\). For any choice of Haar measures \(dx_i\) on the \(K_i\), and for \(\psi\) an additive character of \(K\), put
\[
\tag{4.2.1}
\varepsilon(v,\psi)=
\prod_{i,j}\varepsilon(\chi_{ij},\psi\circ\Tr_{K_i/K},dx_i)^{n_{ij}}.
\]
It follows from (3.3.2) that \(\varepsilon(v,\psi)\) is independent of the choice of the \(dx_i\).

\paragraph{Lemma 4.3.} With the notation 1.9.8, for \(v\in R^+_0(W(\overline K/K))\),
\[
\varepsilon(v,\psi(ax))=\det(v)(a)\,\varepsilon(v,\psi).
\]
It is enough to check this for \(v=(H,\chi)-(H,1)\), where \(H\) corresponds to an extension \(L\) of \(K\). Let \(v_0\) be the virtual representation \([\chi]-[1]\) of \(W(\overline K/L)\). Formula (3.3.3) gives the assertion over \(L\). It is known ([10], XII, §4) that the inclusion of \(K^*\) into \(L^*\) is identified with the transfer
\[
W(\overline K/K)^{\ab}\longrightarrow W(\overline K/L)^{\ab}.
\]
Since \(b(v)=\Ind(v_0)\), the lemma follows from 1.2.

\paragraph{Lemma 4.4.} The theorem is true in the archimedean case.

For \(K\simeq\C\), define \(\varepsilon\) by (1) and (4), and (2) is automatic. For \(K\simeq\R\), one checks from 2.9 that
\[
b:R^+(W(\overline K/K))\longrightarrow R(W(\overline K/K))
\]
is surjective, with kernel generated by the \(\chi_s-\chi_t\) where
\[
\chi_s=(K^*,\omega_s)-[\omega_s]-[\chi^{-1}\omega_{s+1}].
\]
For a virtual representation \(V\), and \(v\in R^+_0(W(\overline K/K))\) such that
\[
b(v)=V-\dim(V)[1],
\]
put
\[
\tag{4.4.1}
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)=\varepsilon(v,\psi)\,\varepsilon(1,\psi,dx)^{\dim V}.
\]
We check that the right-hand side is independent of the choice of \(v\), i.e. that, for \(v\) satisfying \(b(v)=0\), one has \(\varepsilon(v,\psi)=1\). By 4.3, it is enough to check this for \(\psi(x)=\exp(2\pi ix)\). For this choice of \(\psi\), the assertion follows from the preceding determination of \(\Ker(b)\), and from the fact that the right-hand sides of (3.4.1), (3.4.2) are independent of \(s\). It is clear that \(\varepsilon\) defined by (4.4.1) satisfies (1) to (4).

We now restrict to the nonarchimedean case.

\paragraph{4.5.} Let \(K\) be a nonarchimedean local field, \(\overline K\) a separable closure of \(K\), and \(K_{nr}\subset\overline K\) the maximal unramified extension of \(K\). Let \(J\) be an open normal subgroup of the inertia group
\[
I=\Gal(\overline K/K_{nr})
\]
and let \(L\) be the corresponding extension of \(K_{nr}\). For \(\sigma\in I/J\), put
\[
t_{I/J}(\sigma)=\inf\bigl(v_L(\sigma(x)-x)\mid x\text{ integral in }L\bigr),
\]
and define functions \(a_{I/J}\) and \(sw_{I/J}\) by
\[
\tag{4.5.1}
a_{I/J}(\sigma)=-t_{I/J}(\sigma)\quad(\sigma\ne e),
\]
\[
sw_{I/J}(\sigma)=1-t_{I/J}(\sigma)\quad(\sigma\ne e),
\]
\[
\sum_\sigma a_{I/J}(\sigma)=\sum_\sigma sw_{I/J}(\sigma)=0.
\]
By Artin (see [10], VI, §2, th. 1), \(a_{I/J}\) is the character of a representation \(A_{I/J}\) of \(I/J\), the Artin representation. By loc. cit., prop. 2, \(sw_{I/J}\) is likewise the character of a representation \(Sw_{I/J}\), the Swan representation. These representations are defined only up to isomorphism. \(A_{I/J}\) is the sum of \(Sw_{I/J}\) and the augmentation representation of \(I/J\). For \(I\supset J\supset K\), one has (loc. cit., prop. 3)
\[
\tag{4.5.2}
(A_{I/K})^{J/K}\iso A_{I/J},
\qquad
(Sw_{I/K})^{J/K}\iso Sw_{I/J}.
\]
If \(d\) is the valuation of the discriminant \(\delta_{L/K_{nr}}\), i.e. the valuation of the different \(\mathfrak D_{L/K}\) ([10], III, §3, prop. 6), and \(r\) denotes a regular representation, then (loc. cit., prop. 4)
\[
\tag{4.5.3}
A_{I/K}|_{J/K}=d\,r_{J/K}+A_{J/K}.
\]
Let \(V\) be a representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\), trivial on \(J\subset I\), with character \(\psi\). Define the Artin and Swan conductors of \(V\) by
\[
\tag{4.5.4}
a(V)=\dim\Hom_{I/J}(A_{I/J},V)=sw(V)+\dim V-\dim V^I,
\]
\[
sw(V)=\dim\Hom_{I/J}(Sw_{I/J},V)
=\frac1{|I/J|}\sum_\sigma sw_{I/J}(\sigma)\psi(\sigma).
\]
By 4.5.2, these conductors are independent of the choice of \(J\). For \(V\) of dimension \(1\), defined by a quasi-character \(\chi\), one has (loc. cit., prop. 5)
\[
\tag{4.5.5}
a(V)=\text{the conductor }a(\chi)\text{ of }\chi.
\]
The preceding notation is therefore compatible with that introduced in 3.4.

With the notation 1.9.8, one has:

\paragraph{Lemma 4.5.} For \(v\in R^+_0(W(\overline K/K))\) and \(\chi\) an unramified quasi-character of \(K^*\),
\[
\varepsilon(v\cdot\chi,\psi)=\chi(\pi)^{a(v)}\varepsilon(v,\psi).
\]
It suffices to verify this for \(v=(H,\lambda)-(H,\mu)\), where \(H\) defines an extension \(L\) of \(K\), and \(\lambda,\mu\) are two quasi-characters of \(L^*\). Let \(d\) be the valuation of the different of \(L/K\), let \(f\) be the residue-degree, let \(I\subset W(\overline K/K)\) and \(J\subset W(\overline K/L)\) be the inertia groups, and choose \(K\subset J\) small enough. We compute \(a(v)\). The adjunction formula for induced representations, (4.5.3), and (4.5.5) give, for every quasi-character \(\nu\) of \(H\), i.e. of \(L^*\),
\[
\tag{4.5.1'}
a((H,\nu))
=\dim\Hom_{I/K}(A_{I/K},\Ind_H^W(\nu))
=f\,\dim\Hom_{J/K}(A_{J/K}+d\,r_{J/K},[\nu]|_J)
=f\,a(\nu)+fd.
\]
Thus
\[
a(v)=f(a(\lambda)-a(\mu)).
\]
For \(\nu\) a quasi-character of \(L^*\), \(\chi_1\) an unramified quasi-character of \(L^*\), and \(n=n(\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K})\) as in 3.4, one has by (3.4.3.1) or (3.4.3.2)
\[
\frac{\varepsilon(\nu\chi_1,\psi\circ\Tr,dx)}
     {\varepsilon(\nu,\psi\circ\Tr,dx)}
=
\chi_1(\pi_L)^{a(\nu)+n}.
\]
Hence
\[
\varepsilon(v\cdot\chi,\psi)\varepsilon(v,\psi)^{-1}
=
\chi\circ N_{L/K}\bigl(\pi_L^{a(\lambda)-a(\mu)}\bigr)
=
\chi(\pi)^{f(a(\lambda)-a(\mu))},
\]
which is the announced formula.

\paragraph{Terminology 4.6.} Let \(K_1\) be a Galois extension, finite or not, of \(K\), with Galois group \(G\). Let \(\alpha\) be a quasi-character of \(K^*\), and \(\psi\) a nontrivial additive character of \(K\). We shall say that there exists an \(\alpha,\psi\)-theory of constants for \(K_1/K\) if there exists \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}\) of the following type:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item For \(H\) an open subgroup of \(G\), defining a finite extension \(L\) of \(K\), for \(V\) a representation of \(H\), and \(dx\) a Haar measure on \(L\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx)\in\C^*
\]
is defined.
\item For every exact sequence of representations
\[
0\to V'\to V\to V''\to0,
\]
one has
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx)=
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V',dx)\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V'',dx).
\]
Thus \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}\) still makes sense for virtual representations.
\item One has
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,a\,dx)=a^{\dim V}\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx).
\]
In particular, for virtual \(V\) of dimension \(0\), \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx)\) is independent of \(dx\); it is denoted \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V)\).
\item For \(H_1\subset H_2\subset G\), defining \(L_1\supset L_2\), and \(V\) a virtual representation of dimension \(0\) of \(H_1\), one has
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(\Ind_{H_1}^{H_2}V)=\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V).
\]
\item For \(V\) of dimension \(1\), defined by a character \(\chi\) of \(L^*\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx)
=
\varepsilon(\chi\,\alpha\circ N_{L/K},\,\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},\,dx).
\]
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Lemma 4.7.} Let \(K_1/K\) and \(\alpha\) be as above, and let \(\psi\) be a nontrivial additive character of \(K\). There exists at most one \(\alpha,\psi\)-theory of constants for \(K_1/K\). For one to exist, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every
\[
v\in\Ker(R^+_0(G)\longrightarrow R(G)),
\]
one have
\[
\varepsilon(v\cdot\alpha,\psi)=1.
\]
Let \(H\subset G\), \(L\), and \(V\) be as above. By 1.5, there exists \(v\in R^+_0(H)\) whose image in \(R(H)\) is \(V-\dim(V)[1]\). Necessarily
\[
\tag{4.7.1}
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx)
=
\varepsilon(v\cdot(\alpha\circ N_{L/K}),\,\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K})\,
\varepsilon(\alpha\circ N_{L/K},\,\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},\,dx)^{\dim V},
\]
which gives uniqueness. The hypothesis ensures that \(\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}\), defined by this formula, is independent of the choice of \(v\); one easily checks axioms (0) to (4).




\paragraph{Lemma 4.8.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Let \(\beta\) be an unramified quasi-character of \(K^*\), respectively let \(\psi'=\psi(ax)\) be a nontrivial additive character. For there to exist an \(\alpha,\psi\)-theory of constants for \(K_1/K\), it is necessary and sufficient that there exist an \(\alpha\beta,\psi\)-theory, respectively an \(\alpha,\psi'\)-theory. For \(H,L,V\) as above,
\[
\psi_L=\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},\qquad
\beta_L=\beta\circ N_{L/K},\qquad
\alpha_L=\alpha\circ N_{L/K},

one then has
\[
\tag{4.8.1}
\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta,\psi}(V,dx)
=
\beta_L\!\left(\pi^{\,a(V\alpha_L)+\dim(V)n(\psi_L)}\right)
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx),
\]
\[
\tag{4.8.2}
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi'}(V,dx)
=
\|a\|_L^{-\dim(V)}
\det(V\alpha_L)(a)\,
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx).

\end{enumerate}
The first assertion follows from criterion 4.7 and from 4.5, respectively from 4.3. It is enough to prove (4.8.1) and (4.8.2) for \(K=L\). Moreover, one may suppose either that \(V\in R^0(\Gal(K_1/K))\), or that \(V=[1]\). In the first case one applies 4.5 and 4.3. In the second, one uses (4.7.1) and the definition 3.4.3.

We shall say that there exists an \(\alpha\)-theory of constants for \(K_1/K\) if, for every nontrivial additive character \(\psi\), or for one such \(\psi\), there exists an \(\alpha,\psi\)-theory of constants for \(K_1/K\). For \(K_1\supset L\supset K\), there is then an \(\alpha\circ N_{L/K}\)-theory of constants for \(K_1/L\).

\paragraph{Reduction 4.9.} Theorem 4.1 is implied by the following assertion:
\[
(*)\quad
\text{for every nonarchimedean local field }K\text{ and every finite Galois extension }K_1/K,
\]
\[
\text{there exists an unramified quasi-character }\alpha\text{ of }K^*
\text{ and an }\alpha\text{-theory of constants for }K_1/K.

By 4.8, assertion \((*)\) implies that, for \(\overline K\) a separable closure of \(K\), and for any unramified quasi-character \(\alpha\) of \(K^*\), there exists an \(\alpha\)-theory of constants for \(\overline K/K\). The discussion below will allow us to pass from the Galois group to the Weil group.

\paragraph{4.10.} Every representation \(V\) of \(W(\overline K/K)\) is trivial on a suitable finite-index subgroup \(J\) of inertia \(I\). Let \(F\) be a geometric Frobenius. Since \(I/J\) is finite, a suitable power \(F^n\) of \(F\) acts trivially on \(I/J\) by conjugation, and hence is central in \(W(\overline K/K)/J\). We shall say that \(V\) has a \emph{type} if a power \(F^m\) of \(F^n\) has only one eigenvalue \(a\). The type of \(V\) is then the element of the inductive limit
\[
\varinjlim_{n\mid m}\{X_n,\varphi_{n,m}\},\qquad
X_n=\C^*,\quad \varphi_{n,m}(x)=x^{m/n},
\]
defined by \(a\in X_m=\C^*\). Every irreducible representation has a type.

The representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\) of a given type \(\tau\) form an abelian category \(R_\tau\), and the category of all representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\) is the sum of these subcategories. If \(R_\tau(W(\overline K/K))\) is the Grothendieck group of \(R_\tau\), one therefore has
\[
\tag{4.10.1}
R(W(\overline K/K))=\bigoplus_\tau R_\tau(W(\overline K/K)).
\]
The semisimple representations of type \(1\) are exactly the continuous representations of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\). If \(X_\tau\) is a quasi-character of type \(\tau\), and there are quasi-characters, even unramified ones, of all types, then twisting by \(X_\tau\) defines an isomorphism
\[
\tag{4.10.2}
\otimes X_\tau:R(\Gal(\overline K/K))\iso R_\tau(W(\overline K/K)).
\]

Let \(L\) be a finite separable extension of \(K\) in \(\overline K\), and let \(f\) be the residue-degree. If a representation \(V\) of \(W(\overline K/L)\) has type \(\sigma\), the representation induced to \(W(\overline K/K)\) has type \(\sigma^f\). We say that \(V\) is of \(K\)-type \(\tau\) if \(\sigma^f=\tau\). The set of representations of \(W(\overline K/L)\) of a given \(K\)-type is therefore stable under induction.

\paragraph{Variant 4.10.3.} The preceding arguments apply to every group which is an extension of \(\Z\) by a finite group, and to projective limits of such extensions. The case of global Weil groups will be useful later.

\paragraph{4.11.} Assume \((*)\). For every type \(\tau\), let \(X_\tau\) be an unramified character of type \(\tau\). Since there exists an \(X_\tau\)-theory of constants, one can define \(\varepsilon\) on \(R_\tau(W(\overline K/K))\) by
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)=
\varepsilon_{X_\tau,\psi}(V\otimes X_\tau^{-1},dx).
\]
It follows from the uniqueness 4.7 that this \(\varepsilon\) is independent of the choice of \(X_\tau\). One then defines \(\varepsilon\) on \(R(W(\overline K/K))\) by additivity (4.10.1). By 4.7, if a theory 4.1 exists, it is given by this \(\varepsilon\).

We have arranged things so that \(\varepsilon\) satisfies (1), (2), (4), and (3) when \(V_L\) has a type. It remains to verify (3) for \(V_L\) of the form
\[
[\lambda\circ N_{L/K}]-[\mu\circ N_{L/K}],
\]
where \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\) are two unramified quasi-characters of \(K^*\), of given types. By (3.4.3.1), (3.3.2), and (3.3.3), one has
\[
\varepsilon\bigl([\lambda\circ N_{L/K}]-[\mu\circ N_{L/K}],
\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K}\bigr)
=
(\lambda\mu^{-1})\circ N_{L/K}
\left(\pi_L^{\,n(\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K})}\right).
\]
On the other hand, by 4.8,
\[
\varepsilon\bigl(\Ind([\lambda\circ N_{L/K}]-[\mu\circ N_{L/K}]),\psi\bigr)
=
(\lambda\mu^{-1})
\left(\pi_K^{\,a(\Ind(\mu\circ N_{L/K}))+[L:K]\,n(\psi)}\right).
\]
Let \(d\) be the valuation of the different of \(L/K\), \(e\) the ramification index, and \(f\) the residue-degree. Formula (4.5.1) gives
\[
a(\Ind(\mu\circ N_{L/K}))=fd.
\]
The formula to be proved is therefore reduced to
\[
f\,n(\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K})=fd+[L:K]\,n(\psi),
\]
that is,
\[
n(\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K})=d+e\,n(\psi),
\]
which is essentially the definition of the different ([10], III, §3). The key point in the proof of \((*)\) is the following lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma 4.12.} Let \(K'/K\) be a Galois extension of global fields. For every place \(w\) of \(K\), choose a place of \(K'\) above it and denote it again by \(w\). Let \(\alpha\) be a quasi-character of the idèle-class group of \(K\), and let \(v\) be a place of \(K\) which does not decompose in \(K'\). If, for every finite place \(w\ne v\) of \(K\), there exists an \(\alpha_w\)-theory of constants for \(K'_w/K_w\), then there exists an \(\alpha_v\)-theory of constants for \(K'_v/K_v\).

Let \(\psi\) be a nontrivial character of \(\mathbb A_K/K\), and let \(\psi_v\) be its \(v\)-component. We shall construct an \(\alpha_v,\psi_v\)-theory of constants for \(K'_v/K_v\).

One has
\[
\Gal(K'/K)=\Gal(K'_v/K_v).
\]
A subgroup \(H\) of \(\Gal(K'_v/K_v)\) therefore defines, besides an extension \(L_v\) of \(K_v\), an extension \(L\) of \(K\) with completion \(L_v\). Let \(dx\) be the Tamagawa measure on \(\mathbb A_L\), so that \(\mathbb A_L/L\) has volume \(1\), and write
\[
dx=\otimes_w dx_w,
\]
with \(dx_v\) chosen in advance. Every representation \(V_v\) of \(H\) defines a representation \(V\) of \(\Gal(K'/L)\), and, by restriction, representations \(V_w\) of the decomposition groups \(\Gal(K'_w/K_w)\). A global constant \(\varepsilon(V,\alpha)\) was defined in 3.12.

Let \(w\) be a place of \(L\), and again write \(w\) for its image in \(K\). For \(w\) infinite, one has by 4.4 local constants
\[
\varepsilon\bigl(V_w,(\alpha\circ N_{L/K})_w,
(\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K})_w,dx_w\bigr).
\]
For \(w\) finite and different from \(v\), one also has, by hypothesis, constants
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha_w,\psi_w}(V_w,dx_w).
\]
Denoting both kinds by \(\varepsilon_\alpha(V_w,\psi_w,dx_w)\), put
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha_v,\psi_v}(V_v,dx_v)
=
\frac{\varepsilon(V,\alpha)}
{\displaystyle\prod_{w\ne v}\varepsilon_\alpha(V_w,\psi_w,dx_w)}.
\]
One checks that \(\varepsilon_{\alpha_v,\psi_v}\) does not depend on the choice of the decomposition \(dx=\otimes dx_w\), and satisfies axioms (0) to (4). The key point is 3.12(C).

\paragraph{Lemma 4.13.} Let \(K'/K\) be a Galois extension of local fields. There exists a Galois extension of global fields \(^{1}K'/{}^{1}K\), and a place \(v\) of \(^{1}K\) which does not decompose in \(^{1}K'\), such that \(K\) is identified with the completion of \(^{1}K\) at \(v\), and \(K'\) with a subextension of \(^{1}K'_v/{}^{1}K_v\).

This is trivial: first take \(^{1}K'/{}^{1}K\) with \(^{1}K'_v\simeq K'\supset K={}^{1}K_v\), and then replace \(^{1}K\) by the extension defined by a decomposition group at \(v\).

\paragraph{Lemma 4.14.} Let \(K\) be a global field, let \(S\) be a finite set of finite places of \(K\), and let \(m:S\to\mathbb N\). There exists a character \(\alpha\) of the idèle-class group such that, for \(w\in S\), the conductor of \(\alpha_w\) is \(\ge m(w)\), and such that, for every finite place outside \(S\), \(\alpha_w\) is unramified.

If \(K\) is a number field, one can indeed freely specify \(\alpha\) on
\[
\prod_{w\ \mathrm{finite}}\mathcal O_w^*,
\]
because this compact subgroup of \(\mathbb A^*\) meets \(K^*\) only in \(\{1\}\). If \(K\) is a function field, with field of constants \(k\), one has
\[
\prod_w\mathcal O_w^*\cap K^*=k^*,
\]
and one observes that there exist characters of \(\mathcal O_w^*\), of arbitrarily large conductor, which are trivial on \(k^*\).

By 4.12, 4.13, and 4.14, assertion 4.9 \((*)\), and hence 4.1, follow from the following two lemmas, where \(K'/K\) is a finite Galois extension of nonarchimedean local fields, with Galois group \(G\), and \(\alpha\) is a quasi-character of \(K^*\).

\paragraph{Lemma 4.15.} If \(\alpha\) and \(K'/K\) are unramified, there exists an \(\alpha\)-theory of constants for \(K'/K\).

Choose for \(\psi\) a character of \(K\) such that \(n(\psi)=0\). It is then enough to take
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx)=
\left(\int_L dx\right)^{\dim(V)}.
\]

\paragraph{Lemma 4.16.} For \(K'/K\) fixed, there exists \(m\) such that, if the conductor of \(\alpha\) is \(\ge m\), there exists an \(\alpha\)-theory of constants for \(K'/K\).

Let \(\alpha\) have conductor \(m\), and put
\[
n=\left[\frac{m+1}{2}\right].
\]
Then the function \(\alpha(1+a)\) is additive in \(a\) for \(v(a)\ge m-n\). If \(\psi\) is a nontrivial additive character of \(K\), there is therefore a \(y\), of valuation \(-(m+n(\psi))\), well-defined modulo \(\pi^{-(n+n(\psi))}\), such that
\[
\alpha(1+a)=\psi(ya)\qquad\text{for }v(a)\ge n.
\]
For \(\chi\) a quasi-character of \(K^*\), of conductor \(\le m-n\), one then has
\[
\tag{4.16.1}
\varepsilon(\chi\alpha,\psi,dx)
=
\sum_{u\in\mathcal O^*/(1+(\pi^n))}
\int_{\pi^{-m-n(\psi)}u}
\chi^{-1}(x)\alpha^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx.
\]
For \(\pi^{-m-n(\psi)}u\) not contained in \(y(1+(\pi^{m-n}))\), the corresponding partial integral is zero: it is the integral of a nontrivial additive character. Since \(\chi\) is constant with value \(\chi(y)\) on \(y(1+(\pi^{m-n}))\), one has
\[
\varepsilon(\chi\alpha,\psi,dx)
=
\chi^{-1}(y)\,\varepsilon(\alpha,\psi,dx).
\]

We shall show that, for \(m\) sufficiently large, one obtains an \(\alpha,\psi\)-theory of constants for \(K'/K\) by putting, for \(H\subset G\) defining an extension \(L\) of \(K\), and \(V\) a representation of \(H\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\alpha,\psi}(V,dx)
=
\det(V)^{-1}(y)\,
\varepsilon(\alpha\circ N_{L/K},\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},dx)^{\dim V}.
\]
Axioms (0), (1), and (2) are trivial. Axiom (3) follows from the fact that the inclusion of \(K^*\) in \(L^*\) is identified with the transfer
\[
W(\overline K/K)^{\ab}\longrightarrow W(\overline K/L)^{\ab}
\]
([10], XIII, §4), and from 1.2. It remains to prove axiom (4).

Let \(H\subset G\) correspond to \(L/K\), and let \(e\) be the ramification index. Recall that, for \(u\in\mathcal O_L\) and for \(n\) the least integer such that \(ne\ge2v(u)\),
\[
\tag{4.16.2}
N_{L/K}(1+u)=1+\Tr_{L/K}(u)\pmod{\pi^n}.
\]
This is checked by writing the norm of \(1+u\) as the product of its conjugates. We shall write \(O(1)\) for any number bounded in terms of \(K'/K\) alone. By 4.16.2, if \(\alpha\) has conductor \(m\), then \(\alpha\circ N_{L/K}\) has conductor \(em-O(1)\). Moreover, for
\[
\frac{v(a)}e\ge \left[\frac{m+1}{2}\right]+O(1),
\]
one has
\[
\alpha\circ N_{L/K}(1+a)
=
\alpha(1+\Tr(a))
=
\psi(y\,\Tr a)
=
\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K}(ya).
\]
For every character \(\chi\) of \(H\), of conductor \(O(1)\), one can repeat the reasoning used above, obtaining the desired formula
\[
\varepsilon(\chi\,\alpha\circ N_{L/K},\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},dx)
=
\chi^{-1}(y)\,
\varepsilon(\alpha\circ N_{L/K},\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},dx).
\]

\section*{5. Formula list}

\paragraph{5. Formula list.}

\paragraph{(5.1)} Besides the local constant \(\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\) defined in 4.1, we shall also consider, in the nonarchimedean case, the constant \(\varepsilon_0\) defined by
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)
=
\varepsilon_0(V,\psi,dx)\,\det(-F,V^I)^{-1}.
\]
The constants \(\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon_0\) have the following properties.

\paragraph{(5.2)} \(\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon_0\) are additive in \(V\), and hence still make sense for virtual \(V\).

\paragraph{(5.3)} \(\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon_0\) are independent of \(dx\) for virtual \(V\) of dimension \(0\). They are denoted \(\varepsilon(V,\psi)\) and \(\varepsilon_0(V,\psi)\). More precisely,
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,a\,dx)=a^{\dim V}\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx),
\]
and similarly for \(\varepsilon_0\).

\paragraph{(5.4)} One has
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi(ax),dx)
=
\det(V)(a)\,\|a\|^{-\dim V}\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx),
\]
and similarly for \(\varepsilon_0\).

\paragraph{(5.5)} For \(K\) nonarchimedean and \(\chi\) an unramified quasi-character of \(K^*\), one has
\[
\tag{5.5.1}
\varepsilon(V\chi,\psi,dx)
=
\chi\!\left(\pi^{a(V)+\dim(V)n(\psi)}\right)\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx),
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon_0(V\chi,\psi,dx)
=
\chi\!\left(\pi^{\dim(V)+sw(V)+\dim(V)n(\psi)}\right)
\varepsilon_0(V,\psi,dx).
\]
In particular, if
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx,s)=\varepsilon(V\omega_s,\psi,dx),
\]
and similarly for \(\varepsilon_0\), then
\[
\tag{5.5.2}
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx,s)
=
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\,
q^{-(a(V)+\dim(V)n(\psi))s},
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon_0(V,\psi,dx,s)
=
\varepsilon_0(V,\psi,dx)\,
q^{-(sw(V)+\dim(V)(n(\psi)+1))s}.
\]
For \(W\) an unramified representation, one similarly has
\[
\tag{5.5.3}
\varepsilon(V\otimes W,\psi,dx)
=
\det W\!\left(\pi^{a(V)+\dim(V)n(\psi)}\right)
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)^{\dim W},
\]
\[
\varepsilon_0(V\otimes W,\psi,dx)
=
\det W\!\left(\pi^{sw(V)+\dim(V)(n(\psi)+1)}\right)
\varepsilon_0(V,\psi,dx)^{\dim W}.
\]

\paragraph{(5.6)} For \(L/K\) a finite separable extension, \(V_L\) a virtual representation of dimension \(0\) of \(W(\overline K/L)\), and \(V_K\) the representation induced to \(W(\overline K/K)\), one has
\[
\tag{5.6.1}
\varepsilon(V_K,\psi)=\varepsilon(V_L,\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K}),
\]
and similarly for \(\varepsilon_0\). In other words, there exists
\[
\lambda(L/K,\psi,dx_L,dx_K)\in\C^*
\]
such that, for \(V_L\) of arbitrary dimension,
\[
\tag{5.6.2}
\varepsilon(V_K,\psi,dx_K)
=
\lambda(L/K,\psi,dx_L,dx_K)^{\dim(V_L)}
\varepsilon(V_L,\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},dx_L).
\]
The same formula holds for \(\varepsilon_0\).

In Langlands [9], it is (5.6.2) which provides the framework of the theory. Moreover, he systematically takes \(dx\) to be the Haar measure self-dual for \(\psi\), and omits it from the notation. The constants are also normalized to have complex absolute value \(1\). All of this forces on him another formula (5.4).

\paragraph{5.7.} Let \(V^*\) be the dual of \(V\), and let \(dx'\) be the Haar measure dual to \(dx\), relative to \(\psi\). One has
\[
\tag{5.7.1}
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\,
\varepsilon(V^*\omega_1,\psi(-x),dx')=1.
\]
In particular, if \(K\) is nonarchimedean and \(V\) is unitary, so that \(V^*=\overline V\), one has
\[
\tag{5.7.2}
|\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)|^2
=
\left(\frac{dx'}{dx}\right)^{-\dim(V)}
q^{a(V)+\dim(V)n(\psi)}.
\]

\paragraph{5.8.} \(\varepsilon^{-1}\) appears in Tate's local functional equation; putting \(\chi'=\omega_1\chi^{-1}\),
\[
\tag{5.8.1}
\frac{\displaystyle\int \widehat f(x)\chi'(x)d^*x}{L(\chi')}
=
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)\,
\frac{\displaystyle\int f(x)\chi(x)d^*x}{L(\chi)}
\qquad\text{(Tate).}
\]
For \(K\) nonarchimedean, and \(\gamma\in K^*\) of valuation \(1+sw(\chi)+n(\psi)\), (3.4.3.4) gives
\[
\tag{5.8.2}
\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)
=
\int_{\gamma^{-1}\mathcal O^*}\chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx.
\]

\paragraph{5.9.} For \(K\) nonarchimedean, if \(\chi\) is unramified, one has
\[
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)
=
\chi(\pi^{n(\psi)})\,q^{n(\psi)}
\int_{\mathcal O}dx.
\]
In particular, if \(n(\psi)=0\) and \(\int_{\mathcal O}dx=1\), then
\[
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)=1.
\]

\paragraph{5.10.} Take \(K\) nonarchimedean, \(a(\chi)\le1\), \(n(\psi)=-1\), and
\[
\int_{(\pi)}dx=1.
\]
Let \(\chi_k\) and \(\psi_k\) be the characters of \(k^*\) and \(k\) defined by \(\chi\) and \(\psi\). One has
\[
\tag{5.10.1}
\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)=-\tau(\chi_k,\psi_k),
\]
where \(\tau\) is the Gauss sum
\[
\tag{5.10.2}
\tau(\chi_k,\psi_k)
=
-\sum_{x\in k^*}\chi_k^{-1}(x)\psi_k(x),
\]
equal to \(1\) for trivial \(\chi_k\). In particular,
\[
\tag{5.10.3}
\varepsilon_0([\chi]-[1],\psi)=\tau(\chi_k,\psi_k).
\]




\paragraph{5.11.} Let \(K\) be a global field. For \(V\) a representation of the global Weil group of \(K\), \(\psi\) a nontrivial additive character of \(\mathbb A_K/K\), and
\[
dx=\bigotimes_v dx_v
\]
the Tamagawa measure on \(\mathbb A_K\), put
\[
\tag{5.11.1}
L(V)=\prod_v L(V_v),
\]
\[
\tag{5.11.2}
\varepsilon(V)=\prod_v\varepsilon(V_v,\psi_v,dx_v).
\]
The global functional equation is written
\[
\tag{5.11.3}
L(V)=\varepsilon(V)\,L(V^*\otimes\omega_1).
\]

Proofs. Formulas 5.2, 5.3, 5.6, and 5.8 are 4.1, respectively (1), (2), (3), (4), together with 3.3. Formulas 5.4 and 5.5 follow from 4.8. It is enough to prove (5.7.1) for \(V\) defined by a character; the formula then follows from (5.8.1) and from the Fourier inversion formula. Formula (5.7.2) follows from (5.7.1), (5.3), and (5.4). Formula 5.9 follows from (3.4.3.1), and 5.10 from (5.8.2). Finally, 5.11 follows from the preceding facts and from (3.11.4), by known arguments [1], [19].

The restriction of 4.1 to the case of extensions and tamely ramified characters is equivalent to the following identities on Gauss sums (5.10.2). Here \(k\) denotes a finite field with \(q\) elements, and \(\psi\) a nontrivial additive character of \(k\).

\paragraph{5.12. Hasse--Davenport.} Let \(k'\) be an extension of \(k\), of degree \(n\), and let \(\chi\) be a character of \(k^*\). One has
\[
\tag{5.12.1}
\tau(\chi\circ N_{k'/k},\psi\circ\Tr_{k'/k})
=
\tau(\chi,\psi)^n.
\]

\paragraph{5.13.} Let \(\chi\) be a character of \(k^*\), and let \(n\) be an integer prime to \(q\). Let \(X\) be a set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of pairs \((k',\chi')\), consisting of an extension \(k'\) of \(k\) and a character \(\chi'\) of \(k'^*\), such that
\[
\tag{a}
\chi'(x^n)=\chi\circ N_{k'/k}(x),
\]
and
\[
\tag{b}
\chi'\text{ is not of the form }\chi''\circ N_{k'/k''}
\]
for \(k''\) intermediate between \(k\) and \(k'\), i.e.
\[
\chi'^{\,q^{f'}}\ne\chi'
\qquad(0<f'<[k':k]).
\]
Put
\[
\tau(\chi,\psi^n)=
\lambda_n(\chi,\psi)\,
\prod_{(k',\chi')\in X}
\tau(\chi',\psi\circ\Tr_{k'/k}).
\]
Then
\[
\tag{5.13.1}
\lambda_n(\chi,\psi)\text{ is independent of }\chi.
\]
This identity splits into the following two identities.

\paragraph{5.14 (Langlands [9], 7.8).} Let \(\ell\) be a prime number prime to \(q\), let \(f\) be the order of \(q\) in \((\mathbb Z/\ell)^*\), let \(k'\) be an extension of \(k\) of degree \(f\), let \(T\) be a set of representatives of the \(\Gal(k'/k)\)-orbits in the nontrivial characters of order \(\ell\) of \(k'^*\), and let \(\chi\) be a character of \(k^*\). One has
\[
\tau(\chi^\ell,\psi^\ell)\,
\prod_{\mu\in T}\tau(\mu,\psi\circ\Tr_{k'/k})
=
\tau(\chi,\psi)\,
\prod_{\mu\in T}\tau(\chi\circ N_{k'/k}\cdot\mu,\psi\circ\Tr_{k'/k}).
\]

\paragraph{5.15 (Langlands [9], 7.9).} Let \(\ell\) be a prime number dividing \(q-1\), let \(T\) be the set of characters of order \(\ell\) of \(k^*\), let \(\chi\) be a character of \(k^*\) which is not an \(\ell\)-th power, let \(k'\) be an extension of degree \(\ell\) of \(k\), and let \(\chi'\) be a character of \(k'^*\) such that
\[
\chi'(x^\ell)=\chi\circ N_{k'/k}(x).
\]
One has
\[
\tau(\chi,\psi^\ell)\,
\prod_{\mu\in T}\tau(\mu,\psi)
=
\tau(\chi',\psi\circ\Tr_{k'/k}).
\]

\paragraph{5.16. Proofs.} If \(K'/K\) is a tamely ramified extension of nonarchimedean local fields, and if \(\psi\) is an additive character of \(K\) such that
\[
n(\psi)=-1,
\]
then still
\[
n(\psi\circ\Tr_{K'/K})=-1,
\]
so that 5.10 applies both to \(K\) and to \(K'\).

Let \(V\) be a tamely ramified representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\). One can always write it as a sum of representations induced by tame characters \(\chi_i\) of unramified extensions \(K_i/K\):
\[
V=\dim(V)[1]+\sum_i\Ind([\chi_i]-[1])
+\sum_i\bigl(\Ind([1])-[K_i:K][1]\bigr).
\]
Let \(k_i\) be the residue field of \(K_i\), and let \(\widetilde\chi_i\) be the character of \(k_i^*\) defined by \(\chi_i\). For \(dx\) a Haar measure on \(K\) such that
\[
\int_{(\pi)}dx=1,
\]
one finds
\[
\tag{5.16.1}
\varepsilon_0(V,\psi,dx)
=
(-1)^{\dim V}
\prod_i\tau(\widetilde\chi_i,\psi_k\circ\Tr_{k_i/k}).
\]

Formula (5.12) is obtained by taking an unramified extension \(K'/K\), and by writing that, for \(\chi\) a character, here tamely ramified, of \(K^*\), one has
\[
\Ind(\chi\circ N_{K'/K}-[1])
=
\sum_\mu([\chi\mu]-[\mu]),
\]
where \(\mu\) runs through the unramified characters of order dividing \([K':K]\). This formula (5.12) ensures that the right-hand side of (5.16.1) depends only on \(V\).

The identities 5.13 are obtained by taking a totally ramified extension \(K'/K\) of degree \(n\), and by combining 5.6.1 and (5.16.1) for \(V\) induced by a tame character \(\chi\) of \(K'\). Formulas (5.14) and (5.15) are the special case of (5.13.1) where \(n\) is a prime number \(\ell\). In 5.14, respectively 5.15, one supposes that the character \(\chi\) of \(K'^*\) is, respectively is not, an \(\ell\)-th power.

\section*{6. Reduction modulo \(\ell\) of local constants}

Let \(K\) be a nonarchimedean local field, let \(\overline K\) be a separable closure of \(K\), and resume the notation of 2.2. Let \(A\) be a commutative ring in which \(p\) is invertible. The exposition would be simplified, without loss of generality, by assuming \(A\) local, or even a field. For \(x\in K\), \(\|x\|\) has a meaning in \(A\) by hypothesis.

\paragraph{6.1.} A Haar measure \(\mu\) on \(K\), with values in a \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\)-module \(M\), is a finitely additive function on compact open subsets of \(K\), with values in \(M\), invariant under translation. The additive function defined by
\[
\mu_0(a+x\mathcal O)=\|x\|
\]
is the unique Haar measure with values in \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\) such that
\[
\mu_0(\mathcal O)=1,
\]
and every Haar measure with values in \(M\) is of the form
\[
m\mu_0\qquad(m=\mu(\mathcal O)\in M).
\]
One can integrate a locally constant compactly supported function, with values in \(A\), against a Haar measure with values in \(A\).

\paragraph{6.2.} Let
\[
\rho:W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow\GL_A(V)
\]
be a representation of the Weil group on a free, or projective, \(A\)-module \(V\), and let \(J\) be an open normal subgroup of \(I\) on which \(\rho\) is trivial. For every prime number \(\ell\ne p\), the Swan representation \(Sw_{I/J}\) can be realized as a projective \(\mathbb Z_\ell[I/J]\)-module. If \(A\) is a \(\mathbb Z_\ell\)-algebra, then
\[
\Hom_{I/J}(Sw_{I/J},V)
\]
is a projective \(A\)-module. If \(A\) is also local, it is free; its rank is the Swan conductor of \(V\). More generally, one checks that the Swan conductor of \(V\) can always be defined as a locally constant integer-valued function on \(\Spec(A)\). This conductor is invariant under extension of the scalar ring \(A\).

\paragraph{6.3.} If \(\zeta\) is a \(p^n\)-th root of unity in \(A\), the order of \(\zeta\) is a locally constant function on \(\Spec(A)\). Indeed, any two of the cyclotomic polynomials \(\Phi_{p^i}(X)\) generate the unit ideal in \(A[X]\), since \(p\in A^*\). Thus \(A\) is a product of rings \(A_i\), \(1\le i\le n\), the \(i\)-th coordinate of \(\zeta\) satisfying
\[
\Phi_{p^i}(\zeta)=0;
\]
on \(\Spec(A_i)\), \(\zeta\) has order \(p^i\).

If \(\psi\) is a continuous additive character of \(K\) with values in \(A^*\), then it takes its values in the \(p^n\)-th roots of unity. This makes it possible to define \(n(\psi)\) as a locally constant function on \(\Spec(A)\): at a prime ideal \(\lambda\), \(n(\psi)\) is \(+\infty\) if \(\psi\) is trivial modulo \(\lambda\), and otherwise the largest integer \(n\) such that
\[
\psi|_{\pi^{-n}\mathcal O}
\]
is trivial modulo \(\lambda\), or in the localization \(A_\lambda\), which is the same thing. By abuse of language, one says that \(\psi\) is nontrivial if \(n(\psi)\) is nowhere \(+\infty\).

\paragraph{6.4.} For \(\psi\) a nontrivial additive character of \(K\) with values in \(A^*\), for \(\chi\in\Hom(K^*,A^*)\), and for \(dx\) a Haar measure with values in \(A\), one can now define
\[
\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)\in A^*
\]
by formula (3.4.3.4). More precisely, if \(\Spec(A)\) is not connected, one first writes
\[
A=\prod_i A_i
\]
with \(n(\psi)\) and \(Sw(\chi)\) constant on \(\Spec(A_i)\). The \(i\)-th projection of \(\varepsilon_0\) is then defined by (3.4.3.4).

\paragraph{Theorem 6.5.} There exists a unique function \(\varepsilon_0\) of the following type:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item For \(K\) a nonarchimedean local field, \(\overline K\) a separable closure of \(K\), \(A\) a field of characteristic \(\ne p\), \(V\) a representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\) in a finite-dimensional vector space over \(A\), \(\psi\) a nontrivial additive character with values in \(A^*\), and \(dx\) a nonzero Haar measure with values in \(A\), one has
\[
\varepsilon_0(V,\psi,dx)\in A^*.
\]
\item \(\varepsilon_0\) is invariant under extension of the field \(A\).
\item Let \(A\) be a discrete valuation ring of residual characteristic \(\ne p\), let \(F\) be its fraction field, and let \(s=A/\mathfrak m\) be its residue field. For \(V\) a representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\) in a finite-rank free \(A\)-module, and for \(\psi\) and \(dx\) with values in \(A\), one has
\[
\varepsilon_0(V_F,\psi,dx)\in A^*,
\]
and its reduction modulo \(\mathfrak m\) is
\[
\varepsilon_0(V_s,\overline\psi,\overline{dx}).
\]
\item Conditions (1) to (3) of 4.1 are satisfied.
\item For \(\dim V=1\), \(\varepsilon_0\) is given by 6.4.
\end{enumerate}

By Galois descent, it is enough to treat the case where \(A\) is separably closed. If \(A\) has characteristic \(0\), the assertion, being purely algebraic, follows from the case \(A=\C\) treated in 4.1. Thus suppose \(A\) has characteristic \(\ell\ne0\).

Let \(G\) be a finite quotient of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\), and let \(A_0\) be a discrete valuation ring of unequal characteristic, with residue field \(A_0/\mathfrak m=A\). Assume that \(\psi\) has been lifted to an additive character with values in \(A_0^*\), that \(dx\) has been lifted to a Haar measure with values in \(A_0\), and that the fraction field \(E\) of \(A_0\) is large enough, in the sense of 1.4, relative to \(G\).

For every character \(\chi\) of a subgroup of \(G\) with values in \(E^*\), in fact in \(A_0^*\), the corresponding constant \(\varepsilon_0\) is clearly in \(A_0\). By 5.7.1, it is even in \(A_0^*\). It follows that, for every representation \(V_E\) of \(G\) over \(E\),
\[
\varepsilon_0(V_E,\psi,dx)\in A_0^*.
\]
The decomposition homomorphism
\[
d:R_E(G)\longrightarrow R_A(G)
\]
is known to be surjective: virtual representations lift. For \(V\in R_A(G)\), choose \(\widetilde V\in R_E(G)\) with \(d(\widetilde V)=V\), and put
\[
\varepsilon_0(V,\overline\psi,\overline{dx})
=
\text{the reduction modulo }\mathfrak m\text{ of }
\varepsilon_0(\widetilde V,\psi,dx).
\]

By 1.8, to justify this definition it is enough to check that:
\[
(*)\quad
\text{for }H\subset G\text{ defining an extension }L\text{ of }K,
\]
\[
\psi_L=\psi\circ\Tr_{L/K},\quad
\chi',\chi''\text{ characters of }H\text{ with values in }A_0^*,
\]
if \(\chi'\) and \(\chi''\) are congruent modulo \(\mathfrak m\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_0\!\left(\Ind_H^G([\chi']-[\chi'']),\psi\right)
&=\varepsilon_0\bigl([\chi']-[\chi''],\psi_L\bigr)\\
&\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The Swan conductors of \(\chi'\) and \(\chi''\) are both equal to the Swan conductor of \(\chi'\) modulo \(\mathfrak m\). For
\[
y=\pi^{1+Sw(\chi')+n(\psi_L)},
\]
the formulas of 3.4.3 give
\[
\varepsilon_0(\chi',\psi_L,dx_L)=
\int_{y^{-1}\mathcal O_L^*}\chi'^{-1}(x)\psi_L(x)\,dx_L,
\]
and similarly for \(\chi''\). The numerator and denominator are units, and are clearly congruent modulo \(\mathfrak m\). Hence
\[
\frac{\varepsilon_0(\chi',\psi_L,dx_L)}
{\varepsilon_0(\chi'',\psi_L,dx_L)}
\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m}.
\]
For \(V\) a representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\), one therefore obtains a function \(\varepsilon_0\) with the announced properties. Its formal properties are the same as those made explicit in the formula list 5.1 in the case \(A=\C\). To pass from \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) to \(W(\overline K/K)\), one may therefore repeat the argument used in 4.9, with the help of the second formula (5.5.1).

\paragraph{Remark 6.6.} Throughout this section it was essential to work with \(\varepsilon_0\) rather than with \(\varepsilon\). First, this permits the use of the Swan conductor, which is invariant under reduction modulo \(\ell\), rather than the Artin conductor; second, it avoids having to distinguish, in the definition of \(\varepsilon_0(\chi,\psi,dx)\), between the unramified and tamely ramified cases. The meaning of \(\varepsilon_0\) will appear more clearly in the next section.

\section*{7. Modular \(L\)-functions}

\paragraph{7.1.} Let \(X\) be a nonsingular projective curve over \(\F_p\). Assume \(X\) irreducible, let \(K\) be its field of rational functions, and identify the closed points of \(X\) with the places of \(K\). We do not assume that \(X\) is geometrically irreducible, i.e. that \(\F_p\) is algebraically closed in \(K\).

\paragraph{7.2.} Let \(A\) be a noetherian ring. It is known what a constructible sheaf of \(A\)-modules on \(X_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\) is (SGA 4, IX, 2.3). For every \(x\in X\), and for \(k(\overline x)\) a separably closed extension of \(k(x)\), such a sheaf \(\mathcal G\) has a fiber
\[
\mathcal G_{\overline x}
\]
at \(x\), which is a finite-type \(A\)-module. It depends only on the separable closure of \(k(x)\) in \(k(\overline x)\), and \(\Gal(\overline{k(x)}/k(x))\) acts on \(\mathcal G_{\overline x}\) by transport of structure.

Moreover, for \(v\) a place of \(K\), i.e. a closed point of \(X\), and \(\overline K_v\) an algebraic closure of \(K_v\), there is a specialization map
\[
\spc_v:\mathcal G_{\overline{k(v)}}\longrightarrow \mathcal G_{\overline K_v}.
\]
This map is equivariant; its image is therefore contained in \(\mathcal G_{\overline K_v}^{I_v}\). We say that \(\mathcal G\) is unramified at \(v\) if \(\spc_v\) is an isomorphism. A constructible sheaf is unramified almost everywhere.

Once a separable closure \(\overline K\) of \(K\), and a place of \(\overline K\) above each place of \(K\), have been chosen, a constructible sheaf of \(A\)-modules can conversely be defined as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item one gives the fibers \(\mathcal G_{\overline K}\) and \(\mathcal G_{\overline{k(v)}}\), respectively \(A\)-\(\Gal(\overline K/K)\)-modules or \(A\)-\(\Gal(\overline{k(v)}/k(v))\)-modules, of finite type over \(A\);
\item one gives the specialization maps, equivariant for the action of the decomposition groups,
\[
\mathcal G_{\overline{k(v)}}\longrightarrow\mathcal G_{\overline K}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
For (a) and (b) to define a sheaf, it is enough that the specialization maps be isomorphisms almost everywhere.

\paragraph{7.3.} It will be convenient to modify the preceding definition by replacing Galois groups by Weil groups. Let \(Y\) be a scheme over \(\F_p\). Let \(\overline{\F}_p\) be an algebraic closure of \(\F_p\), and let
\[
\overline Y=Y\otimes_{\F_p}\overline{\F}_p.
\]
A Weil sheaf, respectively a constructible Weil sheaf of \(A\)-modules, \(\mathcal G\) on \(Y\), consists of a sheaf, respectively a constructible sheaf of \(A\)-modules, \(\overline{\mathcal G}\) on \(\overline Y\), together with an action of Frobenius on \(\overline{\mathcal G}\), lying above its action on \(\overline Y\).

\emph{Unneeded remark.} Weil sheaves of sets on \(Y\) form a topos \(Y_{\mathrm{Weil}}\). It may be described as a \(2\)-fiber product of topoi: identifying \(\Spec(\F_p)_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\) with the classifying topos \(B\widehat{\mathbb Z}\), one has
\[
Y_{\mathrm{Weil}}\simeq
\overline Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\times_{B\widehat{\mathbb Z}}B\mathbb Z.
\]
We shall simply call the Weil sheaves on \(Y\) sheaves; sheaves on \(Y_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\) will be called étale sheaves.

\paragraph{7.4.} A constructible \(A\)-module sheaf in the sense of 7.3 can be described in Galois terms as in 7.2, replacing everywhere
\[
\Gal(\overline K/K),\qquad
\Gal(\overline K_v/K_v),\qquad
\Gal(\overline{k_v}/k_v)
\]
by
\[
W(\overline K/K),\qquad
W(\overline K_v/K_v),\qquad
W(\overline{k_v}/k_v)=\mathbb Z.
\]

\paragraph{7.5.} Let \(K_v\) be a nonarchimedean local field. A Weil sheaf on \(\Spec(\mathcal O_v)\) is defined by paraphrasing 7.3. Let \(\overline K_v\) be a separable closure of \(K_v\). Giving a constructible Weil sheaf of \(A\)-modules \(\mathcal G\) on \(\Spec(\mathcal O_v)\) is equivalent to giving:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item the fiber \(\mathcal G_{\overline K_v}\), an \(A\)-\(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\)-module, of finite type over \(A\);
\item the fiber \(\mathcal G_{\overline k_v}\), an \(A\)-\(W(\overline k_v/k_v)\)-module, of finite type over \(A\);
\item the \(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\)-equivariant specialization map
\[
\spc_v:\mathcal G_{\overline k_v}\longrightarrow\mathcal G_{\overline K_v}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Let \(v\) be a place of \(K\), and put
\[
X_v=\Spec(\mathcal O_v).
\]
By restriction, every constructible Weil sheaf of \(A\)-modules on \(X\) defines one on \(X_v\).

A sheaf is flat if its fibers are flat \(A\)-modules.

\paragraph{7.6.} Let \(K_v\) be a nonarchimedean local field. Put
\[
\deg(v)=[k_v:\F_p],
\]
and write \(\omega_v^t\) for the unramified quasi-character of \(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\), with values in \(A(t)\), such that
\[
\omega_v^t(F_v)=t^{\deg(v)}.
\]
Let \(\mathcal G\) be a flat constructible sheaf on \(\Spec(\mathcal O_v)\). Put
\[
\tag{7.6.1}
Z(\mathcal G_v,t)
=
\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},\mathcal G_{\overline k_v})^{-1}
=
\det(1-F_v,\mathcal G_{\overline K_v}\otimes\omega_v^t)^{-1}.
\]
For \(p\) invertible in \(A\), define a conductor
\[
\tag{7.6.2}
a(\mathcal G)=sw(\mathcal G_{\overline K_v})+
\dim(\mathcal G_{\overline K_v})-\dim(\mathcal G_{\overline k_v}),
\]
an integer if \(A\) is local, and in general a locally constant function on \(\Spec(A)\).

Assume that \(A\) is a field of characteristic \(\ne p\). Let \(\psi\) be a nontrivial additive character of \(K_v\) with values in \(A^*\), and let \(dx\) be a Haar measure with values in \(A\), as in 6.5. Put
\[
\tag{7.6.3}
\varepsilon(\mathcal G_v,\psi,dx,t)
=
\varepsilon_0(\mathcal G_{\overline K_v}\otimes\omega_v^t,\psi,dx)\,
\det(-F_vt^{\deg(v)},\mathcal G_{\overline k_v})^{-1}.
\]
This is a monomial. By (5.5.2), its degree in \(t\) is
\[
a(\mathcal G)\deg(v).
\]

\paragraph{Remark 7.7.} Let \(M\) be a representation of \(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\). In Galois terms, one defines sheaves \(j_!M\) and \(j_*M\) on \(\Spec(\mathcal O_v)\) by the specialization maps
\[
0\longrightarrow M,
\qquad
M^{I_v}\longrightarrow M.
\]
The quantities \(\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon_0\) of the preceding sections are identified with the quantity (7.6.3) relative respectively to \(j_*M\) and \(j_!M\). Unlike \(j_*\), the functor \(j_!\) commutes with reduction modulo \(\ell\). This may explain why, in §6, we considered only \(\varepsilon_0\).

\paragraph{7.8.} Let \(\mathcal G\) be a constructible sheaf of flat \(A\)-modules on \(X\). For every place \(v\) of \(K\), \(\mathcal G\) induces a sheaf \(\mathcal G_v\) on \(\Spec(\mathcal O_v)\). Put
\[
\tag{7.8.1}
Z(\mathcal G,t)=\prod_v Z(\mathcal G_v,t)\in A[[t]].
\]

\paragraph{7.9.} Assume that \(A\) is a field of characteristic \(\ne p\). On the adele ring of \(K\) there is a unique Haar measure with values in \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\), such that
\[
\int_{\mathbb A/K}dx=1.
\]
One can write
\[
dx=\bigotimes_v dx_v
\]
where \(dx_v\) is a Haar measure on \(K_v\), with values in \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\), and, for almost all \(v\),
\[
\int_{\mathcal O_v}dx_v=1.
\]
The \(dx_v\) define Haar measures with values in \(A\).

If there exists a nontrivial character \(\psi\) of \(\mathbb A/K\), with values in \(A^*\), put
\[
\tag{7.9.1}
\varepsilon(\mathcal G,t)=\prod_v
\varepsilon(\mathcal G_v,\psi_v,dx_v,t).
\]
Almost all terms in the product are equal to \(1\), and the product does not depend on the choice of \(\psi\) nor on the chosen decomposition of \(dx\).

Let \(A'\) be obtained from \(A\) by adjoining a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity. There exists a \(\psi\) with values in \(A'^*\). The corresponding global constant, being independent of \(\psi\), is invariant under \(\Gal(A'/A)\), hence lies in \(A\). This makes it possible to define \(\varepsilon\) even if no character \(\psi\) exists.

\paragraph{7.10.} Let \(i\) denote the inclusion of \(\Spec(K)\) into \(X\). If \(V\) is a representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\), write \(i_*V\) for the sheaf on \(X\) with fiber over \(\overline K\) equal to \(V\), and for which the specialization maps are
\[
\spc_v:V^{I_v}\hookrightarrow V.
\]

\paragraph{Theorem 7.11.} Let \(A\) be a field of characteristic \(\ell\ne p\). Let \(\mathcal G\) be a constructible sheaf of \(A\)-vector spaces, and let \(V\) be the fiber of \(\mathcal G\) over \(\overline K\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(Z(\mathcal G,t)\) is a rational function of \(t\).
\item As \(t\to\infty\), \(Z(\mathcal G,t)\) is asymptotic to \(\varepsilon(\mathcal G,t)\). In other words,
\[
Z(\mathcal G,t)/\varepsilon(\mathcal G,t)
\]
is a formal series in \(t^{-1}\), with constant term \(1\).
\item
\[
Z(i_*V,t)=\varepsilon(i_*V,t)\,Z(i_*V^*,pt^{-1}).
\]
\end{enumerate}

Let \(S\) be a finite set of places containing all the places where the representation \(V\) is ramified. Let \(j\) be the inclusion of \(X-S\) into \(X\), and let \(j_!V\) be the sheaf on \(X\), with generic fiber \(V\), unramified outside \(S\), and with zero fiber at \(v\in S\). One checks immediately that (i) and (ii) are equivalent to
\[
\tag{i'}
Z(j_!V,t)\text{ is a rational function of }t,
\]
\[
\tag{ii'}
\text{as }t\to\infty,\qquad Z(j_!V,t)\sim\varepsilon(j_!V,t).
\]

Assume \(\ell\ne0\). For every representation \(W\) of \(W(\overline K/K)\), unramified outside \(S\), identified with a locally constant sheaf on \(X-S\), put
\[
R^0j_*W=j_*W=i_*W,
\]
and let \(R^1j_*W\) be the sheaf zero outside \(S\), with fiber at \(v\in S\) equal to \(H^1(I_v,W)\). Put
\[
\tag{7.11.1}
Z(Rj_*W,t)=Z(R^0j_*W,t)\,Z(R^1j_*W,t)^{-1}.
\]

Let \(P'\) be the kernel of
\[
t_\ell:I_v\longrightarrow \mathbb Z_\ell(1).
\]
For every representation \(W\) of \(I_v\) over \(A\), one has canonically
\[
\tag{7.11.2}
H^i(I_v,W)=H^i(I_v/P',W^{P'}).
\]
For every representation \(W\) of \(\mathbb Z_\ell(1)\), one has canonically
\[
\tag{7.11.3}
H^0(\mathbb Z_\ell(1),W)=W^{\mathbb Z_\ell(1)}
\quad\text{(invariants),}
\]
\[
H^1(\mathbb Z_\ell(1),W)=W_{\mathbb Z_\ell(1)}(-1)
\quad\text{(twisted coinvariants),}
\]
\[
H^i(\mathbb Z_\ell(1),W)=0\qquad(i\ge2).
\]
Since duality exchanges invariants and coinvariants, one has, cf. 2.3:

\paragraph{Lemma 7.11.4.} For \(\ell\ne0\), \(H^0(I_v,W)\) and \(H^1(I_v,\omega_1W^*)\) are canonically in duality.

We shall not use the fact that this duality can be interpreted as a cup product with values in
\[
H^1(I_v,A(1))=\Hom(I_v,A(1))\simeq A,
\]
generated by \(t_\ell\) modulo \(\ell\).

For \(\ell=0\), in the course of this proof, put
\[
H^1(I_v,W):=H^0(I_v,\omega_1W^*)^*,
\]
and define \(Z(Rj_*W,t)\) by (7.11.1). This definition is justified by 7.11.5 and 7.11.6 below. For \(W\) a representation of \(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\), put also
\[
Z_v(Rj_*W,t)=
\det(1-Ft^{\deg(v)},H^0(I_v,W))^{-1}
\det(1-Ft^{\deg(v)},H^1(I_v,W)).
\]

Let \(A\) be a discrete valuation ring of unequal characteristic \(\ell\ne p\), and let \(W\) be a free \(A\)-module on which \(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\) acts. Let \(\eta\) be the fraction field of \(A\), and \(s=A/\mathfrak m\) its residue field.

\paragraph{Lemma 7.11.5.}
\[
Z_v(Rj_*W_s,t)=Z_v(Rj_*W_\eta,t)\pmod{\mathfrak m}.
\]

The proof below will lose its mystery in §10. Let
\[
P'=\Ker(t_\ell:I\to\mathbb Z_\ell(1)),
\]
let \(\sigma\) be a generator of \(\mathbb Z_\ell(1)\), let \(F\) be a geometric Frobenius, and let \(K^\bullet\) be the complex concentrated in degrees \(0\) and \(1\):
\[
K^\bullet:\quad W^{P'}\xrightarrow{\ \sigma-1\ } W^{P'}.
\]
The endomorphism
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\sigma^i
\]
of \(W^{P'}\) is invertible: it is enough to see this after reduction modulo the maximal ideal of \(A\). After reduction, since some power \(\sigma^{\ell^n}\) is the identity, \(\sigma\) is unipotent, and \(q\) is the unique eigenvalue of \(\sum\sigma^i\).

Let \(\underline F=(F_0,F_1)\) be the endomorphism of \(K^\bullet\) with components
\[
F_0=F,\qquad
F_1=F\circ\left(\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\sigma^i\right)^{-1}.
\]
The quantity
\[
Z=\det(1-F_0t^{\deg(v)},W^{P'})^{-1}
\det(1-F_1t^{\deg(v)},W^{P'})
\]
is compatible with extension of scalars to \(\eta\) or to \(s\). After extension of scalars to \(\eta\), respectively to \(s\), one has
\[
Z=\det(1-Ft^{\deg(v)},H^0(K^\bullet))^{-1}
\det(1-Ft^{\deg(v)},H^1(K^\bullet)).
\]
One concludes by noting that
\[
(H^i(K_\eta^\bullet),H^i(\underline F))\simeq(H^i(I_v,W_\eta),F),
\]
and
\[
(H^i(K_s^\bullet),H^i(\underline F))\simeq(H^i(I_v,W_s),F).
\]

\paragraph{Variant 7.11.6.} Suppose \(A\) is complete, and let \(W\) be an \(A\)-module on which \(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\) acts continuously for the \(\ell\)-adic topology, and no longer, as above, for the discrete topology. Put again
\[
H^1(I_v,W_\eta)=(W_\eta^{P'})_{\mathbb Z_\ell(1)}(-1)
\quad\text{(twisted coinvariants),}
\]
and define \(Z_v(Rj_*W,t)\) as before. The \(H^1\) defined above can be interpreted as a continuous \(H^1\):
\[
\tag{7.11.6.1}
H^1(I_v,W_\eta)=
\varprojlim_n H^1(I_v,W/\ell^n)\otimes_A\eta.
\]
The reduction formula 7.11.5 remains valid, with the same proof.

\paragraph{Lemma 7.11.7.} Formula (iii) is equivalent to
\[
\tag{7.11.8}
Z(j_!V,t)=\varepsilon(j_!V,t)\,Z(Rj_*V,pt^{-1}).
\]
Lemma 7.11.7 follows from the local identity
\[
\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},H^0(I_v,V))^{-1}
=
\det(-F_vt^{\deg(v)},H^0(I_v,V))^{-1}
\det(1-F_v(pt^{-1})^{\deg(v)},H^1(I_v,V^*))^{-1}.
\]
If \(V\) is replaced by the representation \(V\otimes\omega^t\) of \(W(\overline K_v/K_v)\) over \(A((t))\), this formula simplifies to
\[
\det(1-F_v^{-1},H^0(I_v,V))
=
\det(-F_v,H^0(I_v,V))\,
\det(1-F_v,H^1(I_v,\omega_1\otimes V^*)),
\]
which follows from local duality 7.11.4.

We prove 7.11. If \(A=\C\), the infinite product \(Z(j_*V,t)\) converges for \(|t|\) sufficiently small, and
\[
L(V,s)=Z(j_*V,q^{-s}).
\]
Thus \(Z\) is a meromorphic function of \(t\); the functional equation shows that it has moderate growth as \(t\to\infty\), hence is a rational function of \(t\). The functional equation of the \(L\)-function gives (iii).

The statements (i) and (iii) are purely algebraic. Since they are true for \(A=\C\), and so are (i') and (7.11.8), they remain true for \(A\) of characteristic \(0\). Now suppose \(A\) has characteristic \(\ell\ne0\).

Let \(J\) be an open normal subgroup of \(W(\overline K/K)\), containing \(I_v\) for \(v\in S\). If a representation \(V\) of \(W(\overline K/K)/J\) over \(A\) lifts to characteristic \(0\), the assertions (i') and (7.11.8) for \(V\) are obtained by reduction modulo \(\ell\), taking into account, for (7.11.8), 7.11.5. The quantities
\[
Z(j_!V,t),\qquad Z(Rj_*V,t),\qquad \varepsilon(j_!V,t)
\]
depend additively on \(V\), an extension giving a product. To obtain (i) and (7.11.8) in general, it is therefore enough to know that, after extension of scalars, every representation of \(W(\overline K/K)/J\) virtually lifts to characteristic \(0\). The method of 4.10, cf. 4.10.3, reduces this question to the case of finite groups, i.e. to Brauer's theorem.

Statement (ii') follows from (7.11.8) and from the fact that, for \(t=0\), \(Z=1\).

\paragraph{Proposition 7.12.} If \(A\) is a field of characteristic \(p\), and \(\mathcal G\) a constructible sheaf of \(A\)-vector spaces, then \(Z(\mathcal G,t)\) is still a rational function of \(t\).

In the proof of 7.11(i), we did not in fact use the hypothesis \(\ell\ne p\).

\section*{8. \(\ell\)-adic representations of nonarchimedean local Weil groups; Hodge structures and archimedean local Weil groups}

\paragraph{8.1.} Let \(K\) be a nonarchimedean local field. We use the notation of 2.1 and 2.2.

Let \(\ell\) be a prime number \(\ne p\), and let \(E_\lambda\) be a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\); more generally, one could take for \(E_\lambda\) a valued field extending \(\mathbb Q_\ell\). A \(\lambda\)-adic representation \(V\) of \(W(\overline K/K)\) is a representation continuous for the \(\lambda\)-adic topology
\[
\rho:W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow\GL(V)
\]
of \(W(\overline K/K)\) on a finite-dimensional \(E_\lambda\)-vector space.

The following result is proved in [13].

\paragraph{Theorem 8.2 (Grothendieck).} Let \((V,\rho)\) be a \(\lambda\)-adic representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\). There exists
\[
N\in\End(V)(-1),
\]
nilpotent, such that, for \(\sigma\) in a finite-index subgroup of \(I\), one has
\[
\rho(\sigma)=\exp(N\,t_\ell(\sigma)).
\]
This \(N\in\End(V)(-1)\) is unique, hence Galois-invariant; for \(w\in W(\overline K/K)\), one has
\[
\rho(w)N\rho(w)^{-1}=q^{v'(w)}N.
\]
We shall use 8.2 to describe \(\lambda\)-adic representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\) in terms independent of the topology of \(E_\lambda\). This will be done twice: 8.3 is more intrinsic, and 8.4, which is enough for us, is more elementary.

\paragraph{8.3.1.} Up to unique isomorphism, there exists a unique algebraic group \(G\) over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), isomorphic to \(\mathbb G_a\), and endowed with an isomorphism
\[
\mathbb Q_\ell(1)\iso G(\mathbb Q_\ell).
\]
It is denoted \(\mathbb G_a(1)\).

\paragraph{8.3.2.} Let \(J\subset I\) be an open invariant subgroup of \(W(\overline K/K)\), and put
\[
J'=J\cap\Ker(t_\ell).
\]
The quotient \(W(\overline K/K)/J'\) is an extension of the discrete group \(W(\overline K/K)/J\) by \(J/J'\). Applying to this extension
\[
t_\ell:J/J'\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_\ell(1)\longrightarrow\mathbb G_a(1),
\]
one obtains an extension \(W_J^\ell\) of \(W(\overline K/K)/J\) by \(\mathbb G_a(1)\), see Serre [12], II, 1.3:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.7em,row sep=2.3em]
0 \arrow[r] & J/J' \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"t_\ell"'] &
W(\overline K/K)/J' \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\alpha"] &
W(\overline K/K)/J \arrow[r] \arrow[d,equal] & 0\\
0 \arrow[r] & \mathbb G_a(1) \arrow[r] &
W_J^\ell \arrow[r] &
W(\overline K/K)/J \arrow[r] & 0 .
\end{tikzcd}
\]

\paragraph{8.3.3.} The \(W_J^\ell\) form a projective system, and 8.2 means that every \(\lambda\)-adic representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\) factors through an algebraic representation of some \(W_J^\ell\), i.e. through a representation of the group scheme
\[
W^\ell(\overline K/K)=\varprojlim_J W_J^\ell.
\]

\paragraph{8.3.4.} Let \(I_J^\ell\) be the inverse image of \(I/J\subset W(\overline K/K)/J\), and put
\[
I^\ell=\varprojlim_J I_J^\ell.
\]
To \(\bar\sigma\in I/J\), the image of \(\sigma\in I\), associate the element \(\alpha(\sigma)\,t(-t_\ell(\sigma))\) of \(W_J^\ell\). This section defines splittings
\[
I_J^\ell=\mathbb G_a(1)\times I/J,
\qquad
I^\ell=\mathbb G_a(1)\times I.
\]
There is a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.8em,row sep=1.8em]
0 \arrow[r] & I \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & W(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \Z \arrow[r] \arrow[d,equal] & 0\\
0 \arrow[r] & I\times\mathbb G_a(1) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & W^\ell(\overline K/K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \Z \arrow[r] & 0\\
0 \arrow[r] & \mathbb G_a(1) \arrow[r] & \mathbb G_a(1) & &
\end{tikzcd}
\]

\paragraph{8.3.5.} Let \({}'W^\ell\) be the semidirect product of \(W(\overline K/K)\) by \(\mathbb G_a(1)\), on which \(W(\overline K/K)\) acts by
\[
w x w^{-1}=q^{v'(w)}x.
\]
There exist isomorphisms \(\beta\) between \(W^\ell(\overline K/K)\) and \({}'W^\ell(\overline K/K)\) making the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=2.0em]
I\times\mathbb G_a(1)\arrow[r]\arrow[d,equal]&
W^\ell(\overline K/K)\arrow[r]\arrow[d,"\beta"]&
W(\overline K/K)\arrow[d,equal]\\
I\times\mathbb G_a(1)\arrow[r]&
{}'W^\ell(\overline K/K)\arrow[r]&
W(\overline K/K)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
commutative, and one checks easily that any two are conjugate by an element of \(\mathbb G_a(1)(\mathbb Q_\ell)\). The indeterminacy lies in the choice of a lift of a Frobenius \(F\in W(\overline K/K)\), and one uses \(q-1\ne0\); see 8.4.3 below.

\paragraph{8.3.6.} Let \({}'W\) be the group scheme over \(\mathbb Q\), the semidirect product of \(W(\overline K/K)\) by \(\mathbb G_a\), on which \(W(\overline K/K)\) acts by
\[
w x w^{-1}=q^{v'(w)}x.
\]
Every isomorphism
\[
\mathbb G_a\iso\mathbb G_a(1),
\qquad\text{i.e. }\mathbb Q_\ell\iso\mathbb Q_\ell(1),
\]
defines an isomorphism
\[
{}'W_{\mathbb Q_\ell}\iso{}'W^\ell,
\]
hence a class of such automorphisms modulo \(\mathbb Q_\ell^*\), acting on \({}'W^\ell\) through its action on \(\mathbb G_a(1)\). One checks, see 8.4.3 below, that this automorphism group acts trivially on the set of isomorphism classes of representations of \({}'W^\ell\). In total one has obtained:

\noindent\textbf{(8.3.7).} The isomorphism classes of \(\lambda\)-adic representations of \(W(\overline K/K)\) are in natural bijection with the isomorphism classes of representations over \(E_\lambda\) of the group scheme over \(\mathbb Q\), \({}'W\).

\paragraph{Definition 8.4.1.} Let \(E\) be a field of characteristic \(0\). A representation of \({}'W(\overline K/K)\) over \(E\) is a pair
\[
\rho=(\rho',N')
\]
consisting of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item a finite-dimensional representation
\[
\rho':W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow \GL(V)
\]
of \(W(\overline K/K)\) over \(E\);
\item a nilpotent endomorphism \(N'\) of \(V\), such that
\[
\tag{8.4.1.1}
\rho'(w)N'\rho'(w)^{-1}=q^{v'(w)}N'.
\]
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{8.4.2.} Choose a geometric Frobenius \(F\in W(\overline K/K)\) and an isomorphism
\[
\tau:\mathbb Q_\ell(1)\iso\mathbb Q_\ell.
\]
To a \(\lambda\)-adic representation \((V,\rho)\) of \(W(\overline K/K)\) one associates a representation \((\rho',N')\) of \({}'W(\overline K/K)\) on \(E_\lambda\) as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item For \(\sigma\in I\), put, with \(N\) defined by 8.2,
\[
\rho'(F^n\sigma)=\rho(F^n\sigma)\exp(-t_\ell(\sigma)N).
\]
\item \(N'\in\End(V)\) corresponds to \(N\), via \(\tau\).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Lemma 8.4.3.} The isomorphism class of \((\rho',N')\) depends only on \(\rho\), not on the choices of \(F\) and \(\tau\).

Change \(F\) to \(F\varpi\), obtaining \((\rho'',N')\). One has
\[
\rho''(F^n\sigma)=
\exp(n\,t_\ell(\varpi)N)\,\rho'(F^n\sigma)
=
A\,\rho'(F^n\sigma)\,A^{-1},
\]
where
\[
A=\exp((1-q^{-1})^{-1}t_\ell(\varpi)N).
\]
Changing \(\tau\) to \(a\tau\) changes \((\rho',N')\) into \((\rho',aN')\). We shall show that, for every representation \((\rho',N')\) of \({}'W(\overline K/K)\) over a field \(E\), and every \(a\in E^\times\),
\[
(\rho',N')\simeq(\rho',aN').
\]
Let \(I'=\Ker(\rho')\), and choose \(n\) such that \(F^n\) is central in \(W/I'\), for example \(\#\Aut(I/I')\). For \(\alpha\) a conjugacy class in an algebraic closure of \(E\), let \(V^\alpha\) be the largest subspace of \(V\), stable under \(F^n\), such that the eigenvalues of \(F^n|V^\alpha\) lie in \(\alpha\).

One checks:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \(V^\alpha\) is stable under \(\rho'(W(\overline K/K))\), and \(V=\bigoplus V^\alpha\);
\item \(N' V^\alpha\subset V^{q^{-n}\alpha}\).
\end{enumerate}
If \(A\) is the linear transformation preserving the \(V^\alpha\), and such that
\[
A|_{V^\alpha}=\lambda(\alpha),
\]
with
\[
\lambda(q^{-n}\alpha)=a\,\lambda(\alpha),
\]
then
\[
A(\rho',N')A^{-1}=(\rho',aN').
\]
The conclusion 8.3.7 follows at once from 8.4.3.

\paragraph{8.5.} Let \((\rho',N')\) be a representation of \({}'W(\overline K/K)\) over \(E\). Let \(u\) be the unipotent component of \(\rho'(F)\):
\[
\rho'(F)=\rho'(F)^{ss}\,u.
\]
Since \(N'\in\End(V)\) is an eigenvector for conjugation by \(\rho'(F)\), \(u\) and \(N'\) commute. Since a power of \(F\) is central in \(\im(\rho')\), a power of \(u\), and hence \(u\), centralizes \(\im(\rho')\). If
\[
w_1,w_2\in W(\overline K/K)-I,
\]
then \(\rho'(w_1)\) and \(\rho'(w_2)\) have a common power. It follows that \(u^n\) is the unipotent component of \(\rho'(F^n\sigma)\), for \(\sigma\in I\), and, for \(n\ne0\), also that of
\[
\rho'(F^n\sigma)\exp(aN'),
\]
which is conjugate to it by
\[
\exp((1-q^{-n})^{-1}aN').
\]
Put
\[
\tag{8.5.1}
\rho'^{ss}(F^n\sigma)=\rho'(F^n\sigma)u^{-n}.
\]

\paragraph{Definition 8.6.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \((\rho'^{ss},N')\) is the \(F\)-semisimplification of \((\rho',N')\).
\item One says that \((\rho',N')\) is \(F\)-semisimple if \(\rho'=\rho'^{ss}\), i.e. if, for one, respectively for every,
\[
w\in W(\overline K/K)-I,
\]
and for one, respectively for every, \(a\in E\),
\[
\rho'(w)\exp(aN')
\]
is semisimple.
\end{enumerate}
For a \(\lambda\)-adic representation, its \(F\)-semisimplification is again defined by (8.5.1), and \(F\)-semisimplicity as in (ii).

\paragraph{8.7.} The formalism of 8.3 or 8.4 makes it possible to compare \(\lambda\)-adic representations for different fields \(E_\lambda\). To do this, introduce the following classical terminology.

Let \(F\) be an extension of \(E\), and let \(\rho\) be a representation of \({}'W(\overline K/K)\) over \(F\). We say that \(\rho\) is \emph{defined over} \(E\) if, for every, or for one, algebraically closed extension \(\overline F\) of \(F\), \(\rho_{\overline F}\) is isomorphic to its conjugates under \(\Aut(\overline F/E)\).

Let \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) be two extensions of \(E\), and let \(\rho_i\) be a representation of \({}'W(\overline K/K)\) over \(F_i\). We say that \(\rho_1\) and \(\rho_2\) are compatible if they are defined over \(E\), and if, for a common algebraically closed extension
\[
F\supset F_1,F_2\supset E,
\]
the representations \((\rho_1)_F\) and \((\rho_2)_F\) are isomorphic.

\paragraph{Definition 8.8.} Let $E$ be a number field, let $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ be two finite places of $E$ not dividing $p$, and let $\rho_1,\rho_2$ be $\lambda_1$- and $\lambda_2$-adic representations of $W(\overline K/K)$. We say that $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ are compatible if the corresponding representations of ${}'W(\overline K/K)$ are compatible in the sense of 8.7.

We leave to the reader the verification of the following pleasant exercise.

\paragraph{Proposition 8.9.} Suppose that $(V_1,\rho_1)$ and $(V_2,\rho_2)$ are $F$-semisimple, and write $N_i$ for the monodromy operator attached to $(V_i,\rho_i)$. Let $W$ be the unique increasing finite filtration of $V_i$ such that
\[
N_i W^k\subset W^{k-2}
\]
and such that $N_i^j$ induces an isomorphism
\[
\Gr_W^j(V_i)\xrightarrow{\;N_i^j\;}\Gr_W^{-j}(V_i).
\]
For $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ to be compatible, it is necessary and sufficient that the characters of the representations of $W(\overline K/K)$ on the $\Gr_W^j(V_1)$ and $\Gr_W^j(V_2)$ have values in $E$ and be respectively equal.

\paragraph{Example 8.10.} Take $E=\Q$. Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $K$. The representations of
\[
W(\overline K/K)\subset\Gal(\overline K/K)
\]
on
\[
V_\ell(A)=T_\ell(A)\otimes\Q_\ell
\]
are $F$-semisimple and compatible.

\paragraph{Variant 8.11.} Let $G$ be a linear algebraic group over $E$, of characteristic $0$. A representation of ${}'W$ in $G$, over $E$, is a pair $(\rho',N')$ with
\[
\rho':W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow G(E)
\]
trivial on an open subgroup, and $N'\in\Lie(G)$, satisfying (8.4.1.1). For $E=E_\lambda$, the formulas (8.4.2) associate a representation of ${}'W$ in $G$ to every representation, continuous for the $\lambda$-adic topology,
\[
\rho:W\longrightarrow G(E_\lambda).
\]
Its geometric isomorphism class, after extension of scalars to $\overline E_\lambda$, does not depend on the arbitrary choices made: the first part of the proof of 8.4.3 still applies; it remains to show that
\[
(\rho',N')\simeq(\rho',aN').
\]
For this, note that the algebraic subgroup $H$ of $G$ which centralizes $\im(\rho')$ and sends $N'$ to a multiple contains an element $\rho'(F^m)$, hence an element $g$ such that
\[
\ad(g)(N')=q^mN'.
\]
The character $\chi:H\to\mathbb G_m$ giving the action on $N'$ can therefore only be surjective.

If $G$ is defined over a number field $E$, one defines compatibility of $\lambda$-adic representations
\[
W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow G(E_\lambda)
\]
as in 8.7 and 8.8. The formal discussion 8.5, 8.6 generalizes unchanged.

\paragraph{8.12. Local factors.} Let $(\rho',N')$ be a representation of ${}'W(\overline K/K)$ on an $E$-vector space $V$. By abuse of notation put
\[
V^I=\Ker(N')^{\rho'(I)};
\]
this is a representation of $W(\overline k/k)$. Define a conductor, a local $L$-factor, and local constants by
\[
\tag{8.12.1}
a(V)=sw(V,\rho')+\dim V-\dim V^I,
\]
\[
\tag{8.12.2}
Z(V,t)=\det(1-Ft^d,V^I)^{-1},
\]
\[
\tag{8.12.3}
\varepsilon_0(V,t)=\varepsilon_0(\rho',t),
\]
\[
\tag{8.12.4}
\varepsilon(V,t)=\varepsilon_0(\rho',t)\,\det(-Ft,V^I)^{-1}.
\]
If $(\rho',N')$ is defined by a $\lambda$-adic representation $\rho$ on $V$, then
\[
V^I=V^{\rho(I)},
\qquad
Z(V,t)=\det(1-Ft,V^{\rho(I)})^{-1},
\]
and
\[
\varepsilon_0(V,t)=\varepsilon_0(\text{the semisimplification of }V,t).
\]

\paragraph{The archimedean case.} In the nonarchimedean case, what geometry supplies, namely $\ell$-adic cohomology, are $\lambda$-adic representations, i.e. representations of ${}'W(\overline K/K)$, not representations of $W(\overline K/K)$.

In the archimedean case similarly, geometry supplies not representations of $W(\overline K/K)$, but Hodge structures. Here is the rule to use in passing from Hodge structures to representations.

\emph{(A) $K$ complex.} Geometry supplies vector spaces $V$ over $K$, endowed with a Hodge bigrading
\[
V=\bigoplus V^{pq}
\]
(for example, if $X$ is an algebraic variety over $K$, $H^n(X,K)$ has such a decomposition). One deduces a semisimple representation $\rho$ of
\[
W(\overline K/K)=K^*
\]
on $V$ by putting
\[
\rho(z)v^{pq}=z^{-p}\overline z^{-q}v^{pq}\qquad(v^{pq}\in V^{pq}).
\]

\emph{(B) $K$ real.} Geometry supplies vector spaces $V$ over $\overline K$, endowed with a Hodge bigrading
\[
V=\bigoplus V^{pq}
\]
and an involution $F_\infty$ such that
\[
F_\infty(V^{pq})=V^{qp}.
\]
For example, if $X$ is an algebraic variety over $K$, $\Gal(\overline K/K)=\Z/2\Z$ acts on $X(\overline K)$ and $H^n(X(\overline K),\overline K)$ has such a structure. One deduces a semisimple representation $\rho$ of $W(\overline K/K)$ on $V$ by putting
\[
\rho(z)v^{pq}=z^{-p}\overline z^{-q}v^{pq}\qquad(z\in\overline K^*\subset W(\overline K/K),\ v^{pq}\in V^{pq}),
\]
and
\[
\rho(F)v^{pq}=i^{p+q}F_\infty(v^{pq}).
\]
These formulas differ from those of [3], 1.12, moreover disfigured by a misprint on p. 21, 12, because at finite places in loc. cit. I used the inverse of the local class-field-theory isomorphism used here.

At finite places,
\[
H^2(\mathbb P^1,\Q_\ell)\simeq\Q_\ell(1)
\]
corresponds to the quasi-character $\omega_1$. Similarly,
\[
H_2(\mathbb P^1(\overline K),\overline K),
\]
a Hodge structure of type $(-1,-1)$, with $F_\infty=-1$ if $K=\R$, corresponds to the quasi-character $\omega_1$ of $W(\overline K/K)$.

\section*{9. Compatible systems of $\ell$-adic representations}

To simplify the exposition, in this section we consider only representations of Galois groups, rather than representations of Weil groups. As in 7.1, let $X$ be a smooth projective curve over $\F_p$, irreducible over $\F_p$, with function field $K$.

\paragraph{9.1.} Let $E_\lambda$ be a local field, a finite extension of $\Q_\ell$, with $\ell\ne p$. Let $W$ be a $\lambda$-adic representation of $\Gal(\overline K/K)$, i.e. a representation continuous for the topology of $E_\lambda$, almost everywhere unramified, of $\Gal(\overline K/K)$ in a finite-dimensional vector space over $E_\lambda$. Since the Galois group is compact, there exists in $W$ an invariant lattice $W^0$, a lattice being a free $\mathcal O_\lambda$-submodule of $W$ such that $E_\lambda W^0=W$. The local factors
\[
Z_v(W,t)=\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},W_v^{I_v})^{-1}
\]
are therefore in $\mathcal O_\lambda[[t]]$, as is their product $Z(W,t)$. Let $\Gal^0(\overline K/K)$ be the kernel of the map from $\Gal(\overline K/K)$ to $\Gal(\overline{\F}_p/\F_p)$. By a theorem of Grothendieck, $Z(W,t)$ is a rational function; it is even a polynomial if
\[
W^{\Gal^0(\overline K/K)}=W_{\Gal^0(\overline K/K)}=0,
\]
and it satisfies a functional equation
\[
\tag{9.1.1}
Z(W,t)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Gr}}(W,t)Z(W^*\omega_1,t^{-1}),
\]
where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Gr}}$ is a monomial of degree
\[
\sum_v\deg(v)\,a(i_*W_v)
\]
(8.12.1). The reader will find in §10 a summary of Grothendieck's theory.

\paragraph{9.2.} Let $E$ be a number field, let $\LLL$ be an infinite set of finite places of $E$ not dividing $p$, and let $\mathcal O\subset E$ be the ring of elements of $E$ which are $\lambda$-integral for every $\lambda\in\LLL$. We again denote by $\lambda$ the prime ideal of $\mathcal O$ defined by $\lambda$. Suppose given, for every $\lambda\in\LLL$, a $\lambda$-adic representation $V_\lambda$ of $\Gal(\overline K/K)$. Assume that, for every place $v$ of $K$, the $\lambda$-adic representations $V_{\lambda v}$ of $W(\overline K_v/K_v)$ are compatible in the strict sense (8.8). It would in fact suffice to know that their $F$-semisimplifications (8.6) are compatible; cf. also 9.8. The rational function
\[
Z(V_\lambda,t)\in\mathcal O(t)
\]
and the “constant”, i.e. the monomial,
\[
\varepsilon_\lambda(V,t)=\prod_v\varepsilon(V_{\lambda v},\psi_v,dx_v,t)\in\mathcal O(t)
\]
are then independent of $\lambda$; as usual, the $\psi_v$ are the components of a nontrivial character of $\mathbb A/K$, and $\otimes dx_v$ is a Tamagawa measure. Denote this function and this constant by
\[
Z(V,t),\qquad \varepsilon(V,t).
\]

\paragraph{Theorem 9.3.} Under the preceding hypotheses,
\[
\tag{9.3.1}
Z(V,t)=\varepsilon(V,t)Z(V^*,pt^{-1}).
\]
Since $\LLL$ is infinite, the map
\[
\mathcal O\longrightarrow\prod_{\lambda\in\LLL}\mathcal O/\lambda
\]
is injective: it is enough to prove 9.3.1 after reduction modulo $\lambda$, for every $\lambda\in\LLL$. For this we shall put 9.3.1 in a form compatible with reduction modulo $\lambda$, and justified modulo $\lambda$ by (7.11.8).

Let $S$ be a finite set of places of $K$, containing all the places where $V$ ramifies, and let $j$ be the inclusion of $X-S$ in $X$. For every $\lambda$-adic representation $W$, unramified outside $S$, put
\[
Z(j,W,t)=\prod_{v\notin S}\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},W_v)^{-1},
\]
\[
Z(Rj_*W,t)=
\prod_v\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},W_v^{I_v})^{-1}
\Big/
\prod_{v\in S}\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},W_{v,I_v}(-1))^{-1},
\]
\[
\varepsilon(j,W,t)=
\prod_{v\notin S}\varepsilon(W_v,\psi_v,dx_v,t)
\prod_{v\in S}\varepsilon_0(W_v,
\psi_v,dx_v,t).
\]
An easy calculation, already made in 7.11.7, shows that (9.3.1) is equivalent to
\[
\tag{9.3.2}
Z(j,W,t)=\varepsilon(j,W,t)Z(Rj_*W,t).
\]
One concludes by 7.11.6 and 7.11.8.

\paragraph{Stability 9.4.} Let $F$ be an extension of $E$, and let $\LLL_F$ be the inverse image of $\LLL$. A compatible system of $\lambda$-adic representations, $\lambda\in\LLL$, defines by extension of scalars a compatible system of $\lambda$-adic representations, $\lambda\in\LLL_F$. If $\chi$ is a character of the idèle-class group with values in the roots of unity of $F$, the $V_\lambda\chi$ still form a compatible system, to which 9.3 applies.

\paragraph{Corollary 9.5.} Let $\chi$ be a character of the idèle-class group with values in the roots of unity of a finite extension of $E$ (cf. 9.4). Let $S(\chi)$, respectively $S(V)$, be the set of places of $K$ where $\chi$, respectively $V$, ramifies. Suppose
\[
S(V)\cap S(\chi)=\varnothing.
\]
Then
\[
\varepsilon\bigl((V-\dim V[1])([\chi]-[1]),t\bigr)
\]
is, by definition,
\[
\varepsilon(V\chi,t)\varepsilon(V,t)^{-1}
\varepsilon(\chi,t)^{-\dim V}\varepsilon([1],t)^{\dim V}
\]
and therefore
\[
\prod_{v\in S(V)}\chi_v(\pi_v^{a(V_v)})
\cdot
\prod_{v\in S(\chi)}\det V_v(\pi_v^{a(\chi_v)}).
\]
This is a corollary of 5.5.3.

\paragraph{Example 9.6.} For $E=\Q$ and $\LLL$ the set of primes other than $p$, if $F$ is an elliptic curve over $K$, the $H^1(F,\Q_\ell)$ form a compatible system of $\ell$-adic representations. If the modular invariant $j$ is not constant, then, for every character $\chi$ as in 9.5, the corresponding rational functions $Z(F\chi,t)$ are polynomials and satisfy 9.5.

\paragraph{9.7.} We refer to Weil [16] for the relation which 9.5 implies, when $\dim(V)=2$, between $Z(V,t)$ and “modular forms” on $\GL(2,\mathbb A)/\GL(2,K)$. The most interesting case is supplied by 9.6.

More precise results are obtained by invoking Jacquet--Langlands. Let, for example, $E$ be an elliptic curve over $K$, with nonconstant modular invariant. By Langlands' dictionary, see [4], §3, for each place $v$ of $K$, the representation of
\[
\Gal(\overline K_v/K_v)\simeq W(\overline K_v/K_v)
\]
on $H^1(E,\Q_\ell)$ defines an irreducible admissible representation $\pi_v$ of $\GL_2(K_v)$. Since the representations $H^1(E,\Q_\ell)$, for varying $\ell$, are compatible, $\pi_v$ can be defined over $\Q$ and is independent of $\ell$. We shall consider the corresponding complex representation. The representation $\pi_v$ is infinite-dimensional, of weight $2$, i.e. the center $K_v^*$ of $\GL(2,K_v)$ acts by $\omega_2$, and almost always has a $\GL(2,\mathcal O_v)$-invariant vector. By 9.3, 9.6, and [8], 11.5, the restricted tensor product
\[
\pi(E)=\bigotimes_v \pi_v
\]
appears as a direct factor in the admissible representation
\[
L^0_{\omega_2}\bigl(\GL(2,\mathbb A)/\GL(2,K)\bigr)
\]
of $\GL(2,\mathbb A)$ on the space of locally constant functions on $\GL(2,\mathbb A)/\GL(2,K)$ which transform under the center by the quasi-character $\omega_2$, have compact support modulo the center, and are cuspidal.

\paragraph{Theorem 9.8.} Let $\lambda,\mu$ be places of $E$, and let $V_\lambda,V_\mu$ be $\lambda$- and $\mu$-adic representations of $\Gal(\overline K/K)$. Let $S$ be a finite set of places of $K$, and suppose that, for $v\notin S$, the semisimplifications of $V_{\lambda v}$ and $V_{\mu v}$ are compatible. Then, for every place $v$, the semisimplifications of $V_{\lambda v}$ and $V_{\mu v}$ are compatible.

If $S$ is taken large enough to contain all places ramified for $V_\lambda$ or $V_\mu$, the hypothesis reduces to:
\[
(*)\quad
\text{for }v\notin S,\text{ the characteristic polynomials of }F_v
\text{ acting on }V_{\lambda v}\text{ and }V_{\mu v}
\]
\[
\text{belong to }E[t]\text{ and are equal.}
\]
Thus Theorem 9.8 is a partial answer, in the least interesting case of function fields, to question 1 of Serre [12], I, 12.

One should be careful about the following points.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The conclusion concerns only the semisimplifications of the local representations, not their $F$-semisimplifications. In other words, with the notation of 8.4.2, only the semisimplification of $\rho'$ is considered, and not $N$.
\item For $\lambda=\mu$, $V_\lambda=V_\mu$, the theorem asserts that the semisimplifications of the local representations $V_{\lambda v}$ of $W(\overline K_v/K_v)$ are defined over $E$, i.e. have character with values in $E$.
\end{enumerate}
We may, and do, suppose that $S$ contains all places where $I_v$ acts through an infinite group. For $v\notin S$, there is then no longer any distinction between the semisimplification of $V_{\lambda v}$ and its $F$-semisimplification.

Apply Grothendieck's theorem recalled in 9.1:
\[
Z(V_\lambda,t)=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Gr}}(V_\lambda,t)Z(V_\lambda^*\omega_1,t^{-1}).
\]
This identity rewrites as
\[
\prod_{v\in S}Z_v(V_\lambda,t)\prod_{v\in S}Z_v(V_\lambda^*,t^{-1})^{-1}
=
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Gr}}
\frac{\prod_{v\in S}Z_v(V_\lambda^*\omega_1,t^{-1})}
{\prod_{v\in S}Z_v(V_\lambda\omega_1,t)}.
\]
The left-hand side is in $E(T)$, and similarly for $V_\mu$. Hence the right-hand side is in $E(T)$ and independent of $\lambda$. Decomposing the right-hand side as the product of a monomial and of a rational function equal to $1$ at $t=0$, one obtains from the local calculation of 7.11.7 that
\[
\prod_{v\in S}
\frac{\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},V_{\lambda v}^{I_v})}
{\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},V_{\lambda v,I_v}(-1))}
\]
is the same for $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$. The factors of this product are additive in $V_{\lambda v}$, an extension giving a product, by (7.11.6.1); hence they are the same for $V_{\lambda v}$ and for its semisimplification $V_{\lambda v}^{ss}$. For this latter representation, invariants and coinvariants of $I_v$ are the same, and one obtains
\[
\tag{9.8.1}
\prod_{v\in S}
\frac{\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},V_{\lambda v}^{ss,I_v})}
{\det(1-F_v(pt)^{\deg(v)},V_{\lambda v}^{ss,I_v})}
\]
which is the same for $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$.

Let $v_0\in S$, and twist $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$ by a character $\chi$ of the idèle-class group which is unramified at $v_0$ and very ramified at the $v\in S$ other than $v_0$; $\chi$ has values in an extension of $E$, and for existence see 4.14. The hypotheses made on $(V_\lambda,V_\mu)$ are stable under twisting. For suitable $\chi$, after twisting, the factors of 9.8.1 corresponding to $v\ne v_0$ become $1$. The factor corresponding to $v_0$ undergoes a trivial modification, read from $\chi(F_v)$. Put
\[
P_{\lambda v}(T)=\det(1-F_vT,(V_{\lambda v}^{ss})^{I_v}).
\]
Applying the invariance of (9.8.1) to $(V_\lambda\chi,V_\mu\chi)$, one finds that, for every $v\in S$, in $E(T)$,
\[
\tag{9.8.2}
\frac{P_{\lambda v}(T)}{P_{\lambda v}(qT)}=
\frac{P_{\mu v}(T)}{P_{\mu v}(qT)}.
\]
The transformation which sends a rational function $R$, equal to $1$ at $0$, to
\[
R_q(T)=\frac{R(T)}{R(qT)}
\]
is injective: for divisors, one has
\[
\operatorname{div}(R)=\lim_N \operatorname{div}\left(\prod_{0}^{N}R_q(q^iT)\right)
\]
(simple limit). From 9.8.2 one deduces that $P_{\lambda v}(T)\in E[T]$ and that
\[
\tag{9.8.3}
P_{\lambda v}(T)=P_{\mu v}(T).
\]
The hypotheses imposed on $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$ are preserved after replacing $K$ by a finite extension $K'$, replacing $S$ by its inverse image. Since every finite local extension $K'_v/K_v$ is induced by a global extension, because $\Gal(\overline K_v/K_v)$ is a closed subgroup of $\Gal(\overline K/K)$, one finds that 9.8.3 also holds for the representations of $W(\overline K_v/K'_v)$ defined by $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$. Taking for $K'_v$ the various extensions for which the semisimplifications of these representations become unramified, one obtains, for every $F\in W(\overline K_v/K_v)$, $F\notin I_v$,
\[
\det(1-F_vt,V_\lambda)=\det(1-F_vt,V_\mu).
\]
Thus, outside $I_v$, the characters of the representations $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$ coincide.

The same arguments, retaining from 9.8.3 only equality of degrees, show that for every open subgroup $J$ of $I_v$ and every character $\chi$ of $J$,
\[
\dim\bigl((V_{\lambda v}^{ss}\otimes\chi)^J\bigr)
=
\dim\bigl((V_{\mu v}^{ss}\otimes\chi)^J\bigr).
\]
By Brauer's theorem, this implies that
\[
V_{\lambda v}^{ss}|_{I_v}\quad\text{and}\quad V_{\mu v}^{ss}|_{I_v}
\]
have the same character. The characters of $V_\lambda$ and $V_\mu$ therefore coincide on all of $W(\overline K_v/K_v)$, completing the proof.

\paragraph{Proposition 9.9.} Let $(V_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\LLL}$ be an infinite system of $\lambda$-adic representations. Suppose that, for every, or almost every by 9.8, place $v$ of $K$, the semisimplifications of the $V_{\lambda v}$ are compatible. Then, for each $\lambda$,
\[
\tag{9.9.1}
Z_\lambda(V,t)=\varepsilon_\lambda(V,t)Z_\lambda(V^*,pt^{-1}).
\]
Put
\[
Z_\lambda^{ss}(V,t)=
\prod_v\det(1-F_vt^{\deg(v)},(V_{\lambda v}^{ss})^{I_v})^{-1},
\]
\[
\varepsilon_\lambda^{ss}(V,t)=
\prod_v\varepsilon(V_{\lambda v}^{ss},\psi_v,dx_v,t).
\]
A local calculation shows that (9.9.1) is equivalent to
\[
\tag{9.9.2}
Z_\lambda^{ss}(V,t)=
\varepsilon_\lambda^{ss}(V,t)Z_\lambda^{ss}(V^*,pt^{-1}).
\]
The quotient of the local factors at $v$ of the two sides of (9.9.1), or of (9.9.2), is additive in $V_{\lambda v}$, and these quotients coincide in the semisimple case. The identity to be proved, (9.9.2), is independent of $\lambda$. One proves it modulo $\lambda$ for each $\lambda$, and concludes as in 9.3.




\section*{10. Grothendieck's theory}

\paragraph{10.1.} Let \(X\) be a nonsingular projective curve over the finite field \(\F_p\), let \(\overline{\F}_p\) be an algebraic closure of \(\F_p\), and let
\[
\overline X=X\otimes_{\F_p}\overline{\F}_p
\]
be obtained from \(X\) by extension of scalars from \(\F_p\) to \(\overline{\F}_p\). Let \(\ell\) be a prime number prime to \(p\), and let \(\Lambda\) be a noetherian ring killed by a power of \(\ell\).

Let \(G\) be a Weil sheaf, in the sense of 7.3 and 7.4, constructible in flat \(\Lambda\)-modules. We again denote by \(G\) the étale sheaf on \(\overline X\) obtained from it.

\paragraph{10.2.} The étale cohomology groups
\[
H^i(\overline X,G)
\]
are finite-type \(\Lambda\)-modules. Since they need not be projective, they will be of no use to us. We shall have to use a finer object
\[
R\Gamma(\overline X,G)\in\operatorname{Ob}\Dparf(\Lambda)
\]
which gives rise to them. Recall that \(\Dparf(\Lambda)\) may be defined as the category of finite complexes of finite-type projective \(\Lambda\)-modules, the morphisms being morphisms of complexes taken up to homotopy. The object \(R\Gamma(\overline X,G)\) is a finite complex of finite-type projective \(\Lambda\)-modules, concentrated in degrees \(0,1,2\) if desired, well-defined up to homotopy, and whose cohomology groups are the \(H^i(\overline X,G)\).

By transport of structure, the Frobenius substitution
\[
\varphi\in\Gal(\overline{\F}_p/\F_p)
\]
acts on \(R\Gamma(\overline X,G)\): it is an endomorphism of the complex \(R\Gamma(\overline X,G)\), well-determined up to homotopy, and inducing on cohomology the Frobenius automorphism.

\paragraph{10.3.} For \(K\) a finite complex of projective \(\Lambda\)-modules and \(u\) an endomorphism of \(K\), put
\[
\tag{10.3.1}
\det(1-ut,K)=
\prod_i\det(1-ut,K^i)^{(-1)^i}\in\Lambda(t).
\]
If \(u\) and \(v\) are homotopic endomorphisms, then
\[
\det(1-ut,K)=\det(1-vt,K),
\]
so that \(\det(1-ut,K)\) is well-defined for \(K\in\operatorname{Ob}\Dparf(\Lambda)\) and \(u\in\End(K)\). Taking the negative of the coefficient of \(t\) in the formal power-series expansion of \(\det(1-ut,K)\), one defines the trace
\[
\tag{10.3.2}
\Tr(u,K)=\sum_i(-1)^i\Tr(u,K^i).
\]

If \(u\) is a homotopy auto-equivalence, i.e. induces an automorphism on cohomology, one can find \((K',u')\), isomorphic in \(\Dparf(\Lambda)\) to \((K,u)\), with \(u'\) an automorphism of the complex \(K'\). One then puts
\[
\tag{10.3.3}
\det(u,K)=\prod_i\det(u,K^i)^{(-1)^i},
\]
and this quantity is independent of the arbitrary choices made. If \(u^{-1}\) is a homotopy inverse of \(u\), then
\[
\tag{10.3.4}
\det(1-ut,K)=\det(-ut,K)\det(1-u^{-1}t^{-1},K).
\]
If \(D(K)\) is the dual of \(K\), the complex with components
\[
D(K)^i=\Hom(K^{-i},\Lambda),
\]
and if \(\check u\) is a contragredient of \(u\), one also has
\[
\tag{10.3.5}
\det(1-ut,K)=\det(-ut,K)\det(1-\check u\,t^{-1},D(K)).
\]

A sketch of the proof of the following theorem is given in 10.8.

\paragraph{Theorem 10.4 (Grothendieck).} One has
\[
\tag{10.4.1}
Z(G,t)=\det(1-Ft,R\Gamma(\overline X,G))^{-1}.
\]

In applications, the hypotheses imposed on \(G\) are too restrictive. One should take as coefficients an element of \(\Dparf(X,\Lambda)\), i.e. a finite complex \(G^\bullet\) of Weil sheaves of \(\Lambda\)-modules on \(X\), which locally for the étale topology on \(\overline X\) is homotopic to a finite complex of constructible flat \(\Lambda\)-module sheaves. Using 10.3, one can define, for \(x\) a closed point of \(X\),
\[
Z_x(G^\bullet,t)=
\det(1-F_x t^{\deg(x)},G_x^\bullet)^{-1}.
\]
Put
\[
Z(G^\bullet,t)=\prod_x Z_x(G^\bullet,t).
\]
One also defines \(R\Gamma(\overline X,G^\bullet)\), whose cohomology groups are the hypercohomology groups of \(\overline X\) with values in \(G^\bullet\), and one has
\[
\tag{10.4.2}
Z(G^\bullet,t)=
\det(1-Ft,R\Gamma(\overline X,G^\bullet))^{-1}.
\]

\paragraph{10.5.} Every \(G^\bullet\in\Dparf(X,\Lambda)\) has a dual with values in the dualizing complex \(\Lambda(1)\), placed in degree \(-2\):
\[
D(G^\bullet)=R\Hom_\Lambda(G^\bullet,\Lambda(1)[2])\in\Dparf(X,\Lambda).
\]
Its \(i\)-th cohomology sheaf is the \((2+i)\)-th local hyper-Ext of \(G^\bullet\) with \(\Lambda(1)\). Poincaré duality takes the form
\[
\tag{10.5.1}
R\Gamma(\overline X,D(G^\bullet))=D(R\Gamma(\overline X,G^\bullet)).
\]
Applying 10.3.5, one obtains the functional equation
\[
\tag{10.5.2}
Z(G,t)=
\det(-Ft,R\Gamma(\overline X,G))^{-1}\,
Z(D(G),t^{-1}).
\]

\paragraph{10.6.} Let
\[
j:U\hookrightarrow X
\]
be an open subset of \(X\), the complement of a finite set \(S\) of closed points, and let \(G^\bullet\in\Dparf(U,\Lambda)\). Write
\[
j_!:\Dparf(U,\Lambda)\longrightarrow\Dparf(X,\Lambda)
\]
for the derived functor of the exact functor extension by zero, and
\[
Rj_*:\Dparf(U,\Lambda)\longrightarrow\Dparf(X,\Lambda)
\]
for the derived functor of the direct-image functor under \(j\). The local duality theorem, proved in particular in dimension one, gives
\[
\tag{10.6.1}
D(j_!G^\bullet)=Rj_*D(G^\bullet).
\]
For \(G\) a locally constant sheaf of projective \(\Lambda\)-modules on \(U\), with dual \(\check G\), one has
\[
D(G)=\check G(1)[2],
\]
and (10.5.2) takes the form
\[
\tag{10.6.2}
Z(j_!G,t)=
\det(-Ft,R\Gamma(\overline X,j_!G))^{-1}
Z(Rj_*\check G(1),t^{-1}).
\]

\paragraph{10.7.} We shall make this formula explicit. The \(Z\)-functions on both sides are products of local factors. For the left-hand side, the local factor at a closed point \(x\) of \(X\) is
\[
Z_x(j_!G,t)=
\begin{cases}
\det(1-F_x t^{\deg(x)},G_x)^{-1},&x\notin S,\\
1,&x\in S.
\end{cases}
\]
For the right-hand side it is
\[
Z_x(Rj_*\check G(1),t)=
\begin{cases}
\det(1-F_x t^{\deg(x)},\check G_x(1))^{-1},&x\notin S,\\
\det(1-F_x t^{\deg(x)},R\Gamma(I_x,\check G_K(1)))^{-1},&x\in S.
\end{cases}
\]
The last formula is read as follows: \(\check G_K(1)\) defines a representation of \(W(\overline K_x/K_x)\), to which one applies the derived functor of the functor of invariants under \(I\). One thereby obtains a complex of projective \(\Lambda\)-modules on which Frobenius acts, and one performs construction 10.3. Explicitly:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The functor of invariants under \(I\) is the composite of the functor of invariants under
\[
P'_\ell=\Ker(t_\ell):
\quad I\text{-modules}\longrightarrow I/P'_\ell\text{-modules},
\]
and the functor of invariants under \(\Z_\ell(1)\). The first functor is exact and transforms a projective \(\Lambda\)-module into a projective \(\Lambda\)-module. For every \(I\)-module \(V\), one has
\[
R\Gamma(I,V)=R\Gamma(\Z_\ell(1),V^{P'_\ell}).
\]
For every \(\Z_\ell(1)\)-module \(W\), \(R\Gamma(\Z_\ell(1),W)\) may be computed by taking a resolution of \(W\) by coinduced modules
\[
\mathfrak F(\Z_\ell(1),M),
\]
the module of locally constant functions from \(\Z_\ell(1)\) to \(M\), on which \(\Z_\ell(1)\) acts by \(f(x)\mapsto f(\tau x)\). For \(\sigma\) a generator of \(\Z_\ell(1)\), one has an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow\mathfrak F(\Z_\ell(1),W)
\xrightarrow{\alpha_\sigma}
\mathfrak F(\Z_\ell(1),W)\longrightarrow0,
\]
with maps \(m\mapsto(i\mapsto im)\) and
\[
f\longmapsto(i\mapsto f(\sigma i)-\sigma(f(i))).
\]
Passing to invariants, one identifies \(R\Gamma(\Z_\ell(1),W)\) with the complex
\[
\tag{10.7.1}
W\xrightarrow{\,1-\sigma\,}W.
\]

When \(\sigma\) is replaced by \(\sigma^\nu\), \(\nu\in\Z_\ell^*\), the unique commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.5em,row sep=2.5em]
\mathfrak F(\Z_\ell(1),W)\arrow[r,"\alpha_\sigma"]\arrow[d,equal] &
\mathfrak F(\Z_\ell(1),W)\arrow[r]\arrow[d,"\beta"]&0\\
\mathfrak F(\Z_\ell(1),W)\arrow[r,"\alpha_{\sigma^\nu}"']&
\mathfrak F(\Z_\ell(1),W)\arrow[r]&0
\end{tikzcd}
\]
gives, after passing to invariants,
\[
\tag{10.7.2}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.8em,row sep=2.6em]
W\arrow[r,"1-\sigma"]\arrow[d,"1"']&
W\arrow[d,"\frac{1-\sigma^\nu}{1-\sigma}"]\\
W\arrow[r,"1-\sigma^\nu"']&W ,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where, for \(n\) an integer converging in \(\Z_\ell\) to \(\nu\),
\[
\tag{10.7.3}
\frac{1-\sigma^\nu}{1-\sigma}\,w
=
\lim_n\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^iw.
\]
It follows that \(R\Gamma(\Z_\ell(1),W)\) may be represented by any of the complexes (10.7.1), these complexes, as \(\sigma\) varies, being identified by the isomorphisms (10.7.2).

Starting from a \(W(\overline K/K)\)-module \(V\), Frobenius acts by transport of structure on
\[
R\Gamma(I,V)=
\bigl[V^{P'_\ell}\xrightarrow{\,1-\sigma\,}V^{P'_\ell}\bigr].
\]
Whatever the geometric Frobenius \(F\), and whatever the generator \(\sigma\) of \(\Z_\ell(1)\), \(F\) defines
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=4.0em,row sep=2.6em]
V^{P'_\ell}\arrow[r,"1-\sigma"]\arrow[d,"F"']&
V^{P'_\ell}\arrow[d,"F"]\\
V^{P'_\ell}\arrow[r,"1-\sigma^q"']&
V^{P'_\ell},
\end{tikzcd}
\]
which, via (10.7.2), is identified with the automorphism of the complex
\[
\bigl[V^{P'_\ell}\xrightarrow{\,1-\sigma\,}V^{P'_\ell}\bigr]
\]
with components
\[
\left(F,\ \frac{1-\sigma}{1-\sigma^q}\circ F\right),
\]
where
\[
\frac{1-\sigma}{1-\sigma^q}
=
\frac{1-(\sigma^q)^{1/q}}{1-\sigma^q}
\]
is defined as in (10.7.3). Hence
\[
\det(1-Ft,R\Gamma(I,V))
=
\det(1-Ft,V^{P'_\ell})\,
\det\!\left(1-\frac{1-\sigma}{1-\sigma^q}\circ Ft,V^{P'_\ell}\right)^{-1},
\]
where \(F\) denotes any geometric Frobenius and \(\sigma\) any generator of \(\Z_\ell(1)\).

Formula 10.6.2 is rewritten as
\[
\tag{10.7.4}
\begin{aligned}
\prod_{x\notin S}\det(1-F_xt^{\deg(x)},G_x)
&=\det(-Ft,R\Gamma(j_!G))^{-1}\\
&\quad\cdot
\prod_{x\notin S}\det(1-F_xt^{-\deg(x)},\check G_x(1))^{-1}\\
&\quad\cdot
\prod_{x\in S}\Biggl[
\det(1-F_xt^{-\deg(x)},\check G_x^{P'_\ell}(1))^{-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad\cdot
\det\!\left(1-\frac{1-\sigma}{1-\sigma^q}F_xt^{-\deg(x)},\check G_x^{P'_\ell}(1)\right)
\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
\]

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{10.8.} Passing to the projective limit, the preceding results generalize to \(\ell\)-adic, or \(\lambda\)-adic, sheaves, and one obtains the statements of 9.1. The reason, cf. 10.5.2, for the simple formula (9.1.1) is the local result that, for \(G\) locally constant on
\[
U\xrightarrow{j}X,
\]
one has
\[
j_*D(G)=D(j_!G).
\]
However, the formula obtained for the constant, the determinant of \(-Ft\) acting on cohomology, is not very explicit, and there is no local theory for it.

\paragraph{Remark 10.9.} Let \(X\) be a nonsingular projective curve over an algebraically closed field \(k\), and let \(V\) be a locally constant sheaf of free \(\Lambda\)-modules on \(X_{\mathrm{\acute et}}\). For simplicity, assume \(X\) connected of genus \(g\ge1\) and \(\Lambda\) local. Here are two methods for attaching to \(V\) a free \(\Lambda\)-module of rank \(1\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Write \(R\Gamma(X,V)\) as a finite complex \(K\) of free \(\Lambda\)-modules. Then
\[
\det(R\Gamma(X,V))=
\bigotimes_i \det(K^i)^{(-1)^i},
\]
where \(\det\) denotes the maximal exterior power, depends, up to canonical isomorphism, only on \(R\Gamma(X,V)\). We write \(h(V)\) for its dual.

\item Let
\[
K=\sum_i n_iP_i
\]
be a canonical divisor, \(\mathcal O(K)\simeq\Omega_X^1\), and put
\[
N_K(V)=\bigotimes_i \det(V_{P_i})^{\otimes n_i}.
\]
The set of positive canonical divisors forms a projective space, and the \(N_K(V)\) form a necessarily trivial local system on this projective space. Thus the \(N_K(V)\), as \(K\) varies, are canonically isomorphic, and one puts
\[
\langle\Omega^1,V\rangle=N_K(V).
\]
\end{enumerate}

If \((X,V)\) is defined over \(k_0\subset k\), i.e. is obtained by extension of scalars from \((X_0,V_0)\) over \(\Spec(k_0)\), then \(\Gal(k/k_0)\) acts on \(h(V)\) and on \(\langle\Omega^1,V\rangle\) by characters \(\chi_h(V)\) and \(\chi_{\Omega^1}(V)\), with values in \(\Lambda^*\). These constructions generalize to the \(\lambda\)-adic case.

\paragraph{Proposition 10.9.1.} Assume that one of the following conditions is satisfied:
\[
\tag{a}
\Lambda=\Q_\lambda\quad\text{and }V\text{ becomes trivial on a finite cover of }X;
\]
\[
\tag{b}
V\text{ has rank }1.
\]
Then
\[
\tag{10.9.1}
\chi_h(V)=\chi_{\Omega^1}(V)\,\omega_{(1-g)\dim(V)},
\]
where \(\omega_n\) is the character giving the Galois action on \(\Z_\ell(n)\).

A standard argument using Chebotarev reduces us to the case where \(k_0\) is a finite field and \(k\) is its algebraic closure. One has to show that \(\chi_h\) and \(\chi_{\Omega^1}\omega_{(1-g)\dim V}\) take the same value on Frobenius. In case (a), the Weil group \(W(\overline K_0/K_0)\) of \(X_0\) acts continuously, for the discrete topology, on \(V_{\overline K_0}\), so that 9.3 can be applied.

Compare (9.3) with (10.7.4), for \(S=\varnothing\). These are functional equations relating the same \(Z\)-functions. Expressing the fact that they have the same constant gives (10.9.1). It may be more convenient to make this calculation only for virtual \(V\) of dimension \(0\), and to verify (10.9.1) directly for \(V=\Z_\ell\). In case (b), one proceeds in the same way using 10.12.1 below.

It would be very interesting to make (10.9.1) more precise by defining a canonical isomorphism between
\[
h(V)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\langle\Omega^1,V\rangle\otimes \Z_\ell((1-g)\dim(V)),
\]
and to generalize (10.9.1) to the ramified case.

\paragraph{Remark 10.10.} Theorem 10.4 is probably still valid for \(\Lambda\) a noetherian ring of characteristic \(p\), i.e. with \(p\Lambda=0\). The required trace formula should follow from the trace formula in coherent cohomology, the Woods Hole trace formula. The proof has not, however, been written down.

\paragraph{10.11. Proof of 10.4.} In [7], 10.4 is proved only for \(\ell\)-adic sheaves, because the method of proof, based on the Lefschetz trace formula on \(\overline X\) for the powers of the Frobenius endomorphism, required division by an arbitrary integer to pass from Lefschetz to (10.4.1), through the logarithmic derivative of \(Z\).

To obtain 10.4 as stated, one must use SGA 4, XVII, 5.5, the Lefschetz trace formula on the \(\Sym^n(X)\), and expand both sides of 10.4.1 as series, the second expanding as
\[
\det(1-Ft,R\Gamma(\overline X,G))^{-1}
=
\sum_n \Tr(F,\Sym^n(R\Gamma(\overline X,G)))\,t^n,
\]
where \(\Sym^n\) is the derived functor of the nonadditive functor \(n\)-th symmetric power. Moreover, the method of proof of [7], by successive fibrations by curves, reduces the trace formula to be proved to the case of curves.

\paragraph{Trace formula 10.11.1.} Let \(X\) be a projective smooth curve over \(\F_q\), let \(U\) be an open subset of \(X\), and let \(G\) be a locally constant Weil sheaf of projective \(\Lambda\)-modules. One has
\[
\sum_{x\in U(\F_q)}\Tr(F_x,G_x)
=
\Tr(F,R\Gamma(\overline X,j_!G)).
\]
In this formula, the Frobenii are relative to \(\F_q\), and
\[
\overline X=X\otimes_{\F_q}\overline{\F}_q.
\]
One may suppose that \(\F_q\) is the field of constants of the function field \(K\) of \(X\); otherwise both sides are \(0\).

The method of proof presented by Grothendieck in the oral seminar SGA 5 consists in reducing to the Lefschetz formula for the number of fixed points of endomorphisms of complete curves, already proved in [17], by studying the cohomology of covers of \(\overline X\) as representations of the group of the cover.

Let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic closure of \(K\), containing the function field of \(\overline X\). The sheaf \(G\) defines a representation of \(W(\overline K/K)\) on the projective \(\Lambda\)-module \(G_{\overline K}\). The intersection \(J\) of \(\Gal^0(\overline K/K)\) and the kernel of this representation defines a connected étale cover
\[
f:\overline U'\longrightarrow \overline U
\]
of \(\overline U\) on which \(G\) becomes trivial. Put
\[
H=W(\overline K/K)/J.
\]
Then \(H\) is an extension of \(\Z\) by the group \(H_0\) of the cover \(\overline U'/\overline U\), and \(G_{\overline K}\) is a representation of \(H\).

Let \(j'\) be the inclusion of \(\overline U'\) in a nonsingular projective curve \(\overline X'\). The \(\ell\)-adic cohomology with proper support of \(\overline U'\) is a representation of \(H\). Standard arguments allow one to do better, and to define
\[
R\Gamma_c(\overline U',\Z_\ell)
=
R\Gamma(\overline X',j'_!\Z_\ell)
\in\Dparf(\Z_\ell[H_0]),
\]
on which \(H\) acts semilinearly with respect to its action on \(\Z_\ell[H_0]\), the action of \(H_0\subset H\) being the evident action. There is a canonical isomorphism
\[
\tag{10.11.2}
R\Gamma(\overline X,j_!G)
=
\bigl[
R\Gamma(\overline X',j'_!\Z_\ell)
\otimes_{\Z_\ell}G_{\overline K}
\bigr]^{H_0},
\]
compatible with the Frobenius action on both sides.

Write \(R\Gamma(\overline X',j'_!\Z_\ell)\) as a finite complex of projective \(\Z_\ell[H_0]\)-modules on which Frobenius acts. The components of this complex appear as \(\Z_\ell[H^+]\)-modules, for \(H^+\) the submonoid of \(H\), extension of \(\mathbb N\subset\Z\) by \(H_0\). We apply the following lemma to them.

\paragraph{Lemma 10.11.3.} Let \(H^+\) be a semidirect product of \(\mathbb N\) by a finite group \(H_0\), let \(M\) be a \(\Z_\ell[H^+]\)-module, projective of finite rank as a \(\Z_\ell[H_0]\)-module, let \(\Lambda\) be a \(\Z_\ell\)-algebra, and let \(V\) be a \(\Lambda[H^+]\)-module, projective of finite rank as a \(\Lambda\)-module. Let \(\chi_M\) and \(\chi_V\) be the characters of \(M\) and \(V\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There exists a function \(\chi_M^*\) on \(H\), with values in \(\Z_\ell\), such that, for \(h\in H\), and \(H_0(h)\) the centralizer of \(h\) in \(H_0\), one has
\[
\chi_M(h)=\Tr(h,M)=|H_0(h)|\,\chi_M^*(h).
\]
\item For \(F\in H/H_0\),
\[
\Tr\bigl(F,(M\otimes_{\Z_\ell}V)^{H_0}\bigr)
=
\sum_{\substack{h\mapsto F\\ \mathrm{mod}\ H_0\text{-conjugacy}}}
\chi_M^*(h)\chi_V(h).
\]
\end{enumerate}

Applying Lefschetz, one computes \(\chi_M^*\) for \(R\Gamma(\overline X',j'_!\Z_\ell)\), virtually the difference between \(R\Gamma(\overline X',\Z_\ell)\) and an elementary quantity; 10.11.2 and 10.11.3 provide the desired formula.




\paragraph{10.12. The abelian case, and a fanciful application.} Let
\[
\chi:W(\overline K/K)\longrightarrow \Lambda^*
\]
be a quasi-character with values in \(\Lambda^*\), unramified outside a finite set \(S\) of places of \(K\). Let \(j\) be the inclusion of \(U=X-S\) in \(X\), and let \(j_![\chi]\) be the rank-one sheaf on \(U\) defined by \(\chi\), extended by \(0\). Put
\[
\varepsilon(j_![\chi],t)=
\prod_{v\in S}\varepsilon_0(\chi_v\omega_v^t,\psi_v,dx_v)
\prod_{v\notin S}\varepsilon(\chi_v\omega_v^t,\psi_v,dx_v).
\]
Here \(\omega^t\) is as in 7.6, \(\varepsilon_0\) is defined in 6.4, and \(\varepsilon\), for an unramified character, is defined by 3.4.3.3.

\paragraph{Proposition 10.12.1.} One has
\[
\det(-Ft,\RGamma(j_![\chi]))=\varepsilon(j_![\chi],t).
\]
This proposition makes 10.7.4 more precise.

Let \(H\) be the quotient of \(W(\overline K/K)\) by \(\Ker(\chi)\). It is enough to treat the universal case where
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n[H].
\]
This case lifts to characteristic \(0\), and 10.12.1 is obtained by reduction.

\paragraph{10.13.} Take for example, for \(\Lambda\), the dual numbers over \(\mathbb Z/\ell^n\):
\[
\Lambda=\mathbb Z/\ell^n[\epsilon]\qquad(\epsilon^2=0),
\]
let \(\alpha\) be a continuous homomorphism from the idèle-class group \(\bbA^*/K^*\) into the additive group \(\mathbb Z/\ell^n\), and put
\[
\chi=1+\alpha\epsilon.
\]
The ramification of \(\alpha\) is automatically tame. Let \(S\) be a finite set of places outside which \(\alpha\) is unramified. Applying 10.4 to \(\chi\) and to the trivial character, and taking the ratio, one finds that
\[
\tag{10.13.1}
L_S^+(\alpha,t)=
\sum_{v\in S}\alpha_v(\pi_v)\frac{t^{\deg(v)}}{1-t^{\deg(v)}}
=
\sum_n t^n\sum_{\deg(v)\mid n}\alpha_v(\pi_v)
\]
lies in \(\mathbb Z/\ell^n(t)\).

For every place \(v\) of \(K\), choose a uniformizer \(\pi_v\). Adjoin to \(\mathbb Z/\ell^n\) a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity, and let \(\psi\) be a nontrivial additive character of \(\bbA/K\), with values in the \(p\)-th roots of unity. Take the quotient of (10.7.4) for \(\chi\) and of (10.7.4) for the trivial character, and use (10.12.1). After simplification, one obtains the following identity. In the left-hand side, the first two infinite sums are summed by (10.13.1). All sums run over all places of \(K\):
\[
\tag{10.13.2}
\sum_v\alpha_v(\pi_v)\frac{t^{\deg(v)}}{1-t^{\deg(v)}}
+
\sum_v\alpha_v(\pi_v)\frac{q_vt^{-\deg(v)}}{1-q_vt^{-\deg(v)}}
+
\sum_v\alpha_v(-1)\frac{1}{1-t^{\deg(v)}}
\]
\[
=
-\sum_v n_v\alpha_v(\pi_v)
-\sum_v\sum_{x\in k_v^*}\alpha_v(x)\psi_v(\pi_v^{1+n_v}x).
\]
One checks elementarily that the validity of (10.13.2) does not depend on the choice of the uniformizers \(\pi_v\), recalling that, for \(x\in k_v^*\), one has \((q-1)\alpha(x)=0\). In the left-hand side, the \(\alpha_v(-1)\) are of order \(2\), hence vanish if \(\ell\ne2\).

\section*{11. Appendix. The calculation of \(\varepsilon\) modulo roots of unity}

This appendix is a variation on a theme of Lakkis and Dwork. My attention was drawn to their results by J.-P. Serre.

We shall write \(a\sim b\) for the following relation between invertible complex numbers:
\[
a\sim b\quad:\quad a/b\text{ is the product of a root of unity and a power of }q^{1/2}.
\]
Let \(K\) be a nonarchimedean local field, and resume the notation of 2.2.1, 2.2.2, and 3.1. The Haar measure \(dx\) is assumed to satisfy
\[
\int_{\OO}dx=q^{n/2},
\]
with \(n\) an integer. Let \(V\) be a complex representation, of a finite quotient, of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\), and let \(V^P\) denote the invariants under wild inertia. This is a subrepresentation of \(V\).

\paragraph{Theorem 11.1.} One has
\[
\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\sim\varepsilon(V^P,\psi,dx).
\]

\paragraph{Lemma 11.2.} Modulo the equivalence \(\sim\), \(\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\) is independent of \(\psi\) and \(dx\). This follows immediately from 5.3 and 5.4. This lemma will allow us to abbreviate \(\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\) to \(\varepsilon(V)\).

\paragraph{Lemma 11.3.} If the character of \(V\) is real, i.e. if \(V\) is isomorphic to \(V^*\), then
\[
\varepsilon(V)\sim1.
\]
One may suppose that \(\int_{\OO}dx=1\) and that \(n(\psi)=0\). One then applies 5.7.1, 5.4, and 5.5.

Let \(L\) be a finite extension of \(K\) in \(\overline K\), let \(W\) be a representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/L)\), and let \(\Ind(W)\) be the representation induced to \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\).

\paragraph{Lemma 11.4.} One has
\[
\varepsilon_L(W)\sim\varepsilon_K(\Ind(W)).
\]
Let \(W'\) be the trivial representation of the same dimension as \(W\). For \(\psi_L=\psi_K\circ\Tr_{L/K}\), (5.6.1) gives
\[
\varepsilon(W-W',\psi_L)=\varepsilon(\Ind(W)-\Ind(W'),\psi_K),
\]
and one concludes by applying 11.3 to \(W'\) and to \(\Ind(W')\).

\paragraph{11.5.} Let \(L\) and \(W\) be as above, let \(P_L\) be the wild inertia group of \(L\), and regard \(W^{P_L}\) as a representation of
\[
H=P\cdot\Gal(\overline K/L),
\]
trivial on \(P\). One has
\[
\Ind(W)^P=\Ind_H^{\Gal(\overline K/K)}(W^{P_L}).
\]
By 11.4, the theorem for \(W\) is therefore equivalent to the theorem for \(\Ind(W)\). By Brauer, this reduces us to the case where \(V\) has dimension one, defined by a finite-order character \(\chi\) of \(K^*\). If \(\chi\) is tame, \([\chi]^P=[\chi]\), and 11.1 is trivial. If \(\chi\) is wildly ramified, \([\chi]^P=0\), and 11.1 follows from the next lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma 11.6.} For \(\chi\) wildly ramified,
\[
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)\sim1.
\]
Let \(m\) be the conductor of \(\chi\), put
\[
n=\left[\frac{m+1}{2}\right],
\]
and let \(y\in K^*\) have valuation \(-m-n(\psi)\), well-defined modulo \(\pi^{n-m-n(\psi)}\), such that, cf. 4.16,
\[
\chi(1+a)=\psi(ay)\qquad(v(a)\ge n).
\]
Decompose \(\chi\) as \(\chi=\chi_1\chi_2\), where \(\chi_1\) has order prime to \(p\) and \(\chi_2\) has order a power of \(p\). As in 4.16, one proves that
\[
\tag{11.6.1}
\varepsilon(\chi,\psi,dx)
=
\int \chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx
=
\int_{y(1+(\pi^{m-n}))}\chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx
\]
\[
=
\chi_1(y)
\int_{y(1+(\pi^{m-n}))}\chi_2^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\,dx.
\]
If \(m\) is even, \(m=2n\), the integrand is constant and 11.6 is clear. For \(m\) odd, \(m=2n-1\), there are two ways to proceed.

(a) \(\chi^{-1}(x)\psi(x)\) is a nondegenerate quadratic character on
\[
y(1+(\pi^{m-n}))/(1+(\pi^n));
\]
by Weil, the sum of its values is \(\sim1\).

(b) By (11.6.1), it is enough to prove that \(\varepsilon(\chi_2,\psi,dx)\sim1\). The square of this number, multiplied by a suitable power of \(q\), belongs to a field of \(p^N\)-th roots of unity, and has valuation \(1\) at every place of this field, except possibly at the unique place dividing \(p\). It is therefore a root of unity.

\paragraph{Corollary 11.7.} Take \(\psi\) and \(dx\) such that \(n(\psi)=-1\) and
\[
\int_{(\pi)}dx=1.
\]
Then \(\varepsilon(V,\psi,dx)\) is an algebraic integer.

Decomposing \(V\) as \(V^P+W\), one sees that it is enough to treat the following two cases.

(a) \(V\) is tamely ramified. In this case, \(V\) is a sum of representations induced from one-dimensional representations of subgroups, and \(\varepsilon\) is, up to sign, a product of Gauss sums, cf. 5.10.

(b) \(V^P=0\). In this case, \(\varepsilon\sim1\), and it is enough to prove that the complex absolute value \(|\varepsilon|\) is a positive power of \(q\). This follows from 5.7.2, where \(dx'/dx=q\).
