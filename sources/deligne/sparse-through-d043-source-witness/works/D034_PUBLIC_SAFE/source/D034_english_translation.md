\vspace{1em}
We show here how a suitable theory of ``motives'' makes it possible to recover certain results of Shimura on the periods of abelian varieties of CM type [5]. It seemed instructive to begin with an informal description of the properties of motives and of their realizations; this is done in Section 1. Section 2 interprets certain results of Shimura by means of motives. Section 3 defines absolute Hodge cycles and the theory of motives subordinate to them. Section 4 gives an upper bound for the transcendence degree of the field generated by the periods of an arbitrary abelian variety.

\vspace{0.8em}
\noindent\textbf{\S\ 1. \quad \underline{THE LANGUAGE OF MOTIVES}}

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$. There are several definitions of ``motives over $k$'', which are not known to be equivalent, each having its own

<!-- END_PAGE_RECORD physical=2 printed=23 -->

<!-- BEGIN_PAGE_RECORD physical=3 article=2 printed=24 -->
## Source page: physical 3; printed 24

virtues. In Section 3 we shall specify which one is useful to us.

The motives over $k$ form an additive category equipped with a tensor product. Every smooth projective variety $X$ over $k$ defines a motive $h(X)$ over $k$, the \underline{motivic cohomology} of $X$, and every motive over $k$ is obtained from a motive of this form (by cutting it into pieces and by Tate twisting). Finally, the various cohomology theories for algebraic varieties give rise to ``realization'' functors for motives.

Every abelian variety $A$ over $k$ defines a motive $T(A)$ over $k$, its motivic $H_1$, and the functor $A\mapsto T(A)$ is fully faithful. The advantage of embedding the category of abelian varieties in that of motives is that one can take tensor products. But the objects obtained are not geometric: there are no ``functions'' on a motive.

The \underline{rank} of a motive is the dimension of any one of its realizations, which we now describe.

If $k=\C$, the \underline{Betti realization} of a motive $M$ is a free abelian group of finite rank $H_B(M)$, endowed with a \underline{Hodge structure}, that is, with a decomposition
\[
H_B(M)\otimes_{\Z}\C=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}H^{p,q}\qquad(\text{with }\overline{H^{p,q}}=H^{q,p}).
\]
Let us illustrate this with the motive $T(A)$, which is the $H_1$ of an abelian variety $A$. We define $H_B(T(A))=H_1(A,\Z)$, the homology of $A=A(\C)$ viewed as a topological space.

Let $\operatorname{Lie}(A)$ be the Lie algebra of the analytic group $A$, and let $\int:H_1(A,\Z)\to\operatorname{Lie}(A)$ be the homomorphism ``integration over a 1-cycle''. There is an exact sequence of complex analytic groups
\[
0\longrightarrow H_1(A,\Z)\xrightarrow{\int}\operatorname{Lie}(A)\xrightarrow{\exp}A\longrightarrow0.
\]
The Hodge structure on $H_1(A,\C)$ is such that $H_1(A,\C)=H^{0,-1}\oplus H^{-1,0}$. One obtains $H^{0,-1}$ as the kernel of the map $H_1(A,\C)\to\operatorname{Lie}(A)$, the $\C$-linear extension of

<!-- BEGIN_PAGE_RECORD physical=4 article=3 printed=25 -->
## Source page: physical 4; printed 25

$\int:H_1(A,\Z)\to\operatorname{Lie}(A)$.

We now work over a field of characteristic $0$ and choose an algebraic closure $\bar k$ of $k$. For every prime number $\ell$, the \underline{$\ell$-adic realization} of a motive $M$ is a free $\Z_\ell$-module of finite type $H_\ell(M)$ (also endowed with an action of the Galois group of $\bar k$ over $k$). When $k=\C$, there is an isomorphism
\[
H_\ell(M)\simeq H_B(M)\otimes\Z_\ell.
\]
If $A$ is an abelian variety over $k$ and $\bar k$ is an algebraic closure of $k$, the $\ell$-adic realization of $T(A)$ is denoted $T_\ell(A)$; it is the $\Z_\ell$-module
\[
T_\ell(A)=\varprojlim_n A_{\ell^n}(\bar k)
\quad\bigl(\text{where }A_{\ell^n}(\bar k)\text{ is the group of points of order }\ell^n\text{ in }A(\bar k)\bigr).
\]
If $k=\C$, the isomorphism $T_\ell(A)\simeq H_B(T(A))\otimes\Z_\ell$ is easy to describe. For every $n$, the projection of $\gamma\otimes x\in H_B(T(A))\otimes\Z_\ell$ to $A_{\ell^n}(\C)$ is computed as follows: if $m\in H_B(T(A))$ is such that $x-m\in\ell^n(H_B(T(A))\otimes\Z_\ell)$, the exponential of $m/\ell^n\in H_1(A,\Q)$ is the required point of order $\ell^n$.

The \underline{de Rham realization} of $M$ is a finite-dimensional vector space $H_{DR}(M)$ over $k$, equipped with a decreasing filtration $F^\bullet$ (the \underline{Hodge filtration}). When $k=\C$, there is an isomorphism
\[
H_B(M)\otimes\C\simeq H_{DR}(M).
\]
Under this isomorphism, the Hodge filtration is given by $F^p=\bigoplus_{p'>p}H^{p',q}$. In the case of the motive $T(A)$, recall [7] the existence of a ``universal extension'' $E$ of $A$ by a vector group, fitting into an exact sequence of algebraic groups
\[
0\longrightarrow H^1(A,\mathcal O_A)^*\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow0.
\]
We have $H_{DR}(T(A))=\operatorname{Lie}(E)$, and the Hodge filtration is
\[
0=F^1\subset F^0=H^1(A,\mathcal O_A)^*\subset F^{-1}=\operatorname{Lie}(E).
\]
If $k=\C$, then $H_1(E,\Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}H_1(A,\Z)$, giving a morphism
\[
H_1(A,\C)\xleftarrow{\sim}H_1(E,\C)\xrightarrow{\int}\operatorname{Lie}(E)=H_{DR}(T(A)).
\]
This is the desired isomorphism.

\noindent\underline{Examples}: \textbf{1)} Here $k=\Q$. We shall define the \underline{Tate motive} $\Z(1)$; it is the $H_1$ of the algebraic group $\Gm$. To obtain its various realizations, substitute $\Gm$ for $A$ in the definition of $T(A)$. This gives
\begin{compactitem}
\item[$-$] $H_B(\Z(1))=2\pi i\cdot\Z$, the kernel of $\exp:\C\longrightarrow\C^\times$;
\item[] $H_B(\Z(1))\otimes\C=H^{-1,-1}$;
\item[$-$] $H_\ell(\Z(1))=\Z_\ell(1)=T_\ell(\Gm)=\varprojlim_n\mu_{\ell^n}(\bar\Q)$;
\item[$-$] $H_{DR}(\Z(1))=\Q$.
\end{compactitem}
The isomorphism $H_B(\Z(1))\otimes\C\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{DR}(\Z(1))\otimes\C$ sends $(2\pi i)\otimes1$ to $2\pi i$. Recall the isomorphisms $H_1(\Gm)\simeq H_1(\mathbf P^1-\{0,\infty\})\simeq H_2(\mathbf P^1)$. Moreover, for every smooth, proper, connected algebraic curve over $\Q$, $H_2(X)$ is isomorphic to $H_2(\mathbf P^1)$ (and hence to $\Z(1)$). For every integer $d$, define a motive $\Z(d)$ so that $\Z(d)=\Z(d-1)\otimes\Z(1)$. We have $\Z(m+n)\simeq\Z(m)\otimes\Z(n)$ for $m,n\in\Z$.

\textbf{2)} Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\Q$. The motive $T(E)$ has rank 2. Hence the motive $\bigwedge^2T(E)$ has rank 1, and
\[
\bigwedge^2T(E)=\bigwedge^2H_1(E)=H_2(E)=\Z(1).
\]
Let us specify the isomorphism $\bigwedge^2T(E)\simeq\Z(1)$ in the various realizations.

On $H_1(E(\C),\R)\simeq\operatorname{Lie}(E)$, the complex structure defines an orientation. If $(e_1,e_2)$ is an oriented $\Z$-basis of $H_B(T(E))=H_1(E(\C),\Z)$, then $e_1\wedge e_2$ is a basis of $H_B(\bigwedge^2T(E))$; it is associated with $2\pi i\in H_B(\Z(1))$.

The isomorphism $\bigwedge^2(T_\ell(E))\simeq\Z_\ell(1)$ is given by the alternating Weil pairing [6]. Finally,
\[
\bigwedge^2H_{DR}(T(E))=\operatorname{Hom}(H^2_{DR}(E),\Q).
\]
Let $\omega$ and $\eta$ be the usual differentials of the first and second kind. $H^2_{DR}(E)$ is generated by the cup product $\omega\wedge\eta$ of their cohomology classes. Under the isomorphism
\[
H_B(\bigwedge^2T(E))\otimes\C\longrightarrow H_{DR}(\bigwedge^2T(E))\otimes\C,
\]
$e_1\wedge e_2$ maps to the homomorphism sending $\omega\wedge\eta\in H^2_{DR}(E)$ to
\[
\begin{vmatrix}\omega_1&\omega_2\\\eta_1&\eta_2\end{vmatrix}\in\C.
\]
Compatibility of the Betti and de Rham realizations of $\bigwedge^2(T(E))\simeq\Z(1)$ is equivalent to the Legendre relation
\[
\omega_1\cdot\eta_2-\omega_2\cdot\eta_1=2\pi i.
\]

\textbf{3)} Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $\Q$. The cohomology class of a hyperplane section of $A$ in a projective embedding gives a morphism of motives $\bigwedge^2T(A)\to\Z(1)$. In Betti cohomology this corresponds to an alternating form $2\pi i\cdot E$ on $H_B(T(A))$ with values in $2\pi i\cdot\Z$. In de Rham cohomology one likewise has an alternating form $E_{DR}$ on $H_{DR}(T(A))$ with values in $\Q$. It is classical that $F^0$ is a totally isotropic subspace. It follows that one can find differentials of the first kind $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_g$ on $A$ and differentials of the second kind $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_g$ on $A$ such that
\[
E_{DR}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^g\bigl((\omega_i,x)(\eta_i,y)-(\omega_i,y)\cdot(\eta_i,x)\bigr).
\]
Compatibility between $E$ and $E_{DR}$ can be expressed as follows. Let $(e_1,\ldots,e_{2g})$ be a basis of $H_B(T(A))$, let $\Omega$ be the $n\times2n$ matrix with $\Omega_{ij}=\int_{e_j}\omega_i$, and let $N$ be the $n\times2n$ matrix with $N_{ij}=\int_{e_j}\eta_i$. Then
\[
{}^tN\cdot\Omega-{}^t\Omega\cdot N=2i\pi\cdot E.
\]
Finally, the $\ell$-adic realization gives an alternating form on $T_\ell(A)$ with values in $\Z_\ell(1)$: it is the alternating Weil form associated with the divisor of the hyperplane section [6].

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{\S\ 2. \quad \underline{MOTIVES ASSOCIATED WITH ABELIAN VARIETIES OF CM TYPE}}

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed subfield of $\C$ -- for example $\bar\Q$ -- and let $\mathcal A$ be the subcategory of the category of motives over $k$ generated by the $T(A)$ of abelian varieties (under tensor products, passage to duals, and direct factors). The functor ``Betti realization of the motive obtained from $M/k$ by extension of scalars to $\C$'' sends $\mathcal A$ to the category of Hodge structures. It follows formally from Theorem 1 of Section 3 that this functor is fully faithful.

Let $E$ be a CM field, let $S$ be the set of these complex embeddings, and let $-:S\to S$ denote complex conjugation. To each function $\varphi:S\to\Z$ satisfying

<!-- END_PAGE_RECORD physical=6 printed=27 -->

<!-- BEGIN_PAGE_RECORD physical=7 article=6 printed=28 -->
## Source page: physical 7; printed 28

\[
(*)\qquad \varphi(\sigma)+\varphi(\bar\sigma)\quad\text{is independent of }\sigma\in S,
\]
associate a Hodge structure $H_\varphi$ equipped with a module structure over the ring of integers $\mathcal O_E$ of $E$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
H_\varphi&=\mathcal O_E,\quad\text{with the evident module structure, and in the decomposition}\\
H_\varphi\otimes\C&=E\otimes\C\simeq\C^S,\quad\text{the $\C$-factor indexed by $\sigma\in S$ has Hodge type}\\
&\hspace{8em}(\varphi(\sigma),\varphi(\bar\sigma)).
\end{aligned}
\]
One verifies that the $H_\varphi$ lie in the image of $\mathcal A$, and indeed in the image of the subcategory generated by abelian varieties of CM type. This allows one to define the motive $M_\varphi$ over $\bar\Q$ as the motive whose Betti realization is $H_\varphi$.

The de Rham realization $H_{DR}(M_\varphi)$ is a vector space over $\bar\Q$ equipped with an action of $E$. For each $\sigma\in S$, let $H_{DR}(M_\varphi)_\sigma$ be the $\bar\Q$-subspace on which $x\in E$ acts as $\sigma^{-1}(x)\in\bar\Q$. Each of these vector spaces is one-dimensional, generated by an element $\omega_\sigma$, and $H_{DR}(M_\varphi)$ is their direct sum. Under the isomorphism
\[
H_B(M_\varphi)\otimes\C\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{DR}(M_\varphi)\otimes\C,
\]
an element $e$ of $E=H_B(M_\varphi)\otimes\Q$ corresponds to a family $(e_\sigma)$ with $e_\sigma\in H_{DR}(M_\varphi)_\sigma$. It follows immediately that the complex number $p(\sigma,\varphi)$ defined by $p(\sigma,\varphi)e_\sigma=\omega_\sigma$ is independent, up to multiplication by $\bar\Q^\times$, both of $e$ and of the choice of $\omega_\sigma$. This justifies the notation.

The relation between the $p(\sigma,\varphi)$ and Shimura's \underline{invariants} $p_E(\sigma,\varphi)$ (see [5]) is
\[
p_E(\sigma,\varphi)=\frac{p(\sigma,\varphi)}{(2\pi i)^{\varphi(\sigma)}}\quad(\text{modulo }\bar\Q^\times),
\]
which is merely a translation of the definitions.

The identities among Shimura's periods correspond to algebraic properties of the motives $M_\varphi$. Before summarizing them in a table, introduce some notation for an extension $F|E$ of CM fields. Let $S_F$ (respectively $S_E$) be the set of complex embeddings of $F$ (respectively $E$). For $\sigma\in S_F$, write $\sigma|E\in S_E$ for the restriction of the embedding $\sigma$ to $E$.

\[
\operatorname{Res}(\varphi)(\sigma)=\sum_{\tau|E=\sigma}\varphi(\tau);

it satisfies $(*)$.

These identities among periods recover Theorems 1 through 4 of Shimura's article [5]. Note that in [5] Shimura relates the periods $p_K(\sigma,\varphi)$ to values, at a special point, of Hilbert modular forms rational over $\bar\Q$.

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent\textbf{\S\ 3. \quad \underline{ABSOLUTE HODGE CYCLES AND MOTIVES}}

Let $X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\C$. A \underline{Hodge cycle} of codimension $d$ on $X$ is an element of $H^{2d}(X,\Q)$ of type $(d,d)$, or equivalently an element of
\[
H^{2d}(X,\Q)(d)\mathrel{\mathop=\limits^{\mathrm{def}}}H^{2d}(X,\Q)\otimes\Z(d)
\]
of type $(0,0)$. Observe that for $x\in H^{2d}(X,\Q)(d)$, saying that $x$ is of type $(0,0)$ is equivalent to saying that $x$ lies in $F^0$. The cohomology class of an algebraic subvariety of $X$ is a Hodge cycle. Hodge conjectured that every Hodge cycle is a linear combination

<!-- END_PAGE_RECORD physical=8 printed=29 -->

<!-- BEGIN_PAGE_RECORD physical=9 article=8 printed=30 -->
## Source page: physical 9; printed 30

of cohomology classes of subvarieties. Since this conjecture appears inaccessible, we introduce a notion that ``arithmetizes'' that of a Hodge cycle.

The cycles defined here will have all the visible attributes of algebraic cycles, namely $\ell$-adic and de Rham cohomology realizations. An \underline{absolute Hodge cycle} of codimension $d$ on a smooth projective variety $X$ over a field $k$ of characteristic $0$ is a collection of cohomology classes in the groups
\[
H^{2d}(X,\Q_\ell)(d)\quad(\text{respectively }H^{2d}_{DR}(X)(d))
\]
satisfying the following two conditions:
\begin{compactitem}
\item[$-$] the element of $H^{2d}_{DR}(X)(d)$ belongs to $F^0$;
\item[$-$] for every embedding $k\hookrightarrow\C$, the different cohomology classes correspond (through the isomorphisms of Section 1) to a single rational class in the Betti cohomology of $X\otimes_k\C$.
\end{compactitem}
There are inclusions (algebraic cycles) $\subset$ (absolute Hodge cycles) $\subset$ (Hodge cycles). We use this definition to specify the category of motives used in Sections 1 and 2. First let $\mathcal V(k)$ be the category of smooth projective varieties over $k$. One constructs an \underline{additive} category $\mathcal M(k)$, in which the groups $\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)$ are vector spaces over $\Q$, together with the following data:

\textbf{a)} a tensor product $\otimes$, associative, commutative, and distributive with respect to addition of objects;

\textbf{b)} a contravariant functor $H^*$ from $\mathcal V(k)$ to $\mathcal M(k)$, bijective on objects, compatible with sums, and carrying products to tensor products.

In $\mathcal M(k)$, for connected $X$ and $Y$, one has
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(H^*(X),H^*(Y))=Z_{h.a}^{\dim(Y)}(X\times Y),
\]
where $Z_{h.a}^d(Z)$ denotes the vector space over $\Q$ of absolute Hodge cycles of codimension $d$ on $Z$. The category of motives $\mathcal M(k)$ is then constructed exactly as in [2], with algebraic cycles replaced by absolute Hodge cycles. By this new definition, the Künneth components of the diagonal of $X\times X$ are absolute Hodge, and in $\mathcal M(k)$ there is a decomposition
\[
H^*(X)=\bigoplus_i H^i(X).
\]

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Theorem 1}}: \underline{Every Hodge cycle on an abelian variety is absolute Hodge.}

We note the following.

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Corollary}} (Borovoï): \underline{Let $A$ be an abelian variety over a field $k$ of characteristic $0$, and let $\xi$ be a Hodge cycle on $A$. The corresponding $\ell$-adic classes $\xi_\ell$ of $\xi$ are fixed by an open subgroup of $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k|k)$.}

\noindent\textbf{\S\ 4. \quad \underline{PERIODS OF INTEGRALS ON ABELIAN VARIETIES}}

Let $A$ be a complex abelian variety, and let $H_1(A,\C)=H^{0,-1}\oplus H^{-1,0}$ be the Hodge decomposition of $H_B(A)$ (cf. Section 1). Regard $GL(H_1(A))$ as an algebraic group over $\Q$. There is a morphism of complex algebraic groups
\[
\Gm\xrightarrow{\mu}GL(H_1(A,\C))
\]
such that $\mu(z)$ acts by the homothety of ratio $z$ on $H^{-1,0}$ and by the identity on $H^{0,-1}$. The \underline{Hodge group} $G$ of $A$ is the Zariski closure of the subgroup $\mu(\Gm)$ in $GL(H_1(A))$. In other words, $G$ is an algebraic subgroup over $\Q$ of $GL(H_1(A))$. A regular function on a Zariski-open subset of $G$ that is rational over $\Q$ vanishes on $G$ if and only if it vanishes on $\mu(\Gm)$. One checks easily that $G$ contains the homotheties of $H_1(A)$. Here is a second, very useful description of $G$: consider all rational tensors of type $(-N/2,-N/2)$ in the tensor products $\bigotimes^N H_1(A)$. Then $G$ is the subgroup of $GL(H_1(A))$ formed by those linear transformations $g$ for which there is a scalar $\mu$ such that $g\cdot t=\mu^m t$ for every tensor $t$ of type $(m,m)$. Finally, one shows that $G$ is the group fixing all absolute Hodge cycles of type $(0,0)$ in the mixed tensor spaces on $H_1(A)$ (mixed means covariant and contravariant).

Now suppose that $A$ is defined over a subfield $k$ of $\C$. Put $g=\dim(A)$.

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Theorem 2}}: \underline{Let $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_g$ be differentials of the first kind on $A$, and let $\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_g$ be differentials of the second kind on $A$, such that}

\underline{$(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_g,\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_g)$ is a basis of $H^1_{DR}(A)$. Let $K$ be the subfield of $\C$ generated by $k$ and the periods $\int_\gamma\omega_i$, $\int_\gamma\eta_i$ $(1\le i\le g,\ \gamma\in H_1(A,\Z))$; the transcendence degree of $K$ over $k$ is bounded above by the dimension of $G$.}

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Proof}}: $K$ is the field of rationality of the isomorphism
\[
H_{1,B}(A)\otimes_{\Q}\C\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{1,DR}(A)\otimes_k\C
\]
given by ``integration'' over a cycle. This isomorphism preserves the cohomology classes of absolute Hodge cycles. Let $P$ be the algebraic variety of isomorphisms having this property. From the definition of $G$ and Theorem 1, it follows that $P$ is a principal homogeneous space under $G$, defined over $k$, and therefore $\dim(P)=\dim(G)$. We conclude by the following lemma.

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Lemma}}: \underline{Let $z$ be a point of $\mathbb A^N(\C)$; the transcendence degree of $k(z)$ over $k$ is equal to the dimension of the $k$-Zariski closure of $\{z\}$;}

\begin{center}This is a tautology.\hfill Q.E.D.\end{center}

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Remark}}: Example 3 of Section 1 shows that $2\pi i\in K$. One can moreover show that the transcendence degree of $K$ over $k(2\pi i)$ is bounded above by $\dim(G)-1$.

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Corollary}}: \underline{With the notation of Theorem 2, suppose in addition that $A$ is simple of CM type. The transcendence degree of $K$ over $k$ is bounded above by $g+1$.}

\noindent\underline{\textbf{Proof}}: It is enough to show that $\dim(G)<g+1$. Now $\mu(\Gm)$ commutes with the action of the field $E$ on $H_1(A,\Q)$. It follows that $G$ also commutes with this action and that $G$ is an algebraic torus. Moreover, $G$ preserves, up to a factor, the alternating form associated with a hyperplane section (see Example 3 of Section 1). This proves the assertion.

Observe that in this last case one has the following lower bound:
\[
\dim(G)>2+\log_2(g),
\]
due to Ribet [3].
