\section*{Introduction}

According to Hodge, the cohomology space $H^n(X,\C)$ of a compact Kähler variety $X$ is endowed with a \emph{Hodge structure} of weight $n$, that is, with a natural bigrading
\[
H^n(X,\C)=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}H^{pq}
\]
satisfying $\overline{H^{pq}}=H^{qp}$. We show here that the complex cohomology of a nonsingular algebraic variety, not necessarily compact, is endowed with a somewhat more general kind of structure, which presents $H^n(X,\C)$ as a successive extension of Hodge structures of decreasing weights, lying between $2n$ and $n$, whose Hodge numbers $h^{pq}=\dim H^{pq}$ are zero for $p>n$ or $q>n$.

The reader will find in [14] an exposition of the yoga underlying this construction.

The proof, essentially algebraic, rests on the one hand on Hodge theory and on the other hand on Hironaka's resolution of singularities, which permits one, via a spectral sequence, to \emph{express} the cohomology of a nonsingular quasi-projective algebraic variety in terms of the cohomology of nonsingular projective varieties.

Section 1 contains, besides a few elementary routines on filtrations assembled for the reader's convenience, two key results:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item Theorem (1.2.10), which will be used only through its corollary (2.3.5), giving the fundamental properties of mixed Hodge structures;
\item the \emph{lemma on two filtrations} (1.3.16).
\end{enumerate}
Section 2 recalls Hodge theory and introduces mixed Hodge structures. The heart of the paper is no. 3.2, which defines the mixed Hodge structure on $H^n(X,\C)$ and establishes certain degeneracies of spectral sequences.

Section 4 gives various applications, all deduced from (4.1.1) and from the theory of the $(K/k)$-trace for Hodge structures which follows from it (4.1.2). The principal ones are (4.2.6) and (4.4.15).

\section*{1. Filtrations}
\subsection*{1.1. Filtered objects}

\paragraph{(1.1.1)} Let $\calA$ be an abelian category. We shall have to consider filtrations of type $\Z$, usually finite, on the objects of $\calA$.

\paragraph{Definition (1.1.2).} A decreasing, respectively increasing, filtration $F$ of an object $A$ of $\calA$ is a family $(F^n(A))_{n\in\Z}$, respectively $(F_n(A))_{n\in\Z}$, of subobjects of $A$, satisfying
\[
\forall n,m\qquad n\le m\Rightarrow F^n(A)\supset F^m(A)
\]
(resp. $\forall n,m\quad n\le m\Rightarrow F_n(A)\subset F_m(A)$).

A filtered object is an object endowed with a filtration. When no confusion is possible, the same letter will often denote filtrations on different objects of $\calA$.

If $F$ is a decreasing, respectively increasing, filtration on $A$, put $F^\infty(A)=0$ and $F^{-\infty}(A)=A$ (resp. $F_{-\infty}(A)=0$ and $F_{\infty}(A)=A$).

The shifted filtrations of a decreasing filtration $W$ are defined by
\[
W[n]^p(A)=W^{n+p}(A).
\]

\paragraph{(1.1.3)} If $F$ is a decreasing, respectively increasing, filtration of $A$, then the $F_n(A)=F^{-n}(A)$, respectively the $F^n(A)=F_{-n}(A)$, form an increasing, respectively decreasing, filtration of $A$. Thus in principle it is enough to consider decreasing filtrations; unless explicitly stated otherwise, the word \emph{filtration} will henceforth mean \emph{decreasing filtration}.

\paragraph{(1.1.4)} A filtration $F$ of $A$ is called finite if there exist $n$ and $m$ such that $F^n(A)=A$ and $F^m(A)=0$.

\paragraph{(1.1.5)} A morphism from a filtered object $(A,F)$ to a filtered object $(B,F)$ is a morphism $f$ from $A$ to $B$ satisfying
\[
f(F^n(A))\subset F^n(B)\qquad(n\in\Z).
\]
The filtered objects, respectively the finitely filtered objects, of $\calA$ form an additive category in which finite inductive and projective limits exist, hence kernels, cokernels, images, and coimages of a morphism exist.

A morphism $f:(A,F)\to(B,F)$ is called strict, or strictly compatible with the filtrations, if the canonical arrow from $\Coim(f)$ to $\Imop(f)$ is an isomorphism of filtered objects (cf. (1.1.11)).

\paragraph{(1.1.6)} Let ${}^{\circ}$ be the contravariant identity functor from $\calA$ to the dual category $\calA^{\circ}$. If $(A,F)$ is a filtered object of $\calA$, the objects $(A/F^{1-n}(A))^{\circ}$ are identified with subobjects of $A^{\circ}$. The filtration on $A^{\circ}$ dual to $F$ is defined by
\[
F^n(A^{\circ})=(A/F^{1-n}(A))^{\circ}.
\]
The bidual of $(A,F)$ is identified with $(A,F)$. This construction identifies the dual of the category of filtered objects of $\calA$ with the category of filtered objects of $\calA^{\circ}$.

\paragraph{(1.1.7)} If $(A,F)$ is a filtered object of $\calA$, its associated graded object is the object of $\calA^{\Z}$ defined by
\[
\Gr^n(A)=F^n(A)/F^{n+1}(A).
\]
The convention (1.1.6) is justified by the simple formula
\[
\Gr^n(A^{\circ})=\Gr^{-n}(A)^{\circ},
\]
which is verified on the autodual diagram
\begin{equation}\tag{1.1.7.1}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.2em,row sep=1.0em]
& A/F^n(A) & A/F^{n+1}(A) \arrow[l] & 0 \arrow[l] \\
& & A \arrow[ul] \arrow[u] \arrow[dl] \arrow[d] & \Gr^n(A) \arrow[u] \arrow[dr] \\
& F^{n+1}(A) \arrow[r] & F^n(A) & & 0
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{(1.1.8)} Let $(A,F)$ be a filtered object and let $j:X\hookrightarrow A$ be a subobject of $A$. The filtration induced on $X$ by $F$ is the unique filtration on $X$ such that $j$ is strictly compatible with the filtrations; one has
\[
F^n(X)=j^{-1}(F^n(A))=X\cap F^n(A).
\]
Dually, the quotient filtration on $A/X$, the unique filtration such that $p:A\to A/X$ is strictly compatible with the filtrations, is given by
\[
F^n(A/X)=p(F^n(A))\simeq (X+F^n(A))/X\simeq F^n(A)/(X\cap F^n(A)).
\]

\paragraph{Lemma (1.1.9).} If $X$ and $Y$ are two subobjects of $A$, with $X\subset Y$, then on $Y/X\simeq\Ker(A/X\to A/Y)$ the quotient filtration from $Y$ coincides with the filtration induced from $A/X$. In the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.3em,row sep=1.0em]
& A/Y & A/X \arrow[l] \\
& A \arrow[u] \arrow[ur] & Y/X \arrow[u] \\
X \arrow[ur] \arrow[r] & Y \arrow[u] \arrow[ur] &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
the arrows are strict.

\paragraph{(1.1.10)} The filtration of (1.1.9) on $Y/X$ will be called the filtration induced by that of $A$. By (1.1.9), its definition is autodual.

In particular, if $\Sigma:A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C$ is a $0$-sequence and if $B$ is filtered, then
\[
H(\Sigma)=\Ker(g)/\Imop(f)=\Ker(\Coker(f)\to\Coim(g))
\]
is endowed with a canonical induced filtration.

The reader will verify the following.

\paragraph{Proposition (1.1.11).} \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Let $f:(A,F)\to(B,F)$ be a morphism of finitely filtered objects. For $f$ to be strict, it is necessary and sufficient that the sequence
\[
0\to\Gr(\Ker(f))\to\Gr(A)\to\Gr(B)\to\Gr(\Coker(f))\to0
\]
be exact.
\item Let $\Sigma:(A,F)\to(B,F)\to(C,F)$ be a $0$-sequence of strict morphisms. Then one has canonically
\[
H(\Gr(\Sigma))\simeq\Gr(H(\Sigma)).
\]
In particular, if $\Sigma$ is exact in $\calA$, then $\Gr(\Sigma)$ is exact in $\calA^{\Z}$.
\end{enumerate}

In a category of modules, to say that a morphism $f:(A,F)\to(B,F)$ is strict means that every $b\in B$ of filtration $\ge n$ ($b\in F^n(B)$) which is in the image of $A$ is already in the image of $F^n(A)$:
\[
f(F^n(A))=f(A)\cap F^n(B).
\]

\paragraph{(1.1.12)} If $\otimes:\calA_1\times\cdots\times\calA_n\to\calB$ is a multiadditive right exact functor, and if $A_i$ is a finitely filtered object of $\calA_i$ ($1\le i\le n$), one defines a filtration on $\bigotimes_{i=1}^nA_i$ by
\[
F^k\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^n A_i\right)=\sum_{\sum k_i=k}\Imop\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^n F^{k_i}(A_i)\to\bigotimes_{i=1}^n A_i\right)
\]
(sum of subobjects).

Dually, if $H$ is left exact, put
\[
F^k(H(A_i))=\bigcap_{\sum k_i=k}\Ker\left(H(A_i)\to H(A_i/F^{k_i}(A_i))\right).
\]

For exact $H$, the two definitions are equivalent. These definitions are extended to functors contravariant in certain variables by (1.1.6). In particular, for the left exact functor $\Hom$, one puts
\[
F^n(\Hom(A,B))=\{f:A\to B\mid \forall p,\ f(F^p(A))\subset F^{p+n}(B)\}.
\]
Thus
\[
\Hom((A,F),(B,F))=F^0(\Hom(A,B)).
\]

Under the preceding hypotheses, one has evident morphisms
\[
\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\Gr(A_i)\to \Gr\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^nA_i\right)
\]
and
\[
\Gr H(A_i)\to H(\Gr(A_i)).
\]
For exact $H$, these are mutually inverse isomorphisms.

These constructions are compatible with the composition of functors, in a sense which we leave to the reader not to make explicit.

\subsection*{1.2. Opposite filtrations}

\paragraph{(1.2.1)} Let $A$ be an object of $\calA$ endowed with two filtrations $F$ and $G$. By definition, $\Gr_F^p(A)$ is a quotient of a subobject of $A$, and as such is endowed with a filtration induced by $G$ (1.1.10). Passing to the associated graded object, one defines a bigraded object $(\Gr_G^n\Gr_F^p(A))_{n,p\in\Z}$. By a lemma of Zassenhaus, $\Gr_G^n\Gr_F^p(A)$ and $\Gr_F^p\Gr_G^n(A)$ are canonically isomorphic: if one defines the induced filtrations (1.1.10) as quotient filtrations of filtrations induced on a subobject, one has
\[
\Gr_G^n\Gr_F^p(A)\simeq
\frac{F^p(A)\cap G^n(A)}{(F^{p+1}(A)\cap G^n(A))+(F^p(A)\cap G^{n+1}(A))}
\]
\[
\simeq
\frac{G^n(A)\cap F^p(A)}{(G^{n+1}(A)\cap F^p(A))+(G^n(A)\cap F^{p+1}(A))}
\simeq \Gr_F^p\Gr_G^n(A).
\]

\paragraph{(1.2.2)} Let $H$ be a third filtration of $A$. It induces a filtration on $\Gr_F(A)$, and hence on $\Gr_G\Gr_F(A)$. It also induces a filtration on $\Gr_F\Gr_G(A)$. One should note that these filtrations do not in general correspond under the isomorphism (1.2.1). In the expression $\Gr_H\Gr_G\Gr_F(A)$, therefore, $G$ and $H$ play symmetric roles, but $F$ and $G$ do not.

\paragraph{Definition (1.2.3).} Two finite filtrations $F$ and $\overline F$ on $A$ are called $n$-opposite if
\[
\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^{q}(A)=0\qquad\text{for }p+q\ne n.
\]

\paragraph{(1.2.4)} If $A^{p,q}$ is a bigraded object of $\calA$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item $A^{p,q}=0$ except for finitely many pairs $(p,q)$;
\item $A^{p,q}=0$ for $p+q\ne n$,
\end{enumerate}
then one defines two finite $n$-opposite filtrations of $A=\sum_{p,q}A^{p,q}$ by putting
\begin{align}
F^p(A)&=\sum_{p'\ge p} A^{p',q'}, \tag{1.2.4.1}\\
\overline F^q(A)&=\sum_{q'\ge q} A^{p',q'}. \tag{1.2.4.2}
\end{align}
One has
\[
\tag{1.2.4.3}
\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^{q}(A)=A^{p,q}.
\]
Conversely:

\paragraph{Proposition (1.2.5).} \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Let $F$ and $\overline F$ be two finite filtrations on $A$. For $F$ and $\overline F$ to be $n$-opposite, it is necessary and sufficient that
\[
\forall p,q\qquad p+q=n+1\Rightarrow F^p(A)\oplus\overline F^q(A)\iso A.
\]
\item If $F$ and $\overline F$ are $n$-opposite, and if one puts
\[
\begin{cases}
A^{p,q}=0 & \text{for }p+q\ne n,\\
A^{p,q}=F^p(A)\cap\overline F^q(A) & \text{for }p+q=n,
\end{cases}
\]
then $A$ is the direct sum of the $A^{p,q}$, and $F$ and $\overline F$ are deduced from the bigrading $A^{p,q}$ of $A$ by the procedure (1.2.4).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Proof.} (i) The condition $\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^q(A)=0$ for $p+q>n$ means that
\[
F^p\cap\overline F^q=(F^{p+1}\cap\overline F^q)+(F^p\cap\overline F^{q+1})\qquad(p+q>n).
\]
By hypothesis, $F^p\cap\overline F^q$ is zero for $p+q$ sufficiently large; by descending induction one deduces that the condition $\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^q(A)=0$ for $p+q>n$ is equivalent to the condition $F^p(A)\cap\overline F^q(A)=0$ for $p+q>n$. Dually ((1.1.6), (1.1.7), (1.1.10)) the condition $\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^q(A)=0$ for $p+q<n$ is equivalent to $A=F^p(A)+\overline F^q(A)$ for $(1-p)+(1-q)>-n$, that is, for $p+q<n+1$, and (i) follows.

(ii) If $F$ and $\overline F$ are $n$-opposite, we prove by descending induction on $p$ that
\[
\tag{1.2.5.1}
\bigoplus_{p'\ge p} A^{p',q'}\iso F^p(A).
\]
For $F^p(A)=0$, the assertion is evident. The decomposition $A=F^{p+1}(A)\oplus\overline F^{n-p}(A)$ induces on $F^p(A)\supset F^{p+1}(A)$ a decomposition
\[
F^p(A)=F^{p+1}(A)\oplus(F^p(A)\cap\overline F^{n-p}(A)),
\]
and the induction concludes the proof. For $p$ sufficiently small, $F^p(A)=A$. By (1.2.5.1), the $A^{p,q}$ therefore form a bigrading of $A$, and $F$ satisfies (1.2.4.1). That $\overline F$ satisfies (1.2.4.2) follows by symmetry.

\paragraph{(1.2.6)} The constructions (1.2.4) and (1.2.5) establish quasi-inverse equivalences of categories between objects of $\calA$ endowed with two finite $n$-opposite filtrations and bigraded objects of $\calA$ of the type considered in (1.2.4).

\paragraph{Definition (1.2.7).} Three finite filtrations $W$, $F$, and $\overline F$ on an object $A$ of $\calA$ are called opposite if
\[
\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^{q}\Gr_W^n(A)=0\qquad\text{for }p+q+n\ne0.
\]
This condition is symmetric in $F$ and $\overline F$. It means that $F$ and $\overline F$ induce on $W^n(A)/W^{n+1}(A)$ two $(-n)$-opposite filtrations. Put
\[
A^{p,q}=\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^{q}\Gr_W^{-p-q}(A),
\]
whence decompositions (1.2.4), (1.2.5)
\[
\tag{1.2.7.1}
W^n(A)/W^{n+1}(A)=\bigoplus_{p+q=-n}A^{p,q}
\]
which make $\Gr_W(A)$ into a bigraded object.

\paragraph{Lemma (1.2.8).} Let $W$, $F$, and $\overline F$ be three finite opposite filtrations on $A$, and let $\sigma$ be a sequence $(p_i,q_i)_{i\ge0}$ of pairs of integers satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item $p_i\le p_j$ and $q_i\le q_j$ for $i\ge j$;
\item for $i>0$, $p_i+q_i=p_0+q_0-i+1$.
\end{enumerate}
Put $p=p_0$, $q=q_0$, $n=-p-q$, and
\[
A_\sigma=\left(\sum_{0\le i} (W^{n+i}(A)\cap F^{p_i}(A))\right)
\cap
\left(\sum_{0\le i} (W^{n+i}(A)\cap \overline F^{q_i}(A))\right).
\]
Then the projection from $W^n(A)$ to $\Gr_W^n(A)$ induces an isomorphism
\[
A_\sigma\iso A^{p,q}\subset\Gr_W^n(A).
\]

We prove by induction on $k$ the following assertion:
\[
(*_k)
\]
the projection from $W^n(A)/W^{n+k}(A)$ to $\Gr_W^n(A)$ induces an isomorphism from
\[
\frac{\left(\left(\sum_{i<k}(W^{n+i}(A)\cap F^{p_i}(A))\right)+W^{n+k}(A)\right)
\cap
\left(\left(\sum_{i<k}(W^{n+i}(A)\cap\overline F^{q_i}(A))\right)+W^{n+k}(A)\right)}{W^{n+k}(A)}
\]
to $A^{p,q}\subset\Gr_W^n(A)$.

For $k=1$, this is exactly the definition of $A^{p,q}$. By (1.2.5)(i), one has
\[
\tag{1.2.8.1}
F^{p_k}(\Gr_W^{n+k}(A))\oplus \overline F^{q_k}(\Gr_W^{n+k}(A))\iso \Gr_W^{n+k}(A).
\]
Put
\begin{align*}
B&=\sum_{i<k}(W^{n+i}(A)\cap F^{p_i}(A)),\\
C&=\sum_{i<k}(W^{n+i}(A)\cap\overline F^{q_i}(A)),\\
B'&=(W^{n+k}(A)\cap F^{p_k}(A))+W^{n+k+1}(A),\\
C'&=(W^{n+k}(A)\cap\overline F^{q_k}(A))+W^{n+k+1}(A),\\
D&=W^{n+k}(A),\\
E&=W^{n+k+1}(A).
\end{align*}
Formula (1.2.8.1) becomes
\[
B'+C'=D,
\]
and
\[
B'\cap C'=E.
\]
Moreover, since $p_k\le p_i$ $(i\le k)$,
\[
B\cap D\subset F^{p_k}(A)\cap W^{n+k}(A)\subset B'
\]
and since $q_k\le q_i$ $(i\le k)$,
\[
C\cap D\subset \overline F^{q_k}(A)\cap W^{n+k}(A)\subset C'.
\]
The assertion $(*_{k+1})$ therefore follows from $(*_k)$ and from the following lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma (1.2.9).} Let $B$, $C$, $B'$, $C'$, $D$, and $E$ be subobjects of $A$. Assume that
\[
B'+C'=D,
\qquad
B'\cap C'=E,
\]
\[
B\cap D\subset B',
\qquad
C\cap D\subset C'.
\]
Then
\[
((B+B')\cap(C+C'))/E\iso((B+D)\cap(C+D))/D.
\]
To prove surjectivity, one writes
\begin{align*}
((B+B')\cap(C+C'))+D
&=(((B+B')\cap(C+C'))+B')+C'\\
&=((B+B')\cap(C+C'+B'))+C'\\
&=(B+B'+C')\cap(C+C'+B')\\
&=(B+D)\cap(C+D).
\end{align*}
To prove injectivity, one writes
\[
(B+B')\cap(C+C')\cap D=((B+B')\cap D)\cap((C+C')\cap D).
\]
Since $B'\subset D$, one has
\[
(B+B')\cap D=(B\cap D)+B'=B';
\]
similarly,
\[
(C+C')\cap D=C',
\]
and
\[
(B+B')\cap(C+C')\cap D=B'\cap C'=E.
\]
Finally, the proof of (1.2.8) is completed by observing that (1.2.8) is equivalent to $(*_k)$ for large $k$.

\paragraph{Theorem (1.2.10).} Let $\calA$ be an abelian category, and provisionally denote by $\calA'$ the category of objects of $\calA$ endowed with three opposite filtrations $W$, $F$, and $\overline F$. The morphisms in $\calA'$ are the morphisms in $\calA$ compatible with the three filtrations.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\calA'$ is an abelian category.
\item The kernel, respectively cokernel, of an arrow $f:A\to B$ in $\calA'$ is the kernel, respectively cokernel, of $f$ in $\calA$, endowed with the filtrations induced from those of $A$, respectively quotient filtrations from those of $B$.
\item Every morphism $f:A\to B$ in $\calA'$ is strictly compatible with the filtrations $W$, $F$, and $\overline F$; the morphism $\Gr_W(f)$ is compatible with the bigradings of $\Gr_W(A)$ and $\Gr_W(B)$; the morphisms $\Gr_F(f)$ and $\Gr_{\overline F}(f)$ are strictly compatible with the filtration induced by $W$.
\item The functors \emph{forget the filtrations}, $\Gr_W$, $\Gr_F$, $\Gr_{\overline F}$, and
\[
\Gr_W\Gr_F\simeq\Gr_F\Gr_W,
\quad
\Gr_W\Gr_{\overline F}\simeq\Gr_{\overline F}\Gr_W,
\quad
\Gr_F\Gr_{\overline F}\simeq\Gr_{\overline F}\Gr_F
\]
from $\calA'$ to $\calA$ are exact.
\end{enumerate}

Let $\sigma_0(p,q)$ and $\sigma_1(p,q)$ denote the sequences
\[
\sigma_0(p,q)=(p,q),(p,q),(p,q-1),(p,q-2),(p,q-3),\ldots
\]
\[
\sigma_1(p,q)=(p,q),(p,q),(p-1,q),(p-2,q),(p-3,q),\ldots
\]
and, with the notation of (1.2.8), put
\[
A_i^{p,q}=A_{\sigma_i(p,q)}\qquad(i=0,1).
\]
If $f:A\to B$ is compatible with $W$, $F$, and $\overline F$, then
\[
\tag{1.2.10.1}
f(A_i^{p,q})\subset B_i^{p,q}\qquad(i=0,1).
\]
Assertion (iii) therefore follows from the following lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma (1.2.11).} The $A_i^{p,q}$ form a bigrading of $A$. One has
\[
\tag{1.2.11.1}
W^n(A)=\sum_{n+p+q\le0} A_i^{p,q}\qquad(i=0,1),
\]
\[
\tag{1.2.11.2}
F^p(A)=\sum_{p'\ge p} A_0^{p',q},
\]
\[
\tag{1.2.11.3}
\overline F^q(A)=\sum_{q'\ge q} A_1^{p,q'}.
\]

By symmetry, it is enough to prove the assertions for $i=0$. Put $A_0=\bigoplus A_0^{p,q}$, and define on $A_0$ filtrations $W$ and $F$ by the formulae of (1.2.11). The canonical map $i$ from $A_0$ to $A$ is compatible with the filtrations $W$ and $F$. Moreover, by (1.2.8), $\Gr_W(i)$ is an isomorphism, and it induces isomorphisms of graded objects
\[
\tag{1.2.11.4}
\sum_{p+q=-n} A_0^{p,q}\iso \Gr_W^{-n}(A)=\sum_{p+q=-n}A^{p,q}.
\]
The morphism $i$ is therefore an isomorphism, and the $A_0^{p,q}$ form a bigrading of $A$. Formula (1.2.11.1) then expresses that $\Gr_W(i)$ is an isomorphism. By (1.2.11.4), $\Gr_F\Gr_W(i)$ is an isomorphism, hence so are $\Gr_W\Gr_F(i)$ and $\Gr_F(i)$. Formula (1.2.11.2) follows.

\paragraph{(1.2.12)} We prove (1.2.10). Let $f:A\to B$ be in $\calA'$ and endow $K=\Ker(f)$ with the filtrations induced from those of $A$. By (1.2.11), $\Gr_W(K)\to\Gr_W(A)$; moreover, the filtration $F$, respectively $\overline F$, of $K$ induces on $\Gr_W(K)$ the inverse-image filtration of the filtration $F$ on $\Gr_W(A)$. Finally, the subobject $\Gr_W(K)$ of $\Gr_W(A)$ is compatible with the bigrading of $\Gr_W(A)$:
\[
\Gr_W(K)=\bigoplus_{p,q}(\Gr_W(K)\cap A^{p,q}).
\]
It follows that
\[
\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^{q}\Gr_W^n(K)\hookrightarrow \Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^{q}\Gr_W^n(A);
\]
the filtrations $W$, $F$, and $\overline F$ of $K$ are therefore opposite, and $K$ is a kernel of $f$ in $\calA'$. Together with the dual result, this proves (ii).

If $f$ is an arrow of $\calA'$, the canonical morphism from $\Coim(f)$ to $\Imop(f)$ is an isomorphism in $\calA$; by (iii), it is also an isomorphism in $\calA'$, which is therefore abelian.

The functor \emph{forget the filtrations} is exact by (ii). The exactness of the other functors in (iv) follows at once from (ii), (iii), and (1.1.11)(i) or (ii).

\paragraph{(1.2.13)} Let $A$ be an object of $\calA$ endowed with a finite increasing filtration $W$, and with two finite decreasing filtrations $F$ and $\overline F$. The construction (1.1.3) associates to $W_\bullet$ a decreasing filtration $W^\bullet$. We shall say that the filtrations $W_\bullet$, $F$, and $\overline F$ are opposite if the filtrations $W^\bullet$, $F$, and $\overline F$ are opposite, that is, if for every $n$ the filtrations induced by $F$ and $\overline F$ on
\[
\Gr_n^W(A)=W_n(A)/W_{n-1}(A)
\]
are $n$-opposite.

Theorem (1.2.10) carries over trivially to this variant.

\subsection*{1.3. The lemma on two filtrations}

\paragraph{(1.3.1)} Let $K$ be a differential complex of objects of $\calA$, endowed with a filtration $F$. This filtration is called biregular if it induces on each component of $K$ a finite filtration. Recall the definition of the terms $E_r^{pq}(K,F)$, or simply $E_r^{pq}$, of the spectral sequence defined by $F$. Put
\[
Z_r^{pq}=\Ker\left(d:F^p(K^{p+q})\to K^{p+q+1}/F^{p+r}(K^{p+q+1})\right),
\]
and define $B_r^{pq}$ dually by the formula
\[
K^{p+q}/B_r^{pq}=\coker\left(d:F^{p-r+1}(K^{p+q-1})\to K^{p+q}/F^{p+1}(K^{p+q})\right).
\]
These formulae still make sense for $r=\infty$.

One should note that the use made here of the notation $B_r^{pq}$ is different from that of Godement [TF].

By definition one has:
\begin{align}
E_r^{pq}&=\Imop\left(Z_r^{pq}\to K^{p+q}/B_r^{pq}\right), \tag{1.3.1.1}\\
&=Z_r^{pq}/(B_r^{pq}\cap Z_r^{pq}), \tag{1.3.1.2}\\
&=\Ker\left(K^{p+q}/B_r^{pq}\to K^{p+q}/(Z_r^{pq}+B_r^{pq})\right). \tag{1.3.1.3}
\end{align}
One can also write
\begin{align}
B_r^{pq}\cap Z_r^{pq}
&=(dF^{p-r+1}+F^{p+1})\cap(d^{-1}F^{p+r}\cap F^p) \notag\\
&=(dF^{p-r+1}\cap F^p)+F^{p+1}\cap d^{-1}F^{p+r}, \tag{1.3.1.4}
\end{align}
since $dF^{p-r+1}\subset d^{-1}F^{p+r}$ and $F^{p+1}\subset F^p$.


For $r<\infty$, the $E_r$ form a complex graded by the degree $p-r(p+q)$, and $E_{r+1}$ is expressed as the cohomology of this complex:
\[
\tag{1.3.1.5}
E_{r+1}^{p,q}=H\bigl(E_r^{p-r,q+r-1}\xrightarrow{d_r}E_r^{p,q}\xrightarrow{d_r}E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}\bigr).
\]
For $r=0$ one has
\[
\tag{1.3.1.6}
E_0^{**}=\Gr_F(K^*).
\]

\paragraph{Proposition (1.3.2).} Let $K$ be a complex endowed with a biregular filtration $F$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the spectral sequence defined by $F$ degenerates: $E_1=E_\infty$;
\item the morphisms $d:K^i\to K^{i+1}$ are strictly compatible with the filtrations.
\end{enumerate}
We check this when $\calA$ is a category of modules. For fixed $p$ and $q$, the hypothesis that the arrows $d_r$ with source $E_r^{p,q}$ are zero for $r\geq1$ means that, if $x\in F^p(K^{p+q})$ satisfies $dx\in F^{p+r}(K^{p+q+1})$, then there exists $y$ in $K^{p+q}$ such that $dy=0$ and such that $x$ and $y$ have the same image in $E_r^{p,q}$. Modifying $y$ by a boundary and putting $z=x-y$, one obtains
\[
\forall x\in F^p(K^{p+q})\;\bigl(dx\in F^{p+1}(K^{p+q+1})\Rightarrow
\exists z\in F^{p+1}(K^{p+q}),\ dz=dx\bigr),
\]
or, in other words,
\[
\tag{1}
F^{p+1}(K^{p+q+1})\cap dF^p(K^{p+q})=dF^{p+1}(K^{p+q}).
\]
If this condition holds for all $p$ and $q$, then by induction on $r$ one has
\[
F^{p+r}\cap dF^p=dF^{p+r},
\]
which, for $p+r$ large, becomes
\[
\tag{2}
F^p\cap dK=dF^p.
\]
Assertion (2) trivially implies (i), and is equivalent to (ii). This proves (1.3.2).

\paragraph{(1.3.3)} If $(K,F)$ is a filtered complex, we shall denote by $\Dec(K)$ the complex $K$ endowed with the shifted filtration
\[
\Dec(F)^pK^n=Z_1^{p+n,-p}.
\]
This filtration is compatible with the differentials:
\[
dZ_1^{p+n,-p}\subset F^{p+n+1}(K^{n+1})\cap\Ker(d)
       \subset Z_\infty^{p+n+1,-p}\subset Z_1^{p+n+1,-p}.
\]
Since
\[
\tag{1.3.3.1}
Z_1^{p+1+n,-p-1}\subset F^{p+1+n}(K^n)\subset B_1^{p+n,-p}\subset Z_1^{p+n,-p},
\]
the evident map from $Z_1^{p+n,-p}/Z_1^{p+1+n,-p-1}$ to $Z_1^{p+n,-p}/B_1^{p+n,-p}$ is a morphism
\[
\tag{1.3.3.2}
u:E_0^{p,n-p}(\Dec K)\longrightarrow E_1^{p+n,-p}(K).
\]

\paragraph{Proposition (1.3.4).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The morphisms (1.3.3.2) form a morphism of graded complexes from $E_0(\Dec K)$ to $E_1(K)$.
\item This morphism induces an isomorphism on cohomology.
\item Step by step, via (1.3.1.5), it induces isomorphisms of graded complexes
\[
E_r(\Dec(K))\simeq E_{r+1}(K)\qquad(r\ge1).
\]
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} Let $F'$ be the filtration of $K$ defined by
\[
F'^p(K^n)=\Dec(F)^{p-n}(K^n)=Z_1^{p,n-p}.
\]
There are trivial isomorphisms, compatible with the $d_r$ and with (1.3.1.5),
\[
\tag{1.3.4.1}
E_r^{p,n-p}(\Dec K)=E_r^{p+n,-p}(K,F').
\]
The map $u$ is deduced from (1.3.4.1) and from the identity morphism
\[
(K,F')\longrightarrow(K,F).
\]
This proves (i), and it remains to verify that, for $r\ge2$,
\[
E_r^{p,q}(K,F')\simeq E_r^{p,q}(K,F).
\]
Indeed one has
\[
Z_r^{p,q}(K,F')=Z_r^{p,q}(K,F)\qquad(r\ge1),
\]
and
\[
Z_r^{p,q}(K,F')\cap B_r^{p,q}(K,F')=Z_r^{p,q}(K,F)\cap B_r^{p,q}(K,F)\qquad(r\ge2),
\]
and then applies (1.3.1.2).

\paragraph{(1.3.5)} The construction (1.3.3) is not self-dual. The dual construction consists in defining
\[
\Dec^*(F)^pK^n=B_1^{p+n-1,-p+1}.
\]
One then has morphisms
\[
E_0^{p,n-p}(\Dec K)\longrightarrow E_1^{p+n,-p}(K)\longrightarrow E_0^{p,n-p}(\Dec^*K)
\]
and, for $r\ge1$, isomorphisms
\[
E_r^{p,n-p}(\Dec K)\simeq E_{r+1}^{p+n,-p}(K)\simeq E_r^{p,n-p}(\Dec^*K).
\]
Recall that a morphism of complexes is called a quasi-isomorphism if it induces an isomorphism on cohomology.

\paragraph{Definition (1.3.6).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A morphism $f:(K,F)\to(K',F')$ of filtered complexes with biregular filtration is a filtered quasi-isomorphism if $\Gr_F(f)$ is a quasi-isomorphism, i.e. if the $E_1^{p,q}(f)$ are isomorphisms.
\item A morphism $f:(K,F,W)\to(K',F',W')$ between biregular bifiltered complexes is a bifiltered quasi-isomorphism if $\Gr_F\Gr_W(f)$ is a quasi-isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(1.3.7)} Let $K$ be a differential complex of objects of $\calA$, endowed with two filtrations $F$ and $W$. Let $E_r^{p,q}$ be the spectral sequence defined by $W$. The filtration $F$ induces several filtrations on the $E_r^{p,q}$, which we shall compare.

\paragraph{(1.3.8)} Formula (1.3.1.2) identifies $E_r^{p,q}$ with a quotient of a subobject of $K^{p+q}$. Thus the term $E_r^{p,q}$ receives a filtration $F_d$ induced by $F$, called the first direct filtration.

\paragraph{(1.3.9)} Dually, formula (1.3.1.3) identifies $E_r^{p,q}$ with a subobject of a quotient of $K^{p+q}$, and hence gives a new filtration $F_{d*}$ induced by $F$, the second direct filtration.

\paragraph{Lemma (1.3.10).} On $E_0$ and $E_1$ one has $F_d=F_{d*}$. For $r=0$ or $1$, one has $B_r\subset Z_r$, and one applies (1.1.9).

\paragraph{(1.3.11)} Formula (1.3.1.5) identifies $E_{r+1}^{p,q}$ with a quotient of a subobject of $E_r^{p,q}$. The recurrent filtration $F_r$ of the $E_r^{p,q}$ is defined by the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item on $E_0^{p,q}$, $F_r=F_d=F_{d*}$;
\item on $E_{r+1}^{p,q}$, the recurrent filtration is the one induced by the recurrent filtration of $E_r^{p,q}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(1.3.12)} The definitions (1.3.8) and (1.3.9) still make sense for $r=\infty$. If the filtration of $K$ is biregular, the direct filtrations of $E_\infty^{p,q}$ coincide with those of $E_r^{p,q}=E_\infty^{p,q}$ for $r$ large enough, and the recurrent filtration of $E_\infty^{p,q}$ is defined by requiring it to coincide with that of $E_r^{p,q}$ for $r$ large enough.

The filtrations $F$ and $W$ each induce a filtration of $H^*(K)$, and $E_\infty^{p,q}=\Gr_W^p(H^{p+q}(K))$. The filtration $F$ of $H^*(K)$ therefore induces a new filtration on $E_\infty^{p,q}$.

\paragraph{Proposition (1.3.13).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For the first direct filtration, the morphisms $d_r$ are compatible with filtrations. If $E_{r+1}^{p,q}$ is regarded as a quotient of a subobject of $E_r^{p,q}$, then the first direct filtration on $E_{r+1}^{p,q}$ is finer than the filtration induced by the first direct filtration on $E_r^{p,q}$.
\item Dually, the morphisms $d_r$ are compatible with the second direct filtration, and the second direct filtration on $E_{r+1}^{p,q}$ is less fine than the filtration induced by that of $E_r^{p,q}$.
\item $F_d(E_r^{p,q})\subset F_r(E_r^{p,q})\subset F_{d*}(E_r^{p,q})$.
\item On $E_\infty^{p,q}$, the filtration induced by the filtration $F$ of $H^*(K)$ is finer than the first direct filtration and less fine than the second.
\end{enumerate}
The first assertion is evident, the second is its dual, and the third follows by induction. The first assertion of (iv) is easy to check, and the second is dual to it.

\paragraph{(1.3.14)} Denote by $\Dec(K)$, respectively by $\Dec^*(K)$, the complex $K$ endowed with the filtrations $\Dec(W)$ and $F$, respectively $\Dec^*(W)$ and $F$.

It is clear from (1.3.4.1) that the isomorphism (1.3.4) transforms the first direct filtration of $E_r(\Dec K)$ into the first direct filtration of $E_{r+1}(K)$, for $r\ge1$. The dual isomorphism (1.3.5) transforms the second direct filtration of $E_r(\Dec^*K)$ into the second direct filtration of $E_{r+1}(K)$.

\paragraph{Lemma (1.3.15).} If the filtration $F$ is biregular, and if, on the $\Gr_W^p(K)$, the morphisms $d$ are strictly compatible with the filtration induced by $F$, then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item the morphism (1.3.3.2) of graded complexes filtered by $F$
\[
u:\Gr_{\Dec(W)}(K)\longrightarrow E_1(K,W)
\]
is a filtered quasi-isomorphism;
\item dually, the morphism (1.3.5)
\[
u:E_1(K,W)\longrightarrow \Gr_{\Dec^*(W)}(K)
\]
is a filtered quasi-isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
By duality it is enough to prove (i). By (1.3.3) and (1.3.4), the complex $E_1(K,W)$, filtered by $F$, is a quotient of the filtered complex $\Gr_{\Dec(W)}(K)$. Let $U$ be the filtered kernel complex; it is acyclic by (1.3.4) (ii). The long exact cohomology sequence associated with the exact sequence of complexes
\[
0\to\Gr_F^p(U)\to\Gr_F^p(\Gr_{\Dec(W)}(K))\to\Gr_F^p(E_1(K,W))\to0
\]
shows that $u$ is a filtered quasi-isomorphism if and only if $\Gr_F^p(U)$ is an acyclic complex. By (1.3.2), and because $U$ is acyclic, this amounts to requiring that the differentials of $U$ be strictly compatible with the filtration $F$. From (1.3.3.1) one obtains that $U$ is the sum, over $p$, of the complexes
\[
(U^p)^n=B_1^{p+n,-p}/Z_1^{p+1+n,-p-1},
\]
endowed with the filtration induced by $F$.

Each differential $d$ of each of the complexes $U^p$ is included in a commutative diagram of filtered objects of the following type, where, for simplicity, the total or complementary degree has been omitted:
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
B^p/Z^{p+1} & \xrightarrow{\ d\ } & & & B^{p+1}/Z^{p+1}\\[0.5em]
\uparrow & & \Coim(d) & \xrightarrow{(5)} & \Imop(d)\ \downarrow\\[0.5em]
W^{p+1}/Z^{p+1} & \xrightarrow{(3)} & & & B^{p+1}/W^{p+2}\\[0.5em]
\uparrow & & (2) & & \downarrow\\[0.5em]
W^{p+1}/W^{p+2} & \xrightarrow{(1)} & & & W^{p+1}/W^{p+2}
\end{array}
\]
By hypothesis, the morphism (1) is strict. Since the square (2) is just the canonical decomposition of (1), the arrow (3) is a filtered isomorphism. The arrows of the trapezium (4) are isomorphisms; hence they are filtered isomorphisms, because (3) is one. That (5) is a filtered isomorphism means that $d$ is strict. This proves (1.3.15).

\paragraph{Theorem (1.3.16).} Let $K$ be a complex endowed with two filtrations $W$ and $F$, with $F$ biregular. Let $r_0\ge0$ be an integer, and suppose that, for $0\le r<r_0$, the differentials of the graded complex $E_r(K,W)$ are strictly compatible with the filtration $F_r$. Then, for $r\le r_0+1$, one has
\[
F_d=F_r=F_{d*}\quad\text{on }E_r^{p,q}.
\]
The theorem is proved by induction on $r_0$. For $r_0=0$ the hypothesis is empty, and one applies (1.3.10) and (1.3.13) (iii). For $r_0\ge1$, by the induction hypothesis, one has $F_d=F_r=F_{d*}$ on $E_r^{p,q}$ for $r\le r_0$.

By (1.3.15), the morphism $u:E_0(\Dec K)\to E_1(K)$ is a filtered quasi-isomorphism. It therefore induces a filtered isomorphism from $H^*(\Dec K)$ to $H^*(E_1(K))$:
\[
u:(E_1(\Dec K),F_r)\iso(E_2(K),F_r).
\]
Step by step, it follows that the canonical isomorphism from $E_s(\Dec K)$ to $E_{s+1}(K)$ is a filtered isomorphism for the recurrent filtration. On $E_1(\Dec(K))$, $F_r=F_d$ (1.3.10), and we already know (1.3.14) that $u'$ is a filtered isomorphism
\[
u':(E_1(\Dec K),F_d)\iso(E_2(K),F_d).
\]
Thus on $E_2(K)$ one has $F_d=F_r$. Together with the dual result, this proves (1.3.16) for $r_0=1$.

Assume now that $r_0\ge2$. Then the arrows $d_1$ of $E_1(K)$ are strictly compatible with the filtrations; hence so are the arrows $d_0$ of $E_0(\Dec K)$, since $u$ induces an isomorphism of spectral sequences, and one applies the criterion (1.3.2).

For $0<s<r_0-1$, the isomorphism $(E_s(\Dec K),F_r)\simeq(E_{s+1}(K),F_r)$ shows that the $d_s$ are strictly compatible with the recurrent filtrations. By the induction hypothesis one therefore has $F_d=F_r$ on $E_s(\Dec K)$ for $s\le r_0$. The isomorphism $(E_s(\Dec K),F_d)\simeq(E_{s+1}(K),F_d)$ (1.3.13) then shows that $F_d=F_r$ on $E_r(K)$ for $r\le r_0+1$. Together with the dual result, this proves (1.3.16).

\paragraph{Corollary (1.3.17).} Under the general hypotheses of (1.3.16), suppose that, for every $r$, the differentials $d_r$ are strictly compatible with the recurrent filtrations of the $E_r$. Then, on $E_\infty$, the filtrations $F_d$, $F_r$, and $F_{d*}$ coincide, and coincide with the filtration induced by the filtration $F$ of $H^*(K)$.

This follows at once from (1.3.16) and (1.3.13) (iv).

\subsection*{1.4. Hypercohomology of filtered complexes}

In this section we recall some standard constructions in hypercohomology. We do not use the language of derived categories, which would be the most natural here.

Throughout this section, by a ``complex'' we mean a complex bounded below.

\paragraph{(1.4.1)} Let $T$ be a left exact functor from an abelian category $\calA$ to an abelian category $\calB$. Assume that every object of $\calA$ injects into an injective object; the derived functors $R^iT:\calA\to\calB$ are therefore defined. An object $A$ of $\calA$ will be called acyclic for $T$ if $R^iT(A)=0$ for $i>0$.

\paragraph{(1.4.2)} Let $(A,F)$ be an object with a finite filtration, and let $TF$ be the filtration of $TA$ by its subobjects $TF^pA$. If $\Gr_F^p(A)$ is $T$-acyclic, then the $F^p(A)$ are $T$-acyclic as successive extensions of $T$-acyclic objects. The image under $T$ of the sequence
\[
0\to F^{p+1}(A)\to F^p(A)\to\Gr_F^p(A)\to0
\]
is therefore exact, and
\[
\tag{1.4.2.1}
\Gr_{TF}^pTA\iso T\Gr_F^pA.
\]

\paragraph{(1.4.3)} Let $A$ be an object with two finite filtrations $F$ and $W$ such that $\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qA$ is $T$-acyclic. The objects $\Gr_F^pA$ and $\Gr_W^qA$ are then $T$-acyclic, as are the $F^p(A)\cap W^q(A)$. The sequences
\[
0\to T(F^p\cap W^{q+1})\to T(F^p\cap W^q)\to T\bigl((F^p\cap W^q)/(F^p\cap W^{q+1})\bigr)\to0
\]
are therefore exact, and $T(F^p(\Gr_W^qA))$ is the image in $T(\Gr_W^qA)$ of $T(F^p\cap W^q)$. The diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
T(F^p\cap W^q) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & T(F^p\Gr_W^qA) \arrow[r] & T\Gr_W^qA \arrow[d]\\
TF^p\cap TW^q \arrow[rr] && \Gr_{TW}^qTA
\end{tikzcd}
\]
then shows that the isomorphism (1.4.2.1) relative to $W$ carries the filtration $\Gr_{TW}(TF)$ to the filtration $T(\Gr_W(F))$.

\paragraph{(1.4.4)} Let $K$ be a complex of objects of $\calA$. The hypercohomology objects $R^iT(K)$ are computed as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item choose a quasi-isomorphism $i:K\to K'$ such that the components of $K'$ are acyclic for $T$. For example, one may take for $K'$ the simple complex associated with a Cartan--Eilenberg injective resolution of $K$;
\item put
\[
R^iT(K)=H^i(T(K')).
\]
\end{enumerate}
One checks that $R^iT(K)$ does not depend on the choice of $K'$, depends functorially on $K$, and that a quasi-isomorphism $f:K_1\to K_2$ induces isomorphisms
\[
R^iT(f):R^iT(K_1)\iso R^iT(K_2).
\]

\paragraph{(1.4.5)} Let $F$ be a biregular filtration of $K$. A filtered $T$-acyclic resolution of $K$ is a filtered quasi-isomorphism $i:K\to K'$ from $K$ to a biregular filtered complex such that the $\Gr_F^p(K'^n)$ are acyclic for $T$. If $K'$ is such a resolution, the $K'^n$ are acyclic for $T$ and the filtered complex $T(K')$ defines a spectral sequence
\[
E_1^{p,q}=R^{p+q}T(\Gr_F^pK)\Rightarrow R^{p+q}T(K).
\]
This is independent of the choice of $K'$. It is called the hypercohomology spectral sequence of the filtered complex $K$. It depends functorially on $K$, and a filtered quasi-isomorphism induces an isomorphism of spectral sequences.

The differentials $d_1$ of this spectral sequence are the connecting morphisms defined by the short exact sequences
\[
0\to\Gr_F^{p+1}K\to F^pK/F^{p+2}K\to\Gr_F^pK\to0.
\]

\paragraph{(1.4.6)} Let $K$ be a complex. Denote by $\tau_{\le p}(K)$ the following subcomplex:
\[
\tau_{\le p}(K)^n=\begin{cases}
K^n,& n<p,\\
\Ker(d),& n=p,\\
0,& n>p.
\end{cases}
\]
The filtration, called canonical, of $K$ by the $\tau_{\le p}(K)$ is obtained by shifting the trivial filtration $G$ for which $G^0(K)=K$ and $G^1(K)=0$. For the canonical filtration one has
\[
E_1^{p,q}=\begin{cases}
0,& p+q\ne -p,\\
H^{-p},& p+q=-p.
\end{cases}
\]
A quasi-isomorphism $f:K\to K'$ is automatically a filtered quasi-isomorphism for the canonical filtrations.

\paragraph{(1.4.7)} The subcomplexes $\sigma_{\ge p}(K)$ of $K$,
\[
\sigma_{\ge p}(K)^n=\begin{cases}
0,& n<p,\\
K^n,& n\ge p,
\end{cases}
\]
define a biregular filtration, the stupid filtration of $K$.

The hypercohomology spectral sequences attached to the stupid or canonical filtrations of $K$ are the two hypercohomology spectral sequences of $K$.

\paragraph{Example (1.4.8).} Let $f:X\to Y$ be a continuous map between topological spaces, and let $\calF$ be an abelian sheaf on $X$. Let $\calF^*$ be a resolution of $\calF$ by $f_*$-acyclic sheaves. Then $R^if_*\calF\simeq\calH^i(f_*\calF^*)$. Take as $T$ the functor $\Gamma(Y,\cdot)$. The hypercohomology spectral sequence of the complex $f_*\calF^*$, endowed with its canonical filtration (1.4.6),
\[
E_1^{p,q}=H^{2p+q}(Y,R^{-p}f_*\calF)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\calF),
\]
is, up to renumbering, the Leray spectral sequence for $f$ and $\calF$.

\paragraph{(1.4.9)} Let $(K,W,F)$ be a biregular bifiltered complex. To this complex one associates:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item a spectral sequence
\[
{}_WE_1^{p,n-p}=H^n(\Gr_W^pK)\Rightarrow H^n(K),
\]
whose differentials ${}_Wd_1$ are the connecting morphisms deduced from the short exact sequences
\[
0\to\Gr_W^{p+1}K\to W^p(K)/W^{p+2}(K)\to\Gr_W^pK\to0;
\]
\item an analogous spectral sequence for the filtration $F$;
\item exact squares
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small,row sep=large]
0 \arrow[d] & 0 \arrow[d] & 0 \arrow[d] & \\
0 \arrow[r] & \Gr_F^{p+1}\Gr_W^{q+1}K \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & F^p/F^{p+2}(\Gr_W^{q+1}K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \Gr_F^{p}\Gr_W^{q+1}K \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & 0\\
0 \arrow[r] & \Gr_F^{p+1}(W^q/W^{q+2}K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & F^p/F^{p+2}(W^q/W^{q+2}K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \Gr_F^{p}(W^q/W^{q+2}K) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & 0\\
0 \arrow[r] & \Gr_F^{p+1}\Gr_W^{q}K \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & F^p/F^{p+2}\Gr_W^{q}K \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & \Gr_F^{p}\Gr_W^{q}K \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & 0\\
& 0 & 0 & 0
\end{tikzcd}
\]
The exterior rows and columns of this square define connecting morphisms
\[
{}_{F,W}d_1:H^n\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK\longrightarrow H^{n+1}\Gr_F^{p+1}\Gr_W^qK,
\]
\[
{}_{W,F}d_1:H^n\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK\longrightarrow H^{n+1}\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^{q+1}K.
\]
These morphisms satisfy
\[
{}_{F,W}d_1\circ{}_{W,F}d_1+{}_{W,F}d_1\circ{}_{F,W}d_1=0,
\qquad
{}_{F,W}d_1^2=0,
\qquad
{}_{W,F}d_1^2=0.
\]
The morphisms ${}_{F,W}d_1$ are the $d_1$ morphisms of the spectral sequences $E^{(q)}$, with term $E_1^{p,n-p-q}$ equal to
\[
\tag{1.4.9.1}
E_1^{p,n-p-q}=H^n(\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK)\Rightarrow H^n(\Gr_W^qK)={}_WE_1^{q,n-q},
\]
defined by the filtered complex $\Gr_W^q(K)$. This spectral sequence abuts to the filtration induced by $F$ on $H^*\Gr_W^qK$. Similarly, the ${}_{W,F}d_1$ are the $d_1$ of spectral sequences with the same initial terms
\[
\tag{1.4.9.2}
E_1^{p,q,n-p-q}=H^n(\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK)\Rightarrow H^n(\Gr_F^pK).
\]
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
& H^n\Gr_W^qK & \\[0.4em]
{}^{(F,{}_Wd_1)}\nearrow & & \searrow^{({}_Wd_1)}\\[0.4em]
H^n\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK & & H^nK\\[0.4em]
{}_{(W,{}_Fd_1)}\searrow & & \nearrow_{({}_Fd_1)}\\[0.4em]
& H^n\Gr_F^pK &
\end{array}
\]
\end{enumerate}
These constructions are symmetric in $F$ and $W$, via the isomorphism
\[
\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^q\simeq\Gr_W^q\Gr_F^p.
\]

\paragraph{(1.4.10)} One may also interpret the ${}_{W,F}d_1$ as the initial morphisms of a morphism of spectral sequences (1.4.9.2), abutting to ${}_Wd_1$. Indeed, let $C^q$ be the cone of the morphism
\[
W^q(K)/W^{q+2}(K)\longrightarrow\Gr_W^q(K).
\]
In the diagram
\[
\Sigma:\Gr_W^{q+1}(K)[1]\longrightarrow C^q\longleftarrow\Gr_W^q(K),
\]
$u$ is a quasi-isomorphism, and one has
\[
{}_Wd_1=H(u)^{-1}\circ H(i).
\]
In fact, $u$ is even a filtered quasi-isomorphism for $F$, and the preceding construction defines a morphism from the spectral sequence defined by $(\Gr_W^q(K),F)$ to the one defined by $(\Gr_W^{q+1}(K)[1],F)$, a morphism which abuts to ${}_Wd_1$. The initial term of this morphism, deduced from $\Gr_F(\Sigma)$, is precisely ${}_{W,F}d_1$.

\paragraph{(1.4.11)} These constructions pass unchanged to hypercohomology. Let $K$ be a complex endowed with two biregular filtrations $F$ and $W$.

A bifiltered $T$-acyclic resolution of $K$ is a bifiltered quasi-isomorphism $i:K\to K'$ such that the $\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^q(K'^n)$ are $T$-acyclic. Such resolutions always exist. In the special case where $\calA$ is the category of sheaves of $A$-modules on a topological space $X$, and where $T$ is the functor $\Gamma$ from $\calA$ to $A$-modules, one example of a bifiltered $T$-acyclic resolution of $K$ is the simple complex associated with the Godement double-resolution complex $\mathcal C^*(K)$ of $K$, filtered by the $\mathcal C^*(F^p(K))$ and the $\mathcal C^*(W^q(K))$. Since $\mathcal C^*$ is exact, one indeed has
\[
\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^q(\mathcal C^*(K))\simeq \mathcal C^*(\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^q(K)).
\]
No other case will be needed here.

If $K'$ is a bifiltered $T$-acyclic resolution of $K$, the complex $TK'$ is filtered by the $TF^pK'$ and the $TW^qK'$ (1.4.3). Moreover, $\Gr_W^q(K')$ is a filtered $T$-acyclic resolution, for $F$, of $\Gr_W^q(K)$, $\Gr_F^p(K')$ is a filtered $T$-acyclic resolution, for $W$, of $\Gr_F^p(K)$, $\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^q(K')$ is a $T$-acyclic resolution of $\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^q(K)$, and
\[
T\Gr_F^pK'\simeq\Gr_F^pTK'\quad\text{(as a $W$-filtered complex)},
\]
\[
T\Gr_W^qK'\simeq\Gr_W^qTK'\quad\text{(as an $F$-filtered complex)},
\]
\[
T\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK'\simeq\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qTK'.
\]

\paragraph{Lemma (1.4.12).} Under the hypotheses of (1.4.11):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The initial terms of the hypercohomology spectral sequences
\[
\tag{1}
{}_WE_1^{p,n-p}=R^nT(\Gr_W^pK)\Rightarrow R^nT(K)
\]
\[
\tag{2}
{}_FE_1^{p,n-p}=R^nT(\Gr_F^pK)\Rightarrow R^nT(K)
\]
are the abutments of the hypercohomology spectral sequences of the filtered complexes $\Gr_W^qK$ and $\Gr_F^pK$, with $E_1$ term given by
\[
\tag{3}
E_1^{p,n-p-q}=R^nT(\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK)\Rightarrow {}_WE_1^{q,n-q},
\]
\[
\tag{4}
E_1^{q,n-p-q}=R^nT(\Gr_F^p\Gr_W^qK)\Rightarrow {}_FE_1^{p,n-p}.
\]
\item The filtration of ${}_WE_1^{q,n-q}$, the abutment of the spectral sequence (3), is the filtration of ${}_WE_1^{q,n-q}(TK')$ induced by the filtration $F$ of $TK'$.
\item The differentials of the complexes $\Gr_W^q(T(K'))$ are strictly compatible with the filtration $F$ if and only if the hypercohomology spectral sequences (3) degenerate at $E_1$.
\item The morphisms $d_1$ of the spectral sequence (4) are the initial terms of degree-one morphisms of spectral sequences (3) abutting to the morphisms $d_1$ of the spectral sequence (1).
\end{enumerate}
Assertions (i) and (iv) follow from (1.4.9) and (1.4.10) applied to $TK'$, through the isomorphisms (1.4.11). Assertion (ii) is then immediate from the definition of the recurrent filtration $F$, identical with the direct filtrations by (1.3.10) and (1.3.13) (iii), and assertion (iii) follows from (1.3.2).

\section*{2. Hodge structures}
\subsection*{2.1. Pure structures}

\paragraph{(2.1.1)} In what follows, $\C$ will denote an algebraic closure of $\R$: we do not assume that a root $i$ of the equation $x^2+1=0$ has been chosen. The theory will be invariant under complex conjugation (cf. (2.1.14)).

\paragraph{(2.1.2)} Let $S$ denote the real algebraic group $\C^*$, obtained by Weil restriction of scalars from $\C$ to $\R$ of the group $\Gm$:
\[
S=\prod_{\C/\R}\Gm,
\qquad
S(\R)=\C^*.
\]
The group $S$ is a torus, i.e. it is connected and of multiplicative type. It is therefore described by the finitely generated free abelian group
\[
X(S)=\Hom(S_\C,\Gm)=\Hom(S,\Gm)(\C)
\]
of its complex characters, endowed with the action of $\Gal(\C/\R)=\Z/(2)$.

The group $X(S)$ has generators $z$ and $\bar z$, inducing respectively the identity and complex conjugation:
\[
\C^*=S(\R)\longrightarrow S(\C)\longrightarrow \Gm(\C)=\C^*.
\]
Complex conjugation exchanges $z$ and $\bar z$.

\paragraph{(2.1.3)} There is a canonical map
\[
\tag{2.1.3.1}
w:\Gm\longrightarrow S.
\]




which, on real points, induces the inclusion of $\R^*$ into $\C^*$. One has
\[
\tag{2.1.3.2}
z w=\bar z w=\operatorname{Id}.
\]
There is also a map
\[
\tag{2.1.3.3}
N:S\longrightarrow\Gm
\]
which, on real points, is identified with the norm $N_{\C/\R}:\C^*\to\R^*$. One has
\[
\tag{2.1.3.4}
N=z\bar z
\]
and
\[
\tag{2.1.3.5}
N\circ w=(x\mapsto x^2).
\]

\paragraph{Definition (2.1.4).} A real Hodge structure is a finite-dimensional real vector space $V$ endowed with an action of the real algebraic group $S$.

\paragraph{(2.1.5)} By the general theory of groups of multiplicative type, giving a real Hodge structure on $V$ is equivalent to giving a bigrading
\[
V_\C=\C\otimes_\R V=\bigoplus_{p,q}V^{pq}
\]
such that $\overline{V^{pq}}=V^{qp}$. The action of $S$ and the bigrading determine one another by the condition:
\[
\tag{2.1.5.1}
\text{on }V^{pq},\quad S\text{ acts by multiplication by }z^p\bar z^q.
\]

\paragraph{(2.1.6)} Let $(\C,\times)$ be the multiplicative monoid, and put
\[
\overline S=\prod_{\C/\R}(\C,\times).
\]
If $V$ is a real vector space, giving an action of $\overline S$ on $V$ is equivalent to giving on $V$ a bigrading such that $\overline{V^{pq}}=V^{qp}$ and $V^{pq}=0$ for $p<0$ or $q<0$.

\paragraph{(2.1.7)} Let $V$ be a real Hodge structure, defined by a representation $\sigma$ of $S$ and by a bigrading $V^{pq}$. The grading of $V_\C$ by
\[
V_\C^n=\sum_{p+q=n}V^{pq}
\]
is then defined over $\R$. It is called the weight grading. On $V^n=V\cap V_\C^n$, the representation $\sigma w$ of $\Gm$ is multiplication by $x^n$.

We shall say that $V$ is of weight $n$ if $V^{pq}=0$ for $p+q\ne n$, i.e. if $\sigma w$ is multiplication by $x^n$.

\paragraph{(2.1.8)} Let $V$ be a real Hodge structure. The Hodge filtration on $V_\C$ is defined by
\[
F^p(V_\C)=\sum_{p'\ge p}V^{p'q'}.
\]
By (1.2.6), one has:

\paragraph{Proposition (2.1.9).} Let $n$ be an integer. The construction (2.1.8) establishes an equivalence of categories between:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item the category of real Hodge structures of weight $n$;
\item the category of pairs consisting of a finite-dimensional real vector space $V$ and a filtration $F$ on $V_\C=\C\otimes_\R V$ which is $n$-opposed to its complex conjugate $\overline F$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Definition (2.1.10).} A Hodge structure $H$ of weight $n$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item a finitely generated $\Z$-module $H_\Z$, the ``integral lattice'';
\item a real Hodge structure of weight $n$ on $H_\R=\R\otimes_\Z H_\Z$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(2.1.11)} A morphism $f:H\to H'$ is a homomorphism $f:H_\Z\to H'_\Z$ such that $f_\R:H_\R\to H'_\R$ is compatible with the action of $S$, that is, such that $f_\C$ is compatible with the bigrading, or with the Hodge filtration.

Hodge structures of weight $n$ form an abelian category. If $H$ has weight $n$ and $H'$ has weight $n'$, one defines a Hodge structure $H\otimes H'$ of weight $n+n'$ by the formulas:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item $(H\otimes H')_\Z=H_\Z\otimes H'_\Z$;
\item the action of $S$ on $(H\otimes H')_\R=H_\R\otimes H'_\R$ is the tensor product of the actions of $S$ on $H_\R$ and $H'_\R$.
\end{enumerate}
The bigrading, respectively the Hodge filtration, of $(H\otimes H')_\C=H_\C\otimes H'_\C$ is the tensor product of the bigradings, respectively of the Hodge filtrations (cf. (1.1.12)), of $H_\C$ and $H'_\C$.

Similarly one defines the Hodge structure $\Hom(H,H')$, of weight $n'-n$, the Hodge structures $\bigwedge^pH$, of weight $pn$, and the dual Hodge structure $H^*$.

The preceding internal Hom and the group of homomorphisms are related by the following remark.

\paragraph{Remark (2.1.11.1).} $\Hom(H,H')$ is the subgroup of
\[
\Hom(H,H')_\Z=\Hom_\Z(H_\Z,H'_\Z)
\]
formed by the elements of type $(0,0)$.

The actions of $S$ on $H_\R$, $H'_\R$, and $\Hom(H,H')_\R=\Hom(H_\R,H'_\R)$ are indeed related by
\[
s(f(x))=s(f)(s(x)).
\]
For $f$ to be of type $(0,0)$, i.e. invariant under $S$, therefore means that it commutes with the action of $S$.

\paragraph{(2.1.12)} For $A$ a noetherian subring of $\R$, an $A$-Hodge structure of weight $n$ is defined as consisting of a finite-type $A$-module $H_A$ and of a real Hodge structure of weight $n$ on $H_\R=\R\otimes_AH_A$. These definitions are used mainly for $A=\Q$. A Hodge $A$-structure consists of a finite-type $A$-module $H_A$ and a real Hodge structure on $H_A\otimes_A\R$, such that the weight grading is defined over the field of fractions of $A$.

\paragraph{Definition (2.1.13).} The Tate Hodge structure $\Z(1)$ is the Hodge structure of weight $-2$, of rank $1$, purely of bidegree $(-1,-1)$, with integral lattice $2\pi i\Z\subset\C$.

The action of $S$ is therefore multiplication by the inverse of the norm (2.1.3.3). For $n\in\Z$, define $\Z(n)$ to be the $n$-th tensor power of $\Z(1)$: $\Z(n)$ is the Hodge structure of weight $-2n$, rank $1$, purely of bidegree $(-n,-n)$, with integral lattice $(2\pi i)^n\Z\subset\C$. The action of $S$ is multiplication by $N(x)^{-n}$.

\paragraph{(2.1.14)} The choice in $\C$ of a solution $i$ of the equation $x^2+1=0$ determines on every complex variety $X$ purely of dimension $n$ an orientation $\ornt_i(X)$. When $i$ is changed to $-i$, one has
\[
\ornt_{-i}(X)=(-1)^n\ornt_i(X).
\]
The choice of $i$ also defines an element $C$ of order $4$ in $S(\R)$: the image of $i$ under the isomorphism $S(\R)\simeq\C^*$.

Finally it defines an isomorphism between $\Z$ and the integral lattice of $\Z(n)$: multiplication by $(2\pi i)^n$.

Whenever $i$, an orientation of $X$, $C$, or an identification $\Z\simeq\Z(n)_\Z$ occurs in a formula, it will in principle be understood that they are subordinated to the same choice of $i$, and that changing $i$ to $-i$ would give an equivalent definition or formula.

\paragraph{Definition (2.1.15).} A polarization of a Hodge structure $H$ of weight $n$ is a homomorphism
\[
(x,y):H\otimes H\longrightarrow\Z(-n)
\]
such that the real bilinear form $(2\pi i)^n(x,Cy)$ on $H_\R$ is symmetric and positive definite.

\paragraph{(2.1.16)} The real Tate Hodge structure is the real Hodge structure $\R(1)$ underlying $\Z(1)$. Similarly one defines $\R(n)$ underlying $\Z(n)$. A polarization of a real Hodge structure $H$ of weight $n$ is a homomorphism
\[
(x,y):H\otimes H\longrightarrow\R(-n)
\]
such that the real bilinear form $(2\pi i)^n(x,Cy)$ on $H_\R$ is symmetric and positive definite. A polarization is entirely determined by the positive-definite quadratic form $(2\pi i)^n(x,Cy)$ on $H_\R$, subject only to the condition that it be invariant under the compact subtorus of $S$, the kernel of $N$.

One has
\[
(x,y)=(Cx,Cy)=(y,C^2x)=(-1)^n(y,x).
\]
The form $(x,y)$ is therefore symmetric or alternating according to the parity of $n$.

\paragraph{(2.1.17)} The reader will generalize these definitions to $A$-Hodge structures of weight $n$ (2.1.12).

\subsection*{2.2. Hodge theory}

\paragraph{(2.2.1)} Let $X$ be a compact Kähler variety, for example smooth projective. By the holomorphic Poincaré lemma, the De Rham complex $\Omega_X^\bullet$ is a resolution of the constant sheaf $\C$. Thus one has an isomorphism
\[
H^*(X,\C)\simeq\mathbb H^*(X,\Omega_X^\bullet),
\]
and the bête filtration of $\Omega_X^\bullet$ (1.4.5) defines the hypercohomology spectral sequence
\[
\tag{2.2.1.1}
E_1^{pq}=H^q(X,\Omega_X^p)\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\C),
\]
whose abutment is the Hodge filtration of $H^r(X,\C)$.

By Hodge theory [9], [12], one has:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item The spectral sequence (2.2.1.1) degenerates: $E_1=E_\infty$.
\item The Hodge filtration of $H^n(X,\C)$ is $n$-opposed to the complex conjugate filtration.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(2.2.2)} Let $V$ be a local system of real vector spaces on $X$, i.e. a sheaf of $\R$-vector spaces locally isomorphic to a constant sheaf $\R^n$. Suppose that on $V$ there exists a bilinear form
\[
Q:V\otimes V\longrightarrow\R
\]
which is locally constant and definite. For $X$ connected, this is the case if $V$ is defined by a representation of a finite quotient of the fundamental group of $X$.

The assertions (A) and (B) above remain valid exactly as stated, without any change in the proofs, for cohomology with coefficients in $V_\C=V\otimes_\R\C$: the spectral sequence
\[
E_1^{pq}=H^q(X,\Omega_X^p(V))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X,V_\C)
\]
deduced from the De Rham resolution of $V_\C$ by $\Omega_X^\bullet(V_\C)$ degenerates and abuts to a filtration on $H^n(X,V_\C)$ which is $n$-opposed to the complex conjugate filtration.

The vector space $H^n(X,V)$ is therefore endowed with a canonical real Hodge structure of weight $n$.

\paragraph{(2.2.3)} It is shown in [2] that the statements (2.2.1) remain valid for $X$ a complete nonsingular algebraic variety, not necessarily Kähler. The proof in loc. cit., based on a reduction to the projective case via Chow's lemma and resolution of singularities, extends to the setting (2.2.2).

\paragraph{(2.2.4)} Let $\cL$ be an invertible sheaf. Here are two ways to define the class $c_1(\cL)$.

\paragraph{(2.2.4.1)} The sheaf $\cL$ defines an element $c$ in $H^1(X,\cO^*)$. Its image under $df/f:\cO^*\to\Omega_X^1$ lies in $H^1(X,\Omega_X^1)$. More precisely, $df/f$ defines a morphism of complexes
\[
\dlog:\cO^*[-1]\longrightarrow [\,0\to\Omega_X^1\to\Omega_X^2\to\cdots\,]=\sigma_{\ge1}(\Omega_X^\bullet).
\]
This complex maps to $\Omega_X^\bullet$, whence
\[
\dlog:\cO^*[-1]\longrightarrow\Omega_X^\bullet,
\]
and the image of $c$ under $\dlog$ lies in $H^2(\Omega_X^\bullet)$. This construction would still make sense for an algebraic variety over an arbitrary field $k$. For $k=\C$, one also has
\[
H^2(X,\C)\simeq H^2(X,\Omega_X^\bullet),
\]
and hence a class
\[
c_1'(\cL)\in H^2(X,\C).
\]

\paragraph{(2.2.4.2)} The exponential exact sequence
\[
0\to\Z(1)\to\cO\to\cO^*\to0
\]
defines a homomorphism
\[
\partial:H^1(X,\cO^*)\longrightarrow H^2(X,\Z(1)),
\]
hence a class
\[
c_1''(\cL)\in H^2(X,\Z(1)).
\]
If $i$ is chosen, this class is identified with
\[
c_1'''(\cL)\in H^2(X,\Z),
\]
and for $\cL=\cO(D)$, $c_1'''(\cL)$ is just the integral cohomology class defined by $D$, the orientations being defined by $i$.

\paragraph{(2.2.5)} We prove that:
\[
\tag{2.2.5.1}
-\alpha c_1''(\cL)=c_1'(\cL),
\]
for $\alpha$ the natural injection of $\Z(1)$ into $\C$, and that
\[
\tag{2.2.5.2}
-\beta c_1'''(\cL)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}c_1'(\cL),
\]
for $\beta$ the natural injection of $\Z$ into $\C$.

This is verified by contemplating the following diagram of complexes, in which $\simeq$ denotes a quasi-isomorphism, and whose upper rectangle is anticommutative up to homotopy:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.2em,row sep=1.5em]
\C \arrow[r,"\simeq"] \arrow[d,equal] & \Omega_X^\bullet & \sigma_{\ge1}\Omega^\bullet \arrow[l,dashed] \arrow[d,equal]\\
\C & {[\C\to\cO]} \arrow[l] \arrow[r,"\simeq"'] \arrow[d] & \sigma_{\ge1}\Omega^\bullet \arrow[u,equal] \arrow[d,"\dlog"]\\
\Z(1) \arrow[u] & {[\Z(1)\to\cO]} \arrow[l] \arrow[r,"\simeq"'] & \cO^*[-1].
\end{tikzcd}
\]

\paragraph{(2.2.6)} Let $X$ be a nonsingular projective variety purely of dimension $n$. A choice of $i$ defines an orientation of $X$ and an isomorphism $\Z(1)\simeq\Z$. The corresponding trace morphism
\[
H^{2n}(X,\Z(n))\longrightarrow\Z
\]
which follows from it does not depend on the choice of $i$.

According to Hodge, for $i\le n$ the morphism
\[
L^{\,n-i}=c_1''(\cO(1))^{\,n-i}:H^i(X,\Z)\longrightarrow H^{2n-i}(X,\Z(n-i))
\]
is an isomorphism and, combined with Poincaré duality
\[
H^i(X,\Z)\otimes H^{2n-i}(X,\Z(n-i))\longrightarrow
H^{2n}(X,\Z(n-i))\longrightarrow \Z(-i),
\]
it furnishes a polarization on the primitive part $\Ker(L^{\,n-i+1})$ of $H^i(X,\Z)$. It follows that the rational Hodge structures $H^i(X,\Q)$ are polarizable.

\subsection*{2.3. Mixed structures}

\paragraph{Definition (2.3.1).} A mixed Hodge structure $H$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item a finitely generated $\Z$-module $H_\Z$, the ``integral lattice'';
\item a finite increasing filtration $W$ of $H_\Q=\Q\otimes_\Z H_\Z$, called the weight filtration;
\item a finite decreasing filtration $F$ of $H_\C=\C\otimes_\Z H_\Z$, called the Hodge filtration.
\end{enumerate}
It is required that, on $H_\C$, the filtration $W_\C$ deduced from $W$ by extension of scalars, the filtration $F$, and its complex conjugate $\overline F$ form a system $(W_\C,F,\overline F)$ of three opposed filtrations ((1.2.7) and (1.2.13)).

\paragraph{(2.3.2)} Let $W$ again denote the filtration of $H_\Z$ which is the inverse image of the filtration $W$ of $H_\Q$. The axiom of mixed Hodge structures means that, for every $n$, the filtration $F$ induces on $\C\otimes_\Z\Gr_n^W(H_\Z)$ a filtration $n$-opposed to its complex conjugate. By (2.1.9), $\Gr_n^W(H_\Z)$ is endowed with a Hodge structure of weight $n$, with Hodge filtration induced by $F$.

\paragraph{Example (2.3.3).} If $H$ is a Hodge structure of weight $n$, one defines a mixed Hodge structure with the same integral lattice and the same Hodge filtration by putting
\[
W_i(H_\Q)=0\quad\text{for }i<n,\qquad
W_i(H_\Q)=H_\Q\quad\text{for }i\ge n.
\]

\paragraph{(2.3.4)} A morphism $f:H\to H'$ of mixed Hodge structures is a homomorphism $f:H_\Z\to H'_\Z$ compatible with the filtrations $W$ and $F$, and therefore compatible with $\overline F$. One immediately deduces from (1.2.10) the following theorem.

\paragraph{Theorem (2.3.5).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The category of mixed Hodge structures is abelian.
\item The kernel, respectively cokernel, of a morphism $f:H\to H'$ has as integral lattice the kernel, respectively cokernel, $K$ of $f:H_\Z\to H'_\Z$, while $K\otimes\Q$ and $K\otimes\C$ are endowed with the induced, respectively quotient, filtrations from the filtrations $W$ and $F$ of $H_\Q$ and $H_\C$, respectively of $H'_\Q$ and $H'_\C$.
\item Every morphism $f:H\to H'$ is strictly compatible with the filtration $W$ of $H_\Q$ and $H'_\Q$, and with the filtration $F$ of $H_\C$ and $H'_\C$. It induces morphisms of $\Q$-Hodge structures
\[
\Gr_n^W(f):\Gr_n^W(H_\Q)\longrightarrow\Gr_n^W(H'_\Q)
\]
and morphisms strictly compatible with the filtration induced by $W_\C$:
\[
\Gr_F^p(f):\Gr_F^p(H_\C)\longrightarrow\Gr_F^p(H'_\C).
\]
\item The functor $\Gr_n^W$ is an exact functor from the category of mixed Hodge structures to the category of $\Q$-Hodge structures of weight $n$.
\item The functor $\Gr_F^p$ is exact.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(2.3.6)} Let $H$ be a mixed Hodge structure. The $W_n(H_\Z)$, endowed with the filtrations induced by $W$ and $F$, then form mixed Hodge substructures $W_n(H)$ of $H$. The quotient $W_n(H)/W_{n-1}(H)$ is identified with $\Gr_n^W(H_\Z)$ endowed with its Hodge structure (2.3.2), (2.3.3). This Hodge structure will be denoted by $\Gr_n^W(H)$.

\paragraph{(2.3.7)} Put
\[
H^{pq}=\Gr_F^p\Gr_{\overline F}^q\Gr_{p+q}^W(H_\C)
       =\bigl(\Gr_F^p\Gr_{p+q}^W(H)\bigr)^{p,q}.
\]
The Hodge numbers of $H$ are the integers
\[
h^{pq}=\dim_\C H^{pq}.
\]
Thus the Hodge number $h^{pq}$ of $H$ is the Hodge number $h^{pq}$ of the Hodge structure $\Gr_{p+q}^W(H)$.

\paragraph{(2.3.8)} A $\Q$-mixed Hodge structure $H$ is defined as consisting of a finite-dimensional vector space $H_\Q$ over $\Q$, a finite increasing filtration $W$ of $H_\Q$, and a finite decreasing filtration $F$ of $H_\C$, the filtrations $W_\C$, $F$, and $\overline F$ being opposed. Theorem (2.3.5) generalizes trivially to this variant.

\section*{3. Hodge theory of nonsingular algebraic varieties}

\subsection*{3.1. Logarithmic poles and residues}

\paragraph{(3.1.1)} We recall some classical properties of ``logarithmic poles'' of holomorphic differential forms. The reader will find proofs in [3], II, (3.1) to (3.7), for example.

\paragraph{(3.1.2)} A divisor $Y$ in a smooth complex analytic variety $X$ will be said to have normal crossings if the inclusion of $Y$ into $X$ is locally isomorphic to the inclusion of a union of coordinate hyperplanes in $\C^n$; this does not imply that $Y$ is a union of smooth divisors. Let $Y$ be a normal-crossings divisor in $X$, and let $j$ be the inclusion of $X^*=X-Y$ in $X$. Let $\Omega_X^1\langle Y\rangle$ denote the locally free $\cO$-submodule of $j_*\Omega_{X^*}^1$ generated by $\Omega_X^1$ and by the $dz_i/z_i$, where $z_i$ is a local equation of a local irreducible component of $Y$. The sheaf $\Omega_X^p\langle Y\rangle$ of differential $p$-forms on $X$ with logarithmic pole along $Y$ is, by definition, the locally free subsheaf $\bigwedge^p\Omega_X^1\langle Y\rangle$ of $j_*\Omega_{X^*}^p$.

\paragraph{Proposition (3.1.3).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A section $\alpha$ of $j_*\Omega_{X^*}^p$ belongs to $\Omega_X^p\langle Y\rangle$ if and only if $\alpha$ and $d\alpha$ have at worst simple poles along the divisor $Y$.
\item The $\Omega_X^p\langle Y\rangle$ form the smallest subcomplex of $j_*\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet$, stable under exterior product, containing $\Omega_X^\bullet$, and containing the logarithmic differential $df/f$ of every local section, meromorphic along $Y$, of $j_*\cO_{X^*}^*$.
\end{enumerate}
The complex $\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle$ is called the logarithmic De Rham complex of $X$ along $Y$. By (3.1.3) (ii), this complex is contravariant in the pair $(X,X^*)$.

\paragraph{(3.1.4)} Locally on $X$, $Y$ is a union of smooth divisors $Y_i$, and $Y^n$, respectively $\widetilde Y^n$, denotes the union, respectively the disjoint sum, of the intersections $n$ at a time of the $Y_i$. The $Y^n$ glue to a subspace $Y^n$ of $X$, and the $\widetilde Y^n$ glue to the normalization of $Y^n$. One has $\widetilde Y^0=Y^0=X$ and puts $\widetilde Y=\widetilde Y^1$.

The two-element set of orientations of a finite set $E$ with $n$ elements is defined to be the set of generators of $\bigwedge^n\Z^E$. For $n\ge2$, this set is the set of conjugacy classes, under the alternating group, of total orderings of $E$.

If to each point $y$ of $\widetilde Y^n$ one associates the set of the $n$ local components of $Y$ which contain the image in $X$ of a neighborhood of $y$ in $\widetilde Y^n$, then one defines on $\widetilde Y^n$ a local system $E^n$ of $n$-element sets. The local system of orientations of these sets is a torsor under $\Z/(2)$. Through the inclusion of $\Z/(2)$ into $\C^*$, this torsor defines a rank-one complex local system $\varepsilon^n$ on $\widetilde Y^n$, endowed with an isomorphism
\[
(\varepsilon^n)^{\otimes2}\simeq\C.
\]
One has
\[
\varepsilon^n\simeq\bigwedge^n\C^{E^n}.
\]
Locally on $\widetilde Y^n$, $\varepsilon^n$ is endowed with two opposite isomorphisms $\pm\alpha:\varepsilon^n\iso\C$. Put
\[
\varepsilon_\Z^n=\alpha^{-1}\bigl((2\pi i)^{-n}\Z\bigr).
\]
The local system $\varepsilon^n$, endowed with $\varepsilon_\Z^n$, should be regarded as a twisted form of $\Z(-n)$ on $\widetilde Y^n$.

Let $\varepsilon_X^n$, respectively $(\varepsilon_X^n)_\Z$, denote the direct image of $\varepsilon^n$, respectively of $\varepsilon_\Z^n$, under the map from $\widetilde Y^n$ to $X$. One has
\[
\tag{3.1.4.1}
\varepsilon_X^n\simeq\bigwedge^n\varepsilon_X^1\qquad(n\ge0).
\]
If $Y$ is a union of distinct smooth divisors $(Y_i)_{i\in I}$, the choice of a total order on $I$ trivializes the $\varepsilon^n$.

\paragraph{(3.1.5)} Let $W_m(\Omega_X^k\langle Y\rangle)$ denote the submodule of $\Omega_X^k\langle Y\rangle$ formed by linear combinations of products
\[
\alpha\wedge\frac{dt_{i(1)}}{t_{i(1)}}\wedge\cdots\wedge
\frac{dt_{i(m)}}{t_{i(m)}}\qquad(m\le n),
\]
with $\alpha$ holomorphic and with the $t_{i(j)}$ local equations of distinct local components $Y_j$ of $Y$. The weight filtration of $\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle$ is the increasing filtration by the subcomplexes $W_m(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle)$. One has
\[
\tag{3.1.5.1}
W_m(\Omega_X^k\langle Y\rangle)\wedge W_n(\Omega_X^\ell\langle Y\rangle)
\subset W_{m+n}(\Omega_X^{k+\ell}\langle Y\rangle).
\]

Letting $i_n$ denote the map from $\widetilde Y^n$ to $X$, one checks that the correspondence
\[
\alpha\wedge\frac{dt_{i(1)}}{t_{i(1)}}\wedge\cdots\wedge
\frac{dt_{i(n)}}{t_{i(n)}}
\longmapsto
(\alpha\mid Y_{i(1)}\cap\cdots\cap Y_{i(n)})\otimes
(\text{orientation }i(1),\ldots,i(n))
\]
defines isomorphisms of complexes
\[
\tag{3.1.5.2}
\Res:\Gr_n^W(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle)\iso
i_{n*}\Omega_{\widetilde Y^n}^\bullet(\varepsilon^n)[-n],
\]
the ``Poincaré residue''.

\paragraph{(3.1.6)} The following interpretation, in terms of (3.1.5.2), of the Leray spectral sequence for the inclusion of $X^*$ in $X$ was pointed out to me by N. Katz. It will make it possible to verify a point which I had initially considered evident ((3.2.5), (ii), first part).

\paragraph{(3.1.7)} Every point of $X$ admits a fundamental system of Stein open neighborhoods whose trace on $X^*$ is Stein. For $\mathcal F$ a coherent analytic sheaf on $X^*$, one therefore has $R^ij_*\mathcal F=0$ for $i>0$. The De Rham complex $\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet$ is therefore a resolution of the constant sheaf $\C$ by sheaves acyclic for the functor $j_*$. Hence
\[
\tag{3.1.7.1}
H^*(X^*,\C)\simeq\mathbb H^*(X^*,\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet)
\simeq\mathbb H^*(X,j_*\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet),
\]
and the Leray spectral sequence for the morphism $j$ is identified with the hypercohomology spectral sequence for $j_*\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet$, corresponding to the filtration $\tau$ by the subcomplexes $\tau_{\le n}(j_*\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet)$ (1.4.6).

\paragraph{Proposition (3.1.8).} The morphisms of filtered complexes
\[
(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle,W)
\xleftarrow{\ \alpha\ }
(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle,\tau)
\xrightarrow{\ \beta\ }
(j_*\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet,\tau)
\]
are filtered quasi-isomorphisms. They define an isomorphism between the Leray spectral sequence for $j$ in complex cohomology and the hypercohomology spectral sequence of the filtered complex $(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle,W)$ on $X$.

By (1.4.5) and (3.1.7), it suffices to prove the first assertion. In [3], II, (6.9), or in [1], one finds the proof that $\beta$ is a quasi-isomorphism, hence a filtered quasi-isomorphism. One can also compute directly the cohomology sheaves of the two sides: those of $\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle$ are determined by (3.1.5.2), whereas those of $j_*\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet$ are the $R^ij_*\C$, which can be computed topologically.

For $n\ge p$ one has
\[
W_n(\Omega_X^p\langle Y\rangle)=\Omega_X^p\langle Y\rangle,
\]
so that $\alpha$ is a morphism from $\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle$, endowed with $\tau$, to $\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle$, endowed with the decreasing filtration associated with $W$ (1.1.3). By (3.1.5.2), one has
\[
\tag{3.1.8.1}
\mathcal H^i(\Gr_n^W(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle))=
\begin{cases}
0,& i\ne n,\\
\varepsilon_X^n,& i=n.
\end{cases}
\]
From the first line of this formula one deduces that $\alpha$ is a filtered quasi-isomorphism. This proves (3.1.8).

By (3.1.7), the isomorphism (3.1.8.1) defines an isomorphism
\[
\tag{3.1.8.2}
R^nj_*\C\simeq \mathcal H^n(j_*\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet)
\simeq \mathcal H^n(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle)
\simeq \varepsilon_X^n.
\]
The isomorphisms (3.1.4.1) correspond, via (3.1.8.2), to the cup product.

\paragraph{Proposition (3.1.9).} The canonical morphism from $R^nj_*\Z$ to $R^nj_*\C$ identifies, via (3.1.8.2), the sheaf $R^nj_*\Z$ with $(\varepsilon_X^n)_\Z$ (3.1.4).

The question is local on $X$. One can therefore suppose that $X$ is an open polydisc $D^m$, with
\[
D=\{z\in\C\mid |z|<1\},
\]
and that
\[
Y=\bigcup_{k=1}^rY_k,\qquad Y_k=\operatorname{pr}_k^{-1}(0).
\]
The fiber at $0$ of $R^nj_*\Z$ is then the integral cohomology of
\[
X^*=D^{*r}\times D^{m-r},
\qquad
D^*=\{z\in\C\mid 0<|z|<1\}.
\]
The space $X^*$ has the homotopy type of a torus; its cohomology is therefore torsion-free, and the cup product defines isomorphisms
\[
\bigwedge\nolimits^n(R^1j_*\Z)_0\xrightarrow{\sim}(R^nj_*\Z)_0.
\]
It is therefore enough to prove (3.1.9) for $n=1$.

The integral homology $H_1(X^*)$ is generated by the loops $\gamma_k$ winding around the various $Y_k$. One has
\[
\int_{\gamma_k}\frac{dz_k}{z_k}=\pm2\pi i;
\]
the integral cohomology is therefore generated by the $\frac1{2\pi i}\frac{dz_k}{z_k}$, and this proves (3.1.9).

\paragraph{(3.1.10)} Let $\mathcal F$ be a coherent analytic sheaf on $X^*$, given as the restriction to $X^*$ of a coherent analytic sheaf, again denoted $\mathcal F$, on $X$. The meromorphic direct image $j_*^m\mathcal F$ of $\mathcal F$ is the inductive limit
\[
j_*^m\mathcal F=\varinjlim_n \mathcal F(nY).
\]
Locally on $X$, $Y$ is the sum of a finite family $(Y_i)_{i\in I}$ of smooth divisors, and the filtration by order of pole $P$ on $j_*^m\cO_{X^*}$ is defined by the formula
\[
\tag{3.1.10.1}
P^p(j_*^m\cO_{X^*})=
\sum_{\mathbf n\in A_p}\cO_X\Bigl(\sum_i(n_i+1)Y_i\Bigr),
\]
where
\[
A_p=\{(n_i)_{i\in I}\mid \sum_i n_i\le -p\ \text{ and }\ \forall i,\ n_i\ge0\}.
\]
This construction globalizes and provides on $j_*^m\cO_{X^*}$ an exhaustive filtration such that $P^p=0$ for $p>0$.

The filtration by order of pole of the complex $j_*^m\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet=j_*^m\cO_{X^*}\otimes\Omega_X^\bullet$ is the filtration
\[
\tag{3.1.10.2}
P^p(j_*^m\Omega_{X^*}^k)=P^{p-k}(j_*^m\cO_{X^*})\otimes\Omega_X^k.
\]
The filtration $P$ induces on the subcomplex $\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle$ of $j_*^m\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet$ the bête filtration $\sigma_{\ge p}(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle)$, a filtration also called the Hodge filtration $F$.

\paragraph{Proposition (3.1.11).} The inclusion morphism
\[
(\Omega_X^\bullet\langle Y\rangle,F)\longrightarrow(j_*^m\Omega_{X^*}^\bullet,P)
\]
is a filtered quasi-isomorphism.

This statement was suggested to me by [4]. A proof appears in [3], II, (3.13).

\subsection*{3.2. Mixed Hodge theory}

Recall that from now on, by a scheme we mean a scheme of finite type over $\C$, and by a sheaf on $S$ a sheaf on $S^{an}$.

\paragraph{(3.2.1)} Let $X$ be a smooth separated scheme. By Nagata [11], $X$ is a Zariski open subset of a complete scheme $\overline X$. By Hironaka [8], one can take $\overline X$ smooth, and such that $Y=\overline X-X$ is a normal-crossings divisor.

The reader who would like to avoid the reference to Nagata may suppose $X$ quasi-



projective. The smooth completion $\overline X$ can then be chosen projective and such that $Y$ is a union of smooth divisors. When one restricts oneself to such compactifications, Hodge theory is needed only in its standard form (2.2.1).

\paragraph{(3.2.2)} By (3.1.7) and (3.1.8), one has
\[
H^*(X,\C)\simeq H^*(\overline X,\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle).
\]
The Hodge filtration $F$ on the complex $\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle$ is defined to be the filtration $F^p=\sigma_{\ge p}$ by bête truncations (1.4.5). Thus $\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle$ carries two filtrations: $F$ and $W$ (3.1.5).

\paragraph{(3.2.3)} We shall use the existence of bifiltered resolutions
\[
i:\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle\longrightarrow K^\bullet
\]
such that the $\Gr_F^p\Gr_n^W(K^j)$ are sheaves acyclic for the functor $\Gamma$:
\[
H^i(\overline X,\Gr_F^p\Gr_n^W(K^j))=0\qquad \text{for }i>0.
\]
Here are two ways to construct them:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item One may take for $K^\bullet$ the canonical Godement resolution
\[
\mathcal C^\bullet(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle),
\]
filtered by the $\mathcal C^\bullet(W_n(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle))$ and the
$\mathcal C^\bullet(F^p(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle))$. This is a bifiltered resolution because $\mathcal C^\bullet$ is an exact functor.
\item One may take for $K^\bullet$ the $d''$-resolution of $\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle$. Let $\mathcal E_X^{p,q}$ be the sheaf of $C^\infty$ forms of type $(p,q)$; then $K^\bullet$ is the simple complex associated with the double complex of the
\[
\Omega_{\overline X}^{p}\langle Y\rangle\otimes_{\Omega_X^0}\mathcal E_X^{0,q},
\]
a subcomplex of the $j_*\mathcal E_{X^*}^{p,q}$. This complex is filtered by
\[
F^p(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle)\otimes\mathcal E_X^{0,*}
\quad\text{and by}\quad
W_n(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle)\otimes\mathcal E_X^{0,*}.
\]
To prove that this is a bifiltered resolution, one uses the fact that the sheaf $\mathcal E_X^0$ of complex-valued $C^\infty$ functions on $X$ is flat over $\mathcal O$ (a corollary of Malgrange's $C^\infty$ preparation theorem). The sheaves $\Gr_F\Gr_W(K^\bullet)$ are fine, because they are sheaves of modules over the soft sheaf $\mathcal E_X^0$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(3.2.4)} With the notation of (3.2.3), the complex cohomology of $X$ appears as the cohomology of the bifiltered complex $\Gamma(\overline X,K^\bullet)$. Thus there are two spectral sequences abutting to $H^*(X,\C)$. With the notation of (3.1.4), they are
\[
\tag{3.2.4.1}
{}_W E_1^{pq}
 =H^{p+q}(\overline X,\varepsilon_{\overline X}^{-p}[p])
 =H^{2p+q}(\widetilde Y^{-p},\varepsilon^{-p})
 \Longrightarrow H^*(X,\C),
\]
\[
\tag{3.2.4.2}
{}_F E_1^{pq}
 =H^q(\overline X,\Omega_{\overline X}^p\langle Y\rangle)
 \Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\C).
\]
The first of these, up to the renumbering
\[
{}_W E_1^{pq}\longmapsto E_2^{\,2p+q,-p},
\]
is just the Leray spectral sequence of the inclusion $j$.

\paragraph{Theorem (3.2.5).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item On the terms ${}_WE_r^{pq}$ of the spectral sequence (3.2.4.1), the first direct filtration, the second direct filtration, and the recurrent filtration defined by $F$ coincide.
\item The filtration on $H^n(X,\C)$ which is the abutment of the spectral sequence ${}_WE$ is deduced from a filtration $W$ of $H^n(X,\Q)$. Neither it, nor the filtration $F$ which is the abutment of the spectral sequence ${}_FE$, depends on the chosen compactification $\overline X$ of $X$ or on the choice of $K^\bullet$.
\item The filtrations $W[n]$ (1.1.2) and $F$ define on $H^n(X,\Z)$ a mixed Hodge structure, functorial in $X$.
\end{enumerate}

By (3.1.7), the spectral sequence ${}_WE$ is the Leray spectral sequence for $j$, up to renumbering. It is therefore obtained by tensoring with $\C$ a spectral sequence of $\Q$-vector spaces, and the first assertion of (ii) is true. Complex conjugation acts on the ${}_WE_r$; it can be computed via (3.1.10).

\paragraph{Lemma (3.2.6).} The hypercohomology spectral sequences of the filtered complexes
\[
\Gr_n^W(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle),
\]
endowed with the filtration induced by the Hodge filtration, degenerate at $E_1$.

Define $Y^n$ and $\widetilde Y^n$ as in (3.1.4), and let $i_n:\widetilde Y^n\to X$. By (3.1.5.2), one has
\[
\Gr_n^W(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle)
\simeq i_{n*}\Omega_{\widetilde Y^n}^{\bullet}(\varepsilon^n)[-n].
\]
Moreover, the Hodge filtration induces the bête filtration (1.4.5), so that the spectral sequence (3.2.6) is obtained, by translations in the degrees, from the classical spectral sequence
\[
E_1^{pq}=H^q(\widetilde Y^n,\Omega_{\widetilde Y^n}^p(\varepsilon^n))
\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(\widetilde Y^n,\varepsilon^n).
\]
If $\overline X$ is projective and $Y$ is a union of smooth divisors, then $\widetilde Y^n$ is projective, $\varepsilon^n$ is a trivial local system, and classical Hodge theory (2.2.1) gives the degeneration (3.2.6). For the general case, one must refer to [2] (see (2.2.2), (2.2.3)).

Hodge theory further gives that the Hodge filtration on $H^k(\widetilde Y^n,\varepsilon^n)$ is $k$-opposed to its complex conjugate, where complex conjugation is defined in terms of $\varepsilon_\Z^n$ (3.1.4). Taking account here of the translations in the degrees, one has:

\paragraph{Lemma (3.2.7).} The filtration on
\[
{}_W E_1^{-n,k+n}
=H^k(\overline X,\Gr_n^W(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle))
\simeq H^{k-n}(\widetilde Y^n,\varepsilon^n),
\]
which is the abutment of the spectral sequence (3.2.6), is $(k+n)$-opposed to its complex conjugate.

\paragraph{Lemma (3.2.8).} The differentials $d_1$ of the spectral sequence ${}_WE$ are strictly compatible with the filtration $F$.

On the terms $E_1$, there is only one filtration induced by $F$ to consider ((1.3.10) and (1.3.13), (iii)), and $d_1$ is compatible with this filtration ((1.3.13), (i)). This filtration is the abutment of the spectral sequence (3.2.6) ((1.4.8), (iii)). By (3.2.7), the arrow $d_1$
\[
d_1:H^k(\overline X,\Gr_n^W(\Omega^\bullet\langle Y\rangle))
\longrightarrow
H^{k+1}(\overline X,\Gr_{n-1}^W(\Omega^\bullet\langle Y\rangle))
\]
that is,
\[
\tag{3.2.8.1}
d_1:H^{k-n}(\widetilde Y^n,\varepsilon^n)
\longrightarrow
H^{k-n+2}(\widetilde Y^{n-1},\varepsilon^{n-1})
\]
is compatible with filtrations that are $(k+n)$-opposed to their complex conjugates. Since $d_1$ commutes with complex conjugation, it respects the bigrading, and hence the filtration defined by $F$ and $\overline F$; this proves (3.2.8). Moreover, the cohomology of the complex $E_1$ will still be bigraded.

\paragraph{Lemma (3.2.9).} On ${}_WE_r^{pq}$, the recurrent filtration $F$ is $q$-opposed to its complex conjugate.

We prove by induction on $r$ that:

\paragraph{Lemma (3.2.10).} For $r>0$, the differentials $d_r$ of the spectral sequence ${}_WE$ are strictly compatible with the recurrent filtration $F$. For $r\ge2$, they are zero.

For $r=0$, respectively $r=1$, one applies (3.2.6) and (1.4.8), (iii), respectively (3.2.8). For $r\ge2$, it suffices to prove that $d_r=0$. By induction and by (1.3.16), one may suppose that on the terms ${}_WE_s$ $(s\ge r+1)$ one has $F_d=F_r=F_\infty$, and that ${}_WE_r={}_WE_2$. By (1.3.13), (i), $d_r$ is therefore compatible with the filtration $F_r$.

On ${}_WE_r^{pq}={}_WE_2^{pq}$, the filtration $F_r$ is $q$-opposed to its complex conjugate. The morphism
\[
d_r:{}_W E_r^{pq}\longrightarrow {}_W E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}
\]
therefore satisfies, for $r-1>0$,
\[
d_r({}_W E_r^{pq})
=d_r\!\left(\sum_{a+b=q}\bigl(F^a({}_W E_r^{pq})\cap
\overline F^{\,b}({}_W E_r^{pq})\bigr)\right)
\]
\[
\subset
\sum_{a+b=q}\bigl(F^a({}_WE_r^{p+r,q-r+1})\cap
\overline F^{\,b}({}_WE_r^{p+r,q-r+1})\bigr)=0.
\]
This proves (3.2.10), which by (1.3.16) implies (3.2.5) (i).

By (1.3.17), the filtration of ${}_WE_\infty^{pq}$ induced by the filtration $F$ of $H^{p+q}(X,\C)$ is $q$-opposed to its complex conjugate. Since $q=-p+(p+q)$, this proves the first part of (3.2.5) (iii).

\paragraph{(3.2.11)} We prove (ii) and (iii), which will complete the proof.

\paragraph{A. Independence of the choice of $K^\bullet$.} The filtrations $F$ and $W$ of $H^*(X,\C)$ are the abutments of the hypercohomology spectral sequences of $\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle$ for the filtrations $F$ and $W$. These entire spectral sequences do not depend on the choice of $K^\bullet$.

\paragraph{B. Functoriality.} Let $f:X_1\to X_2$ be a morphism of schemes. Suppose given a morphism of smooth compactifications
\[
\tag{3.2.11.1}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em,row sep=2.1em]
X_1 \arrow[r,"f"] \arrow[d,"j_1"'] & X_2 \arrow[d,"j_2"]\\
\overline X_1 \arrow[r,"\overline f"'] & \overline X_2 ,
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $Y_i=\overline X_i-X_i$ are normal-crossings divisors. The canonical morphism (see (3.1.3)) from $f^*\Omega_{\overline X_2}^{\bullet}\langle Y_2\rangle$ to $\Omega_{\overline X_1}^{\bullet}\langle Y_1\rangle$ is then a morphism of bifiltered complexes; on hypercohomology it induces a morphism compatible with $F$ and $W$, and therefore
\[
f^*:H^n(X_2,\Z)\longrightarrow H^n(X_1,\Z)
\]
is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures, for the structures defined by the compactifications $\overline X_i$.

\paragraph{C. Independence of the compactification.} With the notation of B, if $f$ is an isomorphism, then $f^*$ is a bijective morphism of mixed Hodge structures, hence an isomorphism (2.3.5).

If $\overline X_1$ and $\overline X_2$ are two smooth compactifications of $X$, with $Y_i=\overline X_i-X_i$ a normal-crossings divisor, there exists a third smooth compactification $\overline X$, with $Y=\overline X-X$ a normal-crossings divisor, fitting into a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1.2em,column sep=2.4em]
& X \arrow[dl] \arrow[d] \arrow[dr] & \\
\overline X_1 & \overline X \arrow[l] \arrow[r] & \overline X_2 .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Namely, one takes for $\overline X$ a resolution of the singularities of the closure of the diagonal image of $X$ in $\overline X_1\times\overline X_2$. The identity map from $H^n(X,\Z)$, endowed with the mixed Hodge structure defined by $\overline X_1$, to $H^n(X,\Z)$, endowed with that defined by $\overline X_2$, is therefore a composition of two isomorphisms.

To finish the proof of (ii) and (iii), one observes that every morphism $f$ fits into a diagram (3.2.11.1): choose compactifications $\overline X'_1$ and $\overline X_2$ of $X_1$ and $X_2$, and then take for $\overline X_1$ a resolution of the singularities of the closure of the image of $X_1$ in $\overline X'_1\times\overline X_2$.

\paragraph{Definition (3.2.12).} The mixed Hodge structure on the cohomology of a smooth separated algebraic variety is the mixed Hodge structure (3.2.5), (iii).

\paragraph{Corollary (3.2.13).} With the preceding notation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The spectral sequence (3.2.4.1) degenerates at $E_2$, i.e. the Leray spectral sequence for the inclusion $j:X\hookrightarrow\overline X$ degenerates at $E_3$ $(E_3=E_\infty)$.
\item The spectral sequence (3.2.4.2)
\[
{}_F E_1^{pq}=H^q(\overline X,\Omega_{\overline X}^p\langle Y\rangle)
\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\C)
\]
degenerates at $E_1$.
\item The spectral sequence defined by the sheaf $\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle$, endowed with the filtration $W$:
\[
E_1^{-n,k+n}=H^k(\overline X,\Gr_n^W(\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle))
\simeq H^{k-n}(\widetilde Y^n,\Omega_{\widetilde Y^n}^{\bullet}(\varepsilon^n))
\Longrightarrow H^k(\overline X,\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle)
\]
degenerates at $E_2$.
\end{enumerate}

Assertion (i) was proved in (3.2.10).

Consider the four spectral sequences of (1.4.12), relative to the bifiltered complex $\Omega_{\overline X}^{\bullet}\langle Y\rangle$. By (i), one has
\[
\sum_n\dim H^n(X,\C)=\sum_{p,q}\dim {}_WE_2^{p,q}.
\]
The terms ${}_WE_1^{-n,k+n}$ are, for fixed $n$, the abutment of a spectral sequence degenerate at $E_1$ (3.2.6), with initial terms $E_1^{p,n-p}$ (notation of (1.4.12)), the initial terms of spectral sequence (iii). Since ${}_Wd_1$ is strictly compatible with the filtration $F$ that is the abutment of this spectral sequence (3.2.7), and is (1.4.12) the abutment of a morphism between these spectral sequences, beginning with the differentials of (iii), one has
\[
\Gr_F^p({}_WE_2^{n,k-n})\simeq
H^*(E_1^{p,n-1,k-n-p}\to E_1^{p,n,k-n-p}\to E_1^{p,n+1,k-n-p})
\]
and $\Gr_F^*({}_WE_2^{**})$ is the sum of the terms $E_\infty^{**}$ of the spectral sequences (iii). Therefore
\[
\tag{3.2.13.1}
\sum_n \dim H^n(X,\C)=\sum\dim E_\infty^{**}.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\sum_n \dim H^n(X,\C)\le \sum \dim {}_F E_1^{**},
\]
with equality if and only if the spectral sequence (ii) degenerates at $E_1$, and
\[
\sum\dim {}_F E_1^{**}\le \sum\dim E_\infty^{**},
\]
with equality if and only if the spectral sequences (iii) degenerate at $E_2$. Comparing with (3.2.13.1), one obtains (3.2.13).

\paragraph{Corollary (3.2.14).} Let $\omega$ be a meromorphic differential $p$-form on $\overline X$, holomorphic on $X$ and having at worst logarithmic poles along $Y$. Then the restriction $\omega|X$ of $\omega$ to $X$ is closed, and if the cohomology class in $H^p(X,\C)$ defined by $\omega$ is zero, then $\omega=0$.

This is the special case ${}_FE_1^{p0}={}_FE_\infty^{p0}$ of (3.2.13), (ii).

\paragraph{Corollary (3.2.15).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $X$ is a complete smooth algebraic variety, the mixed Hodge structure on $H^n(X,\Z)$ is the classical Hodge structure of weight $n$.
\item The Hodge numbers $h^{pq}$ of the mixed Hodge structure of $H^n(X,\Z)$, for $X$ smooth algebraic, can be nonzero only for $p\le n$, $q\le n$, and $p+q\ge n$.
\end{enumerate}
Assertion (i) is clear. To prove (ii), one observes that, for $Y$ a union of smooth divisors, the rational Hodge structure $\Gr_{n+k}^W(H^n(X,\Q))$ is a quotient of a subobject of a Hodge structure of weight $n+k$, namely
\[
H^{n-k}(\widetilde Y^k,\Q)\otimes\Q(-k).
\]

\paragraph{(3.2.16)} Let $X$ be a smooth separated scheme. It is known that $X$ admits smooth compactifications $\overline X$, and that the subgroup of $H^n(X,\Z)$ which is the image of $H^n(\overline X,\Z)$ is independent of the choice of $\overline X$ (cf. [6], (9.1) to (9.4)).

\paragraph{Corollary (3.2.17).} Under the hypotheses (3.2.16), the image of $H^n(\overline X,\Q)$ in $H^n(X,\Q)$ is $W_n(H^n(X,\Q))$, where $W$ denotes the weight filtration (3.2.12).

One may suppose that $\overline X-X$ is a normal-crossings divisor. The assertion then follows from the fact that $W[-n]$ is the abutment of the Leray spectral sequence for the inclusion $j:X\hookrightarrow\overline X$.

\paragraph{Corollary (3.2.18).} Let $f$ be a morphism from a proper smooth scheme $Y$ to a smooth scheme $X$ admitting a smooth compactification $\overline X$:
\[
Y\xrightarrow{f}X\xrightarrow{j}\overline X.
\]
Then the groups $H^n(X,\Q)$ and $H^n(\overline X,\Q)$ have the same image in $H^n(Y,\Q)$.

Since $f^*$ and $(jf)^*$ are strictly compatible with the weight filtration ((3.2.5), (iii)), it is enough to prove that $\Gr_m^W(f^*)$ and $\Gr_m^W((jf)^*)$ have the same image in $\Gr_m^W H^n(Y,\Q)$. By (3.2.17) and (3.2.15), $\Gr_n^W(j^*)$ is an isomorphism, while $\Gr_m^W(f^*)=0$ for $m\ne n$ because $\Gr_m^W H^n(Y,\Q)=0$ for $m\ne n$.

\paragraph{Remark (3.2.19).} By (3.1.11) and (1.4.5), under the hypotheses of (3.2.5), the Hodge filtration on $H^n(X,\C)$ is the abutment of the hypercohomology spectral sequence of the complex $j_*^m\Omega_{X^*}^{\bullet}$, endowed with the filtration by order of pole (3.1.10): this spectral sequence coincides with (3.2.4.2).

\section*{4. Applications and complements}

\subsection*{4.1. The fixed-part theorem}

\paragraph{Theorem (4.1.1).} Let $S$ be a smooth separated scheme, and let $f:X\to S$ be a proper smooth morphism.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item In rational cohomology, the Leray spectral sequence
\[
E_2^{pq}=H^p(S,R^qf_*\Q)\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\Q)
\]
degenerates $(E_2=E_\infty)$.
\item If $\overline X$ is a nonsingular compactification of $X$, the canonical morphism
\[
H^n(\overline X,\Q)\longrightarrow H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)
\]
is surjective.
\end{enumerate}

For $f$ projective and smooth, assertion (i) is proved in [2]. The proof of [2] is rewritten more readably in [5]; it does not use the smoothness of $S$.

Fix $n$, and prove that (i) implies (ii). We reduce to the case where $S$ is connected and nonempty. If $s\in S$ is a point of $S$, the local system $R^nf_*\Q$ is entirely described by its fiber $(R^nf_*\Q)_s$ at $s$, and by the action of the fundamental group $\pi=\pi_1(S,s)$ on this fiber. One has
\[
H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)\simeq[(R^nf_*\Q)_s]^\pi.
\]
If $X_s=f^{-1}(s)$, the composite arrow
\[
H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)\longrightarrow (R^nf_*\Q)_s\simeq H^n(X_s,\Q)
\]
is therefore injective. Consider the arrows
\[
H^n(\overline X,\Q)\xrightarrow{a}H^n(X,\Q)\xrightarrow{b}
H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)\xrightarrow{c}H^n(X_s,\Q).
\]
The arrows $cb$ and $cba$ have the same image by (3.2.18). The arrow $b$ is an edge homomorphism of the Leray spectral sequence, hence is surjective by hypothesis. Since $c$ is injective, $b$ and $ba$ have the same image and $ba$ is surjective. This proves (ii) for $f$ projective. We now deduce the general case. Again reduce to the case where $S$ is connected and nonempty.

By Chow's lemma and resolution of singularities, there exists a quasi-projective smooth scheme $X'$ and a projective birational morphism
\[
p:X'\longrightarrow X.
\]
There then exists, by Bertini or Sard, a nonempty Zariski open subset $S_1$ of $S$ such that
\[
X'_1=(fp)^{-1}(S_1)
\]
is smooth over $S_1$. Finally let $X_1=f^{-1}(S_1)$, let $\overline X$ be a smooth compactification of $X$, and let $\overline X'_1$ be a smooth compactification of $X'_1$ which dominates $\overline X$:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=1.35em,column sep=2.8em]
\overline X && \overline X'_1 \arrow[ll,"\overline p"']\\
X \arrow[u] & X_1 \arrow[l] & X'_1 \arrow[l,"p"'] \arrow[u]\\
S \arrow[u,"f"] & S_1 \arrow[l,"i"'] \arrow[u,"f_1"] & S_1 \arrow[l,equal] \arrow[u,"f'_1"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
If $s\in S_1$, the morphism $i_*:\pi_1(S_1,s)\to\pi_1(S,s)$ is surjective because the topological codimension of $S-S_1$ is $\ge2$. Therefore
\[
H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)\simeq H^0(S_1,R^nf_{1*}\Q),
\]
and it suffices to prove that
\[
H^n(\overline X,\Q)\longrightarrow H^0(S_1,R^nf_{1*}\Q)
\]
is surjective.

The vertical arrows of the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.2em,row sep=2.2em]
H^n(\overline X,\Q) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"p^*"'] &
H^n(X_1,\Q) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"p^*"'] &
H^0(S_1,R^nf_{1*}\Q) \arrow[d,"p^*"]\\
H^n(\overline X'_1,\Q) \arrow[r] &
H^n(X'_1,\Q) \arrow[r] &
H^0(S_1,R^nf'_{1*}\Q)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
admit as left inverses the Gysin morphisms $\overline p_*$, $p_*$, and $p_*$, defined by Poincaré duality as transposes of the arrows analogous to the preceding ones in cohomology with proper support. Moreover, the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=4em,row sep=2.2em]
H^n(\overline X,\Q) \arrow[r,"u"] \arrow[d,"p^*"'] &
H^0(S_1,R^nf_{1*}\Q) \arrow[d,"p^*"]\\
H^n(\overline X'_1,\Q) \arrow[r,"v"'] &
H^0(S_1,R^nf'_{1*}\Q)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is commutative. Thus $u$ is a direct factor of $v$. The latter is surjective, since $f'_1$ is projective, and hence $u$ is also surjective.

We now prove (i) in the general case. We reduce to the case where $f$ is purely of relative dimension $n$. Let $f\times f$ be the projection of $X\times_SX$ onto $S$.

Let $\delta$ be the image of the cohomology class of the diagonal of $X\times_SX$ in
\[
H^0(S,R^n(f\times f)_*\Q).
\]
By Künneth,
\[
R^n(f\times f)_*\Q\simeq
\sum_{p+q=n}(R^pf_*\Q\otimes R^qf_*\Q).
\]
Let $\delta_{pq}$, for $p+q=n$, denote the components of $\delta$ in this decomposition, and let $\widetilde\delta_{pq}$ be classes in $H^n(X\times_SX)$ whose images are the $\delta_{pq}$.

The $\widetilde\delta_{pq}$ define in the derived category $D^+(S)$ homomorphisms
\[
\delta_p:Rf_*\Q\longrightarrow Rf_*\Q
\]
such that $\mathcal H^q(\delta_p)$ is zero for $p\ne q$, and is the identity for $p=q$. By [2], one therefore has in $D^+(S)$
\[
\tag{4.1.1.1}
Rf_*\Q\simeq\sum_p R^pf_*\Q[-p],
\]
and the Leray spectral sequence degenerates.

\paragraph{Corollary (4.1.2).} Let $f:X\to S$ be a proper smooth morphism whose target is a reduced connected separated scheme $S$. Let $(R^nf_*\Q)^0$ be the largest constant local subsystem of $R^nf_*\Q$, with fiber $H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)$. Then, for each $s\in S$, $(R^nf_*\Q)^0_s$ is a Hodge substructure of
\[
(R^nf_*\Q)_s\simeq H^n(X_s,\Q),
\]
and the Hodge structure induced on $H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)$ is independent of $s$.

Let $s\in S$ and $X_s=f^{-1}(s)$. If $S$ is smooth, and if $\overline X$ is a smooth compactification of $X$, then by (4.1.1) the subspace $(R^nf_*\Q)^0_s$ of $(R^nf_*\Q)_s$ is the image of $H^n(\overline X,\Q)$. Since the restriction map
\[
H^n(\overline X,\Q)\longrightarrow H^n(X_s,\Q)
\]
is a morphism of Hodge structures, its image is a Hodge substructure, and the Hodge structure induced on $H^0(S,R^nf_*\Q)$, a quotient of that of $H^n(\overline X,\Q)$, is independent of $s$.

In the general case, (4.1.2) also means that if $a$ is a global section of $R^nf_*\C$, then its components $a^{pq}$ of type $(p,q)$, \emph{a priori} only continuous sections of the complex vector bundle defined by $R^nf_*\C$, are in fact locally constant, i.e. are sections of $R^nf_*\C$. This is so because $a^{pq}$ is continuous and locally constant on the dense open smoothness locus of $S$, by what precedes.

\paragraph{(4.1.3)} In the case where $S$ is compact, a generalization of (4.1.2) is proved analytically in Griffiths [5].

As corollaries of (4.1.2), let us cite:

\paragraph{(4.1.3.1)} (Griffiths [5]) Let $f:X\to S$ be a proper smooth morphism as in (4.1.2). If a global section $a$ of $R^nf_*\C$ is of Hodge type $(p,q)$ at one point, then $a$ is of type $(p,q)$ everywhere. In particular, if $n=2$ and if, at one point $s$, $a$ is the cohomology class of a divisor of $X_s$, then $a$ is at every point the cohomology class of a divisor and, for $S$ smooth, is even defined by a divisor $D$ on $X$.

\paragraph{(4.1.3.2)} (Grothendieck [7]) Let $S$ be a reduced connected scheme of finite type over $\C$, and let $f_1:X_1\to S$ and $f_2:X_2\to S$ be two abelian schemes over $S$. If a morphism
\[
u:R^1f_{2*}\Z\longrightarrow R^1f_{1*}\Z
\]
comes at one point $s$ of $S$ from a morphism of abelian varieties
\[
u_s:(X_1)_s\longrightarrow (X_2)_s,
\]
then $u$ comes from one, and only one, morphism of abelian schemes
\[
v:X_1\longrightarrow X_2.
\]

\paragraph{(4.1.3.3)} (Cf. Katz [10]) Let $S$ be a smooth connected scheme, let $s$ be a point of $S$, let $f:X\to S$ be a proper smooth morphism, and let $P$ be a direct factor of the local system $R^if_*\Q$ which is pointwise a Hodge substructure. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item The Hodge structure of $P$ is locally constant.
\item The representation $P_s$ of $\pi_1(S,s)$ factors through a finite quotient of $\pi_1(S,s)$.
\item There exists a nonempty finite étale covering $u:S'\to S$ such that $u^*P$ is a constant family of Hodge structures.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{4.2. The semisimplicity theorem}

\paragraph{(4.2.1)} Let $S$ be a topological space. A continuous family of Hodge structures on $S$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item a local system $H_\Z$ of finite-type $\Z$-modules on $S$;
\item for every point $s\in S$, a Hodge structure on the fiber $(H_\Z)_s$, this structure varying continuously with $s$.
\end{enumerate}
A continuous family $H$ of Hodge structures on $S$ is said to have weight $n$ if the fibers $H_s$ $(s\in S)$ have weight $n$.

Similarly, a continuous family of $\Q$-Hodge structures is defined as a local system of $\Q$-vector spaces endowed at each point with a continuously varying $\Q$-Hodge structure.

A polarization of a continuous family $H$ of $\Q$-Hodge structures of weight $n$ is a morphism of local systems from $H_\Q\otimes H_\Q$ into the constant local system $\Q(-n)_\Q$, which at every point $s\in S$ defines a polarization of $H_s$.

\paragraph{(4.2.2)} Suppose $S$ is connected, and let $\mathcal C$ be a strictly full subcategory of the category of continuous families of $\Q$-Hodge structures on $S$. We shall consider the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(4.2.2.\arabic*)]
\item $\mathcal C$ is stable under direct factors, direct sums, and tensor products; the constant Tate families $\Q(n)$ $(n\in\Z)$ belong to $\mathcal C$.
\item Every homogeneous Hodge structure, i.e. one of some weight $n$, in $\mathcal C$ is polarizable.
\item For every $H\in\Ob\mathcal C$, there exists a local system $H'_\Z$ of free $\Z$-modules on $S$ such that $H'_\Z\otimes\Q\simeq H_\Q$.
\item For every $H$ in $\mathcal C$, the largest constant local subsystem $H'$ of $H$ is a constant family of Hodge substructures of $H$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Lemma (4.2.3).} If $\mathcal C$ satisfies the conditions (4.2.2.1) and (4.2.2.2), then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\mathcal C$ is a semisimple abelian subcategory of the abelian category of continuous families of $\Q$-Hodge structures on $S$.
\item If $H\in\Ob\mathcal C$, then its dual $H^*$ and the $\bigwedge^pH$ belong to $\mathcal C$; if $H_1,H_2\in\Ob\mathcal C$, then $\Hom(H_1,H_2)\in\Ob\mathcal C$.
\end{enumerate}

If $H\in\Ob\mathcal C$ and $H_1$ is a subobject of $H$, in the category of continuous families of $\Q$-Hodge structures, we prove that $H_1$ is a direct factor of $H$ in that category. We may suppose $H$ homogeneous. If $\psi$ is a polarization form for $H$, the orthogonal complement of $H_1$ for $\psi$ is indeed a subobject of $H$ complementary to $H_1$. This proves (i).

If $H\in\Ob\mathcal C$ has weight $n$, a polarization of $H$ defines an isomorphism between $H^*$ and $H\otimes\Q(n)$. By (4.2.2.1), one therefore has $H^*\in\Ob\mathcal C$. For arbitrary $H$ in $\mathcal C$, decomposing $H$ into its homogeneous components, one has
\[
H^*=\bigoplus_n (H^n)^*,
\]
whence again $H^*\in\Ob\mathcal C$. Finally, $\bigwedge^pH$ is a direct factor in $\otimes^pH$, and $\Hom(H_1,H_2)\simeq H_1^*\otimes H_2$.

Let $f:X\to S$ be a proper smooth morphism of reduced schemes. The sheaf $R^nf_*\Q$ is then a local system, and for $s\in S$,
\[
(R^nf_*\Q)_s\simeq H^n(X_s,\Q)
\]
is endowed with a $\Q$-Hodge structure. This varies continuously with $s$.

\paragraph{Definition (4.2.4).} Let $S$ be a smooth connected scheme. A continuous family $H$ of $\Q$-Hodge structures on $S^{an}$ will be called algebraic if there exists a nonempty Zariski open subset $U$ of $S$, an integer $k$, and a projective smooth morphism $f:X\to U$ such that $H|U$ is a direct factor of $R^kf_*\Q(k)$.

\paragraph{Proposition (4.2.5).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The category of algebraic continuous families of $\Q$-Hodge structures on $S$ satisfies the conditions of (4.2.2).
\item If a continuous family $H$ of Hodge structures on $S$ is such that its restriction to a dense Zariski open subset $U$ of $S$ is algebraic, then $H$ is algebraic.
\item If $f:X\to S$ is proper and smooth, then $R^if_*\Q$ is algebraic.
\end{enumerate}

Assertion (ii) is evident. We prove (i). Let $\mathcal C_0$ be the set of continuous families of Hodge structures on $S$ which are of the form $R^if_*\Q$, for $f$ projective and smooth. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item By the Künneth formula,
\[
R(f\times g)_*\Q\simeq Rf_*\Q\otimes Rg_*\Q,
\]
$\mathcal C_0$ is stable under tensor product.
\item $\mathcal C_0$ is stable under direct sum:
\[
R(f\amalg g)_*\Q\simeq Rf_*\Q\oplus Rg_*\Q.
\]
\end{enumerate}



\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\item The homogeneous components of every $H\in\mathcal C_0$ are polarizable (cf. (2.2.6)).
\item The objects $H\in\mathcal C_0$ satisfy (4.2.2.3), since
\[
Rf_*\Q\simeq Rf_*\Z\otimes\Q.
\]
\item The objects $H\in\mathcal C_0$ satisfy (4.2.2.4), by (4.1.2).
\end{enumerate}
Let $\mathcal C_1$ be the set of direct factors of objects of $\mathcal C_0$. Then $\mathcal C_1$ is stable under tensor product, direct sum, and direct factor, and satisfies (4.2.2.2) to (4.2.2.4). Moreover $\Q(-1)$ belongs to $\mathcal C_1$, since it is of the form $R^2f_*\Q$ for the projective bundle $f:\bbP^1_S\to S$. The category $\mathcal C_2$ of the $H\otimes\Q(k)$ ($k\in\N$) therefore satisfies (4.2.2.1) to (4.2.2.4).

To deduce (i), it suffices to note that if $H$ is a continuous family of Hodge structures, and $U$ is a dense Zariski open subset of $S$, then a subobject, a polarization, or an integral lattice of $H|U$ extends uniquely to $H$.

We prove (iii). If $f:X\to S$ is projective and smooth, then there exist a dense Zariski open subset $U$ of $S$ and a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.3em,row sep=1.8em]
X' \arrow[r,"p"] \arrow[dr,"f'"'] & X|U \arrow[d,"f|U"]\\
& U
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with $f'$ projective and smooth and $p$ birational and surjective (apply Chow's lemma and resolution of singularities to the generic fiber of $f$). The restriction map
\[
p^*:(Rf_*\Q)|U\longrightarrow Rf'_*\Q
\]
is then a direct injection of continuous families of Hodge structures, with left inverse the Gysin morphism $p_*$; hence $Rf_*\Q$ is algebraic.

\paragraph{Theorem (4.2.6).}\footnote{Added in proof. Let $S$ be a smooth connected scheme. A family of Hodge structures on $S$ is a continuous family $H$ of Hodge structures on $S$ satisfying the following conditions: a) the Hodge filtration of $(H_\C)_s$ varies holomorphically with $s$, i.e. corresponds to a filtration $F$ of $H_\cO=H_\Z\otimes\cO$; b) the covariant derivative $\nabla$ satisfies $\nabla F^p\subset\Omega_S^1\otimes F^{p-1}$. Let $\mathcal C$ be the category of those continuous families of $\Q$-Hodge structures on $S$ which underlie a family of Hodge structures and whose homogeneous direct factors are polarizable. It is clear that $\mathcal C$ satisfies (4.2.2.1) to (4.2.2.3). From results of W. Schmid one can deduce that $\mathcal C$ satisfies (4.2.2.4). This theorem, of which (4.1.2) is a corollary, makes it possible to apply (4.2.6) and its corollaries to objects of $\mathcal C$.} Let $S$ be a connected, locally connected and locally simply connected topological space, endowed with a base point $s$. Let $\mathcal C$ be a category of continuous families of Hodge structures on $S$ satisfying the conditions (4.2.2). Then, if $H\in\Ob\mathcal C$, the representation of $\pi_1(S,s)$ on the fiber $(H_\Q)_s$ is semisimple.

Let $H\in\Ob\mathcal C$. The local system $H_\Q$ defines a complex local system $H_\C=H_\Q\otimes\C$. For every complex local system $V$ on $S$, denote by $V^c$ the complex vector bundle which it defines, identified with the sheaf of its continuous sections.

By definition, the group $S$ (2.1.2) acts on $H_\C^c$. A subbundle of $H_\C^c$ will be called horizontal, or locally constant, if it is defined by a local subsystem of $H_\C$.

\paragraph{Lemma (4.2.7).} Let $V$ be a rank-one local subsystem of $H_\C$. Suppose that a tensor power $V^{\otimes n}$ ($n\ge1$) of $V$ is a trivial local system, i.e. that $\pi_1(S,s)$ acts on $V_s$ through a finite, necessarily cyclic, group. Then, for $t\in S$, $tV^c$ is again locally constant.

For $tV^c$ to be locally constant, it suffices that
\[
(tV^c)^{\otimes n}=tV^{c\otimes n}\subset H_\C^c
\]
be locally constant. But $V^{\otimes n}$ is generated by a global horizontal section $v$, and by hypothesis $tv$ is again horizontal (4.2.2.4).

We proceed by induction on $\dim(H_\Q)_s$. We may suppose $H$ homogeneous and nonzero. Let $d$ be the minimal dimension of the nonzero complex local subsystems of $H_\C$. The sum $W$ of all local subsystems of $H_\C$ of dimension $d$, automatically simple, is ``defined over $\Q$'', i.e. is of the form $W_\Q\otimes\C$ for $W_\Q$ a local subsystem of $H_\Q$. By construction, $W_s$ is a semisimple complex $\pi_1(S,s)$-module, hence $(W_\Q)_s$ is a semisimple $\pi_1(S,s)$-module over $\Q$.

Let $H_\Z$ be a local system of free $\Z$-modules such that $H_\Z\otimes\Q\simeq H_\Q$, and let $W_\Z=H_\Z\cap W_\Q$. If $W$ has dimension $e$, the local system $(\bigwedge^eW)^{\otimes2}$ is trivial. Indeed,
\[
\bigwedge^eW\simeq \bigwedge^eW_\Z\otimes\C,
\]
and $\pi_1(S,s)$ can act on $(\bigwedge^eW_\Z)_s$ only by $\pm1$.

Let $V$ be a complex local subsystem of dimension $d$ of $H_\C$, and let $V'$ be a complement of $V$ in $W$, by semisimplicity of $W$. One has
\[
\bigwedge^eW\simeq \bigwedge^dV\otimes\bigwedge^{e-d}V'.
\]
Apply (4.2.7) to the local subsystem $\bigwedge^dV\otimes\bigwedge^{e-d}V'$ of $\bigwedge^dH_\C\otimes\bigwedge^{e-d}H_\C$. We find that, for $t\in S$,
\[
t(\bigwedge^dV^c\otimes\bigwedge^{e-d}V'^c)=
\bigwedge^d tV^c\otimes\bigwedge^{e-d}tV'^c
\subset \bigwedge^dH_\C^c\otimes\bigwedge^{e-d}H_\C^c
\]
is locally constant. Hence $\bigwedge^d tV^c\subset\bigwedge^dH_\C^c$ and $tV^c\subset H_\C^c$ are locally constant.

By definition of $W$, one has $tV^c\subset W^c$: $W^c$ is stable under $S$, and $W_\Q$ is a Hodge substructure of $H$. If $\psi$ is a polarization of $H$, $H$ is therefore the direct sum of $W$ and of its orthogonal complement, both in $\mathcal C$, and the induction concludes.

\paragraph{Corollary (4.2.8).} Under the hypotheses of (4.2.6):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The algebra $A$ of endomorphisms of the local system $H_\Q$ is semisimple. It admits a unique $\Q$-Hodge structure such that, at each point $s$, $A\otimes H_s\to H_s$ is a morphism of Hodge structures.
\item The center of $A$ is of type $(0,0)$ and the composition law $A\otimes A\to A$ is a morphism of Hodge structures.
\item Let $W$ be a complex local subsystem of dimension $d$ of $H_\C$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item For $t\in S$, $tW^c\subset H_\C^c$ is locally constant and defines a local subsystem $tW$ isomorphic to $W$.
\item A power $(\bigwedge^dW)^{\otimes n}$ ($n\ge1$) of the local system $\bigwedge^dW$ is a trivial local system.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

The algebra $A$ is the commutant of $\pi_1(S,s)$ in $(H_\Q)_s$. Its semisimplicity therefore follows from (4.2.6) and Bourbaki, Algebra, chap. 8, §5, no. 2, prop. 4. Moreover, $A$ is the fiber of the largest constant local subsystem of $\Hom(H,H)$. Since $\Hom(H,H)\in\mathcal C$ ((4.2.3), (ii)), the existence of the Hodge structure in (i) follows from hypothesis (4.2.2.4). It is clear that the composition law is a morphism of Hodge structures. It follows that the center $Z$ of $A$ is a Hodge substructure: $Z_\C$ is bigraded, as the intersection of the kernels of the bihomogeneous morphisms $[x,\,]$ for $x$ bihomogeneous. Finally $Z$, and hence $Z_\C$, is semisimple, and for $(p,q)\ne(0,0)$, $Z^{pq}$ is zero because it is nilpotent.

A complex local subsystem $W$ of $H_\C$ is defined by a projector $e$ of $A_\C$. For $t\in S$, $tW^c$ is defined by the projector $t.e$, and is therefore locally constant. Since the group $S$ is connected, to verify that $tW$ is isomorphic to $W$, it suffices to verify that for $t$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $1$, the $\C[\pi_1(S,s)]$-module $tW_s$ is isomorphic to $W_s$. Let $H_i$ be the isotypic components of the $\C[\pi_1(S,s)]$-module $(H_\C)_s$. One has
\[
W_s=\bigoplus_i(W_s\cap H_i)
\quad\text{and}\quad
tW_s=\bigoplus_i(tW_s\cap H_i).
\]
Moreover, for $t$ sufficiently close to $1$, $tW_s$ is close to $W_s$ in the Grassmannian, and hence
\[
\tag{4.2.8.1}
\dim(tW_s\cap H_i)\le \dim(W_s\cap H_i).
\]
In fact, since $\dim(tW_s)=\dim(W_s)$, equality holds in (4.2.8.1). The various isotypic components of $W_s$ and $tW_s$ therefore have the same respective lengths, and $W_s$ is isomorphic to $tW_s$.

We prove (iii), b). It suffices to treat the case where $H$ is homogeneous and $W_s$ is a simple $\C[\pi_1(S,s)]$-module. Let $H_1$ be the isotypic component of $(H_\C)_s$ which contains $W_s$. By a), $H_1$ is stable under $S$. If $W_s$ has dimension $d$ and $H_1$ has length $k$, one has
\[
\bigwedge^{kd}H_1\simeq(\bigwedge^dW_s)^{\otimes k}
\]
as $\C[\pi_1(S,s)]$-modules. If $\chi$ is the character of $\pi_1(S,s)$ defined by $\bigwedge^dW$, the character $\chi_1$ defined by $\bigwedge^{kd}H_1$ is therefore $\chi^k$. Put $H_2=H_1+\overline{H_1}$. Since $H_1$ is stable under $S$, $H_2$ is defined by a real Hodge substructure of $(H_\R)_s$. Every polarization form on $H$ therefore induces on $H_2$ a nondegenerate bilinear form invariant under $\pi_1(S,s)$.

If $\dim(tH_2)=e$, the representation $(\bigwedge^eH_2)^{\otimes2}$ of $\pi_1(S,s)$ is therefore trivial. Thus:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item for $H_1$ real, $\chi^{2k}$ is trivial;
\item for $H_1$ nonreal, $(H_1\ne\overline{H_1})$, $\chi^k\bar\chi^k$ is trivial.
\end{enumerate}
In both cases one has $|\chi|=1$.

The representation of $\pi_1(S,s)$ on $(H_\C)_s$ is obtained by extension of scalars from a representation defined over $\Q$, namely $(H_\Q)_s$. The conjugates of the representation $W$ therefore occur in $(H_\C)_s$; there are at most $N=\dim(H)$ of them, and for every automorphism $\sigma$ of $\C$ one has
\[
|\sigma(\chi)|=1.
\]
For $\gamma\in\pi_1(S,s)$, $\chi(\gamma)$ is an algebraic integer with at most $N$ complex conjugates, all of absolute value $1$; $\chi(\gamma)$ is therefore a $k$-th root of unity, with $k\le N$. Hence $\chi^{N!}=1$, which proves b).

\paragraph{Corollary (4.2.9).} Let $S$ be a smooth connected separated scheme, endowed with a base point $s\in S$. Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism of schemes such that $R^if_*\Q$ is a local system on $S$, let $G$ be the Zariski closure of the image of $\pi_1(S,s)$ in $\Aut_\C((R^if_*\C)_s)$, and let $G^0$ be the identity component of $G$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item If $f$ is proper and smooth, $G^0$ is semisimple.
\item In general, $G^0$ has no quotient of multiplicative type, i.e. the radical of $G^0$ is unipotent.
\end{enumerate}

We prove a). After replacing $S$ by a finite étale covering, we reduce to the case $G=G^0$. By ((4.2.5), (iii)), the local system $R^if_*\Q$ underlies an algebraic family $H$ of Hodge structures, and hence is subject to (4.2.8) ((4.2.5), (i)). The group $G$ is reductive, since it has a faithful semisimple representation, namely $(R^if_*\C)_s$. If $G$, assumed connected, were not semisimple, it would admit a nontrivial complex representation $\rho$ of rank one. Since $H_s$ is faithful, $\rho$ would be a direct factor in $((H+H^*)^{\otimes m})_s$ for some $m$. This contradicts (4.2.8), (iii), b), because $(H+H^*)^{\otimes m}$ is algebraic and no tensor power of $\rho$ is trivial.

Let $\pi$ be a group, and consider the following property of a representation $\rho$ of $\pi$ on a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$:
\[
(*)\quad
\text{the identity component }G^0\text{ of the Zariski closure }G\text{ of }\rho(\pi)
\text{ in }\Aut(V)\text{ has no quotient of multiplicative type.}
\]

\paragraph{Lemma (4.2.10).} Let $0\to V'\to V\to V''\to0$ be an exact sequence of representations of the preceding type. Then $V$ satisfies $(*)$ if and only if $V'$ and $V''$ satisfy $(*)$.

Let $G$, $G'$, and $G''$ be the groups defined by $V$, $V'$, and $V''$. Like $\pi$, $G$ leaves $V'$ stable, whence a morphism
\[
u=(u',u''):G\longrightarrow G'\times G'',
\]
with unipotent kernel, and such that $u'$ and $u''$ are surjective. Let $N$ be the identity component of the kernel of $u''$. Since $u'$ is surjective, $u'(N)$ is distinguished in $G'$, and therefore has no quotient of multiplicative type. If $\chi$ is a character of $G^0$, then $\chi$ factors through $u(G^0)$, is trivial on $N$, a power of $\chi$ factors through $G''^0$, and $\chi$ is trivial.

We prove (4.2.9), b), by dévissage and by induction on the relative dimension of $f$. We may suppose $X$ reduced.

First suppose that $X$ is quasi-projective. Then there exist a nonempty open subset $U$ of $S$, a dense open subset $X'$ of $X_U=f^{-1}(U)$ smooth over $U$, and a compactification $\overline X'$ of $X'$ over $U$, proper and smooth over $U$. One can choose $\overline X'$ so that an open $X''$ of $\overline X'$ contains $X'$ and is proper over $X_U$:
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=.9,baseline=(current bounding box.center)]
\node (XU) at (0,2) {$X_U$};
\node (Xpp) at (2.2,2) {$X''$};
\node (Xbar) at (4.2,2) {$\overline X'$};
\node (Xp) at (1.25,1.15) {$X'$};
\node (U) at (1.85,0) {$U$};
\draw[->] (Xpp)--node[above] {$p$} (XU);
\draw[->] (Xpp)--node[above] {$k$} (Xbar);
\draw[->] (Xp)--node[left] {$i$} (XU);
\draw[->] (Xp)--node[right] {$j$} (Xpp);
\draw[->] (Xp)--(Xbar);
\draw[->] (XU)--node[left] {$f_U$} (U);
\draw[->] (Xp)--node[left] {$f'$} (U);
\draw[->] (Xpp)--node[right] {$f''$} (U);
\draw[->] (Xbar)--node[right] {$\bar f'$} (U);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
We have long exact sequences of sheaves
\[
\begin{gathered}
\to R^if_{U*}(i_!\Q)\to R^if_{U*}\Q\to R^if_{U*}(\Q/i_!\Q)\to,\\
\to R^if''_*(j_!\Q)\to R^if''_*\Q\to R^if''_*(\Q/j_!\Q)\to,\\
\to R^if'_*\Q\to R^i\bar f'_*\Q\to R^i\bar f'_*(\Q/k_!\Q)\to .
\end{gathered}
\]
For $U$ sufficiently small, these sheaves are local systems and the induction hypothesis applies to $R^if_{U*}(\Q/i_!\Q)$, to $R^if''_*(\Q/j_!\Q)$, and to $R^i\bar f'_*(\Q/k_!\Q)$. By a), condition $(*)$ is satisfied by $R^i\bar f'_*\Q$. By (4.2.10), it is therefore satisfied by $R^if'_*\Q$, and by the dual local system $R^if'_!\Q$ (Poincaré duality). By (4.2.10), condition $(*)$ is satisfied by $R^if''_*(j_!\Q)$, isomorphic to $R^if'_!\Q$ since $p$ is proper, and also by $R^if''_*\Q$. Since the morphism $\pi_1(U)\to\pi_1(S)$ is surjective, $R^if_*\Q$ satisfies (4.2.9), b).

To pass from the quasi-projective case to the general case, it suffices to use the Leray spectral sequence for a covering $(U_i)$ of $X$ by quasi-projective open subsets:
\[
R^qf_*(\bigcap_i U_i,\Q)\Longrightarrow R^qf_*\Q,
\]
and (4.2.10).

\subsection*{4.3. Complement to [2]}

In [2], (5.4) (footnote), I asserted without proof that:

\paragraph{Proposition (4.3.1).} Let $X$ be a proper smooth scheme over $\C$. If a $C^\infty$ form on $X$ satisfies $d'\alpha=d''\alpha=0$ and is cohomologous to zero, then there exists $\beta$ such that $\alpha=d'd''\beta$.

Here is the proof. As in loc. cit., we reduce to the case where $\alpha$ is bihomogeneous, of bidegree $(p,q)$. Since the spectral sequence of the double complex $H^0(X,\mathcal A^{pq})$ degenerates, the exterior differential $d$ is strictly compatible with the filtration by first degree $p$ (1.3.2). If $\alpha\sim0$, there therefore exists a form $\beta_1$ such that $d\beta_1=\alpha$ and such that the components $\beta_1^{p'q'}$ of $\beta_1$ are zero for $p'<p$.

By complex conjugation, there similarly exists $\beta_2$ such that $d\beta_2=\alpha$ and $\beta_2^{p'q'}=0$ for $q'<q$. One has $d(\beta_1-\beta_2)=0$.

Let $\gamma$ be a form satisfying $d'\gamma=d''\gamma=0$ in the cohomology class of $\beta_1-\beta_2$ (loc. cit.). Let $\gamma_1$, respectively $\gamma_2$, be the sum of the components of $\gamma$ of type $(p',q')$ with $p'\ge p$, respectively $q'\ge q$. Putting
\[
\beta'_1=\beta_1-\gamma_1,\qquad \beta'_2=\beta_2+\gamma_2,
\]
one still has $d\beta'_1=d\beta'_2=\alpha$; moreover, $\beta'_1-\beta'_2=\beta_1-\beta_2-\gamma$ is cohomologous to zero: there exists $\delta$ such that
\[
d\delta=\beta'_1-\beta'_2.
\]
Let $\delta_1$ be the sum of the components of type $(p',q')$ of $\delta$ with $p'<p-1$, let $\delta_2$ be the sum of the components for which $q'>q-1$, and let $\beta$ be the component of type $(p-1,q-1)$.
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=.65,baseline=(current bounding box.center)]
\draw[->] (0,0)--(3.6,0) node[right] {$p$};
\draw[->] (0,0)--(0,3.2) node[above] {$q$};
\node at (2.45,2.25) {$(p,q)$};
\node at (1.25,1.35) {$\beta$};
\draw[rounded corners] (-.9,2.5)--(.35,1.8)--(.2,1.3)--(-.9,1.95);
\node[rotate=-35] at (-.35,2.08) {$\beta'_2,\gamma_2,\delta_2$};
\draw[rounded corners] (2.1,-.55)--(3.35,.15)--(3.85,.05)--(2.6,-.8);
\node[rotate=20] at (2.95,-.22) {$\beta'_1,\gamma_1,\delta_1$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
One has
\[
\beta'_2=d\delta_2+d''\beta
\quad\text{and}\quad
\alpha=d\beta'_2=dd\delta_2+dd''\beta=d'd''\beta.
\]

\subsection*{4.4. Homomorphisms of abelian schemes}

We propose to show how, in some cases, one can remove from (4.1.3.2) the hypothesis of algebraicity at one point.

\paragraph{(4.4.1)} For $f:X\to S$ a proper smooth morphism, denote by $R_if_*\Z$ the local system on $S^{an}$ of the homology of the fibers of $f$. At each point $s\in S$, $(R_if_*\Z)_s=H_i(X_s,\Z)$ is endowed with a Hodge structure of weight $-i$, dual to the Hodge structure of $H^i(X_s,\Z)$. This structure varies continuously with $s\in S$.

\paragraph{(4.4.2)} If $f:X\to S$ is an abelian scheme, the exponential defines an exact sequence of sheaves on $S^{an}$
\[
0\to R_1f_*\Z\to\Lie(X)\to X\to0,
\]
and the Hodge filtration is the short exact sequence
\[
0\to\Ker(\alpha)\to R_1f_*\Z\otimes\cO\xrightarrow{\alpha}\Lie(X)\to0.
\]
It is known that:

\paragraph{Reminder (4.4.3).} Let $S$ be a smooth scheme of finite type over $\C$. The construction (4.4.2) establishes an equivalence of categories between:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item the category of abelian schemes over $S$;
\item the category of polarizable continuous families of Hodge structures $H$ of type $(-1,0)+(0,-1)$ on $S$, such that $H_\Z$ is torsion-free and the Hodge filtration varies holomorphically on $S$.
\end{enumerate}
Essential surjectivity of the functor a)$\mapsto$b) follows from Borel [13]; full faithfulness follows from the fact that, for $X_1$ and $X_2$ two abelian schemes over $S$, the $S$-scheme $\Hom_S(X_1,X_2)$ is unramified over $S$, and therefore has the same algebraic and holomorphic sections.

\paragraph{(4.4.4)} Let $Z$ be a commutative field, $H$ a central simple algebra over $Z$, and $*$ a $Z$-linear antiautomorphism of $H$. After extension of scalars, one has $H\simeq\End(V)$ for a vector space $V$ over $Z$, and $*$ is then transposition with respect to a bilinear form $\Phi$ on $V$, unique up to scalar. Suppose that $*$ is involutive; the forms $\Phi(X,Y)$ and $\Phi(Y,X)$ are then proportional:
\[
\Phi(X,Y)=\lambda\Phi(Y,X),\qquad \lambda^2=1.
\]
Put $\varepsilon_*=\lambda$. If $[H:Z]=d^2$, then
\[
\Tr(*:H\to H)=\varepsilon_*d.
\]
Every $Z$-linear automorphism $C$ of $H$ is inner: $Cx=cxc^{-1}$. If $C$ is involutive and commutes with $*$, the elements $c^2$ and $cc^*$ are central, so that $c^*=\lambda c$ with $\lambda$ central. Since $c^{**}=c$, one has $\lambda^2=1$. Put $\varepsilon_C=\lambda$.

One checks easily that the involution $C\circ *$ satisfies
\[
\varepsilon_{C\circ *}=\varepsilon_C\varepsilon_*.
\]

\paragraph{Reminder (4.4.5).} Let $X$ be a simple abelian variety over a field $K$ and let $H$ be the field $\End_K(X)\otimes\Q$. Every polarization of $X$ defines a positive involution $*$ of $H$:
\[
\Tr(hh^*)>0\qquad\text{if }h\ne0.
\]
If $Z_0$ is the subfield invariant under $*$ in the center $Z$ of $H$, then $Z_0$ is totally real and one is in one of the following cases:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item $Z=Z_0$; for every real place $\rho$ of $Z$, $H\otimes_\rho\R$ is a matrix algebra over $\R$, and $\varepsilon_*=-1$.
\item $Z=Z_0$; for every real place $\rho$ of $Z$, $H\otimes_\rho\R$ is a matrix algebra over $\mathbb H$, and $\varepsilon_*=-1$.
\item $Z$ is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of $Z_0$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(4.4.6)} Let $f_i:X_i\to S$ ($i=1,2$) be two abelian schemes over a smooth scheme $S$. The free abelian group
\[
\Hom(R_1f_{1*}\Z,R_1f_{2*}\Z)
=\Gamma\bigl(S,\Hom(R_1f_{1*}\Z,R_1f_{2*}\Z)\bigr)
\]
is then endowed with a Hodge structure of weight $0$ (4.2.8). Moreover, by (2.1.11.1) and (4.4.3), the group $\Hom_S(X_1,X_2)$ is the component of type $(0,0)$ of this group.

\paragraph{(4.4.7)} Let $S$ be a nonempty smooth connected scheme with generic point $\eta$, and let $f:X\to S$ be an abelian scheme over $S$. The center $Z$ of $H=\End(R_1f_*\Z)$ is then of type $(0,0)$ (4.2.8), and is therefore contained in $\End_S(X)\subset \End_{k(\eta)}(X_\eta)$.

A polarization of $X$ defines an involution $*$ of $H$. Its restriction to $\End_S(X)$ is positive, so that $Z$ is a product of totally real fields or quadratic imaginary extensions of totally real fields.

Let $C$ denote the actions of $i\in S(\R)$ on $H_\R=H\otimes\R$ and on the $(R_1f_*\R)_s$, for $s\in S$. On $H_\R$, $C^2=1$, and $C$ commutes with $*$; on $(R_1f_*\R)_s$, $C^2=-1$. If $\psi$ is the alternating form on $R_1f_*\Z$, with integral values, deduced from the polarization of $X$, then
\[
\psi(hx,Cy)=\psi(x,h^*Cy)=\psi(x,C\,C(h^*)y).
\]
It follows that
\[
\tag{4.4.7.1}
\Tr(h\,C(h^*))>0\qquad\text{for }h\ne0.
\]

\paragraph{(4.4.8)} Now suppose that $X_\eta$ is simple, so that $Z$ is a field. Let $\rho:Z\to\C$ be a complex embedding. The algebra
\[
H_\rho=H\otimes_{Z,\rho}\C
\]
is then a matrix algebra and a bigraded direct factor in $H_\C=H\otimes_\Q\C$. Choose an isomorphism
\[
\tag{4.4.8.1}
H_\rho\simeq\End(V_\rho).
\]
Since the infinitesimal generators of $S$ act on $H_\rho$ by derivations, necessarily inner, it follows that $V_\rho$ admits a bigrading, unique up to translation, such that (4.4.8.1) is a bigraded isomorphism. Let $(R_1f_*\C)_\rho$ be the bigraded local system
\[
(R_1f_*\C)\otimes_{Z\otimes\C,\rho}\C,
\]
a direct factor of $R_1f_*\C$. Let
\[
T_\rho=\Hom_{H_\rho}(V_\rho,(R_1f_*\C)_\rho)
\]
be the corresponding local system. One has
\[
\tag{4.4.8.2}
V_\rho\otimes_\C T_\rho\iso (R_1f_*\C)_\rho
\]
as a bigraded isomorphism. Since $(R_1f_*\C)_\rho$ has type $(-1,0)+(0,-1)$, one of the following conditions is satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item $V_\rho$ is bihomogeneous;
\item $T_\rho$ is bihomogeneous.
\end{enumerate}
It is clear that condition a), respectively b), is simultaneously satisfied for $\rho$ and for $\bar\rho$. If condition a) is satisfied at every place, then $H$ is of type $(0,0)$. If condition b) is satisfied at every place, then the Hodge structure on $R_1f_*\Q$ is locally constant.

\paragraph{(4.4.9)} Now suppose that $Z$ is totally real. For every real embedding $\varphi$ of $Z$, put $\varepsilon_{H,\varphi}=+1$ if $H\otimes_{Z,\varphi}\R$ is a matrix algebra, and $\varepsilon_{H,\varphi}=-1$ otherwise. If a complex embedding $\rho$ induces a real embedding $\varphi$, put $\varepsilon_{H,\rho}=\varepsilon_{H,\varphi}$.

Let $\rho:Z\to\C$ factor through $\varphi:Z\to\R$. Complex conjugation on $H_\rho$ is induced by an antilinear automorphism $\sigma_1$ of $V_\rho$, unique up to a real scalar factor, with scalar square. Since $\sigma_1$ commutes with $\sigma_1^2$, $\sigma_1^2$ is real and, normalizing $\sigma_1$, one may suppose that $\sigma_1^2=\pm1$. Then
\[
\tag{4.4.9.1}
\sigma_1^2=\varepsilon_{H,\rho}.
\]
The automorphism of complex conjugation on
\[
(R_1f_*\C)_\rho=((R_1f_*\Q)\otimes_{Z,\varphi}\R)\otimes_\R\C
\]
is ``compatible'' with (4.4.8.2) and may be written
\[
\sigma=\sigma_1\otimes\sigma_2.
\]
Since $\sigma^2=1$, by (4.4.9.1) one has
\[
\tag{4.4.9.2}
\sigma_2^2=\sigma_1^2=\varepsilon_{H,\rho}.
\]

Let $\Phi$ be a polarization form on $R_1f_*\Z$. This form is necessarily of the type
\[
\Phi=\Tr_{Z/\Q}(\psi),
\]
where $\psi$ is a $Z$-bilinear alternating form on $R_1f_*\Q$.

Let $\psi_\rho$ be the induced form on $V_\rho\otimes_\C T_\rho$. The alternating form $\psi_\rho$ is invariant under $\pi_1(S,s)$. Since $T_\rho$ is irreducible, it can be written
\[
\tag{4.4.9.3}
\psi_\rho=A_\rho\otimes B_\rho,
\]
with $A_\rho$ on $V_\rho$, and $B_\rho$ on $T_\rho$, invariant under $\pi_1(S,s)$.

Since $T_\rho$ is irreducible, $B_\rho$ is symmetric or alternating. Since $\psi_\rho$ is alternating, $A_\rho$ is then alternating or symmetric. Since the $T_\rho$ are conjugate to one another, which case occurs is independent of $\rho$. Put
\[
\tag{4.4.9.4}
A_\rho(X,Y)=\varepsilon_V A_\rho(Y,X),
\]
\[
\tag{4.4.9.5}
B_\rho(X,Y)=\varepsilon_T B_\rho(Y,X).
\]
Thus
\[
\tag{4.4.9.6}
\varepsilon_V\varepsilon_T=-1
\]
and it is clear from (4.4.4) that, if $*$ is the involution of $H$ deduced from $\psi$,
\[
\tag{4.4.9.7}
\varepsilon_*=\varepsilon_V.
\]
In case (4.4.8), a), respectively b), normalize the bigradings so that $V_\rho$, respectively $T_\rho$, has bidegree $(0,0)$. Then for nonzero $x,y$ in $V_\rho$ and $T_\rho$:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{case a)}& A_\rho(x,\sigma_1x)\,B_\rho(y,C\sigma_2y)>0,\\[0.3em]
\text{case b)}& A_\rho(x,C\sigma_1x)\,B_\rho(y,\sigma_2y)>0.
\end{array}
\]
Normalizing $A_\rho$ and $B_\rho$, of which only the tensor product is given, one may suppose that
\[
\begin{array}{llll}
\text{case a)}& A_\rho(x,\sigma_1x)>0 & \text{and} & B_\rho(y,C\sigma_2y)>0,\\[0.3em]
\text{case b)}& A_\rho(x,C\sigma_1x)>0 & \text{and} & B_\rho(y,\sigma_2y)>0.
\end{array}
\]
Moreover $A_\rho$ and $B_\rho$, just like $\Phi$, are invariant under the compact subtorus $\Ker(N)$ of $S$, and in particular under $C$. In case a) one has
\[
0<A_\rho(\sigma_1x,\sigma_1\sigma_1x)
=\varepsilon_V\varepsilon_{H,\rho}A_\rho(x,\sigma_1x),
\]
whence $\varepsilon_V\varepsilon_{H,\rho}=1$. Similarly, in case b), one finds $\varepsilon_T\varepsilon_{H,\rho}=1$.

\paragraph{Lemma (4.4.10).} For $\varepsilon_T=\varepsilon_{H,\rho}$, one is in case b); for $\varepsilon_T=-\varepsilon_{H,\rho}$, one is in case a).

\paragraph{Proposition (4.4.11).} Let $S$ be a nonempty smooth connected scheme with generic point $\eta$, let $f:X\to S$ be an abelian scheme over $S$, let $H=\End(R_1f_*\Q)$, and let $Z$ be the center of $H$. Suppose that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $X_\eta$ is simple over $k(\eta)$.
\item $X$ does not become constant on any finite covering of $S$.
\item One of the following conditions is satisfied (cf. (4.4.5)):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(c\arabic*)]
\item $Z$ is imaginary quadratic;
\item $Z$ is totally real, and for every real embedding $\varphi:Z\to\R$, $H\otimes_{Z,\varphi}\R$ is a matrix algebra over $\R$;
\item $Z$ is totally real, and for every real embedding $\varphi:Z\to\R$, $H\otimes_{Z,\varphi}\R$ is a matrix algebra over $\mathbb H$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
\End(X)\iso\End(R_1f_*\Z).
\]

We show that one is in case (4.4.8), a), or in case (4.4.8), b), at all places of $Z$ simultaneously. This is clear from (4.4.8) under hypothesis (c1), and follows from (4.4.10) under hypotheses (c2) or (c3), because not only $\varepsilon_T$ but also $\varepsilon_{H,\rho}$ is then independent of $\rho$.

Condition (4.4.8), b), cannot be satisfied at every place of $Z$, for the Hodge structure would then be locally constant, which by (4.1.3.3) and (4.4.3) violates b). Hence condition a) is satisfied at every place of $Z$, so that $H$ is of type $(0,0)$ (4.4.8). In view of (4.4.3), this completes the proof.

\paragraph{Proposition (4.4.12).} Let $f:X\to S$ be an abelian scheme over a smooth scheme $S$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every abelian scheme $g:Y\to S$, one has
\[
\Hom_S(X,Y)\iso \Hom_S(R_1f_*\Z,R_1g_*\Z).
\]
\item Condition (i) is satisfied for $X=Y$, and the center $Z$ of $\End_S(X)\otimes\Q$ has no complex place $\rho:Z\to\C$ such that the direct factor $R_1f_*\Q\otimes_{Z,\rho}\C$ of $R_1f_*\C$ is purely of Hodge type $(-1,0)$.
\end{enumerate}



One reduces to the case where $S$ is nonempty and connected, with generic point $\eta$, and where $X_\eta$ is a simple abelian variety over $k(\eta)$. If (i) or (ii) is satisfied, then
\[
D=\End(X_\eta)\otimes\Q
\]
is a division algebra, and, for $s\in S$, $(R_1f_*\Q)_s$ is an irreducible representation of $\pi_1(S,s)$.

To prove that (ii) implies (i), one may suppose that $Y_\eta$ is simple and that $(R_1g_*\Q)_s$ is isotypic of type $(R_1f_*\Q)_s$. One then has an isomorphism of continuous families of $\Q$-Hodge structures, with $D$ of type $(0,0)$,
\[
R_1g_*\Q\simeq R_1f_*\Q\otimes_D
\Hom(R_1f_*\Q,R_1g_*\Q).
\]
One has $D\otimes\C\simeq M_n(Z\otimes\C)$. If $e\in D\otimes\C$ is transformed, under such an isomorphism, into the idempotent $e_{11}$, one still has an isomorphism of bigraded vector spaces
\[
(R_1g_*\C)_s\simeq (R_1f_*\C)_s\otimes_{Z\otimes\C}
e\,\Hom(R_1f_*\C,R_1g_*\C).
\]
If condition (ii) is satisfied, and if
\[
e\bigl(\Hom(R_1f_*\C,R_1g_*\C)\bigr)
\]
is not purely of type $(0,0)$, then $(R_1g_*\C)_s$ could not be purely of type $(-1,0)+(0,-1)$, which is absurd. It follows that $\Hom(R_1f_*\Z,R_1g_*\Z)$ is purely of type $(0,0)$, and (i) follows from (4.4.3) and (2.1.11.1).

Let $Z$ be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real number field $Z^0$. If
\[
Z\otimes\R\simeq \C\oplus\cdots\oplus\C,
\]
define an action by homotheties of $S$ on $Z\otimes\R$ by sending $s\in S(\R)\simeq\C^*$ to
\[
(s^2N(s)^{-1},1,\ldots,1).
\]
The pair formed by $Z$ and this action of $S$ on $Z\otimes\R$ is a polarizable Hodge structure $Z_\rho$ of type
\[
(-1,1)+(0,0)+(1,-1),
\]
depending on $Z$ and on the chosen place $\rho:Z\to\C$.

Let $X$ be an abelian scheme over $S$, with $X_\eta$ simple. If the center $Z$ of $\End(R_1f_*\Q)$ is not totally real, it is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real number field. If the second hypothesis of (ii) is violated, the direct factor
\[
R_1f_*\Q\otimes_Z Z_\rho
\]
of the continuous family of polarizable Hodge structures $R_1f_*\Q\otimes_\Q Z_\rho$ is purely of type $(-1,0)+(0,-1)$.

By (4.4.3), the choice of an integral lattice in $R_1f_*\Q\otimes_ZZ_\rho$ defines an abelian scheme $g:Y\to S$, and $\Hom(R_1f_*\Q,R_1g_*\Q)$ is not purely of type $(0,0)$, which violates (i).

\paragraph{Corollary (4.4.13).} Let $S$ be a smooth connected scheme with generic point $\eta$, and let $f:X\to S$ be an abelian scheme over $S$ of relative dimension $g\le3$. Suppose that on no finite étale covering of $S$ does $X$ admit a constant abelian subscheme. Then, for every abelian scheme $g:Y\to S$, one has
\[
\Hom(X,Y)\iso \Hom(R_1f_*\Z,R_1g_*\Z)
\]
and
\[
\Hom(Y,X)\iso \Hom(R_1g_*\Z,R_1f_*\Z).
\]

By duality, one is reduced to proving only the first assertion. One is also reduced to the case where $X_\eta$ is simple. We verify that $X$ satisfies one of the conditions (c) of (4.4.11). Put
\[
H=M_c(D),
\]
where $D$ is a division algebra of rank $b^2$ over its center $Z$, which is known to be either totally real or totally imaginary. Putting $a=[Z:\Q]$, one has
\[
\tag{4.4.13.1}
ab^2c\mid 2g\le6.
\]

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item If $Z$ is totally imaginary and not imaginary quadratic, one must have $a=2g=4$. For $s\in S$, the commutant of $Z$ acting on $(R_1f_*\Q)_s$ is then commutative, so the action of $\pi_1(S,s)$ is abelian, hence factors through a finite group ((4.2.8), (iii), b), or (4.2.9)). This is absurd ((4.1.3.3) and (4.4.3)).

With the notation of (4.4.8), one could also note that the $T_\rho$ are then of rank one, so that condition (4.4.8), b), is satisfied at every place and the Hodge structure is locally constant.

\item If $Z$ is totally real and if the conditions $(c_1)$ and $(c_2)$ are violated, then $a\ge2$ and $b\ge2$, which is absurd.
\end{enumerate}

Let us verify that $X$ satisfies the conditions of (4.4.12). If $Z$ is imaginary quadratic, and if there exists $\rho:Z\to\C$ such that
\[
R_1f_*\Q\otimes_{Z,\rho}\C
\]
is purely of Hodge type $(-1,0)$, then the Hodge structure of $R_1f_*\Q$ is locally constant, which is absurd. This proves (4.4.13).

\paragraph{(4.4.14)} Let $f:X\to S$ be a polarized abelian scheme of dimension $g$ over a smooth connected base $S$. If $s\in S$, the fundamental group $\pi_1(S,s)$ acts on the fiber $H^1(X_s,\Z)$ of $R^1f_*\Z$ at $s$. The polarization of $X$ defines an alternating form on $H^1(X_s,\Z)$, with values in $\Z(-1)$, nondegenerate over $\Q$, and the action of $\pi_1(S,s)$ respects this form. Consider the hypothesis:
\begin{quote}
\noindent\textbf{(4.4.14.1)} The morphism defined by $X$ from $S$ into one of the irreducible components of the corresponding moduli scheme of polarized abelian varieties is dominant.
\end{quote}
The following result was suggested to me by J.-P. Serre.

\paragraph{Corollary (4.4.15).} Let $S$ be a smooth connected scheme and let $f:X\to S$ be a polarized abelian scheme over $S$ satisfying (4.4.14.1). Then, for every abelian scheme $g:Y\to S$, one has
\[
\Hom(X,Y)\iso\Hom(R_1f_*\Z,R_1g_*\Z).
\]

Let $s\in S$. By (4.4.11) and (4.4.12), it suffices to show that the representation of $\pi_1(S,s)$ on $(R_1f_*\Q)_s$ is absolutely irreducible, so that $H=\Q$. This follows from the following lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma (4.4.16).} If $X$ satisfies (4.4.14.1), then the image of $\pi_1(S,s)$ in the group of symplectic automorphisms of $H^1(X_s,\Z)$ is of finite index.

Let $n$ be an integer. After replacing $S$ by a surjective étale cover, defined by a subgroup of finite index in $\pi_1(S,s)$, one may suppose that the local system
\[
{}_nX=\Ker(n\cdot \operatorname{id}:X\to X)
\]
is trivial on $S$. Replacing $X$ by an isogenous abelian scheme, one is then further reduced to supposing that $X$ is principally polarized. Suppose $n\ge3$.

The abelian scheme $X$ then defines a dominant morphism $x$ from $S$ to the ``Jacobi'' moduli scheme $M_n$ of principally polarized abelian schemes endowed with a symplectic isomorphism between ${}_nX$ and $(\Z/n)^{2g}$; moreover, $X$ is the inverse image under $x$ of the universal abelian scheme over $M_n$. It is known that $\pi_1(M_n,x(s))$ maps isomorphically onto the subgroup of $\Sp(H^1(X_s,\Z))$ which is the kernel of reduction modulo $n$.

It remains only to verify:

\paragraph{Lemma (4.4.17).} Let $p:S\to M$ be a dominant morphism of smooth connected schemes. The subgroup $p(\pi_1(S,s))$ of $\pi_1(M,p(s))$ is then of finite index.

Let $t$ be a closed point of the generic fiber of $p$, and let $T'$ be its closure in $S$. There exists a dense Zariski open subset $U$ of $M$ such that
\[
T=p^{-1}(U)\cap T'
\]
is an étale covering of $U$.

There is no loss of generality in taking $s\in T$. In the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=4.2em,row sep=3.2em]
\pi_1(T,s) \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"a"'] & \pi_1(S,s) \arrow[d,"c"]\\
\pi_1(U,p(s)) \arrow[r,"b"'] & \pi_1(M,p(s)),
\end{tikzcd}
\]
the arrow $a$ has image of finite index, because $T$ is a finite étale cover, and the arrow $b$ is surjective, because $M-U$ has real codimension $\ge2$. Thus the arrow $c$ has image of finite index.
