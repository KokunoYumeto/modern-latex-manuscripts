\section*{2.4 Application to Presheaves with Transfers}

Let \(F\) be a presheaf with transfers. A formal argument [7] shows that the presheaves with transfers \(H^i C_*(F)\) are homotopy invariant. By the basic result (Theorem 1) recalled in the first lecture, it follows that for any \(U\), one has
\[
\mathbf H^*(U,C_*(F))=\mathbf H^*(U\times\Aone,C_*(F)) \tag{4.1}
\]
Indeed, as \(U\) and \(U\times\Aone\) are of finite cohomological dimension, both sides are abutment of a convergent spectral sequence
\[
E_2^{pq}=H^p(U,H^qC_*(F))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(U,C_*(F))
\]
and the same for \(U\times\Aone\). By Theorem 1 applied to \(H^qC_*(F)\),
\[
H^p(X,H^qC_*(F)):=H^p(X,aH^qC_*(F))
\]
is the same for \(X=U\) or for \(X=U\times\Aone\). Applying (3.5), we conclude from Proposition 4 \((2)\Rightarrow(1)\) that

\begin{proposition*}[5]
For \(k\) perfect, if \(F\) is a presheaf with transfers, for all \(p\), \(K(C_*(F)[p])\) is \(\Aone\)-local.
\end{proposition*}

Combining Proposition 5 with Proposition 7 we get the second equality in (1.1).

\section*{2.5 End of the Proof of Theorem 2}

For any pointed simplicial sheaf \(G_\bullet\), \(C_\bullet(G_\bullet)\) is a pointed bisimplicial sheaf of which one can take the diagonal \(\Delta C_\bullet(G_\bullet)\). For any pointed sheaf \(G\), one has a natural map \(G\to C_\bullet(G)\), and for a pointed simplicial sheaf \(G_\bullet\), those maps for the \(G_n\) induce
\[
a:G_\bullet\longrightarrow \Delta C_\bullet(G_\bullet).
\]

\begin{proposition*}[6]
The morphism \(a:G_\bullet\to\Delta C_\bullet(G_\bullet)\) is an \(\Aone\)-equivalence.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
We deduce Proposition 6 from Proposition 20.

The two maps \(0,1:F_\bullet\to F_\bullet\wedge\Aone_+\) are equalized by \(F_\bullet\wedge\Aone_+\to F_\bullet\), hence become equal in the \(\Aone\)-homotopy category. If two maps of pointed simplicial sheaves \(F_\bullet\rightrightarrows G_\bullet\) factor as \(F_\bullet\to F_\bullet\wedge\Aone_+\to G_\bullet\), they also become equal. By the adjunction of \(\wedge\Aone_+\) and of \(C_1(-)=\Hom(\Aone_+,-)\), such a factorization can be rewritten as
\[
F_\bullet\longrightarrow C_1(G_\bullet)\longrightarrow G_\bullet.
\]
Particular case: the maps \(C_1(G_\bullet)\to G_\bullet\) become equal in the homotopy category. Evaluated on \(X\), these maps are the restriction maps \(0^*,1^*:G_\bullet(X\times\Aone)\to G_\bullet(X)\).

The affine space \(\A^n\) is homotopic to a point in the sense that
\[
H:\Aone\times\A^n\longrightarrow\A^n,\qquad (t,x)\longmapsto tx
\]
interpolates between the identity map (for \(t=1\)) and the constant map \(0\) (for \(t=0\)). The map \(H\) induces
\[
G_\bullet(S\times\A^n)\longrightarrow G_\bullet(S\times\A^n\times\Aone)
\]
and, composing with \(0,1\) in \(\Aone\), we obtain that
\[
G_\bullet(S\times\A^n)\rightrightarrows G_\bullet(S\times\A^n)
\]
the identity map, and the map induced by \(0:\A^n\to\A^n\), are equal in the \(\Aone\)-homotopy category. The map of simplicial sheaves \(G_\bullet\to C_nG_\bullet\) is hence an \(\Aone\)-equivalence. It has as inverse in the \(\Aone\)-homotopy category the map induced by \(0:\Spec(k)\to\A^n\), and one applies Remark 1.

We now apply Proposition 20 to the bisimplicial sheaves
\[
G_{pq}:=G_p,\qquad
H_{pq}:=C_qG_p:S\longmapsto G_p(S\times\Delta^q)
\]
and to the natural map \(G_{pq}\to H_{pq}\). For fixed \(q\), this is just \(G(S)\to G(S\times\A^q)\), and Proposition 20 gives Proposition 6.
\end{proofdel}

To prove the last equality in (1.1), it suffices to show that:

\begin{lemma*}[1]
For any abelian sheaf \(F\), \(F[p]\to C_*(F)[p]\) induces an \(\Aone\)-equivalence from \(K(F[p])\) to \(K(C_*(F)[p])\).
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proofdel}
For \(G\) a monoid (with unit), the pointed simplicial set \(B_\bullet G\) is given by
\[
B_nG=\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
\hbox{functors from the ordered set }(0,\ldots,n)\hbox{ viewed as a category}\\
\hbox{to }G\hbox{ viewed as a category with one object}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
This construction can be sheafified, and can be applied termwise to a simplicial sheaf of monoids, leading to a pointed bisimplicial sheaf of which one can take the diagonal
\[
BG_\bullet:=\Delta B_\bullet(G_\bullet).
\]
This construction commutes with the construction \(G_\bullet\to\Delta C_\bullet(G_\bullet)\). Indeed, \(B_nG_p\) is naturally isomorphic to \(G_p^n\); the operation \(C_m\) commutes with products, and \(B(\Delta C_\bullet(G_\bullet))\) and \(\Delta C_\bullet(BG_\bullet)\) are both diagonals of the trisimplicial pointed sheaf \(C_\bullet B_\bullet G_\bullet\).

For abelian simplicial sheaves, the operation \(B\) gives again abelian simplicial sheaves, hence can be iterated, and \(\Delta C_\bullet\) commutes with \(B^n\).

Via Dold--Puppe construction, \(B\) corresponds, up to homotopy, to the shift \([1]\) of complexes:
\[
NBG_\bullet\simeq(NG_\bullet)[1].
\]
This can be viewed as an application of the Eilenberg--Zilber Theorem (see [9, Theorem 8.5.1]): one has
\[
NBG_\bullet\simeq BG_*\simeq \Tot B_*G_*\qquad\hbox{(Eilenberg--Zilber),}
\]
and for each \(G_q\), the normalization of \(B_\bullet G_q\) is just \(G_q[1]\), so that the double complexes \(B_*G_*\) and \(H_{pq}:=G_q\) for \(p=1,0\) otherwise, have homotopic \(\Tot\).

If \(G_\bullet\) is an abelian simplicial sheaf, applying Proposition 6 to \(B^pG_\bullet\), we obtain that
\[
B^pG_\bullet\longrightarrow \Delta C_\bullet B^pG_\bullet
       =B^p\Delta C_\bullet G_\bullet                       \tag{6.1}
\]
is an \(\Aone\)-equivalence. The functor \(K\) transforms chain homotopy equivalences into simplicial equivalences. For any simplicial abelian group \(L_\bullet\) (to be \(G_\bullet\) or \(\Delta C_\bullet G_\bullet\)), we hence have a simplicial homotopy equivalence
\[
B^pL_\bullet=KNB^pL_\bullet\simeq K((NL_\bullet)[p]).
\]
Simplicial homotopy equivalences being \(\Aone\)-equivalences, we conclude that (6.1) induces an \(\Aone\)-equivalence
\[
K((NG_\bullet)[p])\longrightarrow K(N(\Delta C_\bullet G_\bullet)[p]).
\]
\end{proofdel}

\section*{2.6 Appendix: Localization}

Let \(C\) be a category and \(S\) be a set of morphisms of \(C\). The localized category \(C[S^{-1}]\) is deduced from \(C\) by ``formally inverting all \(s\in S\)''. With this definition, it is clear that one has a natural functor
\[
\operatorname{loc}:C\longrightarrow C[S^{-1}],
\]
bijective on the set of objects, and that for any category \(D\),
\[
F\longmapsto F\circ\operatorname{loc}:\Hom(C[S^{-1}],D)\longrightarrow\Hom(C,D)
\]
is a bijection from \(\Hom(C[S^{-1}],D)\) to the set of functors from \(C\) to \(D\) transforming morphisms in \(S\) into isomorphisms.

If one remembers that the categories form a 2-category, and if one agrees with the principle that one should not try to define a category more precisely than up to equivalence (unique up to unique isomorphism), the universal property of \(C[S^{-1}]\) given above is doubly unsatisfactory. The easily checked and useful universal property is the following: \(F\mapsto F\circ\operatorname{loc}\) is an equivalence from the category \(\Hom(C[S^{-1}],D)\) to the full subcategory of \(\Hom(C,D)\) consisting of the functors \(F\) which map \(S\) to isomorphisms.

\begin{proposition*}[7]
If \(Y\) in \(C\) is such that the functor
\[
h_Y:C^{op}\longrightarrow \mathrm{Sets}:X\longmapsto\Hom_C(X,Y)
\]
transforms maps in \(S\) into bijections, then
\[
\Hom_C(X,Y)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Hom_{C[S^{-1}]}(X,Y).
\]
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
By Yoneda construction \(Y\mapsto h_Y\), \(C\) embeds into the category \(C^\wedge\) of contravariant functors from \(C\) to \(\mathrm{Sets}\), while \(C[S^{-1}]\) embeds into \(C[S^{-1}]^\wedge\), identified by (a) with the full subcategory of \(C^\wedge\) consisting of the \(F\) transforming \(S\) into bijections. For \(Y\) in \(C\), with image \(\bar Y\) in \(C[S^{-1}]\), and for any \(F\) in \(C[S^{-1}]^\wedge\subset C^\wedge\), one has
\[
\Hom(h_Y,F)=\Hom(h_{\bar Y},F).
\]
Indeed, by (a) and the Yoneda lemma for \(C\) and \(C[S^{-1}]\), both sides are \(F(Y)\). This means that \(h_{\bar Y}\) is the solution of the universal problem of mapping \(h_Y\) into an object of \(C[S^{-1}]^\wedge\subset C^\wedge\). In particular, for \(Y\) as in (b), i.e. in \(C[S^{-1}]^\wedge\), \(h_{\bar Y}\) coincides with \(h_Y\), as claimed by (b).
\end{proofdel}

\begin{proposition*}[8]
Let \((L,R)\) be a pair of adjoint functors between categories \(C\) and \(D\). Let \(S\) and \(T\) be sets of morphisms in \(C\) and \(D\). Assume that \(F\) maps \(S\) to \(T\) and that \(G\) maps \(T\) to \(S\). Then the functors \(\bar L,\bar R\) between \(C[S^{-1}]\) and \(D[T^{-1}]\) induced by \(L\) and \(R\) again form an adjoint pair.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
The functors \(\bar L\) and \(\bar R\) induced by \(F\) and \(G\) are characterized by commutative diagrams
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.4em,row sep=2.2em]
C \arrow[r,"L"] \arrow[d] & D \arrow[d]\\
C[S^{-1}] \arrow[r,"\bar L"] & D[T^{-1}]
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.4em,row sep=2.2em]
D \arrow[r,"R"] \arrow[d] & C \arrow[d]\\
D[T^{-1}] \arrow[r,"\bar R"] & C[S^{-1}].
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Adjunction can be expressed by the data of \(\varepsilon:Id\to RL\) and \(\eta:LR\to Id\), such that the compositions
\[
R\longrightarrow RLR\longrightarrow R,\qquad
L\longrightarrow LRL\longrightarrow L
\]
are the identity automorphisms of \(R\) and \(L\), respectively (see, e.g., [6]).

By the universal property of localization, \(\varepsilon\) induces a morphism \(\bar\varepsilon\to\bar R\bar L\), indeed to define such a morphism amounts to defining a morphism \(\operatorname{loc}\to\bar R\bar L\operatorname{loc}\), and \(\bar R\bar L\operatorname{loc}=\operatorname{loc}RL\). Similarly, \(\eta\) induces \(\bar\eta:\bar L\bar R\to Id\). The morphism \(\bar R\to\bar R\bar L\bar R\to\bar R\), and similarly for \(\bar L\), is induced by \(R\to RLR\to R\), and the proposition follows.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{proposition*}[9]
Suppose that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item The localization \(C[S^{-1}]\) gives rise to a right calculus of fractions.
\item Coproducts exist in \(C\), and \(S\) is stable by coproducts.
\end{enumerate}
Then a coproduct in \(C\) is also a coproduct in \(C[S^{-1}]\).
\end{proposition*}

For the definition of ``gives rise to a right calculus of fractions'' see [10]. It implies that for \(X\) in \(C\), the category of \(s:X'\to X\) with \(s\in S\) is filtering, and that
\[
\Hom_{C[S^{-1}]}(X,Y)=
\operatorname*{colim}_{s:X'\to X}\Hom_C(X',Y).
\]

\begin{proofdel}
For \(X\) in \(C\), let \((S/X)\) be the filtering category of morphisms \(X'\to X\) in \(S\). For \(X\) the coproduct of \(X_\alpha\), \(\alpha\in A\), one has a functor ``coproduct''
\[
\prod_{\alpha\in A}(S/X_\alpha)\longrightarrow(S/X).
\]
It is cofinal: for \(s:X'\to X\) in \(S\), one can construct a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.2em,row sep=2.2em]
X'_\alpha \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"s_\alpha"'] & X_\alpha \arrow[d]\\
X' \arrow[r,"s"'] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(s_\alpha:X'_\alpha\to X\) in \(S\), and \(\coprod s_\alpha\) dominates \(s\). For any \(Y\), it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Hom_{C[S^{-1}]}(X,Y)
&=\operatorname*{colim}_{(S/X)}\Hom_C(X',Y)\\
&=\operatorname*{colim}_{\prod(S/X_\alpha)}\Hom\Bigl(\coprod X'_\alpha,Y\Bigr)\\
&=\operatorname*{colim}_{\prod(S/X_\alpha)}\prod\Hom(X'_\alpha,Y)\\
&=\prod\operatorname*{colim}_{S/X_\alpha}\Hom(X'_\alpha,Y)
 =\prod\Hom_{C[S^{-1}]}(X_\alpha,Y),
\end{aligned}
\]
meaning that \(X\) is also the coproduct of the \(X_\alpha\) in \(C[S^{-1}]\).
\end{proofdel}

\begin{corollary*}[10]
Suppose that in the abelian category \(A\) arbitrary direct sums exist and are exact. Then arbitrary direct sums exist in the derived category \(D(A)\), and the localization functor
\[
C(A)\longrightarrow D(A)
\]
commutes with direct sums.
\end{corollary*}

\begin{proofdel}
The functor \(C(A)\to D(A)\) factors through the category \(K(A)\) of complexes and maps up to homotopy. Direct sums in \(C(A)\) are also direct sums in \(K(A)\). Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Hom_{K(A)}(\oplus K_\alpha,L)
&=H^0\Hom^\bullet(\oplus K_\alpha,L)\\
&=H^0\prod\Hom^\bullet(K_\alpha,L)
 =\prod H^0\Hom^\bullet(K_\alpha,L),
\end{aligned}
\]
as \(\prod\) is exact for abelian groups. Exactness of \(\oplus\) in \(A\) ensures that a direct sum of quasi-isomorphisms is again a quasi-isomorphism, and Proposition 9 applies to \(K(A)\) and the set \(S\) of quasi-isomorphisms, proving the corollary.
\end{proofdel}

If \(A_i\), \(i\ge0\), is an inductive system of objects of \(A\), the colimit of \(A_i\) is the cokernel
\[
\oplus A_i\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\oplus A_i\longrightarrow \operatorname{colim}A_i\longrightarrow0
\]
of the difference of the identity map and of the sum of the \(A_i\to A_{i+1}\). If taking the inductive limit of a sequence is an exact functor, the map \(d\) is injective: it is the colimit of the
\[
\oplus_{i=0}^n A_i\longrightarrow \oplus_{i=0}^{n+1}A_i,
\]
each of which is injective, as its graded for the filtration by the \(\oplus_{i\ge p}A_i\) is the identity inclusion.

Under the assumptions of Corollary 10 if a complex \(K\) is the colimit of an inductive sequence \(K_{(i)}\), and if the sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\oplus K_{(i)}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\oplus K_{(i)}\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow0 \tag{10.1}
\]
is exact, then for any \(L\), the long exact sequence of cohomology reads
\[
\longrightarrow \Hom(K,L)\longrightarrow\prod\Hom(K_{(i)},L)
 \overset{d}{\longrightarrow}\prod\Hom(K_{(i)},L)\longrightarrow
\]
The kernel of \(d\) is simply the projective limit of the \(\Hom(K_{(i)},L)\). The cokernel is \(\operatorname{lim}^1\). One concludes.

\begin{proposition*}[11]
Suppose that in \(A\) countable direct sums exist and are exact. If the complex \(K\) is the colimit of the \(K_{(i)}\), and if the sequence (10.1) is exact, for instance if either:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item in \(A\) inductive limits of sequences are exact;
\item in each degree \(n\), each \(K^n_{(i)}\to K^n_{(i+1)}\) is the inclusion of a direct factor;
\end{enumerate}
then one has a short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{lim}^1\Hom(K_{(i)},L[-1])
\longrightarrow\Hom(K,L)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{lim}\Hom(K_{(i)},L)\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
It remains to check that condition (2) implies the exactness of (10.1). This is to be seen degree by degree. By assumption, the \(A_i:=K^n_{(i)}\) have decompositions compatible with the transition maps \(A_i=\oplus_{j=0}^i B_i\). A corresponding decomposition of (10.1) in direct sum follows, and we are reduced to check exactness of the particular case \((10.1)_{(A,n)}\) of (10.1) when \(B_i=0\) for \(i\ne n\), i.e. when \(A_i\) is a fixed \(A\) from \(i\ge n\), and is \(0\) otherwise. Let \((10.1)_0\) be the sequence \((10.1)_{\Z,n}\) in \(Ab\) for \(A=\Z\). It is a split exact sequence of free abelian groups. Because direct sums exist, \(L\otimes A\), for \(L\) a free abelian group, is defined and functorial in \(L\). It is a sum of copies of \(A\), indexed by a basis of \(L\), and is characterized by
\[
\Hom(L\otimes A,B)=\Hom(L,\Hom(A,B))
\]
(functorial in \(B\)). The sequence \((10.1)_{A,n}\) is \((10.1)_0\otimes A\), and, \((10.1)_0\) being split exact, it splits and in particular is exact.
\end{proofdel}

The truncation \(\sigma_{\le n}K=\sigma_{\ge -n}K\) of a complex \(K\) is the subcomplex which coincides with \(K\) in homological degree \(\le n\) and is \(0\) in homological degree \(>n\). For any complex \(K\), one has
\[
K=\operatorname*{colim}_{\sigma_{\le n}}K
\]
and this colimit satisfies condition (2) of Proposition 11. It follows that

\begin{corollary*}[12]
Under the assumptions of Corollary 10, for any \(K\) and \(L\), one has a short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow\operatorname{lim}^1\Hom(\sigma_{\le n}K,L[-1])
\longrightarrow\Hom(K,L)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{lim}\Hom(\sigma_{\le n}K,L)\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{corollary*}

\section*{3 \(\Aone\)-Equivalences of Simplicial Sheaves on \(G\)-Schemes}

\section*{3.1 Sheaves on a Site of \(G\)-Schemes}

We fix a base scheme \(S\), supposed to be separated noetherian and of finite dimension; fiber product \(X\times_S Y\) will be written simply as \(X\times Y\). We also fix a group scheme \(G\) over \(S\), supposed to be finite and flat. We note \(h_X\) the representable sheaf defined by \(X\).

Let \(QP/G\) be the category of schemes quasi-projective over \(S\), given with an action of \(G\). Any \(X\) in \(QP/G\) admits an open covering \((U_i)\) by affine open subschemes which are \(G\)-stable. This makes it possible to define a reasonable quotient \(X/G\) in the category of schemes over \(S\) (rather than in the larger category of algebraic spaces). For each \(U_i\), \(U_i/G\) is defined as the spectrum of the equalizer
\[
\mathcal O(U_i)\rightrightarrows\mathcal O(U_i\times G),
\]
and \(X/G\) is obtained by gluing the \(U_i/G\). It is a categorical quotient, i.e. the coequalizer of \(G\times X\rightrightarrows X\). The map \(X\to X/G\) is finite, open, and the topological space \(|X/G|\) is the coequalizer of the map of topological spaces \(|G\times X|\rightrightarrows |X|\). One can show that \(X/G\) is again quasi-projective. Remark 2 below shows that this fact, while convenient for the exposition, is irrelevant.

One defines on \(QP/G\) a pretopology [3, II.1.3] by taking as coverings the family of etale maps \(Y_i\to X\) with the following property: \(X\) admits a filtration by closed equivariant subschemes \(\emptyset=X_n\subset\cdots\subset X_1\subset X_0=X\) such that for each \(j\), some map \(Y_i\to X\) has a section over \(X_j-X_{j+1}\). The Nisnevich topology on \(QP/G\) is the topology generated by this pretopology. The category \(QP/G\) with the Nisnevich topology is the \emph{Nisnevich site} \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\).

\begin{remark*}[1]
The corresponding topos is not the classifying topos of [2, IV.2.5]. A morphism \(X\to Y\) can become a Nisnevich covering after forgetting the action of \(G\), and not be a Nisnevich covering. Example: \(S=\Spec(k)\), \(G=\Z/2\), \(X=S\), \(Y=S\coprod S\), and \(G\) permutes two copies of \(S\) in \(Y\).
\end{remark*}

\begin{remark*}[2]
Let \((\mathrm{affine}/G)_{\Nis}\) be the site defined as above, with ``quasi-projective'' replaced by ``affine''. It is equivalent to \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\), in the sense that restriction to \((\mathrm{affine}/G)_{\Nis}\) is an equivalence from the category of sheaves on \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\) to the category of sheaves on \((\mathrm{affine}/G)_{\Nis}\).
\end{remark*}

\begin{remark*}[3]
If \(G\) is the trivial group \(e\), the definition given above recovers the usual Nisnevich topology. For \(G=e\), the condition usually considered: ``every point \(x\) of \(X\) is the image of a point with the same residue field of some \(Y_i\)'', is indeed equivalent to the condition imposed above. This is checked by noetherian induction: if a generic point \(\xi\) of \(X\) can be lifted to \(Y_i\), some open neighborhood \(U\subset X\) of \(\xi\) can be lifted to \(Y_i\), and one applies the induction hypothesis to \(X_1=(X-U)_{\red}\).
\end{remark*}

We write \((QP)_{\Nis}\) for the category of quasi-projective schemes over \(S\), with the Nisnevich topology.

\begin{lemma*}[2]
If \(U:Y_i\to X\), \(i\in I\), is a covering of \(X\) in \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\), there is a covering \(\mathcal V\) of \(X/G\) in \((QP)_{\Nis}\) whose pull-back to \(X\) is finer than \(U\).
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proofdel}
Fix a filtration \(\emptyset=X_n\subset\cdots\subset X_0=X\) as in the definition of the Nisnevich topology. We write \(q\) for the quotient map \(X\to X/G\). For \(x\) in \(X/G\), \((q^{-1}(x))_{\red}\) is in some \(X_j-X_{j+1}\), by equivariance of the \(X_j\), and one of the maps \(Y_i\to X\) has an equivariant section \(s\) over \(X_j-X_{j+1}\). Let \((X/G)^h_x\) be the henselization of \(X/G\) at \(x\). The map \(q\) being finite, the pull-back of \((X/G)^h_x\) to \(X\) is the coproduct of the \(X^h_y\) for \(q(y)=x\). The map from \(Y_i\) to \(X\) being etale, the section \(s\), restricted to \((q^{-1}(y))_{\red}\), extends uniquely to a section (automatically equivariant) of \(Y_i\) over \(\coprod_{q(y)=x}X^h_y\). Writing \((X/G)^h_x\) as the limit of etale neighborhoods of \(x\), one finds that \(x\) has an etale neighborhood \(V(x)\) such that \(Y_i\) has an equivariant section over \(X\times_{X/G}V(x)\). The \(V(x)\) form the required covering \(\mathcal V\).
\end{proofdel}

We define the \(G\)-local henselian schemes to be the schemes \(Y\) obtained in the following way. For \(X\) in \((QP/G)\), \(y\) a point of \(X/G\), and \((X/G)^h_y\) the henselization of \(X/G\) at \(y\), take the fiber product
\[
Y:=X\times_{X/G}(X/G)^h_y.
\]
As \(X\) is finite over \(X/G\), this fiber product is a finite disjoint union of local henselian schemes, and \(G\)-local henselian schemes are simply the \(G\)-equivariant finite disjoint unions of \(Y\) of local henselian schemes, for which \(Y/G\) is local.

\begin{proposition*}[13]
If \(Y\) is \(G\)-local henselian, the functor \(X\mapsto\Hom(Y,X)\) is a point of the site \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\), i.e. it defines a morphism of the punctual site \((\mathrm{Sets})\) to \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\). If \(Y=X\times_{X/G}(X/G)^h_y\), the corresponding fiber functor is
\[
F\longmapsto \operatorname*{colim}_{Y\subset X\times_{X/G}V}F(X\times_{X/G}V),
\]
the colimit being taken over the etale neighborhoods of \(y\) in \(X/G\). The collection of fiber functors so obtained is conservative.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
The functor \(X\mapsto\Hom(Y,X)\) commutes with finite limits. It follows from Lemma 2 that it transforms coverings into surjective families of maps, hence is a morphism of sites \((\mathrm{Sets})\to(QP/G)_{\Nis}\).

To check that the resulting set of fiber functors is conservative, it suffices to check that a family of etale \(f_i:U_i\to X\) is a covering if for any \(G\)-local henselian \(Y\),
\[
\coprod_i \Hom(Y,U_i)\longrightarrow \Hom(Y,X)
\]
is onto. The proof, parallel to that of Lemma 2, is left to the reader.
\end{proofdel}

\section*{3.2 The Brown--Gersten Closed Model Structure on Simplicial Sheaves on \(G\)-Schemes}

We recall that a commutative square of simplicial sets (or pointed simplicial sets)
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=2em]
K \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & L \arrow[d]\\
M \arrow[r] & N
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{13.1}
\]
is homotopy cartesian (or a homotopy pull-back square) if, when \(L\) is replaced by \(L'\) weakly equivalent to it and mapping to \(N\) by a (Kan) fibration \(L\overset{\sim}{\to}L'\to N\), the map from \(K\) to \(L'\times_NM\) is a weak equivalence.

\begin{definition*}[3]
A simplicial presheaf \(F_\bullet\) on \((QP/G)_{\Nis}\) is flasque if \(F(\emptyset)\) is contractible and if for any (upper) distinguished square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=2em]
B \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & Y \arrow[d,"p"]\\
A \arrow[r,"j"'] & X
\end{tikzcd}
\]
(\(p\) etale, \(j\) open embedding, \(B=p^{-1}(A)\), and \(Y-B\simeq X-A\)), the square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.8em,row sep=2em]
F(X) \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & F(Y) \arrow[d,"p"]\\
F(A) \arrow[r,"j"'] & F(B)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is homotopy cartesian.
\end{definition*}

\begin{theorem*}[3]
Let \(f:F_\bullet\to F'_\bullet\) be a morphism of flasque simplicial presheaves. If the induced morphism of simplicial sheaves \(aF_\bullet\to aF'_\bullet\) is a local equivalence, then, for any \(U\) in \(QP/G\), \(F_\bullet(U)\to F'_\bullet(U)\) is a weak equivalence.
\end{theorem*}
