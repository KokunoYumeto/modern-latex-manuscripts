\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries The category of representations of the symmetric group \(S_t\),\\
when \(t\) is not a natural integer}\\[0.5em]
{\large P. Deligne}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
Proceedings of the International Colloquium on Algebraic Groups and Homogeneous Spaces, January 2004, TIFR, Mumbai.
\end{center}

\textbf{Abstract.}
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic \(0\). For \(t\in k\) which is not an integer \(\geq0\), we construct a semi-simple tensor category \(\Rep(S_t)\) which ``interpolates'' the categories of linear representations (over \(k\)) of the symmetric groups \(S_n\), for \(n\) large. When \(t\) is a natural integer, \(\Rep(S_t)\) can be ``specialized'' to a tensor category in at least two ways: as the usual category of representations of \(S_t\), or as a non-semisimple tensor category.

The theory reproduces, in a simpler form, known analogous results for the linear or orthogonal groups, which will be recalled.

\section*{Introduction}

If \(G\) is an affine group scheme over a field \(k\), the category \(\Rep(G)\) of finite-dimensional linear representations of \(G\) over \(k\) is an abelian category. It is endowed with the tensor-product functor for representations
\[
  \otimes:\Rep(G)\times \Rep(G)\longrightarrow \Rep(G).
\]
This functor is \(\ACU\), i.e. it is endowed with associativity and commutativity constraints, and it has a unit object \(\mathbf 1\). It is rigid: each object \(X\) has a dual \(X^\vee\), an object endowed with
\[
  \delta:\mathbf 1\longrightarrow X\otimes X^\vee,\qquad
  \ev:X^\vee\otimes X\longrightarrow \mathbf 1
\]
such that the composite morphisms
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
X \arrow[r,"\delta\otimes X"] &
X\otimes X^\vee\otimes X \arrow[r,"X\otimes \ev"] &
X
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
X^\vee \arrow[r,"X^\vee\otimes\delta"] &
X^\vee\otimes X\otimes X^\vee \arrow[r,"\ev\otimes X^\vee"] &
X^\vee
\end{tikzcd}
\]
are identities. Finally,
\[
  k\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\End(\mathbf 1).
\]
In other words, in the terminology of (Deligne 1990), 2.1, \(\Rep(G)\) is a tensor category over \(k\).

If \(k\) is algebraically closed, the tensor category \(\Rep(G)\) determines \(G\) up to isomorphism (Saavedra 1972). From this point of view, tensor categories generalize affine group schemes. There are examples of tensor categories other than the \(\Rep(G)\), but one has no idea how to classify them all, not even those which are semisimple and finitely \(\otimes\)-generated (and hence analogous to categories \(\Rep(G)\) for \(G\) an algebraic group which is an extension of a finite group by a reductive group).

Another example of a tensor category is the category of super-representations of an affine super group scheme \(G\). A more natural variant is to consider \(G\) endowed with \(\varepsilon:\muTwo\to G\) such that the inner action of \(\muTwo\) on the affine algebra \(\mathcal O(G)\) of \(G\) defines the modulo \(2\) grading of \(\mathcal O(G)\), and the category \(\Rep(G,\varepsilon)\) of super-representations \(V\) for which the action of \(\muTwo\) gives the modulo \(2\) grading of \(V\).

In characteristic \(p\), S. Gelfand and D. Kazhdan (1992) constructed new semisimple tensor categories as ``subquotients'' of representation categories. By contrast, over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic \(0\), the only tensor categories that I know are obtained by ``interpolation'' of categories \(\Rep(G,\varepsilon)\).

Here is a method of interpolation. Start from an infinite family of tensor categories \(C_i\) over \(k\), from a non-trivial ultrafilter \(F\) on the index set \(I\), and take the ultraproduct \(C\) of the \(C_i\). An object of \(C\) is therefore a family \((X_i)\) of objects of the \(C_i\), a morphism \((X_i)\to(Y_i)\) is a germ, with respect to \(F\), of families of morphisms \(X_i\to Y_i\), and the tensor product is given by
\[
  (X_i)\otimes(Y_i)=(X_i\otimes Y_i).
\]
The category \(C\) is tensorial over the field \(k_F\), the ultrapower of \(k\). It is not very friendly, since, even if the \(C_i\) are categories of representations of linear algebraic groups, the \(\Hom\)-groups in \(C\) will be infinite-dimensional. To do better, one can start from tensor categories \(C_i\) over \(k\), each endowed with an object \(X_i\), and consider the full tensor subcategory \(C'\) of \(C\) generated by \(X=(X_i)\). The objects of \(C'\) are then the subquotients of sums of
\[
  T^{r,s}(X):=X^{\otimes r}\otimes (X^\vee)^{\otimes s}.
\]
Assume that
\begin{quote}
\((*)\) for all \(r\) and \(s\), the object \(T^{r,s}(X_i)\) of \(C_i\) has finite length, bounded independently of \(i\).
\end{quote}
Under this hypothesis the objects of \(C'\) have finite length, and the \(\Hom\)-groups are therefore finite-dimensional over \(k_F\) (consider \(\Hom(X,Y)\) and apply 3.2.4).

The definition by ultraproduct is somewhat theological. In good cases, one can associate with each \(i\) a parameter \(\lambda_i\) in \(k\) describing \((C_i,X_i)\), and \(C'\) can be constructed purely in terms of \(\lambda=(\lambda_i)\in k_F\). More precisely, for \(k_1\) an extension of \(k\) and \(\lambda_1\in k_1\) transcendental over \(k\), one can construct a tensor category \(C(\lambda_i)\) over \(k_1\), which gives \(C'\) when one takes \(k_1=k_F\) and \(\lambda_1=(\lambda_i)\). The role of ultraproducts is then only to have drawn attention to condition \((*)\).

The case where \(\lambda_1\) is transcendental is the easy case. More interesting, and more difficult, is to take \(\lambda_1\) arbitrary in an extension \(k_1\) of \(k\), or at least to understand which values of \(\lambda_1\) are singular, and how.

From now on \(k\) is assumed to have characteristic \(0\). The case treated in this article is:
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
I: & \text{the set of integers }\geq0;\\
C_n: & \text{the category of representations of }S_n;\\
X_n: & \text{the permutation representation on }k^n;\\
\text{parameter:} & n;\\
\text{singular values:} & \text{the integers }\geq0.
\end{array}
\]
The following cases are essentially known and will be recalled:
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
I=&\text{the set of integers }\geq0;\\
C_n=&\Rep(\GL(n))\ \text{(resp. }\Rep(O(n))\text{)};\\
X_n=&\text{the evident representation};\\
\text{parameter:}&n;\\
\text{singular values:}&\Z.
\end{array}
\]

When \(C_i\) is the category of representations of a finite group \(G_i\), and \(X_i\) is the permutation representation defined by an action of \(G_i\) on a finite set \(B_i\), condition \((*)\) is equivalent to
\[
  (*')\quad \text{for every \(r\), the number of orbits of \(G_i\) in \(B_i^r\) is bounded independently of \(i\).}
\]
Here are examples in which condition \((*')\) is satisfied. In each of them one fixes a finite field \(\mathbb F_q\), and \(I\) is the set of integers \(\geq0\):
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
(A)&G_n=\GL(n,\mathbb F_q),\quad B_n=\mathbb F_q^n;\\
(B)&G_n=O(n,\mathbb F_q),\quad B_n=\mathbb F_q^n;\\
(C)&G_n=Sp(2n,\mathbb F_q),\quad B_n=\mathbb F_q^{2n}.
\end{array}
\]
In case (A), one can take the parameter \(\lambda_n:=q^n\), and I conjecture that the singular values of the parameter are the \(q^n\), for \(n\geq0\).

I thank the referee for a careful reading, which led me to correct several obscure points.

\section*{1. Terminology and notation}

\para{1.1.}
We shall use the following notation.

\(k\): a field of characteristic \(0\), fixed throughout. For \(I\) a finite set:
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
S_I:&\text{the symmetric group of permutations of }I;\\
k^I:&\text{the \(k\)-vector space freely generated by }I;\\
(e_i)_{i\in I}:&\text{its evident basis.}
\end{array}
\]
If \(I=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), we write \(S_n\) (resp. \(k^n\)) instead of \(S_I\) (resp. \(k^I\)).

\para{1.2.}
Let \((\, ,\, )\) be the symmetric bilinear form on \(k^I\) defined by \((e_i,e_j)=\delta_{ij}\). It identifies \(k^I\) with its dual. Viewing \(k^I\) as the space of functions from \(I\) to \(k\), via
\[
  u\longmapsto \sum u(i)e_i,
\]
one defines an algebra structure on \(k^I\) by
\[
  (u\cdot v)(i)=u(i)v(i).
\]
For this algebra structure,
\[
  (u,v)=\Tr(uv).
\]

\para{1.3.}
The functor \(I\mapsto k^I\), being a left adjoint, preserves inductive limits, and in particular carries disjoint sums to direct sums. It carries finite products to tensor products, by
\[
  \bigotimes_{j\in J} k^{I_j}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
  k^{\prod I_j},\qquad
  \bigotimes e_{a(j)}\longmapsto e_{(a(j))_{j\in J}}.
\]

\para{1.4.}
If a group \(G\) acts on \(I\), it acts on \(k^I\) by transport of structure:
\[
  g e_i=e_{g i}.
\]
The linear representation \(k^I\) of \(G\) is called the permutation representation defined by the \(G\)-set \(I\). The form \((\, ,\, )\) and the algebra structure of 1.2 are \(G\)-invariant. The functor \(I\mapsto k^I\) still carries disjoint sums (resp. finite products) of \(G\)-sets to direct sums (resp. tensor products) of representations.

\para{1.5.}
Let \((U_j)_{j\in J}\) be a finite family of finite sets. A gluing of the \(U_j\) is a finite set \(A\) endowed with a family of injections
\[
  f_j:U_j\hookrightarrow A
\]
such that \(A\) is the union of the \(f_j(U_j)\).

The \(U_j\) being fixed, a gluing datum on the \(U_j\) is an isomorphism class of gluings. If \(r\) is a gluing datum, and \((f_j:U_j\hookrightarrow A)\) is a representative of \(r\), then \(A\) is determined, up to unique isomorphism, by \(r\). This makes harmless the abuse of language, which we shall allow ourselves, of saying ``gluing'' for ``gluing datum''. For example, saying ``the sum over gluings \((f_j:U_j\hookrightarrow A)\) of the \(U_j\) of \(\varphi(A)\)'' means ``the sum over gluing data \(r\) on the \(U_j\) of \(\varphi(A_r)\), for \((f_j:U_j\hookrightarrow A_r)\) a representative of \(r\)''.

A gluing datum on the \(U_j\) identifies with an equivalence relation \(R\) on the disjoint sum of the \(U_j\) which induces the discrete equivalence \(x=y\) on each \(U_j\): to \(R\), attach the maps
\[
  U_j\hookrightarrow \bigl(\coprod U_j\bigr)/R.
\]
For \(J=\{1,2\}\), it also identifies with the data of subsets \(U'_j\subset U_j\) and of a bijection
\[
  \varphi:U'_1\longrightarrow U'_2:
\]
attach to it the maps \(U_j\hookrightarrow U_1\coprod_\varphi U_2\).

For \(K\subset J\) and \((f_j:U_j\hookrightarrow A)\) a gluing of the \(U_j\), the induced gluing of the \(U_j\), \(j\in K\), is the family
\[
  f_j:U_j\hookrightarrow \bigcup_{j\in K} f_j(U_j).
\]

\para{1.6.}
Let \(A\) be a commutative ring. A category \(A\) is said to be \(A\)-linear if the \(\Hom\)-groups are endowed with \(A\)-module structures for which composition is bilinear. For \(B\) a commutative \(A\)-algebra, \(A\otimes_A B\) is then the category deduced from \(A\) by extension of scalars from \(A\) to \(B\): the objects are the same, and
\[
  \Hom(X,Y)=\Hom_A(X,Y)\otimes_A B.
\]

\para{1.7.}
Let \(\mathcal A\) be \(A\)-linear. The additive envelope \(\mathcal A^{\add}\) of \(\mathcal A\) is the additive \(A\)-linear category of finite formal sums of objects of \(\mathcal A\):
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
\text{objects:}&\text{finite families }(A_j)_{j\in J}\text{ of objects of }\mathcal A;\\
\text{morphisms }(A_j)_{j\in J}\to(B_k)_{k\in K}:&
\text{a matrix }(f^j_k)\text{ of morphisms }A_j\to B_k;\\
\text{composition:}&\text{matrix multiplication.}
\end{array}
\]

\para{1.8.}
A pseudo-abelian category (SGA4 I 8.7.8 says ``additive and Karoubian'') is an additive category in which every idempotent endomorphism is the projection onto a direct factor. The pseudo-abelian envelope \(\mathcal A^{\psab}\) of an \(A\)-linear category \(\mathcal A\) is the pseudo-abelian \(A\)-linear category of formal direct factors (images of idempotent endomorphisms) of finite formal sums of objects of \(\mathcal A\). See SGA4 loc. cit.

\para{1.9.}
Let \(\Hom_A(\mathcal A_1,\mathcal A_2)\) denote the category of \(A\)-linear functors from \(\mathcal A_1\) to \(\mathcal A_2\). The constructions 1.7 and 1.8 have a universal property: if \(\mathcal B\) is \(A\)-linear and additive (resp. pseudo-abelian), the restriction functor
\[
  \Hom_A(\mathcal A^{\add},\mathcal B)\longrightarrow\Hom_A(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
\]
(resp.
\[
  \Hom_A(\mathcal A^{\psab},\mathcal B)\longrightarrow\Hom_A(\mathcal A,\mathcal B))
\]
is an equivalence. It is ``the same thing'' to give an \(A\)-linear functor from \(\mathcal A\) to \(\mathcal B\), or from \(\mathcal A^{\add}\) (resp. \(\mathcal A^{\psab}\)) to \(\mathcal B\).

An \(A\)-bilinear functor
\[
  \mathcal A\times\mathcal A\longrightarrow \mathcal A^{\add}
\]
therefore extends to an \(A\)-bilinear functor
\[
  \mathcal A^{\add}\times\mathcal A^{\add}\longrightarrow \mathcal A^{\add},
\]
and this extension is unique up to unique isomorphism. The same holds with ``add'' replaced by ``ps ab''.

If the \(A\)-linear category \(\mathcal A\) is endowed with an \(A\)-bilinear \(\ACU\) tensor product (associative, commutative, with unit), then \(\mathcal A^{\add}\) and \(\mathcal A^{\psab}\) inherit it. If the tensor product of \(\mathcal A\) is rigid, then that of \(\mathcal A^{\add}\) (resp. \(\mathcal A^{\psab}\)) is rigid as well.

\para{1.10.}
A partition \(\lambda\) is a decreasing sequence of integers \((\lambda_a)_{a\ge1}\), zero for \(a\) sufficiently large. Put
\[
  |\lambda|=\sum_a \lambda_a,
\]
and say that \(\lambda\) is a partition of \(\ell\) if \(|\lambda|=\ell\). A sequence \(\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_r\) is viewed as a partition by prolonging it by zeros. The transposed partition \(\lambda^*\) of a partition \(\lambda\) is characterized by the equivalence between \(\lambda_i\ge j\) and \(\lambda^*_j\ge i\). If \(\lambda\) is a partition of \(\ell\), we shall also write \(\lambda\) for the following objects attached to \(\lambda\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item The corresponding isomorphism class of irreducible representations of \(S_\ell\); its class in the Grothendieck group \(R(S_\ell)\) of representations of \(S_\ell\), and its character. The trivial representation of \(S_\ell\) corresponds to the coarse partition \(\ell\) of \(\ell\).
\item For \(N\) large enough that \(\lambda_{N+1}=0\): the dominant weight \((\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N)\) of \(\GL(N)\).
\end{enumerate}

For each \(\ell\) and each partition \(\lambda\) of \(\ell\), assume chosen an irreducible representation of class \(\lambda\) of \(S_\ell\). The category of sets \(U\) with \(\ell\) elements and bijections between them being equivalent to its full subcategory reduced to the object \(\{1,\ldots,\ell\}\), it is the same to give such a representation \(V_\lambda\), or a functor \(U\mapsto V_\lambda[U]\). In particular, the choice of \(V_\lambda\) defines, for every set \(U\) with \(\ell\) elements, a representation of \(S_U\), which we shall again denote by \(\lambda\). It is deduced from \(V_\lambda\) by twisting by the \(S_\ell\)-torsor of bijections from \(\{1,\ldots,\ell\}\) to \(U\).

In the preceding discussion, the base field is \(\Q\). One could even take the base ring to be \(\Z(1/\ell!)\). The case of other base fields or rings is obtained by extension of scalars. If \(X\) is a representation of \(S_U\), put
\[
  X_\lambda:=\Hom(\lambda,X).
  \tag{1.10.1}
\]
It is known that
\[
  \Q[S_U]\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
  \bigoplus_{|\lambda|=\ell}\End(\lambda),
  \tag{1.10.2}
\]
and it follows that the natural \(S_U\)-equivariant map
\[
  \bigoplus_{|\lambda|=\ell}X_\lambda\otimes\lambda
  \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} X
  \tag{1.10.3}
\]
is an isomorphism. This construction still makes sense for \(X\) in a pseudo-abelian \(\Z(1/\ell!)\)-linear category.

\section*{2. The category \(\Rep(S_T)\)}

\para{2.1.}
Fix a finite set \(I\). For \(U\) a finite set, write \([U]_I\) for the permutation representation of \(S_I\) on the set \(\Inj(U,I)\) of injections from \(U\) into \(I\). When \(U\) (resp. \(I\)) is the finite set \(\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\), \(U\) (resp. \(I\)) is replaced by \(n\) in the notation.

For an injection \(\varphi:U\hookrightarrow V\), composition with \(\varphi\),
\[
  \Inj(V,I)\longrightarrow \Inj(U,I),
\]
induces
\[
  \res_\varphi:[V]_I\longrightarrow [U]_I
\]
whose transpose (1.2) is
\[
  \res_\varphi^*:[U]_I\longrightarrow [V]_I.
\]
One has
\[
  \res_\varphi(e_g)=e_{g\varphi},\qquad
  \res_\varphi^*(e_f)=\sum_{g\varphi=f} e_g.
\]

\para{2.2.}
Let \(C\) be a gluing of the finite sets \(U\) and \(V\), and let \(D\) be the fibre product of \(U\) and \(V\) over \(C\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
D \arrow[r,"a'"] \arrow[d,"b'"'] & U \arrow[d,"a",hook]\\
V \arrow[r,"b"',hook] & C .
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{2.2.1}
\]
If \(U':=a'(D)\), \(V':=b'(D)\), and if \(\varphi\) is the bijection \(b'a'^{-1}:U'\overset{\sim}{\to}V'\), then
\[
  U\coprod_\varphi V\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}C.
\]
Put
\[
  \{C\}_I:=\res_{b'}^*\,\res_{a'}
  \qquad
  \text{(resp. }(C)_I:=\res_b\,\res_a^*\text{)}:
  [U]_I\longrightarrow [V]_I.
\]
These morphisms will also be denoted \(\{\varphi\}_I\) and \((\varphi)_I\), and, when this creates no ambiguity, the index \(I\) will be omitted.

Define an ordering on gluing data of \(U\) and \(V\) by saying that \(A\) is coarser than \(C\) (\(A\le C\)) if, with \(D\) and \(\varphi\) as above, the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
D \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & U \arrow[d,hook]\\
V \arrow[r,hook] & A
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is commutative; equivalently, \(A\) is defined by a bijection
\[
  \psi:U''\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}V'',
\]
with \(U'\subset U''\subset U\) and \(V'\subset V''\subset V\), extending \(\varphi\).

For \(f\in \Inj(U,I)\), the image of \(e_f\) under \(\{C\}\) (resp. under \((C)\)) is the sum of the \(e_g\), for \(g\in\Inj(V,I)\), such that \(f\) and \(g\) glue to a map (resp. an injective map) from \(C\) to \(I\). This description shows that
\[
  \{C\}=\sum_{A\le C}(A).
  \tag{2.2.2}
\]
If \(A\le C\) is defined by \(\psi:U''\overset{\sim}{\to}V''\), the ordered set of gluings \(A_1\) between \(A\) and \(C\) is isomorphic to the set, ordered by \(\supset\), of subsets of \(U''-U'\): to \(A_1\), defined by \(\psi_1:U''_1\to V''_1\), where \(U'\subset U''_1\subset U''\) and \(\psi_1\) is the restriction of \(\psi\), attach \(U''_1-U'\). The Möbius function for the ordered set of gluing data is therefore
\[
  \mu(A,C)=(-1)^{|U''|-|U'|}=(-1)^{|C|-|A|},
\]
and (2.2.2) is inverted as
\[
  (C)=\sum_{A\le C}(-1)^{|C|-|A|}\{A\}.
  \tag{2.2.3}
\]

\para{2.3.}
Special cases:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If in (2.2.1) \(a'\) (resp. \(b'\)) is bijective, so that the gluing \(C\) is defined by \(\varphi:U\hookrightarrow V\) (resp. \(\varphi:V\hookrightarrow U\)), then
\[
  (C)=\{C\}=\res_\varphi^*
  \quad
  \text{(resp. }(C)=\{C\}=\res_\varphi\text{)}.
\]
\item If the gluing is defined by a bijection \(\varphi\) from \(U\) to \(V\), then \((\varphi)\) is the functoriality of \(U\mapsto[U]_I\) for bijections, as defined by transport of structure.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{construction*}[2.4]
Let \((U_j)_{j\in J}\) be a finite family of finite sets. The tensor product of the \([U_j]_I\) is naturally isomorphic to the direct sum, over gluings \((u_j:U_j\hookrightarrow C)\), of the \([C]_I\).
\end{construction*}

\begin{proofdel}
The tensor product of the \([U]_I\) is the permutation representation defined by the \(S_I\)-set product of the \(\Inj(U_j,I)\). Each system of injections \(u_j:U_j\hookrightarrow I\) defines the gluing
\[
  u_j:U_j\hookrightarrow \bigcup u_j(U_j).
\]
The corresponding gluing datum depends only on the \(S_I\)-orbit of \((u_j)_{j\in J}\), and this makes \(\prod \Inj(U_j,I)\) the disjoint sum of the \(\Inj(C,I)\), for \((a_j:U_j\hookrightarrow C)\) a gluing datum. The conclusion follows from 1.3.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{examples*}[2.5]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The representation \([\varnothing]_I\) is the unit representation: \(k\) with the trivial action of \(S_I\). The corresponding morphism
\[
  [U]_I\otimes[\varnothing]_I\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}[U]_I
\]
is defined by the evident gluing of \(U\) and \(\varnothing\) into \(U\).
\item The algebra structure
\[
  [U]_I\otimes[U]_I\longrightarrow[U]_I
\]
of \([U]_I\) defined in 1.2 is the projection onto the direct summand corresponding to the evident gluing of \(U\) and \(U\) into \(U\).
\item The self-duality 1.2 of \([U]_I\) is
\[
  [U]_I\otimes[U]_I \xto{(ii)} [U]_I \xto{\res_{\varnothing\to U}} [\varnothing],
\]
and the corresponding morphism \(\delta:\mathbf 1\to[U]_I\otimes[U]_I\) is
\[
  [\varnothing]\xto{\res_{\varnothing\to U}^*}[U]_I
  \longrightarrow [U]_I\otimes[U]_I,
\]
where the second arrow is the inclusion of the direct summand \([U]_I\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{examples*}

\para{2.6.}
Let \(((U_{j,k})_{j\in J_k})_{k\in K}\) be a family of families. It defines the family of the \(U_{j,k}\), indexed by \(\coprod_k J_k\). There is a natural bijection between:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item gluing data \(C\) on the \(U_{j,k}\);
\item systems formed by:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item for each \(k\), a gluing datum \(C_k\) on the \(U_{j,k}\), \(j\in J_k\);
\item a gluing datum \(D\) on the \(C_k\), \(k\in K\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
To \(C\), one attaches the induced gluings, and their gluing into \(C\).

We leave to the reader the verification that the isomorphism
\[
  \bigotimes_{k\in K}\bigotimes_{j\in J_k}[U_{j,k}]_I
  =
  \bigotimes [U_{j,k}]_I
\]
becomes, via the isomorphisms 2.4, the isomorphism of
\[
  \bigoplus_{(C_k)}\bigotimes_{k\in K}[C_k]_I
  =
  \bigoplus_{(D,C_k)}[D]_I
\]
with the sum of the \([C]_I\), defined by the bijections \((A)\leftrightarrow(B)\).

\para{2.7.}
This describes the associativity and commutativity constraints and the unit of the tensor product of representations \([U]_I\) in terms of the description 2.4 of the tensor product. For associativity
\[
  ([U]_I\otimes[V]_I)\otimes[W]_I
  \longrightarrow
  [U]_I\otimes([V]_I\otimes[W]_I),
\]
one identifies both sides with the sum of the \([C]_I\), for \(C\) a gluing of \(U,V,W\). For commutativity
\[
  [U]_I\otimes[V]_I\longrightarrow [V]_I\otimes[U]_I,
\]
use the fact that a gluing of \(U\) and \(V\) identifies with a gluing of \(V\) and \(U\). The unit is \([\varnothing]_I\).

\begin{proposition*}[2.8]
Let \((U_j)_{j\in J}\) and \((V_j)_{j\in J}\) be two families indexed by \(J\), and, for each \(j\), let \(U_j,V_j\hookrightarrow C_j\) be a gluing of \(U_j\) and \(V_j\). Using 2.3, identify the tensor product of the \([U_j]\) (resp. \([V_j]_I\)) with the sum of the \([A]_I\) (resp. \([B]_I\)), for \(A\) (resp. \(B\)) a gluing of the \(U_j\) (resp. \(V_j\)). Then the matrix coefficient
\[
  [A]_I\longrightarrow \bigotimes[U_j]_I
  \longrightarrow \bigotimes[V_j]_I
  \longrightarrow [B]_I
\]
of
\[
  \bigotimes(C_j):\bigotimes[U_j]_I\longrightarrow \bigotimes[V_j]_I
\]
is the sum of the
\[
  (C):[A]_I\longrightarrow[B]_I,
\]
where \(C\) ranges over gluing data on \(A\) and \(B\) which, viewed by 2.6 as gluing data on the \(U_j\) and \(V_j\), induce for each \(j\) the gluing datum \(C_j\) of \(U_j\) and \(V_j\).
\end{proposition*}

The verification, immediate from the description 2.2 of the \((C_j)\), is left to the reader.

\begin{proposition*}[2.9]
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The morphisms \((C):[U]_I\to[V]_I\), where \(U,V\hookrightarrow C\) is a gluing of \(U\) and \(V\) such that \(|C|\le |I|\), form a basis of \(\Hom_{S_I}([U]_I,[V]_I)\).
\item The condition \(|C|\le |I|\) is satisfied by every gluing \(U,V\hookrightarrow C\) if \(|U|+|V|\le |I|\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
By self-duality of the permutation representation \([U]_I\), the group \(\Hom_{S_I}([U]_I,[V]_I)\) identifies with the space of \(S_I\)-invariants in \([U]_I\otimes[V]_I\), which is the permutation representation defined by \(\Inj(U,I)\times\Inj(V,I)\). The \(S_I\)-invariants have a basis indexed by the \(S_I\)-orbits: to an orbit \(K\) corresponds the sum of the \(e_k\), for \(k\in K\). Injections \(u:U\hookrightarrow I\) and \(v:V\hookrightarrow I\) define the gluing datum
\[
  U,V\hookrightarrow u(U)\cup v(V).
\]
Denote it \(C_{u,v}\). It depends only on the orbit of \((u,v)\), and detects it. We leave to the reader the verification that the morphism from \([U]_I\) to \([V]_I\) defined by the orbit of \((u,v)\) is \((C_{u,v})\). The gluing data \(C\) so obtained are those for which \(|C|\le |I|\). For the others, \([C]=0\). This proves (i), and (ii) is trivial.
\end{proofdel}

\begin{proposition*}[2.10]
Let \(U,V,W\) be three finite sets. Let \(U,V\hookrightarrow C\) be a gluing of \(U\) and \(V\), and let \(V,W\hookrightarrow D\) be a gluing of \(V\) and \(W\). Then the composite morphism
\[
  (D)(C):[U]_I\longrightarrow[W]_I
\]
is the sum, over gluings \(a,b,c:U,V,W\hookrightarrow A\) which induce \(C\) and \(D\),
\[
  (D)(C)=\sum_A P_A(|I|)(A'),
  \tag{2.10.1}
\]
where \(A'\) is the gluing of \(U\) and \(W\) induced by \(A\), and where \(P_A(T)\) is the polynomial with integer coefficients
\[
  P_A(T):=(T-|A'|)(T-|A'|-1)\cdots(T-|A|+1).
  \tag{2.10.2}
\]
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
Write
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& V \arrow[dl,"v'"'] \arrow[dr,"v''"] &\\
C && D\\
U \arrow[u,"u"] && W \arrow[u,"w"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
for the gluings. By definition (2.2),
\[
  (D)(C)=\res_w\,\res_{v''}^*\,\res_{v'}\,\res_u^*.
\]
The composite \(\res_{v''}^*\res_{v'}\) is computed by (2.2.2): it is the sum, over gluings \(x,y:C,D\hookrightarrow A\) of \(C\) and \(D\) making
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& V \arrow[dl,"v'"'] \arrow[dr,"v''"] &\\
C \arrow[dr,"x"'] && D \arrow[dl,"y"]\\
U \arrow[u,"u"] & A & W \arrow[u,"w"']
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{2.10.3}
\]
commutative, of the \(\res_y\res_x^*\).

The map which sends \(x,y:C,D\hookrightarrow A\) to
\[
  xu,\quad xv'=yv'',\quad yw:U,V,W\hookrightarrow A
\]
is bijective from the gluing data under consideration to the gluing data of \(U,V,W\) inducing the given data on \(U,V\) and \(V,W\). For each diagram (2.10.3) one has
\[
  \res_w\,\res_y\,\res_x^*\,\res_u^*=\res_{yw}\,\res_{xu}^*,
\]
and the composite \((D)(C)\) is therefore the sum, over \(a,b,c:U,V,W\hookrightarrow A\) inducing \(C\) and \(D\), of the \(\res_c\res_a^*\). Let \(A':=a(U)\cup c(W)\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
U \arrow[r,hook,"a'"] \arrow[dr,"a"'] &
A' \arrow[d,hook,"j"] &
W \arrow[l,hook',"c'"'] \arrow[dl,"c"]\\
& A &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Then
\[
  \res_c\res_a^*=\res_{c'}\,\res_j\,\res_j^*\,\res_{a'}^*,
\]
and \(\res_j\res_j^*\) is multiplication by the number \(P_A(|I|)\) of ways to extend an injection of \(A'\) into \(I\) to an injection of \(A\) into \(I\). Hence
\[
  (D)(C)=\sum_A\res_c\res_a^*
  =
  \sum_A P_A(|I|)\res_{c'}\res_{a'}^*
  =
  \sum_A P_A(|I|)(A').
\]
\end{proofdel}

\para{Remark 2.11.}
An analogous calculation, with (2.2.2) replaced by (2.2.3), expresses the composition of morphisms \(\{C\}\). Assume that
\[
  C=U\coprod_E V,\qquad D=V\coprod_F W:
\]
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& E \arrow[dl,"u"'] \arrow[dr,"v'"] &&
F \arrow[dl,"v''"'] \arrow[dr,"w"] &\\
U && V && W .
\end{tikzcd}
\]
By definition,
\[
  \{D\}\{C\}=\res_w^*\,\res_{v''}\,\res_{v'}^*\,\res_u.
\]
Put \(V_1:=v'(E)\cup v''(F)\):
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
E \arrow[r,hook,"x"] \arrow[dr,"v'"'] &
V_1 \arrow[d,hook,"j"] &
F \arrow[l,hook',"y"'] \arrow[dl,"v''"]\\
& V &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Then
\[
  \res_{v''}\res_{v'}^*
  =
  \res_y\,\res_j\,\res_j^*\,\res_x^*
  =
  P_{C,D}(|I|)\res_y\res_x^*,
\]
for \(P_{C,D}\) the polynomial with integer coefficients
\[
  P_{C,D}(T):=(T-|V_1|)\cdots(T-|V|+1).
  \tag{2.11.1}
\]
The composite \(\res_y\res_x^*\) is
\[
  (V_1):[E]_I\longrightarrow[F]_I.
\]
By (2.2.3), it is an alternating sum of morphisms \(\{A\}\), for \(A\) a gluing of \(E\) and \(F\) coarser than \(V_1\). If \(A_1\) is the fibre product \(E\times_A F\), i.e. for \(A=E\coprod_{A_1}F\),
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& A_1 \arrow[dl] \arrow[dr] &\\
E \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[dr] &
V_1 \arrow[d] &
F \arrow[l,hook'] \arrow[dl]\\
& A &
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& V_1 \arrow[dl] \arrow[dr] &\\
U && V
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\(A_1\) defines a gluing \(A'\) of \(U\) and \(V\), and \(\{D\}\{C\}\) is the sum over \(A\)
\[
  \{D\}\{C\}
  =
  P_{C,D}(|I|)
  \sum (-1)^{|V_1|-|A|}\{A'\}.
  \tag{2.11.2}
\]
