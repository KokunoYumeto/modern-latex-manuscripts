\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Extended Euler congruence}\\[0.6em]
{\large Pierre Deligne}
\end{center}

\textbf{Abstract.} The matricial Euler congruence
\[
\Tr\bigl(A^{p^n}\bigr)\equiv \Tr\bigl(A^{p^{n-1}}\bigr)\pmod{p^n},
\]
previously announced in Arnold, \textit{Japanese J. Math.} \textbf{1}(1), 1--24, 2006, for \(A\in \MN(\Z)\), is given a proof based on extending it to the ring of Witt vectors of length \(n\).

\textbf{Keywords.} Euler congruence; Witt vector.\quad
\textbf{Mathematics Subject Classification (2000).} 11A07; 05B20; 13K05.

The matricial Euler congruence
\begin{equation}
\tag{1}
\Tr\bigl(A^{p^n}\bigr)\equiv \Tr\bigl(A^{p^{n-1}}\bigr)\pmod{p^n},
\end{equation}
which was announced in [1] for \(A\in \MN(\Z)\), can be extended as follows. We replace \(\Z\) with \(\Z/p^n\Z\), and consider \(\Z/p^n\Z\) as being \(\Wn(\F_p)\), the ring of Witt vectors of length \(n\). As geometers, we dislike considering the case of the field \(\F_p\) alone: the main part of the following proof depends on the discovery of an extension to the case of \(\Wn(R)\), where \(R\) is of characteristic \(p\).

\begin{proposition*}
Let \(R\) be an \(\F_p\)-algebra, commutative with unity. The endomorphism
\[
F:x\longmapsto x^p
\]
of \(R\) defines by functoriality an endomorphism \(F\) of \(\Wn(R)\). For \(A\) in \(\MN(\Wn(R))\), the equality
\begin{equation}
\tag{2}
\Tr\bigl(A^{p^n}\bigr)=F\bigl(\Tr\bigl(A^{p^{n-1}}\bigr)\bigr)
\end{equation}
holds in \(\Wn(R)\).
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proofdel}
It suffices to consider the universal case, where \(R\) is the ring of polynomials in \(N^2n\) variables \(X^i_j(k)\) over \(\F_p\), and \(A^i_j\) is the Witt vector whose components are \(X^i_j(k)\). We must prove a polynomial identity in these variables. It suffices to prove it in \(\Wn(R(1/D))\), where \(D\) is the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of \(\bigl(X^i_j(0)\bigr)\). If \(k\) is an algebraically closed field containing \(R[1/D]\), it suffices to prove (2) in \(\Wn(k)\): we may and shall assume that \(R=k\) is algebraically closed and that the image of \(A\) in \(\MN(k)\) is a matrix with distinct eigenvalues.

To simplify, we may also suppose that the image of \(A\) in \(\MN(k)\) is invertible. In this case, the matrix \(A\) is conjugate to a diagonal matrix. It remains to prove that, for \(a_i\in \Wn(k)^*\), \(1\le i\le n\), we have
\[
\sum_i a_i^{p^n}
=F\left(\sum_i a_i^{p^{n-1}}\right)
\left(=\sum_i F\left(a_i^{p^{n-1}}\right)\right).
\]
As \(F\) is an endomorphism, the task is thus reduced to the case \(N=1\).

It remains to show that, for \(a\in \Wn(k)\), the element \(a^{p^{n-1}}\) is a multiplicative representative, that is, a Witt vector of the form \((\lambda,0,0,\ldots,0)\). Indeed, \(F\) acts on a multiplicative representative by raising it to the \(p\)-th power.

Writing \(a=\alpha u\), where \(\alpha\) is a multiplicative representative and \(u\mapsto 1\) in \(k\), we must prove that \(u^{p^{n-1}}=1\).

The ring \(W(k)\) of Witt vectors is a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field \(k\) and maximal ideal \(p\), and \(\Wn(k)\) is its quotient by \(p^n\). We have
\[
u\equiv 1\pmod p,
\]
whence \(u^{p^a}\equiv1\pmod{p^{a+1}}\) follows inductively, as can be seen from the expansion of \((1+p^{a-1}x)^p\).

Finally, (1) follows from (2), because \(F\) is the identity of \(\F_p\).
\end{proofdel}
