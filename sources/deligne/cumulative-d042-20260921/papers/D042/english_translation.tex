\section*{Introduction}

Let $k$ be a commutative field and let $f$ be a formal power series in two indeterminates $x$ and $y$ over $k$:
\[
        f=\sum a_{n,m}x^ny^m.
\]
Let $I(f)$ be the formal power series in one indeterminate $t$
\[
        I(f)=\sum a_{n,n}t^n.
\]
We propose to prove the following result:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Theorem.} \emph{If $f$ represents an algebraic function of $x$ and $y$, and if $k$ has characteristic $p\ne0$, then $I(f)$ represents an algebraic function of $t$.}

\medskip
For $g$ a formal power series in $N$ indeterminates
\[
        g=\sum a_{\boldsymbol n}\boldsymbol x^{\boldsymbol n},
\]
one defines similarly
\[
        I_N(g)=\sum a_{n\cdot\mathbf 1}t^n,
\]
and one again has:

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Corollary.} \emph{If $g$ is algebraic over the field $k(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$, and if $k$ has characteristic $p>0$, then $I_N(g)$ is algebraic over $k(t)$.}

\medskip
Under the stronger hypothesis $g\in k(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$, this result is due to H. Furstenberg [1]. See also 3.10. Our first aim was to give a geometric explanation of Furstenberg's result.

Suppose first that $k=\CC$ and that the formal series $f$ is convergent. Then $I(f)$ is convergent, and admits the following integral representation
\begin{equation}
        I(f)(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{xy=t}f(x,y)\frac{dx\wedge dy}{dt}. \tag{1}
\end{equation}
\clearpage
% Authority physical 2; printed 130
Let us explain the meaning of the right-hand side:

\medskip
\noindent a) Let $\pi:\CC^2\to\CC$ be the map $(x,y)\mapsto xy$. The expression $(dx\wedge dy)/dt$ is a relative holomorphic differential form on $\CC^2-\{(0,0)\}$, relative to the projection $\pi$, i.e. a family of holomorphic differential forms on the fibres, depending holomorphically on $t$. At $\{(0,0)\}$, it is still a section of the relative dualizing sheaf $\omega$, defined in the duality formalism of A. Grothendieck by $R\pi^!\OO=\omega[1]$. In terms of the coordinate $y$ on $\pi^{-1}(t)$, one has
\[
        \frac{dx\wedge dy}{dt}=\frac{d(t/y)\wedge dy}{dt}
        =\left(\frac{dt}{y}\wedge dy\right)/dt=\frac{dy}{y}.
\]

\noindent b) Suppose $f$ converges in the polydisc $B$ of radii $R$. For $|t|$ sufficiently small ($|t|<R^2$), the intersection of $B$ with $\pi^{-1}(t)$ projects isomorphically in the $y$-plane onto the annulus $|t|/R<|y|<R$. Let $r$ be such that $|t|/R<r<R$. The integral $\oint_{xy=t}$ is taken over the cycle $|y|=r$, $|x|=|t|/r$, $xy=t$, which projects isomorphically onto the positively oriented circumference $|y|=r$; it is independent of the choice of $r$.

These explanations show that
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{xy=t} f(x,y)\frac{dx\wedge dy}{dt}
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint f(t/y,y)\frac{dy}{y}
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\sum a_{n,m}t^n y^{m-n}\frac{dy}{y}.
\]
To deduce (1), one exchanges summation and integration, and applies the residue formula: only the terms for which $m=n$ remain.

The integration cycle is the vanishing cycle, in the sense of Lefschetz, for $\pi$, near $(0,0)$. If one takes $r=|t|^{1/2}$, one indeed sees it disappear as $|t|\to0$. This explains the title of the article.

In the definition of $I(f)$, the indeterminates $x$ and $y$ play symmetric roles. In (1), an order between $x$ and $y$ determines two choices of signs, which cancel one another: in the relative differential form, and in the orientation of the vanishing cycle.

Formula (1) suggests that the operation $I$ has an intrinsic meaning in the following setting: start from a morphism of formal schemes $\pi:X\to S$ over $k$, with $X$ isomorphic to $\Spf k[[x,y]]$, $S$ isomorphic to $\Spf k[[t]]$, and $\pi$ having a non-degenerate quadratic singularity. Assume that the two branches of $\pi^{-1}(0)$ are defined over $k$, and choose an order between them. In other words, assume that there are formal coordinate systems $x,y$ on $X$ and $t$ on $S$ in which $\pi$ is $(x,y)\mapsto xy$; these coordinate systems will always be taken so that the first branch of $\pi^{-1}(0):xy=0$ is the branch $x=0$.

Let $\Omega^1_{X/k}$ be the Kähler differentials, defined while taking the topology into account: if $X=\Spf(A)$, with $A$ isomorphic to $k[[x,y]]$ and endowed with the $(x,y)$-adic topology, then $\Omega^1_{X/k}$ corresponds to the $A$-module $\varprojlim\Omega^1_{(A/I)/k}$, the projective limit over the open ideals. Similarly for $S$. In coordinates, $\Omega^1_{X/k}$ is the free $\OO_X$-module with basis $dx,dy$, and $\Omega^1_{S/k}$ is the free $\OO_S$-module with basis $dt$. For a free module $M$ of rank $n$, put $\det M=\bigwedge^nM$. For $L$ free of rank 1, denote by $L^{-1}$ its dual. Put $\omega_{X/k}=\det\Omega^1_{X/k}$, $\omega_{S/k}=\Omega^1_{S/k}$, and let $\omega_{X/S}$ be the relative dualizing sheaf
\[
        \omega_{X/S}=\pi^!\OO_S[1]=\omega_{X/k}\otimes\pi^*\omega_{S/k}^{-1}.
\]
\clearpage
% Authority physical 3; printed 131
In coordinates, it is the free $\OO_X$-module with basis $dx\wedge dy/dt$. We will interpret $I$ as a morphism
\[
        \pi_*\omega_{X/S}\longrightarrow\OO_S,
\]
i.e., for $S=\Spf(V)$, as a $V$-linear morphism
\[
        H^0(X,\omega_{X/S})\longrightarrow V.
\]

Section 1 gives this interpretation: there we prove, for arbitrary $k$, a formula which is an algebraic analogue of (1). The integration sign which appears in (1) is replaced by Grothendieck's local trace morphism. More precisely, let $t$ be a uniformizer of $S$ (for instance $t$ for $S=\Spf(k[[t]])$). We will write a subscript $n$ for reduction modulo $t^n$. We omit the subscript when there is no risk of confusion. For example, we will still write $\pi:X_n\to S_n$ for the morphism $\pi_n$ deduced from $\pi$ by reduction modulo $t^n$. We write $\omega$ for the inverse image $(\omega_{X/S})_n$ of $\omega_{X/S}$ on $X_n$. This is Grothendieck's relative dualizing sheaf $\omega_{X_n/S_n}$. Let $A_n$ be the complete local ring whose formal spectrum is $X_n$; in coordinates, $A_n=k[[x,y]]/((xy)^n)$. It will be convenient to work not with the formal scheme $X_n$, but with the ordinary scheme $X'_n:=\Spec A_n$. The difference is Byzantine: both $X_n$ and $X'_n$ are merely geometric ways of speaking about $A_n$. Let $O$ be the closed point of $X'_n$; in coordinates, $x=y=0$. Grothendieck's local trace morphism is a morphism
\[
        \Tr:H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\omega)\longrightarrow H^0(S_n,\OO).
\]
One has
\[
        H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\omega)=\varinjlim\Ext^1_{A_n}(A_n/I,\omega),
\]
the inductive limit being over the open ideals $I$ of $A_n$. It is to write this inductive limit as a cohomology group with support that we had to introduce $X'_n$.

The role of the integration cycle, the vanishing cycle, will be played by an element $v_n\in H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\OO)$. In coordinates, this element lies in the image of $\Ext^1_{A_n}(A_n/(x+y)^{2n-1},A_n)$. Note that the required exponent of $(x+y)$ grows with $n$.

In Section 2 we consider a scheme in curves $Y$ which is proper and flat over $S$, from which $X$ is obtained by completion at a point, and we relate the $v_n$ to an element of $\Lie\Pic_S Y$. In Section 3 we globalize $X$ and $S$, and prove the theorem and the corollary.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Notation.} We will write base changes as subscripts. For example, if $X$ and $T$ are $S$-schemes, $X_T:=X\times_ST$. Reduction modulo $t^n$ is denoted by a subscript $n$.

For our purposes, the sign conventions used are immaterial. For the intermediate results stated below to be true as written -- and not only up to sign -- one must nevertheless choose them. Our choice is made explicit in the appendix.

\section*{1. Algebraic analogue of (1)}

\subsection*{1.1.}
Keep the notation of the introduction, and let $x,y$ and $t$ be coordinates on $X$ and $S$ of the type already considered.
\clearpage
% Authority physical 4; printed 132
The inclusion of $X'_1$ into $X'_n$ is a homeomorphism, since $X'_1$ is defined in $X'_n$ by a nilpotent ideal. Thus the complement of the origin $\{O\}$ in $X'_n$ has two connected components, corresponding to the two branches $x=0$ and $y=0$ of $X'_1$. Let $u_n$ be the section of the structure sheaf of $X_n-\{O\}$ which has value 1 on the first branch and 0 on the second. In the language of rings, the homomorphism
\[
        A_n=k[[x,y]]/((xy)^n)\longrightarrow k[[x,y]]/(x^n)\times k[[x,y]]/(y^n)
\]
induces an isomorphism
\[
        A_n((x+y)^{-1})\xrightarrow{\sim}
        (k[[x]]/(x^n))((y))\times(k[[y]]/(y^n))((x)),
\]
and $u_n\in A_n((x+y)^{-1})$ has image $(1,0)$. Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
        u_n=\left[\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom{2n-i}{i}y^{2n-1-i}x^i\right]\big/(x+y)^{2n-1}. \tag{1.1.1}
\end{equation}
Define $v_n$ to be the image of $u_n$ by the natural boundary morphism
\[
        H^0(X'_n-\{O\},\OO)\longrightarrow H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\OO).
\]
The long exact sequence of the $\Ext^i(*,A_n)$, applied to the short exact sequence
\[
        0\longrightarrow ((x+y)^N)\longrightarrow A_n\longrightarrow A_n/((x+y)^N)\longrightarrow0
\]
gives a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.5em,row sep=2.1em]
0 \arrow[r] & A_n \arrow[r] \arrow[d,equal] &
(x+y)^{-N}A_n \arrow[r] \arrow[d] &
\Ext^1_{A_n}(A_n/((x+y)^N),A_n) \arrow[d] \\
0 \arrow[r] & H^0(X'_n,\OO) \arrow[r] &
H^0(X'_n-\{O\},\OO) \arrow[r] & H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\OO).
\end{tikzcd}
\]
For $N=2n-1$, this diagram and (1.1.1) show that $v_n$ lies in the image of $\Ext^1_{A_n}(A_n/((x+y)^{2n-1}),A_n)$.

\subsection*{1.2. Proposition.}
\emph{Let $f$ belong to $k[[x,y]]$. For every $n$, one has}
\begin{equation}
        \Tr(f\,dx\wedge dy/dt\cdot v_n)\equiv -I(f)\pmod {t^n}. \tag{1.2.1}
\end{equation}
In (1.2.1), $\Tr$ is Grothendieck's local trace morphism. It is applied to the element of $H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\omega)$ obtained as the product of $f\,dx\wedge dy/dt$ in $H^0(X'_n,\omega)$ and $v_n$ in $H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\OO)$. We prove (1.2.1) under the hypothesis that $k$ is infinite. The general case is reduced to this one by extension of scalars.

Let $I'(f)$ denote the left-hand side of (1.2.1). We have $I'(f)\in k[[t]]/(t^n)$. If $J$ is an open ideal of $A_n$, small enough that $v_n$ lies in the image of $\Ext^1(A_n/J,A_n)$ (for example $J=((x+y)^{2n-1})$), and if $f\in J$, then $fv=0$, and \emph{a fortiori} $I'(f)=0$: the $k[[t]]/(t^n)$-linear map
\[
        I':A_n\longrightarrow k[[t]]/(t^n),\qquad f\longmapsto I'(f)
\]
is continuous.
\clearpage
% Authority physical 5; printed 133
For $\lambda,\mu\in k$, let $\sigma(\lambda,\mu)$ be the automorphism $(x,y)\mapsto(\lambda x,\mu y)$, $t\mapsto\lambda\mu t$ of $(X,S,\pi)$. It preserves $dx\wedge dy/dt$ and $v_n$. By transport of structure, one therefore has
\begin{equation}
        I'(\sigma(\lambda,\mu)^*f)=\sigma(\lambda,\mu)^*f. \tag{1.2.2}
\end{equation}
Put $h_{n,m}(t)=I'(x^ny^m)$. Taking $\mu=\lambda^{-1}$ in (1.2.2), one finds that $h_{n,m}(t)=\lambda^{n-m}h_{n,m}(t)$; hence $h_{n,m}=0$ for $n\ne m$. By continuity and $k[[t]]$-linearity of $I'$, for $f=\sum a_{n,m}x^ny^m$ one has
\[
        I'(f)=I'\left(\sum a_{n,m}x^ny^m\right)
             =I'\left(\sum a_{n,n}x^ny^n\right)
             =I'(\pi^*I(f))=I(f)\cdot I'(1).
\]
Taking $\mu=1$ and $f=1$ in (1.2.2), one obtains $h_{0,0}(\lambda t)=h_{0,0}(t)$, so that $I'(1)=h_{0,0}(t)$ is a constant $c$, and $I'(f)=cI(f)$. This constant is independent of $n$. To prove that $c=-1$, we may, and will, suppose $n=1$.

The scheme $X'_1=\Spec k[[x,y]]/(xy)$ is the union of two nonsingular formal arcs crossing transversely, the first parametrized by $y$, the second by $x$. Sections of $\omega$ on $X'_1$ are systems of differential forms $\alpha$ and $\beta$ on these arcs, with a simple pole at $O$, and $\Res\alpha+\Res\beta=0$. Sections of $\omega$ on $X'_1-\{O\}$ are systems of forms $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with a pole of arbitrary order at $O$, and the composite
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.5em]
H^0(X'_1-\{O\},\omega) \arrow[r] & H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_1,\omega) \arrow[r,"tr"] & k
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is the negative of $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\Res\alpha+\Res\beta$. With this notation, $dx\wedge dy/dt$ corresponds to
\[
        \left(\frac{dy}{y},\,-\frac{dx}{x}\right),
\]
and $(dx\wedge dy/dt)u_1=\left(\frac{dy}{y},0\right)$, whose sum of residues is 1. This proves (1.2.1).

\section*{2. Relation with Pic}

\subsection*{2.1.}
Let $S=\Spec k[[t]]$, let $s$ be the closed point of $S$, let $\eta$ be the generic point, let $\pi:Y\to S$ be a proper flat morphism purely of relative dimension 1, and let $O$ be a rational point of $Y_s:=\pi^{-1}(s)$. Assume that, if $X$ is the completion of $Y$ at $O$, the morphism induced by $\pi$ from $X$ to $S$ is of the type considered in Section 1. This means: $Y$ is regular at $O$, $O$ is an isolated point of non-smoothness of $\pi$, $\pi$ has at $O$ a non-degenerate quadratic singularity, and the two branches of $Y_s$ at $O$ are defined over $k$. An order between these branches is assumed to have been chosen.

It will be convenient to assume in addition that $Y$ is regular and connected. Make the base change $\eta\to S$, and let $Y_\eta$ be the curve over $k((t))$ which is the generic fibre of $\pi:Y\to S$.

\subsection*{2.2. Lemma.}
\emph{The curve $Y_\eta$ is absolutely irreducible and generically smooth over $k((t))$.}

The curve $Y_s$ is connected (because $Y$ is proper over $S$ and connected) and has a rational point, hence is absolutely connected. It is smooth near $O$ (except at $O$),
\clearpage
% Authority physical 6; printed 134
and therefore has rational smooth points over a separable extension $k'$ of $k$. Over $S'=\Spec k'[[t]]$, $Y'=Y\times_SS'$ therefore has a section. The scheme $Y'$ is connected, because $Y_s'$ is connected, and regular, because it is étale over $Y$. It is therefore irreducible, and $Y_\eta'$ is irreducible, hence connected. Having a rational point, it is absolutely connected. This shows that $Y_\eta$ is absolutely connected. Being regular, it is absolutely irreducible. The smooth locus of $Y_\eta$, non-empty by hypothesis, is necessarily dense.

\subsection*{2.3. Interlude.}
Let $C$ be a complete reduced and generically smooth curve (i.e. $C$ absolutely reduced) over a field $K$, and let $D$ be a Cartier divisor on $C$. We will use the following geometric interpretation of $H^1(C,\OO(-D))$.

For simplicity suppose $C$ is connected and $D\ne\varnothing$. This case will suffice for us. Let $\Pic(C;D)$ be the generalized Jacobian which parametrizes invertible sheaves on $C$ trivialized on $D$. More precisely, define $\Pic(C;D)$ to be the $K$-scheme representing the functor on $K$-schemes
\[
T\longmapsto
\left(\begin{gathered}
\text{the set of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves}\\
\text{on $C_T$ trivialized on $D_T$}
\end{gathered}\right)
\]
Here $C_T=C\times_{\Spec K}T$, and similarly for $D$. It is a smooth $K$-scheme. Denote by $\OO(1\text{ on }D)^*$ the sheaf of sections of $\OO^*$ whose restriction to $D$ is 1. One has
\[
        \Pic(C;D)(T)=H^1(C_T,\OO(1\text{ on }D_T)^*).
\]
Taking $T$ to be the spectrum of the dual numbers over $K$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
        \Lie\Pic(C;D)=H^1(C,\OO(-D)). \tag{2.3.1}
\end{equation}

Let $\omega$ be the dualizing sheaf on $C$ ($\Omega^1_{C/K}$ for $C$ smooth). We will use Serre duality between $H^0(C,\omega(D))$ and $H^1(C,\OO(-D))$. It admits the following geometric interpretation, which we shall not use. Suppose first that $C$ is smooth. For every $T$ and every section $u:T\to C$ of $C_T/T$, the image of $u$ is then a Cartier divisor on $C_T$. The invertible sheaf $\OO(u(T))$ is canonically trivialized on the complement of $u(T)$. In particular, if $u$ is a section of $(C-D)_T$, it is trivialized on $D_T$. This construction defines a morphism
\begin{equation}
        j:C-D\longrightarrow\Pic(C;D),\qquad
        u\longmapsto\text{class of }\OO(u(T)). \tag{2.3.2}
\end{equation}
Pullback by $j$ gives a morphism from the vector space of invariant differential forms on $\Pic(C;D)$ -- the dual of the Lie algebra -- into $H^0(C-D,\omega)$. It gives Serre duality, i.e. the following diagram is commutative:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3.5em,row sep=2.5em]
(\Lie\Pic(C;D))^{\vee} \arrow[r,"j^*"] \arrow[d,equal,"{(2.3.1)}"] & H^0(C-D,\omega) \\
H^1(C,\OO(-D))^{\vee} \arrow[r,"\sim","{\text{(Serre)}}"'] & H^0(C,\omega(D)). \arrow[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\clearpage
% Authority physical 7; printed 135
With $C$ assumed only absolutely reduced, and $U$ its smooth locus, the same arguments give $j:U-D\longrightarrow\Pic(C;D)$. One still has (2.3.2), with $H^0(C-D,\omega)$ replaced by
\[
        H^0(U-D,\omega)=H^0(U-D,\Omega^1).
\]

\subsection*{2.4.}
Let $Z$ be a relative Cartier divisor on $Y$, i.e. a divisor no component of which is contained in $Y_s$. Assume that $O\notin Z$. The discussion in 2.3 will be applied with $K=k((t))$, $C=Y_\eta$, and $D=Z_\eta$.

Let $\Pic(Y;Z)^0$ be the group scheme over $S$ parametrizing invertible sheaves on $Y$, trivialized on $Z$, whose restriction to every irreducible component of every geometric fibre has degree 0. For the existence of $\Pic$, see [2], 8.2. It is a smooth group scheme over $S$ with connected fibres. Its special fibre $\Pic(Y;Z)^0_s$ is the neutral component of the group scheme $\Pic(Y_s;Z_s)$ classifying invertible sheaves on $Y_s$ trivialized on $Z_s$.

Let $Y'_s$ be the curve obtained from $Y_s$ by normalization at $O$: there is a map $h:Y'_s\to Y_s$, and $h^{-1}(O)$ consists of two points $O_1$ and $O_2$ corresponding to the two branches of $Y_s$ at $O$; $Y_s$ is obtained from $Y'_s$ by identifying $O_1$ and $O_2$. In particular, $h$ induces an isomorphism from $Y'_s-\{O_1,O_2\}$ to $Y_s-\{O\}$. By abuse of notation, we again write $Z_s$ for the inverse image of $Z_s$ in $Y'_s$. The functor $h^*$ identifies invertible sheaves $\mathscr L$ on $Y_s$ with invertible sheaves $\mathscr L'$ on $Y'_s$ equipped with an isomorphism
\[
        \sigma:\mathscr L'_{O_1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr L'_{O_2}
\]
between the fibres at $O_1$ and $O_2$. A trivialization of $\mathscr L$ on $Z_s$ corresponds to one of $\mathscr L'$. In particular, for $\lambda\in k^*$, one obtains an invertible sheaf $\mathscr L_\lambda$ on $Y_s$, trivialized on $Z_s$, by taking $\mathscr L'_\lambda=\OO$ and taking $\sigma:k\to k$ to be multiplication by $\lambda$. The same construction may be repeated after a base change $T\to s$ (see below); hence there is a morphism of $k$-group schemes
\begin{equation}
        w_1:\GG_m\longrightarrow\Pic(Y_s,Z_s),\qquad \lambda\longmapsto\mathscr L_\lambda. \tag{2.4.1}
\end{equation}

Here is an analogous construction modulo $t^n$. Let $Y_n$ be the reduction of $Y$ modulo $t^n$, and let $q:Y'_n\to Y_n$ agree with $Y_n$ away from $O$ and be obtained near $O$ as follows. Taking ``neighbourhood'' in the étale sense, one can find local coordinates $x,y$ in which $Y_n$ is given by $(xy)^n=0$. Take $Y'_n$ to be the disjoint union of $x^n=0$ and $y^n=0$. Notice that for $n>1$, $Y'_n$ is not flat over $k[[t]]/(t^n)$. A local calculation shows that $\OO_{Y_n}$ injects into $q_*\OO_{Y'_n}$. This remains true after every flat base change $T\to\Spec k[[t]]/(t^n)$. Define $Q_{n,T}$ by the exact sequence
\begin{equation}
        0\longrightarrow\OO_{Y_{n,T}}\longrightarrow q_*\OO_{Y'_{n,T}}\longrightarrow Q_{n,T}\longrightarrow0. \tag{2.4.2}
\end{equation}
It is a sheaf supported on the inverse image $O_T$ of $O$ in $Y_{n,T}$. Writing $\pi^\bullet$ for inverse image in the sense of sheaves under $\pi:Y_{n,T}\to T$, there is a morphism of sheaves
\begin{equation}
        \delta:(\pi^\bullet\OO_T)_{|O_T}\longrightarrow Q_{n,T} \tag{2.4.3}
\end{equation}
which, locally near $O$ for the étale topology, sends $u$ to the image in $q_*\OO_{Y'_{n,T}}/\OO_{Y_{n,T}}$ of the section of $\OO_{Y'_{n,T}}$ equal to $u$ on the first branch and 0 on the second.
\clearpage
% Authority physical 8; printed 136
Passing to the multiplicative version, define $Q^*_{n,T}$ similarly by the exact sequence
\begin{equation}
        0\longrightarrow\OO^*_{Y_{n,T}}\longrightarrow q_*\OO^*_{Y'_{n,T}}\longrightarrow Q^*_{n,T}\longrightarrow0, \tag{2.4.4}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
        \delta^*:(\pi^\bullet\OO_T^*)_{|O_T}\longrightarrow Q^*_{n,T}. \tag{2.4.5}
\end{equation}
Here $\delta^*(u)$ is the image of ``$u$ on the first branch, 1 on the second.''

Taking the boundary map $H^0\to H^1$ in the long exact sequence of cohomology with support in $O_T$ deduced from (2.4.2), respectively (2.4.4), the maps (2.4.3), respectively (2.4.5), give
\begin{equation}
        H^0(T,\OO_T)\longrightarrow H^1_{O_T}(Y_{n,T},\OO)\longrightarrow H^1(Y_{n,T},\OO(-Z_{n,T})) \tag{2.4.6}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
        H^0(T,\OO_T^*)\longrightarrow H^1_{O_T}(Y_{n,T},\OO^*)\longrightarrow H^1(Y_{n,T},\OO(1\text{ on }Z_{n,T})^*). \tag{2.4.7}
\end{equation}
The morphism of functors in $T$ in (2.4.7) gives a morphism of $k[[t]]/(t^n)$-schemes from $\GG_m$ to the reduction modulo $t^n$ of $\Pic_Z(Y)^0$:
\begin{equation}
        w_n:\GG_m\longrightarrow\Pic(Y,Z)^0_n. \tag{2.4.8}
\end{equation}
For $m\le n$, the reduction of $w_n$ modulo $t^m$ is $w_m$, and for $n=1$ one recovers (2.4.1). By (SGA3 IX 3.6), there is a unique morphism (2.4.8) lifting the $k$-morphism (2.4.1) to $k[[t]]/(t^n)$. We shall use its description (2.4.7).

One cannot pass to the limit in $n$ to deduce from the $w_n$ a morphism of group schemes from $\GG_m$ to $\Pic(Y;Z)^0$. However:

\noindent (a) for a scheme $H$ finite over $k[[t]]$ and any $P$, giving a morphism $H\to P$ is equivalent to giving a compatible system of morphisms $H_n\to P_n$ between their reductions modulo $t^n$;

\noindent (b) for two free $k[[t]]$-modules $V$ and $W$, giving a linear map $\Gamma:V\to W$ is equivalent to giving a compatible system of linear maps $V\otimes k[[t]]/(t^n)\to W\otimes k[[t]]/(t^n)$.

For every integer $m$, apply (a) to the subgroup scheme $\mu_m$ of $\GG_m$, the kernel of $x\mapsto x^m:\GG_m\to\GG_m$. Passing to the limit over $n$ gives a morphism
\begin{equation}
        w:\mu_m\longrightarrow\Pic(Y,Z)^0. \tag{2.4.9}
\end{equation}
Applying (b) after passing to Lie algebras gives
\begin{equation}
        dw:\Lie\GG_m\longrightarrow\Lie\Pic(Y;Z)^0. \tag{2.4.10}
\end{equation}
One has $\Lie\GG_m=\OO$, the trivial free module of rank one, and the arguments of 2.3 again give a canonical isomorphism $\Lie\Pic(Y;Z)^0=H^1(Y,\OO(-Z))$. Thus the datum of $dw$ is equivalent to that of the image
\begin{equation}
        dw(1)\in H^1(Y,\OO(-Z)). \tag{2.4.11}
\end{equation}

Let $\omega_{Y/S}=\pi^!\OO_S[1]$ be the relative dualizing sheaf and let $\varphi$ be a section of $\omega_{Y/S}(Z)$. On $X$, the completion of $Y$ at $O$, choose a system of coordinates
\clearpage
% Authority physical 9; printed 137
$x,y$ in which $\pi$ is $(x,y)\mapsto xy$ and the first branch of $X_1$ is $x=0$. In these coordinates, $\varphi$ is written on $X$ as
\[
        \varphi=f(x,y)\,dx\wedge dy/dt.
\]
The restriction of $\varphi_\eta$ to $Y_\eta$ belongs to the $k((t))$-vector space $H^0(Y_\eta,\omega(Z_\eta))$, in Serre duality with $H^1(Y_\eta,\OO(-Z_\eta))$. The aim of this subsection is the following.

\subsection*{2.5. Proposition.}
\emph{With the preceding notation, one has}
\begin{equation}
        I(f)=-\langle dw(1),\varphi\rangle. \tag{2.5.1}
\end{equation}
Over all of $S$ there is likewise a pairing between $\Lie\Pic(Y;Z)^0=H^1(Y,\OO(-Z))$ and $H^0(Y,\omega(Z))$. Under the hypotheses made, these $k[[t]]$-modules are free and the pairing is a perfect duality. The right-hand side of (2.5.1) therefore lies in $k[[t]]$, not merely in $k((t))$. Its reduction modulo $t^n$ is $\langle dw_n(1),\varphi\rangle$. We shall verify (2.5.1) modulo $t^n$ for every $n$.

Let $X_n$ and $Y_n$ be the reductions of $X$ and $Y$ modulo $t^n$. The local trace map $H^1_{\{O\}}(X_n,\omega)\to k[[t]]/(t^n)$ and the global trace map $H^1(Y_n,\omega)\to k[[t]]/(t^n)$ giving Serre duality are related by the following commutative diagram:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2.6em,row sep=2em]
H^1_{\{O\}}(X_n,\omega) \arrow[d] & H^1_{\{O\}}(Y_n,\omega) \arrow[l] \arrow[r] & H^1(Y_n,\omega) \arrow[d] \\
k[[t]]/(t^n) \arrow[rr,equal] & & k[[t]]/(t^n).
\end{tikzcd}
\]
This compatibility reduces 2.5.1 to the lemma.

\subsection*{2.6. Lemma.}
\emph{The morphism}
\[
        H^1_{\{O\}}(X_n,\OO)\xleftarrow{\sim} H^1_{\{O\}}(Y_n,\OO(-Z_n))\longrightarrow H^1(Y_n,\OO(-Z_n))

\emph{sends $v_n$ to $dw_n(1)$.}

The reader may verify that the image of $v_n$ is also the image of 1 under (2.4.6). The lemma then follows from the description of $d$ in terms of the dual numbers $D=(k[[t]]/(t^n))[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ over $k[[t]]/(t^n)$ and from the natural exact sequence of morphisms

\section*{3. Proof of the theorem}

\subsection*{3.1.}
Let $S_0$ be a smooth curve over $k$, that is, a smooth scheme purely of dimension 1; let $s$ be a rational point of $S_0$, and let $S$ be the completion of $S_0$ at $s$. Let $\pi:Y_0\to S_0$ be proper and flat, purely of relative dimension 1, let $Y$ be the inverse image of $Y_0$ over $S$, and let $O\in Y_s$. We again denote by $\pi$ the morphism $Y\to S$ deduced from $\pi:Y_0\to S_0$, and assume that $(\pi:Y\to S,O)$ is of the type considered in §2. We use the notation of Section 2 for $(\pi:Y\to S,O)$.
\clearpage
% Authority physical 10; printed 138
Finally assume, for simplicity, that $Y_0$ is regular and that $\pi$ has geometrically connected fibres. Let $\omega:=\omega_{Y_0/S_0}$ be the relative dualizing sheaf. Where $Y_0$ is smooth over $S_0$, it is $\Omega^1_{Y_0/S_0}$. We again write $\omega$ for its inverse image on $Y$, or on the completion $X$ of $Y$ at $O$. We choose formal coordinates on $X$ and $S$ as before.

Let $\varphi$ be a rational section of $\omega$ on $Y_0$, regular at $O$. On $X$, one can write $\varphi=f(x,y)dx\wedge dy/dt$. The main result is the following.

\subsection*{3.2. Proposition.}
\emph{If $k$ has characteristic $p>0$, then $I(f)$ is algebraic over the field $k(S_0)\subset k((t))$ of rational functions on $S_0$.}

After replacing $\varphi$ by its product with a rational function on $S_0$ vanishing sufficiently at $s$, and replacing $S_0$ by a Zariski open neighbourhood containing $s$, we may and do suppose that, for a suitable relative Cartier divisor $Z_0$ on $Y_0$ not containing $O$, $\varphi$ is a section of $\omega$ on $Y_0-Z_0$.

Let $\Pic(Y_0;Z_0)^0$ be the group scheme over $S_0$ parametrizing invertible sheaves on $Y_0$, trivialized on $Z_0$, of degree 0 on every irreducible component of every geometric fibre; if necessary to apply [2], shrink $S_0$ further. By Serre duality, interpret $\varphi$ as corresponding to a section $\varphi'$ of the dual of the vector bundle $\Lie\Pic(Y_0;Z_0)=R^1\pi_*\OO(-Z_0)$ on $S_0$; in the geometric interpretation (2.3), $\varphi=j^*\varphi'$.

After the base change from $S_0$ to $S$, there is a map $dw:\OO\to\Lie\Pic(Y_0,Z_0)^0_S=\Lie\Pic(Y;Z)^0$ as in (2.4.10), and the proposition follows from 2.5 and the

\subsection*{3.3. Key Lemma.}
\emph{If $p>0$, then $dw$ is algebraic over $S_0$.}

In characteristic $p>0$, the inclusion $\mu_p\subset\GG_m$ induces an isomorphism on Lie algebras:
\[
        \Lie(\mu_p)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lie(\GG_m).
\]
By reduction modulo $t^n$, one verifies that $dw$ is the differential of the morphism (2.4.9) $w:\mu_p\to\Pic_Z(Y)^0$. It remains to use the rigidity of $\mu_p$: for every flat commutative group scheme $P$ over $S_0$, $\Hom(\mu_p,P)$ is represented by an étale scheme over $S_0$. Take $P=\Pic(Y_0;Z_0)$, and let $\widetilde S_0$ be the resulting $S_0$-scheme. By definition, over $\widetilde S_0$ there is a universal morphism
\begin{equation}
        \mathrm{un}:\mu_p\longrightarrow\Pic(Y_0;Z_0)_{\widetilde S_0}. \tag{3.3.1}
\end{equation}
In particular, the morphism 2.4.9 is the inverse image of 3.3.1 under an $S_0$-morphism $b$:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.4em,row sep=1.6em]
S \arrow[rr,"b"] \arrow[dr] & & \widetilde S_0 \arrow[dl] \\
& S_0 &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
Likewise, $dw$ is the inverse image under $b$ of the morphism from $\OO$ into $\Lie\Pic(Y_0;Z_0)_{\widetilde S_0}$ obtained by passing to Lie algebras in (3.3.1); since $\widetilde S_0$ is quasi-finite over $S_0$, this proves its algebraicity.
\clearpage
% Authority physical 11; printed 139
\subsection*{3.4.}
Let $k\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}$ denote the henselization of the local ring of affine space $\Spec k[x_1,\ldots,x_N]$ at 0. The completion of this local ring is $k[[x_1,\ldots,x_N]]$, and it follows from [3] XI §3, Cor. 1, that a series $g\in k[[x_1,\ldots,x_N]]$ is algebraic over the field $k(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ if and only if $g$ belongs to $k\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}$.

\subsection*{3.5. Proof of the theorem.}
By 3.4, saying that $f$ is algebraic means that there exist a surface $Y'_0$ with a rational point $O$, an étale morphism $h$ from $Y'_0$ to the plane $\Aaff^2$ with coordinates $x,y$ sending $O$ to 0, and a section $\widetilde f$ of $\OO$ on $Y'_0$ whose power-series expansion at $O$ is $f$. Let $S'_0$ be the line $\Aaff^1$ with coordinate $t$, and let $\pi'$ be the composite
\[
        \pi':Y'_0\xrightarrow{h}\Aaff^2\xrightarrow{xy}S'_0.
\]
On $Y'_0$, let $\varphi$ be the section $\widetilde f\cdot h^*(dx\wedge dy/dt)$ of the relative dualizing sheaf. Compactifying $Y'_0$ and resolving the singularities introduced gives
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em,row sep=2em]
Y'_0 \arrow[r] \arrow[d,"\pi'"'] & Y_0 \arrow[d,"\pi''"] \\
S'_0 \arrow[r,equal] & S'_0.
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with $\pi''$ proper and flat and $Y_0$ regular. On $Y_0$, $\varphi$ appears as a rational section of the relative dualizing sheaf. In order that $\pi$ have geometrically connected fibres, replace $S'_0$ by the space $S_0$ in the Stein factorization
\[
        Y_0\xrightarrow{\pi}S_0\longrightarrow S'_0
\]
of $\pi''$. It remains to apply 3.2 to $\varphi$.

\subsection*{3.6. Proof of the corollary.}
We proceed by induction on $N$. The case $N=0$ is left to the reader, the case $N=1$ is trivial, the case $N=2$ is the theorem, and we suppose $N\ge3$. For $g$ in $k[[x_1,\ldots,x_N]]$, put
\[
        g=\sum g_{nm}x_{N-1}^n x_N^m
\]
with $g_{nm}\in k[[x_1,\ldots,x_{N-2}]]$, and
\[
        I(g)=\sum g_{nn}x_{N-1}^n.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
        I_N(g)=I_{N-1}I(g). \tag{3.6.1}
\end{equation}
If $g$ is algebraic, then $g\in k\{x_1,\ldots,x_N\}$ by 3.4, and the $g_{nm}$ are algebraic: $g_{nm}\in k\{x_1,\ldots,x_{N-2}\}$. Let $k'$ be the field of fractions of $k\{x_1,\ldots,x_{N-2}\}$, and consider the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=1.2em,row sep=2em]
k[[x_1,\ldots,x_N]] \arrow[r,phantom,"\supset"] \arrow[d,"I"] &
k\{x_1,\ldots,x_{N-2}\}[[x_{N-1},x_N]] \arrow[r,phantom,"\subset"] \arrow[d,"I"] &
k'[[x_{N-1},x_N]] \arrow[d,"I"] \\
k[[x_1,\ldots,x_{N-1}]] \arrow[r,phantom,"\supset"] &
k\{x_1,\ldots,x_{N-2}\}[[x_{N-1}]] \arrow[r,phantom,"\subset"] & k'[[x_{N-1}]]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
In the first row these are $k[x_1,\ldots,x_N]$-algebras, and in the second row $k[x_1,\ldots,x_{N-1}]$-algebras. Apply the theorem to the image $g'$ of $g$, assumed algebraic, in $k'[[x_{N-1},x_N]]$. Since $k'$ is algebraic over the field of fractions of
\clearpage
% Authority physical 12; printed 140
$k[x_1,\ldots,x_{N-2}]$, the series $I(g')$, algebraic over $k'(x_{N-1})$, satisfies a nontrivial equation
\[
        \sum a_iI(g')^i=0
\]
with coefficients $a_i\in k[x_1,\ldots,x_{N-1}]$. The same equation is satisfied by $I(g)$, hence $I(g)$ is algebraic: $I(g)\in k\{x_1,\ldots,x_{N-1}\}$. We conclude by (3.6.1) and the induction hypothesis.

\subsection*{3.7.}
One can extract from the proof of the theorem an estimate for the degree $d$ of the equation expressing that $I(f)$ is algebraic over $k(t)$. With the notation of 3.5 and 3.1, suppose that $S_0$ has degree $\sigma$ over $S'_0$, that $Y_\eta$ has genus $g$, and that $Z_\eta$ has $z'$ geometric points. Put $z=\sup(0,z'-1)$. Here the genus is the genus of the normalization of the curve $Y_{\bar\eta}$ obtained from $Y_\eta$ by extending scalars to an algebraic closure $k(\bar\eta)$ of $k(\eta)$; the first $l$-adic Betti number of $Y_{\bar\eta}-Z_{\bar\eta}$ is $2g+z$. One has
\begin{equation}
        d\le\sigma\cdot p^{g+z}. \tag{3.7.1}
\end{equation}
The degree of $I(f)$ over $k(S_0)$ is $\le p^{g+z}$, and even $\le\sup((p^g-1)p^z,z'!)$.

If $f$ is an algebraic formal power series with integral coefficients and $\sigma$, $g$, and $z$ are defined in terms of $f\in\QQ[[x,y]]$, then for almost every $p$ the degree $d_p$ of the reduction modulo $p$ of $I(f)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
        d_p\le\sigma\cdot p^{g+z}. \tag{3.7.2}
\end{equation}
One can also obtain information modulo $p^m$: using the fact that over a base on which $p^m=0$ one has $\Lie(\mu_{p^m})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lie(\GG_m)$, one finds that, for almost every $p$, the reduction of $I(f)$ modulo $p^m$ satisfies an equation with coefficients in $\ZZ/(p^m)[t]$, not all divisible by $p$, and of degree $\le\sigma\cdot p^{m(g+z)}$.

\subsection*{3.8.}
I do not know how to obtain information modulo $p^m$ for every $p$. If, in the proof of the theorem, one tries to replace $k$ by a commutative ring $A$ in which $p^m=0$, a difficulty appears: constructing $Y_0/S_0$ with reasonably good behaviour, for example flatness, not only near $O$ but everywhere.

\subsection*{3.9.}
The proof in 3.6 of the corollary does not satisfy me. It would be more interesting to treat the case of $n$ variables directly, inspired by the following analogue of the integral formula (1): over $\CC$, for convergent $g$, one has
\begin{equation}
        I(g)(t)=\int_{Z(t)} g\cdot dz_1\ldots dz_n/dt, \tag{1'}
\end{equation}
where the integral is taken over a cycle
\[
        Z(t)=\{(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\mid x_1\ldots x_n=t,
        |x_1|=r_1,\ldots,|x_m|=r_n\}
\]
with $\prod r_i=|t|$.

I hope that a direct proof would give, for algebraic formal power series with integral coefficients, an estimate $d_p\le O(p^\beta)$ for the degree of the reduction of $I(g)$ modulo $p$ over $\FF_p(t)$.

Providing the tools required for a direct proof would be a test for a $p$-adic theory of vanishing cycles.

\subsection*{3.10.}
Furstenberg's proof of the corollary, for rational $g$, shows that if $g\in k[[x_1,\ldots,x_N]]$ is one component of a column vector $h$
\clearpage
% Authority physical 13; printed 141
of elements of $k[[x_1,\ldots,x_N]]$ that satisfies a system of equations $h=Ah^p$, for $A$ a square matrix with polynomial coefficients, then $I(g)$ has the same property and is therefore algebraic. This type of equation is reminiscent of $F$-crystals in crystalline cohomology.

At the same time, $T_p\Pic$ is an avatar of $p$-adic $H^1$. This leads me to hope that Furstenberg's proof and the one given here are less remote from one another than they appear.

\section*{Appendix}
{\large\itshape Signs}\par
\medskip

The treatment in the literature of sign questions in the formalism of duality for coherent algebraic sheaves has seemed confused to me. Hartshorne's \emph{Residues and Duality} (Lect. Notes 20) suffers from the following defects:

\noindent (a) the convention defining the complexes $\Hom^{\bullet}(K,L)$ (I 6 p. 63, 64) and $K\otimes L$ (II 4 p. 93) is not the usual convention; contrary to what is asserted on p. 64, $\Hom^{\bullet}$ does not commute (identically) with translation functors (cf. SGA4 XVII 0.3); likewise for $\otimes$, whose associativity requires the definition of a canonical isomorphism, which is not given. Since the definition of $f^!$ for $f:X\to Y$ smooth and purely of relative dimension $n$ involves tensoring with $\Omega^n_{X/Y}[n]$, this gap matters.

\noindent (b) Insofar as I can nevertheless understand the proposed convention, the definition in III 4.3 of the trace morphism, based on the convention that defines $\gamma$ in III 3.4, seems to me incompatible with III 10.5 TRA1).

The conventions set out below are, I believe, coherent. I use the usual sign convention for $\otimes$ (SGA4 XVII 1.1). I have not restricted myself to dimension 1, which would suffice for the needs of the article.

\noindent (a) For $f:X\to S$ flat, a relative complete intersection, and purely of relative dimension $n$, the relative dualizing sheaf $\omega_{X/S}$ is related to the relative dualizing complex $K_{X/S}:=Rf^!\OO_S$ by
\begin{equation}
        K_{X/S}=\omega_{X/S}[n]. \tag{S.1}
\end{equation}
It is an invertible sheaf. For $X$ smooth over $S$, one has $\omega_{X/S}=\Omega^n_{X/S}$. If, as suggested in my appendix to Hartshorne's book, one defines $Rf^!$ by adjunction, the equality $\omega_{X/S}=\Omega^n_{X/S}$ is a canonical isomorphism whose definition presents a sign problem. Here we adopt the opposite point of view (cf. the way Hartshorne treats smoothable morphisms), in which this equality is evident from the definition of $Rf^!$ and the sign problem lies in the definition of the trace morphism (S.5) below.

If $X$ is the fiber product over $S$ of the $f_i:X_i\to S$ ($i\in I$), where each $X_i$ is flat, a relative complete intersection, and purely of relative dimension $n_i$, with $n=\sum n_i$, then
\begin{equation}
        K_{X/S}=\boxtimes_i K_{X_i/S} \tag{S.2}
\end{equation}
(external tensor product). By definition, giving such an isomorphism means giving, for each total order on $I$, an isomorphism
\begin{equation}
        \omega_{X/S}=\boxtimes_i\omega_{X_i/S}. \tag{S.3}
\end{equation}
We require that, when one passes from the total order $<_1$ to the total order $<_2$, the isomorphism change by the sign $(-1)^N$, where $N=\sum n_in_j$ (the sum over pairs $(i,j)$ with $i<_1j$ and $i>_2j$). For $X_i$ smooth over $S$, one has $\omega_{X_i/S}=\Omega^{n_i}_{X_i/S}$, and (S.3) is given by the exterior product taken in the given order on $I$. It has the required parity property.

For an iterated morphism $f=gh:X\xrightarrow{h}Y\xrightarrow{g}S$, $K_{X/S}$ is the $\otimes$ of the complexes $K_{X/Y}$ and $h^*K_{Y/S}$ via an isomorphism which, when applied to $X_1\times_SX_2\to X_1\to S$, recovers (S.3).
\clearpage
% Authority physical 14; printed 142
\noindent (b) For $f:X\to S$ as in (a) and proper, the trace morphism is a morphism
\begin{equation}
        \Tr:Rf_*K_{X/S}\longrightarrow\OO_S. \tag{S.4}
\end{equation}
The morphism (S.4) induces a morphism of coherent sheaves $\mathcal H^0Rf_*K_{X/S}\to\OO_S$, i.e. a morphism
\begin{equation}
        \Tr:R^nf_*\omega_{X/S}\longrightarrow\OO_S. \tag{S.5}
\end{equation}
Let us specify how one passes from (S.4) to (S.5). For every complex $K$, the complex $K[d]$ has components $(K[d])^i=K^{i+d}$ and differential equal to that of $K$ modified by the sign $(-1)^d$. We identify the $\mathcal H^i(K[d])$ with the $\mathcal H^{i+d}K$ without introducing a sign. The functor $Rf_*$ commutes with the translation functor $[d]$, and (S.5) is deduced from (S.4) by applying the functor $\mathcal H^0$: use the isomorphism
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H^0Rf_*K_{X/S}
&=\mathcal H^0Rf_*(\omega_{X/S}[n])
 =\mathcal H^0(Rf_*(\omega_{X/S})[n])\\
&=\mathcal H^nRf_*\omega_{X/S}=R^nf_*\omega_{X/S}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For $s$ a section of $f$, there is also the local trace morphism
\begin{equation}
        \Tr:Rs^!K_{X/S}\longrightarrow\OO_S. \tag{S.6}
\end{equation}
The definitions of (S.4) and (S.6) present, for every relative dimension $n$, a sign problem. We impose commutativity of the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=3em,row sep=1.5em]
Rs^!K_{X/S} \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & Rf_*K_{X/S} \arrow[d] \\
\OO_S \arrow[r,equal] & \OO_S
\end{tikzcd}
\quad;
\]
This relates, in each relative dimension, the choices (S.4) and (S.6). The morphism in the first row of the diagram is derived from the inclusion morphism $s^!\to f_*$ ((sections with schematic support $s(S)$)$\subset$(all sections)). For $X_i$ as in (a), proper over $S$, and hence giving by (S.3) an isomorphism
\[
        Rf_*K_{X/S}=\bigotimes_i Rf_{i*}K_{X_i/S},
\]
we require that the morphism (S.4) for $X/S$ be the tensor product of the morphisms (S.4) for the $X_i/S$. This relates the conventions used in each relative dimension and leaves only one sign ambiguity. We resolve it by requiring that, for $X$ a smooth curve over an algebraically closed field $k$ and $x\in X(k)$, the local trace morphism
\begin{equation}
        \mathbf H^0_{\{x\}}(X,\Omega^1_X[1])\longrightarrow k, \tag{S.7}
\end{equation}
inducing (S.6) when $s$ is the inclusion of $x$, be the following. The complex $\Omega^1_X[1]$, reduced to the sheaf $\Omega^1_X$ in cohomological degree $-1$, maps quasi-isomorphically to the complex concentrated in degrees $-1$ and 0
\[
        L:=\Omega^1_X(\infty\cdot x)\longrightarrow\Omega^1_X(\infty\cdot x)/\Omega^1_X.
\]
One has
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf H^0_{\{x\}}(X,\Omega^1_X[1])&\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf H^0_{\{x\}}(X,L)
\xleftarrow{\sim}H^0\Gamma_{\{x\}}(X,L)\\
&=(\Omega^1_X(\infty\cdot x)/\Omega^1_X)_x,
\end{aligned}
\]
the polar parts at $x$ of differential forms, and the local trace morphism is taken to be the residue morphism $\Res_x$.

Let us indicate some compatibilities resulting from this choice of signs. To simplify notation, we take $S$ to be the spectrum of a field $k$.

\noindent (c) Let $X$ be a smooth curve over an algebraically closed field $k$, and let $x\in X(k)$. Again call ``trace'' the morphism deduced from (S.7)
\[
        \Tr:H^1_{\{x\}}(X,\Omega^1_X)\longrightarrow k.
\]
Let $L'$ be the complex concentrated in degrees 0 and 1
\[
        L':=\Omega^1_X(\infty\cdot x)\longrightarrow\Omega^1_X(\infty\cdot x)/\Omega^1_X.
\]
It differs by a sign from $L[-1]$. Because of this sign, $\Tr$ is the negative of the composite
\[
        H^1_{\{x\}}(X,\Omega^1_X)\xleftarrow{\sim}H^1\Gamma_{\{x\}}L'
        =\text{(polar parts at }x)\xrightarrow{\Res}k.
\]
\clearpage
% Authority physical 15; printed 143
The sequence of functors $\Gamma_{\{x\}}(X,-)\to\Gamma(X,-)\to\Gamma(X-\{x\},-)$, which is short exact when applied to a flasque sheaf, gives a boundary morphism $\partial:H^0(X-\{x\},\Omega^1)\to H^1_{\{x\}}(X,\Omega^1)$. One has
\[
        \Tr\circ\partial=-\Res_x.
\]
Finally, for $X$ complete, the boundary morphism attached to the short exact sequence of sheaves
\[
        0\longrightarrow\Omega^1_X\longrightarrow\text{(rational differential forms)}
        \longrightarrow\text{(polar parts)}\longrightarrow0,
\]
composed with the trace morphism, is the sum of the residue morphisms at all points $x\in X(k)$.

\noindent (d) For $X=X_1\times X_2$, with $X_i$ complete, locally a complete intersection, and purely of dimension $n_i$, and for $u_i\in H^{n_i}(X_i,\omega_{X_i})$, let $n=n_1+n_2$ and let $\operatorname{pr}_1^*u_1\cup\operatorname{pr}_2^*u_2\in H^n(X,\omega_X)$ be the cup product relative to (S.3): $\operatorname{pr}_1^*\omega_{X_1}\otimes\operatorname{pr}_2^*\omega_{X_2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\omega_X$. The required compatibility of the trace morphisms with products is
\[
        \Tr(\operatorname{pr}_1^*u_1\cup\operatorname{pr}_2^*u_2)
        =(-1)^{n_1n_2}\Tr(u_1)\cdot\Tr(u_2).
\]

\noindent (e) For $k=\CC$, and for the transcendental topology, there is a trace morphism
\[
        \int:H^{2n}(X(\CC),\CC)\longrightarrow\CC.
\]
If $\Omega^{**}$ is the $C^\infty$ de Rham complex, the quasi-isomorphism $\CC\to\Omega^{**}$ gives an isomorphism $H^{2n}(X(\CC),\CC)=(2n\text{-forms})/(\text{exact forms})$, and $\int$ is integration over $X(\CC)$ with its usual orientation. It will be more convenient for us to regard $\int$ as a morphism $\mathbf H^0(X(\CC),\CC[2n])\to\CC$. This even shift in the degrees causes no sign problem. Let $\Omega^*$ be the holomorphic de Rham complex. The quasi-isomorphisms and morphisms
\[
        \CC[2n]\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega^*[2n]\longleftarrow\Omega^n[n]
\]
give in cohomology, for the transcendental topology,
\[
        \varepsilon:\mathbf H^0(X(\CC),\Omega^n[n])\longrightarrow \mathbf H^0(X(\CC),\CC[2n]).
\]
One has $H^0(X(\CC),\Omega^n[n])=H^0(X,\Omega^n[n])$ by GAGA, and the two trace morphisms are related by
\[
        \Tr(u)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^n}\int \varepsilon(u).
\]

\noindent (f) Let $\PP^n$ be projective $n$-space, let $(T_i)_{0\le i\le n}$ be a system of homogeneous coordinates, let $U_i$ be the open set $T_i\ne0$, and set $t_i=T_i/T_0$. Order the index set $[0,n]$ of the open covering $(U_i)$ in the evident way. The section
\[
        \frac{dt_1}{t_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{dt_n}{t_n}
\]
of $\Omega^n$ on the intersection $U_{0\ldots n}$ of the $U_i$ defines a Čech cocycle, and hence a class in $H^n(\PP^n,\Omega^n)$. One has
\[
        \Tr\left(\frac{dt_1}{t_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{dt_n}{t_n}\right)
        =(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}.
\]
