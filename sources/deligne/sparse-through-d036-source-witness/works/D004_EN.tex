\begin{center}
{\small Inventiones math. 8, 238--243 (1969)}\\[3.0em]
{\Large\bfseries Ordinary abelian varieties over a finite field}\\[1.0em]
{\large Pierre Deligne (Bures-sur-Yvette)}
\end{center}

We give a down-to-earth description of the category of ordinary abelian varieties over a finite field $\Fq$. The result obtained was inspired by Ihara [2], ch. V (see also [3]).

\paragraph{1.} Let $p$ be a prime number, let $\Fp$ be the field $\Z/(p)$, let $\overline{\Fp}$ be an algebraic closure of $\Fp$, and, for each power $q$ of $p$, let $\Fq$ be the subfield with $q$ elements of $\overline{\Fp}$. For every algebraic extension $k$ of $\Fp$, let $W_0(k)$ denote the henselian discrete valuation ring, essentially of finite type over $\Z$, absolutely unramified, with residue field $k$; let $W(k)$ be the ring of Witt vectors over $k$, i.e. the completion of $W_0(k)$, let $W=W(\overline{\Fp})$, and let $\varphi$ be an embedding of $W$ in the field $\C$ of complex numbers. We denote by $\Z(1)$ the subgroup $2\pi i\Z$ of $\C$. The exponential map defines an isomorphism between $\Z(1)\otimes\Z_\ell$ and
\[
\Z_\ell(1)(\C)=\varprojlim_n\mu_{\ell^n}(\C).
\]
We denote by $A^*$ the abelian variety dual to an abelian variety $A$. For every field $k$, let $\bar k$ denote an algebraic closure of $k$.

\paragraph{2.} Let $A$ be an abelian variety of dimension $g$, defined over a field $k$ of characteristic $p$. Recall that $A$ is called ordinary if the following equivalent conditions are satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
\item $A$ has $p^g$ points of order dividing $p$ with values in $\bar k$.
\item The ``Hasse--Witt matrix''
\[
F^*:H^1(A^{(p)},\mathcal O_{A^{(p)}})\longrightarrow H^1(A,\mathcal O_A)
\]
is invertible.
\item The neutral component of the group scheme $A_p$, the kernel of multiplication by $p$, is of multiplicative type, hence geometrically isomorphic to a power of $\mu_p$.
\end{enumerate}
If $k=\Fq$, if $F$ is the Frobenius endomorphism of $A$, and if $P_{cA}(F;x)$ is its characteristic polynomial, these conditions are further equivalent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
\item At least half of the roots of $P_{cA}(F;x)$ in $\overline{\Q}_p$ are $p$-adic units. In other words, if $n=\dim A$, the reduction modulo $p$ of the polynomial $P_{cA}(F;x)$ is not divisible by $x^{n+1}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{3.} Let $A$ be an ordinary abelian variety over $\overline{\Fp}$. Let $\widetilde A$ denote the canonical Serre--Tate lift [4] of $A$ over $W$. Recall that $\widetilde A$ depends functorially on $A$ and is characterized by the fact that the $p$-divisible group $T_p(\widetilde A)$ over $W$ attached to $\widetilde A$ [5] is the product of the $p$-divisible groups, uniquely determined by 2. (III), lifting respectively the neutral component and the largest étale quotient of $T_p(A)$. The canonical lift $\widetilde A$ is also the unique lift of $A$ such that every endomorphism of $A$ lifts to $\widetilde A$. Let $T(A)$ denote the integral homology of the complex abelian variety $\Ac$ deduced from $\widetilde A$ and $\varphi$ by extension of scalars from $W$ to $\C$:
\[
T(A)=H_1(\widetilde A\otimes_{\varphi}\C).
\]
It is known that $\widetilde A$ descends uniquely to $W_0(\overline{\Fp})$, so that $\Ac$ depends only on $A$ and on the restriction of $\varphi$ to $W_0(\overline{\Fp})$. The free $\Z$-module $T(A)$ has rank $2\dim(A)$; it is functorial in $A$. Moreover, if $\ell$ is a prime number distinct from $p$, one has functorially
\[
\tag{3.1}
T(A)\otimes\Z_\ell=T_\ell(A).
\]

The canonical lift of the abelian variety $A^*$ dual to $A$ is the dual of $\widetilde A$, so that $(\Ac)^*=A^*_{\C}$ and $T(A)$ and $T(A^*)$ are in perfect duality with values in $\Z(1)$:
\[
\tag{3.2}
T(A)\otimes T(A^*)\longrightarrow\Z(1).
\]
It is indispensable to use $\Z(1)$ rather than $\Z$ in order to obtain a theory invariant under complex conjugation. The pairings (3.2) are compatible, via (3.1), with the pairings
\[
T_\ell(A)\otimes T_\ell(A^*)\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(1);
\]
a morphism $\xi:A\to A^*$ defines a polarization of $A$ if and only if $\xi_{\C}:\Ac\to A^*_{\C}$ defines a polarization of $\Ac$. Put
\[
T'_p(A)=\Hom(\Q_p/\Z_p,A(\overline{\Fp}))
\]
and
\[
T''_p(A)=\Hom_{\Z_p}(T'_p(A^*),\Z(1)\otimes\Z_p).
\]
These $\Z_p$-modules are covariant functors of $A$.

By definition of the canonical lift, the $p$-divisible group $T_p(\widetilde A)$ is the sum of the constant pro-étale group $T'_p(A)$ and of the Cartier dual of $T'_p(A^*)$. For every morphism $u:A\to B$, the induced morphism
\[
u:T_p(\widetilde A)\to T_p(\widetilde B)
\]
is identified with the sum of $u|T'_p(A):T'_p(A)\to T'_p(B)$ and the Cartier transpose of ${}^tu|T'_p(B^*):T'_p(B^*)\to T'_p(A^*)$. Over $\C$, one has canonically $\Z(1)/(p^n)\sim\mu_{p^n}$, whence an isomorphism of functors:
\[
\tag{3.2}
T_{(p)}(A)=T(A)\otimes\Z_p=T'_p(A)\oplus T''_p(A).
\]

\paragraph{4.} Recall that, if $\varphi:X\to Y$ is an isogeny between complex abelian varieties, the exact homotopy sequence reduces to a short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow H_1(X)\longrightarrow H_1(Y)\longrightarrow\Ker(\varphi)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The abelian varieties which are quotients of $X$ by a finite subgroup, and these finite subgroups of $X$, correspond bijectively to the sublattices of $H_1(X)\otimes\Q$ containing $H_1(X)$.

Let $A$ be an ordinary abelian variety over $\overline{\Fp}$. If $n$ is an integer prime to $p$, the finite group subschemes of order $n$ of $A$, of $\widetilde A$, and of $\Ac$ correspond bijectively, and still correspond to the overlattices $R$ of $T(A)$ such that $[R:T(A)]=n$.

Put
\[
V'_p=T'_p(A)\otimes\Q_p,\qquad V''_p(A)=T''_p(A)\otimes\Q_p.
\]
The finite group subschemes of order $p^k$ of $A$ are products of an étale subgroup and an infinitesimal subgroup. The étale subgroups of order $p^k$ of $A$ correspond to those subgroups of order $p^k$ of $\Ac$ for which the corresponding overlattice $R$ of $T(A)$ is contained in $T_{(p)}(A)+V'_p(A)$. By duality, the infinitesimal subgroups of $A$ correspond to the overlattices $R$ of $T(A)$, $p$-isogenous to $T(A)$, i.e. such that $[R:T(A)]$ is a power of $p$, and contained in $T_{(p)}(A)+V''_p(A)$.

In all, the finite subgroups of $A$, or the abelian varieties quotient of $A$, correspond bijectively to the overlattices $R$ of $T(A)$ such that
\[
\tag{4.1}
R\otimes\Z_p=(R\otimes\Z_p\cap V'_p)+(R\otimes\Z_p\cap V''_p).
\]

\paragraph{5.} In particular, $A^{(p)}$, the quotient of $A$ by the largest infinitesimal subgroup of $A$ killed by $p$ for $A$ ordinary, is defined by the overlattice $T(A)^{(p)}$ of $T(A)$, $p$-isogenous to $T(A)$, such that
\[
T(A)^{(p)}\otimes\Z_p=T'_p(A)+\frac{1}{p}T''_p(A).
\]

\paragraph{6.} Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $\Fq$ and let $F:x\mapsto x^q$ be its Frobenius endomorphism. Recall that $A$ is uniquely determined by the pair $(\overline A,F)$ deduced from $(A,F)$ by extension of scalars from $\Fq$ to $\overline{\Fq}$; the endomorphism $F$ of $\overline A$ factors through the relative Frobenius
\[
\Fr^{(q)}:\overline A\longrightarrow \overline A^{(q)}
\]
and an isomorphism
\[
F':\overline A^{(q)}\longrightarrow\overline A.
\]
If $A$ is ordinary, $T(A)$ will denote the $\Z$-module $T(\overline A)$ endowed with the endomorphism $F$ induced by the Frobenius endomorphism of $A$. By 5, by what precedes, and by (3.2), the lattices $T(A)$ and $F(T(A))$ are $p$-isogenous and one has
\[
\tag{6.1}
F(T'_p(A))=T'_p(A),
\]
\[
\tag{6.2}
F(T''_p(A))=qT''_p(A).
\]

\paragraph{7. Theorem.} The functor
\[
A\longmapsto (T(A),F)
\]
is an equivalence of categories between the category of ordinary abelian varieties over $\Fq$ and the category of finite-type free $\Z$-modules $T$ endowed with an endomorphism $F$ satisfying the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $F$ is semisimple, and its eigenvalues have complex absolute value $q^{1/2}$.
\item At least half of the roots in $\overline{\Q}_p$ of the characteristic polynomial of $F$ are $p$-adic units; in other words, if $T$ has rank $d$, the reduction modulo $p$ of the polynomial $P_{cT}(F;x)$ is not divisible by $x^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1}$.
\item There exists an endomorphism $V$ of $T$ such that $FV=q$.
\end{enumerate}
If condition (a) is satisfied, conditions (b) and (c) are equivalent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
\item The module $T\otimes\Z_p$ admits a decomposition, stable under $F$, into two $\Z_p$-submodules $T'_p$ and $T''_p$ of the same dimension, such that $F|T'_p$ is invertible and $F|T''_p$ is divisible by $q$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} (A) We prove that (a)+(b)+(c) imply (d). If $\alpha$ is a complex eigenvalue of $F$, then $\bar\alpha$ is another, with the same multiplicity, and $\alpha\bar\alpha=q$. Apart from those equal to $\pm q^{1/2}$, the eigenvalues of $F$ in $\C$, hence in $\overline{\Q}_p$, fall into pairs of roots $\alpha$ and $q/\alpha$. The roots $\alpha$ and $q/\alpha$ cannot both be $p$-adic units; it follows from (b) that $\pm q^{1/2}$ is not an eigenvalue of $F$, that half of the eigenvalues of $F$ in $\overline{\Q}_p$ are $p$-adic units, say $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{d/2}$, and that the other half consists of $\beta_1=q/\alpha_1,\ldots,\beta_{d/2}=q/\alpha_{d/2}$. Let
\[
T_{(p)}=T\otimes\Z_p,\qquad V_p=T\otimes\Q_p,
\]
let $V'_p$ be the subspace of $V_p$ which is the kernel of $\prod_i(F-\alpha_i)$, and let $V''_p$ be the kernel of the endomorphism $\varphi=\prod_i(F-\beta_i)$. One has
\[
V_p=V'_p\oplus V''_p.
\]
Let $T'_p$ be the projection of $T_{(p)}$ onto $V'_p$ and let $T''_p=T_{(p)}\cap V''_p$. Since $\varphi$ kills $V''_p$ and preserves $T$, it sends $T'_p$ into $T_{(p)}\cap V'_p\subset T'_p$. On the other hand,
\[
\det(\varphi|V'_p)=\prod_{i,j}(\alpha_i-\beta_j)
\]
is a $p$-adic unit, hence $\varphi(T'_p)=T'_p$ and $T_{(p)}\cap V'_p=T'_p$, so that
\[
T_{(p)}=T'_p\oplus T''_p.
\]

(B) \emph{Full faithfulness.} Let $A$ and $B$ be two abelian varieties over $\Fq$, and let $\psi$ be the map
\[
\psi:\Hom(A,B)\longrightarrow\Hom_F(T(A),T(B)).
\]
By Tate's theorem [7] and (3.1), the map
\[
\psi_\ell:\Hom(A,B)\otimes\Z_\ell
\longrightarrow
\Hom_F(T(A),T(B))\otimes\Z_\ell
\]
is an isomorphism for $(\ell,p)=1$, so that $\psi\otimes\Q$ is an isomorphism. It is known that $\Hom(A,B)$ is torsion-free, hence $\psi$ is injective. Let $u:A\to B$ be a morphism such that $T(u)$ is divisible by $n$. The induced morphism
\[
u_{\C}:\overline A_{\C}\longrightarrow\overline B_{\C}
\]
is then divisible by $n$, hence so is $\widetilde u:\widetilde{\overline A}\to\widetilde{\overline B}$ at the generic point of $W$. The kernel of multiplication by $n$ is flat over $W$; thus $\widetilde u$ vanishes on this kernel, $\widetilde u$ and $u$ are divisible by $n$, and $\psi$ is bijective.

(C) \emph{Necessity.} That $(T(A),F)$ satisfies (a) follows from Weil; condition (d), which implies (b) and (c), follows from 6.

(D) \emph{Isogenies.} Let $(T_0,F)$ satisfy (a) (d) and let $T$ be a lattice in $T_0\otimes\Q$, stable under $F$, still satisfying (d). Suppose that $(T_0,F)$ is the image of an abelian variety $A$ over $\Fq$ and let us prove that $(T,F)$ comes from an isogenous abelian variety. Replacing $T$ by $\frac1kT$, which is isomorphic to it, one may suppose that $T\supset T_0$. Condition (d) implies that $T$ satisfies (4.1), and $T$ defines a subgroup $H$ of $\overline A$, which is defined over $\Fq$, and
\[
(T,F)=T(A/H).
\]

(E) \emph{Surjectivity.} The functor $T$ induces a functor $T_\Q$ from the category of ordinary abelian varieties up to isogeny over $\Fq$ to the category of finite-rank $\Q$-vector spaces endowed with an automorphism $F$ satisfying (a) (b). By (D), it is enough to prove that this functor $T_\Q$ is essentially surjective. It is even enough to show that every simple object $(V,F)$ of the image category is attained. By Honda [1] (see also [6]), there exists an abelian variety $A$ over $\Fq$ such that the characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius $F_A$ of $A$ is a power of that of $F$. The third characterization in 2.\footnote{The printed source refers here to 1.; the characterizations of ordinarity are stated in 2., and that reference is restored here.} of ordinary abelian varieties shows that $A$ is ordinary. Moreover, $(T(A)\otimes\Q,F)$ is a sum of copies of $(V,F)$, hence, by (B), the abelian variety up to isogeny $A\otimes\Q$ is a sum of copies of an abelian variety $B$ satisfying
\[
T(B)\otimes\Q=(V,F).
\]

\paragraph{8.} Let $(T,F)$ be a pair satisfying the hypotheses of the theorem, let $2g$ be the rank of $T$, let $A$ be the corresponding abelian variety over $\Fq$, and let $\Ac$ be the complex abelian variety deduced from it (3.). One has
\[
T=H_1(\Ac),
\]
so that $T\otimes\R$ is identified with the Lie algebra of $\Ac$ and, as such, is endowed with a complex structure. Here is, according to J.-P. Serre, how to reconstruct this complex structure in terms of $T$, $F$, and the restriction of $\varphi$ to $W_0(\overline{\Fp})$.

\paragraph{Proposition.} The complex structure defined above on $T\otimes\R$ is characterized by the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*)]
\item The endomorphism $F$ is $\C$-linear.
\item If $v$ is the valuation of the algebraic closure $\overline{\Q}$ of $\Q$ in $\C$ extending the valuation of $W_0(\overline{\Fp})$, the valuations of the $g$ eigenvalues of this endomorphism are strictly positive.
\end{enumerate}
Condition (I) is evident, and condition (II) follows from the fact that the action of $F$ on the Lie algebra of $A$ is congruent to zero modulo $p$. The uniqueness of the complex structure satisfying (I) and (II) follows easily from condition (b) satisfied by $(T,F)$.
