{\Large\bfseries Complete intersections of Hodge level one}\\[0.45em]
Pierre Deligne (Bures-sur-Yvette)
\end{center}

\begin{center}\bfseries Introduction\end{center}

Throughout this article, we shall denote by \(n=2m+1\) a fixed odd integer \(\geq 3\), and by \(\underline a=(a_1,\ldots,a_v)\) a fixed sequence of integers \(>1\), such that the smooth complete intersections
\[
V_n(\underline a)\subset \Pj^{n+v}(\C)
\]
of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) have Hodge level 1. This condition means that, for such a complete intersection \(X\), the intersection of \(v\) hypersurfaces of degrees \(a_1,\ldots,a_v\) which, at the points of \(X\), are smooth and meet transversely,
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item for \(p+q=n\) and \(|q-p|>1\), one has \(H^q(X,\Omega_X^p)=0\);
\item \(H^m(X,\Omega_X^{m+1})\ne0\).
\end{enumerate}
Recall that, by Lefschetz,
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\item \(H^*(X,\Z)\) is torsion-free;
\item for \(k\) odd, \(k\ne n\), one has \(H^k(X,\Z)=0\);
\item for \(p\le n\), \(H^{2p}(X,\Z)\) has rank 1 and is purely of type \((p,p)\); if \(\eta\in H^2(X,\Z)\) is the cohomology class of a hyperplane section, then \(H^{2p}(X,\Q)=\Q\cdot\eta^p\).
\end{enumerate}
The list of possible values of \((n,\underline a)\) appears in [10].

The \emph{intermediate Jacobian} \(J(X)\) of \(X\) is, by definition, the complex torus
\[
J(X)=H^n(X,\Z)\backslash H^n(X,\C)/H^{m+1,m}(X)
     =H^{m,m+1}/H^n(X,\Z).
\]
The cup product on \(H^n(X,\Z)\) defines a Riemann form on \(J(X)\), which is therefore an abelian variety polarized in the principal series (=principally polarized). We show that the construction \(X\mapsto J(X)\) is ``purely algebraic'', i.e. independent of the topology of \(\C\). In particular, if \(X\) is defined over a field \(k\subset\C\), then \(J(X)\) is defined over the same field \(k\). In the case \(n=1\) (which we exclude below only for reasons of convenience), \(J(X)\) is the Jacobian of \(X\). For \(V_3(3)\), \(J(X)\) is the Jacobian of the Fano surface of \(X\) (see [1]). For \(n=3\) and \(X\) unirational, Manin's methods [8] make it possible to express \(J(X)\) using Jacobians. For \(V_n(2,2)\), if \((L_t)_{t\in\Pj^1}\) is

\sourcebreak
% physical page 2 / printed 238 / collected 290
a Lefschetz pencil of hyperplane sections, and if \(C\) is the covering of \(\Pj^1\) whose points are the linear subspaces of dimension \((n-1)/2\) in the \(L_t\), then \(J(X)\) is, up to isogeny, a direct factor of the Jacobian of \(C\). In the other cases, I know no direct interpretation of \(J(X)\). According to Lieberman [7], the Hodge conjecture implies that \(J(X)\) is the quotient of the set of algebraic cycles on \(X\), of codimension \((n+1)/2\) and homologous to zero, by a suitable equivalence relation.

As a corollary of the theory, one obtains (for \(n\) and \(\underline a\) fixed as above) that smooth complete intersections of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) over a finite field \(\F_q\) satisfy the Weil conjecture. For \(V_5(3)\) and \(V_3(4)\), this result is, to my knowledge, new.

\begin{center}\bfseries 1. Over \(\C\)\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{1.1.} Let \(X\) be a nonsingular connected projective complex algebraic variety of dimension \(k\). Duality theory for coherent algebraic sheaves provides an isomorphism (purely algebraic)
\[
\tag{1.1.1}
H^k(X,\Omega_X^k)\iso\C.
\]
On the other hand, Hodge theory (here GAGA + the Poincaré lemma) provides an isomorphism
\[
\tag{1.1.2}
H^k(X,\Omega_X^k)\iso H^{2k}(X,\C).
\]
The image under (1.1.1) of integral cohomology \(H^{2k}(X,\Z)\subset H^{2k}(X,\C)\) is the lattice \((2\pi i)^{-k}\Z\subset\C\).

This leads to the definition of the \emph{Tate Hodge structure} \(\Z(s)\) as the rank-one Hodge structure, pure of type \((-s,-s)\), with integral lattice
\[
\Z(s)_\Z=(2\pi i)^s\Z\subset\Z(s)_\C=\C.
\]
The cup product defines a morphism of Hodge structures
\[
\tag{1.1.3}
H^k(X,\Z)\otimes H^k(X,\Z)\longrightarrow\Z(-k).
\]
The analogues of (1.1.3) in \(\ell\)-adic or De Rham cohomology are as follows.

\noindent\textbf{1.2.} Recall that \(\Z/r(1)\) denotes the group of \(r\)-th roots of unity. We identify \(\Z(1)_{\Z/r}\dfneq\Z(1)_\Z\otimes\Z/r\) with \(\Z/r(1)\) by the map \(x\mapsto\exp(x/r)\). For every prime number \(\ell\), put
\[
\Z_\ell(1)=\varprojlim_r\Z/\ell^r(1)
\qquad\text{(transition maps \(x\mapsto x^{\ell^{r'-r}}\)).}
\]

\sourcebreak
% physical page 3 / printed 239 / collected 291
The exponential map identifies \(\Z(1)_{\Z_\ell}\) with \(\Z_\ell(1)\) and \(\Z(s)_{\Z_\ell}\) with
\[
\Z_\ell(s)\dfneq\Z_\ell(1)^{\otimes s}.
\]
If (1.1.3) is tensored with \(\Z_\ell\), the resulting morphism
\[
\tag{1.2.1}
H^k(X,\Z_\ell)\otimes H^k(X,\Z_\ell)\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(-k)
\]
is just the cup product in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology. It has a purely algebraic meaning.

\noindent\textbf{1.3.} One has
\[
\tag{1.3.1}
H^k(X,\Z)\otimes\C=H^k(X,\Omega_X^*)\dfneq H^k_{\DR}(X).
\]
Here and below, we shall always identify \(\Z(k)_\C=\Z(k)_\Z\otimes\C\) with \(\C\) by the ``defining'' isomorphism that sends the integral lattice to \((2\pi i)^k\Z\). Tensor (1.1.3) with \(\C\). The resulting morphism
\[
\tag{1.3.2}
H^k_{\DR}(X)\otimes H^k_{\DR}(X)\longrightarrow\C
\]
is just the cup product in De Rham cohomology. It has a purely algebraic meaning.

\noindent\textbf{1.4.} Take \(X\) to be a complete intersection \(V_n(\underline a)\). Tensor (1.1.3) by \(\Z(2m)\) and multiply the resulting morphism by \((-1)^m\). One obtains
\[
\tag{1.4.1}
\psi:H^n(X)(m)\otimes H^n(X)(m)\longrightarrow\Z(-1).
\]
By hypothesis, the Hodge structure \(H^n(X)(m)\) is of type \((0,1)+(1,0)\). The form \(\psi\) is a polarization of \(H^n(X)(m)\), and, by Poincaré duality for \(X\), the alternating form \(\psi_\Z\) on the integral lattice \(H^n(X)(m)_\Z\) has discriminant one.

There therefore exists a (unique) abelian variety polarized in the principal series \(J(X)\), endowed with an isomorphism of polarized Hodge structures
\[
\alpha:H^n(X)(m)\iso H^1(J(X)).
\]
We call \(J(X)\) the \emph{intermediate Jacobian} of \(X\).

\noindent\textbf{1.5.} Let \(S\) be a smooth scheme of finite type over \(\C\), and let \(f:X\to S\) be a proper smooth morphism whose fibers are complete intersections \(V_n(\underline a)\). Then \(R^nf_*\Z(m)\) is a variation of polarized Hodge structures on \(S\), of type \((0,1)+(1,0)\) (see [5] I 2). By a theorem of Borel (see [4] 5.1 and 5.2 for the statement), it defines an abelian scheme polarized in the principal series \(u:J(X/S)\to S\), endowed with an isomorphism

\sourcebreak
% physical page 4 / printed 240 / collected 292
of variations of polarized Hodge structures
\[
\alpha:R^nf_*\Z(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z.
\]
This construction expresses that \(J(X)\) varies algebraically with \(X\); it would not be difficult to extend it to the case where \(S\) is an arbitrary scheme over \(\C\).

The analogues in \(\ell\)-adic or De Rham cohomology of the variation of Hodge structures \(R^nf_*\Z(m)\) and of \(\alpha\) are as follows.

\noindent\textbf{1.6.} Tensoring with \(\Z_\ell\), \(\alpha\) defines an isomorphism of \(\Z_\ell\)-sheaves
\[
\tag{1.6.1}
\alpha_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_\ell.
\]
The cup product in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology, multiplied by \((-1)^m\), is a morphism of \(\Z_\ell\)-sheaves
\[
\tag{1.6.2}
\psi_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(1)\otimes R^nf_*\Z_\ell(1)\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(-1)
\]
and \(\alpha_\ell\) transforms \(\psi_\ell\) into the polarization form
\[
\tag{1.6.3}
{}_s\psi_\ell:R^1u_*\Z_\ell\otimes R^1u_*\Z_\ell\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(-1).
\]
The forms (1.6.2) and (1.6.3) have a purely algebraic meaning.

\noindent\textbf{1.7.} The locally free coherent analytic sheaf \(R^nf_*\Z\otimes\mathcal O\) underlies the coherent algebraic sheaf
\[
R^nf_*\Omega^*_{X/S}\dfneq H^n_{\DR}(X/S).
\]
The evident integrable connection on \(R^nf_*\Z\otimes\mathcal O\) is identified with the Gauss--Manin connection on \(H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\). Tensoring with \(\mathcal O\), \(\alpha\) defines an isomorphism of analytic sheaves
\[
\tag{1.7.1}
\alpha_{\DR}:H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\iso H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S).
\]
The Gauss--Manin connections of \(H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\) and \(H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\) are regular ([5] I 4 or [2] II \S 7), and \(\alpha_{\DR}\) is a horizontal isomorphism. Hence \(\alpha_{\DR}\) is algebraic ([2] II 5.9).

The cup product in De Rham cohomology, multiplied by \((-1)^m\), is a horizontal morphism
\[
\tag{1.7.2}
\psi_{\DR}:H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\otimes H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S
\]
and \(\alpha_{\DR}\) transforms \(\psi_{\DR}\) into the polarization form
\[
\tag{1.7.3}
{}_s\psi_{\DR}:H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\otimes H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S.
\]

\sourcebreak
% physical page 5 / printed 241 / collected 293
\noindent\textbf{1.8.} Let \(S_\Z\) be the subscheme of the Hilbert scheme (over \(\Z\)) that parametrizes smooth complete intersections of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) in \(\Pj^{n+v}\). Let \(f:X_\Z\to S_\Z\) be the ``universal'' family of \(V_n(\underline a)\) parametrized by \(S_\Z\). For every ring \(A\), we shall denote by \(f:X_A\to S_A\) the morphism obtained from \(f\) by extension of scalars from \(\Z\) to \(A\).

Put \(G=\PGL_{n+v+1}\). The group scheme \(G\) (over \(\Z\)) acts on \(X_\Z/S_\Z\) through its action on \(\Pj^{n+v}\).

\noindent\textbf{1.9. Proposition.} \emph{Let \(g:Y\to T\) be a proper smooth morphism whose geometric fibers are complete intersections \(V_n(\underline a)\).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Étale-locally on \(T\), \(Y\) is isomorphic to the intersection of \(v\) hypersurfaces \(H_1\ldots H_v\), of degrees \(a_1\ldots a_v\), in projective space \(\Pj_T^{n+v}\) over \(T\).}
\item \emph{In particular, étale-locally on \(T\), there exist \(u:T\to S_\Z\) and a \(T\)-isomorphism \(\iota:Y\iso u^*(X_\Z/S_\Z)\).}
\item \emph{If \((u',\iota')\) is another system as in (ii), then, étale-locally on \(T\), there exists \(a\in G(T)\) such that \((u',\iota')=a(u,\iota)\).}
\item \emph{\(S_\Z\) is smooth over \(\Spec(\Z)\).}
\end{enumerate}

In this paragraph, the only corollary of 1.9 that we shall not use is the smoothness of \(S_\C\). One could even dispense with it by subsequently replacing \(S_\Z\) by the scheme \(S'_\Z\) introduced below.

For every geometric fiber \(Y_{\bar t}\) of \(g\), one has \(H^1(Y_{\bar t},\mathcal O)=H^2(Y_{\bar t},\mathcal O)=0\), so that \(\Pic_T(Y)\) is étale over \(T\). Moreover, \(\Pic(Y_{\bar t})\simeq\Z\), generated by the class of the unique (very) ample invertible sheaf \(\mathcal O(1)\) of degree \(\prod a_i\). Étale-locally on \(T\), there is therefore on \(Y\) a relatively ample invertible sheaf of degree \(\prod a_i\), denoted \(\mathcal O(1)\). Locally on \(T\), it is unique. Since \(H^1(Y_{\bar t},\mathcal O(k))=0\) (FAC), \(g_*\mathcal O(k)\) is locally free and its formation is compatible with base change ([9] p. 19 or EGA III); hence \(\mathcal O(1)\) is very ample, and any choice of a basis of \(g_*\mathcal O(1)\) gives a diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=5.2em,row sep=3.4em]
Y \arrow[r,hook,"j"] \arrow[dr,"g"'] & \Pj_T^{n+v} \arrow[d,"p"]\\
& T
\end{tikzcd}
\]
with \(j^*\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal O(1)\).

We prove that \(j(Y)\) is an intersection of type (i); (iii) follows from the uniqueness of \(\mathcal O(1)\). The maps \(H^0(\Pj_{\bar t}^{n+v},\mathcal O(k))\to H^0(Y_{\bar t},\mathcal O(k))\) are surjective, hence so are \(p_*\mathcal O(k)\to g_*\mathcal O(k)\), and the kernel of this epimorphism of locally free sheaves is locally free; the assertion follows readily.

\sourcebreak
% physical page 6 / printed 242 / collected 294
Let \(\overline{S}'_\Z\) be the scheme over \(\Z\) parametrizing \(v\)-tuples of hypersurfaces \(H_i\subset\Pj^{n+v}\), with \(\deg(H_i)=a_i\). Let \(S'_\Z\) be the open subscheme of \(\overline{S}'_\Z\) parametrizing the \(v\)-tuples \((H_i)\) for which \(\bigcap H_i\) is smooth of dimension \(n\). The scheme \(S'_\Z\) is smooth over \(\Spec(\Z)\), being open in a product of projective spaces. The evident map from \(S'_\Z\) to \(S_\Z\) is surjective by (i). It is smooth (using (i), one readily verifies an infinitesimal criterion for smoothness). Hence \(S_\Z\) is smooth over \(\Spec(\Z)\).

Put \(S=S_\C\), \(X=X_\C\), and let \(f:X\to S\) be the universal family 1.8.

\noindent\textbf{1.10. Fundamental lemma.} \emph{Let \(a:A\to S\) be an abelian scheme over \(S\).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{If there exists a prime number \(\ell\) and an isomorphism of \(\Q_\ell\)-sheaves}
\[
 v_\ell:R^1u_*\Q_\ell\iso R^1a_*\Q_\ell,
\]
\emph{or if there exists a horizontal isomorphism}
\[
 v_{\DR}:H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\iso H^1_{\DR}(A/S),
\]
\emph{then \(A\) is isomorphic to \(J(X/S)\). The isomorphism}
\[
 v':J(X/S)\longrightarrow A
\]
\emph{is unique up to sign.}
\item \emph{If, in addition, \(A\) is endowed with a polarization in the principal series, then \(v'\) is compatible with the polarizations. If \(v_\ell\) (respectively \(v_{\DR}\)) as in (i) is compatible with the polarization forms, then \(v_\ell\) (respectively \(v_{\DR}\)) is induced by \(v'\) or by \(-v'\).}
\end{enumerate}

We shall use the following theorem of Lefschetz.

\noindent\textbf{1.11. Theorem (Lefschetz).} \emph{Let \(s\in S\), and let \(X_s=f^{-1}(s)\).}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{The monodromy representation of \(\pi_1(S,s)\) on \(H^n(X_s,\Q)\) is absolutely irreducible.}
\item \emph{For every prime number \(\ell\), the monodromy representation of \(\pi_1(S,s)\) on \(H^n(X_s,\Z/\ell)\) is absolutely irreducible.}
\end{enumerate}
For an exposition in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology of this theorem, following Lefschetz's proof very closely (cf. [6] Ch. V, III p. 106), see SGA 7 bis.

We prove 1.10. Consider the monodromy representations
\[
\rho_J:\pi_1(S,s)\longrightarrow\GL(H^1(J(X_s),\Z))\simeq\GL(H^n(X_s,\Z))
\]
\[
\rho_A:\pi_1(S,s)\longrightarrow\GL(H^1(A_s,\Z)).
\]
Hypothesis (i) implies that, after extension of scalars to \(\Q_\ell\) or to \(\C\), these representations become isomorphic. The isomorphism of two

\sourcebreak
% physical page 7 / printed 243 / collected 295
representations is invariant under extension of the base field; hence there exists a \(\pi_1(S,s)\)-equivariant isomorphism
\[
v'':H^1(J(X_s),\Q)\iso H^1(A_s,\Q).
\]
By 1.11(i), the image of \(v''^{-1}(H^1(A_s,\Z))\cap H^1(J(X_s),\Z)\) in \(H^1(J(X_s),\Z/\ell)\) is all or nothing. It follows that a rational multiple of \(v''\) is an isomorphism
\[
v'_\Z:H^1(J(X_s),\Z)\iso H^1(A_s,\Z).
\]
By 1.11(i), \(v'_\Z\) is uniquely determined up to sign.

By [3] 4.4.11 and 4.4.12, \(v'_\Z\) comes from an isomorphism of abelian schemes
\[
v':J(X/S)\longrightarrow A,
\]
which, like \(v'_\Z\), is unique up to sign. We recall the principle of the proof. Interpret \(v'_\Z\) as a section of the local system \(\Hom(R^1u_*\Z,R^1a_*\Z)\); by 1.11(i), it is the unique section of this local system up to a scalar. It is also known ([3] 4.12) that the module of all sections defines, at each point of \(s\), a Hodge substructure of \(\Hom(H^1(J(X_s),\Z),H^1(A_s,\Z))\). It follows that \(v'_\Z\) is at each point of \(S\) of Hodge type \((0,0)\), i.e. a morphism of Hodge structures, and the assertion follows.

We prove (ii). By 1.11(i), every polarization \(\psi'\) of \(J(X/S)\) is a rational multiple \(b\,{}_s\psi\) of the given polarization. If \(\psi'\) is in the principal series, \(b\) is a positive invertible integer, i.e. \(\psi'={}_s\psi\), which proves the first assertion. As for the second, a priori by 1.11(i) \(v_\ell=bv'_\ell\) with \(b\in\Q_\ell^*\) (respectively \(v_{\DR}=bv'_{\DR}\) with \(b\in\C^*\)); compatibility with the forms \(\psi_\ell\) or \(\psi_{\DR}\) gives \(b^2=1\), i.e. \(b=\pm1\).

\noindent\textbf{1.12.} By 1.11(i), the isomorphism \(\alpha_\ell\) is characterized up to sign by the fact that it carries \(\psi_\ell\) to \({}_s\psi_\ell\) (1.6). It would be easy to adapt (and simplify) the arguments of [4] \S6 to deduce from this that complete intersections \(V_n(\underline a)\) over finite fields satisfy the Weil conjectures. The same method would prove the following, perhaps more general, result.

\noindent\textbf{1.13. Theorem.} \emph{Let}
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=5em,row sep=3.2em]
X \arrow[r,hook] \arrow[dr] & \Pj_V^r \arrow[d]\\
& \Spec(V)
\end{tikzcd}
\]
\begin{itshape}
be a smooth projective variety of dimension \(2m+2\) over a discrete valuation ring \(V\) of unequal characteristic with finite residue field \(\F_q\). Let \(X_0\)

\sourcebreak
% physical page 8 / printed 244 / collected 296
and \(X_K\) be the special and general fibers of \(X\). Suppose that the ``new'' part of the cohomology of the smooth hyperplane sections \(H\) of \(X_K\) has level \(\leq1\) in the following sense.
\[
(*)\quad \text{For }p+q=2m+1\text{ and }|q-p|>1,\text{ the cokernel}
\]
\[
\Coker\bigl(H^q(X_K,\Omega^p_{X_K})\longrightarrow H^q(H,\Omega^p_H)\bigr)
\]
is zero.

Then, for every smooth hyperplane section \(H_0\) of \(X_0\), the eigenvalues of the Frobenius endomorphism on
\[
\Coker\bigl(H^{2m+1}(X_0,\Z_\ell)\longrightarrow H^{2m+1}(H_0,\Z_\ell)\bigr)
\]
are algebraic integers all of whose complex conjugates have absolute value \(q^{(2m+1)/2}\). These algebraic integers are divisible by \(q^m\).
\end{itshape}

\begin{center}\bfseries 2. Over \(\Q\)\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{2.1.} Let \(\ell_0\) be a prime number. For every extension \(K\) of \(\Q\), consider the following problem:

\noindent\((J,K)\)\quad
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.82\linewidth}
Construct a principally polarized abelian scheme \(u:J_K\longrightarrow S_K\) over \(S_K\) (1.8), endowed with an isomorphism
\[
\alpha_{\ell_0}:R^nf_*\Z_{\ell_0}(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_{\ell_0}
\]
which transforms \(\psi_{\ell_0}\), defined as in (1.6.2), into the polarization form.
\end{minipage}

For \(K=\C\), problem \((J,K)\) has, by 1.10, a solution unique up to unique isomorphism.

\noindent\textbf{2.2.} A general principle says that a problem \(P\), defined over a subfield \(k\) of \(\C\), and having over \(\C\) a solution unique up to unique isomorphism, has a solution (equally unique) over \(k\). Here this gives an abelian scheme \(u:J_\Q\longrightarrow S_\Q\) over \(S_\Q\), endowed with a principal polarization \({}_s\psi\) and an isomorphism \(\alpha_{\ell_0}\) as in \((J,K)\). Moreover, the triple \((J_\Q,{}_s\psi,\alpha_{\ell_0})\) is unique up to unique isomorphism.

\noindent\textbf{2.3.} The preceding principle is justified as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item If the problem \(P\) is to find a number \(a\), with \(a\) having some geometric property, the principle is evident: \(k\) is the field of invariants of \(\Aut(\C/k)\), and a solution \(a\) of \(P\) over \(\C\), being unique, is invariant under \(\Aut(\C/k)\). ``Geometric'' means: ``invariant under extension of scalars.''
\item If the problem \(P\) is to find a vector subspace of a vector space defined over \(k\), having some geometric property, apply the same argument, in the form Bourbaki, Algebra, chap. 2, \S8, Th. 1, to \(\Aut(\C/k)\). It is not required that \(V\) be finite-dimensional.

\sourcebreak
% physical page 9 / printed 245 / collected 297
\item If the problem \(P\) is to find a subscheme \(Y\) of an affine scheme \(W\) defined over \(k\), having some geometric properties, apply b): the ideal of \(Y\) is a vector subspace of the coordinate ring.
\item If, in c), \(W\) were not affine, one reduces to c) by gluing.
\item For every integer \(b\geq3\), let \(M_b\) be the scheme over \(S_{\Z[1/b]}\) whose sections over any \(S_{\Z[1/b]}\)-scheme \(t:T\longrightarrow S_{\Z[1/b]}\) classify principally polarized abelian schemes \(a:A\longrightarrow T\) endowed with an isomorphism
\[
t^*R^nf_*\Z/b(m)\iso R^1a_*\Z/b.
\]
(This isomorphism is compatible with the intersection forms.)

A solution of problem \((J,K)\) is interpreted as a section over \(S_K\) of
\[
M_{\ell_0^\infty}=\varprojlim_c M_{\ell_0^c}.
\]
Apply d) to this section, viewed as a subscheme of \((M_{\ell_0^\infty})_K\).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{2.4. Remark.} When problem \(P\) has a solution over a finite extension of \(k\), the principle stated above is proved by Galois descent. One can often reduce to this case using the finite-extension principle EGA IV 9.1.1.

Let \(\ell\) be a prime number. Let \(K\) be the function field of \(S_\Q\), and let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic closure of \(K\). Every \(\Z_\ell\)-sheaf \(F\) on \(S_\Q\) defines an \(\ell\)-adic representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on the geometric generic fiber \(F_{\overline K}\) of \(F\).

\noindent\textbf{2.5. Lemma.} \emph{The \(\ell\)-adic representations of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) defined by \(R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\) and by \(R^1u_*\Z_\ell\) have the same character.}

Let \(U\) be a Zariski open subset of \(S_\Z\) on which \(\ell\) and \(\ell_0\) are invertible, containing \(S_\Q\), and such that \(J_\Q\) is the restriction to \(S_\Q\) of an abelian scheme \(u':J'\longrightarrow U\). The \(\ell\)-adic representations 2.5 of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) are then unramified outside \(U\): they factor through a representation of \(\pi_1(U,\overline K)\). The same applies to their \(\ell_0\)-adic analogues. Each closed point \(s\) of \(U\) defines a conjugacy class of Frobenius substitutions \(\varphi_s\in\pi_1(U,\overline K)\). One has
\[
\tag{2.5.1}
\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^1u_*\Z_\ell)_{\overline K}\bigr)
=\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^1u_*\Z_{\ell_0})_{\overline K}\bigr)\in\Z:
\]
this is the trace of the Frobenius endomorphism of \(J'_s\). Similarly,
\[
\tag{2.5.2}
\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m))_{\overline K}\bigr)
=\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^nf_*\Z_{\ell_0}(m))_{\overline K}\bigr)\in\Q:
\]
and both sides equal, by the Lefschetz trace formula in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology,
\[
\frac{1}{q^m}\bigl(\#\Pj(k(s))-\#X_s(k(s))\bigr).
\]

\sourcebreak
% physical page 10 / printed 246 / collected 298
By hypothesis,
\[
\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^1u_*\Z_{\ell_0})_{\overline K}\bigr)
=\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^nf_*\Z_{\ell_0}(m))_{\overline K}\bigr),
\]
whence, by (2.5.1) and (2.5.2),
\[
\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^1u_*\Z_\ell)_{\overline K}\bigr)
=\Tr\bigl(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m))_{\overline K}\bigr).
\]
One concludes by observing that the trace is a continuous function on \(\pi_1(U,\overline K)\), and that, by \v{C}ebotarev's density theorem, the Frobenius elements \(\varphi_s\) are dense in \(\pi_1(U,\overline K)\).

\noindent\textbf{2.6. Lemma.} \emph{The representations of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on \(R^nf_*\Q_\ell(m)\) and on \(R^nf_*\Z/\ell(m)\) are irreducible.}

Indeed, the image of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) in \(\GL(R^nf_*\Q_\ell(m))\), or in \(\GL(R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m))\), contains the image of the geometric fundamental group \(\pi_1(S_{\overline\Q},\overline K)\). By 1.11, the action of the latter is already irreducible.

From 2.5 and 2.6 it follows that the \(\ell\)-adic representations of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on \((R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m))_{\overline K}\) and on \((R^1u_*\Z_\ell)_{\overline K}\) are isomorphic. Every isomorphism between these representations comes from an isomorphism
\[
\alpha'_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_\ell.
\]
By 1.11, after extending scalars from \(\Q\) to \(\C\), \(\alpha'_\ell\) becomes an \(\ell\)-adic multiple of the isomorphism (1.6.1): \(\alpha'_\ell=b\alpha_\ell\), with \(b\in\Z_\ell^*\). Over \(\Q\), put \(\alpha_\ell=b^{-1}\alpha'_\ell\).

\noindent\textbf{2.7.} We have thus obtained:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item a principally polarized abelian scheme \(J_\Q\) over \(S_\Q\);
\item for every prime number \(\ell\), an isomorphism compatible with the intersection forms
\[
\alpha_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_\ell;
\]
\item an isomorphism
\[
J_\Q\times_{S_\Q}S_\C\iso J(X_\C/S_\C)
\]
which identifies the preceding isomorphisms with the isomorphisms (1.6.1).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{2.8.} For every extension \(K\) of \(\Q\), let \(W_K\) be the \(K\)-vector space of horizontal morphisms
\[
\alpha':H^n_{\DR}(X_K/S_K)\longrightarrow H^1_{\DR}(J_K/S_K).
\]
One has \(W_K=W_\Q\otimes K\). For \(K=\C\), \(W_\C\) is one-dimensional, and its nonzero elements are isomorphisms. Hence there exists an isomorphism
\[
\alpha':H^n_{\DR}(X_\Q/S_\Q)\iso H^1_{\DR}(J_\Q/S_\Q).
\]

\sourcebreak
% physical page 11 / printed 247 / collected 299
Moreover, \(\alpha'\) is unique up to multiplication by \(\lambda\in\Q^*\). Over \(\C\), one verifies that
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(\alpha'\) respects the Hodge filtrations on the two sides: \(\alpha'(F^{m+1})=F^1\);
\item \(\alpha'\) transforms \(\psi_{\DR}\) into a multiple of \({}_s\psi_{\DR}\):
\[
\alpha'(\psi_{\DR})=\mu(\alpha')\,{}_s\psi_{\DR}\qquad(\mu(\alpha')\in\Q^*).
\]
\end{enumerate}
One has \(\mu(\lambda\alpha')=\lambda^{-2}\mu(\alpha')\).

\noindent\textbf{2.9. Proposition.} \emph{The multiplier \(\mu(\alpha')\) is positive.}

This is checked after extension of scalars from \(\Q\) to \(\R\).

\noindent\textbf{2.10. Conjecture.} \emph{The multiplier \(\mu(\alpha')\) is a square.}

This conjecture is equivalent to the following statements.
\[
\tag{2.10.1}
\text{For a suitable choice of }\alpha',\text{ one has }\alpha'(\psi_{\DR})={}_s\psi_{\DR}.
\]
\[
\tag{2.10.2}
\text{Over }\C,\text{ the isomorphism deduced from }\alpha'\text{ transforms rational cohomology into rational cohomology.}
\]
\[
\tag{2.10.3}
\text{Via 2.7c), the isomorphism }\alpha_{\DR}\text{ (1.7.1) is defined over }\Q.
\]
That this conjecture follows from both the Hodge conjecture and the Tate conjecture is meagre consolation.

Recall that
\[
G=\PGL_{n+v+1}\qquad\text{acts on}\qquad X_\Q/S_\Q\quad(1.8).
\]

\noindent\textbf{2.11. Lemma.} \emph{There exists one and only one action of \(G\) on \(J_\Q/S_\Q\) extending the action of \(G\) on \(S_\Q\). For this action, the isomorphisms \(\alpha_\ell\) (2.7.b)) are equivariant.}

For every extension \(K\) of \(\Q\), consider the following problem.

\noindent\((I,K)\). Let \(\mu:G_K\times_K S_K\longrightarrow S_K\) be the action of \(G_K\) on \(S_K\). Find an isomorphism
\[
a:\mu^*J_K\iso \pr_2^*J_K
\]
which defines an action of \(G_K\) on \(J_K/S_K\). Since \(G\) is connected, the \(\alpha_\ell\) are automatically equivariant. From this, or from Mumford [9] 6.1 p. 115, it follows that \(a\), if it exists, is unique. By the principle stated in 2.2, it is enough to show that \(a\) exists over \(\C\). This follows from the fact that, over \(\C\), the intermediate Jacobian is defined up to unique isomorphism and from the fact that \(\mu^*(X_K/S_K)\) is given as isomorphic to \(\pr_2^*(X_K/S_K)\).

From 1.9 and 2.11 one obtains the following theorem.

\noindent\textbf{2.12. Theorem.} \emph{Up to unique isomorphism, there exists one and only one functor \(J\) of the following type, endowed with the following additional data.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \emph{For \(T\) a scheme of characteristic 0 and \(g:Y\longrightarrow T\) a proper smooth morphism with fibers \(V_n(\underline a)\), \(u:J(Y/T)\longrightarrow T\) is a principally polarized abelian scheme.}
\item \emph{\(J\) is compatible with base changes \(c:T'\longrightarrow T\): isomorphisms \(J(c^*Y/T')\iso c^*J(Y/T)\) are given.}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}\bfseries 3. Over \(\Z\)\end{center}

The theory over \(\Z\) is as unsatisfactory as it is in De Rham cohomology (2.8 to 2.10).

\noindent\textbf{3.1. Proposition.} \emph{There exists a proper birational morphism \(b:\widetilde S\longrightarrow S_\Z\) such that}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \emph{\(b\) induces an isomorphism of \(\widetilde S_\Q\) with \(S_\Q\);}
\item \emph{there exists a principally polarized abelian scheme \(\widetilde J\) over \(\widetilde S\), whose restriction to \(S_\Q\) is \(J_\Q\).}
\end{enumerate}

We use the following lemma.

\noindent\textbf{3.2. Lemma.} \emph{Let \(V\) be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\) of characteristic 0, and let \(g:\Spec(V)\longrightarrow S_\Z\) be a morphism. Then the abelian variety over \(K\) obtained by pullback from \(J_\Q\) has good reduction.}

Let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic closure of \(K\), let \(X_V\) be the inverse image of \(X\) over \(V\), let \(J_K\) be the inverse image of \(J\) over \(\Spec(K)\), and let \(X_{\overline K}\) and \(J_{\overline K}\) be the inverse images of \(X_V\) and \(J_K\) over \(\overline K\).

Let \(k\geq3\) be an integer invertible on \(V\). Since \(X_V\) is smooth over \(V\), the specialization theorem in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology implies that the \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\)-module \(H^n(X_{\overline K},\Z/k)(m)\) is unramified. By 2.7, this \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\)-module is isomorphic to \(H^1(J_{\overline K},\Z/k)\), and it remains to apply the Néron--Ogg--Shafarevich criterion for good reduction.

A short, but too complicated, method for deducing 3.1 from 3.2 is the following. Let \(k\geq3\), and let \(M_k\) be as in 2.3.e). Then \(J_\Q\) defines a section of \(M_k\) over \(S_\Q\). Let \(\widetilde S_k\) be the closure of this section in \(M_k\). To prove that \(b:\widetilde S_k\longrightarrow S_{\Z[1/k]}\) is proper, it is enough to test the valuative criterion of properness on discrete valuation rings whose fraction fields have characteristic 0; indeed, \(\widetilde S_{k\Q}\) is dense in \(\widetilde S_k\). For these rings, it suffices to apply 3.2.

It is easy to check that, over \(\Spec(\Z[1/k_1k_2])\), \(\widetilde S_{k_1}\) coincides with \(\widetilde S_{k_2}\). The \(\widetilde S_k\) glue to form the desired \(\widetilde S\).

\noindent\textbf{3.3.} I conjecture that in 3.1 one may take \(\widetilde S=S\). If this were so, 2.12 would remain true over \(\Z\).

One verifies easily:

\noindent\textbf{3.4. Proposition.} \emph{Let \(a:\widetilde J\longrightarrow\widetilde S\) be as in 3.1. For each prime number \(\ell\), the isomorphism \(\alpha_\ell\) (2.7) extends over \(\widetilde S[1/\ell]\) to}
\[
\alpha_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1a_*\Z_\ell.
\]

\noindent\textbf{3.5.} \emph{The Weil conjecture for the \(V_n(\underline a)\).}

Using 3.4 one can give a variant of the proof, indicated in 1.12, of the Weil conjecture for the \(V_n(\underline a)\).

Let \(X\) be a smooth complete intersection of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) in \(\Pj^{n+v}(\F_q)\). \(X\) corresponds to a point \(x\in S_\Z(\F_q)\).

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \emph{there exists a point \(\widetilde x\in\widetilde S(\F_q)\) above \(x\).}

Indeed, one may lift \(X\) to a complete intersection over the Witt vectors \(W(\F_q)\), which lifts \(x\) to \(x'\in S_\Z(W(\F_q))\). The point \(x'\) defines a point of \(\widetilde S(W(\F_q)\otimes\Q)\), and, since \(b\) is proper, this point extends to \(x''\in\widetilde S(W(\F_q))\). Reducing \(x''\) gives \(\widetilde x\).

\item Let \(J\) be the abelian variety over \(\F_q\), the fiber of \(\widetilde J\) at \(\widetilde x\). If \(\overline X\) and \(\overline J\) are obtained from \(X\) and \(J\) by extension of scalars from \(\F_q\) to one of its algebraic closures \(\overline{\F}_q\), then 3.4 gives an isomorphism
\[
H^n(\overline X,\Z_\ell)(m)\iso H^1(\overline J,\Z_\ell).
\]
This isomorphism commutes with the (geometric) Frobenius endomorphisms, so
\[
\tag{3.5.1}
\det(1-Ft,H^n(\overline X,\Z_\ell))
=\det(1-q^mFt,H^1(\overline J,\Z_\ell)).
\]
The Weil conjecture for \(X\) therefore follows from a theorem of Weil [11] for \(J\).
\end{enumerate}
