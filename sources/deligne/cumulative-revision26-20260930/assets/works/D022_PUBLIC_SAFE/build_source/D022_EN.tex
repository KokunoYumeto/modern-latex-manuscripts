\NumPara{1.} Let \(X\) be a complex algebraic variety (i.e., a separated scheme of finite type over \(\C\)). We also write \(X\) for the usual topological space \(X(\C)\) underlying \(X\). In this lecture, we describe a remarkable filtration on the rational cohomology groups of \(X\), the \emph{weight filtration}, and we give a collection of results concerning its properties. Its definition will be given in §12. For proofs, we refer to the works cited in the bibliography, where the theorems are often proved in more general settings; working over \(\C\) allows us to use simultaneously Hodge theory, actions of Galois groups, and resolution of singularities.

The weight filtration is a finite increasing filtration. We denote it by \(W\). It is also defined in relative situations (or in cohomology with proper support). It depends not only on the topological space \(X\), but also on the way in which \(X\) is realized as an algebraic variety. It is compatible with the Künneth isomorphisms (i.e., under the isomorphism \(H^*(X\times Y)=H^*(X)\otimes H^*(Y)\), one has
\[
W_j\bigl(H^*(X\times Y)\bigr)=\sum_{j'+j''=j}W_{j'}\bigl(H^*(X)\bigr)\otimes W_{j''}\bigl(H^*(Y)\bigr)
\]
and is functorial for algebraic morphisms. More precisely, if \(f:X\to Y\) is algebraic, then \(f^*:H^*(Y)\to H^*(X)\) is strictly compatible with the weight filtrations on \(H^*(Y)\) and \(H^*(X)\): if the class \(x\in H^i(X)\) lies in the image of \(f^*\), it has filtration \(\le i\) if and only if it is the image of a class of filtration \(\le i\). More generally, every natural map is strictly compatible with the weight filtrations.

\NumPara{3.} Roughly speaking, the weight filtration expresses how the cohomology of \(X\) can be built in terms of the cohomology of nonsingular projective varieties. Here are some examples.

\NamedPara{Example 3.1.} If \(X\) is proper (= compact, for example projective) and smooth (= nonsingular), then \(H^i(X)=_{\mathrm{def}}H^i(X,\Q)\) is pure of weight \(i\): \(\Gr_j^W(H^i(X))=0\) for \(i\ne j\). In other words, \(W_{i-1}(H^i(X))=0\) and \(W_i(H^i(X))=H^i(X)\).

\NamedPara{Example 3.2.} Let \(X\) be proper, smooth, connected, of dimension \(d\), and let \(P\) be a point of \(X\). Among the cohomology groups with support \(H^i_{\{P\}}(X)=H^i(X\mathbin{\mathrm{mod}}(X-\{P\}))\), only the one in degree \(2d\) is nonzero, and
\begin{equation}\tag{3.2.1}
H^{2d}_{\{P\}}(X)\simeq H^{2d}(X)=\Q.
\end{equation}
By our principles, \(H^{2d}_{\{P\}}(X)\) is therefore pure of weight \(2d\). The inverse of the isomorphism (3.2.1) may be viewed as a Thom--Gysin isomorphism
\[
\Q=H^0(P)\simeq H^{2d}_{\{P\}}(X);
\]
one observes that it does not preserve weights. The general situation is as follows: for \(Y\) a smooth subvariety of pure codimension \(d\) in a smooth variety \(X\), the Thom--Gysin isomorphism \(H^i(Y)\simeq H_Y^{i+2d}(X)\) carries \(W^k\) to \(W^{k+2d}\). Writing \((n)\) for a shift of \(2n\) in \(W\) (\(W(n)_k=W_{k-2n}\)), this becomes a filtered isomorphism
\[
H^i(Y)(-d)\simeq H_Y^{i+2d}(X).
\]

\NamedPara{Example 3.3.} Let \(X\) be proper and smooth, and let \(Y\) be a smooth (closed) subvariety of pure codimension \(d\). There is an exact sequence
\[
\cdots\to H^i(X)\to H^i(X-Y)\xrightarrow{\delta}H_Y^{i+1}(X)\to\cdots
\]
By 3.1 and 3.2, \(\Gr_j^W(H^i(X-Y))=0\) for \(j\ne i,i+1\); \(W_i\) is the image of \(H^i(X)\).

\NamedPara{Example 3.4.} Let \(X\) be proper and smooth except at an isolated singular point \(P\). Suppose that the variety \(\widetilde X\) obtained from \(X\) by blowing up \(P\) is smooth, and that the exceptional divisor \(D\), the inverse image of \(P\), is smooth: \(X\) is obtained from \(\widetilde X\) (proper and smooth) by contracting \(D\) (proper and smooth) to a point. The space \(X\) has the homotopy type of \([\widetilde X\cup(\text{a cone with base }D)]\), whose cohomology is computed by Mayer--Vietoris; one obtains an exact sequence
\[
\cdots\to H^{i-1}(D)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^i(X)\to H^i(\widetilde X)\oplus H^i(P)\to\cdots
\]
which shows that \(\Gr_j^W(H^i(X))=0\) for \(j\ne i-1,i\); \(W_{i-1}\) is the image of \(\delta\). For

\NamedPara{Example 3.5.} Flag varieties are proper and smooth. The weight filtration on their cohomology is therefore given by 3.1. If \(G\) is a connected reductive group, the same result holds for the cohomology of \(BG\) [1, III]. This makes it possible to calculate the weight filtration on the cohomology of \(G\), which is related by transgression to that of \(BG\). One finds that \(W_iH^i(G)=0\), and that \(W_{i+1}H^i(G)\), zero for even \(i\), is equal to the primitive part of the degree-\(i\) cohomology of \(G\) [1, III]. If \(f:G\to H\) is an algebraic map between varieties of reductive groups, the inverse image of a primitive rational cohomology class of \(H\) is therefore again primitive. For other corollaries, see [1, III].

\NumPara{4.} The weight filtration admits a grading in a very strong sense. There exist gradings \(\Wg\) of the \(H^i(X)\) that split \(W\):
\begin{equation}\tag{4.1}
W_n(H^i(X))=\bigoplus_{j\le n}\Wg_j(H^i(X))
\end{equation}
and that are compatible with the cup product and with higher operations derived from the cup product (Massey products, \ldots). Since the latter are not defined everywhere, the meaning of “compatible with a grading” must be made precise. The simplest approach is to regard a grading as an action of the group \(G_m\), i.e., an action of \(\Q^*\) given by algebraic formulas: to a grading \(\Wg\) one associates the action in which \(\lambda\in\Q^*\) acts on \(\Wg_j\) by multiplication by \(\lambda^j\). “Compatibility” means that \(\Q^*\) acts by automorphisms of \(H^*(X)\), together with its cohomological grading, cup product, and the higher operations derived from the cup product.

\NumPara{5.} For simplicity, suppose that \(X\) is connected, and let \(\Mmin\) be the minimal model of the rational homotopy type of \(X\) in the sense of Sullivan [7]. It is a differential graded algebra in degrees \(\ge0\), connected (\(\Mmin^0=\Q\)), free (anti)commutative as a graded algebra, and generated by its indecomposable elements (i.e., \(d\Mmin\subset(\Mmin^{>0})^2\)). One has \(H^*(\Mmin)=H^*(X)\), and if \(X\) is simply connected, \(\Mmin^{>0}/(\Mmin^{>0})^2=(\pi_*(X)\otimes\Q)^\vee\).

A more precise, and more convenient, statement than the one in §4 is that there exists a grading \(\Wg\) satisfying (4.1), induced by a grading \(\Wg\) of \(\Mmin\) (in degrees \(\ge0\), the sum of gradings of the \(\Mmin^i\)), such that \(d\) and the product are homogeneous of degree 0; in other words, \(\Q^*\) acts by automorphisms of \(\Mmin\)).
