\paragraph{(1.24) Proposition.} Let \(A\) be a chamber, let \(P\) be an intersection of walls of \(A\), let \(F=F(P)\) (1.6), and let \(g\) be an equivalence class of galleries with source \(A\). There exists a gallery class \(g'\) of \((V_P,\mathcal M_P)\), with source \(\pi_F^{-1}(A)\), such that, for every gallery \(H\) of \((V_P,\mathcal M_P)\), \(g\) begins with \(\pi_F(H)\) if and only if \(g'\) begins with \(H\).

Let \(\mathcal S\) be the set of gallery classes \(h\) with source \(\pi_F^{-1}(A)\) in \((V_P,\mathcal M_P)\) such that \(g\) begins with \(\pi_F(h)\). Apply (1.14) to \((V_P,\mathcal M_P)\) and to \(\mathcal S\). By (1.19), condition (i) is satisfied, cf. the proof of (1.20), and (1.24) follows from (1.14)(ii).

\paragraph{(1.25)} The set of galleries may be regarded as the set of arrows of a category \(\operatorname{Gal}_0(V,\mathcal M)\) whose objects are the chambers. Similarly, equivalence classes of galleries are the arrows of a quotient category \(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V,\mathcal M)\). Let \(A,B,C\) be three chambers, let \(E\) be a gallery from \(A\) to \(B\), and let \(F\) be a gallery from \(B\) to \(C\). Although the general categorical convention is different, we shall continue to write \(EF\) for the composite of \(E\) and \(F\). The law \(G\mapsto G^*\), respectively \(G\mapsto -G\), is an anti-equivalence, respectively an equivalence, of \(\operatorname{Gal}_0(V,\mathcal M)\), or of \(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V,\mathcal M)\), with itself, inducing the identity, respectively \(C\mapsto -C\), on objects.

When no confusion is possible, we shall write simply \(\operatorname{Gal}_0\) and \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\) for \(\operatorname{Gal}_0(V,\mathcal M)\) and \(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V,\mathcal M)\), or for a category \(\operatorname{Gal}_0(V_P,\mathcal M_P)\) or \(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V_P,\mathcal M_P)\). By abuse of language, we shall often call the arrows of \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\) galleries.

\paragraph{(1.26) Lemma.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item In \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\), for every gallery \(g\), one has
\[
g\Delta=\Delta(-g).
\]
\item For any \(g\) and \(h\) with source \(A\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\), there exists \(n\) such that \(g\Delta^n\) begins with \(h\). If \(h\) is composed of \(k\) galleries \(u(A_i,A_{i+1})\), one may take \(n=k\).
\end{enumerate}

By induction, it is enough to prove (i) when \(g\) has length one. For \(g=(B,C)\), one has
\[
g\Delta=u(B,C)u(C,-C)=u(B,C)u(C,-B)u(-B,-C)
=u(B,-B)u(-B,-C)=\Delta(-g).
\]
For every chamber \(B\), \(\Delta=u(A,-A)\) begins with \(u(A,B)\). Hence (ii) follows from (i) by induction on \(k\).

The following proposition follows at once from (1.26), from its transform by \(*\), and from (1.19)(i),(ii).

\paragraph{(1.27) Proposition.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item In \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\), the set of all arrows admits a left and right calculus of fractions.
\item Let \(\operatorname{Gal}(V,\mathcal M)\), or simply \(\operatorname{Gal}\), be the category, a groupoid, obtained from \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\) by making all arrows invertible. Call positive those arrows of \(\operatorname{Gal}\) lying in the image of \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\). The canonical functor from \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\) to \(\operatorname{Gal}\) is faithful, and every arrow \(g\) of \(\operatorname{Gal}\) can be written in the forms
\[
g=g_1\Delta^{-n}=\Delta^{-n}g_2
\]
with \(g_1\) and \(g_2\) positive.
\end{enumerate}

If a category \(C\) has only one object \(A\), and if the monoid \(\operatorname{Hom}(A,A)\) is cancellative, to say that all arrows of \(C\) admit a left and right calculus of fractions means that \(\operatorname{Hom}(A,A)\) satisfies Ore's condition on the left and on the right. The calculus of fractions used in (1.27) and below is an immediate generalization of Ore's theory for embedding such a monoid in a group.

\paragraph{(1.28)} For every wall \(M\), the number of times that \(g\in\operatorname{Gal}^+\) crosses \(M\) is well-defined (1.11). This function of \(g\) extends additively to \(g\in\operatorname{Gal}\). More generally, for every intersection of walls \(P\), one could use the universal property of \(\operatorname{Gal}\) to define functors
\[
\pi_P:\operatorname{Gal}(V,\mathcal M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(V_P,\mathcal M_P).
\]

\paragraph{(1.29)} Let \(A\) be a chamber, let \(P\) be an intersection of walls of \(A\), and let \(F=F(P)\) (1.6). The function \(\pi_F\) induces a functor
\[
\tag{1.29.1}
\pi_F:\operatorname{Gal}_0(V_P,\mathcal M_P)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}_0(V,\mathcal M).
\]
The image of this functor is stable under equivalence, and it induces a faithful functor
\[
\tag{1.29.2}
\pi_F:\operatorname{Gal}^+(V_P,\mathcal M_P)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}^+(V,\mathcal M).
\]
From the calculus of fractions and (1.19)(i) follows the following proposition.

\paragraph{(1.30) Proposition.} Under the preceding hypotheses, the functor deduced from (1.29.2)
\[
\pi_F:\operatorname{Gal}(V_P,\mathcal M_P)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(V,\mathcal M)
\]
is faithful.

\paragraph{(1.31) Proposition.} Let \(C\) be a chamber; let \(I\) and \(J\) be two sets of walls of \(C\); put \(K=I\cup J\); let \(P,Q,R\) be the intersections of the walls in \(I,J,K\), and let \(F(P),F(Q),F(R)\) be the corresponding facets in \(C\) (1.6). The facet \(F(R)\) is the smallest facet containing \(F(P)\) and \(F(Q)\). In the groupoid \(\operatorname{Gal}(V,\mathcal M)\), one has
\[
\pi_{F(P)}(\operatorname{Gal}(V_P,\mathcal M_P))\cap
\pi_{F(Q)}(\operatorname{Gal}(V_Q,\mathcal M_Q))
=
\pi_{F(R)}(\operatorname{Gal}(V_R,\mathcal M_R)).
\]

A chamber having \(F(P)\) and \(F(Q)\) as facets also has \(F(R)\) as a facet. This proves (1.31) for objects.

Let \(g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(A,B)\), and suppose that
\[
g=\pi_{F(P)}(g_1)=\pi_{F(Q)}(g_2).
\]
By (1.27)(ii), one has
\[
g_1=\Delta(P)^{-n}g'_1,\qquad
g_2=\Delta(Q)^{-n}g'_2,
\]
with \(g'_i\) positive, for \(n>0\) sufficiently large. Let \(g_1=\pi_{F(P)}(g'_1)\) and \(g_2=\pi_{F(Q)}(g'_2)\). By (1.26)(ii), transformed by \(*\), \(\Delta^n\Delta(P)^{-n}\) is positive, and in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V,\mathcal M)\) one has
\[
\tag{1.31.1}
(\Delta^n\Delta(P)^{-n})g_1=(\Delta^n\Delta(Q)^{-n})g_2.
\]

\paragraph{(1.31.2) Lemma.} If \(h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}^+}(A,B)\) begins both with \(\Delta^n\Delta(P)^{-n}\) and with \(\Delta^n\Delta(Q)^{-n}\), then \(h\) begins with \(\Delta^n\Delta(R)^{-n}\).

We deduce (1.31) from (1.31.2). By (1.31.2), one has
\[
(\Delta^n\Delta(P)^{-n})g_1=(\Delta^n\Delta(Q)^{-n})g_2=(\Delta^n\Delta(R)^{-n})g_3
\]
with \(g_3\) positive. Hence \(g=\Delta(R)^{-n}g_3\). The positive gallery \(g_3=\Delta(R)^n g\) lies in the image of both \(\pi_{F(P)}\) and \(\pi_{F(Q)}\). By (1.28), it therefore crosses zero times every wall not containing \(P\) or \(Q\), i.e. every wall not containing \(R\). It thus lies in the image of \(\pi_{F(R)}\).

We prove (1.31.2). Let \(A(P)=A.\Delta.A(P)\), and similarly for \(Q\) and \(R\). The gallery \(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1}\) with source \(A\) is \(u(A,A(P))\); the one with source \(A(P)\) is \(u(A(P),A)\). By (1.26)(i), \(\Delta\Delta(P)=\Delta(P)\Delta\). Hence
\[
\tag{1.31.3}
\Delta^n\Delta(P)^{-n}=u(A,A(P))\,u(A(P),A)\,u(A,A(P))\cdots
\]
with \(n\) factors.

\paragraph{(1.31.4) Lemma.} \(A(R)\in D(A(P),A(Q))\).

With the notation of (1.2), \(\mathcal M(A,A(P))\) is the set of walls containing \(P\), and \(\mathcal M(A,A(Q))\) is the set of those containing \(Q\). By (1.2), \(\mathcal M(A(P),A(Q))\) is the set of walls containing \(P\) or \(Q\), but not \(R\), namely
\[
\mathcal M(A(P),A(R))\cup \mathcal M(A(Q),A(R)),
\]
and one applies (1.4).

By (1.19)(iii), a gallery class which begins with \(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1}\) and \(\Delta\Delta(Q)^{-1}\) also begins with \(\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1}\), and (1.31.2) follows by induction from the following lemma.

\paragraph{(1.31.5) Lemma.} Let \(P\) and \(R\) be intersections of walls of a chamber \(A\), with \(P\subset R\). Let \(h\) be in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\) with source \(A\). If \(h\) begins both with \(\Delta^n\Delta(P)^{-n}\), \(n>0\), and with \(\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1}\), then \(h\) begins with
\[
(\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1})(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1})^{n-1}.
\]
One may assume \(n>2\). Put \(h=(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1})h'\). With the preceding notation, \(h'\) then begins both with \(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1}=u(A(P),A)\) and with \(u(A(P),A(R))\). Apply (1.19)(iii). The chamber \(C\) of loc. cit. is then separated from \(A(P)\) by every wall \(M\) separating \(A(P)\) from \(A\), i.e. \(M\not\supset P\), or \(A(P)\) from \(A(R)\), i.e. \(M\supset P\) and \(M\not\supset R\). Thus \(C\) is separated from \(A(P)A\) only by walls \(M\supset R\), and
\[
C\in D(A(P)A,A(P)A(R)).
\]
It follows that \(h'\) begins both with \(\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1}\) and with \((\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1})^{n-1}\). Induction on \(n\) shows that \(h'\) begins with
\[
(\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1})(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1})^{n-2},
\]
so that \(h\) begins with
\[
(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1})(\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1})(\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1})^{n-2}.
\]
One concludes by noting that
\[
\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1}\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1}
=\Delta\Delta(R)^{-1}\Delta\Delta(P)^{-1}.
\]

\paragraph{(1.32) Proposition.} For \(F\) a facet of a chamber \(C\), generating an intersection of walls \(P\), one has
\[
\pi_F^{-1}(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V,\mathcal M))
=
\operatorname{Gal}^+(V_P,\mathcal M_P)\subset \operatorname{Gal}(V_P,\mathcal M_P).
\]
Suppose that \(\pi_F(\Delta^{-n}g)=h\) lies in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V,\mathcal M)\). Then \(\pi_F(g)=\Delta(P)^n h\), and by (1.28) every gallery \(H\) representing \(h\) crosses only walls passing through \(F\). Thus \(h=\pi_F(h_1)\), with \(h_1\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+(V_P,\mathcal M_P)\), and the conclusion follows from (1.30).

\section*{2. Buildings}

\paragraph{(2.1)} Let \((V,\mathcal M)\) be as in §1, satisfying (1.8), and let \(r=\dim V\). Let \(S\) be the sphere of radius one in \(V\), for some Euclidean structure on \(V\); more intrinsically, one could take \(S=(V-\{0\})/\R^*_+\). The hyperplanes \(M\in\mathcal M\) cut out a triangulation of \(S\), and we shall again denote by \(S\) both the corresponding simplicial space and its geometric realization. We transfer to \(S\) the terminology used for \(V\): chambers, facets, adjacency, galleries.

\paragraph{(2.2)} Choose a fundamental chamber \(A_0\) in \(S\). We denote by \(I^+\) the space constructed below; it depends on \(V,\mathcal M,A_0\), and is equipped with \(q:I^+\to S\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item Let \(\mathcal G^+\) be the set of equivalence classes of galleries \(g\) with source \(A_0\),
\[
\mathcal G^+=\coprod_B\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}^+}(A_0,B).
\]
\item Let \(Z^+\) be the disjoint sum, indexed by \(g\in\mathcal G^+\), of the closed simplex of \(S\) which is the closure of the open simplex of dimension \(r-1\) equal to the target of \(g\):
\[
Z^+=\coprod_{g\in\mathcal G^+}(\operatorname{target}g)^{-}.
\]
Let \(q'\) be the evident map from \(Z^+\) to \(S\).
\item \(I^+\), with \(q\), is a quotient of \(Z^+\), with \(q'\). If \(G\) is a gallery from \(A_0\) to \(B\), and if \(C\) is a chamber adjacent to \(B\), one glues \((\operatorname{target}g)^{-}\) and \((\operatorname{target}g(BC))^{-}\) along the common closed face of their images in \(S\).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(2.3)} The space \(I^+\) is decomposed into facets, images of facets of \(Z^+\), each mapping bijectively to a facet of \(S\). Its chambers, respectively faces and vertices, are its facets of dimension \(r-1\), respectively \(r-2\) and \(0\).

The vertices of a facet \(F\) are the vertices contained in the closure \(\overline F\) of \(F\). The chambers of \(I^+\) are indexed by \(\mathcal G^+\); \(A_0.g\) denotes the one indexed by \(g\), and \(A_0\) the one indexed by \((A_0)\).

Let \(g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}^+}(A_0,A)\), let \(A=A_0.g\), and let \(H=(C_0,\ldots,C_n)\) be a gallery from \(A\) to \(B\), with class \(h\). Put
\[
A.h=A_0.gh.
\]
The chambers \(A.(C_0,\ldots,C_i)\), \(0\le i\le n\), form a gallery in \(I^+\). Such galleries will be called positive.

\paragraph{(2.4) Lemma.} Let \(F\) be a facet of \(S\), let \(A\) and \(B\) be two chambers of \(S\) having \(F\) as a facet, and let \(g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}^+}(A_0,A)\), \(h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}^+}(A_0,B)\). The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item in \(I^+\), the facets \(q^{-1}(F)\) of \(A_0.g\) and \(A_0.h\) coincide;
\item \(g^{-1}h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(A,B)\) lies in \(\pi_F(\operatorname{Gal})\);
\item there are \(g_1\) and \(h_1\) in \(\pi_F(\operatorname{Gal}^+)\) such that \(gg_1=hh_1\).
\end{enumerate}
Condition (b) is an equivalence relation between galleries from \(A_0\) to a chamber having \(F\) as a facet. This gives (a) \(\Rightarrow\) (b). That (b) implies (c) follows from (1.26)(i). Finally, (c) \(\Rightarrow\) (a) is immediate from the definitions.

From (2.4), (a) \(\Leftrightarrow\) (b), and from (1.31), one immediately obtains:

\paragraph{(2.5) Proposition.} Each facet of \(I^+\) is uniquely determined by the set of its vertices. Thus \(I^+\) may be described as the geometric realization of the following simplicial scheme.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item For \(x\) a vertex of \(S\), let \(\mathcal G(x)\) be the set of \(g\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\) with source \(A_0\) and with target a chamber having \(x\) as a vertex. Let \(\mathcal G(x)/\pi_x\) be the quotient of \(\mathcal G(x)\) by the equivalence relation (2.4)(b), for \(F=x\). Then the set of vertices is
\[
\coprod_x \mathcal G(x)/\pi_x.
\]
\item A set \(E\) of vertices spans a simplex if and only if there exists a gallery \(g\) with source \(A_0\) such that, for \(y\in E\), \(g\) lies in \(\mathcal G(qy)\) and \(y\) is the image of \(g\) in \(\mathcal G(qy)/\pi_{qy}\).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(2.6) Proposition.} Let \(F\) be a facet of \(I^+\). There exists a gallery class \(g\) such that, for \(F\) to be a facet of a chamber \(B=A_0.h\), it is necessary and sufficient that
\[
h=g\,\pi_F(h')
\]
for a suitable \(h'\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\).

Take \(g\) to be a gallery of minimum length such that \(F\) is a facet of \(A=A_0.g\). Let \(B=A_0.h\) be a chamber having \(F\) as a facet. By (2.4)(a) \(\Leftrightarrow\) (c), there are galleries \(h_1\) and \(h_2\) in \((V_{qF},\mathcal M_{qF})\) with
\[
g\pi_F(h_1)=h\pi_F(h_2).
\]
Apply the transform by \(*\) of (1.24). By the minimality of \(g\), one finds that \(h_1\) ends with \(h_2\), say \(h_1=h'h_2\). By (1.19)(ii), \(g\pi_F(h')=h\), which proves (2.6).

\paragraph{(2.7) Notation.} If \(A\) is a chamber of \(I^+\), denote by \(S(A)\) the union of the closed chambers \((A.(qA,B))^{-}\), where \(B\) runs through the chambers of \(S\). One checks that \(q|S(A)\) is an isomorphism from \(S(A)\) to \(S\).

\paragraph{(2.8) Definition.} \(\overline I^{\, +}\) is the space obtained from \(I^+\) by filling in the spheres \(S(A)\).

More precisely, let \(B^r\) be a ball whose boundary is \(S\). If one wants to work simplicially, one takes \(B^r\) to be the cone on the simplicial space \(S\). Define \(\overline I^{\, +}\) from \(I^+\) by attaching a family of copies \(b(A)\) of \(B^r\), indexed by the chambers of \(I^+\).

\paragraph{(2.9.2) Lemma.} Suppose that there exists a chamber \(B=A_0.h\) in \((I^+)_{n+1}\), distinct from \(A=A_0.g\), having \(F\) as a facet. Then there exists a chamber \(B'=A_0.h'\) in \((I^+)_n\), and a wall \(M\) of \(qB'\), containing \(qF\), such that
\[
A=B'.\Delta(M).
\]
Let \(g_0\) be the gallery considered in (2.6). One has
\[
g=g_0\pi_F(g_1),\qquad h=g_0\pi_F(h_1).
\]
The hypotheses imply that \(\operatorname{lg}(g_1)\ne0\). For a suitable wall \(M\) of \(qA\), one then has \(g_1=h'\Delta(M)\), and one puts \(B'=\pi_Fh'\).

For the closed chamber \(\overline A\), \(\overline A\cap(I^+)_n\) is the union of a nonempty set of closed faces, and in \((I^+)_{n+1}\) the points of
\[
\overline A-(\overline A\cap(I^+)_n)
\]
belong only to the single closed chamber \(\overline A\). There are two cases.

Case 1. \(\overline A\cap(I^+)_n\ne\partial\overline A\). In this case, there is no sphere \(S(B)\) with
\[
A\subset S(B)\subset (I^+)_{n+1}.
\]
We take \(\varphi_t|\overline A\) to be a collapse of \(\overline A\) onto \(\overline A\cap(I^+)_n\).

Case 2. \(\overline A\cap(I^+)_n=\partial\overline A\). Put \(A=A_0.g\). For every wall \(M\) of \(qA\), it follows from (2.7.2) that \(g\) ends with \(\Delta(M)\). By (1.19)(iii), \(g\) therefore ends with \(\Delta\): \(A=B.\Delta\), with \(B\) uniquely determined by \(A\), by (1.19)(ii). In passing from \((\overline I^{\, +})_{n+1}\) to \((\overline I^{\, +})_n\), \(A\) and the interior of the ball \(b(B)\) disappear. We take \(\varphi_t|b(B)\) to be a collapse of \(b(B)\) onto \(S(B)-A\). As seen in case 1, every ball which disappears is of the preceding type. This completes the construction of \(\varphi_t\), and proves (2.7).

\paragraph{(2.10)} The space \(I\), with \(q:I\to S\), is defined like \(I^+\), replacing \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\) by \(\operatorname{Gal}\):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item put \(\mathcal G=\coprod_B\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(A_0,B)\);
\item put \(Z=\coprod_{g\in\mathcal G}(\operatorname{target}g)^{-}\);
\item let \(q'\) be the evident map from \(Z\) to \(S\). The space \(I\), with \(q\), is a quotient of \(Z\), with \(q'\). If \(g\in\mathcal G\) has target \(B\) and \(C\) is adjacent to \(B\), one glues \((\operatorname{target}g)^{-}\) and \((\operatorname{target}g(BC))^{-}\) along the common closed face of their images in \(S\).
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, if two chambers \(B\) and \(C\) have a common facet \(F\), if \(g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(A_0,B)\), and if \(h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(A_0,C)\), then, in \(I\), the closed facets \(q'^{-1}(F)^{-}\) of \((\operatorname{target}g)^{-}\) and of \((\operatorname{target}h)^{-}\) are identified if and only if
\[
hg^{-1}\in \pi_F\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(\pi_F^{-1}B,\pi_F^{-1}C).
\]

\paragraph{(2.11)} As for \(I^+\), one defines chambers, faces, and open or closed facets of \(I\). One defines \(A_0\), and \(A.h\), for \(A\) a chamber and \(h\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}\) with source \(qA\), as in (2.3). The analogue of (2.4)(a) \(\Leftrightarrow\) (b) is here trivially true. As in (2.5), one deduces from (1.31) that each facet of \(I\) is uniquely determined by its set of vertices, and \(I\) appears as the geometric realization of a simplicial scheme with a description of type (2.5). For every chamber \(A\) of \(I\), define a sphere \(S(A)\) as in (2.7); \(q\) induces an isomorphism from \(S(A)\) to \(S\). As in (2.8), define a building \(\overline I\), equipped with \(q:\overline I\to B^r\), by filling in all the spheres \(S(A)\).

\paragraph{(2.12)} Besides \(q\) and its chamber decomposition, \(I\) has the following additional structure. A composition \(B.g\) is defined, which associates to a chamber \(B\) of \(I\), whose image in \(S\) is \(B\), and to \(g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(B,C)\), a chamber of \(I\) whose image in \(S\) is \(C\). The construction \(g\mapsto B.g\) identifies the analogue of \(I\) obtained by choosing \(B\) as fundamental chamber with the space \(I\). In particular,
\[
B.(gh)=(B.g).h.
\]

\paragraph{(2.13)} For every chamber \(A\) of \(I\) above \(A_0\), define a map
\[
i_A:I^+\longrightarrow I
\]
and similarly \(i_A:\overline I^{\, +}\to\overline I\), compatible with the projection to \(S\), respectively \(B^r\), by sending the closed chamber \((A.g)^{-}\) of \(I^+\), respectively the ball \(b(A.g)\) of \(\overline I^{\, +}\), to the chamber, respectively ball, of the same name in \(I\), respectively \(\overline I\).

\paragraph{(2.14) Proposition.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(i_A\) identifies \(I^+\) with a closed subspace of \(I\), and \(\overline I^{\, +}\) with a closed subspace of \(\overline I\).
\item One has
\[
I=\varinjlim i_{A_0.\Delta^{-2n}}(I^+),
\qquad
\overline I=\varinjlim i_{A_0.\Delta^{-2n}}(\overline I^{\, +}).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The assertions (i) or (ii) for \(\overline I^{\, +}\) and \(\overline I\) follow from the corresponding assertions for \(I^+\) and \(I\).

We prove (i) for \(I^+\) and \(I\). Let \(B\) and \(C\) be two chambers of \(S\) with a common facet \(F\). Let \(g\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}^+}(A_0,B)\) and \(h\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}^+}(A,C)\). Suppose that the facets \(q^{-1}F\) of \((A.g)^{-}\) and \((A.h)^{-}\) are equal in \(I\). Then
\[
g^{-1}h=\pi_F(e)
\quad\text{for}\quad
e\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Gal}}(\pi_F^{-1}B,\pi_F^{-1}C).
\]
By (1.26), write \(e=e_1e_2^{-1}\), with \(e_i\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\). By (1.29), \(g\pi_F(e_1)=h\pi_F(e_2)\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\), and the facets \(q^{-1}F\) of \((A.g)^{-}\) and \((A.h)^{-}\) are therefore already equal in \(I^+\).

For (ii), it is enough to note that every gallery \(g\) with source \(A_0\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}\) can be written
\[
g=\Delta^{-2n}g'
\]
with \(g'\) in \(\operatorname{Gal}^+\).

From (2.14) one obtains \(\pi_i(I)=\varinjlim\pi_i(I^+)\). Since \(I\) is a CW-complex, (2.9) gives:

\paragraph{(2.15) Theorem.} The space \(\overline I\) is contractible.

The space \(I\) is therefore the bouquet of the spheres \(S(A)\), as \(A\) ranges through the chambers of \(I\).

\section*{3. Coverings}

\paragraph{(3.1)} Let \(V_\C\) be the complexification of \(V\), and let \(M_\C\) be the complexification of \(M\), for \(M\in\mathcal M\). Put
\[
Y=V_\C-\bigcup_{M\in\mathcal M}M_\C.
\]
In this section, we construct by gluing a space above \(Y\). The gluing data will be described using \(I\) and \(\overline I\). The facets of \(S\) and the facets of \(V\) other than \(\{0\}\) are in canonical bijection, and we shall constantly pass from one to the other. In general we shall use the same notation for the corresponding facets; when necessary the correspondence will be denoted \(F\mapsto [F]\).

\paragraph{(3.2) Lemma.} Let \(A,B,C\) be three chambers of \(I\), with \(C\subset S(A)\cap S(B)\). Then \(q\) induces an isomorphism from \(S(A)\cap S(B)\) to the intersection of \(S\) with the closed half-spaces \(D'_M(qC)\), containing \(qC\), bounded by a wall \(M\) which separates \(qA\) from \(qB\).

The intersection of the \(D'_M(qC)\) is a union of closed chambers. If \(C'\) is one of them, then \(qA\) and \(qB\) lie on the same side of every wall separating \(qC\) from \(C'\). It follows that the lifts, to \(S(A)\) or \(S(B)\), of a minimal gallery from \(qC\) to \(C'\) coincide, and \((C')^{-}\subset q(S(A)\cap S(B))\).

Conversely, if a facet \(F\) is not in the intersection of the \(D'_M(qC)\), then there is a wall \(M\) such that \(F\subset M\), separating \(qA\) from \(qB\) and \(F\) from \(qC\). Suppose, for contradiction, that \(F\subset q(S(A)\cap S(B))\). Let \(D\) be a chamber of \(S\) having \(F\) as a facet, and let \((C_0,C_1,\ldots,C_{n+1})\) be a gallery from \(qC\) to \(D\). Let \(M_i\) be the wall separating \(C_i\) from \(C_{i+1}\), and let \(\alpha_i\), respectively \(\beta_i\), be \(+1\) or \(-1\) according as \(C_{i+1}\) and \(A\), respectively \(B\), are or are not on the same side of \(M_i\). By hypothesis, \(F\) is a facet of
\[
C.\Delta(M_0)^{\alpha_0}\cdots \Delta(M_n)^{\alpha_n}
\]
and of
\[
C.\Delta(M_0)^{\beta_0}\cdots \Delta(M_n)^{\beta_n}.
\]
By (2.4) and (1.24), since \(M\not\supset F\), this gives contradictory equalities: one side equals \(1\), the other \(-1\).

\paragraph{(3.3) Lemma.} Let \(\Re\) and \(\Im\) be the real and imaginary part maps from \(V_\C\) to \(V\). Let \(v\in V_\C\), and let \(F\) be the facet of \(V\) such that \(\Re v\in F\). For \(v\) to belong to \(Y\), it is necessary and sufficient that \(\operatorname{pr}_F(\Im v)\) lie in a chamber of \((V_F,\mathcal M_F)\).

This is clear.

What follows is based on the following intuition: to every walk in \(I\), performed by following a gallery of the form
\[
\Delta(M_1)^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots \Delta(M_k)^{\varepsilon_k},\qquad \varepsilon_i=\pm1,
\]
there corresponds a path, or path class, in \(Y\). Its image under \(\Re\) in \(V\) passes from chamber to chamber, crossing successively the walls \(M_1,\ldots,M_k\) at points \(m_i\). For \(\varepsilon_i=1\), respectively \(-1\), the path passes through one of the two components \(R\) of \(\Re^{-1}(m_i)\cap Y\), in bijection by \(\Im\) with \(V-M_i\): the one such that \(\Im R\) contains, respectively does not contain, the preceding chamber.

\paragraph{(3.4) Notation.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If \(C\) is a chamber of \((V,\mathcal M)\), let \(Y(C)\) be the open subset of \(Y\) consisting of the \(x\in V_\C\) satisfying the following condition: if \(F\) is the facet of \(V\) such that \(\Re x\in F\), then \(\operatorname{pr}_F(\Im x)\in\pi_F^{-1}(C)\).
\item Let \(A\) and \(B\) be two chambers of \(I\). If \(S(A)\cap S(B)\) contains a chamber \(C\), temporarily denote by \(Y'(A,B)\) the intersection of the open half-spaces \(D'_M(qC)^\circ\) for \(M\) as in 3.2. Put
\[
Y(A,B)=Y(qA)\cap Y(qB)\cap \Re^{-1}(Y'(A,B))\subset V_\C.
\]
If \(S(A)\cap S(B)\) contains no chamber, put \(Y(A,B)=\varnothing\).
\item For a facet \(F\) of \(V\), the star \(\operatorname{Et}(F)\) is the union of the open facets \(G\) of \((V,\mathcal M)\) such that \(F\subset\overline G\).
\item For a facet \(F\) of \(V\) and a chamber \(C\) of \((V_F,\mathcal M_F)\), put
\[
V(F,C)=\{\,v\in V_\C\mid \Re v\in\operatorname{Et}(F),\ \operatorname{pr}_F(\Im v)\in C\,\}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(3.5) Lemma.} Let \(G\) be a facet of \(V\). The \(V(F,C)\), for \(F\) a facet such that \(G\subset\overline F\) and \(C\) a chamber of \(V_F\), form an open covering of
\[
Y\cap \Re^{-1}(\operatorname{Et}G).
\]
In particular, taking \(G=\{0\}\), the \(V(F,C)\) cover \(Y\).

If \(x\in Y\cap\Re^{-1}(\operatorname{Et}G)\) and \(\Re x\in F\), then \(x\) lies in one of the \(V(F,C)\), by (3.3).

\paragraph{(3.6)} Let \(Y'\) be the disjoint sum, indexed by the balls \(b(A)\) of \(\overline I\),
\[
Y'=\coprod_{b(A)}Y(qA).
\]
