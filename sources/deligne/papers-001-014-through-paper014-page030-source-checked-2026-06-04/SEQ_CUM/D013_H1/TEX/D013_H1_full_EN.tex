\section*{Introduction}

Throughout this article, let \(n=2m+1\) be a fixed odd integer \(>3\), and let \(\underline a=(a_1,\ldots,a_v)\) be a fixed sequence of integers \(>1\), such that the smooth complete intersections
\[
V_n(\underline a)\subset\Pj^{\,n+v}(\C)
\]
of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) have Hodge level \(1\). This means that, for such a complete intersection \(X\), namely the intersection of \(v\) hypersurfaces of degrees \(a_1,\ldots,a_v\) which are smooth and meet transversely along \(X\),
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item for \(p+q=n\) and \(|q-p|>1\), one has \(H^q(X,\Omega_X^p)=0\);
\item \(H^m(X,\Omega_X^{m+1})\ne0\).
\end{enumerate}
Recall that, by Lefschetz,
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\item \(H^*(X,\Z)\) is torsion-free;
\item for \(k\) odd, \(k\ne n\), one has \(H^k(X,\Z)=0\);
\item for \(p\le n\), \(H^{2p}(X,\Z)\) has rank \(1\) and is purely of type \((p,p)\); if \(\eta\in H^2(X,\Z)\) is the cohomology class of a hyperplane section, then \(H^{2p}(X,\Q)=\Q\cdot\eta^p\).
\end{enumerate}
The list of possible values of \((n,\underline a)\) appears in [10].

The intermediate Jacobian \(J(X)\) of \(X\) is, by definition, the complex torus
\[
J(X)=H_n(X,\Z)\backslash H^n(X,\C)/H^{m+1,m}(X)
      =H^{m,m+1}/H_n(X,\Z).
\]
The cup product on \(H^n(X,\Z)\) defines a Riemann form on \(J(X)\), which is therefore a principally polarized abelian variety. We prove that the construction \(X\mapsto J(X)\) is purely algebraic, i.e. independent of the topology of \(\C\). In particular, if \(X\) is defined over a field \(k\subset\C\), then \(J(X)\) is defined over the same field \(k\).

When \(n=1\), a case excluded below only for convenience, \(J(X)\) is the Jacobian of \(X\). For \(V_3(3)\), \(J(X)\) is the Jacobian of the Fano surface of \(X\) (see [1]). For \(n=3\) and \(X\) unirational, Manin's methods [8] make it possible to express \(J(X)\) in terms of Jacobians. For \(V_n(2,2)\), if \((L_t)_{t\in\Pj^1}\) is a Lefschetz pencil of hyperplane sections and if \(C\) is the covering of \(\Pj^1\) whose points are the linear subspaces of dimension \((n-1)/2\) in the \(L_t\), then \(J(X)\) is, up to isogeny, a direct factor of the Jacobian of \(C\). In the other cases I know no direct interpretation of \(J(X)\). According to Lieberman [7], the Hodge conjecture implies that \(J(X)\) is the quotient of the set of algebraic cycles on \(X\), of codimension \((n+1)/2\) and homologous to zero, by a suitable equivalence relation.

As a corollary of the theory one obtains, for \(n\) and \(\underline a\) fixed as above, that smooth complete intersections of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) over a finite field \(\F_q\) satisfy the Weil conjecture. For \(V_5(3)\) and \(V_3(4)\), this result is, to my knowledge, new.

\section*{1. Over \(\C\)}

\paragraph{1.1.} Let \(X\) be a nonsingular connected projective complex algebraic variety of dimension \(k\). Duality for coherent algebraic sheaves gives a purely algebraic isomorphism
\[
\tag{1.1.1}
H^k(X,\Omega_X^k)\iso\C.
\]
On the other hand, Hodge theory, here GAGA plus the Poincaré lemma, gives an isomorphism
\[
\tag{1.1.2}
H^k(X,\Omega_X^k)\iso H^{2k}(X,\C).
\]
The image, under (1.1.1), of integral cohomology \(H^{2k}(X,\Z)\subset H^{2k}(X,\C)\) is the lattice \((2\pi i)^{-k}\Z\subset\C\).

This leads to the definition of the Tate Hodge structure \(\Z(s)\) as the rank-one Hodge structure, pure of type \((-s,-s)\), with integral lattice
\[
\Z(s)_\Z=(2\pi i)^s\Z\subset \Z(s)_\C=\C.
\]
The cup product defines a morphism of Hodge structures
\[
\tag{1.1.3}
H^k(X,\Z)\otimes H^k(X,\Z)\longrightarrow\Z(-k).
\]

\paragraph{1.2.} Recall that \(\Z/r(1)\) denotes the group of \(r\)-th roots of unity. We identify
\[
\Z(1)_\Z\otimes\Z/r \simeq \Z/r(1)
\]
by the map \(x\mapsto\exp(x/r)\). For every prime number \(\ell\), put
\[
\Z_\ell(1)=\varprojlim \Z/\ell^a(1),
\]
with transition maps \(x\mapsto x^\ell\). The exponential map identifies \(\Z(1)_\Z\otimes\Z_\ell\) with \(\Z_\ell(1)\), and
\[
\Z(s)_\Z\otimes\Z_\ell\simeq \Z_\ell(s):=\Z_\ell(1)^{\otimes s}.
\]
If (1.1.3) is tensored with \(\Z_\ell\), the resulting morphism
\[
\tag{1.2.1}
H^k(X,\Z_\ell)\otimes H^k(X,\Z_\ell)\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(-k)
\]
is just the cup product in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology. It has a purely algebraic meaning.

\paragraph{1.3.} One has
\[
\tag{1.3.1}
H^k(X,\Z)\otimes\C=H^k(X,\C)=_{\mathrm{dfn}}H^k_{\DR}(X).
\]
Here and below we shall always identify \(\Z(k)_\C=\Z(k)_\Z\otimes\C\) with \(\C\) by the defining isomorphism which sends the integral lattice to \((2\pi i)^k\Z\). Tensor (1.1.3) with \(\C\). The resulting morphism
\[
\tag{1.3.2}
H^k_{\DR}(X)\otimes H^k_{\DR}(X)\longrightarrow\C
\]
is just the cup product in De Rham cohomology. It has a purely algebraic meaning.

\paragraph{1.4.} Now take \(X\) to be a complete intersection \(V_n(\underline a)\). Tensor (1.1.3) by \(\Z(2m)\), and multiply the resulting morphism by \((-1)^m\). One obtains
\[
\tag{1.4.1}
\psi:H^n(X)(m)\otimes H^n(X)(m)\longrightarrow\Z(-1).
\]
By hypothesis, the Hodge structure \(H^n(X)(m)\) is of type \((0,1)+(1,0)\). The form \(\psi\) is a polarization of \(H^n(X)(m)\), and, by Poincaré duality for \(X\), the alternating form \(\psi_\Z\) on the integral lattice \(H^n(X)(m)_\Z\) has discriminant one.

Therefore there exists a unique principally polarized abelian variety \(J(X)\), endowed with an isomorphism of polarized Hodge structures
\[
\alpha_\Z:H^n(X)(m)\iso H^1(J(X)).
\]
We call \(J(X)\) the intermediate Jacobian of \(X\).

\paragraph{1.5.} Let \(S\) be a smooth scheme of finite type over \(\C\), and let \(f:X\to S\) be a proper smooth morphism whose fibers are complete intersections \(V_n(\underline a)\). Then \(R^nf_*\Z(m)\) is a variation of polarized Hodge structures on \(S\), of type \((0,1)+(1,0)\) (see [5], §2). By a theorem of Borel (see [4], 5.1 and 5.2, for the statement), it defines a principally polarized abelian scheme
\[
u:J(X/S)\longrightarrow S,
\]
endowed with an isomorphism of variations of polarized Hodge structures
\[
\alpha:R^nf_*\Z(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z.
\]
This construction expresses that \(J(X)\) varies algebraically with \(X\). It would not be difficult to extend it to the case where \(S\) is an arbitrary scheme over \(\C\).

The analogues in \(\ell\)-adic and De Rham cohomology of the variation of Hodge structures \(R^nf_*\Z(m)\) and of \(\alpha\) are the following.

\paragraph{1.6.} Tensoring with \(\Z_\ell\), \(\alpha\) defines an isomorphism of \(\Z_\ell\)-sheaves
\[
\tag{1.6.1}
\alpha_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_\ell.
\]
The \(\ell\)-adic cup product, multiplied by \((-1)^m\), is a morphism of \(\Z_\ell\)-sheaves
\[
\tag{1.6.2}
\psi_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\otimes R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(-1),
\]
and \(\alpha_\ell\) transforms \(\psi_\ell\) into the polarization form
\[
\tag{1.6.3}
s\psi_\ell:R^1u_*\Z_\ell\otimes R^1u_*\Z_\ell\longrightarrow\Z_\ell(-1).
\]
The forms (1.6.2) and (1.6.3) have a purely algebraic meaning.

\paragraph{1.7.} The locally free coherent analytic sheaf \(R^nf_*\Z\otimes\mathcal O\) underlies the coherent algebraic sheaf
\[
R^nf_*\Omega^\bullet_{X/S}=_{\mathrm{dfn}}H^n_{\DR}(X/S).
\]
The evident integrable connection on \(R^nf_*\Z\otimes\mathcal O\) is identified with the Gauss--Manin connection on \(H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\). Tensoring \(\alpha\) with \(\mathcal O\) gives an isomorphism of analytic sheaves
\[
\tag{1.7.1}
\alpha_{\DR}:H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\iso H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S).
\]
The Gauss--Manin connections of \(H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\) and \(H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\) are regular ([5], 14, or [2], II, §7), and \(\alpha_{\DR}\) is horizontal. Hence \(\alpha_{\DR}\) is algebraic ([2], II, 5.9).

The De Rham cup product, multiplied by \((-1)^m\), is a horizontal morphism
\[
\tag{1.7.2}
\psi_{\DR}:H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\otimes H^n_{\DR}(X/S)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S,
\]
and \(\alpha_{\DR}\) transforms \(\psi_{\DR}\) into the polarization form
\[
\tag{1.7.3}
s\psi_{\DR}:H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\otimes H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_S.
\]

\paragraph{1.8.} Let \(S_\Z\) be the subscheme of the Hilbert scheme over \(\Z\) parametrizing smooth complete intersections of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) in \(\Pj^{\,n+v}\). Let
\[
f:X_\Z\longrightarrow S_\Z
\]
be the universal family of \(V_n(\underline a)\) parametrized by \(S_\Z\). For every ring \(A\), let \(f:X_A\to S_A\) denote the morphism obtained from \(f\) by extension of scalars from \(\Z\) to \(A\).

Put \(G=\PGL_{n+v+1}\). The group scheme \(G\) over \(\Z\) acts on \(X_\Z/S_\Z\) through its action on \(\Pj^{\,n+v}\).

\paragraph{1.9. Proposition.} Let \(g:Y\to T\) be a proper smooth morphism whose geometric fibers are complete intersections \(V_n(\underline a)\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Étale-locally on \(T\), \(Y\) is isomorphic to the intersection of \(v\) hypersurfaces \(H_1,\ldots,H_v\), of degrees \(a_1,\ldots,a_v\), in projective space \(\Pj^{\,n+v}\) over \(T\).
\item In particular, étale-locally on \(T\), there exist \(u:T\to S_\Z\) and a \(T\)-isomorphism \(t:Y\iso u^*(X_\Z/S_\Z)\).
\item If \((u',t')\) is another system as in (ii), then, étale-locally on \(T\), there exists \(a\in G(T)\) such that \((u',t')=a(u,t)\).
\item \(S_\Z\) is smooth over \(\Spec(\Z)\).
\end{enumerate}

In this section, the only corollary of 1.9 that we shall not use is the smoothness of \(S_\C\). One could even avoid it by subsequently replacing \(S_\Z\) by the scheme \(S'_\Z\) introduced below.

For every geometric fiber \(Y_{\bar t}\) of \(g\), one has \(H^1(Y_{\bar t},\mathcal O)=H^2(Y_{\bar t},\mathcal O)=0\), so that \(\Pic_T(Y)\) is étale over \(T\). Moreover, \(\Pic(Y_{\bar t})\simeq\Z\), generated by the class of the unique very ample invertible sheaf \(\mathcal O(1)\) of degree \(\prod a_i\). Étale-locally on \(T\), there is thus a relatively ample invertible sheaf of degree \(\prod a_i\) on \(Y\), denoted \(\mathcal O(1)\). Locally on \(T\), it is unique. Since \(H^1(Y_{\bar t},\mathcal O(k))=0\) (FAC), \(g_*\mathcal O(k)\) is locally free and commutes with base change. Hence \(\mathcal O(1)\) is very ample, and any choice of a basis of \(g_*\mathcal O(1)\) gives a diagram
\[
Y\hookrightarrow \Pj^{\,n+v}_T\longrightarrow T
\]
with \(j^*\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal O(1)\).

It remains to prove that \(j(Y)\) is an intersection of type (i); then (iii) follows from the uniqueness of \(\mathcal O(1)\). The multiplication maps on homogeneous sections are surjective, hence so are the maps \(\pi_*\mathcal O(k)\to g_*\mathcal O(k)\), and the kernels of these epimorphisms of locally free sheaves are locally free. The assertion follows readily.

Let \(S'_\Z\) be the scheme over \(\Z\) parametrizing \(v\)-tuples of hypersurfaces \(H_i\subset\Pj^{\,n+v}\), with \(\deg(H_i)=a_i\). Let \(S_\Z^{\prime\,0}\) be the open subscheme of \(S'_\Z\) parametrizing tuples \((H_i)\) for which \(\bigcap H_i\) is smooth of dimension \(n\). The scheme \(S_\Z^{\prime\,0}\) is smooth over \(\Spec(\Z)\), being open in a product of projective spaces. The evident map from \(S_\Z^{\prime\,0}\) to \(S_\Z\) is surjective by (i). It is smooth: using (i), one readily verifies an infinitesimal criterion of smoothness. Hence \(S_\Z\) is smooth over \(\Spec(\Z)\).

Put \(S=S_\C\), \(X=X_\C\), and let \(f:X\to S\) be the universal family of 1.8.

\paragraph{1.10. Fundamental lemma.} Let \(a:A\to S\) be an abelian scheme over \(S\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If there exists a prime number \(\ell\) and an isomorphism of \(\Q_\ell\)-sheaves
\[
v_\ell:R^1u_*\Q_\ell\iso R^1a_*\Q_\ell,
\]
or if there exists a horizontal isomorphism
\[
v_{\DR}:H^1_{\DR}(J(X/S)/S)\iso H^1_{\DR}(A/S),
\]
then \(A\) is isomorphic to \(J(X/S)\). The isomorphism
\[
v':J(X/S)\iso A
\]
is unique up to sign.
\item If, in addition, \(A\) is endowed with a principal polarization, then \(v'\) is compatible with the polarizations. If \(v_\ell\), respectively \(v_{\DR}\), as in (i), is compatible with the polarization forms, then \(v_\ell\), respectively \(v_{\DR}\), is induced by \(v'\) or by \(-v'\).
\end{enumerate}

We shall use the following theorem of Lefschetz.

\paragraph{1.11. Theorem (Lefschetz).} Let \(s\in S\), and let \(X_s=f^{-1}(s)\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The monodromy representation of \(\pi_1(S,s)\) on \(H^n(X_s,\Q)\) is absolutely irreducible.
\item For every prime \(\ell\), the monodromy representation of \(\pi_1(S,s)\) on \(H^n(X_s,\Z/\ell)\) is absolutely irreducible.
\end{enumerate}
For an exposition in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology of this theorem, following very closely Lefschetz's proof (cf. [6], chap. V, III, p. 106), see SGA 7 bis.

We prove 1.10. Consider the monodromy representations
\[
\rho_J:\pi_1(S,s)\to\GL(H^1(J(X_s),\Z))\simeq\GL(H^n(X_s,\Z)),
\]
\[
\rho_A:\pi_1(S,s)\to\GL(H^1(A_s,\Z)).
\]
Hypothesis (i) implies that, after extension of scalars to \(\Q_\ell\) or to \(\C\), these representations become isomorphic. Isomorphism of two representations is invariant under extension of the base field; hence there is a \(\pi_1(S,s)\)-equivariant isomorphism
\[
v'':H^1(J(X_s),\Q)\iso H^1(A_s,\Q).
\]
By 1.11(i), the image of \(v''^{-1}(H^1(A_s,\Z))\) in \(H^1(J(X_s),\Z/\ell)\) is all or nothing. It follows that a rational multiple of \(v''\) is an isomorphism
\[
v'_\Z:H^1(J(X_s),\Z)\iso H^1(A_s,\Z).
\]
By 1.11(i), \(v'_\Z\) is uniquely determined up to sign. By [3], 4.4.11 and 4.4.12, \(v'_\Z\) comes from an isomorphism of abelian schemes
\[
v':J(X/S)\iso A,
\]
which, like \(v'_\Z\), is unique up to sign. We recall the principle of the proof. Interpret \(v'_\Z\) as a section of the local system \(\Hom(R^1u_*\Z,R^1a_*\Z)\); by 1.11(i), it is the unique section of this local system up to a scalar. It is also known ([3], 4.12) that the module of all sections defines, at each point of \(S\), a Hodge substructure of \(\Hom(H^1(J(X_s),\Z),H^1(A_s,\Z))\). It follows that \(v'_\Z\) is everywhere of Hodge type \((0,0)\), i.e. a morphism of Hodge structures, and the assertion follows.

We prove (ii). By 1.11(i), every polarization \(\psi'\) of \(J(X/S)\) is a rational multiple \(b\psi\) of the given polarization. If \(\psi'\) is principal, then \(b\) is a positive invertible integer, i.e. \(\psi'=\psi\), proving the first assertion. For the second, a priori \(v_\ell=bv'_\ell\), respectively \(v_{\DR}=bv'_{\DR}\), and compatibility with the forms \(\psi_\ell\) or \(\psi_{\DR}\) gives \(b^2=1\), i.e. \(b=\pm1\).

\paragraph{1.12.} By 1.11(i), the isomorphism \(\alpha_\ell\) is characterized up to sign by the fact that it carries \(\psi_\ell\) to \(s\psi_\ell\) (1.6). It would be easy to adapt, and simplify, the arguments of [4], §6, to deduce from 1.11 that complete intersections \(V_n(\underline a)\) over finite fields satisfy the Weil conjectures. The same method would prove the following, perhaps more general, result.

\paragraph{1.13. Theorem.} Let
\[
X\hookrightarrow \Pj^N_{\Spec(V)}
\]
be a smooth projective variety of dimension \(2m+2\) over a discrete valuation ring \(V\) of unequal characteristic and finite residue field \(\F_q\). Let \(X_0\) and \(X_K\) be the special and general fibers. Suppose that the new part of the cohomology of the smooth hyperplane sections \(H\) of \(X_K\) has level \(\le1\) in the following sense:
\[
(*)\quad
\text{for }p+q=2m+1\text{ and }|q-p|>1,\quad
\Coker\bigl(H^q(X_K,\Omega^p_{X_K})\to H^q(H,\Omega^p_H)\bigr)=0.
\]
Then, for every smooth hyperplane section \(H_0\) of \(X_0\), the eigenvalues of Frobenius on
\[
\Coker\bigl(H^{2m+1}(X_0,\Z_\ell)\to H^{2m+1}(H_0,\Z_\ell)\bigr)
\]
are algebraic integers all of whose complex conjugates have absolute value \(q^{(2m+1)/2}\). These algebraic integers are divisible by \(q^m\).

\section*{2. Over \(\Q\)}

\paragraph{2.1.} Let \(\ell_0\) be a prime number. For every extension \(K\) of \(\Q\), consider the following problem:

\[
(J,K)\quad
\begin{minipage}{0.82\linewidth}
Construct a principally polarized abelian scheme \(u:J_K\to S_K\) over \(S_K\), endowed with an isomorphism
\[
\alpha_{\ell_0}:R^nf_*\Z_{\ell_0}(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_{\ell_0}
\]
which transforms \(\psi_{\ell_0}\), defined as in (1.6.2), into the polarization form.
\end{minipage}
\]
For \(K=\C\), problem \((J,K)\) has, by 1.10, a solution unique up to unique isomorphism.

\paragraph{2.2.} A general principle says that a problem \(P\), defined over a subfield \(k\) of \(\C\), and having over \(\C\) a solution unique up to unique isomorphism, has a solution, equally unique, over \(k\). Here this gives an abelian scheme
\[
u:J_\Q\to S_\Q
\]
over \(S_\Q\), endowed with a principal polarization \(s\psi\) and with an isomorphism \(\alpha_{\ell_0}\) as in \((J,K)\). Moreover, the triple \((J_\Q,s\psi,\alpha_{\ell_0})\) is unique up to unique isomorphism.

\paragraph{2.3.} The preceding principle is justified as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item If the problem \(P\) is to find a number \(a\) having some geometric property, the principle is evident: \(k\) is the field of invariants of \(\Aut(\C/k)\), and a solution \(a\) of \(P\) over \(\C\), being unique, is invariant under \(\Aut(\C/k)\). ``Geometric'' means invariant under extension of scalars.
\item If the problem \(P\) is to find a vector subspace of a vector space defined over \(k\), having some geometric property, apply the same argument, in the form Bourbaki, Algebra, chap. 2, §8, th. 1, to \(\Aut(\C/k)\). It is not required that \(V\) be finite-dimensional.
\item If the problem \(P\) is to find a subscheme \(Y\) of an affine scheme \(W\) defined over \(k\), having some geometric properties, apply b): the ideal of \(Y\) is a vector subspace of the coordinate ring.
\item If, in c), \(W\) were not affine, one reduces to c) by gluing.
\item For every integer \(b\ge3\), let \(M_b\) be the scheme over \(S_\Z[1/b]\) whose sections over any \(S_\Z[1/b]\)-scheme \(t:T\to S_\Z[1/b]\) classify principally polarized abelian schemes \(a:A\to T\) endowed with an isomorphism
\[
t^*R^nf_*\Z/b(m)\iso R^1a_*\Z/b,
\]
compatible with the intersection forms. A solution of problem \((J,K)\) is interpreted as a section over \(S_K\) of
\[
M_\infty=\varprojlim_b M_b.
\]
Apply d) to this section, viewed as a subscheme of \((M_\infty)_K\).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2.4. Remark.} When problem \(P\) has a solution over a finite extension of \(k\), the principle stated above is proved by Galois descent. One can often reduce to this case using the finite-extension principle EGA IV, 9.1.1.

Let \(\ell\) be a prime number. Let \(K\) be the function field of \(S_\Q\), and let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic closure of \(K\). Every \(\Z_\ell\)-sheaf \(F\) on \(S_\Q\) defines an \(\ell\)-adic representation of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on the geometric generic fiber \(F_{\bar\eta}\) of \(F\).

\paragraph{2.5. Lemma.} The \(\ell\)-adic representations of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) defined by \(R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\) and by \(R^1u_*\Z_\ell\) have the same character.

Let \(U\) be a Zariski open subset of \(S_\Z\), containing \(S_\Q\), on which \(\ell\) and \(\ell_0\) are invertible and such that \(J_\Q\) is the restriction to \(S_\Q\) of an abelian scheme \(u':J'\to U\). The \(\ell\)-adic representations are then unramified outside \(U\): they factor through a representation of \(\pi_1(U,\bar\eta)\). The same applies to their \(\ell_0\)-adic analogues. Each closed point \(s\) of \(U\) defines a conjugacy class of Frobenius substitutions \(\varphi_s\in\pi_1(U,\bar\eta)\). One has
\[
\tag{2.5.1}
\Tr(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^1u_*\Z_\ell)_{\bar\eta})
=\Tr(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^1u_*\Z_{\ell_0})_{\bar\eta})\in\Z,
\]
the trace of the Frobenius endomorphism of \(J_s\). Similarly,
\[
\tag{2.5.2}
\Tr(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m))_{\bar\eta})
=\Tr(\varphi_s^{-1},(R^nf_*\Z_{\ell_0}(m))_{\bar\eta})\in\Q,
\]
and both sides are, by the Lefschetz trace formula in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology,
\[
q_s^{-m}\bigl(\#\Pj^{\,n+v}(k(s))-\#X_s(k(s))\bigr).
\]
By hypothesis the equality of traces holds for \(\ell_0\), and by (2.5.1) and (2.5.2) it holds for \(\ell\). One concludes by observing that the trace is a continuous function on \(\pi_1(U,\bar\eta)\), and, by Chebotarev's density theorem, the Frobenius elements \(\varphi_s\) are dense in \(\pi_1(U,\bar\eta)\).

\paragraph{2.6. Lemma.} The representations of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on \(R^nf_*\Q_\ell(m)\) and on \(R^nf_*\Z/\ell(m)\) are irreducible.

Indeed, the image of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) in \(\GL(R^nf_*\Q_\ell(m))\), or in \(\GL(R^nf_*\Z/\ell(m))\), contains the image of the geometric fundamental group \(\pi_1(S_{\overline\Q},\bar\eta)\). By 1.11, the action of the latter is already irreducible.

From 2.5 and 2.6 it follows that the \(\ell\)-adic representations of \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on \((R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m))_{\bar\eta}\) and on \((R^1u_*\Z_\ell)_{\bar\eta}\) are isomorphic. Every isomorphism between these representations comes from an isomorphism
\[
\alpha'_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_\ell.
\]
By 1.11, after extending scalars from \(\Q\) to \(\C\), \(\alpha'_\ell\) becomes an \(\ell\)-adic multiple of the isomorphism (1.6.1): \(\alpha'_\ell=b_\ell\alpha_\ell\), with \(b_\ell\in\Z_\ell^*\). Over \(\Q\), put \(\alpha_\ell=b_\ell^{-1}\alpha'_\ell\).

\paragraph{2.7.} We have thus obtained:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item a principally polarized abelian scheme \(J_\Q\) over \(S_\Q\);
\item for every prime \(\ell\), an isomorphism compatible with the intersection forms
\[
\alpha_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_\ell;
\]
\item an isomorphism
\[
J_\Q\times_{S_\Q}S_\C\iso J(X_\C/S_\C)
\]
which identifies the preceding isomorphisms with the isomorphisms (1.6.1).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{2.8.} For every extension \(K\) of \(\Q\), let \(W_K\) be the \(K\)-vector space of horizontal morphisms
\[
\alpha':H^n_{\DR}(X_K/S_K)\longrightarrow H^1_{\DR}(J_K/S_K).
\]
One has \(W_K=W_\Q\otimes K\). For \(K=\C\), \(W_\C\) is one-dimensional, and its nonzero elements are isomorphisms. Hence there exists an isomorphism
\[
\alpha':H^n_{\DR}(X_\Q/S_\Q)\iso H^1_{\DR}(J_\Q/S_\Q).
\]
Moreover, \(\alpha'\) is unique up to multiplication by \(\lambda\in\Q^*\). Over \(\C\), one verifies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(\alpha'\) respects the Hodge filtrations on the two sides: \(\alpha'(F^{m+1})=F^1\);
\item \(\alpha'\) transforms \(\psi_{\DR}\) into a multiple of \(s\psi_{\DR}\):
\[
\alpha'(\psi_{\DR})=\mu(\alpha')\,s\psi_{\DR},\qquad \mu(\alpha')\in\Q^*.
\]
\end{enumerate}
One has \(\mu(\lambda\alpha')=\lambda^{-2}\mu(\alpha')\).

\paragraph{2.9. Proposition.} The multiplier \(\mu(\alpha')\) is positive.

This is checked after extension of scalars from \(\Q\) to \(\R\).

\paragraph{2.10. Conjecture.} The multiplier \(\mu(\alpha')\) is a square.

This conjecture is equivalent to the following statements:
\[
\tag{2.10.1}
\text{for a suitable choice of }\alpha',\text{ one has }\alpha'(\psi_{\DR})=s\psi_{\DR};
\]
\[
\tag{2.10.2}
\text{over }\C,\text{ the isomorphism deduced from }\alpha'\text{ transforms rational cohomology into rational cohomology;}
\]
\[
\tag{2.10.3}
\text{via 2.7 c), the isomorphism }\alpha_{\DR}\text{ of (1.7.1) is defined over }\Q.
\]
That this conjecture follows from both the Hodge conjecture and the Tate conjecture is meagre consolation.

Recall that
\[
G=\PGL_{n+v+1}
\]
acts on \(X_\Q/S_\Q\) (1.8).

\paragraph{2.11. Lemma.} There exists a unique action of \(G\) on \(J_\Q/S_\Q\) extending the action of \(G\) on \(S_\Q\). For this action, the isomorphisms \(\alpha_\ell\) of 2.7 b) are equivariant.

For every extension \(K\) of \(\Q\), consider the following problem. If
\[
\beta:G_K\times S_K\longrightarrow S_K
\]
is the action of \(G_K\) on \(S_K\), find an isomorphism
\[
a:\beta^*J_K\iso \operatorname{pr}_2^*J_K
\]
which defines an action of \(G_K\) on \(J_K/S_K\). Since \(G\) is connected, the \(\alpha_\ell\) are automatically equivariant. From this, or from Mumford [9], 6.1, p. 115, it follows that \(a\), if it exists, is unique. By the principle stated in 2.2, it is enough to show that \(a\) exists over \(\C\). This follows from the fact that, over \(\C\), the intermediate Jacobian is defined up to unique isomorphism, and from the fact that \(\beta^*(X_K/S_K)\) is given as isomorphic to \(\operatorname{pr}_2^*(X_K/S_K)\).

From 1.9 and 2.11 one obtains the following theorem.

\paragraph{2.12. Theorem.} Up to unique isomorphism, there exists a unique functor \(J\) of the following type, endowed with the following additional data:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item for \(T\) a scheme of characteristic \(0\) and \(g:Y\to T\) a proper smooth morphism with fibers \(V_n(\underline a)\), \(u:J(Y/T)\to T\) is a principally polarized abelian scheme;
\item \(J\) is compatible with base change \(c:T'\to T\): isomorphisms \(J(c^*Y/T')\iso c^*J(Y/T)\) are given;
\item compatible isomorphisms
\[
\alpha_\ell:R^ng_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1u_*\Z_\ell
\]
are given;
\item when restricted to smooth schemes over \(\C\), the functor \((J,\alpha_\ell)\) becomes isomorphic to the intermediate-Jacobian functor constructed in §1.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{3. Over \(\Z\)}

The theory over \(\Z\) is as unsatisfactory as it is in De Rham cohomology (2.8 to 2.10).

\paragraph{3.1. Proposition.} There exists a proper birational morphism
\[
b:S\longrightarrow S_\Z
\]
such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(b\) induces an isomorphism over \(S_\Q\);
\item there is a principally polarized abelian scheme \(J\) over \(S\), whose restriction to \(S_\Q\) is \(J_\Q\).
\end{enumerate}

We use the following lemma.

\paragraph{3.2. Lemma.} Let \(V\) be a discrete valuation ring, with fraction field \(K\) of characteristic \(0\), and let \(g:\Spec(V)\to S_\Z\) be a morphism. Then the abelian variety over \(K\) obtained by pullback from \(J_\Q\) has good reduction.

Let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic closure of \(K\), let \(X_V\) be the inverse image of \(X\) over \(V\), let \(J_K\) be the inverse image of \(J\) over \(\Spec(K)\), and let \(X_{\overline K}\), \(J_{\overline K}\) be the inverse images over \(\overline K\). Let \(k\ge3\) be an integer invertible on \(V\). Since \(X_V\) is smooth over \(V\), the specialization theorem in \(\ell\)-adic cohomology implies that the \(\Gal(\overline K/K)\)-module \(H^n(X_{\overline K},\Z/k)(m)\) is unramified. By 2.7, this module is isomorphic to \(H^1(J_{\overline K},\Z/k)\), and it remains to apply the Néron--Ogg--Shafarevich criterion for good reduction.

A short, but too complicated, method for deducing 3.1 from 3.2 is the following. Let \(k\ge3\), and let \(M_k\) be as in 2.3 e). Then \(J_\Q\) defines a section of \(M_k\) over \(S_\Q\). Let \(S_k\) be the closure of this section in \(M_k\). To prove that \(b:S_k\to S_\Z[1/k]\) is proper, it is enough to test the valuative criterion of properness on discrete valuation rings whose fraction fields have characteristic \(0\); indeed, \(S_{k,\Q}\) is dense in \(S_k\). For these rings, it suffices to apply 3.2.

It is easy to check that over \(\Spec(\Z[1/k_1k_2])\), \(S_{k_1}\) coincides with \(S_{k_2}\). The \(S_k\) glue to form the desired \(S\).

\paragraph{3.3.} I conjecture that in 3.1 one may take \(S=S_\Z\). If this were so, 2.12 would remain true over \(\Z\).

One verifies easily:

\paragraph{3.4. Proposition.} Let \(a:J\to S\) be as in 3.1. For each prime number \(\ell\), the isomorphism \(\alpha_\ell\) of 2.7 extends over \(S[1/\ell]\) to
\[
\alpha_\ell:R^nf_*\Z_\ell(m)\iso R^1a_*\Z_\ell.
\]

\paragraph{3.5. The Weil conjecture for \(V_n(\underline a)\).} Using 3.4 one can give a variant of the proof, indicated in 1.12, of the Weil conjecture for the \(V_n(\underline a)\).

Let \(X\) be a smooth complete intersection of dimension \(n\) and multidegree \(\underline a\) in \(\Pj^{\,n+v}(\F_q)\). It corresponds to a point \(x\in S_\Z(\F_q)\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item There exists a point \(\tilde x\in S(\F_q)\) above \(x\). Indeed, one may lift \(X\) to a complete intersection over the Witt vectors \(W(\F_q)\), which lifts \(x\) to \(x'\in S_\Z(W(\F_q))\). The point \(x'\) defines a point of \(S(W(\F_q)\otimes\Q)\), and, since \(b\) is proper, this point extends to a point of \(S(W(\F_q))\). Reducing gives \(\tilde x\).
\item Let \(J\) be the abelian variety over \(\F_q\), the fiber of \(J\) at \(\tilde x\). If \(X\) and \(J\) are obtained from \(X\) and \(J\) by extension of scalars from \(\F_q\) to an algebraic closure \(\overline{\F}_q\), then 3.4 gives an isomorphism
\[
H^n(X,\Z_\ell)(m)\iso H^1(J,\Z_\ell).
\]
This isomorphism commutes with the geometric Frobenius endomorphisms, so
\[
\tag{3.5.1}
\det(1-Ft,H^n(X,\Z_\ell))=
\det(1-q^mFt,H^1(J,\Z_\ell)).
\]
The Weil conjecture for \(X\) therefore follows from Weil's theorem [11] for \(J\).
\end{enumerate}
