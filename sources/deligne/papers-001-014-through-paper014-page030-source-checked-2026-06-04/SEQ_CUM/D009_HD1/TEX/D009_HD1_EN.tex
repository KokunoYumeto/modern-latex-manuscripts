{\Large\bfseries HODGE THEORY I}\\[1.6em]
{\large by PIERRE DELIGNE}
\end{center}

We propose to give a heuristic dictionary between statements in $\ell$-adic cohomology and statements in Hodge theory. This dictionary has, in particular, [3] and Grothendieck's conjectural theory of motives [2] as sources. Up to now, it has mainly served to formulate conjectures in Hodge theory, and it has sometimes suggested a proof of them.

\paragraph{Definition 1.1.} A mixed Hodge structure $H$ consists of
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item a finitely generated $\Z$-module $H_\Z$ (the ``integral lattice'');
\item a finite increasing filtration $W$ on $H_\Q=H_\Z\otimes\Q$ (the ``weight filtration'');
\item a finite decreasing filtration $F$ on $H_\C=H_\Z\otimes\C$ (the ``Hodge filtration'').
\end{enumerate}
These data are subject to the axiom:

There exists on $\Gr_W(H_\C)$ a unique bigrading by subspaces $H^{p,q}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item
\[
\Gr_W^n(H_\C)=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}H^{p,q};
\]
\item the filtration $F$ induces on $\Gr_W(H_\C)$ the filtration
\[
\Gr_W(F)^p=\bigoplus_{p'\ge p}H^{p',q'};
\]
\item $\overline{H^{p,q}}=H^{q,p}$.
\end{enumerate}
A morphism $f:H\to H'$ is a homomorphism $f_\Z:H_\Z\to H'_\Z$ such that $f_\Q:H_\Q\to H'_\Q$ and $f_\C:H_\C\to H'_\C$ are respectively compatible with the filtrations $W$ and $F$.

The Hodge numbers of $H$ are the integers
\[
\tag{1.2}
h^{pq}=\dim H^{p,q}=h^{qp}.
\]
One says that $H$ is pure of weight $n$ if $h^{pq}=0$ for $p+q\ne n$ (i.e. if $\Gr_W^i(H)=0$ for $i\ne n$). One also says that $H$ is a Hodge structure of weight $n$.

The Tate Hodge structure $\Z(1)$ is the Hodge structure of weight $-2$, purely of type $(-1,-1)$, for which $\Z(1)_\C=\C$ and $\Z(1)_\Z=2\pi i\Z=\Ker(\exp:\C\to\C^*)\subset\C$. Put $\Z(n)=\Z(1)^{\otimes n}$.

One can show that mixed Hodge structures form an abelian category. If $f:H\to H'$ is a morphism, then $f_\Q$ and $f_\C$ are strictly compatible with the filtrations $W$ and $F$ ([1], 2.3.5).

\paragraph{2.} Let $A$ be a normal integral ring of finite type over $\Z$, let $K$ be its field of fractions, and let $\barK$ be an algebraic closure of $K$. Let $K_{nr}$ be the largest subextension of $\barK$ unramified at every prime ideal of $A$. It is known, or we define,
\[
\pi_1(\Spec(A),\barK)=\Gal(K_{nr}/K).
\]
For every closed point $x$ of $\Spec(A)$, defined by a maximal ideal $m_x$ of $A$, the residue field $k_x=A/m_x$ is finite; the point $x$ defines a conjugacy class of ``Frobenius substitutions'' $\varphi_x\in\pi_1(\Spec(A),\barK)$. Put $q_x=\#k_x$ and $F_x=\varphi_x^{-1}$.

Let $K$ be a field of finite type over the prime field of characteristic $p$, let $\barK$ be an algebraic closure of $K$, let $\ell$ be a prime number $\ne p$, and let $H$ be a finite type $\Zl$-module (or $\Ql$-module) endowed with a continuous action $\rho$ of $\Gal(\barK/K)$. In what follows we shall always suppose that there exists $A$ as above, with $\ell$ invertible in $A$, such that $\rho$ factors through
\[
\pi_1(\Spec(A),\barK)=\Gal(K_{nr}/K).
\]
We shall say that $H$ is pure of weight $n$ if, for every closed point $x$ of a nonempty open subset of $\Spec(A)$, the eigenvalues $\alpha$ of $F_x$ acting on $H$ are algebraic integers all of whose complex conjugates have absolute value
\[
|\alpha|=q_x^{n/2}.
\]

\paragraph{Principle 2.1.} If the Galois module $H$ ``comes from algebraic geometry'', there exists on $H_{\Ql}=H\otimes_{\Zl}\Ql$ a unique increasing filtration $W$ (the ``weight filtration''), invariant under Galois, such that $\Gr_n^W(H)$ is pure of weight $n$.

One may think that $\Gr_n^W(H)$ is moreover semisimple.

When resolution of singularities is available, one can often give a conjectural definition of $W$, whose correctness follows from the Weil conjectures [5] (cf. 6).

Let $\mu$ be the subgroup of $\barK^*$ formed by the roots of unity. The Tate module $\Zl(1)$, defined by
\[
\Zl(1)=\Hom(\Ql/\Zl,\mu),
\]
is pure of weight $-2$. Put $\Zl(n)=\Zl(1)^{\otimes n}$.

It is trivial that every morphism $f:H\to H'$ is strictly compatible with the weight filtration.

Principle 2.1 agrees with the fact that every extension of $\Gm$ (``weight $-2$'') by an abelian variety (``weight $-1>-2$'') is trivial.

\paragraph{3. Translation.} The Galois modules which appear in $\ell$-adic cohomology have, over $\C$, mixed Hodge structures as analogues. In addition one has the dictionary
\[
\begin{array}{r@{\quad\vrule\quad}l}
\text{pure module of weight }n & \text{Hodge structure of weight }n\\
\text{weight filtration} & \text{weight filtration}\\
\text{homomorphism compatible with Galois} & \text{morphism}\\
\text{Tate module }\Zl(1) & \text{Tate Hodge structure }\Z(1).
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{4.} Let $X$ be a complex algebraic variety (= a scheme of finite type over $\C$, which we shall suppose separated). There exists a subfield $K$ of $\C$, of finite type over $\Q$, such that $X$ can be defined over $K$ (i.e. arises by extension of scalars from $K$ to $\C$ from a $K$-scheme $X'$). Let $\barK$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ in $\C$. The Galois group $\Gal(\barK/K)$ then acts on the $\ell$-adic cohomology groups $H^*(X,\Zl)$; one has
\[
H^*(X(\C),\Z)\otimes\Zl=H^*(X,\Zl)=H^*(X'_{\barK},\Zl).
\]

According to 3, one should expect the cohomology groups $H^n(X(\C),\Z)$ to carry natural mixed Hodge structures. This can be proved (see [1], 3.2.5, for the case where $X$ is smooth; the proof is algebraic, starting from classical Hodge theory [6]). For $X$ projective and smooth, the Weil conjectures imply that $H^n(X,\Zl)$ is pure of weight $n$, while classical Hodge theory endows $H^n(X,\Z)$ with a Hodge structure of weight $n$. For every morphism $f:X\to Y$ and for $K$ large enough, $f^*:H^*(Y,\Zl)\to H^*(X,\Zl)$ commutes with Galois (by transport of structure); likewise, $f^*:H^*(Y,\Z)\to H^*(X,\Z)$ is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures. For $X$ smooth, the cohomology class in $H^{2n}(X,\Zl(n))$ of an algebraic cycle of codimension $n$, $Z$, defined over $K$, is Galois-invariant, i.e. defines
\[
c(Z)\in\Hom_{\Gal}(\Zl(-n),H^{2n}(X,\Zl)).
\]
Similarly, the cohomology class $c(Z)\in H^{2n}(X(\C),\Z)$ is purely of type $(n,n)$, i.e. corresponds to
\[
c(Z)\in\Hom_{\mathrm{H.M.}}(\Z(-n),H^{2n}(X(\C),\Z)).
\]

\paragraph{5.} If $f:H\to H'$ is a Galois-compatible morphism between $\Ql$-vector spaces of different weights, then $f=0$. Likewise, if $f:H\to H'$ is a morphism of pure mixed Hodge structures of different weights, then $f$ is torsion. A more useful remark is the following.

\paragraph{Scholium 5.1.} Let $H$ and $H'$ be Hodge structures of weights $n$ and $n'$, with $n>n'$. Let $f:H_\Q\to H'_\Q$ be a homomorphism such that $f:H_\C\to H'_\C$ respects $F$. Then $f=0$.

\paragraph{6.} Let $X$ be a projective smooth variety over $\C$, let $D=\sum_1^nD_i$ be a normal-crossings divisor in $X$, a sum of smooth divisors, and let $j$ be the inclusion into $X$ of $U=X-D$. For $Q\subset[1,n]$, put
\[
D_Q=\bigcap_{i\in Q}D_i.
\]
In $\ell$-adic cohomology, one has canonically
\[
\tag{6.1}
R^qj_*\Zl=\bigoplus_{\#Q=q}\Zl(-q)_{D_Q},
\]
and the Leray spectral sequence for $j$ is
\[
\tag{6.2}
E_2^{pq}=\bigoplus_{\#Q=q}H^p(D_Q,\Ql)\otimes\Zl(-q)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(U,\Ql).
\]
According to the Weil conjectures [5], $H^p(D_Q,\Ql)$ is pure of weight $p$, so that $E_2^{pq}$ is pure of weight $p+2q$. As a quotient of a subobject of $E_2^{pq}$, $E_r^{pq}$ is also pure of weight $p+2q$. By 5, $d_r=0$ for $r\ge3$, since the weights $p+2q$ and $p+2q-r+2$ of $E_r^{pq}$ and $E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}$ are different. Hence $E_3^{pq}=E_\infty^{pq}$. Up to reindexing, the weight filtration of $H^*(U,\Ql)$ is the abutment of (6.2):
\[
\tag{6.3}
\Gr_n^W(H^k(U,\Ql))=E_3^{2k-n,n-k}.
\]

\paragraph{7.} In integral cohomology, for the usual topology, the Leray spectral sequence for $j$ is
\[
\tag{7.1}
{}'E_2^{pq}=\bigoplus_{\#Q=q}H^p(D_Q,\Z)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(U,\Z).
\]
Since every $D_Q$ is a nonsingular projective variety, ${}'E_2^{pq}$ is endowed with a Hodge structure of weight $p$. Put $E_2^{pq}={}'E_2^{pq}\otimes\Z(-q)$ (a Hodge structure of weight $p+2q$). As an abelian group, $E_2^{pq}={}'E_2^{pq}$; it is useful to regard (7.1) as a spectral sequence with initial term $E_2^{pq}$. According to 3, one should expect
\[
d_2:E_2^{pq}\to E_2^{p+2,q-1}
\]
to be a morphism of Hodge structures. This is proved by interpreting $d_2$ as a Gysin morphism. Thus $E_3^{pq}$ is endowed with a Hodge structure of weight $p+2q$. According to 3, one expects that, modulo torsion, the spectral sequence (6.4) degenerates at $E_3$ ($E_3=E_\infty$), and that the vanishing of the $d_r$ ($r\ge3$) is an application of 5.1. This program is carried out in [1] 3.2. There the weight filtration of $H^*(U,\Q)$ is defined as the abutment of (7.1), up to the reindexing (6.3).

In fact, in order to endow cohomology groups $H^*$ with a mixed Hodge structure, the key point up to now has always been to find a spectral sequence $E$ abutting to $H^*$ such that the $\ell$-adic analogue of $E_2^{pq}$ is conjecturally pure (of weight $p+2q$); $E_2^{pq}$ must then carry a natural Hodge structure (of weight $p+2q$), and the filtration $W$ is the abutment of $E$.

\paragraph{8.} Let $\Spec(V)$ be the spectrum of a henselian discrete valuation ring (a henselian trait), with fraction field $K$ and residue field $k$ of finite type over the prime field of characteristic $p$. Let $\barK$ be an algebraic closure of $K$ and let $H$ be a finite-dimensional vector space over $\Ql$ ($\ell\ne p$), on which $\Gal(\barK/K)$ acts continuously. According to Grothendieck, it is known ([4], appendix) that a finite-index subgroup of the inertia group $I$ acts unipotently. Replacing $V$ by a finite extension, one reduces to the case in which the action of all of $I$ is unipotent (the semistable case); it then factors through the largest pro-$\ell$ quotient $I_\ell$ of $I$, canonically isomorphic to $\Zl(1)$.

\paragraph{Principle 8.1.} In the semistable case, if the Galois module $H$ ``comes from algebraic geometry'', there exists a unique increasing filtration $W$ of $H$ (the ``weight filtration'') such that $I$ acts trivially on $\Gr_n^W(H)$ and such that $\Gr_n^W(H)$, as a Galois module under $\Gal(\bar k/k)\simeq\Gal(\barK/K)/I$, is pure of weight $n$.

Compare 2.1 and the appendix of [4].

When resolution of singularities is available, one can sometimes give a conjectural definition of $W$, whose validity follows from the Weil conjectures. With the help of resolution and Weil, it is often easy to show that in any case $H$ admits a dévissage into Galois modules (under $\Gal(\bar k/k)$) that are pure.

Suppose $H$ is semistable. For $T\in I_\ell$, define $\log T$ as the finite sum
\[
-\sum_{n>0}(\Id-T)^n/n.
\]
The map $(T,x)\mapsto\log T(x)$ is identified with a homomorphism
\[
\tag{8.2}
M:\Zl(1)\otimes H\longrightarrow H.
\]
Since $\Zl(1)$ has weight $-2$, one necessarily has (cf. 5)
\[
\tag{8.3}
M(\Zl(1)\otimes W_n(H))\subset W_{n-2}(H)
\]
and $M$ induces
\[
\tag{8.4}
\Gr(M):\Zl(1)\otimes\Gr_n^W(H)\longrightarrow\Gr_{n-2}^W(H).
\]

\paragraph{8.5.} If $X$ is a nonsingular projective variety over an algebraically closed field $k_0$, define
\[
L:\Zl(-1)\otimes H^*(X,\Zl)\longrightarrow H^*(X,\Zl)
\]
to be cup product with the cohomology class of a hyperplane section. One will note a formal analogy between $L$ and $M$: just as $M$ is defined by an action of $\Zl(1)$, one may regard $L$ as defined by an action of $\Zl(-1)$; $L$ raises degree by $2$, and $\Gr M$ (8.4) lowers it by $2$.

\paragraph{9.} Let $D$ be the unit disk, $D^*=D-\{0\}$, and let $X$
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
X&\subset&\mathbb P^r(\C)\times D\\[-0.2em]
{}^{f}\searrow&&\swarrow^{\pr_2}\\[-0.2em]
& D&
\end{array}
\]
be a family of projective varieties parametrized by $D$, with $f$ proper and $f|D^*$ smooth. Keep the notation of 8, and recall that in the analogy between a henselian trait and a small neighborhood of $0$ in the complex line, one has the following dictionary (note that the spectrum of the ring of germs of holomorphic functions at $0$ is a henselian trait):
\[
\begin{array}{r@{\quad\vrule\quad}l}
D & \Spec(V)\\
D^* & \Spec(K)\\
\text{a universal covering }\wt D^*\text{ of }D^* & \Spec(\barK)\\
\text{fundamental group }\pi_1(D^*) & \text{inertia group }I\\
\text{(with }\pi_1(D^*)=\Z\simeq\Z(1)_\Z\text{)} & \text{(with }I_\ell=\Zl(1)\text{)}\\
X & \text{projective scheme }X\text{ over }\Spec(V)\\
X^*=f^{-1}(D^*) & X_K\\
\wt X=X\times_D\wt D^* & X_{\barK}\\
\text{local system }R^if_*\Z|D^* & \text{Galois module }H^i(X_{\barK},\Zl)\\
H^i(\wt X,\Z) & H^i(X_{\barK},\Zl).
\end{array}
\]
Note that $\wt X$ is homotopy equivalent to each of the fibers $X_t=f^{-1}(t)$ ($t\in D^*$): $H^i(X_K,\Zl)$ again has $H^i(X_t,\Z)$ as analogue, and the action of $I$ corresponds to the monodromy transformation $T$.

Here again, it is known that a finite-index subgroup of $\pi_1(D^*)$ acts unipotently on $H^i(\wt X,\Q)=H^i(X_t,\Q)$. Place ourselves in the semistable case where all of $\pi_1(D^*)$ acts unipotently (this amounts to replacing $D$ by a finite covering), and let $T$ be the action of the canonical generator of $\pi_1(D^*)$.

By 3 and 8, one expects $H^i(\wt X,\Q)\simeq H^i(X_t,\Q)$ to be endowed with an increasing filtration $W$, $\Gr_n^W(H^i(\wt X,\Q))$ to be endowed with a Hodge structure of weight $n$, $\log T(W_n)\subset W_{n-2}$, and $\log T$ to induce a morphism of Hodge structures
\[
M_n:\Z(-1)\otimes\Gr_n^W(H^i)\longrightarrow\Gr_{n-2}^W(H^i).
\]
One would moreover like (8.2), and not only (8.3) and (8.4), to have an analogue.

In fact, for each vector $u$ in the tangent space to $D$ at $\{0\}$, one succeeds in defining a mixed Hodge structure $\calH_u$ on $H^i(\wt X,\Z)$. The filtration $W$ and the Hodge structures on the $\Gr_n^W(H^i)$ are independent of $u$, and the dependence of $\calH_u$ on $u$ is expressed simply in terms of $T$. In analogy with (8.2), one finds that, for every $u$, $\log T$ induces a homomorphism of mixed Hodge structures
\[
M:\Z(1)\otimes H^i(\wt X,\Z)\longrightarrow H^i(\wt X,\Z).
\]
Finally, the analogy 8.5 is not misleading (but here the fact that $f|D^*$ is supposed proper and smooth is no doubt essential). One proves that
\[
(\log T)^k:\Gr_{n+k}^W(H^n(\wt X,\Q))\longrightarrow\Gr_{n-k}^W(H^n(\wt X,\Q))
\]
is an isomorphism for every $k$ (cf. [6], IV 6, cor. to th. 5). This characterizes the filtration $W$. Up to now, an analogue of Hodge's positivity theorem (cf. [6], IV 7, cor. to th. 7) is available only in very special cases. One hopes that the mixed structures $\calH_u$ determine, as $t\to0$, the asymptotic behavior of the family of pure structures $H^i(X_t,\Z)$ ($t\in D^*$).
