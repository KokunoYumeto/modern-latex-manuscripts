Let \(Y'(b(A))\) denote the component with index \(b(A)\). There is an evident projection
\[
p':Y'\longrightarrow Y.
\]
Let \(R\) be the following relation on \(Y'\): for \(x\in Y'(b(A))\) and \(y\in Y'(b(B))\), \(R(x,y)\) means that
\[
p'(x)=p'(y)\quad\text{and}\quad p'(x),p'(y)\in Y(A,B).
\]
This is an equivalence relation, because, cf. (3.2),
\[
Y(A,B)\cap Y(B,C)\subset Y(A,C).
\]
One has
\[
\tag{3.6.1}
\text{the }R\text{-saturation of an open subset of }Y'\text{ is again open.}
\]
Put \(\widetilde Y=Y'/R\). There is an evident projection
\[
p:\widetilde Y\longrightarrow Y.
\]
For every ball \(b(A)\) of \(\widehat I\), let \(\widetilde Y(b(A))\) denote the image of \(Y'(b(A))\) in \(\widetilde Y\).

The following theorem implies the theorem stated at the beginning of the introduction.

\paragraph{(3.7) Theorem.} \(\widetilde Y\) is a contractible covering of \(Y\).

\paragraph{A. \(\widetilde Y\) is a covering of \(Y\).} We shall show, for \(V(F,C)\) as in (3.4)(iv), that \(p^{-1}V(F,C)\) is a sum of copies of \(V(F,C)\).

a) Let \(b(A)\) be a ball of \(\widehat I\). For \(B\) a chamber such that \(F\subset B^{-}\), let \(C'_B\) be the chamber of \(I\)
\[
C'_B=A\,u(A,B)\,u(\pi_F(C),B)^{-1}.
\]
By construction, \(B\subset q(S(A)\cap S(C'_B))\), and in particular, if \(F\ne\{0\}\),
\[
\tag{3.7.1}
[F]\subset q(S(A)\cap S(C'_B)).
\]
We prove that
\[
\tag{3.7.2}
\widetilde Y(b(A))\cap p^{-1}V(F,C)
\subset
\bigcup_{F\subset B^-}\widetilde Y(b(C'_B)).
\]
Let \(x\) lie in the left-hand side. Let \(G\) be the facet of \(V\) to which \(\mathcal Rpx\) belongs. One has \(F\subset G^{-}\). Choose \(B\) to be a chamber of which \(G\) is a facet. By hypothesis,
\[
\operatorname{pr}_G\mathcal Ipx\in\pi'_G(A)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\operatorname{pr}_F\mathcal Ipx\in C.
\]
Thus \(\pi'_G(A)=\pi'_G(\pi_FC)\). It follows from (3.2) that every chamber having \(G\) as a facet lies in \(q(S(A)\cap S(C'_B))\), that \(px\) lies in \(Y(A,C'_B)\), and hence that \(x\in\widetilde Y(b(C'_B))\).

b) Put
\[
Y'(F,C)=
\bigcup_{q(C')=C}
\bigl(Y'(b(C'))\cap p'^{-1}V(F,C)\bigr).
\]
By a), set-theoretically,
\[
p^{-1}V(F,C)=Y'(F,C)/R.
\]
It is even an isomorphism of topological spaces, by (3.6.1), since \(Y'(F,C)\) is open in \(Y'\). By (3.2), the equivalence relation induced by \(R\) on
\[
\bigcup_{q(C')=C}Y'(b(C'))
\]
is trivial. We conclude by observing that, for every \(C'\) lying over \(C\),
\[
V(F,C)\subset Y(b(C')).
\]

\paragraph{B. An open covering of \(\widetilde Y\).}

\paragraph{(3.7.3) Notation.} (i) For \(F\) a facet of \(I\),
\[
\widetilde Y(F)=
\bigcup_{F\subset S(C)}
\bigl(\widetilde Y(b(C))\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(qF)\bigr).
\]
(ii) For \(o(A)\), the center of a ball \(b(A)\) of \(\widehat I\),
\[
\widetilde Y(o(A))=\widetilde Y(b(A))\cap p^{-1}V(\{0\},A).
\]

\paragraph{(3.7.4) Lemma.} (i) The \(\widetilde Y(s)\), for \(s\) a vertex of \(\widehat I\), form a covering of \(\widetilde Y\) whose nerve is \(\widehat I\): a family of \(\widetilde Y(s)\)'s has nonempty intersection if and only if the corresponding \(s\)'s are the vertices of a facet of \(\widehat I\).

(ii) If a facet \(F\) of \(I\) has vertices \(s_0,\ldots,s_p\), then
\[
\widetilde Y(F)=\widetilde Y(s_0)\cap\cdots\cap\widetilde Y(s_p).
\]

(iii) If \(F\subset S(A)\), then \(\widetilde Y(o(A))\cap\widetilde Y(F)\) is contractible; \(\widetilde Y(o(A))\) is contractible.

\paragraph{(3.7.4.1)} Let \(F_i\), \(i=1,2\), be two facets of \(I\), and \(C_i\), \(i=1,2\), two chambers of \(I\) such that \(F_i\subset S(C_i)\) and
\[
\bigl(\widetilde Y(b(C_1))\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(qF_1)\bigr)
\cap
\bigl(\widetilde Y(b(C_2))\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(qF_2)\bigr)
\ne\varnothing.
\]
Then \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) span a facet \(F\) of \(I\), unique by (2.5)--(2.11), \(F\subset S(C_i)\), and the preceding intersection coincides with
\[
\widetilde Y(b(C_1))\cap \widetilde Y(b(C_2))
\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F).
\]
By hypothesis,
\[
Y(C_1,C_2)\cap\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(qF_1)\cap
\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(qF_2)\ne\varnothing;
\]
therefore \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) lie in \(S(C_1)\cap S(C_2)\) and span a facet \(F\) there. Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{Et}(qF_1)\cap\operatorname{Et}(qF_2)=\operatorname{Et}(qF),
\]
which gives the announced formula.

From (3.7.4.1), it follows that \(\widetilde Y(F_1)\cap\widetilde Y(F_2)\) is empty if \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) do not span a common facet \(F\), and otherwise is equal to
\[
\bigcap_{F\subset S(C)}
\widetilde Y(b(C))\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F)
=\widetilde Y(F)
\]
(take \(C_1=C_2\)). This proves (ii) and part of (i).

\paragraph{(3.7.4.2)} If \(o(A)\ne o(B)\), then
\[
\widetilde Y(o(A))\cap\widetilde Y(o(B))=\varnothing.
\]
If \(qA=qB\), then \(\widetilde Y(b(A))\cap \widetilde Y(b(B))=\varnothing\). If \(qA\ne qB\), then
\[
p\widetilde Y(o(A))\cap p\widetilde Y(o(B))=\varnothing.
\]

\paragraph{(3.7.4.3)} If \(F\not\subset S(A)\), then
\[
\widetilde Y(o(A))\cap\widetilde Y(F)=\varnothing.
\]
If \(F\subset S(B)\), then \(F\not\subset S(A)\cap S(B)\) and
\[
\operatorname{Et}(qF)\cap Y(A,B)
=\operatorname{Et}(qF)\cap p(\widetilde Y(b(A))\cap\widetilde Y(b(B)))=\varnothing.
\]
It is clear that:
\[
\tag{3.7.4.4}
\text{if }F\subset S(A),\text{ then }p\text{ induces an isomorphism from }
\widetilde Y(o(A))\cap\widetilde Y(F)
\]
onto
\[
Y(qA)\cap\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(qF).
\]
Lemma (3.7.4) then follows from:

\paragraph{(3.7.5) Lemma.} For every facet \(F\) of \(V\), and every chamber \(A\),
\[
Y(A)\cap\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F)
\]
is contractible.

Let \(v_0\) be a vector such that \(\mathcal Rv_0\in F\) and \(\mathcal Iv_0\in A\). Then, for \(0\le t\le1\),
\[
\varphi_t:v\longmapsto v_0+t(v-v_0)
\]
maps \(Y(A)\) and \(\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F)\) into themselves. One has
\[
\varphi_0(Y(A)\cap\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F))=\{v_0\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\varphi_1=\operatorname{Id}.
\]

\paragraph{C. Study of \(\widetilde Y(F)\), \(F\) a facet of \(I\).} By (3.5), (3.7.1), and (3.7.2),
\[
\tag{3.7.6}
\widetilde Y(F)=
\bigcup_{F\subset B^-}
\widetilde Y(b(B))\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F).
\]
Choose a fundamental chamber \(A_{F,0}\) in \((V_F,\mathcal M_F)\), and let \(I_F\) be the corresponding building. Also choose \(\widetilde A_{F,0}\) in \(I\), lying over \(\pi_F(A_{F,0})\). For each chamber \(B=\widetilde A_0.g_F\) of \(I_F\), denote by \(\pi_F(B)\) the chamber \(\widetilde A_{F,0}\cdot\pi_F(g_F)\) of \(I\). By (2.10), (3.7.6) can be rewritten as
\[
\widetilde Y(F)=
\bigcup_{B\text{ in }I_F}
\widetilde Y(b(\pi_F(B)))\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F).
\]
Let
\[
Y_F=(V_F)_\C-\bigcup_{M\in\mathcal M_F}M_\C
\]
and let \(\widetilde Y_F\) be the covering of \(Y_F\) defined using \(I_F\). For any \(B\) and \(C\) in \(I_F\), it follows from (1.30) that the chambers of \(S\) in
\[
Y(\pi_F(B),\pi_F(C))\cap\operatorname{Et}(F)
\]
are exactly the \(\pi_F(D)\), for \(D\) in \(Y_F(B,C)\).

The gluings defining \(\widetilde Y(F)\) and \(\widetilde Y_F\) are therefore compatible, and there is a unique Cartesian diagram
\[
\tag{3.7.7}
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde Y_F&\longleftarrow&\widetilde Y(F)\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
Y_F&\xleftarrow{\operatorname{pr}_F}&\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F)\subset Y
\end{array}
\]
which sends
\[
\widetilde Y(b(\pi_F(B)))\cap p^{-1}\mathcal R^{-1}\operatorname{Et}(F)
\]
to \(\widetilde Y_F(b(B))\), for \(B\) a chamber of \(I_F\). The first vertical arrow, and hence also the second, are coverings.

It is easy to verify that the map \(\operatorname{pr}_F\) of (3.7.7) is a homotopy equivalence. Hence:
\[
\tag{3.7.8}
\text{Lemma. }\quad
\widetilde Y_F\text{ and }\widetilde Y(F)\text{ have the same homotopy type.}
\]

\paragraph{D. End of the proof.} We prove (3.7) by induction on \(r=\dim V\). Let \(\mathcal U\) be the open covering of \(\widetilde Y\) by the \(\widetilde Y(s)\), for \(s\) a vertex of \(\widehat I\). By (3.7.4)(i), \(\widehat I\) is the nerve of \(\mathcal U\). By (3.7.4), (3.7.8), and the induction hypothesis, the nonempty intersections of opens belonging to \(\mathcal U\) are contractible. This implies [6] that \(\widetilde Y\) and \(\widehat I=\operatorname{Nerf}(\mathcal U)\) have the same homotopy type. We conclude by (2.15).

\section*{4. Braid groups}

\paragraph{(4.1)} Let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional real vector space and \(W\subset\GL(V)\) a finite group generated by reflections. Assume \(V^W=\{0\}\). Let \(\Phi\) be any \(W\)-invariant Euclidean structure, and let \(\mathcal M\) be the set of hyperplanes \(M\) such that the orthogonal reflection with respect to \(M\) lies in \(W\). It is known that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \((V,\mathcal M)\) satisfies 1.6 ([1], V, 3.9, prop. 7);
\item \(W\) permutes the chambers of \((V,\mathcal M)\) simply transitively ([1], V, 3.2, th. 1);
\item if \(x\in V\) belongs to the closed chamber \(\overline A\), its stabilizer is generated by the reflections with respect to the walls of \(A\) containing \(x\) ([1], V, 3.3, prop. 1);
\item it follows from c) that \(W\) acts freely on
\[
Y_W=V_\C-\bigcup_{M\in\mathcal M}M_\C.
\]
\end{enumerate}
By b), if \(C_0\) and \(C_1\) are two chambers, there is a unique \(w\in W\) such that \(wC_0=C_1\); these \(w\)'s induce a transitive system of bijections between the walls of the different chambers. Passing to the quotient, one obtains a set \(D\) with \(\dim(V)\) elements and, for each chamber \(C\), a bijection \(\varphi_C\) from \(D\) to the set of walls of \(C\). One has
\[
\tag{4.1.1}
\varphi_{wC}(i)=w\varphi_C(i)\qquad(w\in W).
\]
For the definition of the Coxeter matrix \((m_{ij})_{i,j\in D}\) and of the Coxeter graph, we refer to [1], V, 3.4.

\paragraph{(4.2)} Choose a chamber \(A_0\) of \((V,\mathcal M)\). For \(i\in D\), let \(w_i\) be the reflection with respect to the wall \(\varphi_{A_0}(i)\). [1], V, 3.2, th. 1 says that \(W\) is generated by the \(w_i\), with these generators subject only to the relations
\[
(w_iw_j)^{m_{ij}}=e.
\]

\paragraph{(4.3)} Let \(X_W=Y_W/W\): by (4.1)d), \(Y_W\) is a covering of \(X_W\), with group \(W\). Let \(y_0\in A_0\) have image \(x_0\in X_W\). For each \(i\in D\), let \(\widetilde \ell_i\) be the homotopy class of paths in \(Y_W\), from \(y_0\) to \(w_i(y_0)\), which, for \(y_0\in A_0\), contains the broken line with successive vertices
\[
y_0,\quad y_0+iy_0,\quad w_i(y_0)+iy_0,\quad w_i(y_0).
\]
The image \(\ell_i\) of \(\widetilde\ell_i\) in \(X_W\) is a loop based at \(x_0\).

\paragraph{(4.4) Theorem.} (i) The fundamental group \(\pi_1(X_W,x_0)\) is generated by the \(\ell_i\), subject only to the relations
\[
\tag{4.4.1}
\prodalt(m_{ij};\ell_i,\ell_j)=\prodalt(m_{ji};\ell_j,\ell_i).
\]
(ii) The universal covering of \(X_W\) is contractible: \(X_W\) is a \(K(\pi,1)\).

Assertion (i) is proved in Brieskorn [3]. Another proof will be sketched in (4.18) below. As explained in the introduction, (ii) follows from (3.7) and (4.1).

\paragraph{(4.5)} In §1 we were largely inspired by Garside's study [4] of Artin's braid group; we can now derive from the results obtained information about all groups of the type \(\pi_1(X_W,x_0)\). The translation rests on (4.6) and (4.7) below.

\paragraph{(4.6)} Let \(G=(C_0,\ldots,C_n)\) be a gallery, and let \(M_i\) be the wall separating \(C_{i-1}\) from \(C_i\), \(1\le i\le n\). One has
\[
\varphi^{-1}_{C_{i-1}}(M_i)=\varphi^{-1}_{C_i}(M_i).
\]
Let \(\ell(G)\) be the element
\[
\varphi^{-1}_{C_1}(M_1)\cdots \varphi^{-1}_{C_n}(M_n)
\]
of \(L^+(D)\), the free monoid on \(D\). The map \(\ell\) induces a bijection from the set of galleries \(G\) with prescribed source to \(L^+(D)\). One has
\[
\tag{4.6.1}
\ell(w(G))=\ell(G)\qquad(w\in W),
\]
\[
\tag{4.6.2}
\ell(G_0G_1)=\ell(G_0)\ell(G_1)
\]
for composable \(G_0\) and \(G_1\). Let
\[
w:L(D)\longrightarrow W
\]
be the homomorphism extending the map \(i\mapsto w_i\) from \(D\) to \(W\).

\paragraph{(4.7) Proposition.} For every gallery \(G\) with source \(A_0\), one has
\[
A_0\cdot G=w(\ell(G))(A_0).
\]
This is an avatar of the identity
\[
[(w_1\cdots w_{n-1})w_n(w_1\cdots w_{n-1})^{-1}]
[(w_1\cdots w_{n-2})w_{n-1}(w_1\cdots w_{n-2})^{-1}]
\cdots [w_1]=w_1\cdots w_n.
\]

\paragraph{(4.8)} From this proposition it follows that, for \(w\in W\), the integer \(d(A_0,w(A_0))\) is the minimum length of words in the \(w_i\) equal to \(w\). More precisely, \(\ell\) induces a bijection from the set of minimal galleries from \(A_0\) to \(wA_0\) to the set of words \(m\in L(D)\), of minimal length, such that \(w=w(m)\).

\paragraph{(4.9) Definition.} (i) The braid monoid \(G^+\) of type \(D\) is the monoid with unit generated by \(D\), whose generators \(i\in D\) are subject only to the relations
\[
\tag{4.9.1}
\prodalt(m_{ij};i,j)=\prodalt(m_{ij};j,i).
\]
(ii) The braid group \(G\), or generalized braid group, of type \(D\), is the group generated by \(D\), with the generators subject to (4.9.1).

\paragraph{(4.10)} Notice that \(W\) also admits the presentation
\[
\tag{4.10.1}
\prodalt(m_{ij};w_i,w_j)=\prodalt(m_{ij};w_j,w_i),
\]
\[
\tag{4.10.2}
w_i^2=e,
\]
so that \(i\mapsto w_i\) extends to a homomorphism from \(G\) to \(W\).

\paragraph{(4.11)} One checks from the definitions that two galleries \(G_0\) and \(G_1\) with the same source are equivalent if and only if \(\ell(G_0)\) and \(\ell(G_1)\) have the same image in \(G^+\). Taking account of (4.9), (1.12) therefore admits the following translation ([1], IV, 1.6, prop. 5).

\paragraph{(4.12) Reminder.} Let \(w\in W\). The image in \(G^+\) of the words \(m\in L(D)\), of minimal length among those satisfying \(w=w(m)\), depends only on \(w\). This image will be denoted \(r(w)\); \(r\) is a section of \(w:G^+\to W\). By (4.7),
\[
r(w)=\ell(u(A_0,wA_0)).
\]
Similarly, [1], IV, 1.4, lemma 2 corresponds to (1.11).

\paragraph{(4.13)} The quotient category of \(\operatorname{Gal}_+(V,\mathcal M)\) by \(W\) is defined as follows:
\[
\text{a) its set of objects is reduced to one element, namely }
\operatorname{Ob}(\operatorname{Gal}_+(V,\mathcal M))/W.
\]
b) The monoid of its arrows is \(\operatorname{Fl}(\operatorname{Gal}_+(V,\mathcal M))/W\), and passage to the quotient
\[
\operatorname{Gal}_+(V,\mathcal M)\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}_+(V,\mathcal M)/W
\]
is a functor.

This category, having only one object, is identified with the monoid of its arrows. By (4.6.1), (4.6.2), and (4.11), \(\ell\) identifies this monoid with \(G^+\).

Similarly one defines \(\operatorname{Gal}(V,\mathcal M)/W\). This category has only one object and, by its universal property, \(\ell\) extends to an isomorphism from the group of its arrows to \(G\):
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
\operatorname{Gal}_+(V,\mathcal M)&\longrightarrow&
\operatorname{Gal}_+(V,\mathcal M)/W&\xrightarrow[\ell]{\sim}&G^+\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
\operatorname{Gal}(V,\mathcal M)&\longrightarrow&
\operatorname{Gal}(V,\mathcal M)/W&\xrightarrow[\ell]{\sim}&G .
\end{array}
\]

\paragraph{(4.14) Theorem.} (i) In \(G^+\), left and right translations are injective.

(ii) \(G^+\) satisfies Ore's condition on the left and on the right. Thus, by (i), \(G^+\) embeds in \(G\).

(iii) For \(J\) a subset of \(D\), let \(G_J^+\), respectively \(G_J\), be the submonoid, respectively subgroup, of \(G^+\), respectively \(G\), generated by the \(i\in J\). Then the evident map identifies \(G_J^+\), respectively \(G_J\), with the braid monoid, respectively braid group, of type \(J\).

(iv) One has \(G_J^+=G_J\cap G^+\).

(v) If \(J\) and \(K\) are two subsets of \(D\), one has \(G_J\cap G_K=G_{J\cap K}\).

(vi) Let \(n(i)\) be the number of elements of \(G^+\) of length \(i\), and put
\[
f=\sum n(i)t^i.
\]
For \(J\subset D\), let \(m(J)\) be the number of walls passing through the intersection of the walls of any chamber \(A\), indexed by \(J\), that is, the number of reflections in the Weyl group \(W_J\). Then
\[
f=\left(\sum_{J\subset D}(-1)^{|J|}t^{m(J)}\right)^{-1}.
\]
These assertions translate respectively: (i), (1.1)(i) and (ii); (ii), (1.26)(ii) and its transform by passage to opposite galleries, cf. also (4.16) below; (iii), (1.30); (iv), (1.32); (v), (1.31); (vi), (1.21), the \(f_A\) of loc. cit. all being equal to \(f\).

Let \(x,y\in G^+\). We say that \(x\) begins with \(y\) if there exists \(z\in G^+\) such that \(x=yz\). Proposition (1.19)(iii) translates as follows.

\paragraph{(4.15) Lemma.} Let \(x\in G^+\). There exists a chamber \(C\) such that, for every \(w\in W\), \(x\) begins with \(r(w)\) if and only if \(wA_0\in D(A_0,C)\).

\paragraph{(4.16)} Let \(J\) be a subset of \(D\), and let \(P\) be the intersection of the walls \(\varphi_{A_0}(i)\), \(i\in J\). Let \(\Delta(J)\) be the element \(\ell(u(A_0,A_0\cdot A(P)))\) of \(G^+\); put \(\Delta=\Delta(D)\). Let \(i\mapsto\bar i\) be the opposition involution of \(D\). We also denote by \(w\mapsto\bar w\) the automorphisms of \(L^+(D)\), \(G^+\), and \(G\) sending \(i\) to \(\bar i\); one has \(m_{ij}=m_{\bar i\bar j}\). Translating (1.26) with the help of (4.4), one finds that, for \(g\) in \(G^+\) or in \(G\),
\[
\tag{4.16.1}
g\Delta=\Delta\bar g.
\]
In particular, \(\Delta^2\) is central.

For every \(i\in D\), \(\Delta\) begins with \(i\): one has \(\Delta=ix\) in \(G^+\). Hence:

\paragraph{(4.17) Proposition.} \(G\) is obtained from \(G^+\) by making the central element \(\Delta^2\) invertible.

\paragraph{(4.18)} The description (2.11), (2.5)--(2.7) of the building \(I\) attached to \((V,\mathcal M)\) can be translated as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item the set of vertices of \(I\) is
\[
I^0=\coprod_{i\in D}G/G_{D-\{i\}};
\]
\item for a set of vertices to span a simplex, it is necessary and sufficient that it be contained in the set of classes \(gG_{D-\{i\}}\), for some \(g\in G\);
\item the fundamental sphere \(S_0=S(A_0)\) is the union of the fundamental simplex, spanned by the lateral classes of \(e\) in the \(G/G_{D-\{i\}}\), and its transforms by the \(r(w)\), \(w\in W\).
\end{enumerate}
The preceding description makes evident a left action of \(G\) on \(I\). It respects the set of spheres \(S(A)\) and the correspondence \(A\mapsto S(A)\).

By transport of structure, \(G\) acts on the covering \(\widetilde Y_W\) of \(Y_W\) (3.6), and one has an equivariant morphism
\[
(G-\widetilde Y_W)\longrightarrow (W-Y_W).
\]
It follows that \(G\) is the fundamental group of \(X_W=Y_W/W\), and (4.4)(i).

\paragraph{(4.19)} For completeness, let us show that the word problem, the conjugacy problem, and the question of computing the center of \(G\) can be solved as in [4]. If the Coxeter graph \(D\) of \(W\) is a disjoint union of graphs \(D_\alpha\), the braid group of type \(D\) is the product of the braid groups of types \(D_\alpha\). The essential case is therefore that of a connected graph \(D\).

\paragraph{(4.20) The word problem.} There is a decision procedure for determining whether two elements \(m\) and \(n\) of \(L(D)\) have the same image in \(G\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item Whether \(m\) and \(n\) in \(L^+(D)\) have the same image in \(G^+\) is decidable, because the imposed relations do not change word length and there are only finitely many words of a given length. One may also use (1.22).
\item For any \(m_i\), \(i=1,2\), in the free group \(L(D)\) generated by \(D\), one can compute \(m'_i\in L^+(D)\) and \(k\ge0\) such that \(m_i\) and \(m'_i\Delta^{-k}\) have the same image in \(G\) (cf. (4.17)); then \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) have the same image in \(G\) if and only if \(m'_1\) and \(m'_2\) have the same image in \(G^+\).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{(4.21) Theorem.} If the Coxeter graph \(D\) is connected, nonempty, and the opposition involution is trivial, respectively nontrivial, the center of \(G\) is infinite monogenic, generated by \(\Delta\), respectively by \(\Delta^2\).

Consider the following property of \(x\in G^+\):
\[
(*)\qquad
\text{for every }i\in D,\text{ there exists }i'\in D\text{ such that }ix=xi'.
\]
It is enough to prove that, if \(x\) satisfies \((*)\) and \(x\ne e\), then \(x=\Delta y\) with \(y\in G^+\). By induction on the length of \(x\), it will follow that, if \(x\) satisfies \((*)\), then \(x\) is of the form \(\Delta^k\). In particular, the center of \(G^+\) is contained in the set of the \(\Delta^k\), \(k\ge0\); by (4.14)(i) and (4.17), the center of \(G\) is contained in the set of the \(\Delta^k\), \(k\in\Z\), and the assertion follows from (4.16.1).

Let \(x\in G^+\) satisfy \((*)\). Let \(C\) be as in (4.15), and let \(w\) be the image in \(W\) of \(\ell(u(A_0,C))\). We may write \(x=r(w)y\), with \(y\in G^+\). Let \(i\in D\). By \((*)\), \(ix=r(w)y'\) begins with \(r(w)\); by (1.23), it follows that \(ir(w)\) begins with \(r(w)\), i.e. \(ir(w)=r(w)i''\). In particular, for every \(i\), there exists \(i''\) with \(iw=wi''\); this means that every wall of \(A_0\) is also a wall of \(wA_0\). Among the \(2^{|D|}\) open simplicial cones bounded by the walls of \(A_0\), only \(A_0\) and \(-A_0\) are chambers; they are the only ones whose angles between faces are all \(\le\pi/2\), using here the connectedness of \(D\). If \(x\ne e\), then \(w\ne e\), and therefore \(wA_0=-A_0\). Since then \(\Delta=r(w)\), this completes the proof.

\paragraph{(4.22) The derived group.} Let \(D'\) be the graph with vertex set \(D\), two vertices being joined by an edge if and only if \(m_{ij}\) is odd. Let \(D_0\) be the set of connected components of \(D'\). Define an epimorphism
\[
\operatorname{Lg}:G\longrightarrow\Z^{D_0}
\]
by sending \(i\) to the basis vector indexed by the connected component of \(i\) in \(D'\). One verifies at once that \(\operatorname{Lg}\) identifies \(\Z^{D_0}\) with the largest abelian quotient of \(G\).

\paragraph{(4.23) The conjugacy problem.} The problem is to give a decision procedure for determining whether two elements \(x\) and \(y\) of \(G\), given explicitly as images of words in \(L(D)\), are conjugate. Since \(\Delta^2\) is central, \(\Delta^{-2k}a\) and \(\Delta^{-2k}b\) are conjugate if and only if \(a\) and \(b\) are. By (4.17), it is enough to treat the case where \(x,y\in G^+\). Similarly, if \(x\) and \(y\) are conjugate, there is \(a\in G^+\) such that
\[
xa=ay.
\]
If \(x\) and \(y\) are conjugate, \(\operatorname{Lg}(x)=\operatorname{Lg}(y)\). There are only finitely many \(x\in G^+\) of given length \(\operatorname{Lg}(x)\), and \(W\) is finite. The existence of a decision procedure then follows from (4.20) and the following lemma.

\paragraph{(4.24) Lemma.} Let \(x,y\in G^+\). If \(x\) and \(y\) are conjugate, there exists a sequence
\[
x_0=x,\ x_1,\ldots,x_n=y
\]
of elements of \(G^+\), and a sequence of elements \(w_i\) of \(W\), such that
\[
x_{i+1}r(w_i)=r(w_i)x_i.
\]

Let \(a\in G^+\) be such that \(xa=ay\). Write
\[
a=r(w_0)\cdots r(w_{n-1}),
\]
where \(w_i\) is the element of \(W\) of maximum length such that \(r(w_i)\cdots r(w_{n-1})\) can be written in the form \(r(w_i)b_i\), with \(b_i\in G^+\) (4.15). Let \(a_i=r(w_0)\cdots r(w_i)\). We shall prove that
\[
x_i=a_i^{-1}xa_i
\]
lies in \(G^+\), so that the \(x_i\) and \(w_i\) answer the problem.

It follows from (1.23) that, for any \(w\in W\), if \(xa\) begins with \(r(w)\), then \(xr(w_0)\) also begins with \(r(w)\). In particular, since
\[
xa=ay=r(w_0)b_0y
\]
begins with \(r(w_0)\), one has \(xr(w_0)=r(w_0)x_1\) with \(x_1\in G^+\). The proof is completed by induction on \(n\).

\paragraph{Remark.} Let \(\sigma\) be an automorphism of the Coxeter graph \(D\), and again denote by \(\sigma\) the corresponding automorphism of \(G\). One has \(\Delta^\sigma=\Delta\). The preceding arguments still apply to the question of deciding, for \(x,y\in G\), whether there exists \(a\in G\) such that \(xa=ay^\sigma\). We reduce to taking \(x,y\), and \(a\), in \(G^+\). In that case, with the preceding notation, if
\[
xa=ay^\sigma,
\]
then the \(a_i^{-1}xa_i\) lie in \(G^+\). Taking for \(\sigma\) the involution \(i\mapsto\bar i\), one obtains a criterion analogous to (4.24) for the conjugacy of \(\Delta x\) and \(\Delta y\), \(x,y\in G^+\).
