\noindent\textbf{3.2.11.} Appelons $C(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})^{+}$ (resp.\ $K(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})^{+}$) the under-cat\'{e}gorie full of $C(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})$ (resp.\ $K(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})$) form\'{e}e of the $K$ such that, for $M$ sufficiently petit, one ait $K^{n,m} = 0$ for $m < M$. For $K$ in $C(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})^{+}$, one denotes $\varepsilon_{*}K$ the complex (in $C^{+}(A(-1))$) d\'{e}finite by $(\varepsilon_{*}K)^{p} = \bigoplus_{n+m=p} p(\varepsilon_{n})_{*}K^{n,m}$ (the sum is finite) and $d = \sum d(\alpha)$ (one has abusivement not\'{e} $d(\alpha)$ the morphism d\'{e}duit of $d(\alpha)$ by direct image). One dispose of a isomorphism \'{e}vident $\varepsilon_{*}(K[1]) = (\varepsilon_{*}K)[1]$, and the functor of $K(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})^{+}$ in $K^{+}(A(-1))$ d\'{e}duit of $\varepsilon_{*}$ by passage to the quotient transforme triangles distingu\'{e}s en triangles distingu\'{e}s. For $K$ in $C^{+}(A(-1))$, the syst\`{e}me of the morphisms of adjonction $K^{n} \to p(\varepsilon_{o})_{*}p(\varepsilon_{o})^{*}K^{n}$ is a morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.2.11.1}
a : K \longrightarrow \varepsilon_{*}\varepsilon^{*}K\quad.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:3.2.12}
The functors $\varepsilon^{*}: K^{+}A(-1) \longrightarrow K(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})^{+}$ and $\varepsilon_{*}: K(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})^{+} \longrightarrow K^{+}A(-1)$ forment a couple of functors adjoints, with $a$ \eqref{eq:3.2.11.1} for morphism of adjonction.
\end{proposition}

For every cat\'{e}gorie additive $\cB$, the group of the homomorphismes of $K$ in $L$, in $K(\cB)$, is the $H^{0}$ of the complex $\Hom^{\bullet}(K,L)$ of composantes
\[
\Hom(K,L)^{d} = \prod_{m-n=d} \Hom(K^{n}, L^{m})
\]
and of diff\'{e}rentielle donn\'{e}e on $\Hom(K,L)^{d}$ by $d(f) = d \circ f - (-1)^{d} f \circ d$. We pr\'{e}ciserons \ref{prop:3.2.12} en montrant that for $K$ in $K^{+}(A(-1))$ and $L$ in $K(\mathrm{tot}\, A^{+})^{+}$, the morphism induces by $a$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.2.12.1}
\Hom(\varepsilon^{*}K, L)^{\bullet} \longrightarrow \Hom(\varepsilon_{*}\varepsilon^{*}K, \varepsilon_{*}L)^{\bullet} \longrightarrow \Hom(K, \varepsilon_{*}L)^{\bullet}
\end{equation}
is a quasi-isomorphism. Filtrons $L$ by a filtration d\'{e}croissante of quotients successifs the complexes r\'{e}duits \`{a} a $L^{n,m}$ (by example, filtrer $L$ by the $\bigoplus_{n+m \geq A} L^{n,m}$, and the quotients successifs $\bigoplus_{n+m=A} L^{n,m}$ by the $\bigoplus_{n \geq B} L^{n,A-n}$; this deuxi\`{e}me filtration is finite). On the complexes $\Hom^{\bullet}$, one r\'{e}cup\`{e}re a filtration s\'{e}by\'{e}e and compl\`{e}te, and it is enough to montrer that \eqref{eq:3.2.12.1} devient a quasi-isomorphism apr\`{e}s passage to the gradu\'{e}: one can supposer, and one assumed, $L$ concentr\'{e} en a seul bidegr\'{e}. Filtrons $K$ by the filtration by the $\sigma_{\geq n}$. This fois, the filtration obtained on $\Hom^{\bullet}$ is finite degr\'{e} by degr\'{e}, and one se ram\`{e}ne \`{a} supposer $K$ concentr\'{e} en a seul degr\'{e}. By translation, one se ram\`{e}ne enfin \`{a} supposer $K$ r\'{e}duit \`{a} a object $A$ en degr\'{e} $0$, and $L$ r\'{e}duit \`{a} a object $B$ of $A(n)$, en bidegr\'{e} $(n,0)$. one has then
\[
\Hom(\varepsilon^{*}K, L)^{d} = \prod_{\substack{\alpha: \Delta_{n-d} \to \Delta_{n} \\ n-d \geq 0}} \Hom_{\varepsilon_{n}}(A, B)\quad:
\]
the complex $\Hom(\varepsilon^{*}K, L)^{\bullet}$ is the tensor product of $\Hom_{\varepsilon_{n}}(A,B)$ by the complex of the cha\^{i}nes not d\'{e}g\'{e}n\'{e}r\'{e}es of the simplexe type $\Delta_{n}$, d\'{e}cal\'{e} of $-n$. The c\^{o}ne of \eqref{eq:3.2.12.1}, translat\'{e} by $[-1]$, is therefore donn\'{e} by the m\^{e}me formule, without the restriction $n-d \geq 0$, and it reste \`{a} utiliser that the complex augment\'{e} of the cha\^{i}nes not d\'{e}g\'{e}n\'{e}r\'{e}es of the simplexe type $\Delta_{n}$ is homotope \`{a} z\'{e}ro.

\noindent\textbf{3.2.13.} Bien that that let inutile for the sequence, we shall exhiber a morphism of complexes repr\'{e}sentant the morphism of adjonction of $\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}L$ in $L$ (3.2.13 and 3.2.14). A morphism of complexes $\underline{b}$ of $\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}L$ in $L$ is (3.2.8) a syst\`{e}me of $\alpha$-maps of degr\'{e} $s(\alpha)-b(\alpha)$, $\underline{b}(\alpha): p(\varepsilon_{s(\alpha)})^{*}(\varepsilon_{*}L) \longrightarrow L^{b(\alpha),*}$, v\'{e}rifiant (3.2.8.3). A $\alpha$-map of $p(\varepsilon_{s(\alpha)})^{*}(\varepsilon_{*}L)$ in $L^{b(\alpha),*}$ s'identifie \`{a} a $\varepsilon_{b(\alpha)}$-map $b'(\alpha)$ of $\varepsilon_{*}L$ in $L^{b(\alpha),*}$. We \'{e}crirons formally $\underline{b} = \sum_{\alpha} b'(\alpha) \otimes \alpha$, identifiant ainsi $\underline{b}$ \`{a} a \'{e}l\'{e}ment of the product on $n$ of the groups
\begin{align*}
\{&\varepsilon_{n}\text{-application de }\varepsilon_{*}L\text{ dans }L^{n,*}) \otimes (\text{cha\^{i}nes non d\'{e}g\'{e}n\'{e}r\'{e}es de }\Delta_{n}\}\quad.
\end{align*}

A son tour, a $\varepsilon_{n}$-map $b'$ of degr\'{e} $s$ of $\varepsilon_{*}L$ in $L^{n,*}$ s'identifie \`{a} a family of $\varepsilon_{n}$-maps $b'_{p}: p(\varepsilon_{p})_{*}L^{p,*} \longrightarrow L^{n,*}$, of degr\'{e}s $s+p$. We ne consid\'{e}rons that of the maps $b'_{p}$ sum of maps of the types suivants, index\'{e}s by $\beta: \Delta_{p} \to \Delta_{n}$: the compos\'{e} of the $\varepsilon_{p}$-map natural of $\varepsilon_{p*}L^{p,*}$ in $L^{p,*}$, and of a $\beta$-map $b''(\beta): L^{p,*} \longrightarrow L^{n,*}$. We \'{e}crirons formally $b' = \sum_{\beta} b''(\beta)$.

To the total, this we am\`{e}ne \`{a} chercher $\underline{b}$ under the form
\[
\underline{b} = \sum_{\alpha,\beta} b''(\alpha,\beta) \otimes \alpha\quad,
\]
with $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of m\^{e}me but, $b''(\alpha,\beta)$ \'{e}tant a $\beta$-morphism of $L^{s(\beta),*}$ in $L^{b(\beta),*}$, of degr\'{e} $s(\alpha)-b(\alpha)+s(\beta) = (s(\beta)-b(\beta)) + s(\alpha)$. For each factorisation $\beta = \beta_{1} \cdots \beta_{s(\alpha)}$, one has \`{a} sa disposition a $\beta$-map of this degr\'{e}: the compos\'{e} of the $d(\beta_{i})$.

We shall montrer that the syst\`{e}me suivant convient:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.2.13.1}
\underline{b} = \sum_{k,m} \sum_{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}} d(\beta_{1}) \cdots d(\beta_{k}) \otimes (0, \beta_{1}(0), \beta_{1}\beta_{2}(0), \ldots, \beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{k}(0))\;.
\end{equation}
The sum porte on $k,m$ and on the sequences of $k$ maps composables of which the compos\'{e} a for but $\Delta_{m}$. The sequence vide ($k=0$) is permise: for each $m$, one has a terme $\Id_{L^{m,*}} \otimes (0)$. one has not\'{e} $(n_{0},\ldots,n_{k+1})$ the map $i \mapsto n_{i}$ of $\Delta_{k}$ in $\Delta_{m}$; remplacer by $0$ (or omettre the terme) si this map n'is not strictement croissante.

It s'agit of montrer that $\underline{b}$ is a morphism of complexes, and that $\underline{b}$ correspond by the adjonction (\ref{prop:3.2.12}):
\[
\Hom(\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}L, L) = \Hom(\varepsilon_{*}L, \varepsilon_{*}L)
\]
\`{a} the map identique of $\varepsilon_{*}L$, i.e.\ that the morphism of complexes compos\'{e}
\[
\varepsilon_{*}L \xrightarrow{\quad a\quad} \varepsilon_{*}\varepsilon^{*}\varepsilon_{*}L \xrightarrow{\quad \varepsilon_{*}\underline{b}\quad} \varepsilon_{*}L
\]
is homotope \`{a} the identit\'{e}. One v\'{e}rifie facilement that this morphism is m\^{e}me \'{e}gal \`{a} the identit\'{e}: it ne d\'{e}pend that of the composantes $b'(\alpha) \otimes \alpha$ of $\underline{b}$, for $\alpha: \Delta_{0} \to \Delta_{m}$, and these composantes are of the type $\Id_{L^{m,*}} \otimes (0)$.

\noindent\textbf{3.2.14.} $\underline{b}$ \emph{is a morphism of complexes}. the identit\'{e} \`{a} v\'{e}rifier s'\'{e}crit $d\underline{b} = \underline{b}d$, i.e.
\begin{multline*}
(*) \sum_{\alpha} \sum_{k,m=s(\alpha)} \sum_{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}} d(\alpha)d(\beta_{1})\cdots d(\beta_{k}) \otimes \alpha \circ (0,\beta_{1}(0),\ldots,\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{k}(0)) \\
= \sum_{k,m} \sum_{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}} (-1)^{k} \sum_{\alpha} d(\beta_{1})\cdots d(\beta_{k})d(\alpha) \otimes (0,\beta_{1}(0),\ldots,\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{k}(0)) \\
+ \sum_{k,m} \sum_{\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}} \sum_{i} (-1)^{i} d(\beta_{1})\cdots d(\beta_{k}) \otimes (0,\beta_{1}(0),\ldots,\widehat{\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{i}(0)},\ldots,\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{k}(0))\;.
\end{multline*}
The sums on $k,m,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}$ are as en \eqref{eq:3.2.13.1}. The sum on $i$ va of $0$ \`{a} $k$, sauf for $k=0$, o\`{u} it is vide. One notera that si $c = (0,\beta_{1}(0),\ldots,\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{k}(0))$ is a cha\^{i}ne d\'{e}g\'{e}n\'{e}r\'{e}e of $\Delta_{m}$, one has $\sum (-1)^{i} c \circ \partial_{i} = 0$ mod cha\^{i}nes d\'{e}g\'{e}n\'{e}r\'{e}es. For v\'{e}rifier the identit\'{e} $(*)$, it is enough to montrer that for each $k \geq 0$ (corresponding \`{a} $k$ in the deux prime termes and \`{a} $k-1$ for the troisi\`{e}me), one has
\begin{multline*}
\sum_{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{k}} d(\beta_{0})\cdots d(\beta_{k}) \otimes (\beta_{0}(0), \beta_{0}\beta_{1}(0), \ldots, \beta_{0}\cdots\beta_{k}(0)) \\
- (-1)^{k} \sum_{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{k}} d(\beta_{0})\cdots d(\beta_{k}) \otimes (0, \beta_{0}(0), \ldots, \beta_{0}\cdots\beta_{k-1}(0)) = \\
= \sum_{\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{k}} \sum_{-1 \leq i \leq k} (-1)^{i+1} d(\beta_{0})\cdots d(\beta_{k}) \otimes (0, \beta_{0}(0), \ldots, \widehat{\beta_{0}\cdots\beta_{i}(0)}, \ldots, \beta_{0}\cdots\beta_{k}(0))\;.
\end{multline*}
The sums portent on the cha\^{i}nes of $k+1$ morphisms composables, and $(n_{0},\ldots,n_{k})$ d\'{e}signe a map of $\Delta_{k}$ in $\Delta_{b(\beta_{0})}$ (and $0$ si this map n'is not injective). The termes to the membre of gauche correspondent \`{a} $i=-1$ and $i=k$ to the membre of line. For conclure, it is enough to v\'{e}rifier that for each $k \geq 0$, each $i$ ($-1 < i < k$) and each choix of $\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{i-1}$, of the compos\'{e} $\beta = \beta_{i}\beta_{i+1}$, and of $\beta_{i+2},\ldots,\beta_{k}$, choix which suffit \`{a} fixer the cha\^{i}ne $(0,\beta_{0}(0),\ldots,\beta_{0}\cdots\beta_{i}(0)^{\wedge},\ldots,\beta_{0}\cdots\beta_{k}(0))$, one has
\[
\sum_{\beta_{i}\beta_{i+1}=\beta} d(\beta_{0})\cdots d(\beta_{k}) = 0\quad.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:4.1.1}
Si $f: X \to Y$ is a morphism affine, the functor $Rf_{*}: D_{c}^{b}(X,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}) \to D_{c}^{b}(Y,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})$ is $t$-exact \`{a} line.
\end{theorem}

One se ram\`{e}ne \`{a} prouver the \'{e}nonc\'{e} analogue en $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-cohomology (cf.\ 4.0).

Si the sheaf $F$ is constructible, the integer $d(F)$ that M.~Artin attached to} $F$ is the more petit integer $d$ such that $F$, vu as object of the cat\'{e}gorie d\'{e}riv\'{e}e, let in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq d}$. For $K$ constructible in the cat\'{e}gorie d\'{e}riv\'{e}e, $d(K) := \sup(i + d(H^{i}K))$ is of m\^{e}me the more petit $d$ such that $K$ let in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq d}$. For that $K$ let in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}$, it is necessary and sufficient that the $H^{i}K[-i]$ the let. For v\'{e}rifier that $Rf_{*}$ envoie ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(X)$ in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(Y)$, it is enough therefore of v\'{e}rifier that for every sheaf $F$ with $d(F) \leq d$, one has $d(R^{i}f_{*}F) \leq d-i$. C'is this that affirme SGA4 XIV 3.1.

By map of the dualit\'{e} of Verdier, the th\'{e}or\`{e}me \ref{thm:4.1.1} se dualise en the

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:4.1.2}
Under the m\^{e}me hypoth\`{e}se, $Rf_{!}$ is $t$-exact \`{a} gauche.
\end{corollary}

We shall denote ${}^{p}f_{*}$ and ${}^{p}f_{!}$ the functors ${}^{p}H^{0}Rf_{*}$ and ${}^{p}H^{0}Rf_{!}$ of the perverse sheaves on $X$ in the perverse sheaves on $Y$. The prime is exact \`{a} line, the second \`{a} gauche. For the compos\'{e} $fg$ of deux morphisms affine, one has
\[
{}^{p}(fg)_{*} = {}^{p}f_{*}\,{}^{p}g_{*} \quad \text{et} \quad {}^{p}(fg)_{!} = {}^{p}f_{!}\,{}^{p}g_{!}\qquad \text{(1.3.17)(iv)}
\]
We \'{e}crirons $f_{*}$ and $f_{!}$ for $Rf_{*}$ and $Rf_{!}$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:4.1.3}
Si $f: X \to Y$ is quasi-finite and affine, the functors $f_{!}$ and $f_{*}$ are $t$-exacts.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, $f_{*}$ ($:= Rf_{*}$) is $t$-exact \`{a} gauche (2.2.5) and \`{a} line (\ref{thm:4.1.1}). The cas of $f_{!}$ is dual (2.2.5 and \ref{cor:4.1.2}).

Supposons $k$ alg\'{e}briquement clos. For $Y = \Spec(k)$, \ref{thm:4.1.1} fournit the corollary SGA4 XIV 3.2:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:4.1.4}
For $X$ affine on $k$ alg\'{e}briquement clos, and all sheaf \'{e}tale $F$ on $X$, $H^{i}(X,F) = 0$ for $i > \dim X$.
\end{corollary}

More g\'{e}n\'{e}ralement, one has

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:4.1.5}
Supposons $k$ alg\'{e}briquement clos. Si $K$ is in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(X)$ and that $u: Z \to X$ is a morphism quasi-finite of source affine, one has $H^{i}(Z,K) = 0$ for $i > 0$.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, $u^{*}K$ is in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(Z)$ (analogue of 2.2.5) and one applique \ref{thm:4.1.1} \`{a} the projection of $Z$ on $\Spec(k)$.

This corollary admet the r\'{e}ciproque suivante.

\begin{corollary}[R\'{e}ciproque~4.1.6]\label{cor:4.1.6}
Supposons $k$ alg\'{e}briquement clos, and let $K$ in $D_{c}^{b}(X)$. Si, for every open of Zariski affine $V$ of $X$, one has $H^{i}(V,K) = 0$ for $i > 0$, then $K$ is in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{\leq 0}(X)$.
\end{corollary}

We v\'{e}rifierons of abord the

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:4.1.7}
Let $X$ affine smooth irr\'{e}ductible of dimension $d \geq 1$ on $k$ alg\'{e}briquement clos, and $F$ a sheaf smooth on $X$. Si $F \neq 0$, there exists a open affine $U$ of $X$, of the form $X[1/f]$ ($f \neq 0$), such that $H^{d}(U,F) \neq 0$.
\end{lemma}

Let $f_{1},\ldots,f_{d} \in \Gamma(X,\cO_{X})$ such that the under-sch\'{e}ma $S$ of $X$ d\'{e}finite by the \'{e}quations $f_{i} = 0$ let finite not vide. Si $F \neq 0$, one has $H^{2d}_{S}(X,F) \cong (F \mid S)(-d) \neq 0$ (puret\'{e}), and the exact sequence
\[
H^{2d-1}(X-S,F) \longrightarrow H^{2d}_{S}(X,F) \longrightarrow H^{2d}(X,F)
\]
shows that $H^{2d-1}(X-S,F) \neq 0$. Indeed, of apr\`{e}s \ref{cor:4.1.4}, $H^{2d}(X,F) = 0$ puisque $2d > d$. Recouvrons $X-S$ by the ouverts $U_{i} := X[1/f_{i}]$ and, for $I \subset [1,d]$, posons $U_{I} = \bigcap_{i \in I} U_{i}$. The $U_{I}$ are affine, and in what follows spectral of \v{C}ech
\[
E_{1}^{pq} = \bigoplus_{|I|=p+1} H^{q}(U_{I},F) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X-S,F)\quad,
\]
one has therefore $E_{1}^{pq} = 0$ for $q > d$. one has also $E_{1}^{pq} = 0$ for $p > d-1$. The terme $E_{1}^{d-1,d}$ is the seul which contribue to the $H^{2d-1}$; it is therefore not zero: one has
\[
H^{d}(X[(\prod f_{i})^{-1}], F) \neq 0\quad.
\]

\noindent\textbf{4.1.8.} \emph{Proof of \ref{cor:4.1.6}}. Let $F_{i}$ ($0 \leq i \leq \dim X$) a sequence croissante of ferm\'{e}s of $X$, of dernier terme $X$. Posons $F_{-1} = \emptyset$. One assumed the sequence $F_{\bullet}$ choisie of telle sorte that $W_{i} = F_{i} - F_{i-1}$ (with the structure r\'{e}duite) let smooth purement of dimension $i$ and that the $H^{j}K$ let smooth on the $W_{i}$. Prouvons by r\'{e}currence descendante on $i$ that, on $U_{i} = X - F_{i-1}$, $K$ is in $D^{\leq -i}$ (for the $t$-structure natural).

the hypoth\`{e}se of r\'{e}currence: ``$K \mid U_{j}$ is in $D^{\leq -j}$ for $j > i$'' is vide for $i = \dim X$. It assure that, on $U_{i}$, $\tau_{\leq -i}K$ is in ${}^{p}D^{\leq 0}$, and that for $j \geq -i$, $H^{j}K \mid U_{i}$ is a sheaf smooth on $W_{i}$, prolong\'{e} by z\'{e}ro. For every open affine $V$ of $U_{i}$, the triangle $(\tau_{\leq -i}K, K, \tau_{> -i}K)$ fournit a exact sequence longue of cohomology
\[
H^{j}(V, \tau_{\leq -i}K) \longrightarrow H^{j}(V,K) \longrightarrow H^{j}(V, \tau_{> -i}K) \longrightarrow\;.
\]
of apr\`{e}s \ref{cor:4.1.5}, $H^{j}(V, \tau_{\leq -i}K) = 0$ for $j > 0$. the hypoth\`{e}se $H^{j}(V,K) = 0$ for $j > 0$ fournit therefore that for $j > 0$
\[
H^{j}(V, \tau_{> -i}K) = H^{j}(V \cap W_{i}, \tau_{> -i}K) = 0\quad.
\]
of apr\`{e}s \ref{cor:4.1.4}, the terme $E_{2}$ of the spectral sequence
\[
E_{2}^{pq} = H^{p}(V \cap W_{i}, H^{q}(\tau_{> -i}K)) \Rightarrow H^{p+q}(V \cap W_{i}, \tau_{> -i}K)
\]
is zero for $p > i$. Si $H^{j}(\tau_{> -i}K) = 0$ for $j > q$, one has therefore
\[
H^{i}(V \cap W_{i}, H^{q}(\tau_{> -i}K)) = H^{i+q}(V \cap W_{i}, \tau_{> -i}K)
\]
(zero si $q > -i$). Posons $L = H^{q}(\tau_{> -i}K)$. C'is a sheaf smooth on $W_{i}$ and it s'agit, of the hypoth\`{e}se $H^{i}(V \cap W_{i}, L) = 0$ for every $V \subset U_{i}$ affine, of d\'{e}duire that $L = 0$. C'is clear for $i = 0$. Supposons therefore $i \geq 1$. Si $L$ \'{e}tait not zero, it existerait $V_{1} \subset U_{i}$, affine, such that $L \mid V_{1} \cap W_{i}$ let not zero. of apr\`{e}s \ref{lem:4.1.7} appliqu\'{e} to the composantes irr\'{e}ductibles of $V_{1} \cap W_{i}$, it existerait $f_{1} \in \Gamma(V_{1} \cap W_{i}, \cO)$ such that $H^{i}(V_{1} \cap W_{i}[1/f_{1}], L) \neq 0$. This $f_{1}$ se rel\`{e}ve en $f \in \Gamma(V_{1}, \cO)$ and, for $V = V_{1}[1/f]$, one has $H^{i}(V \cap W_{i}, L) \neq 0$: contradiction.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:4.1.9}
For $k = \mathbb{C}$, the analogue of \ref{thm:4.1.1} en cohomology complex is vrai, and can se d\'{e}duire of \ref{thm:4.1.1} gr\^{a}this to the th\'{e}or\`{e}mes of comparaison. En voici a interpr\'{e}tation partielle, en termes of modules holonomes.

Prenons $X$ smooth purement of dimension $d$. On $X$, there is identit\'{e} between the notions of sheaf (alg\'{e}brique) quasi-coh\'{e}rent \`{a} connexion int\'{e}grable and of $\mathcal{D}$-module, quasi-coh\'{e}rent as $\cO$-module (or as $\mathcal{D}$-module, that revient to the m\^{e}me). \`{A} each tel sheaf $(\mathcal{V}, \nabla)$ is associ\'{e} son de Rham complex $\Omega^{*}(\mathcal{V})$, and, on $X(\mathbb{C})$, the de Rham complex analytic $\Omega^{*}(\mathcal{V})^{\mathrm{an}}$ of composantes the $\Omega^{i}(\mathcal{V}) \otimes_{\cO} \cO^{\mathrm{an}}$. One sait that, si the $\mathcal{D}$-module $\mathcal{V}$ is holonome, $\Omega^{*}(\mathcal{V})[d]^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a perverse sheaf, and that the functor $\mathcal{V} \mapsto \Omega^{*}(\mathcal{V})[d]^{\mathrm{an}}$ induces a \'{e}quivalence of the cat\'{e}gorie of the $\mathcal{D}$-modules holonomes \`{a} singularit\'{e}s r\'{e}guli\`{e}res (y compris \`{a} the infini) with celle of the perverse sheaves.

Si $f: X \subset Y$ is a embedding open in $Y$ smooth purement of dimension $d$, via this \'{e}quivalence, ${}^{p}f_{*}$ correspond to the functor direct image of sheaves quasi-coh\'{e}rents (the connexion suit). Si $f$ is en outre affine, it is exact, as pr\'{e}vu by \ref{cor:4.1.3}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:4.1.10}
Let $j: U \into X$ a embedding open affine, and $i: D \into X$ the inclusion of the ferm\'{e} compl\'{e}mentaire (by example: $D$ a diviseur (of Cartier), $U = X-D$). Recall that one \'{e}crit $j_{*}$ and $i^{!}$ for $Rj_{*}$ and $Ri^{!}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Si $G$ is a perverse sheaf on $U$, $j_{!}G$ and $j_{*}G$ are pervers.
\item[(ii)] Si $F$ is a perverse sheaf on $X$, $i^{*}F$ is in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{[-1,0]}$, and $i^{!}F$ in ${}^{p}D_{c}^{[0,1]}$. One dispose of sequences exact of perverse sheaves

\end{corollary}

the assertion (i) is a cas particulier of \ref{cor:4.1.3}. The sequences exact longues of cohomology perverse of the triangles $(j_{!}j^{*}F, F, i_{*}i^{*}F)$ and $(i_{*}i^{!}F, F, j_{*}j^{*}F)$ fournissent \eqref{eq:4.1.10.1}, \eqref{eq:4.1.10.2} and the nullit\'{e} of the ${}^{p}H^{i}i_{*}i^{*}F = i_{*}{}^{p}H^{i}i^{*}F$ (resp.\ ${}^{p}H^{i}i_{*}i^{!}F = i_{*}{}^{p}H^{i}i^{!}F$) for $i \neq -1,0$ (resp.\ $0,1$) therefore (ii).

\noindent\textbf{4.1.11.} Prenons in \eqref{eq:4.1.10.1} $F$ of the form $j_{*}G$. One obtains for the sheaves of cohomology perverse of $i^{*}j_{*}G$ a exact sequence

\noindent\textbf{4.2.1.} Si $T: \cD_{1} \to \cD_{2}$ is a exact functor between cat\'{e}gories triangul\'{e}es equipped with $t$-structures, one dit that $T$ is of \emph{amplitude cohomologique} $[a,b]$ ($-\infty \leq a \leq b \leq \infty$) si $T\cD_{1}^{\leq 0} \subset \cD_{2}^{\leq b}$ (for $b \neq \infty$) and that $T\cD_{1}^{\geq 0} \subset \cD_{2}^{\geq a}$ (for $a \neq -\infty$), i.e.\ si $T[b]$ is $t$-exact \`{a} line (for $b \neq \infty$) and that $T[a]$ is $t$-exact \`{a} gauche (for $a \neq -\infty$). \begin{proposition}\label{prop:4.2.5}
Si $f$ is smooth of dimension relative $d$, \`{a} fibers g\'{e}om\'{e}triques connected (and therefore by d\'{e}finition not vides), the functor $t$-exact $f^{*}[d]$ induces a functor pleinement fid\`{e}the of the perverse sheaves on $Y$ in the perverse sheaves on $X$.
\end{proposition}

In the proof, we shall use the functor usuel ${}^{0}f_{*}$ for the sheaves. It will be not\'{e} ${}^{0}f_{*}$.

For $K$ and $L$ in $D_{c}^{b}(Y,\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})$, one has, parce that $f$ is smooth,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.2.5.1}
f^{*}\RHom(K,L) \isom \RHom(f^{*}K, f^{*}L)\quad.
\end{equation}
for $f$ quelconque, one aurait plut\^{o}t
\[
f^{!}\RHom(K,L) \isom \RHom(f^{*}K, f^{!}L)\quad.
\]
For $K$ and $L$ pervers, $\RHom(K,L)$ is in $D_{c}^{\geq 0}$. Of m\^{e}me for $\RHom(f^{*}K, f^{*}L) = \RHom(f^{*}K[d], f^{*}L[d])$. Appliquant ${}^{0}f_{*}H^{0}$ \`{a} \eqref{eq:4.2.5.1}, one obtains therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.2.5.2}
{}^{0}f_{*}f^{*}H^{0}\RHom(K,L) \longrightarrow {}^{0}f_{*}H^{0}\RHom(f^{*}K[d], f^{*}L[d])\;.
\end{equation}
Admettons provisoirement that for $H$ on $Y$ one ait
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.2.5.3}
H \isom {}^{0}f_{*}f^{*}H\quad.
\end{equation}
Of \eqref{eq:4.2.5.2}, one d\'{e}duit then by passage to the sections globales that
\[
\Gamma H^{0}\RHom(K,L) \isom \Gamma H^{0}\RHom(f^{*}K[d], f^{*}L[d])\quad.
\]
To the deux membres, $\RHom$ is in $D_{c}^{\geq 0}$, of o\`{u}
\[
\Gamma H^{0}\RHom = H^{0}\R\Gamma\RHom = H^{0}\RHom = \Hom\;,
\]
and the assertion.

The proof of \eqref{eq:4.2.5.3} se ram\`{e}ne to the cas of the sheaves of sets. The morphism $f$ a locally a section; this permet of supposer that $f$ a section $e$. \noindent\textbf{5.1. Rappels of [1]}.

\noindent\textbf{5.1.1.} Let $X_{0}$ a sch\'{e}ma of type finite on $\mathbb{F}_{q}$, and $X$ the sch\'{e}ma on $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}$ which s'en d\'{e}duit by extension of scalars. The Galois group of $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}$ on $\mathbb{F}_{q}$ a for g\'{e}n\'{e}rateur topologique the Frobenius $\varphi: x \mapsto x^{q}$, and a repr\'{e}sentation $\ell$-adique of this Galois group, i.e.\ a $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}$-sheaf smooth of rank $n$ on $\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q})$, is d\'{e}termin\'{e} by the matrice of Frobenius $F \in GL(n, \overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})$, d\'{e}finite \`{a} conjugaison pr\`{e}s. One dit that this sheaf is \emph{pur of weight} $w$ ($w \in \mathbb{Z}$) si for every value proper $\alpha$ of $F$, one has $|\alpha|^{2} = q^{w}$ (for every embedding of $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ in $\mathbb{C}$, one has $|\alpha| = q^{w/2}$).

It is dit \emph{pur of weight} $w$ s'it is extension successive of sheaves ponctuellement purs of weight $w$ on the adh\'{e}rences of strates of a stratification of $X$. For $X$ normal irr\'{e}ductible, that signifie that the restriction of $F$ to the point g\'{e}n\'{e}rique is pure of weight $w$, that it admet a prolongement smooth on a open $j: U \into X$ and that $j_{!}j^{*}F \to F$ is a isomorphism (by d\'{e}vissage and r\'{e}duction to the cas smooth, [1] 3.4.1).

Under the hypoth\`{e}ses of \ref{thm:5.2.1}, supposons $K$ in ${}^{p}D_{m}^{\leq 0}$. Then ${}^{p}H^{0}K$ is pure of weight $w$.
\end{lemma}

Si $K$ is in ${}^{p}D_{m}^{\leq 0}$, one has ${}^{p}H^{i}K = 0$ for $i > 0$, and one ne s'int\'{e}resse that \`{a} ${}^{p}H^{0}K$. Let therefore $F$ a perverse sheaf of weight $\leq 0$, with $DF$ of weight $\leq 0$, and prouvons that $F$ is pure of weight $0$. Recall that for every perverse sheaf $F$, $F$ is extension successive of $i_{*}i^{*}F$ ($i$ parcourt the inclusions $Y \into X$ of under-sch\'{e}mas ferm\'{e}s irr\'{e}ductibles), and that the functor $i^{*}$ is $t$-exact \`{a} line. one has therefore $i^{*}F$ in ${}^{p}D_{m}^{\leq 0}$, of weight $\leq 0$, with $Di^{*}F$ of weight $\leq 0$ (dualit\'{e} of Verdier). One se ram\`{e}ne \`{a} supposer $X$ irr\'{e}ductible and $F$ \`{a} support $X$. Let $j: U \into X$ a open not vide of $X$ to the-dessus duquel $F$ is smooth. The triangle $(j_{!}j^{*}F, F, i_{*}i^{*}F)$, for $i$ the inclusion of the ferm\'{e} compl\'{e}mentaire, shows that $j_{!}j^{*}F$ is of weight $\leq 0$, and son dual $j_{*}j^{*}DF$ is of weight $\leq 0$. The sheaf $j^{*}F$ is smooth on $U$, of weight $\leq 0$. Supposons-the of weight $< 0$, i.e.\ of weight $\leq -1$. The sheaf $j_{*}j^{*}DF = D(j_{!}j^{*}F)$ serait then of weight $\geq 1$, and ne saurait \^{e}tre of weight $\leq 0$. The sheaf $j^{*}F$ is therefore pure of weight $0$. Puisque $F$ is \`{a} support $X$, one has a exact sequence
\[
0 \longrightarrow A \longrightarrow j_{!}j^{*}F \longrightarrow F \longrightarrow B \longrightarrow 0\quad,
\]
with $A$ and $B$ \`{a} support in $X-U$. The sheaf $j_{!}j^{*}F$ is of weight $\leq 0$, with $D(j_{!}j^{*}F) = j_{*}j^{*}DF$ of weight $\leq 0$; as $j^{*}F$ is pur of weight $0$, $j_{!}j^{*}F$ is pur of weight $0$ (image of a morphism of purs, 5.1.2(f)). The under-quotients $A$, $F$, $B$ of $j_{!}j^{*}F$ are therefore of weight $\leq 0$. For $A$ and $B$, the dual is of weight $\leq 0$, car this are of the under-quotients of $j_{!}j^{*}F$, of which the dual is of weight $\leq 0$. the hypoth\`{e}se of r\'{e}currence noeth\'{e}rienne on $X$, appliqu\'{e}e \`{a} $X-U$, shows that $A$ and $B$ are purs of weight $0$. It en r\'{e}sulte that $F$ is pur of weight $0$.

\noindent\textbf{5.2.4.} Prouvons \ref{thm:5.2.1}. One se ram\`{e}ne by translation \`{a} supposer that $w = 0$, i.e.\ that $K$ is of weight $\leq 0$ and $DK$ of weight $\leq 0$. of apr\`{e}s \ref{lem:5.2.2}, one has then $K$ in ${}^{p}D_{m}^{\leq 0} \cap {}^{p}D_{m}^{\geq 0}$, i.e.\ $K$ is a perverse sheaf. The proposition r\'{e}sulte then of \ref{lem:5.2.3}.

\noindent\textbf{5.3.3.} Prouvons \ref{thm:5.3.1} by r\'{e}currence noeth\'{e}rienne on $X_{0}$. the assertion is local on $S_{0}$; one can therefore supposer $S_{0}$ affine, then, the probl\`{e}me \'{e}tant of nature local on $X_{0}$, supposer $X_{0}$ affine.
