\title{Complex Vector Bundles with Discrete Structure Group}
\author{Pierre Deligne and Dennis Sullivan}
\date{C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris, t. 281, Serie A, 22 December 1975}
\maketitle

Every complex vector bundle whose structure group can be reduced to a discrete group, and whose base is a compact polyhedron, becomes trivial after passage to a finite covering of the base.

\begin{theorem*}
Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex local system of rank $n$ on a compact polyhedron $X$, and let $\mathscr V$ be the corresponding complex vector bundle. There exists a finite surjective covering $\pi:\widetilde X\to X$ such that the inverse image of $\mathscr V$ on $\widetilde X$ is trivial as a vector bundle.
\end{theorem*}

We may assume that $X$ is connected and has a base point $p$. Once a basis of $V_p$ has been chosen, the local system $V$ corresponds to a homomorphism
\[
\rho:\pi_1(X,p)\to \GL(n,\C).
\]
Since $\pi_1(X,p)$ is finitely generated, there is a subring $A$ of $\C$, finitely generated over $\Z$, such that $\rho$ factors through $\GL(n,A)$: choose a finite set of generators $(g_i)$ for $\pi_1(X,p)$ and let $A$ be generated by the matrix coefficients of the $g_i$. Let $m_1$ and $m_2$ be two maximal ideals of $A$ such that the finite fields $A/m_1$ and $A/m_2$ have distinct characteristics. We take for $\widetilde X$ the covering of $X$ corresponding to the subgroup of $\pi_1(X,p)$ consisting of the elements $g$ such that $\rho(g)\equiv1$ modulo both $m_1$ and $m_2$. Its index divides the order of $\GL(n,A/m_1)\times\GL(n,A/m_2)$. Showing that this $\widetilde X$ works is equivalent to proving the following proposition.

\begin{proposition*}
Suppose that $V$ is defined by a representation $\rho:\pi_1(X,p)\to\GL(n,A)$, where $A$ is a subring of $\C$ finitely generated over $\Z$. If there are two maximal ideals $m_1$ and $m_2$ of $A$, of distinct residue characteristics, such that $\rho$ is trivial modulo $m_1$ and modulo $m_2$, then $\mathscr V$ is trivial.
\end{proposition*}

Let $d$ and $N$ be integers with $\dim X\le d$ and $N$ large enough, say $N\ge d/2$. The Grassmannian $G_{n,N}$ of $n$-planes in $\C^{n+N}$ is a sufficiently good approximation to $B\GL(n,\C)$ that
\[
[X,G_{n,N}]=[X,B\GL(n,\C)],
\]
and $\mathscr V$ corresponds to a homotopy class of maps $v:X\to G_{n,N}$. Let $X'$ be the fibration over $X$ whose fiber over $x\in X$ is the space, $(2N+1)$-connected and hence $(d+1)$-connected, of linear injections from $\mathscr V_x$ into $\C^{n+N}$. Let $v':X'\to G_{n,N}$ send an injection to its image. The projection $\pi:X'\to X$ has a section $s$, unique up to homotopy, and $v=v's$.

\[
\begin{tikzcd}[row sep=2em,column sep=3em]
X' \arrow[rr,"v'"] \arrow[d,"\pi"'] && G_{n,N}\\
X \arrow[u,"s"'] \arrow[urr,"v"'] &&
\end{tikzcd}
\]

The following conditions are equivalent: (a) $\mathscr V$ is trivial; (b) $v\sim0$, i.e. $v$ is homotopic to a constant map; (c) $v'$ is homotopic to zero on the $d$-skeleton of $X'$, i.e. for every $w:K\to X'$ with $\dim K\le d$ (or just for $K$ the $d$-skeleton of a triangulation of $X'$), one has $v'w\sim0$; (d) the composite
\[
v'':X'\xrightarrow{v'}G_{n,N}\to \cosq_d(G_{n,N}),
\]
where $\cosq_d(G_{n,N})$ is the $d$-th stage of the Postnikov decomposition of $G_{n,N}$, having the same homotopy groups $\pi_i$ for $i\le d$, is homotopic to zero.

The equivalence of (c) and (d) is general for any map $v'$. The equivalence of (b) and (c) follows from the fact that, for every complex $K$ of dimension $\le d$, the map $[K,X']\to[K,X]$ is an isomorphism.

Since $G_{n,N}$ is simply connected, it follows from the Hasse principle for maps \cite[Theorem 3.1]{SullivanMaps} that $v''\sim0$ if and only if, for every $l$, its $l$-adic completion
\[
v''_l:X'\widehat{\ }_l\to \cosq_d(G_{n,N})\widehat{\ }_l=\cosq_d((G_{n,N})\widehat{\ }_l)
\]
is homotopic to zero. The proposition follows from the following lemma.

\begin{lemma*}
Suppose $V$ is defined by $\rho:\pi_1(X,p)\to\GL(n,A)$, where $A$ is a subring of $\C$ finitely generated over $\Z$. If there is a maximal ideal $m$ of $A$, of residue characteristic different from the prime number $l$, such that $\rho$ is trivial modulo $m$, then $v''_l\sim0$.
\end{lemma*}

We may suppose that $X$ is a union of faces of the simplex $\Delta\subset\R^M$ spanned by the basis vectors. For each face $\sigma$ of $\Delta$, let $\sigma_\R$ be the affine subspace spanned by $\sigma$, and let $\sigma_\Z$ be the affine subvariety of affine space $\mathbf A^M_\Z$ over $\Z$ whose real points are $\sigma_\R$. Let $X_\Z$ be the union of the $\sigma_\Z$ for $\sigma\subset X$. This is a scheme over $\Z$; for any ring $B$, denote by $X_B$ the scheme over $B$ obtained from it by extension of scalars. The inclusion of $X$ into $X_\Z(\C)\subset\C^M$ is a homotopy equivalence. For $l\neq p$, the mod $l$ cohomology of $X$, or of $X_\Z(\C)$, agrees with the mod $l$ etale cohomology of $X_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p}$. By Mayer-Vietoris, this follows from the corresponding fact for affine space (SGA 4, XV 2.2).

The representation $\rho$ gives a vector bundle $\mathscr V_A$ on $X_A$. The bundle $\mathscr V$ is obtained by extension of scalars to $\C$, and its reduction modulo $m$ is trivial. The definition of $X'$ is purely algebraic, and gives a diagram of schemes over $A$:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=2em]
X_A & X'_A \arrow[l,"s_A"'] \arrow[r,"v'_A"] & (G_{n,N})_A .
\end{tikzcd}
\]

Use a subscript $1$ for the analogous objects attached to the unit representation of $\pi_1(X,p)$ in $\GL(n,A)$. The morphisms of schemes $v'_A$ and $v'_{1A}$ become isomorphic after reduction modulo $m$. If $k$ is an algebraic closure of $A/m$, and if $B$ is the strict Henselization of $A$ at $k$, then there is a chain of isomorphisms between morphisms of $l$-completed homotopy types:
\[
v'_l\sim (v'_\C)_l\sim (v'_B)_l\sim (v'_k)_l\sim (v'_{1k})_l\sim (v'_{1B})_l\sim (v'_{1\C})_l\sim v'_{1l}.
\]
Therefore
\[
v''_l\sim v''_{1l}\sim0.
\]
In this chain, the two extreme terms are completions of morphisms between ordinary spaces \cite[Section 3]{SullivanMaps}; the other terms are completions of morphisms between etale homotopy types \cite{ArtinMazur}.

\begin{remarks*}
(a) If $N$ is large and $2p\ge d$, the map induced by Chern classes
\[
\cosq_d(G_{n,N})\to \cosq_d\!\left(\prod_1^nK(\Z,2i)\right)
\]
is an isomorphism after localization at $p$. One may deduce that the hypothesis of the proposition can be replaced by the following one: $\rho$ is trivial modulo a maximal ideal $m$ of residue characteristic $p$ such that $H_*(X)$ has no $p$-torsion and $2p\ge\dim X$.

(b) In (a), and in the proposition, one may replace ``$\rho$ is trivial modulo $m$'' by ``modulo $m$, the image of $\rho$ is contained in the unipotent radical of a Borel subgroup.''
\end{remarks*}

\begin{remarks*}
(c) The theorem strengthens the differential-geometric theorem \cite{Milnor} asserting that the real Chern classes of a vector bundle with integrable connection are trivial. Its proof recalls Grothendieck's algebraic proof of that fact \cite{GrothendieckChern}.

(d) The problem was suggested to one of us by \cite{SullivanInf}; the theorem extends the range of application of computations made there (Theorems C.2 and D.2).

(e) The analogue of the theorem for real vector bundles with discrete structure group in $\operatorname{SL}(n,\R)$ is false: the real Euler class may be nonzero already for $n=2$ and $X$ a Riemann surface.
\end{remarks*}

\begin{problem*}
Does the real analogue of the theorem hold for bundles whose structure group can be reduced to $\operatorname{SL}(n,\Z)$ \cite{SullivanCR}, or for $n$ odd? Is the real Euler class the only obstruction?
\end{problem*}

\begin{remarks*}
(f) The technique of reducing modulo two maximal ideals, and a problem of Atiyah about vector bundles, are described in the introduction to \cite{ArtinMazur}. The algebraic classifying map, namely the diagram above, and the methods of \cite{SullivanInf} used here, solve Atiyah's problem.
\end{remarks*}
