\section*{Introduction}

Let $k$ a field commutative and $f$ a serie formelle en deux indeterminees $x$
and $y$ on $k$:
\[
f=\sum a_{n,m} x^n y^m .
\]
Let $I(f)$ the serie formelle en a indeterminee $t$
\[
I(f)=\sum a_{n,n} t^n .
\]
We we proposons of prouver the result suivant:

\begin{theorem}
Si $f$ represente a function algebraic of $x$ and $y$, and si $k$ is of
caracteristique $p\neq 0$, $I(f)$ represente a function algebraic of $t$.
\end{theorem}

For $g$ a serie formelle en $N$ indeterminees one defines similarly
\[
g=\sum a_\nu x^\nu, \qquad I_N(g)=\sum a_{\nu\cdot\mathbf{1}} t^{|\nu|},
\]
and one has again:

\begin{corollary}
Si $g$ is algebraic over the field $k(x_1,\dots,x_N)$, and si $k$ is of
caracteristique $p>0$, $I_N(g)$ is algebraic on $k(t)$.
\end{corollary}

Under the hypothese more forte $g\in k(x_1,\dots,x_N)$, this result is of the a
H.~Furstenberg~\cite{Furstenberg}. See also 3.10. Notre but prime etait of
doner a explication geometric to the result of Furstenberg.

Supposons all of abord that $k=\CC$ and that the serie formelle $f$ let convergente.
The serie $I(f)$ is then convergente, and admet the representation integral suivante
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1}
I(f)(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{xy=t} f(x,y)\,\frac{dx\wedge dy}{dt}.
\end{equation}

Expliquons the sens of the membre of line:

a) Let $\pi:\CC^2\to\CC$ the map $(x,y)\mapsto xy$. the expression
$(dx\wedge dy)/dt$ is a form differentielle holomorphic relative on
$\CC^2-\{(0,0)\}$, relative a the projection $\pi$, i.e.\ a family of forms
differentielles holomorphic on the fibers, dependant holomorphiquement of $t$.
En $(0,0)$, c'is again a section of the sheaf dualisant relative $\omega$, defined
in the formalism of duality of A.\ Grothendieck by
$R\pi^!\OO=\omega[1]$. En terme of the coordonnee $y$ on $\pi^{-1}(0)$, one has
\[
\frac{dx\wedge dy}{dt}=\frac{d(t/y)\wedge dy}{dt}=\frac{-dt\wedge dy}{y}.
\]

b) Supposons $f$ convergente in the polydisque $B$ of rayons $R$. For $|t|$
sufficiently petit ($|t|<R^2$), the intersection of $B$ and of $\pi^{-1}(t)$ se projette
isomorphiquement in the plan of the $y$ on the couronne $|t|/R<|y|<R$. Let $r$
such that $\sqrt{|t|}<r<R$. the integral
\[
\int_{xy=t}
\]
is prise on the cycle $|y|=r$, $|x|=|t|/r$, $xy=t$, which se projette
isomorphiquement on the circonference $|y|=r$, orientee positivement; it ne
depend not of the choix of $r$.

These explications montrent that
\[
\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{xy=t} f(x,y)\,\frac{dx\wedge dy}{dt}
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|y|=r} f(t/y,y)\,\frac{dy}{y}
=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|y|=r}\sum a_{n,m}t^n y^{m-n}\frac{dy}{y}.
\]
For en deduire \eqref{eq:1}, one echange sommation and integration, and one applique the
formule of the residus: seuls subsistent the termes or $m=n$.

The cycle of integration is the vanishing cycle (to the sens of Lefschetz) for $\pi$,
pres of $(0,0)$. Si one prend $r=|t|^{1/2}$, one the voit effectivement disparaitre for
$|t|\to 0$. This explique the titre of the article.

In the definition of $I(f)$, the indeterminees $x$ and $y$ jouent a role
symmetric. In \eqref{eq:1}, a ordre between $x$ and $y$ determine deux choix of signes, which
s'annihilent: in the form differentielle relative, and in the orientation of the vanishing cycle.

The formule \eqref{eq:1} suggere that the operation $I$ a sens intrinseque in the cadre
suivant: one part of a morphism of schemes formels $\pi:\mathfrak{X}\to\mathfrak{S}$ on
$k$, with $\mathfrak{X}$ isomorphic a $\Spf k[[x,y]]$, $\mathfrak{S}$ isomorphic a
$\Spf k[[t]]$, and $\pi$ ayant a singularity quadratique not-degeneree. One assumed that
the deux branches of $\pi^{-1}(0)$ are defined on $k$, and one choisit a ordre between
they. En of other termes, one assumed that there exists of the systemes of coordonnees formelles
$x,y$ on $\mathfrak{X}$ and $t$ on $\mathfrak{S}$ in lesquels $\pi$ let
$(x,y)\mapsto xy$; one prendra toujours these systemes of coordonnees of telle sorte that the
premiere branche of $\pi^{-1}(0)$: $xy=0$ let the branche $x=0$.

Let $\Omega^1_{\mathfrak{X}/k}$ the differentielles of Kahler, defined en tenant
compte of the topologie: si $\mathfrak{X}=\Spf(A)$, with $A$ isomorphic a $k[[x,y]]$
equipped with the topologie $(x,y)$-adique, $\Omega^1_{\mathfrak{X}/k}$ correspond to the $A$-module
$\varprojlim \Omega^1_{(A/I)/k}$ (projective limit on the ideals ouverts). Similarly
for $\mathfrak{S}$. En coordonnees, $\Omega^1_{\mathfrak{X}/k}$ is the
$\OO_{\mathfrak{X}}$-module free of base $dx,dy$ and $\Omega^1_{\mathfrak{S}/k}$ is the
$\OO_{\mathfrak{S}}$-module free of base $dt$. For $M$ a module free of rank $n$,
posons $\det M=\wedge^n M$. For $L$ free of rank $1$, notons $L^{-1}$ son dual.
Posons $\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/k}=\det\Omega^1_{\mathfrak{X}/k}$,
$\omega_{\mathfrak{S}/k}=\det\Omega^1_{\mathfrak{S}/k}$ and let
$\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{S}}$ the sheaf dualisant relative
\[
\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{S}}=\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/k}\otimes\pi^*\omega_{\mathfrak{S}/k}^{-1}.
\]

En coordonnees, c'is the $\OO_{\mathfrak{X}}$-module free of base
$dx/x\,dy/dt$. One interpretera $I$ as a morphism
\[
\pi_*\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{S}}\to\OO_{\mathfrak{S}},
\]
i.e., for $\mathfrak{S}=\Spf(V)$, as a morphism $V$-lineaire
\[
H^0(\mathfrak{X},\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{S}})\to V.
\]

The section 1 fournit this interpretation: one y proves, for $k$ quelconque,
a formule which is a analogue algebraic of \eqref{eq:1}. The signe of integration,
which apparait in \eqref{eq:1}, y is remplace by the morphism trace local of
Grothendieck. Precisons. Let $t$ a uniformisante of $\mathfrak{S}$ (by example $t$
for $\mathfrak{S}=\Spf(k[[t]])$). We shall denote by $n$ en indice a reduction modulo
$t^n$. One omettra the indice quand it n'y a not risque of confusion. By example, one
notera again $\pi:X_n\to S_n$ the morphism $\pi$, deduces of $\pi$ by reduction modulo
$t^n$. One notera $\omega$ the inverse image (of $\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{S}}$) of
$\omega_{\mathfrak{X}/\mathfrak{S}}$ on $X_n$. C'is the sheaf dualisant relative
$\omega_{X_n/S_n}$, of Grothendieck. Let $A_n$ the ring local complet of which $X_n$ is
the spectre formel (en coordonnees: $k[[x,y]]/((xy)^n)$). It we will be commode of
travailer not not with the scheme formel $X_n$, but with the scheme ordinaire
$X'_n:=\Spec A_n$. The difference is byzantine: tant $X_n$ that $X'_n$ are a facon
geometric of parler of $A_n$. Let $O$ the point closed of $X'_n$ (en coordonnees:
$x=y=0$). The morphism trace local of Grothendieck is a morphism
\[
\Tr:H^0_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\omega)\to H^0(X'_n,\OO)=k[t]/(t^n).
\]
one has $H^0_{\{O\}}(X_n,\omega)=\varinjlim\Ext^1_{A_n}(A_n/I,\omega)$, (filtered colimit on the
ideals ouverts $I$ of $A_n$). C'is for ecrire this filtered colimit as a group of
cohomology a support that it we a fallu introduire $X'_n$.

The role of the cycle of integration (the vanishing cycle) will be joue by a element
$v_n\in H^0_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\OO)$. En coordonnees, this element is in the image of
$\Ext^1_{A_n}(A_n/((x+y)^{2n-1}),A_n)$. Prendre garde that the exposant of $(x+y)$ requis croit
with $n$.

To the section 2, one considers a scheme en curves $Y$ proper and plat on
$\mathfrak{S}$, of which $\mathfrak{X}$ se deduces by completion en a point, and one relie the
$v_n$ a element of $\Lie\Pic^\natural Y$. To the section 3 one globalise
$\mathfrak{X}$ and $\mathfrak{S}$, and one proves the theorem, and the corollary.

\paragraph{Notations.} One ecrira en indice the changements of base. By example, for $X$
and $T$ of the $S$-schemes, $X_T=X\times_S T$. The reduction modulo $t^n$ is notee with a
indice $n$. For notre propos, the conventions of signes utilisees are without importance.
For that the results intermediaires enonces let vrais --- and not only to the signe
pres --- it faut toutefois the choisir. Notre choix is explicite in the appendice.


\section{Analogue algebraic of \eqref{eq:1}}

\subsection{}

Gardons the notations of the introduction, and let $x$, $y$ and $t$ of the coordonnees
on $\mathfrak{X}$ and $\mathfrak{S}$, of the type deja considered.

the inclusion of $X'_1$ in $X'_n$ is a homeomorphisme, puisque $X'_1$ is defined
in $X'_n$ by a ideal nilpotent. The complement of the origine $\{O\}$ in $X'_n$ a
therefore deux composantes connected, corresponding to the deux branches $x=0$ and $y=0$ of
$X'_1$. Let $u_n$ the section of the sheaf structural of $X_n-\{O\}$ which vaut $1$ on
the premiere branche and $0$ on the seconde. In the langage of the rings:
the homomorphisme
\[
A_n=k[x,y]/((xy)^n)\to k[x,y]/(x^n)\times k[x,y]/(y^n)
\]
induces a isomorphism
\[
A_n((x+y)^{-1})\xrightarrow{\sim}(k[[x]]/(x^n))((y))\times (k[[y]]/(y^n))((x)),
\]
and $u_n\in A_n((x+y)^{-1})$ a for image $(1,0)$. One can expliciter:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.1}
u_n=\frac{x^n}{x^n+y^n}.
\end{equation}

One defines $v_n$ as being the image of $u_n$ by the morphism cobord natural
\[
H^0(X'_n-\{O\},\OO)\to H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\OO).
\]
The exact sequence longue of the $\Ext^i(*,A_n)$, appliquee a the exact sequence courte
\[
0\to ((x+y)^N)\to A_n\to A_n/((x+y)^N)\to 0
\]
given lieu a commutative diagram
\[

\]
For $N=2n-1$, this diagram and \eqref{eq:1.1.1} montrent that $v_n$ is in the image of
$\Ext^1_{A_n}(A_n/((x+y)^{2n-1}),A_n)$.

\subsection{}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1.2}
Let $f$ in $k[[x,y]]$. For every $n$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.2.1}
\Tr(f\,dx/x\,dy/dt, v_n)\equiv -I(f)\pmod{t^n}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

In \eqref{eq:1.2.1}, $\Tr$ is the morphism trace local of Grothendieck. It is
applique a the element of $H^0_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\omega)$ product of $f\,dx\wedge dy/dt$ in
$H^0_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\omega)$ and of $v_n$ in $H^0_{\{O\}}(X'_n,\OO)$. We shall prove
\eqref{eq:1.2.1} under the hypothese that $k$ is infini. One se ramene a this cas by a
extension of scalars.

Notons $I'(f)$ the membre of gauche of \eqref{eq:1.2.1}. one has
$I'(f)\in k[t]]/(t^n)$. Si $J$ is a ideal open of $A_n$, sufficiently petit for that $v_n$
let in the image of $\Ext^1(A_n/J,A_n)$ (by example $J=((x+y)^{2n-1})$) and that
$f\in J$, one has $fv=0$ and a fortiori $I'(f)=0$: the map $k[t]]/(t^n)$-lineaire
$I':A_n\to k[[t]]/(t^n):f\mapsto I'(f)$ is continue.

For $\lambda,\mu\in k$, let $\sigma(\lambda,\mu)$ the automorphism
$(x,y)\mapsto(\lambda x,\mu y)$, $t\mapsto\lambda\mu t$ of $(\mathfrak{X},\mathfrak{S},\pi)$.
It respecte $dx/x\,dy/dt$ and $v_n$. By transport of structure, one has therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.2.2}
I'(\sigma(\lambda,\mu)^*f)=\sigma(\lambda,\mu)^*I'(f).
\end{equation}
Posons $h_{n,m}(t)=I'(x^ny^m)$. Faisant in \eqref{eq:1.2.2} $\mu=\lambda^{-1}$, one trouve that
$h_{n,m}(t)=\lambda^{n-m}h_{n,m}(t)$: one has $h_{n,m}=0$ for $n\neq m$. By continuite and
$k[[t]]$-linearite of $I'$, one has then for $f=\sum a_{n,m}x^ny^m$
\[
I'(f)=I'\bigl(\sum_n a_{n,n}x^ny^n\bigr)=I'\bigl(\sum_n a_{n,n}(xy)^n\bigr)=I'\bigl(\pi^*I(f)\bigr)=I(f)\cdot I'(1).
\]
Faisant in \eqref{eq:1.2.2} $\mu=1$ and $f=1$, one trouve $h_{0,0}(\lambda t)=h_{0,0}(t)$,
of sorte that $I'(1)=h_{0,0}(t)$ is a constant $c$, and that $I'(f)=c\,I(f)$.

This constant is independante of $n$. For prouver that $c=-1$, one can therefore
supposer, and one supposera, that $n=1$.

The scheme $X'_1=\Spec k[[x,y]]/(xy)$ is the reunion of deux arcs of curve
formels not singuliers se coupant transversalement, parametres the prime by
$y$, the second by $x$. The sections of $\omega$ on $X'_1$ are the systemes of forms
differentielles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ on these arcs, with a pole simple en $O$, and
$\Res\alpha+\Res\beta=0$. The sections of $\omega$ on $X'_1-\{O\}$ are the systemes of
forms $\alpha$ and $\beta$ with a pole of ordre quelconque en $O$, and the morphism compose
\[
H^0(X'_1-\{O\},\omega)\to H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_1,\omega)\xrightarrow{\Tr} k
\]
is the oppose of $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\Res\alpha$ (residu en $O$ of the form on the premiere
branche). By definition, the morphism cobord
\[
H^0(X'_1-\{O\},\OO)\to H^1_{\{O\}}(X'_1,\OO)
\]
envoie $u_1=\frac{x}{x+y}$ on $v_1$. It envoie $1-u_1=\frac{y}{x+y}$ on $-v_1$ and, a fortiori,
envoie $1=u_1+(1-u_1)$ on $v_1-v_1=0$, as it se must puisque $H^1(X'_1,\OO)=0$.
For $c=I'(1)$, one has
\[
c=\Tr(dx\wedge dy/dt, v_1)=-\Res_0(d(t/y)\wedge dy/dt)=-\Res_0(-dy/y)=-1.
\]
This acheve the proof.

\subsection{}

Let $\pi:Y\to S$ a scheme en curves proper and plat on $S=\Spf(k[[t]])$,
and $O$ a singularity quadratique not degeneree of $Y$, the deux branches of $Y$ en
$O$ being defined on $k$. One se propose of relier the $v_n$ of 1.1 a element of
$\Lie\Pic^\natural Y$.

\subsection{}

One assumed $Y$ connected, and one sets $K=k((t))$. The fiber generique $Y_K$ is then
connected. Indeed, let $\overline{K}$ a algebraic closure of $K$, and
$\overline{Y}=Y\times_S\Spec(\overline{K})$. Puisque $Y$ is plat on $S$ and $Y_s$ reduit,
$Y$ is reduit, and $Y_K$ is reduit. By passage a the normalisee, one se ramene a supposer
$Y$ normal. The fiber speciale $Y_s$ a then to the more deux composantes irreductibles
(se coupant transversalement en $O$), therefore a on a extension separable $k'$ of $k$ of the

therefore is algebraic. This type of equation is reminiscent of the $F$-cristaux en
cohomology cristalline.

Parallelement, $T_p\Pic$ is a avatar of the $H^1$ $p$-adique. This me fait esperer
that the proof of Furstenberg and celle datum ici are less eloignees
the a of the other that it n'y parait.

\subsection*{Signes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Signes}

The traitement in the litterature of the questions of signes in the formalism
of duality of the sheaves algebraic coherents m'a semble confus. The texte of
Hartshorne, Residues and Duality (Lect.\ Notes~20), souffre of the defauts
suivants:

(a) the convention which defines the complexes $\Hom^*(K,L)$ (I~6~p.~63,~64) and
$K\otimes L$ (II~4~p.~93) n'is not the convention usuelle; contrairement a this which
is affirme p.~64, $\Hom^*$ ne commute not (identiquement) to the functors of
translation (cf.\ SGA4~XVII~0.3); similarly for $\otimes$ of which the associativity requiert
the definition of a isomorphism canonical, which n'is not datum. Of the fait that
in the definition of $f^!$ for $f:X\to Y$ smooth purement of dimension relative $n$
apparait the tensor product with $\Omega^n_{X/Y}[n]$, this lacune importe.

(b) For autant that je puisse neanmoins comprendre the convention proposee,
the definition III~4.3 of the morphism trace, basee on the convention which in
III~3.4 defines $\gamma$, me semble incompatible a III~10.5 (TRA1).

The conventions exposees ci-dessous are je crois coherentes. J'y utilise the
convention of signe usuelle for $\otimes$ (SGA4~XVII~1.1). Je ne me suis not limite
a the dimension~1, which suffirait to the needs of the article.

(a) For $f:X\to S$ plat of intersection complete relative, purement of dimension
relative $n$, the sheaf dualisant relative $\omega_{X/S}$ is lie to the dualizing complex
relative $K_{X/S}:=Rf^!\OO_S$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S.1}
K_{X/S}=\omega_{X/S}[n].
\end{equation}
C'is a sheaf inversible. For $X$ smooth on $S$, one has
$\omega_{X/S}=\Omega^n_{X/S}$. Si, as suggere in mon appendice to the livre of
Hartshorne, one defines $Rf^!$ by adjonction, the equality
$\omega_{X/S}=\Omega^n_{X/S}$ is a isomorphism canonical, of which the definition pose
a probleme of signe. We adoptons ici the point of vue inverse (cf.\ the facon
of which Hartshorne traite of the morphisms lissifiables), or this equality is evidente
on the definition of $Rf^!$ and or the probleme of signe is in the definition of the morphism
trace \eqref{eq:S.5} ci-dessous.

Si $X$ is the fiber product on $S$ of the $f_i:X_i\to S$ ($i\in I$), or chacun of the $X_i$
is plat of intersection complete relative purement of dimension relative $n_i$ with
$n=\sum n_i$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S.2}
K_{X/S}=\boxtimes_i K_{X_i/S}
\end{equation}
(tensor product externe). By definition, the datum of a tel isomorphism is
the datum, for each ordre total on $I$, of a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S.3}
\omega_{X/S}=\bigotimes_i \omega_{X_i/S}.
\end{equation}
One exige that quand one passe of the ordre total $<_1$ a the ordre total $<_2$, the isomorphism
change by the signe $(-1)^N$, $N=\sum n_in_j$ (sum on the $(i,j)$ with $i<_1 j$
and $i>_2 j$). For $X_i$ smooth on $S$, one has $\omega_{X_i/S}=\Omega^{n_i}_{X_i/S}$ and \eqref{eq:S.3}
is given by the product exterieur, pris in the ordre given on $I$. It a the property of
parite voulue.

For a morphism itere $f=gh:X\xrightarrow{h}Y\xrightarrow{g}S$, $K_{X/S}$ is the
$\otimes$ of the complexes $K_{X/Y}$ and $h^*K_{Y/S}$ by a isomorphism which, applique a
$X_1\times_S X_2\to X_1\to S$, redonne \eqref{eq:S.2}.

(b) For $f:X\to S$ as en (a) and proper, the morphism trace is a morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S.4}
\Tr:Rf_*K_{X/S}\to\OO_S.
\end{equation}
The morphism \eqref{eq:S.4} induces a morphism of sheaves coherents
$\mathscr{H}^{-n}Rf_*K_{X/S}\to\OO_S$, i.e.\ a morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S.5}
\Tr:R^nf_*\omega_{X/S}\to\OO_S.
\end{equation}
Precisons comment one passe of \eqref{eq:S.4} a \eqref{eq:S.5}. For every complex
$K$, the complex $K[d]$ is the complex of composantes $(K[d])^i=K^{i+d}$ and of
differentielle celle of $K$ modifiee by the signe $(-1)^d$. One identifie the
$\mathscr{H}^i(K[d])$ to the $\mathscr{H}^{i+d}(K)$ without introduire of signe. The functor $Rf_*$
commute to the functor of translation $[d]$, and \eqref{eq:S.5} se deduces of
\eqref{eq:S.4} by map of the functor $\mathscr{H}^0$: utiliser the isomorphism
\[
\mathscr{H}^0 Rf_*K_{X/S}=\mathscr{H}^0 Rf_*(\omega_{X/S}[n])=\mathscr{H}^0(Rf_*(\omega_{X/S})[-n])
=\mathscr{H}^n Rf_*\omega_{X/S}=R^nf_*\omega_{X/S}.
\]

For $s$ a section of $f$, one dispose also of the morphism trace local
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S.6}
\Tr:Rs^!K_{X/S}\to\OO_S.
\end{equation}
