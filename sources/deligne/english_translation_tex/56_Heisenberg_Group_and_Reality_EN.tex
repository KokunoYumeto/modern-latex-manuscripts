\section*{1}

Let $H$ be the complex Heisenberg group, an extension of $\mathbb{C}^2$ by $\mathbb{C}^*$. It has coordinates $(x,y,\lambda)$, with group law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1}
(x, y, \lambda)(x', y', \lambda') = \bigl(x + x', y + y', \lambda\lambda'\exp(\pi i(xy' - x'y))\bigr).\tag{1.1}
\end{equation}

The involution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.2}
T \colon (x, y, \lambda) \mapsto (-\bar{x}, -\bar{y}, \bar{\lambda})\tag{1.2}
\end{equation}
is an automorphism of $H$.

The coordinate system $x,y,\lambda \colon H \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{C}^2 \times \mathbb{C}^*$ displays the section $\mathbb{C}^2 \times \{1\}$ of the projection of $H$ onto $\mathbb{C}^2$. On this section one has $T(g)=g^{-1}$. Each line $D\subset \mathbb{C}^2$ has, as its lift by this section, a one-parameter subgroup of $H$, which we again denote by $D$. The commutator $(u,v)=uvu^{-1}v^{-1}$ of two elements $u=(u_1,u_2)$ and $v=(v_1,v_2)$ of $\mathbb{C}^2$ is $\exp(2\pi i\langle u,v\rangle)\in \mathbb{C}^*$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3}
\langle u, v \rangle = u_1v_2 - u_2v_1.\tag{1.3}
\end{equation}

Let $H_{\mathbb R}$ be the real form defined by $x,y\in \mathbb R$ and $|\lambda|=1$. The group $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$ acts on $H$, and $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ acts on $H_{\mathbb R}$, which is identified set-theoretically with $\mathbb R^2\times \mathrm U^1$.

It is known, by the Stone--von Neumann theorem [2], that $H_{\mathbb R}$ has a unique irreducible unitary representation $V$ in which $\lambda\in \mathrm U^1$ acts by multiplication by $\lambda$. In the Schrödinger model of $V$, one has $V=L^2(\mathbb R)$ and the action of $H_{\mathbb R}$ is
\begin{align*}
(X,0,1)&\colon f(z)\mapsto f(z+X),\\
(0,y,1)&\colon f(z)\mapsto e^{2\pi iyz}f(z).\tag{1.4}
\end{align*}

From the uniqueness of $V$ one obtains a projective unitary action of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ on $V$, compatible with the action of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ on $H_{\mathbb R}$ (A. Weil [3], D. Shale [1]).

For the action of $H_{\mathbb R}$ on $V=L^2(\mathbb R)$, the $C^\infty$ vectors, i.e. those $v\in V$ for which $h\mapsto hv$ is $C^\infty$ in $h\in H_{\mathbb R}$, form the Schwartz space $\mathcal S$ of $C^\infty$ rapidly decreasing functions on $\mathbb R$. The holomorphic vectors, i.e. those $v\in V$ for which $h\mapsto hv$ extends to a holomorphic map from $H$ to $V$, are the $L^2$ functions on $\mathbb R$ which extend to holomorphic functions on $\mathbb C$ such that, in every horizontal strip $|\Impart z|<A_1$, one has for every $A_2\in \mathbb R$
\[
|f(z)| = O(\exp(-A_2|z|)).
\]

The complex group $H$ acts on the space $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$ of holomorphic vectors by the formulae (1.4).

For the holomorphic action of $H$ on $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$ one does not have the same uniqueness as for the unitary action of $H_{\mathbb R}$ on $V$. If one did, the projective action of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ on $V$ would extend to a projective action of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$ on $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$; this action would lift to a genuine action of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$, since $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$ is simply connected; and the projective action of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ would lift to a genuine action. This is not the case: only the double cover of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ acts. Our purpose is to make explicit what happens.

\section*{2}

Let $S=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)$ be the space of lines in $\mathbb C^2$. For $D\in S$, lifted into $H$ as in no. 1, let $W_D$ be the space of holomorphic functions on $H$ satisfying
\[
\varphi(\lambda d\cdot h)=\lambda\varphi(h)
\]
for $\lambda\in \mathbb C^*$ and $d\in D$, endowed with the action of $H$ given by
\[
g\colon \varphi(h)\mapsto \varphi(hg).
\]

For $E\not\supset D$, restriction to $E$ is a bijection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1}
r_E\colon W_D\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O(E),\tag{2.1}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal O(E)$ is the space of holomorphic functions on $E$.

If $R$ is a holomorphic representation of $H$, on which each $\lambda\in \mathbb C^*$ acts by $\lambda$, and if $\omega$ is a $D$-invariant linear form on $R$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.2}
\omega\mapsto \omega(hw)\tag{2.2}
\end{equation}
is a morphism from $R$ to $W_D$.

\section*{3. Example}

Let $V$ be, as in nos. 1 and 2, the irreducible unitary representation of $H_{\mathbb R}$, let $\mathcal S\subset V$ be the space of its $C^\infty$ vectors, and let $\mathcal S'\supset V$ be the dual of the space of $C^\infty$ vectors of the dual representation; in the Schrödinger model this is the space of tempered distributions.

Let $D_t\in S$ be the line generated by $(-t,1)$. If $\Impart t>0$, the linear form, written in the Schrödinger model,
\[
\omega_t\colon f\mapsto \int f(x)\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\,dx
\]
is defined on $V$, and even on $\mathcal S$. The form $\omega_t$ is a holomorphic vector in the dual of $V$, fixed by $D_t$. In terms of the infinitesimal generator $-t\partial_x+2\pi ix$ of $D_t$, invariance is expressed by
\[
\int [(-t\partial_x+2\pi ix)f(x)]\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\,dx
 = \int f(x)(t\partial_x+2\pi ix)\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\,dx=0.
\]

By (2.2), $\omega_t$ defines
\[
Q_t\colon V^{\mathrm{hol}}\to W_{D_t},
\]
which extends to $V$ and even to $\mathcal S'$.

Let $E$ be the $x$-axis. The map $r_E\circ Q_t\colon V\to \mathcal O(E)$ sends $f\in V$ to
\[
f(x,t)=r_E\circ Q_t(f)\qquad (\Impart t>0),
\]
a solution of the heat equation
\[
\partial_t f=\frac{i}{4\pi}\partial_x^2 f,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\left(\partial_t-\frac{i}{4\pi}\partial_x^2\right)f(x,t)=0.
\]

For real $t$, the form $\omega_t$ remains defined on $\mathcal S\subset V$. For $t=0$ it is $f\mapsto f(0)$, and for $t=\infty$ it is $\int f(x)\,dx$. The homomorphism $Q_t$ remains defined on $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$. For $t=0$, $r_E\circ Q_0$ is the identity in the Schrödinger model.

Assume $\Impart(t)>0$. Let $\bar D_t$ be the complex conjugate line of $D_t$. In the affine line $S-\{\bar D_t\}$, the ``half-plane'' $\{D_u\mid \Impart u>0\}$ is a disk centered at $D_t$. The map $r_{\bar D_t}\circ Q_t\colon V\to W_{D_t}\to \mathcal O(D_t)$ gives the holomorphic model of $V$: the action of $H$ is given by
\begin{align*}
d\in D_t&\colon f(x)\mapsto f(x+d),\\
e\in D_t&\colon f(x)\mapsto \exp(-2\pi i\langle e,x\rangle)f(x),\tag{3.1}
\end{align*}
and the Hilbert structure for which $(f,f)$ is the integral of
\[
f(d)\overline{f(d')}\exp(2\pi i\langle d,d'\rangle)
\]
is invariant under $H_{\mathbb R}$ and identifies $V$, via $r_{\bar D_t}\circ Q_t$, with the space of functions $f$ of finite norm. For $d=(-tz,z)$, the factor $\exp(2\pi i\langle d,\bar d\rangle)$, the commutator of $d$ and $\bar d$, is
\[
\exp(2\pi i\langle d,\bar d\rangle)=\exp(2\pi i(t-\bar t)|z|^2).
\]

\section*{4}

Fix $E$. The isomorphisms $r_E$ in (2.1) allow one to regard the $W_D$ with $D\not\supset E$ as forming a family of representations of $H$ acting on the fixed space $\mathcal O(E)$, the action of $H$ depending holomorphically on $D$. We spell out the dependence on $E$ of $r_E$. Choose coordinates as in no. 1, with $E$ the $x$-axis. Let $E'$ be generated by $(1,u)$ and let $D$ be generated by $(-t,1)$. We take $x$ as coordinate on both $E$ and $E'$. Then
\[
(x',ux',1)=(-tux',ux',1)(x,0,1)(0,0,\lambda)
\]
for $(1+tu)x'=x$ and $\lambda=\exp(\pi iuxx')$. Thus $r_{E'}\circ r_E^{-1}$ sends $f_E\in \mathcal O(E)$ to $f_{E'}\in \mathcal O(E')$ with $f_{E'}(x')=\lambda f_E(x)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.1}
f_{E'}\left(\frac{x}{1+tu}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{\pi ti}{1+tu}x^2\right)f_E(x).\tag{4.1}
\end{equation}

This formula is holomorphic in $t$: the $W_D$ form a holomorphic vector bundle $\mathscr V$ of infinite dimension on $S$, on which $H$ acts.

To understand (4.1), one may note that the point of $E$ with coordinate $x$ has the same image in $\mathbb C^2/D$ as the point of $E'$ with coordinate $x/(1+tu)$.

From (4.1) one deduces that the following growth condition on $v\in W_D$ is independent of the choices of $E\not\supset D$ and of the coordinate $z$ on $E$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.2}
\text{for }f=r_E(v),\quad \exists A\quad |f(z)|<O(\exp(A|z|^2)).\tag{4.2}
\end{equation}

Let $W_D^?\subset W_D$ be defined by this condition. The $W_D^?$ form a holomorphic subbundle $\mathscr V^?$ in holomorphic representations of $H$ inside $\mathscr V$.

\section*{5. Proposition}

Let $T>0$ and consider the following conditions on an entire function $f$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)$_T$]
\item There is a holomorphic function $g(z,t)$, for $z\in \mathbb C$ and $|t|<T$, satisfying the heat equation $(\partial_t-\partial_z^2)g=0$ and the initial condition $g(z,0)=f(z)$;
\item $|f(z)|<O(\exp(|z|^2/4T))$;
\item $\displaystyle\iint |f(z)\overline{f(z)}|\exp(-|z|^2/2T)\,|dz\wedge d\bar z|<\infty$.
\end{enumerate}

Then, if $T<T_1$, each of (i)$_T$, (ii)$_{T_1}$, (iii)$_{T_1}$ implies each of (i)$_{T_1}$, (ii)$_T$, (iii)$_T$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof.} (i)$_T\Rightarrow$(ii)$_T$. The heat equation remains valid for the derivatives of $g$, and therefore
\[
\partial_t^n(z^{2n}g)=\partial_z^{2n+1}g=\partial_t^n(z\partial_z^{2n}g).
\]
Since $g(0,t)$ is holomorphic for $|t|<T_1$, if $T<T_2<T_1$, then at $(0,0)$
\[
|\partial_t^n g/n!|<O(1/T_2^n),
\]
hence
\[
|\partial_z^{2n}g|=O(n!/T_2^n),
\]
and an analogous estimate holds for $\partial_z^{2n+1}g$. The Taylor series of $f$ then gives
\[
|f(z)|<\sum_n \binom{2n}{n}\frac{|z|^{2n}}{(2n)!}\frac{n!}{T_2^n}.
\]
One has
\[
\frac{1}{2n+1}\binom{2n}{n}\leq \frac{2^{2n}}{(n!)^2},
\]
so
\[
|f(z)|<O\left(\sum_n \frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{|z|^2}{4T}\right)^n\right),
\]
and this sum is $\exp(|z|^2/4T)$.

(ii)$_T\Rightarrow$(i)$_T$. It suffices to take $g$ given by the heat kernel:
\[
g(z,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\int_{i\mathbb R} f(z+x)\exp(-x^2/4t)\,dx.
\]
Changing $x$ to $-ix$ changes the orientation of the cycle $i\mathbb R$ and the sign of the integrand, and hence does not change the integral. Condition (ii) ensures convergence for $|t|<T$.

The relation between (ii) and (iii) is clear: (ii)$_{T_1}\Rightarrow$(iii)$_T$, and (iii)$_{T_1}\Rightarrow$(ii)$_T$ is verified by writing $f(z)$ as the mean of its values on a disk centered at $z$ and of radius $1/|z|$, and by estimating that mean by Cauchy--Schwarz.

\section*{6}

In homage to the Stone--von Neumann theorem, the bundle $\mathscr W$ on $S$ is, projectively, endowed with a holomorphic connection for which the action of $H$ is horizontal.

Let us make precise what ``projectively'' means.

On the projective line $S$ there is the ample invertible sheaf $\mathcal O(1)$. Let $\mathcal O(-1)$ be its dual. A square root $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$ of $\mathcal O(-1)$ is an invertible sheaf $L$ together with an isomorphism $L^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O(-1)$. Locally such a square root exists and, locally, two square roots are isomorphic, but the isomorphism is not unique: there is a $\pm 1$ ambiguity. This ambiguity obstructs the global existence of $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$. It is not on $\mathscr V$, but on
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.1}
\mathscr V(-\tfrac12):=\mathscr V\otimes \mathcal O(-\tfrac12)\tag{6.1}
\end{equation}
that there is a connection
\[
\nabla\colon \mathscr V(-\tfrac12)\to \mathscr V(-\tfrac12)\otimes \Omega^1.
\]

Note that if $\mathcal O(-\frac12)'$ and $\mathcal O(-\frac12)''$ are two square roots of $\mathcal O(-1)$, and $\nabla$ is a connection on $\mathscr V\otimes \mathcal O(-\frac12)'$, its transport to $\mathscr V\otimes \mathcal O(-\frac12)''$ by an isomorphism of square roots
\[
\alpha\colon \mathcal O(-\tfrac12)'\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O(-\tfrac12)''
\]
does not depend on the choice of $\alpha$, which can be changed locally only by a constant $\pm 1$. Thus the notion of a ``connection on $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$'' is well defined, independently of the global existence or choice of $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$.

Let $D\in S$ and let $t$ be a tangent vector at $D$. For $s$ a local section of $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$, we must define $\nabla_t s$ in the fiber of $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$ at $D$. We proceed as follows. After making an auxiliary choice $u$, we define $\nabla_t^u s$ for $s$ a local section of $\mathscr V$, or of $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$. This derivation $\nabla_t^u$ depends on $u$, with dependence of the form
\[
\nabla_t^{u+x}(s)=\nabla_t^u(s)+a\cdot s(D),
\]
where $a$ takes opposite values for $\mathscr V$ and for $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$. For $s=s_1\otimes s_2$, with $s_1$ a local section of $\mathscr V$ and $s_2$ a local section of $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$,
\[
\nabla_t(s):=\nabla_t^u(s_1)s_2(D)+s_1(D)\nabla_t^u(s_2)
\]
is independent of the choice of $u$.

Let $D\in S$ be a line in $\mathbb C^2$. A tangent vector $t$ at $D\in S$ is identified with a linear map $t\colon D\to \mathbb C^2/D$, i.e. with an element of $(\mathbb C^2/D)\otimes D^*$. To $u'\otimes v'\in (\mathbb C^2/D)\otimes D^*$ one attaches $t(d)=\langle u,d\rangle v'$, where $u$ is any representative of $u'$. Our auxiliary datum will be a lift of $t$, i.e. a lift of $t$ to $\mathbb C^2/D\otimes \mathbb C^2$. We write it as $t=u'\otimes u$, with $u\in \mathbb C^2$ mapping to $u'$ in $\mathbb C^2/D$.

The tangent space at the origin of $H$ is $\mathfrak z=\mathbb C^2\times \mathbb C$. For $z$ in this tangent space, let $\partial_z$ be the corresponding right-invariant vector field. One has
\[
[\partial_x,\partial_y]=-\langle x,y\rangle'
\]
with, on the right, the bracket in the Lie algebra of $H$. For $x,y\in \mathbb C^2$, computing $[x,y]$ as an infinitesimal commutator, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.2}
[\partial_x,\partial_y]=-2\pi i\langle x,y\rangle(\partial_0,\partial_0,1).\tag{6.2}
\end{equation}

Let $\mathscr W_\bullet$ be the quotient of the enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra of right-invariant vector fields by the relation $\delta(0,0,1)=1$. In $\mathscr W_\bullet$, one deduces from (6.2) that, for $d\in D$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.3}
[\partial_u^2,\partial_d]=2\cdot 2\pi i\langle u,d\rangle\partial_u=\langle u,d\rangle\partial_u=\langle \bar t(d)\rangle.\tag{6.3}
\end{equation}

Compute to first order around $D$. For $\varepsilon^2=0$ and $\varphi_0(g)\in W_D$, so that $\partial_d\varphi_0=0$, one has
\[
\partial_{d+\varepsilon t(d)}[\varphi_0(g)+\varepsilon\partial_u^2\varphi_0(g)/(4\pi i)]
=\varepsilon\bigl([\partial_d,\partial_u^2]\varphi_0(g)+\partial_d\partial_u^2\varphi_0(g)\bigr)=0,
\]
since $\partial_d(\partial_u^2\varphi_0/(4\pi i))=-[-\partial_u^2/4\pi i,\partial_d]\varphi_0$; apply (6.3). To first order around $D$, the function of $g$ and $D'$ defined by
\[
\varphi(g,D+\varepsilon t)=\varphi_0(g)+\varepsilon(\partial_u^2/4\pi i)\varphi_0(g)
\]
is therefore a section of the bundle $\mathscr V$. If $\varphi(g,D')$ is a local section of $\mathscr V$, this gives the derivative $\nabla_t^u$ in $W_D$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.5}
\nabla_t^u=\partial_t\varphi((g,D))+(\partial_u^2/4\pi i)\varphi(g,D).\tag{6.5}
\end{equation}

Let us compute the dependence of (6.5) on $u$. If $u$ is replaced by $u+x$ with $x\in D$, then in $\mathscr W_\bullet$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.6}
\partial_{u+x}^2=(\partial_u+\partial_x)^2=\partial_u^2+2\partial_u\partial_x+[\partial_x,\partial_u]
=\partial_u^2+2\partial_u\partial_x+2\pi i\langle u,x\rangle.\tag{6.6}
\end{equation}
For $\varphi\in W_D$, one has $\partial_x\varphi=0$, and (6.6) simplifies to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.7}
\partial_{u+x}^2\varphi=\partial_u^2\varphi+2\pi i\langle u,x\rangle\varphi,\tag{6.7}
\end{equation}
hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.8}
\partial_{u+x}^2\varphi/(4\pi i)=\partial_u^2\varphi/(4\pi i)+\tfrac12\langle u,x\rangle\varphi.\tag{6.8}
\end{equation}

The choice of $u$ also allows one to define the derivative $\nabla_t^u$ with respect to $t$ of a local section of $\mathcal O(1)$. A section of $\mathcal O(1)$ on $U\subset S$ is a homogeneous function of degree $1$ on the inverse image of $U$ in $\mathbb C^2$, and one differentiates with respect to $\langle u,d\rangle\partial_u$ to obtain a homogeneous function of degree $1$ on $D$. When $u$ is changed to $u+x$,
\[
\nabla_t^{u+x}(s)=\langle u+x,d\rangle\partial_{u+x}s=\nabla_t^u(s)+\langle u,x\rangle s.
\]

This derivation induces one on $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$, with
\[
\nabla_t(s^{-1/2})=-\tfrac12\nabla_t(s)\cdot s^{-3/2},
\]
and, for dependence on $u$, where now $s$ is a local section of $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.9}
\nabla_t^{u+x}(s)=\nabla_t^u(s)-\tfrac12\langle u,x\rangle s.\tag{6.9}
\end{equation}

The dependences on $u$ in (6.8) and (6.9) cancel, giving a derivation $\nabla_t$ for $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$.

Since the connection $\nabla$ is defined in terms of right-invariant vector fields on $H$, it commutes with the action of $H$.

\section*{7}

We now write the connection of no. 6 in coordinates. Choose coordinates as in no. 1 and let $E$ be the $x$-axis. On $S$, $\mathcal O(1)$ has a section $s$ with a simple zero at $E$. It is unique up to a factor and trivializes $\mathcal O(1)$ on $S-E$. Thus there is a square root $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$ on $S-E$, trivialized by a square root $s^{-1/2}$ of $s^{-1}$. By such a trivialization, the desired connection is transported to a connection on the bundle $\mathscr V$. We compute it.

Let $D_t$ be the line generated by $(-t,1)$. Via $r_E$ in (2.1), a local section of $\mathscr V$ is identified with a function $f(z,t)$ holomorphic in $z$ and $t$. We compute its derivative with respect to $t$. In no. 6, one may take for $t$ the map
\[
D_t\to \mathbb C^2,\qquad (-tz,z)\mapsto (-1,0)z;
\]
this is $d\mapsto -\langle(1,0),d\rangle(1,0)$.

The corresponding derivation of $d^*\in \mathcal O(1)$ annihilates $s$, so one simply has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.1}
\nabla_t f=\bigl(\partial_t-\partial_z^2/4\pi i\bigr)f,\tag{7.1}
\end{equation}
and the local horizontal sections are the solutions of the heat equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:7.2}
\bigl(\partial_t-\partial_z^2/4\pi i\bigr)f=0.\tag{7.2}
\end{equation}

From no. 5 one deduces that through any $s\in W_D^?(-\frac12)$ there passes a local horizontal section of $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$. It is automatically a section of $\mathscr V^?(-\frac12)$. It exists in a neighborhood of $D$ whose size increases as $s$ grows more slowly. Notice that in infinite dimension a connection need not define a local trivialization, even if through every point there passes a local horizontal section.

The connection $\nabla$ on $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$ defines one on the corresponding projective-space bundle
\[
\mathbb P(\mathscr V)=\mathbb P(\mathscr V(-\tfrac12)).
\]
Its local horizontal sections are the images of nonzero horizontal sections of $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$. Although the sphere $S$ is simply connected, one has the following.

\section*{8. Surprise}

The projective-space bundle $\mathbb P(\mathscr V)$ over $S$ has no global horizontal section.

The argument of no. 1 would already lead one to expect this: apply it to the projective representation of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$ given by the action of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$ on the projective space of global horizontal sections of $\mathbb P(\mathscr V)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{First proof.} Divide $S$ into two hemispheres $S_1$ and $S_2$ and choose $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$ on each $S_i$. On $S_1\cap S_2$ one has, locally, an isomorphism
\[
\alpha\colon \mathcal O(-\tfrac12)_1\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O(-\tfrac12)_2,
\]
defined up to sign; but $\alpha$ can be defined globally only on the double cover of the circle $S_1\cap S_2$, with monodromy $-1$. If there were global horizontal projective sections of $\mathbb P(\mathscr V)$, there would be horizontal sections $f_1$ and $f_2$ of $\mathscr V(-\frac12)$ on $S_1$ and $S_2$ which, on $S_1\cap S_2$, would agree up to sign. On $S_1\cap S_2$, this would allow us to normalize
\[
\alpha\colon \mathcal O(-\tfrac12)_1\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O(-\tfrac12)_2
\]
by requiring $\alpha(f_1)=f_2$, contradicting the nonexistence of $\alpha$ on $S_1\cap S_2$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Second proof.} Choose coordinates as in no. 1. Let $E$ be the $x$-axis and $D$ the $y$-axis. As in no. 2, identify $W_D$ with the space of entire functions $f(z)$. Let $f\neq 0$ in $W_D$. By the proposition, for there to exist on $S-E$ a horizontal section of $\mathbb P(\mathscr V)$ passing through $f$, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ satisfy the growth condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:8.1}
\forall \varepsilon>0\qquad |f(z)|<O(\exp(\varepsilon |z|)).\tag{8.1}
\end{equation}
If $E'$ is another line, then by (4.1), the existence of a horizontal section on $S-E'$ passing through $f$ is expressed by a growth condition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:8.2}
\forall \varepsilon>0\qquad |f(z)\exp(az^2)|<O(\exp(\varepsilon |z|^2)),\tag{8.2}
\end{equation}
with a suitable $a\neq 0$.

Put $g(z)=f(z)\exp(az^2/2)$. By (8.1) and (8.2), $|g(z)|$ is bounded by a multiple both of $|\exp(\varepsilon |z|^2+az^2/2)|$ and of $|\exp(\varepsilon |z|^2-az^2/2)|$. Thus, in every direction except those for which $az^2$ is purely imaginary, $g$ has quadratic exponential decrease. In all directions it has, uniformly, at most quadratic exponential growth. By Phragmén--Lindelöf, $g$ has quadratic exponential decrease in every direction; hence it is zero, and $f=0$.

\section*{9}

In the coordinates of no. 1, let $D_t$ be the line generated by $(-t,1)$. Let $d=(-tz,z)$. For the commutator of $d$ and $\bar d$ one has
\[
(d,\bar d)=\exp(-2\pi i(t-\bar t)|z|^2)\in \mathbb C^*:
\]
the commutator is real positive, and
\[
(d,\bar d)>1\iff \Impart(t)>0.
\]

The lines $D$ for which $(d,\bar d)>1$ for a generator $d$ of $D$ therefore form a disk in $S$, said to be attached to the real structure $H_{\mathbb R}$.

The real structures $L\subset \mathbb C^2$ on which $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ is real form a homogeneous space under $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$, with $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$ the stabilizer of $\mathbb R^2$. Each defines a real structure $H_L$ on $H$, hence a complex conjugation $\sigma_L$ with fixed points $L\cdot \mathrm U^1$.

The disks in $S$ likewise form a homogeneous space under $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$, the stabilizer of the disk associated with $H_{\mathbb R}$ being $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$. The correspondence
\[
L\mapsto X(L):=\{D\mid \langle d,\sigma_L(d)\rangle>1\text{ for }d\text{ a generator of }D\}
\]
is therefore a bijection from the space of the real structures under consideration to the space of disks in $S$.

Passing from a disk to the complementary disk corresponds, on real structures, to $L\mapsto iL$.

\section*{10}

The computations of no. 3 admit the following interpretation. Let $X$ be an open disk in $S$, corresponding to a real structure $L$, hence to real forms $H_L$ of $H$ and $\mathrm{SL}(L)$ of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$. Then $\Gamma(X,\mathscr V(-\frac12)|_{V=0})$ has an $H_L$-invariant pre-Hilbert structure, and its completion
\[
\Gamma(X,\mathscr V(-\tfrac12)|_{V=0})\widehat{\ }
\]
is the irreducible unitary representation of $H_L$. One has
\[
\Gamma(X,\mathscr V(-\tfrac12)|_{V=0})
\subset \Gamma(X,\mathscr V|_{V=0})
\subset \Gamma(X,\mathscr V(-\tfrac12)|_{V=0})\widehat{\ }.
\]

The double cover $\mathrm{SL}(L)'$ of $\mathrm{SL}(L)$ acts on $\mathscr V(-\frac12)|_X$, compatibly with its action on $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$, and therefore acts on $\Gamma(X,\mathscr V(-\frac12)|_{V=0})$. This is the action which normalizes the action of $H_L$ and induces the natural action of $\mathrm{SL}(L)'$ on $H_L$.

The subrepresentation $\Gamma(X,\mathscr V(-\frac12)|_{V=0})$ may be interpreted as the space of real-analytic vectors for the action of $\mathrm{SL}(L)'$.

This description shows that the projective representation of $\mathrm{SL}(L)$ extends to the monoid of those $g\in \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$ such that $gX\supset X$.

\section*{11}

Everything above generalizes from $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb C)$ to $\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\mathbb C)$: replace $\mathbb C^2$ by $\mathbb C^{2n}$, endowed with a nondegenerate alternating form $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ that is real on $\mathbb R^{2n}$; replace $H$ by $\mathbb C^{2n}\times \mathbb C^*$ with group law
\[
(x,\lambda)(y,\mu)=\bigl(x+y,\lambda\mu\exp(\pi i\langle x,y\rangle)\bigr);
\]
and replace $S$ by the space of Lagrangian, i.e. maximal isotropic, subspaces $D$ of $\mathbb C^{2n}$. The disk attached to a real structure becomes the Siegel space of those $D$ for which $|\langle d,\bar d\rangle|>1$ for $d\neq 0$ in $D$. The invertible sheaf $\mathcal O(-\frac12)$ is to be replaced by $\det(D)^{-1/2}$.

\section*{12. Remark}

Alongside representations of $H$ in which $\mathrm U^1\subset \mathbb C^*$ acts by multiplication by $\lambda$, consider similarly representations in which the action is by $\lambda^{-1}$. One defines, as in no. 2, a holomorphic bundle $\mathscr V'$ of such representations over $S$; on $\mathscr V'(-\frac12)$ there is again a connection, given by a heat equation.

On a connected and simply connected open set $U$ of $S$, consider $\mathscr W=\mathscr V(-\frac12)$, its connection, and the action of $H$. In $\Gamma(U,\mathscr W)|_{V=0}$, for each $x\in U$ one has a linear form fixed by the corresponding line, defined up to a factor: $f\mapsto f(\xi)$ for $f\in \mathscr V$. For each $x\in U$, there exists a vector $v(x)$ fixed by $D_x$; in the Schrödinger model, when $x$ corresponds to $E=$ the $x$-axis, this is the constant $1$. It is unique up to a factor.

Fix the real structure $H_{\mathbb R}$, hence a real structure $S_{\mathbb R}\subset S$, a circle, and a decomposition of $S-S_{\mathbb R}$ into two disks $U_1$ and $U_2$. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] The complex conjugate of $\Gamma(U_1,\mathscr W)|_{V=0}$ is $\Gamma(U_2,\mathscr W')|_{V=0}$.
\item[(b)] The Hermitian form on $\Gamma(U_1,\mathscr W)|_{V=0}$, invariant under $H_{\mathbb R}$, can be reinterpreted as a bilinear pairing between $\Gamma(U_1,\mathscr W)|_{V=0}$ and $\Gamma(U_2,\mathscr W')|_{V=0}$ that is invariant under $H$.
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Question.} Let $C$ be a Jordan curve in $S$, separating $S-C$ into two open sets $U_1$ and $U_2$. Is there a unique separating $H$-invariant bilinear form pairing $\Gamma(U_1,\mathscr W)|_{V=0}$ and $\Gamma(U_2,\mathscr W')|_{V=0}$, induced by a perfect duality between $\Gamma(U_1,\mathscr W)|_{V=0}$ and $\Gamma(U_2,\mathscr W')|_{V=0}$?
