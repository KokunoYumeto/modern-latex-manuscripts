\begin{abstract}\noindent
We determine the group of automorphisms generated, inside the endomorphisms of the cohomology of an abelian variety over a finite field, by the Lefschetz operators and by complex multiplications.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Algebraic geometry (abelian varieties, \'etale cohomology); 14F20, 14K22

\section*{1.}

Let $k$ be an algebraic closure of the field $\mathbf{F}_p$ with $p$ elements. We denote by $\ell$ a prime number different from $p$. We fix, over $k$, an isomorphism $\mathbf{Q}_\ell \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbf{Q}_\ell(1)$.

Let $A$ be an abelian variety over $k$. We write $Z$, or $Z(A)$, for the group of cycles with rational coefficients on $A$, graded by codimension:
\[
Z = \bigoplus_i Z^i .
\]
On $Z^i$ we have the relations of numerical equivalence and of cohomological equivalence, the latter for \'etale cohomology with coefficients in $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$. Two cohomologically equivalent cycles are numerically equivalent.

Let $A_j$ be the simple factors of $A$, let $E_j$ be the center of $\End(A_j) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$, and let $F$ be the compositum, inside an algebraic closure of $\mathbf{Q}$, of the Galois closures of the $E_j$; thus $F$ is either totally real or a CM field. If $\ell$ is a prime number, let $D_\ell \subset \Gal(F/\mathbf{Q})$ be a decomposition group at $\ell$; let $c \in \Gal(F/\mathbf{Q})$ be complex conjugation.

\begin{theorem}
If $c \in D_\ell$, then numerical equivalence and cohomological equivalence, for $\ell$-adic \'etale cohomology, coincide for $A$.
\end{theorem}

A less precise theorem, in which the condition on $\ell$ depended, for a simple variety, on the choice of a maximal field of complex multiplications, was proved in [2].

The following remarks are immediate:

(a) For a given $A$, the condition of Theorem 1 is satisfied, by the \v{C}ebotarev theorem, for a set $\mathcal{L}$ of prime numbers $\ell$ of nonzero density, either analytic or naive.

(b) If the condition of Theorem 1 is satisfied for $A$, it is also satisfied for the powers $A^n$ of $A$. There is therefore a set $\mathcal{L}$, of density $> 0$, such that the conclusion is true for every power $A^n$. This had been observed by Milne [5].

Let $H = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{2g} H^i(A, \mathbf{Q}_\ell) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{2g} H^i$, where $g = \dim(A)$. Let $D = \End(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$.

The group $D^\times$ acts on $H$, as does the group, isomorphic to $\SL(2, \mathbf{Q})$, supplied by Lefschetz theory. Both preserve algebraic cohomology classes. Let $G \subset \GL(H)$ be the Zariski closure of the group generated by $D^\times$ and $\SL(2)$. The key point in our proof of Theorem 1 is a description of $G$, or rather of the group $G_{\mathbf{Q}_\ell}$ over $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$ obtained from it by extension of scalars. In \S\,4 we shall show how this description reduces to the following two cases.

First let $E$ be a supersingular elliptic curve and let $A = E^n$. The Zariski closure of the action of $\End(A)^\times$ on $H \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell = \Lambda^\bullet(H^1 \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ is the group $\GL(H^1 \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$. Let $V = H^1 \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell$, and let $V^*$ be its dual.

\begin{theorem}
The group $G_{\mathbf{Q}_\ell}$ is isomorphic to the group $\CSpin(V \oplus V^*)$ of spin similitudes, where $V \oplus V^*$ is endowed with its natural quadratic form.

It acts on $H \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell = \bigoplus_i H^i(A, \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ as the sum of its two half-spin representations. In particular, the representation on classes of even degree, respectively odd degree, is irreducible.
\end{theorem}

At the other extreme, let $A$ be an irreducible variety such that $\End(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q} = D$ is a division algebra, of reduced degree $n$, over an imaginary quadratic field $E$; $A$ has dimension $n$.

Let $c \in \Gal(E/\mathbf{Q})$ be complex conjugation, and choose an embedding $j$ of $E$ into $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$. The $E$-module structure on $H^1(A, \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ gives a decomposition $H^1 = H^{10} \oplus H^{100}$, where $\lambda \in E$ acts on $H^{10}$, respectively on $H^{100}$, by multiplication by $j(\lambda)$, respectively by $j(c\lambda)$. Let $V = V_1 \oplus V_2$ be the decomposition dual to $H^1(A, \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$. Over $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$, the Zariski closure of $D^\times$ becomes the group $\GL(V_1) \times \GL(V_2)$.

Endow
\[
H^\bullet(A, \mathbf{Q}_\ell) = \bigoplus_{p,q} \Lambda^p V_1 \otimes \Lambda^q V_2
\]
with the grading given by $k := p - q$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grad}
\mathrm{gr}^k = \bigoplus_{p-q=k} \Lambda^p V_1 \otimes \Lambda^q V_2 .
\end{equation}
The part of degree $(-n)$ is therefore $\Lambda^0 V_1 \otimes \Lambda^n V_2$, of dimension $1$, and the part of degree $(-n+1)$ is $V_1 \otimes \Lambda^n V_2 \oplus \Lambda^0 V_1 \otimes \Lambda^{n-1} V_2$, of dimension $2n$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:3}
\emph{(i)} For an isomorphism of $\bigoplus_k \Lambda^p V_1 \otimes \Lambda^q V_2$ with $\Lambda^\bullet(\mathrm{gr}^{-n+1})$ carrying $\mathrm{gr}^k$ to $\Lambda^{\frac{n+k}{2}} \mathrm{gr}^{-n+1}$, the group $G_{\mathbf{Q}_\ell}$ identifies with the group generated by $\GL(\mathrm{gr}^{-n+1})$ acting on $\Lambda^\bullet \mathrm{gr}^{-n+1}$ and by the homotheties $x \mapsto \lambda x$.

\emph{(ii)} The $\mathrm{gr}^k$ ($-n \leq k \leq n$) are therefore irreducible, pairwise nonisomorphic representations of $G$. The subspaces $\mathrm{gr}^k$ and $\mathrm{gr}^\ell$ are orthogonal for the cup product if $k + \ell \neq 0$, and are in perfect duality if $k + \ell = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\section*{2.}

We now show how to reduce Theorem 1, and in fact a slightly stronger variant; see \S\,5, to the analysis of the group $G$.

(a) Let $Z_0$ be the $\mathbf{Q}$-vector subspace of $H^\bullet(A, \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ which is the image of $Z$, and let $Z_1$ be the $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$-vector subspace generated by $Z_0$. We shall prove that the intersection form is nondegenerate on $Z_1$. Its restriction to $Z_0$, which is rational, then has zero kernel: it is nondegenerate and induces a surjection from $Z_1$ to $\Hom(Z_0, \mathbf{Q}_\ell)$. Hence $\dim_{\mathbf{Q}_\ell}(Z_1) \geq \dim_{\mathbf{Q}}(Z_0)$, the elements of a basis of $Z_0$ are linearly independent over $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$, and $Z_0 \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\ell \xrightarrow{\sim} Z_1$.

(b) We shall write $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$ for a sufficiently large finite Galois extension of $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$. It is then enough to verify the nondegeneracy of the intersection form on $Z_\lambda := Z_1 \otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_\ell} \mathbf{Q}_\lambda \subset H_\lambda := H^\bullet(A, \mathbf{Q}_\lambda)$.

(c) Let $L$ be the Lefschetz operator on $H$ deduced from a polarization of $A$, and let $\Lambda : H^i \to H^{i-2}$ be the associated operator [4]. The exponentials of the nilpotent endomorphisms $L$ and $\Lambda$ of $H$ generate a subgroup $S$, isomorphic to $\SL(2, \mathbf{Q})$. Let $G$ be the Zariski closure, in $\GL(H)$, of the subgroup generated by $S$ and by $(\End(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q})^\times$. It preserves $Z_1$, hence also $Z_\lambda$; the fact that $\Lambda$ preserves algebraic cycles is due to Lieberman; see [4]. Since $G$ contains the group $\mathbf{G}_m$ corresponding to the grading of $H^\bullet(A)$, coming from the action of $(\mathbf{Q} \cdot 1)^\times \subset \End(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$, and also the group corresponding to the same grading shifted by $\dim(A)$, coming from the diagonal matrices in $S$, it contains the homotheties $x \mapsto \lambda x$.

(d) Suppose that $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$ is a sufficiently large Galois extension of $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$. We shall check that $H_\lambda$ is a sum of absolutely irreducible, pairwise nonisomorphic representations $W$ of $G$, and that the intersection form defines nondegenerate pairings on $W \times W'$, where $W \mapsto W'$ is an involution of the factors. Suppose there is a $\sigma \in \Gal(\mathbf{Q}_\lambda/\mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ exchanging the factors in duality. Since $Z_\lambda$ is stable under the Galois group, the intersection form is then nondegenerate; this will finish the proof.

Let $E$ be the center of $\End(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$: $E$ is a product of CM fields $E_i$; by convention a totally real field is allowed here as a CM field. Suppose that $E$ has the following property, equivalent to the hypothesis ``$c \in D_\ell$'' of Theorem 1:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyp}
\text{there exist a Galois extension $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$ of $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$ and $\sigma \in \Gal(\mathbf{Q}_\lambda/\mathbf{Q}_\ell)$ such that}
\end{equation}
\[
\text{for every $i$ and every embedding $\tau : E_i \to \overline{\mathbf{Q}}_\ell$, one has $\sigma\tau = \tau \circ c$,}
\]
\[
\text{where $c$ is complex conjugation on } E_i .
\]

We shall then verify below the existence of a $\sigma$ exchanging $W$ and $W'$. Under this hypothesis, Theorem 1 follows.

\section*{3.}

We recall some linear algebra, referring to [3], which is based on [1]. Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $K$; here $K$ is one of $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$, $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$, or $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$. Let $V^*$ be its dual, and endow $W = V \oplus V^*$ with the nondegenerate quadratic form
\[
Q(v + \lambda) = \langle \lambda, v \rangle \qquad (v \in V, \lambda \in V^*) .
\]
Let $C$ be the associated Clifford algebra, the quotient of the tensor algebra $T(V \oplus V^*)$ by the ideal generated by the elements $x \cdot x - Q(x)$, for $x \in V \oplus V^*$. It is a $\mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z}$-graded algebra [3]; let $C = C^+ \oplus C^-$ denote this grading. Let $W$ act on the exterior algebra $\Lambda^\bullet V$ of $V$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clifford}
v \cdot x = v \wedge x \quad (v \in V, x \in \Lambda^\bullet V) \qquad
\lambda \cdot x = \lambda \rfloor x \quad (\lambda \in V^*, x \in \Lambda^\bullet V)
\end{equation}
where $\lambda \rfloor x = i(\lambda)x$. The relation defining $C$ is satisfied, and \eqref{eq:clifford} therefore extends to a $C$-module structure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clifford-end}
C \longrightarrow \End(\Lambda^\bullet V) .
\end{equation}
The morphism \eqref{eq:clifford-end} is an isomorphism: see [1], Theorem~2; alternatively, if $V = V' \oplus V''$, then $C$ is the tensor product, in the $\mathbf{Z}/2$-graded sense, of the analogous algebras $C'$ and $C''$, reducing the assertion to the easy case where $V$ has rank one.

The image of $C^+$ under \eqref{eq:clifford-end} is the algebra of endomorphisms of $\Lambda^\bullet V$ preserving the $\mathbf{Z}/2$-grading of $\Lambda^\bullet V$.

The group $C^\times$ acts on the algebra $C$ by conjugation. The subgroup $H$ which normalizes $W \subset C$ respects the $\mathbf{Z}/2$-grading of $C$, and is therefore made of even or odd elements. Its action on $W$ defines a map $H \to \Ogrp(W)$ whose kernel is the center of $C^\times$. The subgroup $(C^+)^\times \cap H$ of $(C^+)^\times$ which normalizes $W$ is the group of spin similitudes $\CSpin(W)$. It maps to $\SO(W)$:
\[
1 \longrightarrow \mathbf{G}_m \longrightarrow \CSpin(W) \longrightarrow \SO(W) \longrightarrow 1 .
\]
Thus $\CSpin(W)$ is the group of automorphisms of $\Lambda^\bullet V$ preserving the $\mathbf{Z}/2$-grading and the subspace $W$ of $\End(\Lambda^\bullet V)$. Its Lie algebra is the Lie algebra of even endomorphisms $T$ of $\Lambda^\bullet V$ such that, with $W$ identified as a subspace of $\End(\Lambda^\bullet V)$, one has $[T, W] \subset W$. The representation $\Lambda^\bullet V$ of $\CSpin(W)$ is the sum of the two half-spin representations.

It is clear that $\GL(V)$, acting on $\Lambda^\bullet V$, is a subgroup of $\CSpin(W)$. It lifts, in $\CSpin$, the Levi subgroup of $\SO(W)$ which is the intersection of the two opposite parabolic subgroups preserving the maximal isotropic subspaces $V$ and $V^*$ of $W$.

The maximal isotropic subspace $V^* \subset W \subset C$ and the line $K \cdot 1$ in $\Lambda^\bullet V$ determine one another: $V^*$ is the subspace of $W$ annihilating $1$, and $K \cdot 1$ is the subspace of $\Lambda^\bullet V$ annihilated by $V^*$. By the same rule, $V$ corresponds to the line $\Lambda^{\max} V$.

The same story repeats for every maximal isotropic subspace of $W$. The case of interest to us is the one where $V = V_1 \oplus V_2$, and where $W$ is decomposed as the sum of two maximal isotropic subspaces by
\[
W = (V_1 \oplus V_2^*) \oplus (V_1^* \oplus V_2) .
\]
The annihilator of $V_1^* \oplus V_2$ is the line $\Lambda^{\max} V_2 \subset \Lambda^\bullet V$. Fix a generator $\Omega$ of it. The inclusion into $W$ of the complementary isotropic subspace $V_1 \oplus V_2^*$ extends to an algebra morphism
\[
\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*) \longrightarrow C ,
\]
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:isom}
I : \Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*) \longrightarrow \Lambda^\bullet V, \qquad \lambda \longmapsto \lambda \cdot \Omega
\end{equation}
is an isomorphism. The supercommutator with $x \in W$ stabilizes $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*) \subset C$ and induces a superderivation. For $x \in V_1^* \oplus V_2$, identified with the dual of $V_1 \oplus V_2^*$, this derivation is the interior product $x \rfloor$. Via the isomorphism $I$, the action of $v \in V_1 \oplus V_2^* \subset W$ therefore becomes exterior multiplication, and the action of $x \in V_1^* \oplus V_2 \subset W$, which kills $\Omega$, becomes interior multiplication.

The exterior product in $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*)$, transported by $I$, defines a new product, denoted $*$, on $\Lambda^\bullet V$. If $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*)$ is endowed with the grading for which $V_1$, respectively $V_2^*$, has degree $1$, respectively $-1$, then the product is compatible with the grading. The isomorphism $I$ transports this to the evident grading of $\Lambda^\bullet V$, translated by $\dim(V_2)$ so as to place $\Omega$ in degree $0$. Thus the product $*$ is compatible with the evident translated grading. The natural grading of $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*)$, for which $V_2^*$ has degree $1$, is also compatible with the product. One then obtains on $\Lambda^\bullet V$ the grading $\mathrm{gr}^k$ of \eqref{eq:grad}, shifted by $\dim(V_2)$; the element $1 \in \Lambda^\bullet V_2$, of degree $0$ for $\mathrm{gr}^k$, is of degree $\dim(V_2)$.

For $v_1 \in V_1$, $v_2 \in V_2$, let us compute $(v_1 \wedge v_2)^\wedge : \Lambda^\bullet V \to \Lambda^\bullet V$ using the isomorphism \eqref{eq:isom}. We have the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*) & \longrightarrow & \Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*) \\
\downarrow\rlap{$\scriptstyle I$} & & \downarrow\rlap{$\scriptstyle I$} \\
\Lambda^\bullet V & \longrightarrow & \Lambda^\bullet V
\end{array}
\]
and the lower map is a tensor product of maps. If $a \in \Lambda^\bullet V_1$ and $b \in \Lambda^\bullet V_2$, then
\[
(v_1 \wedge v_2)(a \wedge b) = (-1)^{\deg a} (v_1 \wedge a) \wedge (v_2 \wedge b) .
\]
For the isomorphism $\Lambda^\bullet V_2^* \to \Lambda^\bullet V_2$ defining $I$, the map $b \mapsto v_2 \wedge b$ is transported to $b^* \mapsto v_2 \rfloor b^*$. It follows that $(v_1 \wedge v_2)^\wedge$ is transported to the endomorphism $v_1 \wedge v_2 \rfloor$ of $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*)$, which is immediately checked to be a derivation. This derivation preserves $V_1 \oplus V_2^*$.

\section*{4.}

Computing the group $G$ from \S\,1, after extension to a sufficiently large extension of $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$, is amusing. Here is the story.

(a) Let $E$ be the center of $D = \End(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}$, a product of CM fields $E_i$; $D$ is central simple over $E$.

(b) After extending scalars from $\mathbf{Q}$ to a sufficiently large $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$, the algebra $E$ becomes a sum of copies of $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$, indexed by the set $\Sigma$ of $\mathbf{Q}$-algebra morphisms $E \to \mathbf{Q}_\lambda$. After extending scalars from $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$ to $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$, $H^1(A)$ becomes $\bigoplus H_\sigma$ for $\sigma \in \Sigma$, where
\[
H_\sigma = \{\alpha \in H^1(A, \mathbf{Q}_\lambda) : [z]\alpha = \sigma(z)\alpha \ (z \in E)\} ,
\]
with $[z]$ denoting the action of $E$ on $H^1(A)$ deduced from its action on $A$. The algebra $D \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\lambda$ is $\prod_\sigma \End(H_\sigma)$.

The group $G$, acting on $\Lambda^\bullet\bigl(\bigoplus_\sigma H_\sigma\bigr)$, is generated by the product of the $\GL(H_\sigma)$ and by a ``diagonal'' $\SL(2)$. Let $L : H^i(A, \mathbf{Q}_\lambda) \to H^{i+2}(A, \mathbf{Q}_\lambda)$ be the Lefschetz operator, which we identify with a cohomology class in $H^2(A, \mathbf{Q}_\lambda) = \Lambda^2\bigl(\bigoplus H_\sigma\bigr)$; Lemma 2.1 of [2] implies that
\[
L = \sum_v L_v
\]
where $v$ runs through the quotient set $\Sigma/\{1, c\}$, complex conjugation acting on $\Sigma$ through its action on $E$, and where
\[
L_v \in \Lambda^2(H_\sigma \oplus H_{c\sigma})
\]
for $v = \{\sigma, c\sigma\}$. The embedding $\sigma$ may be fixed by $c$, in which case $L_v \in \Lambda^2 H_\sigma$. Each $L_v$ lies in the closure of the orbit of $L$ under $\prod \GL(H_\sigma)$, and therefore in the Lie algebra of $G$.

The same arguments give an analogous decomposition of the operator $\Lambda$; the commutation relations are preserved by the components $L_v$ and $\Lambda_v$. Consequently, $G$ is also generated by the product of the $\GL(H_\sigma)$ and by one copy of $\SL(2)$, defined over $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$, for each orbit of $c$ in $\Sigma$. Thus $G$ is a product acting on a tensor product.

(c) \textit{The story for $\{\sigma\}$, where $\sigma = c\sigma$.}

This corresponds to a possible $\mathbf{Q}$-factor of $E$, hence to a factor of $A$ isogenous to $B = E^n$, where $E$ is a supersingular elliptic curve over $k$.

Let $V = H_\sigma = H^1(B, \mathbf{Q}_\lambda)$, a vector space of even dimension. Let $W = V \oplus V^*$. The Lefschetz class $L \in \Lambda^2 V$ is an isomorphism from $V$ to $V^*$. We shall show that the group generated by $\GL(V)$ and by the subgroup $\SL(2)$ associated with $L$ is $\CSpin(W)$ in its spin representation on $H^\bullet(B) = \Lambda^\bullet V$.

One immediately checks that, for its action on $\Lambda^2 V$ by left multiplication, $L$ satisfies
\[
[L, W] \subset W .
\]
Consequently, $L$ is an element of $\mathrm{Lie}(\CSpin(W))$; it belongs to the Lie algebra of the unipotent radical $N$ of the Levi subgroup defined by $W = V \oplus V^*$ (\S\,3), and is a nonsingular element of it. The conjugates by $\GL(V)$ of the exponential of $L$ therefore generate $N$. Similarly, $\Lambda$ and its conjugates generate the Lie algebra of the opposite unipotent subgroup. Hence the group generated by $\GL(V)$ and $\SL(2)$ contains the group $\Spin$; we have already seen that it contains the homotheties (\S\,2, (c)). Its Zariski closure over $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$ is therefore $\CSpin(V \oplus V^*)$, proving Theorem 2.

(d) \textit{The story for $\{\sigma, c\sigma\}$, where $\sigma \neq c\sigma$.}

In this case $H_v = H_\sigma \oplus H_{c\sigma} = V_1 \oplus V_2$, and $(\End(A) \otimes \mathbf{Q}_\lambda)^\times$ acts on $H_v$ by $\GL(V_1) \times \GL(V_2)$.

Write, as in \S\,3,
\[
\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2) \xleftarrow{\ I^{-1}\ } \Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*) .
\]
We endow $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2)$ with the product $*$, which under $I^{-1}$ becomes the exterior product on the right. We may write ([2, Lemma 2.1])
\[
L = \sum e_i \wedge f_i \in \Lambda^2(V_1 \oplus V_2)
\]
with $e_i \in V_1$ and $f_i \in V_2$. The isomorphism $I^{-1}$ exchanges the action of $L$ on $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2)$ with the action on the right-hand side given by
\[
\sum e_i \wedge f_i \rfloor .
\]
This is a derivation (\S\,3); indeed, in the bases of $V_1$ and $V_2^*$ given by $(e_i, f_i^*)$, it is the sum of the endomorphisms
\[
x = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathfrak{sl}(2)
\]
of $k^2$, hence the endomorphism of $k^{2n}$ thus obtained, extended as a Lie-algebra action to $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2^*) = \Lambda^\bullet(k^{2n})$. Therefore the action of $L$ on $\Lambda^\bullet(V_1 \oplus V_2)$ leaves the product $*$ invariant in the Lie-algebra sense; that is, it is a derivation.

Moreover the element $h = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ acts on $\Lambda^i V$ by $i - n = \mathrm{gr}^1(\Lambda^i V)$, where $\mathrm{gr}^1$ is the translated natural grading (\S\,3). Since this grading is compatible with the product $*$, the latter is invariant under the Borel subalgebra of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$. But in a representation of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$, a vector annihilated by a Borel subalgebra is annihilated by all of $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$. It follows that $*$ is invariant under $\SL(2)$.

Consider the decomposition of $\Lambda^\bullet V$ given by \eqref{eq:grad}:
\[
\mathrm{gr}^k \Lambda^\bullet V = \bigoplus_{p-q=k} \Lambda^p V_1 \otimes \Lambda^q V_2 .
\]
Note that this decomposition is invariant under $L$ and $\Lambda$. Indeed, $E$, via its embedding in $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$, acts on $\Lambda^\bullet V$. Let $\mathrm{U}(1)$ be the group $\{\lambda \in E : N_{E/F} \lambda = 1\}$, where $F$ is the subalgebra of $E$ formed by the fixed points of $c$. If $\lambda \in \mathrm{U}(1)$, then $\lambda$ acts on $\mathrm{gr}^k$ by the scalar $\lambda^{p-q}$ in $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$; it commutes with $L$ and $\Lambda$, and the $\lambda^{p-q}$ are distinct characters of $\mathrm{U}(1)$. Summarizing:

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:4.1}
Endow $\Lambda^\bullet V$ with the product $*$ and with the grading \eqref{eq:grad}.

\emph{(i)} The algebra $\Lambda^\bullet V$ is generated by $\mathrm{gr}^{1-n} = V_1 \otimes \Lambda^n V_2 \oplus \Lambda^0 V_1 \otimes \Lambda^{n-1} V_2$.

\emph{(ii)} The product $* : \mathrm{gr}^i \times \mathrm{gr}^j \to \mathrm{gr}^{i+j}$ is invariant under $\SL(2)$, $\SL(V_1)$, and $\SL(V_2)$.

\emph{(iii)} The grading of $\Lambda^\bullet V$ is preserved by $G$.
\end{lemma}

The image of $G$ in $\GL(\mathrm{gr}^{n-1})$ contains $\GL(V_1)$, $\GL(V_2) \simeq \GL(\Lambda^{n-1} V_2)$, an isomorphism $\Lambda^0 V_1 \otimes \Lambda^n V_2 \xrightarrow{\sim} V_1 \otimes \Lambda^n V_2$ given by $L$, and its inverse given by $\Lambda$. Hence this image is $\GL(\mathrm{gr}^{n-1})$. Property (i) identifies $\Lambda^\bullet V$ with $\Lambda^\bullet(\mathrm{gr}^{n-1})$, compatibly with the action of $\SL(2) \times \SL(V_1) \times \SL(V_2)$. The group generated by $\{zs_1, z^{-1}s_2\}$, where $z$ is a scalar and $s_i \in \SL(V_i)$, inside $\GL(V_1) \times \GL(V_2)$ acts on $\Lambda^\bullet V$ by the action of $\GL(\mathrm{gr}^{1-n})$ described in Theorem 3. The group $G$ is therefore generated by $\GL(\mathrm{gr}^{1-n})$, isomorphic to $\GL(2n)$, and by a one-dimensional torus acting by global homotheties on $\Lambda^\bullet V$; this proves Theorem 3(i). It is clear that, already for the action of $\GL(\mathrm{gr}^{1-n})$, the representations obtained are absolutely irreducible and pairwise nonisomorphic. Finally, if $k + \ell \neq 0$, the spaces $\mathrm{gr}^k$ and $\mathrm{gr}^\ell$ are not dual representations, even for the action of $\GL(2n) \subset G$. Since the cup product is nondegenerate, $\mathrm{gr}^k$ and $\mathrm{gr}^\ell$ are therefore in perfect duality precisely in the complementary case.

\section*{5.}

Once the description of $G$ has been obtained, here is the proof of Theorem 1.

Write
\[
H_\lambda = H^\bullet(A, \mathbf{Q}_\lambda) = \bigotimes_{v \in \Sigma/c} H_v^\bullet
\]
where $H_v^\bullet$ is as in \S\,4. We suppose $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$ sufficiently large and Galois over $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$.

Under the action of the product, henceforth again denoted $G$, of the groups described in \S\,4, the space $H_\lambda$ decomposes as a direct sum indexed by the functions $\sigma \mapsto k(\sigma)$, for $\sigma \in \Sigma$, where
\begin{align}
k(\sigma) &\in \mathbf{Z} \text{ if } \sigma \neq \sigma c \text{; then } k(\sigma c) = -k(\sigma), \label{eq:k1}\\
k(\sigma) &\in \mathbf{Z}/2\mathbf{Z} \text{ if } \sigma = \sigma c \text{, the possible supersingular factor.} \label{eq:k2}
\end{align}

In \eqref{eq:k1}, $k(\sigma)$ is given by Theorem \ref{thm:3}, extended as in \S\,4 to the case where $E$ is a CM field rather than an imaginary quadratic field. Notice that replacing $\sigma$ by $\sigma c$ interchanges the spaces $V_1$ and $V_2$ of \S\,4, which gives the relation \eqref{eq:k1}.

Let $K$ be the group of functions satisfying \eqref{eq:k1} and \eqref{eq:k2}. Since the $\mathbf{Q}_\lambda$-vector space generated by algebraic cycles is stable under $G$ and under the cup product, it corresponds to a submonoid $\mathrm{Alg}$ of $K$. Moreover,
\begin{align}
&\mathrm{Alg} \subset K_0 = \{k : \sum_{\sigma \mod c} k(\sigma) \text{ is even}\}, \label{eq:alg1}\\
&H_\lambda(k) \text{ is orthogonal to } H_\lambda(\ell) \text{ if } k + \ell \neq 0; \label{eq:alg2}\\
&H_\lambda(k) \text{ and } H_\lambda(-k) \text{ are in duality;} \label{eq:alg3}\\
&\mathrm{Alg} \text{ is stable under } \Gal(\mathbf{Q}_\lambda/\mathbf{Q}_\ell). \label{eq:alg4}
\end{align}

Suppose that the Galois group contains an element acting as $(-1)$ on $K$. Then, on every $\mathbf{Q}_\ell$-rational subspace of $H_\lambda$ which is invariant under $G$, the duality given by the intersection form is nondegenerate. This applies in particular when, with the notation of \S\,1, complex conjugation $c \in \Gal(E/\mathbf{Q})$ belongs to the decomposition group of $\ell$; hence Theorem 1 follows.

A little less suffices: for every stable submonoid to be a subgroup of $K$, and therefore for the space of algebraic cycles to contain, together with the space associated with $k$, its dual associated with $-k$, it is enough that $K \otimes \mathbf{Q}$ have no fixed line under the action of $\Gal(\mathbf{Q}_\lambda/\mathbf{Q}_\ell)$. In other words, if $\ell$ is unramified in the factors of $E$, it is enough that $\mathrm{Frob}_\ell$ acting on $\Sigma$ satisfy:
\[
\text{for every } \sigma \in \Sigma\text{, there exists } a \in \mathbf{Z} \text{ such that } \mathrm{Frob}_\ell^a(\sigma) = \sigma c .
\]
