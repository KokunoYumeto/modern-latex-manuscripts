\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:0}

Let $X$ the complement in the group multiplicatif $\Gm$ of the group $\mu_N$ of the racines $N$iemes of the unite. In \cite{1}, A.~B.~Goncharov and moi-same have etudie various groupo\"{\i}of the fondamentaux pro-unipotents of $X$, vus as of the objects motiviques. By the sequence, we under-entendrons ``pro-unipotent'' and parlerons simplement of groupo\"{\i}of the fondamentaux. The present article is consacre to the cas exceptionnels or $N = 2,3,4$ or $8$, and a variante for $N=6$.

Let $N \geq 1$, $k$ a field generated on $\Q$ by a racine primitive $N$ieme of the unite $\zeta$, $\mathcal{O}$ the ring of ses integers, $\A^1$ the line affine on $k$, of coordonnee $u$, and $X := \Gm - \mu_N \subset \A^1 \subset \Pone$.

En each $a \in \{0, \infty\} \cup \mu_N$, one choisit a vecteur tangent $t_a$ of which the coordonnees $du(t_a)$, for $a=\infty$, and $du^{-1}(t_a)$, for $a=0$, are of the racines of the unite in $k$. Lesquelles ne we importe not: si $t_a$ and $t'_a$ correspondent a deux choix of racines of the unite, one dispose of a chemin motivic canonical between the points-base tangentiels $t'_a$ and $t'_{a'}$ (cf~\cite{1}~5.4), which permet of the identifier: the space motivic $P(X)$ of the classes of homotopie of chemins of $t_a$ a $t_b$ in $X$ (\cite{1}~4.4) ne depend not, a isomorphism unique pres, of the choix faits. We shall denote simplement $a$ the point-base tangentiel $t_a$, ecrirons $P_{b,a}(X)$ or simplement $P_{b,a}$ for $P(X)_{t_b,t_a}$ and appellerons $P_{b,a}$ the space motivic of the chemins of $a$ a $b$. For $a=b$, we the appellerons also the motivic fundamental group $\pi_1(X,a)$ of $X$ en $a$. The $P_{b,a}$, equipped with the composition of the chemins, forment the groupo\"{\i}of fondamental motivic $P(X,0,\{\infty\} \cup \mu_N)$.

This groupo\"{\i}of fondamental motivic is of Tate mixte on $k$, and a bonne reduction en dehors of $N$. For $N = 2,3,4$ or $8$, we shall prove that it generated the Tannakian category $MT(\mathcal{O}[1/N])$ of the mixed Tate motives on $k$ a bonne reduction en dehors of $N$. Let $Y$ the $k$-scheme $\Pone - \{0,1,\infty\}$ (resp.~$\Pone - \{0,1,-1,\infty\}$ si $N=8$). Puisque $X \subset Y$, the $\pi_1(Y,0)$-torseur $P_{\zeta,0}(Y)$ is a quotient of the $\pi_1(X,0)$-torseur $P_{\zeta,0}(X)$. We shall prove that it is enough deja a engendrer $MT(\mathcal{O}[1/N])$. Si $A$ is the algebra affine of $P_{\zeta,0}(Y)$ (the ring commutative in the category of the Ind-objects of $MT(\mathcal{O}[1/N])$ such that $P_{\zeta,0}(Y) = \Spec(A)$) this signifie that the under-motives of the Ind-motive $A$ engendrent by $\otimes$, $\oplus$, duaux and under-quotients the category $MT(\mathcal{O}[1/N])$.

For $N=6$ and $Y = \Pone - \{0,1,\infty\}$, $P_{\zeta,0}(Y)$ is a bonne reduction partout and generated $MT(\mathcal{O})$.

Notre proof utilise a structure integer on a gradue of the algebra of Lie of a Galois group motivic, and sa reduction modulo $2$ si $N=2,4,8$, modulo $3$ si $N=3,6$. This structure integer me laisse of autant more perplexe that it ne correspond not a scheme en group pro-smooth on $\Z$: the set of the derivations interieures of sa reduction modulo a nombre prime $p$ n'is not stable by elevation a the power $p$.

Considerons the nombres complexes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:0.1}
\zeta(s_1,\ldots,s_k; \varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_k) := \sum_{n_1 > \cdots > n_k > 0} \frac{\varepsilon_1^{n_1} \cdots \varepsilon_k^{n_k}}{n_1^{s_1} \cdots n_k^{s_k}},
\end{equation}
or $k \geq 0$, or the $s_i$ are of the integers $\geq 1$, or the $\varepsilon_i$ are of the racines $N$iemes of the unite and or $(s_1, \varepsilon_1) \neq (1,1)$. We the appellerons {\em values of multilogarithmes rel.~$\mu_N$}. the hypothese $(s_1,\varepsilon_1) \neq (1,1)$ assure the convergence of (\ref{eq:0.1}).

For $w$ a integer $\geq 0$, let $W_w$ the space of the combinaisons lineaires a coefficients rationnels of the $\zeta(s_1,\ldots,s_k; \varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_k)$ of weight $\sum s_i = w$. Let $D_d W_w$ the under-space generated by ceux which are of profondeur $k \leq d$. The product induces on the direct sum of the $W_w$ a structure of algebra graduee filtree.

Fixons $w$ and $d$. For $N = 2,3,4$ or $8$, we definirons to the paragraphes 6 and 7 a family explicite $L$ of elements of $D_d W_w$ and prouverons, as corollary of nos results motiviques, that it generated $D_d W_w$ on $\Q$. The conjecture 5.6, consequence of the conjecture A2 which is a variante of a conjecture of Grothendieck, implique that $L$ is same a base of $D_d W_w$.

For $N=6$, we shall prove to the section 8 of the results paralleles, or n'apparaissent that of the periods of mixed Tate motives on $k$, a bonne reduction partout.

For $N=2$, D.~Broadhurst a defined $L$ en 1997. It conjecturait that $L$ etait a base, and had obtained for this conjecture a evidence numerique ecrasante. C'is sa conjecture which m'a fait esperer that for $N=2$, $P_{-1,0}(\Pone - \{0,1,\infty\})$ generated $MT(\Z[1/2])$.

Nos methodes ne fournissent not of expression explicite of a $\zeta(s,\varepsilon)$ rel.~$\mu_N$ ($N=2,3,4,8$), of weight $w$ and of profondeur $d$, as combinaison lineaire a coefficients rationnels of elements of $L$, ni same of borne for the hauteur of the coefficients.

\subsection{}
A scheme en group pro-unipotent is a projective limit filtrante of algebraic groups unipotents. We en rencontrerons and as fundamental groups, and as ``groups of Galois motiviques''. Leur etude se ramene souvent a celle of leur algebra of Lie. the algebra of Lie of $H = \lim H_i$ is by definition the projective limit $\Lie H = \lim \Lie H_i$, equipped with sa topologie of projective limit, and the functor $\Lie$ is a equivalence of categories, of the schemes en group pro-unipotents toward the limites projective filtrantes of algebras of Lie nilpotentes of dimension finite. One denotes $\exp$ the functor inverse.

\subsection{(cf \cite{1} A.1 a.9)}
Let $H = \lim H_i$ pro-unipotent, $h = \lim h_i$ son algebra of Lie, and $U^{\wedge}h$ the projective limit of the completes $U^{\wedge}h_i$ of the algebras enveloppantes $Uh_i$, for leur filtration by the powers of the ideal of augmentation (algebra enveloppante complete). one has between $H$, $h$ and $U^{\wedge}h$ the relations (A) (B) (C) (D) suivantes.

\textbf{(A)} Let $\Delta: U^{\wedge}h \to U^{\wedge}h \otimes U^{\wedge}h$ the coproduit. The under-space $h$ of $U^{\wedge}h$ is the set of the elements primitifs.

A space vector $V$ of dimension finite can be vu as a scheme: c'is the set of the points on $\overline{K}$ of the scheme affine $V$, spectre of the algebra symmetric $\Sym(V^{\vee})$ of the functions polynomes on $V$, and, more generally, for every $K$-algebra $A$, $V \otimes A$ is the set of the points of $V$ with values in $A$. Similarly for a projective limit filtrante $V = \lim V_j$ of vector spaces of dimension finite: si $V^{\vee}$ is the dual topologique $\colim V_j^{\vee}$ of $V$, one identifie $V$ to the scheme $\lim \overline{V_j} = \Spec \Sym(V^{\vee})$ which represente the functor $A \mapsto V \otimes A = \lim V_j \otimes A$. We appliquerons this a $h$ and a $U^{\wedge}h$.

\textbf{(B)} One dispose of a isomorphism of schemes $\exp: h \to H$, caracterise by the property that for every representation lineaire of dimension finite $\rho$ of $H$, corresponding a representation nilpotente $d\rho$ of $h$, one has $\rho(\exp(x)) = \exp(d\rho(x))$ for $x$ in $h$. This isomorphism transforme the loi of Campbell-Hausdorff of $h$ en the loi of group of $H$.

\textbf{(C)} $H$ s'identifie to the under-scheme en group of the group multiplicatif of $U^{\wedge}h$ form of the elements groupaux, i.e.~such that $\Delta g = g \otimes g$, the exponentielle $\exp: h \to H$ devenant the exponentielle $\sum x^n/n!$.

\textbf{(D)} the inclusion of $H$ in $U^{\wedge}h$ induces a isomorphism of vector spaces of the dual topologique of $U^{\wedge}h$ with the algebra affine $\mathcal{O}(H)$ of $H$: a form lineaire $f$ on $U^{\wedge}h$ correspond the restriction of $f$ a $H$. The form lineaire on $h$ differentielle en $0$ of this restriction is the restriction of $f$ a $h$. The transpose of the coproduit $\Delta$ given the product in $\mathcal{O}(H)$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:1.9}
\emph{(i)} The filtration $F_{\bullet}$ of the algebra affine of $\exp(L)$ is stable by the translations a gauche and a line.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:1.12}
Let $B$ and $C$ of the algebras graduees and filtrees of type $(\ast)$, and $u: B \to C$ a morphism gradue and compatible to the filtrations. It induces $\overline{u}: m_B/m_B^2 \to m_C/m_C^2$. Si $\overline{u}$ is injective and strict (resp.~surjective and strict) then $u$ the is also.
\end{proposition}

Recall that ``injective and strict'' signifie injective and such that the filtration of the source let induite by celle of the but. Dualement, ``surjective and strict'' signifie surjective and such that the filtration of the but let quotient of celle of the source.

\begin{proof}
Filtrons $B$ and $C$ by leur filtration $m$-adique, and passons to the gradue associated. The gradue of $B$, equipped with the graduation and of the filtration of which it herite of $B$, s'identifie a the algebra symmetric of $m_B/m_B^2$. Similarly for $C$. Si $\overline{u}$ is injective (resp.~surjective, resp.~strict), $\Gr(u)$ the is therefore also. The filtration $m$-adique of $B$ is compatible a sa graduation, and induces on each composante homogene $B_n$ a filtration finite. Similarly for $C$. The proposition resulte therefore of the lemma suivant, applique a each $u_n: B_n \to C_n$.
\end{proof}

\section{Rappels on the mots of Lyndon}

\subsection{}
For $A$ a set, notons $A^{\ast}$ the mono\"{\i}of a unite librement generated by $A$. C'is the set of the mots ecrits in the alphabet $A$. Supposons $A$ equipped with a ordre total $\prec$. The definition inductive (a) (b) (c) suivante of the mots of Lyndon equivaut a the definition of \cite{3}.

(a) The mot vide n'is not of Lyndon.

Let $w$ a mot not vide, $\Supp(w)$ the set of the lettres which figurent in $w$, $z$ the more grand element of $\Supp(w)$ and $A'$ the set of the mots $az^p$ ($a \prec z$, $p \geq 0$), ordonne lexicographiquement.

(b) Si $w$ commence by $z$, $w$ n'is of Lyndon that s'it is reduit a $z$.

Si $w$ ne commence not by $z$, $w$ s'ecrit in such a way unique as a mot $w'$ of the alphabet $A'$, i.e.~there exists a unique mot $w' \in A'^{\ast}$ of image $w$ in $A^{\ast}$.

(c) The mot $w$ is of Lyndon if and only if the mot $w'$ is of Lyndon.

This definition n'is not a cercle vicieux car in (c), the longueur of $w'$ is strictement more petite that celle of $w$. One can the see as a algorithme which, after a nombre of iterations to the more egal a the longueur of $w$, determine si the mot $w$ is of Lyndon.

Let $L_0$ the algebra of Lie librement generated on $\Z$ by deux elements $e$ and $f$. One the regarde as plongee in the algebra associative $\Ass$ librement generated by $e$ and $f$. The under-algebra $L$ kernel of

\end{equation}
is librement generated by the $(\ad e)^n(f)$ for $n \geq 1$ (Bourbaki loc.~cit.).

Let $A$ the set of the integers $\geq 1$. There is parfois interet a regarder $A$ as being the set of the $(\ad e)^n(f)$. One ordonne $A$ by $\geq$, note $\prec$:
\[
\cdots \prec 3 \prec 2 \prec 1.
\]
A monome en $e$ and $f$ of degree $m$ en $e$ and of degree $d$ en $f$ s'ecrit
\[
e^{n_1-1}f \cdot e^{n_2-1}f \cdots e^{n_d-1}f \cdot e^{n_{d+1}}
\]
with $n_i \geq 1$ for $1 \leq i \leq d$ and $\sum n_i = m$. We ordonnerons the set of these monomes by the ordre lexicographique of $(n_1,\ldots,n_d)$, the integers being this fois ordonnes by $\leq$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:2.4}
For each mot of Lyndon $w = (n_1,\ldots,n_d)$ of $A^{\ast}$, the polynome of Lie $P_w$ applique to the $(\ad e)^{n_i-1}(f)$ satisfies
\[
P_w = e^{n_1-1}f \cdot e^{n_2-1}f \cdots e^{n_d-1}f + \text{ reste},
\]
or the reste is combinaison lineaire of monomes of same degrees en $e$ and $f$, and strictement more petits in the ordre 2.3.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
one has
\[
(\ad e)^{n-1}(f) = \sum_{p \geq 0} (-1)^p \binom{n-1}{p} e^{n-1-p}fe^p = e^{n-1}f + \text{ reste},
\]
or the reste is combinaison lineaire of $e^qfe^r$ with $q \leq n-1$. one has therefore
\[
(\ad e)^{n_1-1}(f) \cdots (\ad e)^{n_d-1}(f) = e^{n_1-1}f \cdots e^{n_d-1}f + \text{ reste},
\]
or the reste is combinaison lineaire of monomes strictement more petits in the ordre 2.3. Appliquant (2.2.1), one deduces 2.4.
\end{proof}

Revenons to the hypotheses of A.1, and assume that $\sigma(k) \subset \C$ let stable by conjugaison complex. Tel is the cas si $\sigma$ is a embedding reel, si $k$ is CM, by example cyclotomique, or si $k$ is galoisien on $\Q$. By transport of structures, it agit on $G_\omega$ and $T_{\sigma/\omega}$ is stable under the involution $c$ of $G_\omega(\C)$. Noter that $c$ agit by son action on $G_\omega$ and on $\C$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:A.7}
There exists $a_\sigma \in T_{\sigma/\omega}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A.7.1}
c(a_\sigma) = a_\sigma \tau(-1).
\end{equation}
Si $a_\sigma = u \tau(\lambda)$ with $u \in U_\omega(\C)$ and $\lambda \in 2\pi i \Q^*$, (A.7.1) equivaut a this that $c$ fixe $u$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fixons $\lambda$ in $2\pi i \Q^*$, and let $T_{\sigma/\omega}^\lambda$ the set of the $u \in U_\omega(\C)$ such that $u \tau(\lambda)$ let in $T_{\sigma/\omega}$. C'is a $U_\omega(\Q)$-torseur for the action (A.1.3). the involution $c$ of $U_\omega(\C)$ stabilise $T_{\sigma/\omega}^\lambda$: si $u \tau(\lambda)$ is in $T_{\sigma/\omega}$, $c(u \tau(\lambda))$ and $c(u \tau(\lambda)) \tau(-1)$ the are also, and $c(u \tau(\lambda)) \tau(-1) = c(u) \tau(-\lambda) \tau(-1) = c(u) \tau(\lambda)$.

the involution $c$ of $T_{\sigma/\omega}^\lambda$ fait of $T_{\sigma/\omega}^\lambda$ a $U_\omega(\Q)$-torseur equivariant, rel.~the involution $\tau(-1) \circ c$:
\[
x \mapsto \tau(-1) c(x) \tau(-1)^{-1} \text{ de } U_\omega(\Q).
\]
Indeed,
\[
c(u.\tau(\lambda) x \tau(\lambda)^{-1}) = c(u).\tau(-\lambda) c(x) \tau(-\lambda)^{-1}
\]
\[
= c(u).\tau(\lambda) \tau(-1)[c(x)] \tau(\lambda)^{-1}.
\]
All $U_\omega(\Q)$-torseur equivariant is trivial, ainsi that it follows by devissage of the nullite of $H^1(\Z/2, V) = 0$ for $V$ a $\Q$-space vector equipped with a action of $\Z/2$. There exists therefore $u \in T_{\sigma/\omega}^\lambda$ fixe under $c$, and $u \tau(\lambda)$ satisfies (A.7.1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:A.8}
the element $v_\sigma$ of (5.1.1) can be choisi fixe by the compose of $\tau(-1)$ and of the automorphism of $\Pi$ induces by the conjugaison complex of $k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choisissons $a_\sigma \in T_{\sigma/\omega}$ of the form $a_\sigma = u \tau(2\pi i)$ and verifiant (A.7.1). Let $u_1$ the image of $u$ in $IU_\omega(\C)$ and definissons $v_\sigma$ by (A.3.1):
\[
\dch = u_1 \circ \tau(2\pi i)[v_\sigma].
\]
The conjugaison complex of $k$ induces a involution of $\Pi$, deduces of $e_a \mapsto e_{\bar{a}}$ on $L$. This involution, and the conjugaison complex of $\C$, definissent the involution $c$ of $\Pi(\C)$. Tant $\dch$ that $u_1$ are fixes by this involution; $\tau(2\pi i)[v_\sigma]$ the is therefore also. Puisque $c(\tau(2\pi i)[v_\sigma]) = \tau(-2\pi i)[c(v_\sigma)] = \tau(2\pi i)[\tau(-1)[c(v_\sigma)]]$, $v_\sigma$ is fixe by $\tau(-1) \circ c$.
\end{proof}
