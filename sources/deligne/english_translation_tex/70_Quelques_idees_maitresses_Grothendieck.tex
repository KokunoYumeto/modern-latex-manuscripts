\maketitle

\noindent\textbf{Resume.} This article tente of expliquer quatre concepts mathematiques fondamentaux crees by Grothendieck: the schemes, the topos, the six operations and the motives.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} We try to explain four fundamental ideas invented by Grothendieck: schemes, topos, the six operations and motives.

\bigskip
\noindent In \emph{Recoltes and semailles} (troisieme part), Grothendieck ecrit:

\begin{quote}
\guillemotleft Prenons by example the tache of demontrer a theorem which reste hypothetique (a what, for certain, semblerait se reduire the travail mathematique). Je vois deux approches extremes for s'y prendre. the a is celle of the marteau and of the burin, quand the probleme pose is vu as a grosse noix, dure and smooth, of which it s'agit of atteindre the interieur, the chair nourriciere protegee by the coque. The principe is simple: one sets the tranchant of the burin against the coque, and one tape fort. To the need, one recommence en plusieurs endroits differents, jusqu'a this that the coque se casse --- and one is content. {[}\ldots{]}

\guillemotleft Je pourrais illustrer the deuxieme approche, en gardant the image of the noix that it s'agit of ouvrir. The premiere parabole which m'is venue a the esprit tantot, c'is that one plonge the noix in a liquide emollient, of the eau simplement pourquoi not, of temps en temps one frotte for that it penetre mieux, for the reste one laisse faire the temps. The coque s'assouplit to the fil of the semaines and of the mois --- quand the temps is mur, a pression of the main suffit, the coque s'ouvre as celle of a avocat mur a point! Or again, one laisse murir the noix under the soleil and under the pluie and can-be also under the gelees of the hiver. Quand the temps is mur c'is a pousse delicate sortie of the substantifique chair which aura perce the coque, as en se jouant --- or for mieux dire, the coque se will be open of it-same, for lui laisser passage. {[}\ldots{]}

\guillemotleft The reader which serait tant let peu familier with certain of mes travaux n'aura aucune difficulte a reconnaitre lequel of these deux modes of approche is ``the mien''.\guillemotright{} [1985, p.~552--553]
\end{quote}

A peu more loin [Ibid., p.~554, note (***)], Grothendieck met en before quatre examples: Riemann-Roch, structure of the $\pi_1$ prime a the caracteristique for the curves, rationalite of the functions $L$ for the schemes of type finite over a field finite and theorem of reduction semi-stable for the abelian varieties.

Je me recalls mon effarement, en 1965--66 after the expose of Grothendieck [SGA~5] prouvant the theorem of changement of base for $Rf_!$: devissages, devissages, rien ne semble se passer and pourtant a the fin of the expose a theorem clairement not trivial is the.

Bien of the idees of Grothendieck we are devenues si familieres, are si parfaitement adequates a leur object, that we oublions that they etaient loin of be evidentes a leur naissance, that we oublions same leur auteur. Mon but in this article is of decrire quatre of these idees: schemes, topos, six operations, motives.

\section{Schemes}

the invention of the schemes is the premiere of the idees of Grothendieck a laquelle one pense, can-be parce that it a ete the more vite acceptee. the expose of Serre a Stockholm en 1962 commence by: \guillemotleft Je voudrais exposer ici some-uns of the developpements recents of the geometrie algebraic. Je dois preciser that je prends this dernier terme to the sens which is devenu the sien depuis some annees: celui of the theorie of the schemes.\guillemotright{} This acceptation a ete facilitee by the parution rapide, grace a the collaboration of Dieudonne, of the EGA.

the audace of Grothendieck's definition is of accepter that all ring commutative (a unite) $A$ definisse a scheme affine $\Spec(A)$, i.e.\ of ne not chercher a se limiter a category of ``bons'' rings (integres, reduits, noetheriens, \ldots). This a prix. The points of $\Spec(A)$ (prime ideals of $A$) n'have not a sens geometric maniable, and the sheaf structural $\mathcal{O}$ n'is not a sheaf of functions. Quand one has a construct a scheme, one ne commence not en general by construct the set of ses points.

More important can-be: the parti pris of batir a theorie relative, of which temoigne the omnipresent
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=-0.5ex,scale=1.2]
\node (X') at (0,1) {$X'$};
\node (X) at (1.5,1) {$X$};
\node (S') at (0,0) {$S'$};
\node (S) at (1.5,0) {$S$};
\draw[->] (X') -- (X);
\draw[->] (X') -- (S');
\draw[->] (X) -- (S);
\draw[->] (S') -- (S);
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
of the exposes of Grothendieck. The cas classique of a variety defined over a field $k$ devient the cas particulier $S = \Spec(k)$. In a theorie relative, with a $S$-scheme $X$ (=~scheme $X$ on $S$), i.e.\ with a morphism of schemes $f \colon X \to S$, one considers systematiquement the scheme $X'$ deduces of $X$ by a changement of base $u \colon S' \to S$, i.e.\ the fiber product $X' := S' \times_S X$ and sa projection $f'$ on $S'$:
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=-0.5ex,scale=1.2]
\node (X') at (0,1) {$X'$};
\node (X) at (1.8,1) {$X$};
\node (S') at (0,0) {$S'\!$};
\node (S) at (1.8,0) {$S$};
\draw[->] (X') -- (X);
\draw[->] (X') -- (S') node[midway,left] {$\scriptstyle f'$};
\draw[->] (X) -- (S) node[midway,right] {$\scriptstyle f$};
\draw[->] (S') -- (S) node[midway,below] {$\scriptstyle u$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\]

In the category of the schemes, the products fibers exist toujours: si permettre that all ring commutative definisse a scheme affine given droit of cite a of the schemes bizarres, the permettre fournit a category of schemes ayant of bonnes properties.

A property of $X$ on $S$ will be dite \emph{geometric} si it a of bonnes properties of invariance by changement of base. Analogue classique: for a variety $X$ defined over a field $k$, the set of the points of $X$ on a extension algebriquement close $\overline{k}$ of $k$ (by example: domaine universal of Weil) is considered as ``geometric'', the set of the $k$-points being ``arithmetic''.

Si $X$ is a scheme on $S$, and that $u \colon S' \to S$ is a morphism of schemes, a $S'$-point $p$ of $X$ is a morphism of $S$-schemes:
\[
\begin{tikzpicture}[baseline=-0.5ex,scale=1.2]
\node (X) at (1.5,1) {$X$};
\node (S') at (0,0) {$S'$};
\node (S) at (1.5,0) {$S$};
\draw[->] (X) -- (S);
\draw[->] (S') -- (S);
\draw[->,dashed] (S') -- (X) node[midway,above right] {$\scriptstyle p$};
\end{tikzpicture}
\]
One denotes $X(S')$ the set of the $S'$-points of $X$. It s'identifie a the set of the sections of $X' \to S'$.

\begin{example}
Let $X$ the scheme affine over a field $k$ defined by of the equations $P_\alpha(X_1,\ldots,X_n) = 0$:
\[
X = \Spec\bigl(k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/(P_\alpha)\bigr).
\]
Let $k'$ a $k$-algebra. the set $X(k') := X(\Spec(k'))$ is the set of the solutions in $k'^n$ of the equations $P_\alpha = 0$. For $k'$, extension algebriquement close $\overline{k}$ of $k$, c'is the set $X(\overline{k})$ that Weil regarde as under-jacent a $X$.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
The scheme $\GL_N$ on $\Spec(\mathbb{Z})$ is such that for every ring commutative $A$ (automatiquement a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra: $\Spec(A)$ is a scheme on $\Spec(\mathbb{Z})$), $\GL_N(\Spec(A))$ ``is'' $\GL_N(A)$.
\end{example}

In these examples, the intuition geometric that one has of $X$, scheme on $S$, is bien refletee in $X(S')$. Mieux that in the set under-jacent a $X$. In the cas of the example 1.2, this set under-jacent n'is not a group, then that each $\GL_N(S')$ the is. More precisely, it is utile of attacher to the scheme $X$ on $S$ the contravariant functor of the $S$-schemes in the sets
\[
S' \longmapsto \text{ensemble } X(S') \text{ des } S'\text{-points de } X.
\]
In the category of the $S$-schemes, it s'agit simplement of the functor representable
\[
h_X \colon S' \longmapsto \Hom(S', X)
\]
attached to $X$. of after the lemma of Yoneda, the functor $X \mapsto h_X$ is fully faithful. Plutot that of penser a $S$-scheme $X$ as being a space annele, equipped with $X \to S$, with of the properties convenables, it is souvent commode of y penser as being a functor
\[
S\text{-points} \colon (\text{Sch\'emas}/S)^0 \longrightarrow (\text{Ens}),
\]
which a the vertu of be representable. Quand one veut define a ``moduli space'', the premiere etape is of define the functor corresponding. Typiquement, define this functor requiert a theorie relative.

\begin{example}
Let $X$ projective over a field $k$. Question: that signifie ``moduli space of the under-schemes fermes of $X$''? For $u \colon S' \to \Spec(k)$, let $X'$ on $S'$ deduces of $X$ by changement of base. Let $\mathcal{H}(S')$ the set of the under-schemes fermes $Z'$ of $X'$, plats on $S'$ (plats of presentation finite, si one ne veut not supposer $S'$ noetherien). Reponse: c'is a scheme $\Hilb(X)$ representant the functor $S' \mapsto \mathcal{H}(S')$.
\end{example}

For be viable, this point of vue requiert that one dispose of methodes to verify si a functor is representable. The plupart of the exposes of FGA are consacres a this probleme. A solution definitive, prolongeant these travaux, a ete obtained by M.~Artin [1969], all to the less si one accepte of remplacer the category of the schemes by celle, more natural, of the spaces algebraic. More natural: to the same sens that the topologie étale is more natural that celle of Zariski.

\section{Topos}

Let $u \colon S' \to S$ a morphism of schemes, the ``morphism of changement of base''. The theorie of the descent [Grothendieck FGA, Seminaire Bourbaki 190, 1959--60] considered of the problemes of the types suivants. \emph{Descent of properties}: let $X$ a scheme on $S$ and $X'$ on $S'$ deduces of $X$ by changement of base. Assume that $X'/S'$ ait a property $P$. Can-one conclure that $X/S$ satisfies $P$? \emph{Descent of morphisms}: let $X$, $Y$ on $S$, $X'$, $Y'$ deduits by changement of base and $g' \colon X' \to Y'$ a morphism of $S'$-schemes. Quand $g'$ provient-it by changement of base of $g \colon X \to Y$? \emph{Descent of objects}: let $X'$ on $S'$. Quelle is the datum of descent on $S'$ requise for construct $X$ on $S$ of which $X'$ se deduise by changement of base?

Si $S'$ is the sum disjointe of the ouverts $(U_i)_{i \in I}$ of a cover of $S$, the changement of base a $S'$ is essentiellement the restriction a each $U_i$, and the problemes precedents are of the problemes of localisation on $S$ and of gluing. Recoller exige typiquement the consideration of the intersections deux a deux of the $U_i \cap U_j$, and en termes of $u \colon S' \to S$, the sum disjointe of the $U_i \cap U_j$ is simplement the fiber product $S' \times_S S'$.

The theorie of the topos permet of transposer en theorie of the descent the intuition topologique. For $u \colon S' \to S$, a morphism of changement of base of a type considered en theorie of the descent, by example fidelement plat and quasi-compact (fqc), the changement of base of $S$ a $S'$ devient a localisation. A datum of descent is the analogue of a datum of gluing.

A antecedent a the theorie of the descent is the descent galoisienne, corresponding a $S$, spectre of a field $k$ and $S'$, spectre of a extension galoisienne. Ici, $S' \times_S S'$ is sum of copies of $S'$ indexees by $\Gal(k'/k)$. The proofs, and in particular the theorie of Galois, are toutefois more simples in the cadre more general of the theorie of the descent. Selon a mot of Cartier: Grothendieck proves the theorie of Galois, and the descent galoisienne, by descent galoisienne.

the outil that is the theorie of the topos a permis the construction of the étale cohomology of the schemes, and c'is the son succes the mieux connu. A sheaf on $X$ for the topologie of Zariski is a contravariant functor of the category of the ouverts of Zariski of $X$ in celle of the sets, with a condition of gluing for $(U_i)_{i \in I}$, a cover open of Zariski of $U$. En topologie étale, the site zariskien, considered more haut, is remplace by the site étale: the category of the ouverts of Zariski is remplacee by celle of the $f \colon U \to X$ etale on $X$, and the recouvrements by the families couvrantes $(U_i)_{i \in I}$, i.e.\ a morphism surjective of scheme on $X$ of the sum disjointe of the $U_i$ in $U$. A antecedent: the introduction by Serre of the notion of space principal homogene isotrivial (=~locally trivial for a topologie proche of the topologie étale). In ses articles a Kansas [1955] and to the Tohoku [1957], Grothendieck had shows that, a category of sheaves being datum, a notion of groups of cohomology en resulte. The topologie étale fournit ainsi the étale cohomology. That the groups $H^1$ obtained let raisonnables n'is not surprenant. The miracle is that the $H^i$ superieurs let eux also raisonnables.

For Grothendieck, the importance of the theorie of the topos depasse of beaucoup the seul cas of the topologie étale. The titre given a SGA~4 en is temoin: ``Theorie of the topos and étale cohomology of the schemes''.

\textbf{Other maps:}

\medskip
\textbf{(A)} \emph{Cohomology cristalline.} The cohomology cristalline is celle of the topos cristallin, and this definition rend clear sa fonctorialite. The topos cristallin is toutefois of usage delicat and it is souvent necessary of passer a interpretation en termes of complexes of~Rham.

\textbf{(B)} \emph{Spaces rigides analytiques.} The sheaves rigides analytiques of Tate are of the sheaves coherents on a topos annele suitable and leur cohomology, the cohomology corresponding.

\textbf{(C)} \emph{Feuilletages.} A variety $X$ equipped with a feuilletage $\mathcal{F}$ defines a ``quotient'' $X/\mathcal{F}$, which is a topos locally isomorphic a celui of the sheaves on a variety. This point of vue semble avoir ete eclipse by celui of Connes which associated plutot a $\mathcal{F}$ a $C^*$-algebra not commutative.

J'aimerais again mentionner the usage of ``gros'' sites (topos) by Grothendieck, in particular for interpreter of the spaces classifiants.

\section{The six operations}

The formalism of the six operations presuppose celui of the categories derivees. For a historique of this dernier, je refer to the texte of Illusie [1990]. Idee of base: for all sortes of groups of cohomology, leur definition fournit not only these groups, but again a complex $K$ of which they are the groups of cohomology. Typiquement, this complex n'is not uniquement determine, but it the is a quasi-isomorphism pres: for deux variantes $K'$, $K''$ of $K$, one dispose of $K'''$ and of morphisms $K' \leftarrow K''' \rightarrow K''$ induisant of the isomorphisms en cohomology. In the derived category, $K$ en devient unique a isomorphism unique pres.

The six operations are of the functors between categories derivees. It s'agit of the tensor product derive $\overset{L}{\otimes}$, of the $\Hom$ interne $\mathrm{R}\!\Hom$ (donnant naissance to the $\mathcal{E}\!\mathit{xt}^i$ local), and for $f \colon X \to Y$ a morphism of schemes, of deux functors of direct image: $Rf_*$ and $Rf_!$, and of deux functors of inverse image: $Lf^*$ and $Rf^!$.

The formalism of these operations a of abord ete degage en cohomology of the sheaves coherents [Grothendieck 1963]. Ici, for the images direct, there is lieu of ne considerer that of the morphisms proper, for lesquels $Rf_* = Rf_!$. The formalism fournit a version relative of the duality of Serre, under the form of a adjonction between the functors $Rf_!$ and $Rf^!$. It fournit also a formule of the points fixes more generale that the ``Woodshole fixed point formula'', which en is contemporaine (Summer institute one algebraic geometry, Whitney estate, Woodshole, Massachusetts 1964).

Miraculeusement, the same formalism s'applique en étale cohomology with of the proofs tres differentes. It fournit again formalism of duality (of Poincare) and formules of points fixes (a the Lefschetz).

\section{Motives}

Let $X$ a algebraic variety on $k$ algebriquement clos. For each nombre prime $\ell$ prime a the caracteristique, the topologie étale fournit of the groups of cohomology $\ell$-adique $H^i_{\text{\'et}}(X, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$. Si $k$ is a under-field of $\mathbb{C}$, one dispose of isomorphisms of comparaison
\[
H^i\bigl(X(\mathbb{C}), \mathbb{Z}\bigr) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_\ell \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} H^i_{\text{\'et}}(X, \mathbb{Z}_\ell).
\]
For $k$ of caracteristique $> 0$, it n'exists not of cohomology integer fonctorielle donnant lieu a of tels isomorphisms. Neanmoins, the $H^i_{\text{\'et}}(X, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ have, for $\ell$ variable, a ``air of family''. For $i=1$, and $X$ projective and smooth, $\Pic^0(X)$ joue the role of the inexistante theorie integer: it redonne the $H^1_{\text{\'et}}(X, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ and is a object ``on $\mathbb{Z}$'', en this that the groups of homomorphismes between schemes abeliens are of type finite.

The theorie of the motives is of abord a tentative for trouver a substitut a the inexistante cohomology integer, expliquant the air of family between the $H^i_{\text{\'et}}(X, \mathbb{Z}_\ell)$, specialement for $X$ projective and smooth. One reconnait the patte of the Maitre in the idee that the probleme n'is not of define this that is a motive: the probleme is of define the category of the motives, and of degager the structures that it porte. These structures devraient permettre of prouver the Weil conjecture on the model of Serre [1960]. See Grothendieck [1969].

C'is a this occasion that Grothendieck a invente the notion of Tannakian category, developpee in the these of Saavedra [1972]. It s'agissait of formaliser the notion of tensor product of motives, corresponding by the formule of K\"unneth to the product of varieties.

Same si the theorie of the motives n'a not atteint son but original, son influence a ete grande. I refer to the conference of Seattle on the motives [Jannsen and al., 1994] for a panorama of ses maps.
