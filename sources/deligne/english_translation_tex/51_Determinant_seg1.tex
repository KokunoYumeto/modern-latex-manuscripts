\subsection{Rappels: twisting analytic (\cite{14})}

Let $X$ a surface of Riemann compacte, equipped with a metrique hermitienne
$g$, and $V$ a fiber vector holomorphic on $X$, equipped with a structure hermitienne
$h$. Notons $\Omega^1$ the fiber en lines of sections local holomorphic the 1-
forms holomorphic and $\Omega^{0,q}(V)$ the sheaf of the $(0,q)$-forms $C^\infty$ with values in
$V$. We normaliserons as suit the structure prehilbertienne on $\Gamma(X, \Omega^{0,q}(V))$
defined by $g$ and $h$: (a)~one regarde $\bar\partial$ as the datum of a structure hermi-
tienne on $\Omega^1$; (b)~The product scalaire is the integral of a densite on $X$.
(c)~Locally, si $s_0$ is a section $C^\infty$ of longueur a of $\Omega^1$ and that $v_i$ is
a section of $\Omega^{0,0}(V)$ (resp.\ $s_0^{-1} \otimes v_i$ a section of $\Omega^{0,1}(V)$), this densite
is:
\[
\text{pour } (v_1, v_2): \quad \frac{1}{2\pi i} \langle v_1, v_2 \rangle_h \cdot s_0 \wedge \bar s_0 ;
\]
\[
\text{pour } (s_0^{-1} \otimes v_1, s_0^{-1} \otimes v_2): \quad \frac{1}{2\pi i} \langle v_1, v_2 \rangle_h \cdot s_0^{-1} \bar s_0^{-1} .
\]
Recall that for $z = x + iy$, one has $\frac{1}{2\pi i} dz \wedge d\bar z = \frac{1}{\pi} dx \wedge dy$.

The groups of cohomology of $V$ are ceux of the complex $\bar\partial$ of $V$:
\[
\bar\partial: \Gamma(X, \Omega^{0,0}(V)) \longrightarrow \Gamma(X, \Omega^{0,1}(V)) .
\]
The structures prehilbertiennes of the $\Gamma(X, \Omega^{0,q}(V))$ induisent of the structures hermi-
tiennes on $H^0(X,V) = \Ker(\bar\partial)$ and $H^1(X,V) = \Coker(\bar\partial) = \Ker(\bar\partial^*) = \Ker(\bar\partial^*)$. Of the,
a structure hermitienne $\langle , \rangle_0$ on $\det H^*(X,V)$. Let $\lambda_i$ the values
proper $\neq 0$ of $\bar\partial^*\bar\partial$, comptees with leur multiplicite, and $\det' \bar\partial^*\bar\partial := \prod \lambda_i :=
\exp(-\zeta'(0))$, or $\zeta(s)$ is the prolongement analytic of $\sum \lambda_i^{-s}$. The twisting
analytic of $V$ is the structure hermitienne
\[
\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_{\text{t.a.}} := (\det' \bar\partial^*\bar\partial)^{-1} \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle_0
\]
on $\det H^*(X,V)$. One veut the calculer.

A factor $\pi$ a ete introduit in the definition of the structure prehilbertienne
of $\Gamma(X, \Omega^{0,1}(V))$ for assurer the validite of the lemma suivant.

\begin{lemma}
The twisting analytic is compatible a the duality of Serre.
\end{lemma}

Let $V' := \mathscr{H}om(V, \Omega^1)$. The duality of Serre is a duality between $H^q(X,V)$
and $H^{1-q}(X,V')$. It induces a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3.1}
\det H^*(X,V) \cong \det H^*(X,V')
\end{equation}
that one veut prouver be a isometrie.

The morphism
\[
H^q(X,V) \otimes H^{1-q}(X,V') \longrightarrow \CC
\]
is the compose of the cup-product and of the morphism trace $H^q(X,V) \otimes H^{1-q}(X,V') \to
H^1(X, V \otimes V') \to H^1(X, \Omega^1) \xrightarrow{\Tr} \CC$. Si $H^*(X, \Omega^{0,*}_X)$ is calcule by the complex
$\bar\partial$, $\Tr$ is $\pi^{-1}$ fois the integration on $X$ (a signe dependant of the conventions
pres, without importance for 1.3). The duality of Serre is therefore induite by the
duality $\int_X \alpha \wedge \beta$ between the complexes $\bar\partial$ of $V$ and $V'$ and \eqref{eq:1.3.1} en resulte.

\subsection{Calculer $\det H^*(X,L)$}

The regle suivante permet of attacher a deux fibers en line $L$ and $M$ on
$X$ a space vector of rank a $\langle L, M \rangle$: (a)~Deux sections meromorphes a
diviseurs disjoints $\ell$ and $m$ of $L$ and $M$ definissent a element not zero
$\langle \ell, m \rangle$ of $\langle L, M \rangle$. (b)~Quand one change $\ell$ or $m$, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.1}
\begin{aligned}
\langle f\ell, m \rangle &= f(\div m) \cdot \langle \ell, m \rangle \\
\langle \ell, fm \rangle &= f(\div \ell) \cdot \langle \ell, m \rangle .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Si $v_P(f)$ is the valuation of $f$ en $P$ (i.e.\ the ordre of the zero, or $-1$'ordre of the
pole), one has $\div \ell := \sum v_P(\ell) \cdot P$; one sets
\[
f(\sum n_P \cdot P) := \prod f(P)^{n_P} .
\]
That the regles (a), (b) let coherentes equivaut to the theorem of A.~Weil that for
deux functions meromorphes a diviseurs disjoints, one has $f(\div g) = g(\div f)$.

For $L$ a fiber en line on $X$, posons
\[
\det H^*(X, L - \Oo) := \det H^*(X,L) \otimes (\det H^*(X,\Oo))^{-1} .
\]
In such a way parallele, algebraic, one defines a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.2}
(\det H^*(X, L - \Oo))^{\otimes 2} \cong \langle L, L \otimes (\Omega^1)^{\otimes(-1)} \rangle .
\end{equation}
These constructions are valables over a field (the base quelconque, voire on
a base quelconque).

\subsection{Calculer the twisting analytic}

Si the fibers en lines $L$ and $M$ on $X$ are equipped with metriques hermi-
tiennes, one defines as suit a structure hermitienne on $\langle L, M \rangle$. Notons
$\|\cdot\|$ a longueur carree and posons $\|f\|_P := |f|(P) \cdot \|e\|_P$ for of the sections
meromorphes $\ell$ and $m$ as en 1.4, one defines

\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.5.1}
\log \|\langle \ell, m \rangle\|^2 = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int d'd'' \log \|\ell\|^2 \cdot \log \|m\|^2 + \log \|\ell\|^2 [\div m] + \log \|m\|^2 [\div \ell] .
\end{equation}
or $d'd''$ is pris to the sens of the distributions.

Supposons $X$ and $L$ equipped with metriques hermitiennes $g$ and $h$ as en
1.2. One munit $\Oo$ of the structure hermitienne trivial ($\|1\|^2 = 1$). One dispose
of a structure hermitienne ``twisting analytic'' on $\det H^*(X, L - \Oo)^{\otimes 2}$, deduite
of celles of $\det H^*(X,L)$ and $\det H^*(X,\Oo)$.

Notre result principal is that \eqref{eq:1.4.2} is a isometrie, the membre of
gauche being equipped with the structure hermitienne twisting analytic and celui of line
of the structure \eqref{eq:1.5.1}. Recall that a metrique on $X$ is vue as a
structure hermitienne on $\Omega^1$.

Concretement, this signifie that a construction purement algebraic permet
of attacher a of the sections meromorphes a diviseurs disjoints $\ell$ and $m$ of $L$
and $L \otimes (\Omega^1)^{\otimes(-1)}$ a element not zero $\langle \ell, m \rangle$ of $(\det H^*(X, L - \Oo))^{\otimes 2}$, with a
variance \eqref{eq:1.4.1}, and that the longueur carree of this element, for the metrique tor-
sion analytic, is given by \eqref{eq:1.5.1}.

\subsection{Incarner a integral of classes of Chern}

Let $f: X \to S$ a morphism of schemes, proper, plat and purement of a
dimension relative $N$. Let $I$ a set finite of indices, $c_i^p$ ($p > 0$, $i \in I$)
of the indeterminees, with $c_i^p$ of weight $p$, $P$ a polynome isobare of weight $N+1$
en the $c_i^p$ a coefficients integers, $V_i$ of the fibers vector on $X$, of classes
of Chern $c_i^p := c_p(V_i)$ and integrons on the fibers of $f$ the polynome $P(c_i^p)$
en the classes of Chern of the $V_i$ for obtenir on $S$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.1}
c := \int_{X/S} P(c_p(V_i)) .
\end{equation}

The definition \eqref{eq:2.1.1} a sens in various contextes. En cohomology $l$-
adique ($\ell$ inversible on $S$), one has
\[
c \in H^2(S, \ZZ_\ell(1)) .
\]
For $S$ separe of type finite on $\CC$, and en theorie of Hodge mixte, $c$ is a
cohomology class integer purement of type $(1,1)$ [mise en garde: je n'en
ai not of proof with the generalite dite; that $P(c_i^p(V_i))$ is purement
of type $(N+1, N+1)$ resulte of (P.~Deligne, theorie of Hodge III, Publ.\ Math.\ IHES 44 (1974) p.~5-73, 9.1.1 and 9.1.2); reste a see that the morphism of
Gysin $\int_{X/S}$ is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures of $H^{2N+2}(X, \QQ)$
in $H^2(S, \QQ)$; for $X$ and $S$ smooth, the duality of Poincare ramene the properties
of $\int_{X/S}$ a celles, connues, of the inverse image].

For $X$ and $S$ quasi-projective and smooth over a field algebriquement clos,
one can travailler in the rings of Chow. In this case, $c$ is a class
of equivalence lineaire of diviseurs.

In all the cas, the class $c$ is candidate a be the premiere class
of Chern of a fiber en lines on $S$. Quand one can travailler in the rings
of Chow, $c$ is deja the class of isomorphie of a fiber en line.

\begin{probleme}

inversible $\int_{X/S} P(V_i, i \in I)$ on $S$, ayant $c$ as premiere class of Chern.
\end{probleme}

\`A cause of the difficultes of signes auxquelles given lieu this probleme, je pre-
fererais y supposer $I$ totalement ordonne. Parmi the fonctorialites voulues,
one has:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.3}
\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2em}{%
Soit $(\Vect\text{-is})_X$ la cat\'egorie des fibr\'es vectoriels sur $X$ et des iso-
morphismes de fibr\'es vectoriels. Alors, $\int_{X/S} P$ est un foncteur de la cat\'egorie
$(\Vect\text{-is})_X^I$ dans la cat\'egorie $\Pic(S)$ des faisceaux inversibles sur $S$ et de
leurs isomorphismes. Il est compatible aux changements de base $S' \to S$.}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.4}
\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2em}{%
Soient $j \in I$, $P_j$ le polyn\^ome de poids $N$
\[
P_j := \sum_{p \geq 1} (\rank V_j - p + 1) c_j^{p-1} \frac{\partial}{\partial c_j^p} P
\]
(localement constant sur $X$) et $n_j$ l'entier (localement constant sur $S$)
\[
n_j := \int_{X/S} P_j(c_i^p(V_i)) .
\]
Pour $L_j$ des faisceaux inversibles sur $S$, on a
\[
P(c_p(V_i \otimes f^* L_j)) \equiv P(c_p(V_i)) + \sum_j P_j(c_p(V_i)) \cdot c_1(L_j)
\]
modulo le carr\'e de l'id\'eal engendr\'e par les $f^*(c_1(L_j))$, d'o\`u
\[
\textstyle \int_{X/S} P(c_p(V_i \otimes f^* L_j)) \cong \int_{X/S} P(c_p(V_i)) \otimes \bigotimes_j L_j^{\otimes n_j} .
\]
Pour $\lambda$ une section sur $S$ de $\Oo_S^*$, ceci am\`ene \`a demander que l'automorphisme
de multiplication par $\lambda$ sur $V_j$ agisse sur $\int_{X/S} P(V_i)$ par $\lambda^{n_j}$.}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.5}
\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2em}{%
\textbf{Additivit\'e en $P$.} Pour $P = P' + P''$, on veut un isomorphisme canonique
$\int_{X/S} P \cong \int_{X/S} P' \otimes \int_{X/S} P''$.}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.6}
\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2em}{%
\textbf{Additivit\'e par suites exactes.} Pour l'\'enoncer, il sera commode de poser
$c_i^0 = 1$. Pour $a \in I$, soit $\tilde{I}$ d\'eduire de $I$ en d\'edoublant $a$ en $a'$ et $a''$.
Soit $\tilde{P}$ le polyn\^ome en les $c_i^p$ ($i \in \tilde{I}$) obtenu en rempla\c{c}ant dans $P$ chaque
$c_a^p$ par $\sum_{r+s=p} c_{a'}^r \cdot c_{a''}^s$ ($r,s \geq 0$). Soit enfin une suite exacte
$0 \to V_a' \to V_a \to V_a'' \to 0$
et posons $V_{a'} := V_a'$, $V_{a''} := V_a''$. On veut un isomorphisme canonique entre
$\int_{X/S, P(V_i, i \in I)}$ et $\int_{X/S, \tilde{P}(V_i, i \in \tilde{I})}$.}
\end{equation}

In the cas that one sait traiter, the definition of this isomorphism pose of the
problemes of signes. They disparaissent in the version affaiblie suivante, relative
a seul of the functors $\int_{X/S} P$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.7}
\parbox{\dimexpr\linewidth-2em}{%
\textbf{Invariance par semi-simplification.} Soient des suites exactes $0 \to V_i' \to
V_i \to V_i'' \to 0$ et $sV_i := V_i' \oplus V_i''$. On veut un isomorphisme canonique
\[
\textstyle \int_{X/S} P(V_i, i \in I) \cong \int_{X/S} P(sV_i, i \in I) .
\]}
\end{equation}

More precisely, one definira to the section~4 the category of the fibers virtuels;
each fiber $V$ defines a fiber virtuel $[V]$, each exact sequence courte $V' \hookrightarrow
V \twoheadrightarrow V''$ defines a isomorphism $[V] \cong [V' \oplus V'']$ and one veut that the functor $\int_{X/S} P$
se factorise by a functor $\int_{X/S} P$ defined on the category of the fibers virtuels:
\[
\textstyle \int_{X/S} P([V_i]) = \int_{X/S} P(V_i) .
\]

\subsection{Rappel}

Recall that a \emph{category of Picard} is a category $P$, not vide, of which
every fleche is a isomorphism, equipped with a functor $+: P \times P \to P$ and of a
contrainte of associativity for $+$ (Saavedra~\cite{15} I 1.1., Mac~Lane~\cite{11}) and
such that for each object $P$ the functors $X \mapsto P + X$ and $X \mapsto X + P$ let
of the auto-equivalences of $P$. These axiomes impliquent the existence of a object unite
$0$ (Saavedra~\cite{15} I 1.3), unique a isomorphism unique pres, and that each object
$X$ admet a oppose: a object $-X$ equipped with a isomorphism $X + (-X) \cong 0$. A
tel object is unique a isomorphism unique pres.

A \emph{category of Picard commutative} is a category of Picard equipped with a
contrainte of commutativity compatible a the contrainte of associativity (Saavedra~\cite{15}, I 1.2, Mac~Lane~\cite{11}). In a telle category, one sait define the ``sum''
of a family finite $(x_i)_{i \in I}$ of objects, with the properties habituelles. Mise
en garde: for $X = Y$, the isomorphism of symetrie $X + Y \to Y + X$ n'is en general
not the identity. This n'is not inquietant: one has the same chose for the direct sum
of vector spaces.

The functor $X \mapsto -X$ is equipped with the datum of compatibility a $+$ suivante:
the isomorphism $(X + Y) + ((-X) + (-Y)) = (X + (-X)) + (Y + (-Y)) = 0 + 0 = 0$
fait of $(-X) + (-Y)$ a oppose of $X + Y$. This datum is compatible a the asso-
ciativite and a the commutativity. The functor $X \mapsto -X$ is involutif: by symetrie
of $+$, the isomorphism $X + (-X) \cong 0$ defines a isomorphism $(-X) + X \cong 0$ which
fait of $X$ the oppose of $-X$: $X \xrightarrow{\sim} -(-X)$.

\textbf{Mise en garde:} the diagram
\begin{equation}\label{eq:4.1.1}
\begin{tikzcd}
((-X) + X) + (-X) \arrow[r, "="] \arrow[d] & (-X) + (X + (-X)) \arrow[d] \\
0 + (-X) = (-X) = (-X) + 0 & (-X) + 0 \arrow[l, "="]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
n'is en general not commutative. \`A cause of (4.1.1), it faut be prudent quand
one ecrit a isomorphism by $=$ and that one soumet this $=$ to the regles of the calcul algebraic.

$A = B + X$, one deduces, by addition of $(-X)$ to the deux membres a isomorphism

which differe en general of the original. By the sequence, we attacherons en principe
