\begin{abstract}
\noindent Let $k$ a field of caracteristique $0$ and $t$ in $k$, assumed ne not be a integer $\geq 0$. We construisons a tensor category semisimple $\Rep(S_t)$ which ``interpole'' the categories of the representations lineaires (on $k$) of the groups symmetric $S_n$, $n$ grand. En a value integer $\geq 0$ of $t$, to the less deux tensor categories ``specialisation'' of $\Rep(S_t)$ exist, the a the category of representations of $S_t$, the other not semisimple.

The theorie reproduit, en more simple, of the results connus analogues for the groups lineaires or orthogonaux, which will be rappeles.

\vspace{0.3cm}
\noindent Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$. For $t$ in $k$ which is not an integer $\geq 0$, we construct a semisimple tensor category $\Rep(S_t)$ which ``interpolates'' the categories of linear representations (over $k$) of the symmetric groups $S_n$, $n$ large. When $t$ becomes a natural integer, $\Rep(S_t)$ can be ``specialized'' to a tensor category in at least two ways: as the usual category of representations of $S_t$, or as a not semisimple tensor category.

Known parallel results for the series of linear or orthogonal groups will be recalled in sections 9 and 10.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

Si $G$ is a scheme en group affine over a field $k$, the category $\Rep(G)$ of the representations lineaires of dimension finite of $G$ on $k$ is a abelian category. It is equipped of the functor tensor product of representations $\otimes : \Rep(G) \times \Rep(G) \to \Rep(G)$. This functor is ACU, i.e. it is equipped with contraintes of associativity and of commutativity, and it admet a object unite $\mathbf{1}$. It is rigide, i.e. each object $X$ admet a dual $X^\vee$; a object equipped with $\delta : \mathbf{1} \to X \otimes X^\vee$ and of $\ev : X^\vee \otimes X \to \mathbf{1}$ such that the morphisms composes
\[
\xymatrix@R=1.5pc{
X \ar[r]^{\delta \otimes X} & X \otimes X^\vee \otimes X \ar[r]^{X \otimes \ev} & X \\
X^\vee \ar[r]^{X^\vee \otimes \delta} & X^\vee \otimes X \otimes X^\vee \ar[r]^{\ev \otimes X^\vee} & X^\vee
}
\]
Let identity. Enfin, $k \isom \End(\mathbf{1})$. En of other termes, $\Rep(G)$ is, in the terminologie of (Deligne 1990) 2.1, a \emph{tensor category} on $k$.

Si $k$ is algebriquement clos, the tensor category $\Rep(G)$ determine $G$ a isomorphism pres (Saavedra 1972). Of this point of vue, the tensor categories generalisent the schemes en groups affine. One dispose of other examples of tensor categories that the $\Rep(G)$, but one n'a aucune idee of comment the classifier all, ni same celles which are semi-simples of $\otimes$-generation finite (and by the analogues to the categories $\Rep(G)$ for $G$ a algebraic group extension of a group finite by a group reductive).

A other example of tensor category is the category of the super representations of a super scheme en group affine $G$. A variante more natural is of considerer $G$ equipped with $\eps : \mu_2 \to G$ such that the action interieure of $\mu_2$ on the algebra affine $\mathcal{O}(G)$ of $G$ of\'finisse the graduation mod $2$ of $\mathcal{O}(G)$, and the category $\Rep(G, \eps)$ of the super representations $V$ for lesquelles the action of $\mu_2$ given the graduation mod $2$ of $V$.

En caracteristique $p$, S.~Gelfand and D.~Kazhdan (1992) have constructs of nouvelles tensor categories semi-simples as ``under-quotients'' of categories of representations. By against, on $k$ algebriquement clos of caracteristique $0$, the seules tensor categories that je connaisse are obtained by ``interpolation'' of categories $\Rep(G, \eps)$.

Voici a methode of interpolation: one part of a family infinie of tensor categories $\mathcal{C}_i$ on $k$, of a ultra\'filtre not trivial $\mathcal{F}$ on the set of indices $I$, and one prend the ultraproduit $\mathcal{C}$ of the $\mathcal{C}_i$. A object of $\mathcal{C}$ is therefore a family $(X_i)$ of objects of the $\mathcal{C}_i$, a morphism $(X_i) \to (Y_i)$ is a germe selon $\mathcal{F}$ of family of morphisms $X_i \to Y_i$ and the tensor product is given by $(X_i) \otimes (Y_i) = (X_i \otimes Y_i)$. The category $\mathcal{C}$ is tensor over the field $k_{\mathcal{F}}$ ultrapuissance of $k$. It is peu sympathique en this that, same si the $\mathcal{C}_i$ are of the categories of representations of algebraic groups lineaires, of the groups $\Hom$ in $\mathcal{C}$ will be of dimension infinie. For faire mieux, one can partir of tensor categories $\mathcal{C}_i$ on $k$, chacune being equipped with a object $X_i$, and considerer the subcategory tensor full $\mathcal{C}'$ of $\mathcal{C}$ generated by $X = (X_i)$. The objects of $\mathcal{C}'$ are therefore the under-quotients of the sums of $T^{r,s}(X) := X^{\otimes r} \otimes X^{\vee \otimes s}$. Assume that
\begin{equation*}
(\ast) \quad \text{Quels que soient $r$ et $s$, l'objet $T^{r,s}(X_i)$ de $\mathcal{C}_i$ est de longueur finie, born\'ee ind\'ependamment de $i$.}
\end{equation*}
Under this hypothese, the objects of $\mathcal{C}'$ are of longueur finite, and the groups $\Hom$ are therefore of dimension finite on $k_{\mathcal{F}}$ (considerer $\Hom(X,Y)$ and appliquer 3.2.4).

The of\'finition by ultraproduit is quelque peu theologique. In of bons cas, one can associer a each $i$ a parametre $\lambda_i$ in $k$, which decrit $(\mathcal{C}_i, X_i)$, and $\mathcal{C}'$ can be construite purement en terme of $\lambda = (\lambda_i) \in k_{\mathcal{F}}$. More precisely, for $k_1$ a extension of $k$ and $\lambda_1 \in k_1$ transcendant on $k$, one can construct a tensor category $\mathcal{C}(\lambda_i)$ on $k_1$, which given $\mathcal{C}'$ quand one prend $k_1 = k_{\mathcal{F}}$ and $\lambda_1 = (\lambda_i)$. The role of the ultraproduits n'is more that of avoir attire the attention on the condition $(\ast)$.

The cas ``$\lambda_1$ transcendant'' is the cas easy. More interessant, and more difficult, is of prendre $\lambda_1$ quelconque in a extension $k_1$ of $k$, or to the less of comprendre quelles values of $\lambda_1$ are ``singulieres'', and comment.

One assumed dorenavant $k$ of caracteristique $0$. The cas traite in the article is the suivant:
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*]
\item[$I$:] the set of the integers $\geq 0$;
\item[$\mathcal{C}_n$:] the category of representations of $S_n$;
\item[$X_n$:] the representation of permutation on $k^n$;
\item[parametre:] $n$;
\item[values singulieres:] the integers $\geq 0$.
\end{itemize}

The cas suivants are essentiellement connus and will be rappeles:
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*]
\item[$I$:] the set of the integers $\geq 0$;
\item[$\mathcal{C}_n$:] $\Rep(\GL(n))$ (resp $\Rep(O(n))$);
\item[$X_n$:] representation evidente;
\item[parametre:] $n$;
\item[values singulieres:] $\Z$.
\end{itemize}

In the cas or $\mathcal{C}_i$ is the category of representations of a group finite $G_i$, and or $X_i$ is the representation of permutation defined by a action of $G_i$ on a set finite $B_i$, the condition $(\ast)$ equivaut a
\begin{equation*}
(\ast') \quad \text{quel que soit $r$, le nombre d'orbites de $G_i$ dans $B_i^r$ est born\'e ind\'ependamment de $i$.}
\end{equation*}

Voici of the examples or the condition $(\ast')$ is verifee. In chacun, one fixe a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ and $I$ is the set of the integers $\geq 0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item $G_n = \GL(n, \mathbb{F}_q)$, $B_n = \mathbb{F}_q^n$;
\item $G_n = O(n, \mathbb{F}_q)$, $B_n = \mathbb{F}_q^n$;
\item $G_n = \Sp(2n, \mathbb{F}_q)$, $B_n = \mathbb{F}_q^{2n}$.
\end{enumerate}

In the cas (A), one can prendre for parametre $\lambda_n := q^n$, and je conjecture that the values singulieres of the parametre are the $q^n$ for $n$ integer $\geq 0$.

Je remercie the referee of a lecture attentive, which m'a amene a corriger plusieurs points obscurs.


\section{Terminologie and Notations}

\subsection{}
One utilisera the notations suivantes.

$k$: field of caracteristique $0$, fixe in every the sequence.

For $I$ a set finite:
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*]
\item $S_I$: symmetric group of the permutations of $I$;
\item $k^I$: space vector on $k$ librement generated by $I$;
\item $(e_i)_{i \in I}$: sa base evidente.
\end{itemize}
Si $I = \{1, \ldots, n\}$, one ecrit $S_n$ (resp.~$k^n$) plutot that $S_I$ (resp.~$k^I$).

\subsection{}
Let $(\,,)$ the form bilineaire symmetric on $k^I$ defined by $(e_i, e_j) = \delta_{ij}$. It identie $k^I$ a son dual. Regardant $k^I$ as the space of the functions of $I$ in $k$, by $u \mapsto \sum u(i) e_i$, one defines on $k^I$ a structure of algebra by $(u \cdot v)(i) = u(i) v(i)$. For this structure of algebra, one has $(u, v) = \Tr(uv)$.

\subsection{}
The functor $I \mapsto k^I$ being a adjoint a gauche, it respecte the limites inductives, and in particular transforme disjoint sums en direct sums. It transforme products finite en tensor products, by
\[
\bigotimes_{j \in J} k^{I_j} \isom k^{\prod I_j} : \otimes e_{a(j)} \longmapsto e_{(a(j))_{j \in J}}.
\]

\subsection{}
Si a group $G$ agit on $I$, it agit on $k^I$ by transport of structures: $ge_i = e_{gi}$. One appelle the representation lineaire $k^I$ of $G$ the representation of permutation defined by the $G$-set $I$. The form $(\,,)$ and the structure of algebra of 1.2 are $G$-invariantes. The functor $I \mapsto k^I$ transforme again disjoint sums (resp.~products finite) of $G$-sets en direct sums (resp.~tensor products) of representations.

\subsection{}
Let $(U_j)_{j \in J}$ a family finite of sets finite. A \emph{gluing} of the $U_j$ is a set finite $A$ equipped with a family of injections $f_j : U_j \inj A$ such that $A$ let the reunion of the $f_j(U_j)$.

The $U_j$ being consideres as fixes, a \emph{datum of gluing} on the $U_j$ is a class of isomorphie of recollements. Si $r$ is a datum of gluing, and $(f_j : U_j \inj A)$ a representant of $r$, $A$ is determine a isomorphism unique pres by $r$. This rend anodin the abus of langage, that we we permettrons, of dire ``gluing'' for ``datum of gluing''. By example, of dire ``sum on the recollements $(f_j : U_j \inj A)$ of the $U_j$ of $\varphi(A)$'' for ``sum on the data of recollements $r$ on the $U_j$ of $\varphi(A_r)$, for $(f_j : U_j \inj A_r)$ a representant of $r$''.

A datum of gluing on the $U_j$ s'identie a relation of equivalence $R$ on the sum disjointe of the $U_j$ which induise the equivalence discrete $x = y$ on each $U_j$: a $R$ associer the $U_j \inj (\bigsqcup U_j)/R$. For $J = \{1, 2\}$, it s'identie also a the datum of under-sets $U_j' \subset U_j$ and of a bijection $\varphi : U_1' \to U_2'$: lui associer the $U_j \inj U_1 \cup_\varphi U_2$.

For $K \subset J$ and $(f_j : U_j \inj A)$ a gluing of the $U_j$, the gluing induces of the $U_j$ $(j \in K)$ is the family of the $f_j : U_j \inj \bigcup_{j \in K} f_j(U_j)$.

\subsection{}
Let $A$ a ring commutative. A category $\mathcal{A}$ is dite $A$\emph{-lineaire} si the groups $\Hom$ are equipped with structures of $A$-module for lesquelles the composition is bilineaire. For $B$ a $A$-algebra commutative, $\mathcal{A} \otimes_A B$ is then the category deduite of $\mathcal{A}$ by extension of scalars of $A$ a $B$: same objects, and $\Hom(X,Y) = \Hom_A(X,Y) \otimes_A B$.

\subsection{}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ $A$-lineaire. the enveloppe additive $\mathcal{A}^{\text{add}}$ of $\mathcal{A}$ is the categories additive $A$-lineaire of the sums formelles finite of objects of $\mathcal{A}$:
\begin{itemize}[label={},leftmargin=*]
\item objects: families finite $(A_j)_{j \in J}$ of objects of $\mathcal{A}$;
\item morphisms $(A_j)_{j \in J} \to (B_k)_{k \in K}$: matrice $(f_{kj})$ of morphisms $A_j \to B_k$;
\item composition: product matriciel.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{}
A category pseudo-abelienne (SGA4 I 8.7.8 dit ``additive and Karoubian'') is an additive category in laquelle all idempotent endomorphism is the projection on a direct factor. the enveloppe pseudo-abelienne $\mathcal{A}^{\text{ps\;ab}}$ of a category $A$-lineaire $\mathcal{A}$ is the category $A$-lineaire pseudo-abelienne of the direct summands formels (images of endomorphisms idempotents) of sums formelles finite of objects of $\mathcal{A}$. See SGA4 loc.~cit.

\subsection{}
Notons $\Hom_A(\mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2)$ the category of the functors $A$-lineaires of $\mathcal{A}_1$ in $\mathcal{A}_2$. The constructions 1.7 and 1.8 have a property universelle: si $\mathcal{B}$ is $A$-lineaire and additive (resp.~pseudo-abelienne), the functor of restriction $\Hom_A(\mathcal{A}^{\text{add}}, \mathcal{B}) \to \Hom_A(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B})$ (resp.~$\Hom_A(\mathcal{A}^{\text{ps\;ab}}, \mathcal{B}) \to \Hom_A(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B})$) is a equivalence. It ``revient to the same'' of se donner a functor $A$-lineaire of $\mathcal{A}$ in $\mathcal{B}$ or of $\mathcal{A}^{\text{add}}$ (resp.~$\mathcal{A}^{\text{ps\;ab}}$) in $\mathcal{B}$.

A functor $A$-bilineaire $\mathcal{A} \times \mathcal{A} \to \mathcal{A}^{\text{add}}$ se prolonge therefore en a functor $A$-bilineaire $\mathcal{A}^{\text{add}} \times \mathcal{A}^{\text{add}} \to \mathcal{A}^{\text{add}}$, and this prolongement is unique a isomorphism unique pres. Similarly with ``add'' remplace by ``ps ab''.

Si the category $A$-lineaire $\mathcal{A}$ is equipped with a tensor product $A$-bilineaire ACU (associatif, commutative, a unite), $\mathcal{A}^{\text{add}}$ and $\mathcal{A}^{\text{ps\;ab}}$ en heritent. Si the tensor product of $\mathcal{A}$ is rigide, celui of $\mathcal{A}^{\text{add}}$ (resp.~$\mathcal{A}^{\text{ps\;ab}}$) the is also.

\subsection{}
A partition $\lambda$ is a sequence decroissante of integers $(\lambda_a)_{a \geq 1}$, nuls for $a$ sufficiently grand. One sets $|\lambda| = \sum \lambda_a$ and one dit that $\lambda$ is a partition of $\ell$ si $|\lambda| = \ell$. One regarde a sequence $\lambda_1 \geq \lambda_2 \geq \cdots \geq \lambda_r$ as a partition en the prolongeant by of the zeros. The partition $\lambda^*$ transposee of a partition $\lambda$ is caracterisee by the equivalence between $\lambda_i \geq j$ and $\lambda^*_j \geq i$. Si $\lambda$ is a partition of $\ell$, one notera again $\lambda$ the objects suivants attached a $\lambda$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item The class of isomorphie of representations irreductibles of $S_\ell$ corresponding; sa class in the group of Grothendieck $R(S_\ell)$ of the representations of $S_\ell$, and son character. The representation trivial of $S_\ell$ correspond a the partition grossiere $\ell$ of $\ell$.
\item For $N$ sufficiently grand for that $\lambda_{N+1} = 0$: the weight dominant $(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_N)$ of $\GL(N)$.
\end{enumerate}

One supposera choisie for each $\ell$ and each partition $\lambda$ of $\ell$ a representation irreducible of class $\lambda$ of $S_\ell$. The category of the sets $U$ a $\ell$ elements and of the bijections between eux being equivalent a sa full subcategory reduite a the object $\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$, it revient to the same of se donner a telle representation $V_\lambda$, or a functor $U \mapsto V_\lambda[U]$. In particular, the choix of $V_\lambda$ defines, for every set $U$ a $\ell$ elements, a representation of $S_U$, that we shall denote again $\lambda$. It is deduite of $V_\lambda$ by twisting by the $S_\ell$-torseur of the bijections of $\{1, \ldots, \ell\}$ with $U$.

In this which precede, the field of base is $\Q$. One pourrait same prendre the ring of base $\Z[1/\ell!]$, The cas of other field or rings of base s'en deduces by extension of scalars. Si $X$ is a representation of $S_U$, one sets
\begin{equation}
X_\lambda := \Hom(\lambda, X). \tag{1.10.1}
\end{equation}
One sait that
\begin{equation}
\Q[S_U] \isom \bigoplus_{|\lambda| = \ell} \End(\lambda) \tag{1.10.2}
\end{equation}
and it follows that the map $S_U$-equivariante natural
\begin{equation}
\bigoplus_{|\lambda| = \ell} X_\lambda \otimes \lambda \isom X \tag{1.10.3}
\end{equation}
is a isomorphism. This construction garde a sens for $X$ in a category pseudo-abelienne $\Z[1/\ell!]$-lineaire.


\section{The Category $\Rep(S_t)$}

\subsection{}
Fixons a set finite $I$. For $U$ a set finite, notons $[U]_I$ the representation of permutation of $S_I$ on the set $\Inj(U, I)$ of the injections of $U$ in $I$. For $U$ (resp.~$I$) the set finite $\{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$, one remplace in the notation $U$ (resp.~$I$) by $n$.

For $\varphi : U \inj V$ a injection, the composition with $\varphi$: $\Inj(V, I) \to \Inj(U, I)$ induces
\[
\res_\varphi : [V]_I \to [U]_I
\]
of transpose e (1.2)
\[
\res^*_\varphi : [U]_I \to [V]_I.
\]
one has $\res_\varphi(e_g) = e_{g\varphi}$ and $\res^*_\varphi(e_f) = \sum_{g\varphi = f} e_g$.

\subsection{}
Let $C$ a gluing of the sets finite $U$ and $V$, and $D$ the fiber product of $U$ and $V$ on $C$:
\begin{equation}
\xymatrix@R=1.5pc@C=1.5pc{
U \ar@{^{(}->}[r]^{a} & C \\
D \ar@{^{(}->}[u] \ar@{^{(}->}[r]_{b} & V \ar@{^{(}->}[u]_{b'}
\ar@{^{(}->}[ul]
} \tag{2.2.1}
\end{equation}
Si $U' := a'(D)$, $V' := b'(D)$ and si $\varphi$ is the bijection $b' a'^{-1} : U' \isom V'$, one has $U \cup_\varphi V \isom C$. Posons
\[
\{C\}_I := \res^*_{b'} \res_{a'} \quad \text{(resp } (C)_I := \res_{b'} \res^*_{a'} \text{)} : [U]_I \longrightarrow [V]_I.
\]
These morphisms will be also notes $\{\varphi\}_I$ and $(\varphi)_I$ and, quand that ne cree not of ambiguite, the indice $I$ will be omis.

One defines a relation of ordre between data on recollements of $U$ and $V$ en disant that $A$ is more grossier that $C$ ($A \leq C$), si, $D$ and $\varphi$ being as ci-dessus, the diagram
\[
\xymatrix@R=1.5pc@C=1.5pc{
U \ar@{^{(}->}[r] \ar@{^{(}->}[dr] & A \\
D \ar@{^{(}->}[u] \ar@{^{(}->}[r] & V \ar@{^{(}->}[u]
}
\]
is commutative, i.e. si $A$ is defined by a bijection $\psi : U'' \isom V''$, with $U' \subset U'' \subset U$ and $V' \subset V'' \subset V$, which prolonge $\varphi$.

For $f$ in $\Inj(U, I)$, the image of $e_f$ by $\{C\}$ (resp~$(C)$) is the sum of the $e_g$, $g$ in $\Inj(V, I)$, such that $f$ and $g$ se recollent en a map (resp. a map injective) of $C$ in $I$. This description shows that
\begin{equation}
\{C\} = \sum_{A \leq C} (A). \tag{2.2.2}
\end{equation}

Si $A \leq C$ is defined by $\psi : U'' \isom V''$, the set ordonne of the recollements $A_1$ between $A$ and $C$ is isomorphic a the set ordonne by $\supset$ of the parties of $U'' - U'$. \`A $A_1$, defined by $\psi_1 : U_1'' \to V_1''$, or $U' \subset U_1'' \subset U''$ and or $\psi_1$ is the restriction of $\psi$, associer $U_1'' - U'$. The function of Moebius for the set ordonne of the data of recollements is therefore $\mu(A,C) = (-1)^{|U''| - |U'|} = (-1)^{|C| - |A|}$, and (2.2.2) s'inverse en
\begin{equation}
(C) = \sum_{A \leq C} (-1)^{|C| - |A|} \{A\}. \tag{2.2.3}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Cas particuliers:}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Si in (2.2.1) $a'$ (resp.~$b'$) is bijective, of sorte that the gluing $C$ is defined by $\varphi : U \inj V$ (resp.~$\varphi : V \inj U$), one has $(C) = \{C\} = \res^*_\varphi$ (resp.~$(C) = \{C\} = \res_\varphi$).
\item Si the gluing is defined by a bijection $\varphi$ of $U$ with $V$, $(\varphi)$ is the fonctorialite of $U \mapsto [U]_I$ for the bijections, such that defined by transport of structures.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{construction}
Let $(U_j)_{j \in J}$ a family finite of sets finite. The tensor product of the $[U_j]_I$ is naturally isomorphic a the direct sum, etendues to the recollements $(u_j : U_j \inj C)$, of the $[C]_I$.
\end{construction}

\begin{proof}
The tensor product of the $[U]_I$ is the representation of permutation defined by the $S_I$-set product of the $\Inj(U_j, I)$. Each systeme of injections $u_j : U_j \inj I$ defines the gluing $u_j : U_j \inj \cup u_j(U_j)$. The datum of gluing $r$ corresponding ne depend that of the $S_I$-orbite of $(u_j)_{j \in J}$, and this fait of $\prod \Inj(U_j, I)$ the sum disjointe of the $\Inj(C, I)$, for $(a_j : U_j \inj C)$ a datum of gluing. One conclut by 1.3.
\end{proof}

\end{equation}

\prod_{1 \leq i < j \leq N} ((\lambda_i - i) - (\lambda_j - j)) \Big/ \prod_{1 \leq i < j \leq N} (j - i). \tag{10.6.7}

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\[

\[

\end{proof}

\section*{Appendix B. Proof of the Conjecture in 8.20, by Victor Ostrik}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Appendix B}

The goal of this appendix is to present proofs of Conjecture from Section 8.20 and Conjecture 8.21.1. We preserve the notations from Section 8. Thus $k$ is a field of characteristic $0$, $\mathcal{T}$ is a tensor category over $k$, $T$ is an object of $\mathcal{T}$ with the structure of algebra as in 8.2, $I = \Spec(T)$ and $S_I$ is the group scheme (in $\mathcal{T}$) of automorphisms of $I$, see 8.10. Finally $\eps : \pi \to S_I$ is the natural homomorphism from the fundamental group $\pi$ of $\mathcal{T}$ and $\Rep(S_I, \eps)$ is the full subcategory of $\Rep(S_I)$ consisting of such representations $\rho : S_I \to \GL(V)$ that the action $\rho \circ \eps$ of $\pi$ one $V$ coincides with the canonical action, see 8.20. The following result is conjectured in 8.20:

\begin{proposition}
Any object of the category $\Rep(S_I, \eps)$ is isomorphic to a subquotient of a direct sum of some tensor powers of $T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the full subcategory of $\Rep(S_I, \eps)$ whose objects are subquotients of sums of tensor powers of $T$. It is clear that $\mathcal{C}$ is a tensor category. Let $H$ be the fundamental group of $\mathcal{C}$. Obviously, $S_I$ is contained in (ind-objects of) $\mathcal{C}$ and acts functorially compatibly with tensor products one the objects of $\mathcal{C}$. Thus we have a homomorphism $S_I \to H$. One the other hand since $H$ acts one $T$ we have an obvious map $H \to \GL(T)$ and since $T$ generates $\mathcal{C}$ this is actually an embedding $H \subset \GL(T)$. Moreover since $H$ respects the multiplication morphism $T \otimes T \to T$ we have $H \subset S_I \subset \GL(T)$. The composition of homomorphisms $S_I \to H \subset S_I$ is the identity and therefore $H = S_I$. Now the result follows from (Deligne 1990) 8.17.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[cf. Conjecture 8.21.1]
Let $F : \Rep(S_t, k) \to \mathcal{T}$ be a tensor functor preserving the commutativity constraint (= $\otimes$-functor ACU). Let $F_1$ be the corresponding functor $\Rep(S_t, k) \to \Rep(S_I, \eps)$, see 8.20.1. Assume that $t$ is not an integer $\geq 0$. Then $F_1$ is an equivalence of tensor categories.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
First we note that the functor $F_1$ is fully faithful. Indeed according to 8.11 the functor $F_1$ is surjective one Hom's. Arguing in the same way as in 8.19 we see that to prove its injectivity one Hom's we need to prove that $[U]_I$ is nonzero. But this is obvious since $\dim [U]_I = t(t-1)\cdots(t-|U|+1) \neq 0$. Now Corollary is an immediate consequence of Proposition B1.
\end{proof}

With the notations of 10.18, a parallel argument shows that any object of the category $\Rep(\GL(X), \eps)$ is isomorphic to a subquotient of a sum of $X^{\otimes p} \otimes X^{\vee \otimes q}$, and that the answer to the question (10.18.1) is positive.
