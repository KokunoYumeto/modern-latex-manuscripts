Si $f$ and $g$ are deux functions meromorphes on a surface of Riemann $S$, the tame symbol of $f$ and $g$ en $x \in S$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1}
(f,g)_x = (-1)^{v(f)v(g)}\left(\frac{g^{v(f)}}{f^{v(g)}}\right)(x),
\end{equation}
or $v(f)$ is the valuation of $f$ en $x$: the ordre of the zero, the oppose of the ordre of the pole. The rapport $g^{v(f)}/f^{v(g)}$ is of valuation $0$, therefore can be evalue en $x$. The definition \eqref{eq:1.1} is due
a J.~Tate (cf.~[3], p.~65) and, in [3], III, n${}^\circ$~4, $(f,g)_x$ apparait as the symbol local
for the group multiplicatif. En tant that tel, for $S$ compacte, it given lieu a formule
of the product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.2}
\prod_{x \in S} (f,g)_x = 1
\end{equation}
(presque all the factors are egaux a $1$) which generalise the loi of reciprocite of Weil
$f(\operatorname{div}(g)) = g(\operatorname{div}(f))$ for $f$ and $g$ a diviseurs disjoints. Si $K$ is the field of the fractions of a
ring of valuation discrete of field residuel $k$, the definition \eqref{eq:1.1} garde a sens for
define $(\,,) : K^* \otimes K^* \to k^*$ and (to the signe pres) this morphism s'interprete en $K$-theorie
as the compose of the product $K_1(K) \otimes K_1(K) \to K_2(K)$ with the cobord $K_2(K) \to K_1(k)$.

Let $D$ the region of $\C$ bordee by a curve $C^\infty$ $\Gamma$ and $f$, $g$ deux functions mero-
morphes on a neighborhood of $D$, without zero ni pole on $\Gamma$. The point of depart of this article
a ete a formule exprimant the product of the $(f,g)_x$ ($x \in D$) as the monodromy on $\Gamma$
of a fiber en lines plat on $\Gamma$ defined by the restrictions of $f$ and $g$ a $\Gamma$. Concretement
c'is a formule exprimant this product by a integral on $\Gamma$.

Selon a suggestion of O.~Gabber, the point essential is the construction, in the
derived category of the category of the sheaves of groups abeliens on a surface of
Riemann $S$, of a morphism of degree $1$ of $\cO^* \otimes \cO^*$ in $\C^*$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3}
\cO^* \otimes^L \cO^* \to \C^*[1].
\end{equation}
This morphism is given by a formule explicite to the niveau of complexes resolvant $\cO^*$
and $\C^*$. This permet of en donner a interpretation local, as loi which a deux functions
holomorphic inversibles $f$ and $g$ attached a $\C^*$-torseur (= space principal homogene,
see 5.2) $(f,g)$ or, this which revient to the same, a fiber en lines plat.

En dimension quelconque, one dispose again of a morphism analogue, with values in the complex $\cO^* \xrightarrow{d\log} \Omega^1$, quasi-isomorphic a $\C^*$ en dimension $1$ but not en dimension $>1$. This morphism fournit a loi which, a $f$ and $g$ holomorphic inversibles, attached
a fiber en lines $(f,g)$ a connexion (en general not integrable). En fait, this morphism
is simplement celui which given the product en cohomology of Beilinson-Deligne ($\Z(1), \Z(1)$
toward $\Z(2)$) and we donnons a interpretation corresponding of $(f,g)$ en termes of
theorie of Hodge to the \S~3.

One dispose of constructions analogues on the varieties $C^\infty$, $\cO^*$ being remplace by
the sheaf of the functions inversibles $C^\infty$ and $\cO^* \xrightarrow{d\log} \Omega^1$ by son analogue $C^\infty$. Less of
regularite is possible (cf.~4.6).

For $G$ a group analytic complex commutative, \eqref{eq:1.3} admet a generalisation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4}
\cO^* \otimes^L G(\cO) \to G(\C)(1)[1],
\end{equation}
permettant of attacher a $G$-torseur plat $(f,g]$ a $f$ holomorphic inversible and $g$ holomorphic
with values in $G$ on a surface of Riemann $S$. The monodromy of $(f,g]$ is analogue
to the symbols local of [3], chap.~III, and \eqref{eq:1.4} given lieu a of the analogues analytiques
complexes of the field of class geometric. Ainsi, si $\cL$ is a sheaf inversible trivialise
a the infini and $g$ a function holomorphic with values in $G$ one deduces of \eqref{eq:1.4} a symbol
$(\cL,g] \in G$ with $(\cO(x), g] = g(x)$. C'is a analogue of [3], III, \S~3, Th.~1.

In the cas of the cercle $S^1$, $f, g \mapsto {}$ monodromy of $(f,g) \in \C^*$ identifie, en a sens
a preciser, the group multiplicatif of the functions inversibles on $S^1$ a son proper dual ``of
Cartier'' (4.4, 4.7).

For avoir a states proper, it faut veiller a the regularite of the functions utilisees.
In what follows of the introduction, we pretendrons, a tort, that it s'agit simplement of the
group $\Omega^0(S^1)$ of the functions $C^\infty$ inversibles, a values complexes, on $S^1$.

This autodualite is antisymetrique. It fournit a analogue analytic of the result
of Contou-Carrere selon lequel the functor en groups which, a each ring commutative $A$ attached the group multiplicatif $(A[[T]][T^{-1}])^*$, is son proper dual of Cartier.

We prouvons also a variante tordue by a systeme local of $\Z$-modules libres of
type finite on $S^1$.

Si $S$ is a surface of Riemann a bord, of bord a cercle $S$, the values to the bord
of functions holomorphic inversibles on $S$ forment a under-space isotrope maximal,
en a sens a preciser, of $\Omega^0(S)$. We prouvons also a variante quand the bord of $S$
is the sum disjointe of plusieurs cercles, and a variante tordue by a systeme local
on $S$. One can see these results as paralleles a the autodualite of the jacobienne,

In all this which precede, we have omis of the twists a the Tate ($= {}$ tensor products

To the dernier section, we utilisons these outils for a construction preliminaire
a celle of theories conformes of the champs holomorphic attached to certain orbifoldes
of the type quotient of a tore by a group finite. Let $B$ a form bilineaire paire and
defined positive on $\Z^n$ and $S$ a cercle oriente. A $B$, more a datum auxiliaire (see 5.1),
we attachons a central extension $\tilde{\Gamma}$ of $\Omega^0(S)^n$ by $\C^*$, $\C^*$ being of indice finite in
the centre, and egal to the centre si $B$ is of discriminant $1$. There is lieu of regarder $\tilde{\Gamma}$ as

construisons a lifting canonical of $\Omega^0(S) \subset \Omega^0(S)^n$ in $\tilde{\Gamma}$. The under-space
$\Omega^0(S)^n$ jouit, vis-a-vis of the commutateur and of the structure reelle, of a property of positivite which permet of considerer son lifting as a polarisation complex of $\tilde{\Gamma}$.
one has also of the variantes for $S$ ayant plusieurs cercles to the bord, and $\Z^n$ remplace by a

\section{The construction principal}

\subsection{}

Let $X$ a variety analytic complex. We shall denote $\cO$ the sheaf of ses
functions holomorphic and $\cO^*$ celui of the functions holomorphic inversibles. Si $G$ is a
group analytic complex commutative, one denotes $G(\cO)$ the sheaf of the functions holomorphic of $X$ in $G$ and $G(\C)$ the sheaf constant $G$: celui of the functions locally
constants. For $G$ the group multiplicatif $\Gm$, note $\C^*$, $G(\cO)$ is $\cO^*$. One notera $G$
multiplicativement. For $X$ of dimension $1$, one has a exact sequence of sheaves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.1}
0 \to G(\C) \to G(\cO) \xrightarrow{g^{-1}dg} \Omega^1 \otimes \operatorname{Lie}(G) \to 0
\qquad (\dim X = 1).
\end{equation}
One sets $\Z(n) := (2\pi i)^n\Z$ and the one ecrit $(n)$ for a tensor product with $\Z(n)$. Souvent, we choisirons a racine carree $i$ of $-1$. This defines a orientation of $\C$, therefore
of $X$, and a isomorphism $\Z \xrightarrow{\sim} \Z(1) : 1 \mapsto (2\pi i)$, by lequel we identifierons $G$ and $G(1)$.

\subsection{}

Notons $[A \xrightarrow{f} B]_{-n}$ the complex reduit a $A$ and $B$ en degrees $n$ and $n+1$,
of differentielle $f$. We construirons, in the derived category, a morphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.2.1}
\cO^* \otimes^L G(\cO) \to [G(\cO) \xrightarrow{g^{-1}dg} \Omega^1 \otimes \operatorname{Lie}(G)]_{-1}(1).
\end{equation}
It is enough for that of construct a complex of sheaves $K$, a quasi-isomorphism
$a : K \to \cO^*$ of $K$ with the complex reduit a $\cO^*$ en degree $0$ and a morphism of complexes
$K \otimes G(\cO) \to [G(\cO) \xrightarrow{g^{-1}dg} \Omega^1 \otimes \operatorname{Lie}(G)](1)$.

One prend for $K$ the complex $[\Z(1) \to \cO]_{-1}$ of differentielle the inclusion of
$\Z(1) = 2\pi i\Z$ in $\cO$, and for $a$ en degree $0$, $a^0 : \cO \to \cO^*$ the exponentielle. The fait that $a$
is a quasi-isomorphism traduit the exactitude of
\[
0 \to \Z(1) \to \cO \xrightarrow{\exp} \cO^* \to 0.
\]
Reste a define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.2.2}
[\Z(1) \to \cO]_{-1} \otimes G(\cO) \to [G(\cO) \xrightarrow{g^{-1}dg} \Omega^1 \otimes \operatorname{Lie}(G)]_{-1}(1).
\end{equation}
For $F$ a sheaf of vector spaces complexes, by example $\operatorname{Lie}(G) \otimes \Omega^1$, one identifie
$F$ a $F(1)$ by $f \otimes n \mapsto nf$ ($n \in 2\pi i\Z$). One prend for composantes of \eqref{eq:2.2.2}
the identity: $\Z(1) \otimes G(\cO) \to G(\cO)(1)$
and
$f \otimes g \mapsto f \cdot (g^{-1}dg) : \cO \otimes G(\cO) \to \operatorname{Lie}(G) \otimes \Omega^1 \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{Lie}(G) \otimes \Omega^1(1)$.

Si the one choisit $i \in \C$, of or a identification of $\cF$ with $\cF(1)$ for every sheaf $\cF$,
these fleches s'identifient plutot, respectivement, a the identity and a
\[
f \otimes g \mapsto \frac{1}{2\pi i} f \cdot (g^{-1}dg).
\]

\subsection{}

By passage a the cohomology, \eqref{eq:2.2.1} defines
\[
H^i(X, \cO^*) \otimes H^j(X, G(\cO)) \to H^{i+j+1}(X, [G(\cO) \xrightarrow{g^{-1}dg} \Omega^1 \otimes \operatorname{Lie} G]_{-1}(1)).
\]
For $i = j = 0$, the membre of line is the group of the classes of isomorphie of $G(1)$-
torseurs holomorphic on $X$, equipped with a connexion (not assumed integrable). Parce
that \eqref{eq:2.2.1} is given by a formule explicite, to the niveau of the complexes, the map
se precise en a loi local which, a $f$ holomorphic inversible and a $g$ function holomorphic
with values in $G$, attached a $G(1)$-torseur holomorphic a connexion $(f,g]$ bien defined
a isomorphism unique pres.

It we will be commode of choisir $i \in \C$ of sorte that $G$ s'identifie a $G(1)$. It s'agit
then of construct a $G$-torseur a connexion.

The construction will be local. Locally, on a open $U$, one aura a choisir a datum auxiliaire $\lambda$, section on $U$ of a sheaf $\cH$. The sheaf $\cH$ n'aura not en
general of section global, but it is locally not vide. One construira a $G$-torseur a connexion $(f,g]_{(\lambda)}$ on $U$ ($\lambda \in \cH(U)$) and a systeme transitif of isomorphisms between the $(f,g]_{(\lambda)}$.
This definira $(f,g]$ on $U$ a isomorphism unique pres, of the that $\cH(U) \neq \emptyset$. The
construction is en outre compatible a the restriction a open $V \subset U$: the $(f,g]$ defined
locally se recollent en the $(f,g]$ voulu.

The sheaf of the data auxiliaires is celui of the logarithmes of $f$. Si $\lambda = \log f$
is a logarithme of $f$: $f = \exp(\log f)$, one prend for $(f,g]_{(\lambda)}$ the torseur trivial $G(\cO)$
equipped with the connexion $\nabla$ for which
\[
\nabla s = s^{-1}ds + \frac{1}{2\pi i} \log f \cdot g^{-1}dg.
\]

Si the one remplace $\lambda = \log f$ by $\lambda' = \log f + n \cdot 2\pi i$, the isomorphism
\[
(f,g]_{(\lambda')} \to (f,g]_{(\lambda)}
\]
is $G(\cO) \to G(\cO) : s \mapsto s \cdot g^n$.

En of other termes, the choix of $\log f$ defines a trivialisation $\{\log f, g\}$ of $(f,g]$, and
\begin{align}
\nabla\{\log f, g\} &= \frac{1}{2\pi i} \log f \cdot g^{-1}dg, \label{eq:2.3.1} \\
\{\log f + n \cdot 2\pi i, g\} &= \{\log f, g\} \cdot g^n. \label{eq:2.3.2}
\end{align}
The courbure $R_{\nabla}$ of $\nabla$ of $(f,g]$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.3}
R_{\nabla} = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \frac{df}{f} \wedge g^{-1}dg \in \Omega^2 \otimes \operatorname{Lie}(G).
\end{equation}

\subsection{}

The reader more a the aise with the langage of the cocycles of Cech that with celui
of 2.3 can traduire 2.3 as suit. Let $(U_i)_{i \in I}$ a cover open of $S$, such that, on each $U_i$, $f$ admette a logarithme $(\log f)_i$. The $G$-torseur $(f,g]$ is then
defined by the cocycle
\[
c_{ij} = g^{((\log f)_j - (\log f)_i)/2\pi i}
\]
and sa connexion by the $1$-forms, with values in $\operatorname{Lie} G$, $\frac{1}{2\pi i} (\log f)_i \cdot g^{-1}dg$.

\subsection{Variant}

On a variety $C^\infty$, one attached similarly a function inversible $f$
and a map $g$ with values in $G$ a torseur a connexion $(f,g]$. En dimension
reelle $1$, the connexion is automatiquement integrable and $(f,g]$ is a $G$-torseur plat.

\subsection{}

Prenons for $X$ a surface of Riemann $S$. One is en dimension $1$ and, composant \eqref{eq:2.2.1} with the inverse of the quasi-isomorphism
\[
G(\C)[1] \xrightarrow{\sim} [G(\cO) \xrightarrow{g^{-1}dg} \Omega^1 \otimes \operatorname{Lie} G]_{-1}
\]
deduces of \eqref{eq:2.1.1}, tordu by $\Z(1)$, one obtains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.6.1}
\cO^* \otimes^L G(\cO) \to G(\C)(1)[1].
\end{equation}
By passage a the cohomology, \eqref{eq:2.6.1} induces of the morphisms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.6.2}
H^i(S, \cO^*) \otimes H^j(S, G(\cO)) \to H^{i+j+1}(S, G(\C)(1)).
\end{equation}
For $i = j = 0$, the membre of line $H^1(S, G(\C)(1))$ is the group of the classes of isomorphie
of $G(\C)(1)$-torseurs and \eqref{eq:2.6.2} attached to $f$ and $g$ the class of isomorphie of the $G(\C)(1)$-
torseur of the sections horizontales of $(f,g]$. This $G(\C)(1)$-torseur will be again note $(f,g]$.

in $H^1(X, \mathscr{G})$.

The gerbes interpretent similarly the $H^2$. A gerbe on $X$ is a champ on $X$,
admettant locally a object and of which all the objects are locally isomorphes. Rap-
pelons that a champ on $X$ is the datum of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*)]
\item For each open $U$, the category $G(U)$ of the objects of $G$ on $U$;
\item For $V \subset U$, a notion of morphism of a object of $G(V)$ in a object
of $G(U)$. The morphisms se composent; for $U = V$, it s'agit simplement of the mor-
phismes in $G(U)$; each object $Q$ of $G(U)$ admet a ``restriction'' $Q|V$ of $Q$ a $V$,
equipped with $Q|V \to Q$, with, for $P$ in $G(V)$,
\[
\Hom(P, Q|V) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Hom(P, Q);
\]
\item En a sens suitable, the objects and the morphisms se recollent: connaitre
a object on $U$ revient a the connaitre locally on $U$.
\end{enumerate}

Si $\mathscr{G}$ is a gerbe on $X$ and that, for every object $P$ of $\mathscr{G}$ on a open $U$, the
sheaf $\Aut(P)$ on $U$ of the automorphismes of $P$:
\[
\Aut(P)(V) = \Aut(P|V)
\]
is commutative, this sheaf $\Aut(P)$ ne depend not of the choix of $P$ and the $\Aut(P)$ se recollent
en a sheaf of groups abeliens on $X$, appele the \emph{lien} of $\mathscr{G}$. For $\mathscr{G}$ a sheaf of
groups abeliens, a gerbe of lien $\mathscr{G}$, or liee by $\mathscr{G}$ is a gerbe $\mathscr{G}$ equipped with a isomorphism of son lien with $\mathscr{G}$.

\begin{example}
The champ of the $\mathscr{G}$-torseurs is a gerbe of lien $\mathscr{G}$. One the appelle the
\emph{gerbe trivial} of lien $\mathscr{G}$.
\end{example}

\begin{miseengarde}
The gerbes liees by $\mathscr{G}$ forment a $2$-category. This correspond
to the fait that one interprete a $H^2$.
\end{miseengarde}

Continuons a supposer $\mathscr{G}$ commutative; $H^2(X, \mathscr{G})$ s'interprete as the set
of the classes of equivalence of gerbes liees by $\mathscr{G}$. Si $\mathscr{G}_i$ is a gerbe liee by $\mathscr{G}_i$ ($i = 1, 2$),
