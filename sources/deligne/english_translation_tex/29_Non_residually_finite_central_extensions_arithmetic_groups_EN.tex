\section*{GROUP THEORY}

\begin{flushleft}
\textbf{Non-residually finite central extensions of arithmetic groups.} Note ($^*$) by \textbf{Pierre Deligne}, Member of the Academy.
\end{flushleft}

A variant of the known methods for treating the congruence subgroup problem gives results such as the following. Let $\widetilde{\Sp}(2n,\mathbf Z)$ be the inverse image of $\Sp(2n,\mathbf Z)$ in the universal covering of $\Sp(2n,\mathbf R)$; this is a central extension of $\Sp(2n,\mathbf Z)$ by
\[
\pi_1\Sp(2n,\mathbf R)=\mathbf Z.
\]
If $n\ge2$, then $\widetilde{\Sp}(2n,\mathbf Z)$ is not separated for the topology of subgroups of finite index. More precisely, every subgroup of finite index in $\widetilde{\Sp}(2n,\mathbf Z)$ contains $2\pi_1\Sp(2n,\mathbf R)$.

\subsection*{1. $S$-arithmetic groups}

Let $G$ be an almost simple simply connected isotropic group over a global field $F$. To simplify the statements, we shall also assume that $G(F)$ is perfect, that is, equal to its commutator subgroup; this is the case except possibly when $G$ is a trialitarian form of $D_4$. Let $S$ be a finite set of places of $F$, containing all the archimedean places, and suppose that
\begin{equation}\tag{i}
\sum_{v\in S}\rank(G/F_v)\ge2.
\end{equation}

For each place $v$ of $F$, put $G_v=G(F_v)$. For each finite set $T$ of places, put similarly
\[
G_T=\prod_{v\in T}G_v,
\qquad
G_{T'}=\prod_{v\notin T}G_v
\]
where the second product is restricted. Thus, for $\mathbf A$ the adele ring of $F$,
\[
G(\mathbf A)=G_T\times G_{T'}.
\]

A subgroup of $G(F)$ is called $S$-congruence if it is the inverse image of a compact open subgroup of $G_S$. It is called $S$-arithmetic if it is commensurable with an $S$-congruence subgroup, equivalently if it contains, with finite index, a subgroup of finite index in an $S$-congruence subgroup. The $S$-congruence subgroups, respectively the $S$-arithmetic subgroups, form a fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity for group topologies $\mathscr T(c)$ and $\mathscr T(a)$ on $G(F)$. For a fixed $S$-congruence subgroup $\Gamma$, one obtains the same fundamental systems by considering only the $S$-congruence subgroups normal in $\Gamma$, respectively only the normal subgroups of finite index in $\Gamma$. This ensures the existence of the completions
\[
G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(c),
\qquad
G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)
\]
of $G(F)$ for $\mathscr T(c)$ and $\mathscr T(a)$. The topology $\mathscr T(c)$ is the topology induced from $G_S$. Since $G(F)$ is dense in $G_S$ by strong approximation, one has
\[
G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(c)=G_S.
\]
The completion $G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$ is an extension of $G_S$ by the profinite group
\[
C(S,G)=\varprojlim \Gamma/\Delta,
\]
where the inverse limit is over pairs $\Delta\subset\Gamma$ with $\Delta$ normal of finite index in $\Gamma$ and $\Gamma$ $S$-congruence.

For $G$ of rank at least $2$, Raghunathan ($^1$) proved that
\begin{equation}\tag{ii}
\text{$G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$ is a central extension of $G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(c)=G_S$,}
\end{equation}
and he finishes checking that the same conclusion holds under the sole hypothesis (i).

Let $\widetilde G_S$ be a topological central extension of $G_S$ by a discrete group $A$, and let $\widetilde{G(F)}$ be the inverse image of $G(F)$ in $\widetilde G_S$. A subgroup of $\widetilde{G(F)}$ is called $S$-congruence if it is the inverse image of an $S$-congruence subgroup of $G(F)$, and is called $S$-arithmetic if it is commensurable with an $S$-congruence subgroup. We again denote the corresponding topologies by $\mathscr T(c)$ and $\mathscr T(a)$, and the separated completions by
\[
\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(c),
\qquad
\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a).
\]
Again $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(c)=G_S$, while $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$ is an extension of $G_S$ by a profinite group $\widetilde C(S,G)$, itself an extension of $C(S,G)$ by
\[
\widehat A=\varprojlim A/(A\cap\Gamma)
\qquad\text{($\Gamma$ $S$-arithmetic).}
\]

\begin{proposition}
If condition (ii) holds, then $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$ is a central extension of $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(c)=G_S$.
\end{proposition}

Recall that, if a group $\widetilde H$ is a central extension of a group $H$, then the commutator map $\widetilde H\times\widetilde H\to\widetilde H$ factors through a map, still denoted $(\,,):H\times H\to\widetilde H$. In particular, since $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$ is a central extension of $G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$ by $\widehat A$, the commutator map
\[
\widetilde C(S,G)\times\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)\to\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)
\]
factors through $C(S,G)\times G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$. By (ii), it factors through a bimultiplicative map
\[
C(S,G)\times G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)\to\widehat A.
\]
Since $G(F)$ is perfect and dense in $G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$, this map is trivial.

More concretely, the proposition says that, for every $S$-arithmetic subgroup $\Delta$, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1a)] For every conjugate $\Delta^g$ of $\Delta$, with $g\in\widetilde{G(F)}$, there exists an $S$-congruence subgroup $\Gamma$ such that
\[
\Gamma\cap\Delta=\Gamma\cap\Delta^g.
\]
\item[(1b)] $\widetilde{G(F)}$ acts trivially on the finite group
\[
C_\Delta=\varprojlim\Gamma/(\Gamma\cap\Delta).
\]
\end{enumerate}
These conditions are also equivalent to
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1c)] For every $g\in G(F)$, there exists an $S$-congruence subgroup $\Gamma$ such that $(g,\Gamma)\subset\Delta$.
\end{enumerate}
One has $\widetilde C(S,G)=\varprojlim C_\Delta$.

\subsection*{2. Central extensions of adelic groups}

From now on we assume that
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(iii)] $\widetilde G_S$ is perfect.
\end{enumerate}

The most interesting case would be that in which $\widetilde G_S$ is the universal central extension of $G_S$ by a discrete group, the ``universal covering'' of $G_S$, but we do not want to assume the existence of such a universal extension.

A topological central extension $E$ of $G(\mathbf A)$ by a discrete group $X$ is determined by the extensions $E_S$ and $E_{S'}$ of $G_S$ and $G_{S'}$ by $X$ obtained by inverse image in $E$. Since $G_S$ is perfect, one has
\[
E\simeq(E_S\times E_{S'})/\{(x,-x)\mid x\in X\}.
\]
If the extension $E_S$ is dominated by $\widetilde G_S$, that is, if it is obtained from $\widetilde G_S$ by pushout through a map $f:A\to X$, then $f$ is unique, and giving $E$ is the same as giving $E_{S'}$ together with $f$; this is an equivalence of categories. In this case
\[
E\simeq \widetilde G_S\times E_{S'}/\{(a,-f(a))\mid a\in A\}.
\]

Let $\mathscr L$ be the category of topological central extensions of $G(\mathbf A)$ by a finite group, split above $G(F)$, and such that $E_S$ is dominated by $\widetilde G_S$:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small, row sep=small]
1 \arrow[r] & X \arrow[r] \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow] & E \arrow[r] \arrow[d] & G(\mathbf A) \arrow[r] & 1 \\
& & G(F) & &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
It would amount to the same to require only that the extension be splittable over $G(F)$, since $G(F)$ is perfect and a splitting, if it exists, is unique. The category $\mathscr L$ is cofiltered. For $(E,s)$ in $\mathscr L$, the closure in $E$ of
\[
(\text{image of }\widetilde G_S)\cdot sG(F)
\]
maps to $G(\mathbf A)$; it maps to a closed subset, because $X$ is finite, and it contains $G_SG(F)$, which is dense by strong approximation. Thus the full subcategory $L$ of $\mathscr L$ consisting of those $(E,s)$ for which $(\text{image of }\widetilde G_S)\cdot sG(F)$ is dense in $E$ is cofinal. Since there is at most one arrow between two objects of $L$, it is just a filtered ordered set.

\begin{proposition}
Under hypotheses (ii) and (iii), there is a natural isomorphism between $\widetilde C(S,G)$ and the inverse limit, over $\mathscr L$ or over $L$, of the groups $X$. Via this isomorphism, the natural map from $A$ to $\widetilde C(S,G)$ is the inverse limit of the maps $-f:A\to X$, where $f$ is such that $E_S$ is obtained from $\widetilde G_S$ by pushout through $f$.
\end{proposition}

Let $E$ be a topological central extension of $G(\mathbf A)$ by a discrete group $X$. To give a splitting $s$ of $E$ above $G(F)$ is to give an isomorphism between the extension of $G(F)$ by $X$ obtained from $E_S$, and the opposite of the extension obtained from $E_{S'}$. If $E_S$ is dominated by $\widetilde G_S$ and $f$ is as above, this is the same as giving a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
1 \arrow[r] & X \arrow[r] & E_{S'} \arrow[r] & G_{S'} \arrow[r] & 1 \\
1 \arrow[r] & A \arrow[u, "-f"] \arrow[r] & \widetilde{G(F)} \arrow[u, "i"] \arrow[r] & G(F) \arrow[u] \arrow[r] & 1.
\end{tikzcd}
\]

It follows that $\mathscr L$ is equivalent to the category $\mathscr L'$ of commutative diagrams
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small]
& 1 \arrow[r] & X \arrow[r] & E_{S'} \arrow[r,"q"] & G_{S'} \arrow[r] & 1 \\
\text{(1)} & & & \widetilde{G(F)} \arrow[u,"i"] \arrow[r] & G(F) \arrow[u] &
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where $E_{S'}$ is a topological central extension of $G_{S'}$ by a finite group. One reconstructs $E$ as
\[
\widetilde G_S\times E_{S'}/\{(a,t(a))\mid a\in A\},
\]
and the splitting over $G(F)$ by
\[
s(g)=(\tilde g,\widetilde{t\tilde g})\bmod (a,t(a)),
\]
for $\tilde g\in\widetilde{G(F)}\subset\widetilde G_S$ mapping to $g\in G(F)$. The subcategory $L$ corresponds to the subcategory $L'$ of diagrams (1) in which $t$ has dense image.

Let $(E_{S'},t)$ be in $L'$, and let $\mathscr T$ be the topology on $\widetilde{G(F)}$ induced by that of $E_{S'}$. If $K$ is a compact open subgroup of $E_{S'}$ disjoint from $X$, then the intersections of $\Delta=t^{-1}K$ with the $S$-congruence subgroups form a fundamental system of neighborhoods for $\mathscr T$. For $\Gamma$ an $S$-congruence subgroup defined by a compact open subgroup $K_1\subset qK$, one moreover has
\begin{equation}\tag{2}
X\simeq XK/K\simeq q^{-1}K_1/(K\cap q^{-1}K_1)\simeq \Gamma/(\Delta\cap\Gamma)\simeq C_\Delta.
\end{equation}
Since $X$ is finite, $\Delta$ is $S$-arithmetic. Conversely, Proposition 1 ensures that a fundamental system of $S$-arithmetic groups is obtained in this way. The filtered ordered set $L'$ is identified with the set of germs, for the topology of $S$-congruence subgroups, of $S$-arithmetic groups, and the isomorphism of Proposition 2 is defined as the inverse limit of the isomorphisms (2).

\subsection*{3. Quasi-split groups}

Let $\mu$ be the group of roots of unity of $F$. For each non-complex place $v$ of $F$, let $\mu_v$ be the group of roots of unity in $F_v$; for a complex $v$, put $\mu_v=\{1\}$. Deodhar proved that, for $G$ quasi-split, there exists a universal topological central extension $\widetilde G_v$ of $G_v$ by a discrete group, the ``universal covering'', and that for $v$ not real its kernel $\pi_1(G_v)$ is canonically a quotient of $\mu_v$. I have completed this result by showing that in fact $\pi_1(G_v)=\mu_v$. For $v$ real, one has $\pi_1(G_v)=\mathbf Z$ or $\mathbf Z/2$. In all cases, $\mu_v$ occurs as a quotient of $\pi_1(G_v)$.

For almost all $v$, the extension $\widetilde G_v$ splits over the natural maximal compact subgroup of $G_v$. This makes it possible to define the restricted product of the $\widetilde G_v$, an extension of $G(\mathbf A)$ by the direct sum of the $\pi_1(G_v)$. Push it out by
\[
R:\bigoplus\pi_1(G_v)\to\bigoplus\mu_v\hookrightarrow\mu,
\qquad
\sigma((x_v))=\prod_v x_v^{[\mu_v:\mu]}.
\]
One obtains a central extension of $G(\mathbf A)$ by $\mu$, and it follows from Deodhar ($^2$) and C. Moore ($^3$) that this is the universal topological central extension of $G(\mathbf A)$ by a discrete group which splits over $G(F)$:
\begin{equation}\tag{3}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small, row sep=small]
1 \arrow[r] & \mu \arrow[r] & \widetilde{G(\mathbf A)} \arrow[r] \arrow[dr, dashrightarrow] & G(\mathbf A) \arrow[r] & 1 \\
& & & G(F) \arrow[u] &
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}

Take $\widetilde G_S$ to be the universal covering of $G_S$, and write $R_S$ for the restriction of $R$ to
\[
\pi_1G_S=\prod_{v\in S}\pi_1G_v.
\]
Proposition 2 identifies the completion $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a)$ of $\widetilde{G(F)}$ with the inverse image $\widetilde{G(\mathbf A)}_S$ of $G_S=\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(c)$ in (3), and gives an exact commutative diagram
\begin{equation}\tag{4}
\begin{tikzcd}
1 \arrow[r] & \mu \arrow[r] & \widetilde{G(\mathbf A)}_S \arrow[r] & G_S \arrow[r] & 1 \\
& \prod_{v\in S}\pi_1(G_v) \arrow[u,"-R_S"] \arrow[r] & \widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a) \arrow[u,"i"] \arrow[r] & G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(a) \arrow[u] \arrow[r] & 1.
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}

In particular:
\begin{theorem}
Let $G/F$ be quasi-split, and let $\widetilde{G(F)}$ be the inverse image of $G(F)$ in the universal covering of $G_S$. If
\[
\sum_{v\in S}\rank(G_v)\ge2,
\]
then the intersection of the $S$-arithmetic subgroups of $\widetilde{G(F)}$ is the kernel of
\[
R_S:\prod_{v\in S}\pi_1(G_v)\to\mu.
\]
\end{theorem}

For $\Sp(2n,\mathbf Q)$ and $S=\infty$, this is the result announced in the introduction. For $\SL(n,K)$, where $K$ is real quadratic and $S=\infty$, the result obtained generalizes that of J. Millson ($^4$).

\begin{problem}
Does every discrete subgroup of finite covolume in the universal covering of $\Sp(2n,\mathbf R)$, with $n\ge2$, contain $2\pi_1\Sp(2n,\mathbf R)$? ($^5$).
\end{problem}

\subsection*{4. The factor system of theta functions}

When condition (i) fails, Proposition 1 is no longer available. It is nevertheless still possible to study those $S$-arithmetic subgroups satisfying a condition of type (1a).

Let $S$ be a nonempty finite set of places, containing all archimedean places, and let $T\supset S$ be such that $(G,T)$ satisfies condition (ii). The interesting case is that in which $S$ consists of a single place at which $G$ has rank $1$. Let $\widetilde G_S$ again be a topological central extension of $G_S$ by a discrete group, and let $\widetilde{G(F)}$ be the inverse image of $G(F)$ in $\widetilde G_S$. For an $S$-arithmetic subgroup $\Delta$ of $\widetilde{G(F)}$, consider the condition:
\begin{quote}
($\star$) there exists a $T$-arithmetic subgroup $U$ of $\widetilde{G(F)}$ such that, for every conjugate $\Delta^g$ of $\Delta$ with $g\in U$, there exists an $S$-congruence subgroup $\Gamma$ with
\[
\Gamma\cap\Delta=\Gamma\cap\Delta^g
\]
($U$-invariance of the germ of $\Delta$).
\end{quote}

\begin{proposition}
The completion $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a^*)$ of $\widetilde{G(F)}$ for the topology of $S$-arithmetic subgroups satisfying ($\star$) is a central extension of $\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(c)=G_S$.
\end{proposition}

More concretely, we have to verify that if $(U,\Delta)$ satisfies ($\star$), then $\Delta$ satisfies (1c). To prove this, we may first shrink $U$ and $\Delta$. In particular, we may suppose $\Delta\subset U$ by replacing $\Delta$ with $\Delta\cap U$. Then $U$ acts by conjugation on the finite group
\[
C^U_\Delta=\varprojlim (\Gamma\cap U)/(\Gamma\cap\Delta)
\]
where $\Gamma$ runs over the $S$-congruence subgroups. Replacing $U$ by the kernel of this action, and $\Delta$ by $\Delta\cap\Gamma$, we may also suppose that $U$ acts trivially on $C^U_\Delta$.

Since $U$ is $T$-arithmetic, its completion $U(c)\subset G_T$ is of finite index in a subgroup $G_{T-S}\times L$, where $L\subset G_T$ is compact open. Since $G_{T-S}$ has no open subgroup of finite index, $U(c)$ itself is of the form $G_{T-S}\times L$. Condition ($\star$) implies that the $\Delta\cap\Gamma$, with $\Gamma$ $S$-congruence, form a fundamental system of neighborhoods of the identity for a group topology on $U$. The corresponding completion $U(\Delta)$ is an extension of $U(c)$ by $C^U_\Delta$, hence a central extension of $G_{T-S}\times L$. Replace $L$ by an open subgroup $L_1$ over which this extension is trivial, and replace $U$ and $\Delta$ by the inverse images of $L_1$. Since $G_{T-S}$ is perfect, a central extension of $G_{T-S}\times L$ is determined by its restrictions to the two factors. This reduces us to the case in which the central extension $U(\Delta)$ of $G_{T-S}\times L$ is the inverse image of a central extension $\widetilde G_{T-S}$ of $G_{T-S}$ by $C^U_\Delta$. By construction, $U$ lifts into $\widetilde G_{T-S}$ and $\Delta$ is the inverse image of a compact open subgroup $K$ of $\widetilde G_{T-S}\times L$:
\begin{equation}\tag{5}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=small, row sep=small]
& K \arrow[r, hook] & \widetilde G_{T-S}\times L \\
& \Delta \arrow[u] \arrow[r] & U \arrow[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}

Let $\widetilde{G(F)}_T$ be the central extension of $G(F)$ obtained as the inverse image of $G(F)$ in
\[
\widetilde G_T=\widetilde G_S\times\widetilde G_{T-S},
\]
and let $\widetilde U$ be the lifting (5) of $U$ into $\widetilde{G(F)}_T$. For $g\in G(F)$, Proposition 1 gives $(g,\Gamma_T)\subset\widetilde U$ for a $T$-congruence subgroup $\Gamma_T$ defined by a sufficiently small $L'\subset L$. If the $S$-congruence subgroup $\Gamma$ defined by $K'\subset G_{T-S}\times L'$, with inverse image $K''$ in $\widetilde G_{T-S}\times L'$, is small enough that $(g,K')\subset K$, then $(g,\Gamma)\subset\Delta$, which proves (1c).

The kernel
\[
\widetilde C^{\sim *}(S,G)=\Ker(\widetilde{G(F)}\hat{\mkern6mu}(a^*)\to G(F)\hat{\mkern6mu}(c))
\]
is still described by Proposition 2, with the same proof, and the calculation can be carried out in the quasi-split case, as in no. 3. Here is an application, with $G=\SL(2,\mathbf Q)$, $S=\{\infty\}$, and $T=\{\infty,p\}$. Let $\widetilde{\SL(2,\mathbf R)}$ be the double cover of $\SL(2,\mathbf R)$. The factor systems of theta functions, restricted to arbitrarily small congruence subgroups of $\SL(2,\mathbf Z)$, may be interpreted as defining a germ $\tau$, for the congruence subgroup topology, of liftings of $\SL(2,\mathbf Z)$ into $\widetilde{\SL(2,\mathbf R)}$. This germ is invariant under $\GL(2,\mathbf Q)$. If one defines a modular form of half-integral weight $k/2$ to be a function on the Poincare upper half-plane which, under the action of a congruence subgroup of $\SL(2,\mathbf Z)$, transforms like the theta function of a quadratic form in $k$ variables, this amounts to saying that if
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\ c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\GL(2,\mathbf Q)
\]
has positive determinant and $f$ is modular of weight $k/2$, then $f(-z^{-})$ and
\[
(cz+d)^{k/2}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)
\]
are also modular. Conversely, one has:

\begin{proposition}
For every prime $p$, the germ $\tau$ is the unique germ of a lifting of $\SL(2,\mathbf Z)$ into $\widetilde{\SL(2,\mathbf R)}$ which is invariant under a subgroup of finite index of $\SL(2,\mathbf Z[1/p])$.
\end{proposition}

The arguments leading to the theorem of no. 3 show that every arithmetic subgroup of $\SL(2,\mathbf Z)$ whose germ is invariant under a subgroup of finite index of $\SL(2,\mathbf Z[1/p])$ is congruence.

Let $\Gamma\subset\SL(2,\mathbf Z)$ be a congruence subgroup, and let $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ be two liftings of $\Gamma$ into $\widetilde{\SL(2,\mathbf R)}$. Let $\Delta$ be the subgroup of $\Gamma$ on which $\tau_1=\tau_2$. If the germs of $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$ are distinct and invariant under a $\{p\}$-arithmetic group, then $\Delta$ satisfies ($\star$) and is not a congruence subgroup.
