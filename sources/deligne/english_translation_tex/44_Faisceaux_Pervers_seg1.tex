\section*{INTRODUCTION}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction (to the volume)}

This colloque on the Singuliers (rencontre organisee to the CIRM of the 6 to the 10 juillet 1981, in the cadre of the activites of the G.E.E.O.) a eu for origine the correspondence of Riemann--Hilbert and the fait that, in the cas of the singularities, the correspondence met en rapport of the $\cD$-modules holonomes reguliers with of the perverse sheaves, and that these derniers are lies a the cohomology of intersection. In [1], [2], the auteurs definissent the cohomology of intersection of pseudovarietes PL orientees of dimension paire, and in particular of the algebraic varieties complexes. For a projective variety complex, these groups satisfy the duality of Poincare and, for the varieties normales, one can the interpreter as of the groups of cohomology $L^{2}$ (of after Cheeger, see the conference of Zucker). The point of vue adopte in these articles, which a also guide M.~Goresky and R.~MacPherson in leur etude of the cohomology of intersection, is celui of the duality and of the theorie of Morse. A other origine se trouve in the theorie of the representations of Weyl of the algebraic groups semi-simples complexes. In the cas of the groups finite, the theorie a ete construite by I.~Bernstein, I.~Gelfand and S.~Gelfand a the aide of categories of modules, and Kazhdan and Lusztig y have decouvert the relation between multiplicites and polynomes caracteristiques of the groups of cohomology of intersection of varieties of Schubert. Bernstein, Beilinson and Deligne have remark that the one can refaire this theorie in such a way purement topologique en considerant the perverse sheaves (``perverse sheaves''). the idee of considerer these sheaves a also ete trouvee by Kashiwara.

L.~Illusie a shows in [2] comment, en caracteristique $p$, the theorie of the perverse sheaves permet of obtenir a Lefschetz theorem difficult relative, which inclut the theorem of decomposition for the complexes purs. Gabber a shows a theorem of purete which entraine that the perverse sheaves simples are purs, and a weight filtration on the perverse sheaves mixtes, which permet of deduire the theorem of decomposition for the complexes mixtes. These results of Gabber are contenus in son article [1] on the purete of the cohomology of intersection, or it given also the calcul of the dimension of the support of the sheaf of homology of intersection of a under-scheme closed.

\markboth{INTRODUCTION}{INTRODUCTION}

Il avait été d'abord prévu que O.~Gabber soit coauteur du présent article. Il a préféré s'en abstenir, pour ne pas être coresponsable des erreurs ou imprécisions qui s'y trouvent. Il n'en est pas moins responsable de bien des idées que nous exploitons et le lecteur lui est redevable de nombreuses critiques qui, nous l'espérons, ont permis d'améliorer le manuscrit.

\medskip

Dans [4], [5], [6], M.~Goresky et R.~MacPherson ont défini et étudié la cohomologie d'intersection d'espaces convenables $X$. Dans [4], [5], $X$ est une pseudo-variété PL orientée, i.e. pour $X$ de dimension $n$, un espace PL admettant un sous-espace PL fermé rare $S$ de dimension $\leq n{-}2$ dont le complément est une variété PL orientée purement de dimension $n$. L'espace $X$ est muni d'une \emph{stratification} $S$, i.e. d'une filtration par des sous-espaces PL fermés
\[
\emptyset = X_{-1} \subset X_{0} \subset \cdots \subset X_{n-1} = S \subset X_{n} = X\;,
\]
$X_{i}$ of dimension reelle $\leq i$ (therefore $X_{i} = X_{i-1}$ for $i$ impair). A variety parente of dimension $i$ (resp. $i{-}1$) on $X$ is by definition a under-variety PL of dimension $i$ of which the bord is contenu in $X_{i-1}$. For each \emph{perversite} $p$ (a sequence $p_{2}, \dotsc, p_{n}$ of integers, such that $p_{2} = 0$ and that $p_{k+1} = p_{k}$ or $p_{k} {+} 1$), they definissent the $i$\textsuperscript{e} group $\IH^{i}_{p}(X)$ of \emph{homology of intersection of perversite} $p$ of $X$ as the group of the cycles singuliers PL of dimension $i$ of $X$ which intersectent $X_{n-k}$ selon a part of dimension $\leq i {-} k {+} p_{k}$ to the more, modulo a relation of homology suitable. This group is independant of $S$. For $p$ maximal (resp. of homology $h(X)$), in [6], the conditions on $X$ are affaiblies, and they prouvent the invariance topologique of the groups $\IH^{i}_{p}(X)$.

\medskip

The cas which we interesse the more is celui or the stratification $S$ can be prise a strates all of dimension paire, and or $p$ is the perversite \emph{intermediaire}, datum with the notations ci-dessus by $p_{2k} = k{-}1$ (and $p_{2k+1} = k-1$, or $k$, indifferemment). We shall denote $\IH^{i}(X)$ the groups with $p$ intermediaire. They satisfy the duality of Poincare ([4] 3). After extension of scalars of $\QQ$ a $\RR$, one can the interpreter as of the groups of cohomology $L^{2}$ (see the expose of J.~Cheeger a this conference).

\medskip

For $X(\CC)$ the space topologique (topologie usuelle) under-jacent a algebraic variety complex normal connected, of dimension complex $n$, one can prendre $S$ a strates algebraic, therefore automatiquement of dimension reelle paire. The groups of homology of intersection, for the perversite intermediaire, are therefore defined. For $X$ only assumed irreducible, one normalise $\tilde{X} \to X$, and one sets $\IH^{i}_{\cdot}(X(\CC)) := \IH^{i}_{\cdot}(\tilde{X}(\CC))$.

\medskip

Tensorises with $\CC$, these groups admettent a description en termes of modules holonomes. For the donner, it we will be commode of travailler with the modules holonomes algebraic (modules quasi-coherents a connexion integrable satisfaisant a condition of finitude suitable). Supposons $X$ plonge in a algebraic variety $Z$ smooth purement of dimension complex $d$. One assumed $Z$ closed in $Z$. Let $\bar{X}$ the lieu smooth of $X$ and $j$ the morphism of inclusion of $\bar{X}$ in $X$. A isomorphism unique pres, there exists then on $\bar{X}$ a unique module holonome irreducible a support $\bar{X}$, a note $j_{!*}\cO_{\bar{X}}$, which on $\bar{X}$ coincide with the module holonome $\cO_{\bar{X}}$.

\medskip

To the membre of line and to the choix of the hypercohomologie of $X$, one defines, en adoptant a variante of the conventions of [3],
\[
\IH^{i}(X(\CC)) \otimes \CC = \HH^{d+i}(X, j_{!*}\cO_{\bar{X}})\;.
\]


To the membre of line and to the choix of the hypercohomologie of $X$, one defines, en adoptant a variante of the conventions of [3],
\[
\IH^{i}(X(\CC)) \otimes \CC = \HH^{d+i}(X, j_{!*}\cO_{\bar{X}})\;.
\]

The groups $\IH^{i}(X(\CC)) \otimes \CC$, tensorises with $\overline{\QQ}_{\ell}$, admettent a description analogue as groups of hypercohomologie. One shows that for $X$ a algebraic variety irreducible of dimension $n$, there exists in the derived category $D(X(\CC), \QQ)$, a sheaf of cohomology $\underline{\HH}^{i}(\IC)$ constructibles and nuls for $i < -n$ (i.e. $i < -\pdim X$), verifiant the conditions suivantes:

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{(a)} There exists a open of Zariski dense $U \subset X$ such that on $U(\CC)$, $\IC$ let isomorphic to the complex reduit to the sheaf constant $\QQ$, place en degree $-2n$.

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(b)} For $i > -2n$, $\pdim \Supp \underline{\HH}^{i}(\IC) \leq i - 2n - 2$.

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(c)} For $i < -2n$, the codimension of the support of $\underline{\HH}^{i}(\IC)$ is $\geq -i - 2n - 2$.

\medskip

It we will be commode of numeroter autrement the groups $\IH^{i}_{\cdot}(X(\CC))$. In what follows of this introduction, we poserons
\[
\IH^{i}(X(\CC)) := \IH^{i}_{n-i}(X(\CC))
\]
and $\IC^{*} := \IC[-n]$, of sorte that
\[
\IH^{i}(X(\CC)) = \HH^{i}(X(\CC), \IC^{*})\;.
\]

The groups $\IH^{i}(X(\CC))$ (resp. $\IH^{i}_{c}(X(\CC))$) ne are not nuls that for $i \in [0, 2n]$ (resp. $i \in [-2n, 0]$). The conditions (a), (b), (c) se recrivent: every under-variety irreducible proper $\Sigma$ of $X$, of dimension $d$, admet a open of Zariski dense $V(\Sigma)$ such that:

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{(a')} for $i \geq -d$, $\underline{\HH}^{i}(\IC^{*})|V(\Sigma) = 0$;

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(b')} for $i \leq -d$, $\underline{\HH}^{i}_{V(\Sigma)}(\IC^{*}) = 0$.

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{0.1.} Let $X$ a algebraic variety complex. We definissons a \emph{perverse sheaf} on $X$ as a object $K$ of the derived category $D(X(\CC), \QQ)$, a sheaves of cohomology $\underline{\HH}^{i}K$ constructibles and nuls for $|i|$ sufficiently grand (i.e., $K \in D^{b}_{c}(X(\CC), \QQ)$), verifiant the conditions suivantes: every under-variety irreducible $\Sigma$ of $X$, of dimension $d$, admet a open of Zariski dense $V$ such that

\medskip

\noindent
\textbf{(a'')} for $i > -d$, $\underline{\HH}^{i}(K)|V = 0$;

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(b'')} for $i < -d$, $\underline{\HH}^{i}_{V}(K) = 0$.

\medskip

Noter that, for $\Sigma$ irreducible, one n'exclut not $\Sigma = X$. For $X$ irreducible, $\IC^{*}$ is a perverse sheaf.

The condition (a'') equivaut a
\[
\pdim \Supp \underline{\HH}^{i}K \leq -i
\]
(of or $\underline{\HH}^{i}K = 0$ for $i > 0$). A condition of this type had ete introduite by M.~Artin in sa proof of the theorem of dimension cohomologique of the varieties affine (SGA~4~XIV~1~a~4).

\medskip

Of the conditions of the type (c') are familieres en theorie of the sheaves algebraic coherents, under the nom of conditions of profondeur.

\medskip

The perverse sheaves n'being ni of the sheaves, ni pervers, the terminologie requiert a explication. The mot ``pervers'' n'enchante not certain of we. It vient of ``perversite'', in $\IH^{i}(X)$, the ``perversite'' $p$ indique of combien one permet to the cycles of devier of the transversalite a stratification. Pourquoi ``sheaves'' ? Si one travaille with of the coefficients complexes (plutot that rationnels: remplacer $D^{b}_{c}(X(\CC), \QQ)$ by $D^{b}_{c}(X(\CC), \CC)$), and that $X$ is smooth purement of dimension $n$, the functor $K \mapsto \cO^{*}K[n]$ (de Rham complex analytic) is a equivalence of the category of the modules holonomes (algebraic) a singularities regulieres (y compris a the infini) on $X$ with celle of the perverse sheaves on $X$. For $X$ quelconque, plonge in $Z$ smooth purement of dimension $d$, and $X$ a perverse sheaf on $X$, one can considerer the modules holonomes on $Z$ a support in $X$, and the functor $K \mapsto \Omega^{*}(K)[d]$ ($\Omega^{*}(K)$ = de Rham complex). The modules holonomes on $X$ smooth are of the sheaves (to the sens of $\cO_{X}$-modules), and leurs properties suggerent certain of celles of the perverse sheaves. By example the suivantes (with $\cO$ a new $\QQ$ as field of coefficients):

\section{Under-categories abeliennes of a triangulated category}

\subsection{Categories triangulees}

For the definition and the properties fondamentales of the categories triangulees, we renvoyons a J.-L.~Verdier [10]. Notre but in this numero is of expliquer the axiomes of Verdier -- specialement celui of the octaedre -- and of donner some complements.

\begin{definition}[1.1.1]
A \emph{triangulated category} is an additive category $\cD$, equipped with a \emph{functor of translation} $X \longmapsto X[1]$ and of a set of ``triangles'' $X \to Y \to Z \to X[1]$, the \emph{triangles distingues}. One exige that the functor of translation Let auto-equivalence of categories and that the set of the triangles distingues satisfies the axiomes TR~1 a TR~4 of [10] p.~4.
\end{definition}

We shall denote $(1)$ the functor $X \mapsto X[1]$ ($n$\textsuperscript{eme} itere $X[n]$). In the diagrams, a fleche of degree $n$ will be affublee of $(n)$. The triangles distingues will be souvent notes simplement $X \to Y \to Z \xrightarrow{(1)}$ (or simplement $(X, Y, Z)$) and one omettra of marquer $(1)$ the fleche of degree 1, si that ne cree not of ambiguite. One sets $\Hom^{n}(X, Y) := \Hom(X, Y[n])$.

The category opposee $\cD^{\mathrm{opp}}$ of $\cD$ will be equipped of the functor of translation $X \mapsto X[-1]$. A fleche of degree $n : X \to Y$ fournit therefore in $\cD^{\mathrm{opp}}$ a fleche of the same degree of $Y$ in $X$. The triangles distingues of $\cD^{\mathrm{opp}}$ are the triangles $(Z, Y, X)$, for $(X, Y, Z)$ distingue in $\cD$. Equipped with these triangles, $\cD^{\mathrm{opp}}$ is triangulee. This permettra of raisonner by duality.

In a triangulated category, all morphism $f : X \to Y$ is the base of a triangle distingue $(X, Y, Z)$, unique a isomorphism (not generalement unique) pres (see 1.1.10 for a cas of unicite). One appelle $Z$ the \emph{cone} of $f$ (or, more correctement, a cone of $f$). A triangle $(X, Y, Z)$ is distingue if and only if $Y \to Z \to X[1] \xrightarrow{-f[1]} Y[1]$ the is. The triangles distingues can tourner (TR~2: si $(X, Y, Z)$ is distingue, $(Y, Z, X[1])$ and $(Z[-1], X, Y)$ the are).

\subsection{t-categories}

\begin{definition}[1.3.1]
A \emph{t-category} is a triangulated category $\cD$, equipped with deux under-categories pleines $\cD^{\leq 0}$ and $\cD^{\geq 0}$, such that, posant $\cD^{\leq n} := \cD^{\leq 0}[-n]$ and $\cD^{\geq n} := \cD^{\geq 0}[-n]$, one ait:

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(i)} For $X$ in $\cD^{\leq 0}$ and $Y$ in $\cD^{\geq 1}$, one has $\Hom(X, Y) = 0$.

\noindent
\textbf{(ii)} one has $\cD^{\leq 0} \subset \cD^{\leq 1}$ and $\cD^{\geq 0} \supset \cD^{\geq 1}$.

\noindent
\textbf{(iii)} For every $X$ in $\cD$, there exists a triangle distingue $(A, X, B)$ with $A$ in $\cD^{\leq 0}$ and $B$ in $\cD^{\geq 1}$.
\end{definition}

One will say also that $(\cD^{\leq 0}, \cD^{\geq 0})$ is a \emph{t-structure} on $\cD$. Son \emph{coeur} is the full subcategory $\cC := \cD^{\leq 0} \cap \cD^{\geq 0}$.
