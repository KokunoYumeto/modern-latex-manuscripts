The premiere of the ``conjectures standard'' (Grothendieck [19], Kleiman [20]),
celle of type Lefschetz, demande that certain cohomology classes let
``directs'' of cycles algebraic. Of every facon, si the motives are the ``direct summands'' of cohomology of algebraic varieties $X$ defined by of the projectors, cycles algebraic on
$X \times X$, leur definition n'is raisonnable that si one dispose of sufficiently of cycles
algebraic. On this probleme: the construction of cycles algebraic
interessants, the progres have ete maigres.

Grothendieck a tente of dresser a catalogue of constructions projective of
cycles (contours apparents), cf.~[19, p.~197]. For ceux of which one has calcule the
cohomology class, this class s'exprime en termes of classes of Chern of
fibers vector evidents. Bien that je ne connaisse not of against-example, it me
semble peu probable that the ring of the classes of cycles on $X \times X$ generated
by the diagonale, the diviseurs and the images inverses of the classes of Chern of $X$
by the $\pr_i$ ($i = 1, 2$) contienne toujours the cycles requis by the premiere
conjecture standard, by example the composantes of Kunneth of the diagonale.

Plutot that of chercher a construct of the cycles, one can chercher a construct
of the fibers vector, for ensuite prendre leurs classes of Chern. C'is ainsi
en fait that K.~Kodaira and D.~C.~Spencer (1953) prouvent the conjecture of
Hodge for the diviseurs (a Lefschetz theorem), the group $\Pic(X) =
H^1(X, \mathcal{O}^*)$ being accessible, on $\C$, by the exact sequence exponentielle
$$
0 \to \Z \to \mathcal{O} \xrightarrow{\exp} \mathcal{O}^* \to 0.
$$
Similarly, on a abelian variety, it is more easy of ecrire the functional equation of the functions $\theta$ (a cocycle for a fiber en lines) that of define a function $\theta$, it-same fournissant the diviseur $\theta = 0$.

Malheureusement, en rank superieur, one ne sait not more construct of the fibers vector of which the classes of Chern let interessantes that one ne sait construct of the cycles interessants.

Si $\ell \in \C$, the conjecture of Hodge fournirait the cycles voulus. En direction of this conjecture, deux difficultes have ete decouvertes. The premiere: Atiyah and Hirzebruch have shows that it ne pouvait be vraie that rationnellement (cf.~Atiyah--Hirzebruch [1]).

The seconde: of the point of vue of the theorie of Hodge, the class of a cycle $Z$ of codimension $d$ on $X$ projective and smooth vit naturally not in $H^{d,d} := H^{d,d} \cap H^{2d}$, but in a extension $E^d$ of this group by the jacobienne intermediaire
$$
J^d(X) := H^{2d-1}(X, \C) / F^d + H^{2d-1}(X, \Z),
$$
or $F^d$ designe the $d$-ieme terme of the Hodge filtration of $H^{2d}(X, \C)$.

In the category of the mixed Hodge structures, it s'agit respectivement of
$\Hom(\Z(d), H^{2d}(X))$ and of $\Ext^1(\Z(d), H^{2d-1}(X))$. The conjecture of Hodge demande that the group of the classes of cycles s'envoie on a under-group of indice finite of the quotient $H^{d,d}$. By against, one n'a aucune idee of this that devrait be the image in $E^d$. Outre the inconfort esthetique that cause this ignorance, it rend generalement inapplicable the methode of P.~A.~Griffiths for prouver the conjecture of Hodge by recurrence on the dimension of the variety $X$. This methode is inspiree of celle that Lefschetz utilise for the surfaces, Lefschetz [24]. the idee is that, si $(H_t)_{t \in P^1}$ is a pinceau of sections hyperplanes, construct the cycle $Z$ on $X$ revient a construct the cycles $Z_t = Z \cap H_t$. In of the bons cas (a) the cohomology class $c \in H^{2d}(X)$ that one veut be celle of a cycle $Z$ a restriction zero on the $H_t$, (b) si $Z$ exists, sa cohomology class $c$ determine the class of the $Z_t$ in the jacobiennes intermediaires $J^d(H_t)$, and (c) the construction of $Z$ revient a celle of cycles $Z_t$ of classes definissant a section datum of the family of the $J^d(H_t)$. One can then conclure si all element of $J^d(H_t)$ is the class of a cycle of codimension $d$ cohomologue a zero. By this methode, Zucker [32] proves the conjecture of Hodge for the hypersurfaces cubiques in $\mathbf{P}^5$. Noter toutefois that, si all element of $J^d(H_t)$ is the class of a cycle cohomologue a zero, then $H^{2d-1}(H_t)$ is of type of Hodge $\{(d, d-1), (d-1, d)\}$ (see \S1.6), the converse being a consequence of the conjecture of Hodge appliquee to the product of $H_t$ by a abelian variety suitable. Moreover, this surjectivite implique the existence of a curve $C_t$ and of a cycle algebraic $W_t$ on $H_t \times C_t$ envoyant $H^1(C_t)$ on $H^{2d-1}(H_t)$. One can then s'attendre a this that $H^{2d}(X)$ let controle by the $H^2$ of the surface fibree on $\mathbf{P}^1$ of fibers the $C_t$, and for a $H^2$ the conjecture of Hodge is of every facon disponible.

\section{Motives}

Selon this that one can and veut faire, one dispose of plusieurs definitions of the
motives --- or of aucune. There is lieu of distinguer the motives purs, typiquement
four-nis by the cohomology of projective varieties not singulieres, and the motives
mixtes, or of the varieties ouvertes or singulieres are permises. The notion of
motive on $S$ (family of motives parametree by $S$) pose of other problemes again.

For $k$ a field, one voudrait in all the cas that the category of the motives on $k$ Let abelian category $\Q$-lineaire $\mathcal{M}(k)$ with of the groups
$\Hom$ of dimension finite. In the cas pur, one voudrait semisimple and
graduee (by the weight). In the cas mixte, each motive $M$ devrait admettre
a filtration finite croissante $W$ with $\Gr_n^W(M)$ pur of weight $n$. Other
structures essentielles:

\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] Each algebraic variety $X$ on $k$ must avoir of the groups of cohomology motiviques $H^i_{\mot}(X)$, objects of $\mathcal{M}(k)$. In a cas pur one se limite to the varieties $X$ projective not singulieres, and $H^i_{\mot}(X)$ is a motive pur of weight $i$.

\item[(b)] For chacune of the theories of cohomology usuelles $H$, one must disposer of a functor ``realisation'' $\real_H$ and of isomorphisms $H^*(X) \xrightarrow{\sim} \real_H H^*_{\mot}(X)$.

\item[(c)] A tensor product $\otimes$, auquel the functors of realisation are compatible.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tannakian structure}

The structure (c) permet of appliquer the theorie of the Tannakian categories,
inventee by Grothendieck for etudier the formalism of the motives.

\textit{References:} Saavedra [28], Deligne--Milne [12], Deligne [15].

For $k$ of caracteristique $0$, this theorie dit that the category of the motives on $k$, with son tensor product, must be equivalent a celle of the representations lineaires of a scheme en groups affine $G$ on $\Q$. Noter that deux groups $G_1$ and $G_2$ forms interieures the a of the other have the same categories of representations. The Galois group motivic $G$ n'is therefore not uniquement determine by $\mathcal{M}(k)$. The theorie indique that the choix of $G$ and of a equivalence $\omega: \Rep(G) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{M}(k)$ equivaut a celui of a exact functor compatible to the tensor product of $\mathcal{M}(k)$ in the $\Q$-vector spaces: a functor fiber $\omega$. A this functor fiber correspond $G := \underline{\Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega)$, the scheme en groups of the $\otimes$-automorphismes of $\omega$. One can see $\omega$, or plutot $(\omega, G)$ as a ``theorie of cohomology with values in the $\Q$-vector spaces''.

For $k$ equipped with a embedding in $\C$, a theorie of cohomology possible is: ``cohomology singular of the space topologique of the points complexes''. For $k = \Q$, a other choix possible is: ``de Rham cohomology''.

En caracteristique $p$, a example of Serre shows that it ne can not y avoir of theorie of cohomology raisonnable with values in the $\Q$-vector spaces: si $E$ is a curve elliptique supersinguliere, the algebra $\End(E) \otimes \Q$ is a algebra of quaternions, therefore n'a not of representation lineaire of dimension deux on $\Q$. Of the existence of a theorie of cohomology with values in the vector spaces on a extension of $\Q$ resulte that $\mathcal{M}(k)$ must toutefois be the category of representations of a gerbe suitable.

The theorie tannakienne is a analogue lineaire of a theorie ensembliste, celle of the $\pi_1$ profini, egalement due a Grothendieck (SGA1). Analogie: si $\mathcal{T}$ is the category of the coverings etale finite of a scheme connected $S$ (resp. a Tannakian category on $K$) and $\omega$ a functor fiber, with values in the sets finite (resp. in the $K$-vector spaces of dimension finite), $\mathcal{T}$ is naturally equivalent a the category of the $G$-sets finite (resp. of the representations of $G$), for $G$ the group profini $\Aut(\omega)$ (resp. the scheme en groups affine $\underline{\Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega)$). Of the terminologie ``Galois group motivic'', the theorie of the $\pi_1$ devenant in the cas of the spectres of field celle of Galois.

\subsection{Size of the motivic Galois group}

En general, the Galois group motivic $G$ is enorme. Sauf for $k$ algebraic over a field finite one s'attend by example a this that it admette as quotient $\PGL(2)$. It ne ``sait not'' that of the motives $M$ can former a family dependant algebriquement of a parametre $t$.

Bien that $G$ let enorme, son existence facilite the maniement of objects ``motiviques''.

\begin{example}
The category $\Ind\mathcal{M}(k)$ of the ind-objects of the category $\mathcal{M}(k)$ of the motives on $k$ herite of the tensor product of $\mathcal{M}(k)$. This permet of define a algebra of Hopf commutative in $\Ind\mathcal{M}(k)$ as being a object $H$ equipped with a product and coproduit: $H \otimes H \to H$ and $H \to H \otimes H$, ainsi that of unite, counite and antipode: $\mathbf{1} \to H$, $H \to \mathbf{1}$, $H \to H$ verifiant of the axiomes convenables. One defines the category of the schemes en groups affine motiviques as being the opposee of the category of the algebras of Hopf commutatives of $\Ind\mathcal{M}(k)$. Si $\omega$ is a functor fiber, with values in the vector spaces $V$ on $\Q$, of Galois group motivic $G := \underline{\Aut}^{\otimes}(\omega)$, the functor $\omega$ induces a equivalence of $\Ind\mathcal{M}(k)$ with the category of representations lineaires --- not necessarily of dimension finite --- of $G$, and $H \mapsto \Spec(\omega(H))$ is a equivalence of the category of the schemes en groups affine motiviques with celle of the schemes en groups affine on $\Q$, equipped with a action of $G$.

Voici trois examples of tels objects.

\textbf{1.3.1.} The version motivic of $\pi_1(X, x)$ rendu unipotent, cf.~Deligne [14], specialement the \S\S5, 7, and 10 a 13.

\textbf{1.3.2.} The Galois group motivic: it admet a version motivic $G_{\mot}$ such that for every functor fiber $\omega$, the Galois group motivic corresponding let deduces of $G_{\mot}$ by map of $\omega$. It s'agit of a construction valable in every Tannakian category, cf.~Deligne [15, \S8].

In the analogie with the theorie of the $\pi_1$ profini, son analogue is the suivant: for $x$ variable, the $\pi_1(X, x)$ forment a systeme local on $X$, or more precisely a object en groups in the category of the pro-objects of celle of the coverings etale of $X$. The $\pi_1$ en $x$ s'obtient en prenant the fiber en $x$.

\textbf{1.3.3.} Let $\mathcal{A}$ a full subcategory of celle of the motives mixtes on $k$, stable by $\otimes$, dual and under-quotients, and let $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ss}}$ the full subcategory of the direct sums of motives purs in $\mathcal{A}$. One dispose of the exact functor and compatible to the tensor product: $M \mapsto \Gr^W(M)$, of $\mathcal{A}$ in $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ss}}$. There exists in $\Ind\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ss}}$ a scheme en groups pro-unipotent motivic $U$, agissant functorially on $\Gr^W(M)$ for $M$ in $\mathcal{A}$, and such that $M \mapsto \Gr^W(M)$ Let equivalence of $\mathcal{A}$ with the category of representations (in $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ss}}$) of $U$. C'is a consequence of Deligne [15, 8.17].

One can similarly define and manier schemes, torseurs,\ldots motiviques.
\end{example}

\subsection{The abelianized motivic Galois group}

The Galois group motivic rendu abelien $G^{\ab}$ a taille more raisonnable. It is independant of the functor realisation choisi (and of son existence). Conjecturalement, si $k_1 \subset k_2$ are deux field algebriquement clos, $G^{\ab}$ is the same for $k_1$ and $k_2$ and the subcategory corresponding of motives is generated by the $H^1_{\mot}$ of abelian varieties of type CM.

En caracteristique $0$, si one prend the motives to the sens of the absolute Hodge cycles and that one se limite a the subcategory generated by the $H^1$ of abelian varieties, one sait calculer $G^{\ab}$. For $k = \overline{\Q}$, one sait same calculer the quotient of $G$ by the group derive of $\Ker(G \to \Gal(\overline{\Q}/\Q))$. See Deligne [13] or the expose of Schappacher a this conference.

For $k = \overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$, all motive devrait be direct sum of motives purs and the Galois group motivic $G$ devrait contenir a element $F$, the Frobenius, of which the powers let denses in $G$. In particular, $G$ devrait be commutative. The calcul conjectural of $\mathcal{M}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p)$ a ete fait by Grothendieck (not publie). See Langlands--Rapoport [23] and the expose of Milne a this conference.

\subsection{Abelian varieties and 1-motives}

A source of 1.1(a), (b) is the example suivant. A curve projective and smooth $X$ defines sa jacobienne $J(X) = \Pic^0(X)$. The abelian varieties a isogenie pres on $k$ formant a abelian category semisimple with of the $\Hom(A, B)$ of dimension finite on $\Q$ and for each theorie of cohomology usuelle $H$, the dual $H_1(X)$ of $H^1(X)$, se deduces of $J(X)$ by map of a functor ``realisation''. Noter that $H_1(X)$ is also the twist a the Tate $H^1(X)(1)$ of $H^1(X)$. For the cohomology $\ell$-adique, $\real_{\ell}(A)$ is the module of Tate $T_{\ell}(A) = \varprojlim A_{\ell^n}(\bar{k}) \otimes \Qell$. For $k$ of caracteristique $0$ and the theorie of Rham, $\real_{\DR}(A)$ is the algebra of Lie of the extension additive universelle of $A$.

One veut therefore pouvoir identifier the abelian varieties a of the motives of weight $-1$ particuliers. For certain maps (by example the etude of the Shimura varieties), one serait deja content of disposer of a category of motives contenant the abelian varieties and stable by $\otimes$. En caracteristique $0$, the theorie of the absolute Hodge cycles fournit a telle theorie (Deligne--Brylinski [11], Deligne-Milne [12], or the expose of Panchishkin a this conference). Si one ne se limite not to the motives purs, a other example cle is celui of the curves smooth not necessarily completes. More generally, one can considerer a curve connected projective and smooth $Y$, $S$ and $T$ disjoints in $Y$ and the cohomology $H^1(Y - S, \rel T)$. A 1-motive $K$ is a complex of schemes en groups reduit to the degrees $-1$ and $0$, with, on the algebraic closure, $K^{-1}$ a $\Z$-module free of type finite and $K^0$ a extension of a abelian variety by a tore. Supposons for simplifier $k$ algebriquement clos. For each theorie of cohomology usuelle, the $H^1$ considered, or plutot son twist a the Tate $H^1(Y - S, \rel T)(1)$, se deduces then of the 1-motive suivant by map of a functor realisation.

Let $J_{S,T}(Y)$ the jacobienne generalisee classifiant the sheaves inversibles of degree $0$ on $Y$ trivialises on $T$. C'is a extension of the abelian variety $\Pic^0(Y)$ by the tore of group of characters $\Ker(\Z^T \to \Z)$. Each $s \in S$ defines the sheaf inversible $\mathcal{O}(s)$, trivialise on $T$ and of degree $1$, of or
\begin{equation}
\Ker(\Z^S \to \Z) \to J_{S,T}(Y).
\tag{1.5.1}
\end{equation}
C'is the 1-motive promis. See Deligne [9, \S10].

\begin{remark}
For $S, T \neq \emptyset$, the 1-motive (1.5.1) determine also the category of the sheaves inversibles on $Y - S$, trivialises on $T$: a point $x \in J_{S,T}(Y)$ defines a tel sheaf inversible $\mathcal{L}_x$, and a isomorphism of $\mathcal{L}_x$ with $\mathcal{L}_y$ which let the identity on $T$ s'identifie a $k \in \Ker(\Z^S \to \Z)$, with $y - x = \delta k$.
\end{remark}

A new, one veut pouvoir identifier the 1-motives a of the motives (mixtes) particuliers. For certain maps (the etude a the infini of the Shimura varieties) one serait deja content of disposer of a category of motives contenant the 1-motives and stable by $\otimes$. En caracteristique $0$, one sait the faire (Brylinski [7]).

\subsection{Moduli of motives and Shimura varieties}

The abelian varieties have of the moduli spaces and ceux-ci permettent of interpreter algebriquement certain quotients of spaces hermitiens symmetric by of the groups arithmetiques. The cas of the motives is more complique. Si $S$ is a scheme on $\C$ and $M$ a family of motives purs parametree by $S$, $M$ fournit en Hodge realization a variation of Hodge structures $\mathcal{M}_{\C}$ on $S(\C)$, polarisee si $M$ the is. Of the, a map $\varphi_0$ of $S(\C)$ in the space $\mathscr{H}$ classifiant the Hodge structures polarisees of nombres of Hodge ceux of $M$.

The variation $\mathcal{M}_{\C}$ fournit in particular a systeme local of vector spaces complexes $\mathcal{M}_{\C}$ equipped with a Hodge filtration $F$ variant continument. the holomorphie of $F$ and the condition of ``transversalite'' decouvertes by Griffiths [17] (see also Griffiths [18]) can s'exprimer as suit. Let $T$ the fiber tangent of $S$, $S$ being vue as a variety $C^{\infty}$. The structure complex of $S$ fournit a structure complex on $T$. Let $\varphi: T \otimes_{\R} \C \to T$ the prolongement $\C$-lineaire of the identity of $T$ and let $T'' \subset T \otimes_{\R} \C$ the kernel of $\varphi$. Definissons the Hodge filtration of $T \otimes \C$ by
$$
F^{-1} = T \otimes \C, \quad F^0 = T'', \quad F^1 = 0.
$$
For $\xi$ and $\eta$ of the sections $C^{\infty}$ of $T \otimes \C$ and of $\mathcal{M}_{\C}$, the structure plate of $\mathcal{M}_{\C}$ permet of define $\nabla_{\xi} \eta$. The condition is that $\xi, \eta \mapsto \nabla_{\xi} \eta$ let compatible to the Hodge filtrations:
\begin{itemize}
\item[] $\xi$ in $T''$, $\eta$ in $F^i$, $\nabla_{\xi} \eta$ in $F^i$: holomorphie;
\item[] $\xi$ in $T$, $\eta$ in $F^i$, $\nabla_{\xi} \eta$ in $F^{i-1}$: transversalite.
\end{itemize}

On the space classifiant $\mathscr{H}$ there exists a structure complex and a distribution holomorphic $\tau \subset T$ such that holomorphie and transversalite se traduisent as holomorphie of the map classifiante $\varphi: S(\C) \to \mathscr{H}$ and tangence of $\tau(S)$ a $\tau$.

The distribution $\tau$ is en general not integrable. the set of the points of $\mathscr{H}$ which correspondent a direct factor of a $H^i(X)$, $X$ projective not singular, is the reunion, denombrable of under-varieties tangentes a $\tau$. For $\tau$ different of the fiber tangent all integer, one n'en a aucune description, same conjecturale.

A argument analogue, egalement of the a Griffiths, explique pourquoi the points of the jacobienne intermediaire $J^d(X)$ ne can not all be classes of cycles algebraic, sauf lorsque $H^{2d-1}(X)$ is of type $\{(d-1, d), (d, d-1)\}$. Let $f: X \to S$ a family of projective varieties not singulieres. One sets $X_s = f^{-1}(s)$. The jacobiennes intermediaires $J^d(X_s) = H^{2d-1}(X_s, \C) / F^d + H^{2d-1}(X_s, \Z)$ forment a fiber holomorphic $J^d$ en tores complexes and the axiome of transversalite assure that the connexion of Gauss-Manin (exprimant that the $H^{2d-1}(X_s, \C)$ forment a systeme local) passe to the quotient for define a operateur differentiel $D$, defined on the sheaf of sections of $J^d$ and with values in celui of the 1-forms a value in the fiber holomorphic of the $H^{2d-1}(X_s, \mathcal{O})/F^{d+1}$. A family algebraic $(Z_s)_{s \in S}$ of cycles algebraic cohomologues a zero on the $X_s$ defines a section holomorphic $z$ of $J^d$. The result of Griffiths is that $Dz = 0$. The section $z$ defines a family holomorphic of mixed Hodge structures, extension of $\Z$ (type $(0,0)$) by $H^{2d-1}(X)(d)$, and $Dz = 0$ equivaut a this that this family satisfies the axiome of transversalite.


\subsection{}

Quand the distribution $\tau$ is the fiber tangent all integer, $\mathscr{H}$ is a quotient arithmetic of a domaine hermitien symmetric. This description, of the points complexes of the Shimura varieties as modules of Hodge structures suggere that these varieties are of the moduli spaces of motives.

Let $\mathbb{S}$ the $\R$-algebraic group $\C^*$: $\mathbb{S} = R_{\C/\R} \mathbb{G}_m$. Se donner a action of $\mathbb{S}$ on the space vector reel $V$ revient a se donner a decomposition of $V \otimes \C$ en direct sum of $V^{p,q}$ with $V^{q,p} = \overline{V^{p,q}}$, $z \in \mathbb{S}(\R) = \C^*$ agissant on $V^{p,q}$ by multiplication by $z^{-p} \bar{z}^{-q}$. One defines $w: \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{S}$ (resp. $\mu: \mathbb{G}_m \to \mathbb{S}_{\C}$, defined on $\C$) by the condition that for every $V$, $w(\lambda)$ (resp. $\mu(\lambda)$) agisse on $V^{p,q}$ by multiplication by $\lambda^{p+q}$ (resp. $\lambda^{-p}$).

The data definissant a variety of Shimura $\Sh_K(G, X)$ are a group reductive $G$ on $\Q$, a $G(\R)$-class of conjugaison of morphisms $h: \mathbb{S} \to G_{\R}$, with $w_h := h \circ w$ central (therefore independant of $h$) and a under-group compact open $K$ of $G(\Af)$. Placons-we in the cas or $w_X$ is defined on $\Q$ and or $\Int h(i)$ is a involution of Cartan of the quotient $G/w_X(\mathbb{G}_m)$.

The field dual $E(G, X)$ is the field of definition of the class of conjugaison of $\mu_h$ ($h$ quelconque in $X$). C'is a under-field of $\C$. The variety of Shimura is defined on $E(G, X)$, with for points complexes $K \backslash X \times G(\Af) / G(\Q)$. Sa construction in the cas general is due a Borovoi [6].

One assumed $c \geq a$ and it faut prouver the nullite of $\Ext^i(\mathbf{1}, H^a_{\mot}(X)(c))$ for $i > (c-a) + a = c$. This inegalite equivaut a $c > a + (2c - i - a) \geq \dim(X) + (2c - i - a)$.

That 3.7.1 Let isomorphism impliquerait also of the theorems of Lefschetz faibles for the groups of Chow, and the conjectures on the groups of Chow presentees a this conference by J.~P.~Murre.

For $M$ the motive unite, (3.8.1)(ii) demande that

It n'is not clear for moi si one must esperer a analogue on $\Z$ of (3.9.1). Si one defines the $H^n_{\abs}(\Spec(k), \Z(j))$ as being of the groups of Chow superieurs, (3.9.1) is trivialement vrai.

A question more concrete: assume that one ait pu define in such a way ``independante of $\ell$'', of the cohomology classes galoisienne in the $H^n(\Gal(\bar{k}/k), \Z_\ell(j))$. Si $n > j$, (3.9.1) demande that these classes let of twisting. Must-one same the esperer nulles?

\begin{example}
Let $G$ a algebraic group absolument simple and simply connected over a field $k$. On the algebraic closure $\bar{k}$ of $k$, the relations between cohomology of $G$, $BG$, and $BT$, for $T$ a tore maximal, montrent in chacune of the theories of cohomology usuelles that $H^i(G) = 0$ for $i = 1, 2$ and that $H^3(G)(2)$ a generateur natural $\alpha$. Placons-we en cohomology $\ell$-adique. On $\bar{k}$, the cohomology $\ell$-adique absolute of $G$ is direct sum of celle of the point and of a cohomology reduite $\tilde{H}$, liee a celle of $BG$; by a exact sequence of Hochschild-Serre. one has therefore $H^i(G, \Z_\ell(2)) = 0$ for $i \leq 2$ and $H^3(G, \Z_\ell(2))$ is isomorphic a $\Z_\ell$, with a generateur natural $\alpha$. This generateur is primitif, therefore defines a homomorphisme
$$
\alpha: G \to H^3(\Spec(k), \Z_\ell(2)).
$$
One can verify that, as prevu by 3.9, this fleche is zero: by Merkurjev-Suslin [27] the $H^3$ is without twisting, and a argument of trace shows that the image is of twisting: $G$ se deploie on a finite extension $k'$ of $k$ and $G(k')$ is egal a son group derive, of the less si $|k'| \geq 4$.

The same cercle of idee permet of construct a central extension canonical of $G(k)$ by $H^2(\Spec(k), \Z/n(2))$, egal a $K_2(k)/nK_2(k)$ by Merkurjev-Suslin [27]. Recall that for $G$ deploye, a central extension canonical of $G(k)$ by $K_2(k)$ a ete construite by Matsumoto [25, 5.11].
\end{example}

Si $F$ is a number field, (3.7.1) predit that in the category of the motives on $F$, one has
\begin{equation}
\tag{3.12.1}
\Ext^1(\mathbf{1}, \Q(1)) = K_{2j-1}(F) \otimes \Q,
\end{equation}
with nullite of the $\Ext^n$, $n \geq 2$. the assertion (3.12.1) impose of the restrictions severes a the category of the motives extensions iterees of motives of Tate, and for the systemes of realisations attached a object of this category. See Deligne [14] or a example considered en detail is celui of the algebra of Lie of the $\pi_1$ rendu unipotent of the projective line less trois points.

Voici a programme for prouver of the consequences of (3.12.1) for of tels systemes of realisations.

\begin{itemize}
\item[(A)] Define a Tannakian category $\mathcal{T}$, with for objects simples the powers tensorielles $\Q(n)$, $n \in \Z$, of a object of rank a $\Q(1)$, for which (3.12.1) let vrai ``by definition''.

the idee is that the groups of Chow superieurs of Bloch $\Ch^n(\Spec(F), i)$ are donnes by a complex simplicial gradue by the codimension $n$ of the cycles and that the product is presque given by a structure of algebra commutative simpliciale graduee. ``Presque'' car the intersection of deux cycles n'is not toujours defined, only ``en general''. Rationnellement, it devrait be possible of remplacer this presque algebra by a vraie algebra differentielle bigraduee commutative, with $d$ of degree $(1, 0)$, and
$$
A^{m,n} = 0 \text{ si } m < 0,
$$
$A^{*,0}$ reduit a $\Q$ en degree $0$,
$$
H^i(A^{*,n}) \text{ reduit a } K_{2n-i}(F) \otimes \Q \text{ en degre } i.
$$
One can then define $\mathcal{T}$ as a category suitable of modules differentiels bigrades and of morphisms a homotopie pres.

This part of the programme a ete essentiellement realisee by May, [26]. For a variante cubique, see the expose of Bloch in this volume.

\item[(B)] Define, for chacune of the theories cohomologiques usuelles, a functor fiber ``realisation'' on the Tannakian category $\mathcal{T}$ of (A).

\item[(C)] Montrer that the motives of Tate mixtes auxquels one s'interesse, by example, $\Lie \pi_1(\mathbf{P}^1 - \{0, 1, \infty\})$ --- or plutot the systemes of leurs realisations --- proviennent of a object of $\mathcal{T}$.
\end{itemize}
