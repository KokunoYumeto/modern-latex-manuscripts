\section{Transformations of Coxeter}

\subsection{}

Let $W$ a group of Coxeter finite, $D$ son diagram of Dynkin, and $V$ the space of sa representation natural, and $R \subset W$ the set of the reflexions. One appellera \emph{racine} a vecteur unitaire orthogonal a hyperplan radiciel. Si $\alpha$ is a racine, one denotes $V^{+}(\alpha)$ the demi-space $\{x \mid (x,\alpha) \geq 0\}$, $H(\alpha) = \partial V^{+}(\alpha)$ the mur corresponding and $r_{\alpha} \in R$ the reflexion with respect to $H(\alpha)$. One assumed $D$ irreducible $\neq A_1$.

\subsection{}

Let $c$ a transformation of Coxeter. The faits suivants are prouves in Bourbaki, \textit{Lie}, VI \S 2.

\smallskip

\noindent
\textup{(i)} Let $h$ the ordre of $c$, and $P \subset V$ the more grand under-space of $V$ or $c$ n'a that the values proper $\exp(\pm 2\pi i/h)$. one has $\dim(P) = 2$. One oriente $P$ for that $c|P$ Let rotation of amplitude $\dfrac{2\pi}{h}$.

\smallskip

\noindent
\textup{(ii)} Aucun mur ne contient $P$. The murs decoupent on $P$ a systeme of hyperplans radiciels of type $I_2(h)$.

\smallskip

\noindent
\textup{(iii)} The murs of $V$ contenant a same mur of $P$ are deux a deux orthogonaux. One denotes $s(\alpha)$ the involution product of reflexions correspondants.

\smallskip

\noindent
\textup{(iv)} Let $C_0$ a chambre of $P$, $C$ the unique chambre of $V$ the contenant, and $d_1^{+}$ and $d_2^{+}$ the demi-lines limitant $C_0$, pris in the sens direct, and $d_1$, $d_2$ the lines correspondantes. Then, the murs of $C$ are ceux contenant $d_1$ or $d_2$,

\newpage

\noindent
and $c = s(d_2)\, s(d_1)$; $s(d_i)$ stabilise $P$, and sa restriction a $P$ is the reflexion with respect to $d_i$; $c|P$ is therefore a transformation of Coxeter of $P$.

\bigskip

Let $c \in W$ a element of Coxeter, and $\alpha$ a racine.

\begin{corollary}[1.3]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item With the notations of \textup{1.2(iv)}, the group dihedral $W'$ generated by $s(d_1)$ and $s(d_2)$ is the stabilisateur of $P$. C'is also the stabilisateur of $\{c, c^{-1}\}$. It agit fidelement on $P$, and sa restriction a $P$ is the group of Weyl of $P$. The centralisateur of $c$ is the under-group $\langle c \rangle$ generated by $c$.

\item There exists a chambre $C$, of which $\alpha$ let racine simple, and a enumeration $(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_\ell)$ of the racines simples of $C$, with $\alpha = \alpha_1$, such that $c = r_{\alpha_1} \cdots r_{\alpha_\ell}$.

\item For $C$ as en \textup{(ii)}, the line of intersection of the murs $H(\alpha_i)$ ($i \neq 1$) is the under-space of $V$ fixe under $r_\alpha c$. It ne depend therefore not of $C$.

The under-group $W_d$ of $W$ which fixe $d$ is of Coxeter, generated by the $r_{\alpha_i}$ ($i \neq 1$). Sa representation natural is on $V/d$. $r_\alpha c \in W_d$ is a transformation of Coxeter of $W_d$.

\item The sommet of $D$ corresponding to the mur $H(\alpha)$ of $C$ ($C$ as en \textup{(ii)}) is independant of $C$. One the note $\overline{\alpha}$.

\item $\langle c \rangle$ agit librement on the set of the racines. Deux racines are in the same orbite if and only if they have same image in $D$.

\item Let $C_0$ and $C$ as en \textup{1.2(iv)}. For each mur $M$ of $C$, let $\alpha(M)$ the racine corresponding such that $H = H(\alpha)$ and that $V^{+}(\alpha)$ contienne (resp.\ ne contienne not) $C$ si $M$ passe by $d_2$ (resp.\ $d_1$). Then, $\{\alpha(M) \mid M \text{ mur de } C\}$ is a section of the set of the orbites of $\langle c \rangle$, and $\overline{\alpha(M)}$ is the class in $D$ of the mur $M$ of $C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\noindent
\textit{Proof.} --- \leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\alpha$) $W'$ stabilise $\{c, c^{-1}\}$: $s(d_i)\, c\, s(d_i)^{-1} = c^{-1}$.

$\beta$) $\{c, c^{-1}\}$ determine $P$, the stabilisateur of $\{c, c^{-1}\}$ stabilise therefore $P$.

$\gamma$) A element $w \in W$ which stabilise a chambre $C_0$ of $P$ is the identity (car $C_0$ is a chambre). [Puisque $W'$ is transitif on the chambres of $P$] Puisque $W'$ is the group of Weyl of $P$, c'is the stabilisateur of $P$ all integer. Enfin, $\langle c \rangle$ is the centralisateur of $c$ in $W'$, therefore in $W$.

\item One prend the chambre $C_0$ of $P$ of mur $H(\alpha) \cap P$, such that, with the notations of 1.2(iv), $H(\alpha) \cap P = d_2$ and $V^{+}(\alpha) \supset C_0$. Puisque $c = s(d_2)\, s(d_1)$, this choix convient.

\item one has $r_\alpha c = r_{\alpha_2} \cdots r_{\alpha_\ell}$; the premiere assertion of (iv) en resulte facilement. That $r_\alpha c$ let of Coxeter se voit en prenant $C$ as en (ii).

\item One va verify that $\overline{\alpha}$ ne depend that of $\alpha$ and $d$: si $C'$ and $C''$ are deux chambres of which $\alpha$ is racine simple, and such that the demi-line appuyee to the mur $H(\alpha)$ let $d^{+} = d \cap V^{+}(\alpha)$, there exists $w \in W_d$ envoyant $C'$ on $C''$ (the chambres contenant $d^{+}$ correspondent biunivoquement a celles of $V/d$). Puisque $w$ respecte $d$, it respecte $H(\alpha)$, and (iv) en resulte.

\item \setcounter{enumi}{5} \'Ecrivant $c = s(d_2)\, s(d_1)$, one voit aussitot that $\overline{\alpha(M)} =$ class of the mur $M$ of $C$ for $M \ni d_2$. Let $C_0' = s(d_1)(C_0)$, of or $C' = s(d_1) C$, and $d_0 = s(d_1)(d_2)$. one has $c = s(d_1)\, s(d_0)$. For $M \ni d_1$, $\overline{\alpha(M)}$ is therefore the class in $D$ of the mur $M$ of $C'$; puisque $s(d_1)$ respecte $M$, c'is also the class of the mur $M$ of $C$.

It is clear that the $\alpha(M)$ forment a section of the set of the orbites of $\langle c \rangle$ in the set of the racines and that the action of $\langle c \rangle$ is free [one se ramene to the cas dihedral]. Puisque $\overline{c^{-1}\alpha} = \overline{\alpha}$ (transport of structure), (v) en resulte. \qed
\end{enumerate}

\newpage

\begin{remark}[1.4]
Ne supposons more $D$ irreducible $\neq A_1$. The assertions (ii), (iii), (iv) valent again. [The same part of (v)] vaut en remplacant $\langle c \rangle$ by the centralisateur of $c$. One notera $h(D)$ the ordre of this centralisateur:
\[
h\bigl(\textstyle\bigsqcup D_i\bigr) = \prod h(D_i).
\]
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[1.5]
Let $W$ a group of Coxeter finite and $c \in W$ a element of Coxeter. Let $\Xi_c$ (resp.\ $\Xi_c'$) the set of the mots of $\ell$ racines $\alpha_1 \cdots \alpha_\ell$ (resp.\ of the mots of $\ell$ reflexions $r_{\alpha_1} \cdots r_{\alpha_\ell}$) ($\ell = \text{rang}$) such that $r_{\alpha_1} \cdots r_{\alpha_\ell} = c$ (resp.\ $r_{\alpha_1} \cdots r_{\alpha_\ell} = c$). The nombre $F(D) = \#\,\Xi_c$ and $F'(D) = \#\,\Xi_c'$ ne dependent that of $D$, and satisfy $F(D) = 2^\ell\, F'(D)$. one has, for $D$ irreducible,
\[
F(D) = h(D) \sum_{i \in D} F\bigl(D - \{i\}\bigr),
\]
of or
\[
F'(D) = \frac{h(D)}{2} \sum_{i \in D} F'\bigl(D - \{i\}\bigr).
\]

of after 1.3(iv), one has indeed
\[
F(D) = \sum_{d \text{ racine}} F\bigl(D - \{\overline{d}\}\bigr),
\]
and one applique 1.3(v), complete by 1.4.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[1.6]
one has $F(\varnothing) = F'(\varnothing) = 1$. By induction a partir of the, one trouve that for of the diagrams $D_i$ of rank $\ell_i$, and $D = \bigsqcup D_i$, $\ell = \sum \ell_i$, one has
\[
F(D) = \frac{\ell!}{\prod \ell_i!} \prod F(D_i),
\]
and similarly for $F'$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[1.7]
For $c$ fixe, one has defined a map $\alpha \mapsto \overline{\alpha}$ of the set of the racines in $D$. one has $\overline{-\alpha} =$ transforme of $\overline{\alpha}$ by the involution of opposition. Of the lors, notant $D'$ the quotient of $D$ by the involution of opposition, $\alpha \mapsto \overline{\alpha}$ passe to the quotient and defines $\overline{\cdot} : R \to D$.
\end{remark}

\section{Action of the group of braids}

Let $W$ a group of Coxeter finite, $D$ son diagram of Dynkin, $D'$ the quotient of $D$ by the involution of opposition, $\ell$ the rank of $W$, $c$ a transformation of Coxeter and $\Xi_c'$ as en 1.5. Let $B_\ell$ the group of braids a $\ell$ brins, of generateurs canonical $(w_i)_{1 \leq i \leq \ell-1}$. One the fait agir on $\Xi_c'$ by
\[
w_i : \quad (r_1, \ldots, r_\ell) \longmapsto (r_1, \ldots, r_{i-1}, r_{i+1}, r_{i+1}\, r_i\, r_{i+1}, r_{i+2}, \ldots, r_\ell)
\]
(the generateur $w_i$ agit en croisant $r_i$ and $r_{i+1}$).

\begin{theorem}[2.1]
$B_\ell$ agit transitivement on $\Xi_c'$.
\end{theorem}

One procede by recurrence on $\ell$. Si $D$ is sum disjointe of diagrams $D_i$, and si the theorem is vrai for the $D_i$, it the is for $D$. This we ramene a supposer $D$ irreducible. The cas $D = \varnothing$ or $A_1$ are trivial.

\begin{lemma}[2.1.1]
Let $\sigma = (r'_1, \ldots, r'_\ell)$ and $\sigma' = (r''_1, \ldots, r''_\ell)$ in $\Xi_c'$. Si $r'_1 = r''_1$, then $\sigma$ and $\sigma'$ are in the same orbite of $B_\ell$.
\end{lemma}

Let $d$ the under-space of $V$ fixe by $r'_1 c$. One applique the hypothese of recurrence a $W_d$ and a $\Xi'_{r'_1 c}$ (1.3(iii)).

\smallskip

Disons that deux racines $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are \emph{equivalentes} s'there exists $\sigma' = (r'_1, \ldots, r'_\ell)$ and $\sigma'' = (r''_1, \ldots, r''_\ell)$ in $\Xi_c$, conjugues under $B_\ell$, and such that $r'_1 = r_\alpha$ and $r''_1 = r_\beta$. It reste a prouver that deux racines are toujours equivalentes.

\smallskip

(a) $\alpha$ and $c\alpha$ are equivalentes: one prend $\sigma = (r_\alpha, r_{\alpha'}, \ldots)$ in $\Xi_c'$ (1.3(ii)); one has $w_1 \cdots w_{\ell-1}\, w_\ell(\sigma) = (c^{-1} r_\alpha c, \ldots)$.

\smallskip

\newpage

(b) $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$ are equivalentes: $r_\alpha = r_{-\alpha}$.

of after 1.3(v) and 1.7, there exists therefore a relation of equivalence on $D'$ such that $\alpha \sim \beta$ if and only if $\overline{\alpha}$ and $\overline{\beta}$ have of the images equivalentes on $D'$. Puisque $W$ agit transitivement on $D^*$, this equivalence ne depend not of the choix of $c$. One denotes $\sim$ the equivalence image converse on $D$.

\smallskip

(c) For $i$, $j$ deux sommets not lies of $D$, one has $i \sim j$.

One prend $c = r_i\, r_j \cdots$ (product of reflexions fondamentales). one has
\[
w_1(r_i, r_j, \ldots) = (r_j, r_i, \ldots)
\]
and $\overline{\alpha}_i = i$, $\overline{\alpha}_j = j$.

\smallskip

(d) For $i$, $j$ as lies ($i$ pendant), one has $i \sim j$.

One prend $c = r_j\, r_i \cdots$ (product of reflexions fond.).
\[
w_1(r_j, r_i, \ldots) = (r_i, \underbrace{r_j\, r_i\, r_j}_{\substack{\text{r\'eflexions fondamentales pour} \\ \text{la chambre } r_j C, \text{ car } r_j \text{ commute \`a } \cdots}}, \ldots)
\]
and $\overline{\alpha}_j = j$, $\overline{\alpha}_i = i$ (a see with the chambre $r_j C$).

\bigskip

Of (c) and (d) resulte the equivalence of all the sommets, and the Theorem.

\section{Calcul of $F'(D)$}

\begin{corollary}[3.1]
$F'(A_\ell) = (\ell+1)^{\ell-1}$.
\end{corollary}

In this case, tu as indeed demontre directement ta conjecture, tandis that, by 2.1, $\Xi_c'$ ne form that a orbite under $B_\ell$, and one applique ton calcul of the indice.

C'is also equivalent a the formule that tu indiques on the arbres: for that a product of $\ell$ transpositions in a set a $\ell+1$ elements Let $(\ell+1)$-cycle, it faut and suffit that the graphe obtained en liant the points in these transpositions Let arbre. Puisqu'there is $\ell!$ $(\ell+1)$-cycles, and $\ell!$ ordres totaux on a arbre given, one has
\[
F'(A_\ell) = \#\{\text{arbres de support un ensemble \`a } (\ell+1) \text{ \'el\'ements donn\'e}\}.
\]

\subsection*{3.2}

The corollary 1.5 fournit
\[
F'(A_\ell) = \frac{\ell+1}{2} \sum_{\substack{i+j=\ell-1 \\ i,j \geq 0}} \frac{(\ell-1)!}{i!\, j!}\, F'(A_i)\, F'(A_j), \quad \text{soit}
\]
\[
2\ell \, \frac{F'(A_\ell)}{\ell!} = (\ell+1) \sum_{i+j=\ell-1} \frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}\, \frac{F'(A_j)}{j!}.
\]

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(3.2.1)} En terme of the serie generatrice $\displaystyle a(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}\, t^{i+1}$, this s'ecrit
\[
2t \, \partial_t\bigl(\tfrac{a}{t}\bigr) = \partial_t(a^2), \quad \text{soit}
\]
\[
da - a\,\frac{dt}{t} = a\, da.
\]

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(3.2.2)} $\dfrac{da}{a} - da = \dfrac{dt}{t}$ \quad and puisque $a'(0) = 1$

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(3.2.3)} $a \cdot e^{-a} = t$

\smallskip

\noindent
(This can also se see on the interpretation arborescente: one exprime that one ote a sommet a arbre, en evitant a norme of arbres, chacun with a sommet marque (celui lie a $\alpha$), and reciproquement.)

\newpage

\begin{corollary}[3.3]
$F'(B_\ell) = \ell^\ell$.
\end{corollary}

Ici, $W$ is the under-group of $GL(\ell, \mathbb{R})$ which respecte a trame $\pm\varepsilon_i$.
\[
|W| = 2^\ell \cdot \ell!, \qquad h = 2\ell \quad \text{par ex : } c : \varepsilon_1 \to \varepsilon_2 \to \cdots \to \varepsilon_\ell \to -\varepsilon_1
\]
nombre of elements of Coxeter: $2^{\ell-1} (\ell-1)!$.

For that a product of $\ell$ reflexions let of Coxeter, it faut and suffit that

\smallskip

\noindent
($\alpha$) a and a seule of between they let of the type $(\varepsilon_i \to -\varepsilon_i$, $\varepsilon_j$ fixe)

\smallskip

\noindent
($\beta$) the graphe suivant: a arete by chacune of the reflexions [which n'is not] of reflexion $\varepsilon_i \to \pm\varepsilon_j$ --- Let arbre.

\smallskip

Of the lors, the nombre of mots of $\ell$ reflexions of which the product is of Coxeter is:
\[
\# \text{ arbres} \cdot \# \text{ orientations sur les ar\^etes de l'arbre} \cdot 2^{\ell-1} \cdot \underbrace{\ell}_{\substack{\varepsilon_i \to \pm\varepsilon_j}} \cdot \underbrace{\ell}_{\substack{\text{indices des} \\ \text{r\'eflexions ($\alpha$) sa place}}}
\]
\[
= \ell^{\ell-2} \cdot (\ell-1)! \cdot 2^{\ell-1} \cdot \ell^2 \qquad \text{d'apr\`es 3.1}
\]
and the cardinal of each $\Xi_c'$ is therefore
\[
\frac{\ell^{\ell-2}\, (\ell-1)! \, 2^{\ell-1}\, \ell^2}{2^{\ell-1}\, (\ell-1)!} = \ell^\ell.
\]

\subsection*{3.4}

The corollary 1.5 fournit [one sets $B_0 = \varnothing$, $B_1 = A_1$] for $\ell \geq 1$
\[
F'(B_\ell) = \ell \sum_{\substack{i,j \geq 0 \\ i+j=\ell-1}} \frac{(\ell-1)!}{i!\, j!}\, F'(A_i)\, F'(B_j), \quad \text{soit}
\]
\[
\frac{F'(B_\ell)}{\ell!} = \sum_{i+j=\ell-1} \frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}\, \frac{F'(B_j)}{j!}.
\]

En terme of the serie generatrice
\[
\textbf{(3.4.1)} \qquad b(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \frac{F'(B_i)}{i!}\, t^i,
\]
this s'ecrit
\[
b(t) = a(t)\, b(t) + 1, \quad \text{i.e.}
\]
\[
\textbf{(3.4.2)} \qquad b(t) = \frac{1}{1 - a(t)}.
\]

\newpage

\begin{corollary}[3.5]
$F'(D_\ell) = 2\, (\ell-1)^\ell$ \qquad ($\ell \geq 2$).
\end{corollary}

Let the serie generatrice
\[
d(t) = 1 + \sum_\ell \frac{F'(D_\ell)}{2 \cdot \ell!}\, t^\ell.
\]

The corollary 1.5 fournit
\[
F'(D_\ell) = (\ell-1) \Biggl[\, \sum_{\substack{i,j \geq 0 \\ i+j=\ell-1 \\ j \geq 2}} \frac{(\ell-1)!}{i!\, j!}\, F'(A_i)\, F'(D_j) \;+\; 2\, F'(A_{\ell-1}) \Biggr],
\]
\[
\ell \, \frac{F'(D_\ell)}{2 \cdot \ell!} = (\ell-1) \Biggl[\, \sum_{\substack{i,j \geq 0 \\ i+j=\ell-1 \\ j \geq 2}} \frac{F'(A_i)}{i!}\, \frac{F'(D_j)}{j!} \;+\; \frac{F'(A_{\ell-1})}{(\ell-1)!} \Biggr].
\]

Multipliant by $t^{\ell-1}$, and prenant the sum, that given

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(3.5.1)} $\partial_t d = t\, \partial_t\bigl(\tfrac{a \cdot d}{t}\bigr)$, \quad let
\[
\partial_t d = \partial_t d \cdot a + d \cdot t\, \partial_t\bigl(\tfrac{a}{t}\bigr), \quad \text{o\`u } t\, \partial_t\bigl(\tfrac{a}{t}\bigr) = a \cdot \partial_t a
\]
\[
\partial_t d\, (1-a) = d \cdot a \cdot \partial_t a
\]
\[
\frac{\partial_t d}{d} = \frac{a\, \partial_t a}{1-a} = \partial_t \bigl(-a - \log(1-a)\bigr)
\]
\[
d = \frac{e^{-a}}{1-a} \qquad \text{(\`a une constante pr\`es, et le terme constant est bon)}
\]

\smallskip

\noindent
\textbf{(3.5.2)} $\displaystyle d = \frac{t}{a(1-a)}$; $\quad \dfrac{a \cdot d}{t} = b$.

\smallskip

Reportant in (3.5.1), one trouve
\[
\partial_t d = t\, \partial_t b, \quad \text{i.e.\ 3.5}.
\]

\smallskip

J'ai also satisfies that $F'(E_6) = 2^9 \cdot 3^4$, [$F'(E_7) = 2^9 \cdot 3^6 \cdot 5^2$], and, with the aide of a machine, $F'(E_8) = 2^9 \cdot 3^8 \cdot 5^7 \cdot 7$. [Je n'ai not eu the courage of calculer $F_4$, $F_6$.] Notons this verification. one has proves ta conjecture.

\section{Complements}

\subsection*{4.1}

The result suivant precise 1.3(ii).

Let $W$ a group of Coxeter finite of diagram of Dynkin $D$ irreducible. Let $\leq$ a ordre total on $D$, and $\alpha$ a racine. For each chambre $C$ of which $\alpha$ is racine simple, let $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_\ell$ the racines simples associees a $C$, in the ordre $\leq$, and let
\[
\varepsilon(C) = r_\alpha\, r_{\alpha_1} \cdots \widehat{r}_\alpha \cdots r_{\alpha_\ell}
\]
(the product of the $r_{\alpha_i}$ in the ordre $\leq$, sauf that $r_\alpha$ is mis devant).

\begin{proposition}[4.2]
the map $C \mapsto \varepsilon(C)$ of the set of the chambres of which $\alpha$ is racine simple in the set of the transformations of Coxeter is bijective.
\end{proposition}

the injectivite se proves as 1.3(iv): with the notations of \textit{loc cit}, let $w \in W_d$ envoyant $C'$ on $C''$. It respecte $\alpha$ and the ordre of the murs, therefore respecte $\varepsilon(C') = \varepsilon(C'')$. Puisque $w$ centralise a transformation of Coxeter and fixe a racine, $w = 1$ (1.3(i) and (v)).

\smallskip

It reste a compter:

\smallskip

(a) There is $|W|/h$ transformations of Coxeter (1.3(i)).

\smallskip

(b) Si $k_\alpha$ is the nombre of sommets of the diagram of Dynkin corresponding a racines conjuguees a $\alpha$, then $\alpha$ a $k_\alpha h$ conjugues under $W$ (1.3(v)). The nombre of couples (racine conjuguee a $\alpha$; chambre of which $\beta$ is racine simple) is $W \cdot k_\alpha$; the nombre of chambres of which $\alpha$ is racine simple is therefore $|W|/h$.
