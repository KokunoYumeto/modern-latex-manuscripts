\title{Hodge Theory I}
\author{Pierre Deligne}
\date{Actes, Congres intern. math., 1970, Tome 1, pp. 425--430}
\maketitle

The purpose of this note is to give a heuristic dictionary between statements in $l$-adic cohomology and statements in Hodge theory. This dictionary has, among its sources, Serre's work \cite{Serre} and Grothendieck's conjectural theory of motives \cite{Demazure}. Up to now it has mainly served to formulate conjectures in Hodge theory, and at times it has suggested proofs of them.

\section{}
\begin{definition}\label{def:p9-1.1}
A mixed Hodge structure $H$ consists of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] a finitely generated $\Z$-module $H_\Z$, the integral lattice;
\item[(b)] a finite increasing filtration $W$ on $H_\Q=H_\Z\otimes\Q$, the weight filtration;
\item[(c)] a finite decreasing filtration $F$ on $H_\C=H_\Z\otimes\C$, the Hodge filtration.
\end{enumerate}
These data are subject to the following axiom: on $\Gr_W(H_\C)$ there is a unique bigrading by subspaces $H^{p,q}$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\displaystyle \Gr_W^n(H_\C)=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}H^{p,q}$;
\item[(ii)] the filtration $F$ induces on $\Gr_W(H_\C)$ the filtration
\[
F^p=\bigoplus_{p'\ge p}H^{p',q'};
\]
\item[(iii)] $H^{p,q}=\overline{H^{q,p}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

A morphism $f:H\to H'$ is a homomorphism $f:H_\Z\to H'_\Z$ such that $f:H_\Q\to H'_\Q$ and $f_\C:H_\C\to H'_\C$ are compatible respectively with the filtrations $W$ and $F$.

The Hodge numbers of $H$ are the integers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-1.2}
h^{p,q}=\dim_\C H^{p,q}=h^{q,p}.
\end{equation}
We say that $H$ is pure of weight $n$ if $h^{p,q}=0$ for $p+q\neq n$, equivalently if $\Gr_i^W(H)=0$ for $i\neq n$. We also call such an object a Hodge structure of weight $n$.

The Tate Hodge structure $\Z(1)$ is the Hodge structure of weight $-2$, purely of type $(-1,-1)$, for which $\Z(1)_\C=\C$ and
\[
\Z(1)_\Z=2\pi i\Z=\Ker(\exp:\C\to\C^*)\subset\C.
\]
Put $\Z(n)=\Z(1)^{\otimes n}$.

The mixed Hodge structures form an abelian category. If $f:H\to H'$ is a morphism, then $f_\Q$ and $f_\C$ are strictly compatible with the filtrations $W$ and $F$ \cite[2.3.5]{Hodge2}.

\section{}
Let $A$ be a normal integral ring of finite type over $\Z$, let $K$ be its field of fractions, and let $\overline K$ be an algebraic closure of $K$. Let $K^{nr}$ be the largest subextension of $\overline K$ unramified at every prime ideal of $A$. One knows, or one defines,
\[
\pi_1(\Spec A,\overline K)=\Gal(K^{nr}/K).
\]
For every closed point $x$ of $\Spec A$, defined by a maximal ideal $m_x$ of $A$, the residue field $k_x=A/m_x$ is finite. The point $x$ defines a conjugacy class of Frobenius substitutions $\varphi_x\in \pi_1(\Spec A,\overline K)$. Put $q_x=\#k_x$ and $F_x=\varphi_x^{-1}$.

Let $K$ be a field of finite type over the prime field of characteristic $p$, let $\overline K$ be an algebraic closure of $K$, let $l\neq p$ be prime, and let $H$ be a finitely generated $\Z_l$-module, or $\Q_l$-vector space, endowed with a continuous action $\rho$ of $\Gal(\overline K/K)$. We shall always suppose below that there exists $A$ as above, with $l$ invertible in $A$, such that $\rho$ factors through
\[
\pi_1(\Spec A,\overline K)=\Gal(K^{nr}/K).
\]
We say that $H$ is pure of weight $n$ if, for every closed point $x$ of a nonempty open subset of $\Spec A$, the eigenvalues $\alpha$ of $F_x$ acting on $H$ are algebraic integers all of whose complex conjugates have absolute value $q_x^{n/2}$.

\begin{principle}\label{pr:p9-2.1}
If the Galois module $H$ comes from algebraic geometry, then on $H\otimes_{\Z_l}\Q_l$ there is a unique increasing filtration $W$, invariant under Galois, such that $\Gr_n^W(H)$ is pure of weight $n$.

One may also expect $\Gr_n^W(H)$ to be semisimple.
\end{principle}

When resolution of singularities is available, one can often give a conjectural definition of $W$, whose correctness follows from the Weil conjectures \cite{Weil}; see section \ref{sec:p9-6}.

Let $\mu_{l^\infty}$ be the subgroup of $\overline K^*$ formed by the roots of unity. The Tate module
\[
\Z_l(1)=\Hom(\Q_l/\Z_l,\mu_{l^\infty})
\]
is pure of weight $-2$. Put $\Z_l(n)=\Z_l(1)^{\otimes n}$.

It is immediate that every morphism $f:H\to H'$ is strictly compatible with the weight filtration.

Principle \ref{pr:p9-2.1} agrees with the fact that every extension of $\mathbf G_m$ (weight $-2$) by an abelian variety (weight $-1>-2$) is trivial.

\section{Translation}\label{sec:p9-3}
The Galois modules that occur in $l$-adic cohomology have, over $\C$, mixed Hodge structures as their analogues. We have moreover the following dictionary:
\[
\begin{array}{|l|l|}
\hline
\text{module pure of weight } n & \text{Hodge structure of weight } n\\
\hline
\text{weight filtration} & \text{weight filtration}\\
\hline
\text{Galois-compatible homomorphism} & \text{morphism}\\
\hline
\text{Tate module }\Z_l(1)&\text{Tate Hodge structure }\Z(1)\\
\hline
\end{array}
\]

\section{}\label{sec:p9-4}
Let $X$ be a complex algebraic variety, i.e. a scheme of finite type over $\C$, assumed separated. There is a subfield $K$ of $\C$, of finite type over $\Q$, over which $X$ can be defined; thus $X$ is obtained by extension of scalars from a $K$-scheme $X'$. Let $\overline K$ be the algebraic closure of $K$ in $\C$. Then $\Gal(\overline K/K)$ acts on the $l$-adic cohomology groups $H^*(X_{\overline K},\Z_l)$, and
\[
H^*(X(\C),\Z)\otimes\Z_l=H^*(X,\Z_l)=H^*(X'_{\overline K},\Z_l).
\]

By section \ref{sec:p9-3}, one should expect the groups $H^n(X(\C),\Z)$ to carry natural mixed Hodge structures. This can in fact be proved; see \cite[3.2.5]{Hodge2} for the case in which $X$ is smooth. The proof is algebraic, starting from classical Hodge theory \cite{Weyl}. For $X$ projective and smooth, the Weil conjectures imply that $H^n(X_{\overline K},\Q_l)$ is pure of weight $n$, while classical Hodge theory endows $H^n(X(\C),\Z)$ with a Hodge structure of weight $n$. For every morphism $f:X\to Y$ and for $K$ large enough, the pullback $f^*:H^*(Y_{\overline K},\Z_l)\to H^*(X_{\overline K},\Z_l)$ commutes with Galois. Likewise, $f^*:H^*(Y(\C),\Z)\to H^*(X(\C),\Z)$ is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures.

For $X$ smooth, the cohomology class in $H^{2n}(X_{\overline K},\Z_l(n))$ of an algebraic cycle $Z$ of codimension $n$, defined over $K$, is Galois invariant, i.e. it defines
\[
c(Z)\in \Hom_{\Gal}(\Z_l(-n),H^{2n}(X_{\overline K},\Z_l)).
\]
Similarly, the cohomology class $c(Z)\in H^{2n}(X(\C),\Z)$ is purely of type $(n,n)$, i.e. it corresponds to
\[
c(Z)\in \Hom_{\mathrm{Hodge}}(\Z(-n),H^{2n}(X(\C),\Z)).
\]

\section{}\label{sec:p9-5}
If $f:H\to H'$ is a Galois-compatible morphism between $\Q_l$-vector spaces of different weights, then $f=0$. Likewise, if $f:H\to H'$ is a morphism of pure mixed Hodge structures of different weights, then $f$ is torsion. A more useful observation is the following.

\begin{scholium}\label{sch:p9-5.1}
Let $H$ and $H'$ be Hodge structures of weights $n$ and $n'$ with $n>n'$. Let $f:H_\Q\to H'_\Q$ be a homomorphism such that $f_\C:H_\C\to H'_\C$ respects $F$. Then $f=0$.
\end{scholium}

\section{}\label{sec:p9-6}
Let $\overline X$ be a smooth projective variety over $\C$, let $D=\bigcup D_i$ be a normal crossings divisor in $\overline X$, a sum of smooth divisors, and let $j$ be the inclusion in $\overline X$ of $U=\overline X-D$. For $Q\subset[1,n]$, put $D_Q=\bigcap_{i\in Q}D_i$.

In $l$-adic cohomology we have canonically
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-6.1}
\mathscr R^qj_*\Z_l=\bigoplus_{|Q|=q}(i_Q)_*\Z_l(-q),
\end{equation}
and the Leray spectral sequence for $j$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-6.2}
{}'E_2^{p,q}=\bigoplus_{|Q|=q}H^p(D_Q,\Q_l)\otimes \Z_l(-q)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(U,\Q_l).
\end{equation}

By the Weil conjectures \cite{Weil}, $H^p(D_Q,\Q_l)$ is pure of weight $p$, hence ${}'E_2^{p,q}$ is pure of weight $p+2q$. Being a quotient of a subobject of ${}'E_2^{p,q}$, ${}'E_r^{p,q}$ is again pure of weight $p+2q$. By Scholium \ref{sch:p9-5.1}, $d_r=0$ for $r\ge3$, since the weights $p+2q$ and $p+2q-r+2$ of ${}'E_r^{p,q}$ and ${}'E_r^{p+r,q-r+1}$ are different. Thus ${}'E_3^{p,q}={}'E_\infty^{p,q}$. Up to a reindexing, the weight filtration of $H^*(U,\Q_l)$ is the abutment of \eqref{eq:p9-6.2}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-6.3}
\Gr_n^W(H^k(U,\Q_l))={}'E_2^{2k-n,n-k}.
\end{equation}

\section{}\label{sec:p9-7}
For the usual topology and integral cohomology, the Leray spectral sequence for $j$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-7.1}
{}'E_2^{p,q}=\bigoplus_{|Q|=q}H^p(D_Q,\Z)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(U,\Z).
\end{equation}
Since each $D_Q$ is a nonsingular projective variety, ${}'E_2^{p,q}$ carries a Hodge structure of weight $p$. Put $E_2^{p,q}={}'E_2^{p,q}\otimes\Z(-q)$, a Hodge structure of weight $p+2q$. As an abelian group, $E_2^{p,q}={}'E_2^{p,q}$; it is useful to regard \eqref{eq:p9-7.1} as a spectral sequence with initial term $E_2^{p,q}$. By section \ref{sec:p9-3}, one expects $d_2:E_2^{p,q}\to E_2^{p+2,q-1}$ to be a morphism of Hodge structures. This is proved by interpreting $d_2$ as a Gysin morphism. Thus $E_3^{p,q}$ has a Hodge structure of weight $p+2q$. Again by section \ref{sec:p9-3}, one expects that, modulo torsion, the spectral sequence \eqref{eq:p9-6.2} degenerates at $E_3$ and that the vanishing of $d_r$ for $r\ge3$ is an application of Scholium \ref{sch:p9-5.1}. This program is carried out in \cite[3.2]{Hodge2}. The weight filtration of $H^*(U,\Q)$ is defined there as the abutment of \eqref{eq:p9-7.1}, up to the reindexing \eqref{eq:p9-6.3}.

In fact, in order to put mixed Hodge structures on cohomology groups $H^*$, the key point so far has always been to find a spectral sequence $E$ abutting to $H^*$ such that the $l$-adic analogue of $E_2^{p,q}$ is conjecturally pure, of weight $p+2q$. Then $E_2^{p,q}$ should carry a natural Hodge structure of weight $p+2q$, and the filtration $W$ is the abutment of $E$.

\section{}\label{sec:p9-8}
Let $\Spec(V)$ be the spectrum of a Henselian discrete valuation ring, with fraction field $K$ and residue field $k$ of finite type over the prime field of characteristic $p$. Let $\overline K$ be an algebraic closure of $K$, and let $H$ be a finite-dimensional $\Q_l$-vector space, $l\neq p$, on which $\Gal(\overline K/K)$ acts continuously. By Grothendieck \cite[Appendix]{Tate}, a finite-index subgroup of the inertia group $I$ acts unipotently. After replacing $V$ by a finite extension, we reduce to the case where all of $I$ acts unipotently, the semistable case. The action then factors through the largest pro-$l$ quotient $I_l$ of $I$, canonically isomorphic to $\Z_l(1)$.

\begin{principle}\label{pr:p9-8.1}
In the semistable case, if the Galois module $H$ comes from algebraic geometry, then there is a unique increasing filtration $W$ of $H$, the weight filtration, such that $I$ acts trivially on $\Gr_n^W(H)$ and such that $\Gr_n^W(H)$, as a Galois module under $\Gal(\overline k/k)\subset \Gal(\overline K/K)/I$, is pure of weight $n$.
\end{principle}

Compare this with Principle \ref{pr:p9-2.1} and with the appendix to \cite{Tate}. When resolution of singularities is available, one can sometimes give a conjectural definition of $W$ whose validity follows from the Weil conjectures. Using resolution and Weil, it is often easy to show at least that $H$ can be broken up into Galois modules, under $\Gal(\overline k/k)$, that are pure.

Assume that $H$ is semistable. For $T\in I$, define $\log T$ by
\[
-\sum_{n>0}(\mathrm{Id}-T)^n/n.
\]
The map $(T,x)\mapsto \log T(x)$ identifies with a homomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-8.2}
M:\Z_l(1)\otimes_{\Z_l}H\to H.
\end{equation}
Since $\Z_l(1)$ has weight $-2$, necessarily
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-8.3}
M(\Z_l(1)\otimes W_n(H))\subset W_{n-2}(H),
\end{equation}
and $M$ induces
\begin{equation}\label{eq:p9-8.4}
\Gr_n(M):\Z_l(1)\otimes \Gr_n^W(H)\to \Gr_{n-2}^W(H).
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}\label{def:p9-8.5}
If $X$ is a nonsingular projective variety over an algebraically closed field $k$, define
\[
L:\Z_l(-1)\otimes H^*(X,\Z_l)\to H^*(X,\Z_l)
\]
to be cup product with the cohomology class of a hyperplane section.
\end{definition}

There is a formal analogy between $L$ and $M$. Just as $M$ is defined by an action of $\Z_l(1)$, one may regard $L$ as defined by an action of $\Z(-1)$. The operator $L$ raises degree by $2$, and $\Gr_n M$ in \eqref{eq:p9-8.4} lowers it by $2$.

\section{}\label{sec:p9-9}
Let $D$ be the unit disk, $D^*=D-\{0\}$, and let
\[
\begin{CD}
X @>{\subset}>> \mathbf P^r(\C)\times D\\
@V{f}VV @VV{\mathrm{pr}_2}V\\
D @= D
\end{CD}
\]
be a family of projective varieties parametrized by $D$, with $f$ proper and $f|_{D^*}$ smooth.

Keep the notation of section \ref{sec:p9-8}. In the analogy between a Henselian trait and a small neighborhood of $0$ in the complex line, one has the following dictionary:
\[
\begin{array}{|l|l|}
\hline
D&\Spec(V)\\
\hline
D^*&\Spec(K)\\
\hline
\text{a universal cover }\widetilde D^*\text{ of }D^*&\Spec(\overline K)\\
\hline
\pi_1(D^*)&\text{inertia group }I\\
\hline
\pi_1(D^*)=\Z\simeq\Z(1)&I_l=\Z_l(1)\\
\hline
X&\text{projective scheme over }\Spec(V)\\
\hline
X^*=f^{-1}(D^*)&X_K\\
\hline
X_D=X\times_D\widetilde D^*&X_{\overline K}\\
\hline
\mathscr R^if_*\Z|_{D^*}&H^i(X_{\overline K},\Z_l)\\
\hline
H^i(X_t,\Z)&H^i(X_{\overline K},\Z_l)\\
\hline
\end{array}
\]

Note that $X_D$ is homotopy equivalent to each fiber $X_t=f^{-1}(t)$ for $t\in D^*$. Thus $H^i(X_t,\Z)$ again corresponds to $H^i(X_{\overline K},\Z_l)$, and the action of $I$ corresponds to the monodromy transformation $T$.

Here too, a finite-index subgroup of $\pi_1(D^*)$ acts unipotently on $H^i(X_t,\Q)=H^i(X_D,\Q)$. Put ourselves in the semistable case, where all of $\pi_1(D^*)$ acts unipotently; this amounts to replacing $D$ by a finite cover. Let $T$ be the action of the canonical generator of $\pi_1(D^*)$.

By sections \ref{sec:p9-3} and \ref{sec:p9-8}, one expects $H^i(X_t,\Q)\simeq H^i(X_D,\Q)$ to be endowed with an increasing filtration $W$, the groups $\Gr_n^W H^i(X_t,\Q)$ to be endowed with Hodge structures of weight $n$, and
\[
\log T(W_n)\subset W_{n-2},
\]
with $\log T$ inducing a morphism of Hodge structures
\[
M:\Z(-1)\otimes\Gr_n^W(H^i)\to \Gr_{n-2}^W(H^i).
\]

One would like \eqref{eq:p9-8.2}, not only \eqref{eq:p9-8.3} and \eqref{eq:p9-8.4}, to have an analogue. In fact, for each tangent vector $u$ to $D$ at $0$, one can define a mixed Hodge structure $\mathscr H_u$ on $H^i(X_t,\Z)$. The filtration $W$ and the Hodge structures on $\Gr_n^W(\mathscr H_u)$ are independent of $u$, and the dependence of $\mathscr H_u$ on $u$ is expressed simply in terms of $T$. In analogy with \eqref{eq:p9-8.2}, one finds that, for every $u$, $\log T$ induces a homomorphism of mixed Hodge structures
\[
M:\Z(1)\otimes H^i(X,\Z)\to H^i(X,\Z).
\]

Finally, the analogy with Definition \ref{def:p9-8.5} is not misleading, though here the assumption that $f|_{D^*}$ is proper and smooth is probably essential. One proves that
\[
(\log T)^k:\Gr_{n+k}^W(H^n(X_t,\Q))\to \Gr_{n-k}^W(H^n(X_t,\Q))
\]
is an isomorphism for every $k$; see \cite[IV, 6, corollary to Theorem 5]{Weyl}. This characterizes the filtration $W$. Up to now, an analogue of the Hodge positivity theorem \cite[IV, 7, corollary to Theorem 7]{Weyl} is available only in very special cases. One hopes that the mixed structures $\mathscr H$ determine the asymptotic behavior, as $t\to0$, of the family of pure structures $H^i(X_t,\Z)$ for $t\in D^*$.
