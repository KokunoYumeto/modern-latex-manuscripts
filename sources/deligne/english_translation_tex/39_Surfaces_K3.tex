Rudakov and Shafarevitch [14] (cf. also Nygaard [12]) have etabli that the surfaces K3 n'admettent not of champs of vecteurs not nuls.

the object of this expose is of prouver, a partir of [14], that every surface K3 polarisee en car. $p > 0$ se releve en car. zero (1.8). The proof repose on the fait that the result of Rudakov-Shafarevitch entraine, for the surfaces K3, the existence of a bonne theorie of deformations, permettant in particular of ``controler'' the lieu of the variety formelle verselle or se deforme a sheaf inversible not trivial given. The seuls ingredients utilises are of a part the relations habituelles, for the de Rham cohomology, between the connexion of Gauss-Manin and the operation of Kodaira-Spencer, of other part the existence of a class of Chern cristalline for the sheaves inversibles.

One designe by $k$ a field algebriquement clos of car. $p > 0$, and the one denotes $W = W(k)$ the ring of the vecteurs of Witt on $k$.

\item Let $T = T_{X_0/k} = (\Omega_{X_0/k}^1)^{\vee}$ the fiber tangent a $X_0$. one has $H^i(X_0, T) = 0$ si $i = 0$ or $2$, and $\dim_k H^1(X_0, T) = 20$.

\begin{proof}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1.1}
T_{X_0/k} \xrightarrow{\sim} \Omega_{X_0/k}^1,

and by sequence $H^0(X_0, \Omega_{X_0/k}^1) = 0$, therefore $H^2(X_0, \Omega_{X_0/k}^1) = 0$ by duality of Serre. of other part, one has $H^1(X_0, \mathcal{O}) = 0$ and $H^2(X_0, \mathcal{O}) \simeq k$ by definition of a surface K3. Recall moreover (SGA~5~VII~4.11) that $\sum_{i+j=n} (-1)^{i+j} h^{ij} = c_2$ and that $c_2 = 24$ by Riemann-Roch. Therefore $h^{11} = 20$, of or b), compte tenu of \eqref{eq:1.1.1}. It follows of a) that the spaces $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/k)$ are of dimension $1, 0, 22, 0, 1$ for $i = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4$. the assertion c) en decoule grace a the exact sequence ``of the coefficients universels'' [2, VII 1.1.1]:
\[
0 \to H^i_{\cris}(X_0/W) \otimes_W k \to H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/k) \to \Tor^W_1(H^{i+1}_{\cris}(X_0/W), k) \to 0. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{}

Let $L_0$ a sheaf inversible on $X_0$. Notons $\Def(X_0, L_0)$ the functor on the category $\mathcal{A}$ of the $W$-algebras artiniennes local of field residuel $k$ associant a each object $A$ of $\mathcal{A}$ the set of the classes of isomorphie of couples $(X, L)$ forms of a deformation plate (therefore smooth) $X$ of $X_0$ on $A$ and of a prolongement of $L_0$ en a sheaf inversible $L$ on $X$. Notons of other part $\Def(X_0)$ the functor associant a each $A \in \Ob\,\mathcal{A}$ the set of the classes of isomorphie of deformations plates of $X_0$ on $A$: of after \ref{cor:1.2}, this functor is (pro-)represente by $S$. one has a morphism ``oubli of the prolongement of $L_0$'':
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.4.1}
\Def(X_0, L_0) \to \Def(X_0).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:1.5}
The functor $\Def(X_0, L_0)$ is pro-representable, and the morphism \eqref{eq:1.4.1} is a immersion closed, defined by a equation.
\end{proposition}

The premiere assertion signifie that there exists a more grand under-scheme formel closed
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.5.0}
\Sigma(L_0) \subset S
\end{equation}
such that $L_0$ se prolonge en a sheaf inversible to the-dessus of $\mathcal{X} \times_S \Sigma(L_0)$, and that this prolongement is unique.

One verifies aisement that the functor $\Def(X_0, L_0)$ satisfait to the conditions of Schlessinger of existence of a enveloppe. One dispose therefore of a scheme formel $S' = \Spf(R')$, or $R'$ is a $W$-algebra local noetherienne complete of field residuel $k$, and of a deformation $(\mathcal{X}', \mathcal{L}')$ of $(X_0, L_0)$ on $S'$ such that, for every $A \in \Ob\,\mathcal{A}$, the fleche associated
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.5.1}
\Hom(R', A) \to \Def(X_0, L_0)(A)
\end{equation}
let surjective, and bijective for $A = k[\epsilon]$, $\epsilon^2 = 0$. Let $R$ the ring of $S$. As $S$ pro-represente $\Def(X_0)$, $\mathcal{X}'$ defines a homomorphisme $u: R \to R'$ such that the compose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.5.2}
\Hom(R', A) \xrightarrow{\eqref{eq:1.5.1}} \Def(X_0, L_0)(A) \xrightarrow{\eqref{eq:1.4.1}} \Def(X_0)(A) = \Hom(R, A)
\end{equation}
let $\Hom(u, A)$. For etablir the premiere assertion of \ref{prop:1.5}, it is enough therefore of prouver that $u$ is surjective, car then \eqref{eq:1.5.2} will be injective, therefore \eqref{eq:1.5.1} bijective. of after a lemma bien connu [15, 1.1], it revient to the same of prouver that, si $\mathfrak{m}$ (resp.~$\mathfrak{m}'$) is the ideal maximal of $R$ (resp.~$R'$), the map $\mathfrak{m}/pR + \mathfrak{m}^2 \to \mathfrak{m}'/pR' + \mathfrak{m}'^2$ induite by $u$ is surjective, or again that the map lineaire tangente a the origine a \eqref{eq:1.4.1},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.5.3}
\Hom(u, k[\epsilon]): \Def(X_0, L_0)(k[\epsilon]) \to \Def(X_0)(k[\epsilon])
\end{equation}
is injective. Let $T'$ the sheaf on $X_0$ of the automorphismes of the deformation trivial of $(X_0, L_0)$ to the-dessus of $k[\epsilon]$. one has a exact sequence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.5.4}
0 \to \mathcal{O}_{X_0} \to T' \to T \to 0,
\end{equation}
or $T' \to T$ is defined by oubli of $L_0$, $T = T_{X_0}$ being considered as sheaf of the automorphismes of the deformation trivial of $X_0$ to the-dessus of $k[\epsilon]$. It is standard that \eqref{eq:1.5.3} s'identifie canonically a the fleche $H^1(X_0, T') \to H^1(X_0, T)$ deduite of $T' \to T$. As $H^1(X_0, \mathcal{O}) = 0$, the exact sequence of cohomology of \eqref{eq:1.5.4} shows therefore that \eqref{eq:1.5.3} is injective.

It reste a prouver that the immersion closed $S' \hookrightarrow S$ is defined by a seule equation, i.e. that the ideal $I = \Ker(u)$ is monogene. For that, considerons $S'' = \Spf(R/\mathfrak{m}I)$; c'is a epaississement of $S'$ in $S$, of ideal of carre zero $I/\mathfrak{m}I$. the obstruction a etendre the sheaf $\mathcal{L}'$ defined more haut to the-dessus of $\mathcal{X} \times_S S''$ is a element
\[
a \in H^2(X_0, \mathcal{O}_{X_0}) \otimes I/\mathfrak{m}I,
\]
that one regardera as a element of $I/\mathfrak{m}I$ by the choix of a base of $H^2(X_0, \mathcal{O}_{X_0})$. Let $Z = \Spf(R/\mathfrak{m}I + (f))$ or $f \in I$ releve $a$. one has therefore $S' \subset Z \subset S'' \subset S$, and by construction (and fonctorialite of the obstruction), $\mathcal{L}'$ se prolonge a $\mathcal{X} \times_S Z$. But the property universelle of $S'$ entraine that $S' = Z$, i.e. $\mathfrak{m}I + (f) = I$. Therefore, by Nakayama, $f$ generated $I$, this which acheve the proof.

We pouvons maintenant enoncer the result principal of the expose:

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:1.6}
Let $L_0$ a sheaf inversible not trivial on $X_0$. Then, with the notations of \ref{prop:1.5}, the scheme formel $\Sigma(L_0)$, pro-representant $\Def(X_0, L_0)$, is plat on $W$, of dimension relative $19$.
\end{theorem}

En of other termes, si $f$ is a equation of $\Sigma(L_0)$ in $S$ (\ref{prop:1.5}), $p$ ne divise not $f$. That signifie again that $\Sigma(L_0)$ ne contient not the reduction $S_0$ of $S \bmod p$, i.e. that $L_0$ ne can se prolonger to the-dessus of $\mathcal{X} \times_S S_0$.

The proof of \ref{thm:1.6} will be datum to the n2. We terminerons this numero by some consequences of \ref{thm:1.6}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:1.7}
Let $L_0$ a sheaf inversible not trivial on $X_0$. There exists a trait $T$ finite on $W$, a deformation of $X_0$ en a scheme formel $\mathcal{X}$ plat on $T$, and a prolongement of $L_0$ en a sheaf inversible $\mathcal{L}$ on $\mathcal{X}$.
\end{corollary}

It s'agit of prouver that there exists a $W$-morphism $T \to \Sigma(L_0)$, or $T$ is a trait finite on $W$. As $p$ is not diviseur of zero in the ring $R'$ of $\Sigma(L_0)$, there exists (EGA~0$_{\mathrm{IV}}$~16.4.1) of the elements $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ of the ideal maximal of $R'$ formant with $p$ a systeme of parametres of $R'$ (therefore $n = 19$). The quotient $B = R'/(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ is quasi-finite on $W$, therefore finite on $W$. There exists by sequence a $W$-homomorphisme local of $B$ in a ring of valuation discrete complet $C$ finite on $W$, and the homomorphisme compose $R' \to B \to C$ repond a the question.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:1.8}
Let $L_0$ a sheaf inversible ample on $X_0$. There exists a trait $T$ finite on $W$, a deformation of $X_0$ en a scheme proper and smooth $X$ on $T$, and a prolongement of $L_0$ en a sheaf inversible ample $\mathcal{L}$ on $X$.
\end{corollary}

Appliquant the theorem of algebrisation of Grothendieck (EGA~III~5.4.5), one deduces of \ref{cor:1.7} the theorem of lifting annonce.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:1.9}
One ignore si every surface K3 on $k$ se releve en a scheme proper and smooth on $W$. Ogus [13, 2] shows that: a) every surface K3 on $k$ se releve on $W$ sauf can-be in the cas ``superspecial'', not elliptique, which en fait n'exists not si the one admet the conjecture of Artin [1]; b) si $p > 2$, every surface K3 on $k$ se releve on $W$. For the instant therefore, seul the cas particulier of \ref{cor:1.8} or $p = 2$ and $X_0$ is superspeciale n'is not absorbe by these results. Signalons of other part that the article of Ogus precite contient of the complements interessants on the structure of $\Sigma(L_0)$, cf. also the expose suivant for the cas or $X_0$ is ordinaire.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:1.10}
Si $k$ is the algebraic closure of a finite field on lequel the surface $X_0$ is defined, the Frobenius corresponding agit in such a way semisimple on $H^2_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_0, \mathbb{Q}_{\ell})$ ($\ell$ prime a $p$).
\end{corollary}

That resulte of [5]: with the notations of \ref{cor:1.8}, the fiber generique of $X$ is a surface K3 (the fait for a surface of be a surface K3 is stable by generisation, car it s'exprime by ``$K$ algebriquement equivalent a zero and $\chi(\mathcal{O}) = 2$''), therefore $X_0$ satisfies the hypothese of [5, 1.2]; the conclusion decoule of [5, 6.6] and of the fait that the action of Frobenius on the $H^1$ $\ell$-adique of a abelian variety over a field finite is semisimple ([16], [11, p.~203]).

\section{DE RHAM COHOMOLOGY OF $\mathcal{X}/S$ AND PROOF OF \ref{thm:1.6}}\label{sec:2}

One conserve the notations of the n1: $X_0$ is a surface K3 on $k$, and $\mathcal{X}/S$ designe sa $W$-deformation universelle (\eqref{eq:1.3}). The reader familier with the de Rham cohomology is invite a sauter the numeros 2.1--2.10, which ne font that rappeler of the faits standard concernant the connexion of Gauss-Manin, the Hodge filtration, the operation of Frobenius, and the class of Chern of a sheaf inversible.

\subsection{}

Notons $\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{\bullet}$ the de Rham complex of the scheme formel relative $\mathcal{X}/S$ (by definition, $\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{\bullet}$ is the projective limit of the modules of differentielles habituels $\mathcal{X} \times_S S'/S'$, or $S'$ parcourt the voisinages infinitesimaux of $\Spec(k)$ in $S$). Let $f: \mathcal{X} \to S$ the projection. By definition, the de Rham cohomology of $\mathcal{X}/S$ is formee of the $\mathcal{O}_S$-modules
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.1}
H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \overset{\text{déf}}{=} R^i f_*(\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{\bullet}),
\end{equation}
tandis that the cohomology of Hodge of $\mathcal{X}/S$ is formee of the $\mathcal{O}_S$-modules
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.2}
H^j(\mathcal{X}, \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^i) \overset{\text{déf}}{=} R^j f_*(\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^i).
\end{equation}

As the $\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}$-modules $\Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^i$ are locally libres of type finite, the $\mathcal{O}_S$-modules \eqref{eq:2.1.1} and \eqref{eq:2.1.2} are coherents en vertu of the finiteness theorem of Grothendieck (EGA~III~3.4.2). one has of other part the spectral sequence habituelle (``spectral sequence of Hodge''):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1.3}
E_1^{i,j} = H^j(\mathcal{X}, \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^i) \Rightarrow H^{i+j}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:2.2}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item The spectral sequence \eqref{eq:2.1.3} degenere en $E_1$; the $\mathcal{O}_S$-modules $H^j(\mathcal{X}, \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^i)$ are libres of type finite, and the fleches canonical $H^j(\mathcal{X}, \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^i) \otimes k \to H^j(X_0, \Omega_{X_0/k}^i)$ are of the isomorphisms.
\item The $\mathcal{O}_S$-modules $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S)$ are libres of type finite, and the fleches canonical $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \otimes k \to H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/k)$ are of the isomorphisms.
\item The cup-product $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \otimes H^{4-i}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \to H^4_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \simeq \mathcal{O}_S$ is a duality parfaite.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

As the tableau of the nombres of Hodge of $X_0/k$ is ``entrelarde of zeros'' (\ref{prop:1.1}~a)), the critere usuel (EGA~III~7.5.4), applique to the functors cohomologiques $M \mapsto H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{X}, \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^i \otimes f^* M)$ entraine aussitot the seconde assertion of a); the premiere en resulte. the assertion b) decoule of a), and implique c), by the duality of Poincare for $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/k)$.

\subsection{}

Notons $\Omega_{S/W}^{\bullet}$ the de Rham complex of the differentielles ``formelles'' of $S/W$: $\Omega_{S/W}^{0} = \varprojlim \mathcal{O}_{S_n}$, $\Omega_{S/W}^{1} = \varprojlim \Omega^1_{S_n/W_n}$, or $S_n$ is the reduction $\bmod p^{n+1}$ of $S/W$, is free on $\mathcal{O}_S$, of base $dt_1, \ldots, dt_{20}$, si $S \simeq \Spf W[[t_1, \ldots, t_{20}]]$. The $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S)$ are equipped with a connexion integrable canonical, the connexion of Gauss-Manin,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.1}
\nabla: H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \to H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \otimes \Omega^1_{S/W}.
\end{equation}
The definition the more simple of \eqref{eq:2.3.1} consiste a paraphraser the construction of Katz-Oda [9], en partant of the extension canonical
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.0}
0 \to f^*\Omega^1_{S/W} \to \Omega^1_{\mathcal{X}/W} \to \Omega^1_{\mathcal{X}/S} \to 0,
\end{equation}
or $\Omega^1_{\mathcal{X}/W}$ is the module of the differentielles complete. One can also utiliser the fait that $H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S)$ is the value en $S$ of a cristal en $\mathcal{O}$-modules on the site cristallin of $S_0/W$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.2}
H^i_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) = R^i(f_0)_{\cris*}(\mathcal{O}_{X_0/W})(S),
\end{equation}
or
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.2.1}
(f_0: \mathcal{X}_0 \to S_0) = f \times_S S_0, \quad S_0 = S \otimes_W k \; (= \Spf(k[[t_1, \ldots, t_{20}]]))
\end{equation}
and $(f_0)_{\cris}: (X_0/W)_{\cris} \to (S_0/W)_{\cris}$ is the morphism corresponding of the sites cristallins: it s'agit ici of a ``variante formelle'' of the result of Berthelot [2, V~3.6], that it is easy of deduire of (loc.~cit.), applique to the morphisms induits by $f$ on the voisinages infinitesimaux of $\Spec(k)$ in $S_0$.

On the a or the other of the definitions precedentes, one sees that the cup-product \ref{prop:2.2}~c) is horizontal: one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.3}
\langle \nabla x, y \rangle + \langle x, \nabla y \rangle = \nabla \langle x, y \rangle,
\end{equation}
quels that let $x, y \in H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S)$.

Notons $F^{\bullet}_{\Hdg} H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S)$ the Hodge filtration of $H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S)$, aboutissement of \eqref{eq:2.1.3}: si $\tau_{\geqslant i} \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{\bullet}$ (resp. $\sigma_{\geqslant i} \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{\bullet}$) designe the de Rham complex tronque (by zero en degree $< i$), the inclusion $\sigma_{\geqslant i} \hookrightarrow \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{\bullet}$ given therefore (en vertu of \ref{prop:2.2}~a)) a isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.4}
H^2(\mathcal{X}, \sigma_{\geqslant i} \Omega_{\mathcal{X}/S}^{\bullet}) \xrightarrow{\sim} F^i_{\Hdg} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S).
\end{equation}

one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.3.5}
H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) = F^0_{\Hdg} \supset F^1_{\Hdg} \supset F^2_{\Hdg} \supset F^3_{\Hdg} = 0;
\end{equation}

Let $p^n$ the more grande power of $p$ divisant $x$, posons $y = p^{-n}x$; the image $y_0$ of $y$ in $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0) = H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(\mathcal{X}/S) \bmod p$ is therefore not zero. of other part, one has again $Fy = py$, $\nabla y = 0$. The premiere of these relations entraine, of after \eqref{eq:2.6.1}, that $y_0 \in F^1_{\Hdg} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0)$. As one has $y_0 \neq 0$ and $\nabla y_0 = 0$, one ne can avoir $y_0 \in F^2_{\Hdg} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0)$, car that contredirait the injectivite of $\gr^2 \nabla: F^2_{\Hdg} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0) \to \Omega^1_{S_0/k} \otimes \gr_{F^1} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0)$ (\ref{cor:2.4.4}). Therefore the image of $y_0$ in $\gr_{F^1} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0)$ is not zero, but as $\nabla y_0 = 0$, that contredit the fait that $\gr^1 \nabla: \gr_{F^1} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0) \to \Omega^1_{S_0/k} \otimes \gr_{F^0} H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X_0/S_0)$ is a isomorphism. This contradiction acheve the proof of \ref{thm:1.6}.


\section{APPENDICE: CLASSES OF CHERN CRISTALLINES AND INTERSECTIONS}\label{sec:3}
\footnote{by P. Deligne and L. Illusie}

The but of this appendice is of etablir that, on a surface proper and smooth on $k$, the nombre of intersection of deux diviseurs is the trace of the cup-product of leurs classes of Chern cristallines (this compatibility ne figure apparemment not in the litterature).

\subsection{}

Let $X$ a scheme proper and smooth on $k$. Recall [2, VI~3.3.6] that one sait associer a all under-scheme closed $Y$ of $X$, smooth on $k$, of codimension $d$ in $X$, a cohomology class
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.1}
c^{\ell}(Y) \in H^{2d}_{\cris}(X/W).
\end{equation}
Si $Y'$, $Y''$ are of the under-schemes fermes smooth of codimensions $d'$, $d''$, se coupant transversalement, one has [2, VI~4.3.15]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.2}
c^{\ell}(Y') \cdot c^{\ell}(Y'') = c^{\ell}(Y' \cap Y'')
\end{equation}
(in $H^{2(d'+d'')}_{\cris}(X/W)$). of other part, si $X'$ is a scheme proper and smooth on $k$, $f: X' \to X$ a $k$-morphism, $Y$ a under-scheme closed smooth of codimension $d$ such that $Y \times_X X'$ let smooth of codimension $d$ in $X'$, then one has [2, VI~4.3.13]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.3}
c^{\ell}(Y \times_X X') = f^* c^{\ell}(Y).
\end{equation}
Enfin, si $a$ is a point rational of $X/k$, one has [2, VII~3.1.7]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.1.4}
\Tr_{X/W}(c^{\ell}(a)) = 1.
\end{equation}
In particular, si $X$ is connected, $c^{\ell}(a)$ ne depend not of $a$, puisque $\Tr_{X/W}: H^{2n}_{\cris}(X/W) \to W$ is a isomorphism ($n = \dim X$) [2, VII~2.1.1].

\subsection{}

A all fiber vector $L$ on $X$ one sait associer of the classes of Chern [3, 2.4]
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.2.1}
c_i(L) \in H^{2i}_{\cris}(X/W),
\end{equation}
nulles for $i > \rg(L)$. These classes dependent functorially of $L$: si $f: X' \to X$ is a $k$-morphism, one has (loc.~cit.)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.2.2}
c_i(f^* L) = f^* c_i(L).
\end{equation}
Si $D$ is a diviseur effective smooth on $X$, one dispose therefore of a part of the cohomology class $c^{\ell}(D) \in H^2_{\cris}(X/W)$, of other part of the class of Chern $c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(D)) \in H^2_{\cris}(X/W)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:3.2.3}
Let $D$ a diviseur effective smooth on $X$. Si $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ is tres ample, one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.2.3.1}
c^{\ell}(D) = c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(D)).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:3.2.4}
Berthelot (not publie) a satisfies that \eqref{eq:3.2.3.1} vaut without the hypothese that $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$ let tres ample. The proof is sufficiently compliquee. For this that we have en vue, \ref{prop:3.2.3} we suffira.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \ref{prop:3.2.3}]
Grace a \eqref{eq:3.1.3} and \eqref{eq:3.2.2}, one se ramene aussitot to the cas or $X$ is a projective space $\mathbb{P}^N_k$ and $D$ a hyperplan. Posons $\mathbb{P}^N_k = X'$, and let $D'$ a hyperplan of $X'$ relevant $D$. one has $H^2_{\cris}(X/W) = H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X'/W)$. of after [2, VI~3.3.5], $c^{\ell}(D)$ is the cohomology class of Rham of $D'$, laquelle se calcule a the aide of the extension of $\mathcal{O}_{X'/W}[-1]$ by $\Omega^{\bullet}_{X'/W}$ given by the de Rham complex of $X'/W$ a poles logarithmiques the long of $D'$. Si $(t_0, \ldots, t_N)$ are of the coordonnees homogenes on $X'$, one deduces facilement that $c^{\ell}(D)$ is the class of the $1$-cocycle $(dt_j/t_j - dt_i/t_i)$ ($i < j$) of the cover standard. of other part, the class of Chern $c_1(\mathcal{O}_{X'}(1)) \in H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X'/W)$ se deduces, of after [3, 2.3], of the cocycle $t_j/t_i$ by the homomorphisme $\dlog: \mathcal{O}_{X'}^* \to \Omega^1_{X'/W}$ [1]. one has therefore bien $c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(D)) = c^{\ell}(D)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:3.3}
Let $X$ a surface proper and smooth on $k$. Si $D_1$, $D_2$ are of the diviseurs on $X$, one has
\[
(D_1 \cdot D_2) = \Tr_{X/W}(c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(D_1)) \cdot c_1(\mathcal{O}_X(D_2)))
\]
(or $(D_1 \cdot D_2)$ designe the nombre of intersection of $D_1$, $D_2$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Si $D_1$, $D_2$ are effective, smooth, tres amples, and transverses, the conclusion resulte of \eqref{eq:3.1.2}, \eqref{eq:3.1.4}, and \ref{prop:3.2.3}. For we ramener a this cas, choisissons a diviseur $H$ tres ample on $X$. For $n$ sufficiently grand, $D_1 + nH$ and $D_2 + nH$ are tres amples. By Bertini, there exists therefore of the diviseurs effective smooth, tres amples, $D_1'$, $D_1''$, $D_2'$, $D_2''$ such that
\[
D_1 = D_1' - D_1'', \quad D_2 = D_2' - D_2'',
\]
and the one can supposer moreover that the couples $(D_1', D_1'')$, $(D_1, D_2')$, $(D_1', D_2'')$, $(D_1'', D_2')$, $(D_1'', D_2'')$ are transverses. By additivite of $c_1$ and bilinearite of $(\cdot)$, one is ramene to the cas particulier envisage, this which acheve the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{}

Let new $X$ a scheme proper and smooth on $k$. One ignore si, for deux under-schemes fermes smooth of $X$, similarly codimension, the fait of be algebriquement equivalents entraine that they have same cohomology class cristalline (one the sait only for of the under-schemes of dimension $0$, cf.~\eqref{eq:3.1.4}!). En revanche, si
\[
\NS(X) = \Pic(X)/\Pic^0(X)
\]
designe the group of Neron-Severi of $X$, the homomorphisme
\[
c_1: \Pic(X) \to H^2_{\cris}(X/W)
\]
s'annule on $\Pic^0(X)$, car $\Pic^0(X)$ is $p$-divisible and $H^2_{\cris}(X/W)$ of type finite on $W$, therefore given by passage to the quotient a homomorphisme
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.4.1}
c_1: \NS(X) \to H^2_{\cris}(X/W).
\end{equation}

Supposons maintenant that $X$ Let surface. of after the theorem of the index of Hodge, the form of intersection on $\NS(X) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ is not degeneree. Compte tenu of \ref{thm:3.3}, it follows that the homomorphisme
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3.4.2}
c_1 \otimes \mathbb{Q}: \NS(X) \otimes \mathbb{Q} \to H^2_{\cris}(X/W) \otimes \mathbb{Q}
\end{equation}
is injective.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:3.4}
the homomorphisme $c_1: \NS(X) \to H^2_{\cris}(X/W)$ is injective.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
That resulte immediatement of the injectivite of \eqref{eq:3.4.2}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:3.5}
One can montrer [6, II~5.10] that \eqref{eq:3.4.1} defines a injection of $\NS(X) \otimes \mathbb{Z}_p$ in $H^2_{\cris}(X/W)$. It n'is without doute not vrai en general that \eqref{eq:3.4.1} induise a injection of $\NS(X)/p\NS(X)$ in $H^2_{\cris}(X/W)/pH^2_{\cris}(X/W)$, i.e. that the map class of Chern en de Rham cohomology envoie injectivement $\NS(X)/p\NS(X)$ in $H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(X/k)$. C'is cependant the cas si $H^1_{\cris}(X/W)$ is without twisting and that the spectral sequence of Hodge toward of Rham of $X/k$ degenere en $E_1$, cf.~[13, 1.4] and [6, II~5.18], conditions realisees by example by a surface K3.
\end{remark}
