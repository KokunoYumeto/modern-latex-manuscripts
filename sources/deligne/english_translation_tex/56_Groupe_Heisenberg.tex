\section*{1}

Let $H$ the group of Heisenberg complex, extension of $\mathbb{C}^2$ by $\mathbb{C}^*$. It admet of the coordonnees $(x, y, \lambda)$, the loi of group being given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.1}
(x, y, \lambda)(x', y', \lambda') = \bigl(x + x', y + y', \lambda\lambda'\exp(\pi i(xy' - x'y))\bigr).\tag{1.1}
\end{equation}

the involution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.2}
T \colon (x, y, \lambda) \mapsto (-\bar{x}, -\bar{y}, \bar{\lambda})\tag{1.2}
\end{equation}
is a automorphism of $H$.

The systeme of coordonnees $x, y, \lambda \colon H \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathbb{C}^2 \times \mathbb{C}^*$ met en evidence the section $\mathbb{C}^2 \times \{1\}$ of the projection of $H$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$. On this section $T(g) = g^{-1}$. Each line $D \subset \mathbb{C}^2$ a for lifting by this section a under-group a parametre of $H$, that one notera again $D$. The commutateur $(u, v) = uvu^{-1}v^{-1}$ of deux elements $u = (u_1, u_2)$ and $v = (v_1, v_2)$ of $\mathbb{C}^2$ is $\exp(2\pi i\langle u, v \rangle) \in \mathbb{C}^*$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1.3}
\langle u, v \rangle = u_1v_2 - u_2v_1.\tag{1.3}
\end{equation}

Let $H_\mathbb{R}$ the form reelle: $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$, $|\lambda| = 1$. The group $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ agit on $H$ and $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ agit on $H_\mathbb{R}$, identifie ensemblistement a $\mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathrm{U}^1$.

One sait (theorem of Stone--von Neumann [2]) that $H_\mathbb{R}$ admet a unique representation unitaire irreducible $V$ for which $\lambda \in \mathrm{U}^1$ agit by multiplication by $\lambda$. In the model of Schrodinger of $V$, one has $V = L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and the action of $H_\mathbb{R}$ is:
\begin{align*}
(X, 0, 1) &\colon f(z) \mapsto f(z + X), \\
(0, y, 1) &\colon f(z) \mapsto e^{2\pi iyz}f(z).\tag{1.4}
\end{align*}

Of the unicite of $V$ one deduces a action projective unitaire of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ on $V$, compatible a the action of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ on $H_\mathbb{R}$ (A.~Weil [3], D.~Shale [1]).

For the action of $H_\mathbb{R}$ on $V = L^2(\mathbb{R})$, the vecteurs $C^\infty$, i.e. the $v \in V$ such that $h \mapsto hv$ let $C^\infty$ en $h \in H_\mathbb{R}$, forment the space of Schwartz $\mathcal{S}$ of the functions $C^\infty$ a decroissance rapide on $\mathbb{R}$. The vecteurs holomorphic, i.e. the $v \in V$ such that $h \mapsto hv$ se prolonge en a map holomorphic of $H$ in $V$, are the functions $L^2$ on $\mathbb{R}$ se prolongeant en a function holomorphic on $\mathbb{C}$ such that, in every bande horizontale $|\Impart z| < A_1$ one ait for every $A_2 \in \mathbb{R}$
\[|f(z)| = O(\exp(-A_2|z|)).\]

The group complex $H$ agit on the space $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$ of the vecteurs holomorphic by the formules (1.4).

One ne dispose not for the action holomorphic of $H$ on $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$ of the same unicite that for the action unitaire of $H_\mathbb{R}$ on $V$. Otherwise, the action projective of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ on $V$ se prolongerait en a action projective of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ on $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$, this action se releverait en a vraie action of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$, car $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ is simply connected, and the action projective of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ se releverait en a vraie action. This n'is not the cas: seul the covering double of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ agit. Notre but is of expliciter this which se passe.

\section*{2}

Let $S = \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{C})$ the space of the lines of $\mathbb{C}^2$. For $D \in S$, releve in $H$ as to the n\textsuperscript{o}~1, let $W_D$ the space of the functions holomorphic on $H$ verifiant
\[\varphi(\lambda d \cdot h) = \lambda\varphi(h)\]
for $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^*$, $d \in D$, equipped with the action of $H$: $g \colon \varphi(h) \mapsto \varphi(hg)$.

For $E \not\supset D$, the restriction a $E$ is a bijection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.1}
r_E \colon W_D \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}(E),\tag{2.1}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{O}(E)$ being the space of the functions holomorphic on $E$.

Si $R$ is a representation holomorphic of $H$, on laquelle each $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^*$ agit by $\lambda$, and $\omega$ a form lineaire $D$-invariante on $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:2.2}
\omega \mapsto \omega(hw)\tag{2.2}
\end{equation}
is a morphism of $R$ in $W_D$.

\section*{3. Example}

Let as to the n\textsuperscript{os}~1, 2 $V$ the representation unitaire irreducible of $H_\mathbb{R}$, $\mathcal{S} \subset V$ the space of ses vecteurs $C^\infty$ and let $\mathcal{S}' \supset V$ the dual of the space of the vecteurs $C^\infty$ of the representation duale (in the model of Schrodinger: the space of the distributions temperees).

Let $D_t \in S$ the line generated by $(-t, 1)$. Si $\Impart t > 0$, the form lineaire, ecrite in the model of Schrodinger,
\[\omega_t \colon f \mapsto \int f(x)\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\,dx\]
is defined on $V$, and same on $\mathcal{S}$. The form $\omega_t$ is a vecteur holomorphic of the dual of $V$, fixe by $D_t$. En terme of the generateur infinitesimal $-t\partial_x + 2\pi ix$ of $D_t$, the invariance s'ecrit
\[\int \bigl[(-t\partial_x + 2\pi ix)f(x)\bigr] \cdot \exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\,dx = \int f(x) \cdot (t\partial_x + 2\pi ix)\exp(-\pi ix^2/t)\,dx = 0.\]

By (2.2), $\omega_t$ defines
\[Q_t \colon V^{\mathrm{hol}} \to W_{D_t}\]
which se prolonge a $V$ and same a $\mathcal{S}'$.

Let $E$ the axe of the $x$. the map $r_E \circ Q_t \colon V \to \mathcal{O}(E)$ envoie $f \in V$ on
\[f(x, t) = r_E \circ Q_t(f) \qquad (\Impart t > 0)\]
solution of the equation of the chaleur
\[\partial_t f = \frac{i}{4\pi}\partial_x^2 f, \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad \Bigl(\partial_t - \frac{i}{4\pi}\partial_x^2\Bigr)f(x, t) = 0.\]

For $t$ reel, the form $\omega_t$ reste defined on $\mathcal{S} \subset V$. For $t = 0$, c'is $f \mapsto f(0)$. For $t = \infty$, c'is $\int f(x)\,dx$. the homomorphisme $Q_t$ reste defined on $V^{\mathrm{hol}}$. For $t = 0$, $r_E \circ Q_0$ is, in the model of Schrodinger, the identity.

Supposons $\Impart(t) > 0$. Let $\bar{D}_t$ the complex conjuguee of $D_t$. In the line affine $S - \{\bar{D}_t\}$, the ``demi-plan'' $\{D_u \mid \Impart u > 0\}$ is a disque of centre $D_t$. the map $r_{\bar{D}_t} \circ Q_t \colon V \to W_{D_t} \to \mathcal{O}(D_t)$ fournit the model holomorphic of $V$: the action of $H$ is given by
\begin{align*}
d \in D_t &\colon f(x) \mapsto f(x + d), \\
e \in D_t &\colon f(x) \mapsto \exp(-2\pi i\langle e, x \rangle)f(x),\tag{3.1}
\end{align*}
the structure hilbertienne or $(f, f)$ is the integral of $f(d)\overline{f(d')}\exp(2\pi i\langle d, d' \rangle)$ is invariante by $H_\mathbb{R}$ and $r_{\bar{D}_t} \circ Q_t$ identifie $V$ a the space of the $f$ of norme finite. For $d = (-tz, z)$, the factor $\exp(2\pi i\langle d, \bar{d} \rangle) = \text{commutateur de } d \text{ et } \bar{d}$ is
\[\exp(2\pi i\langle d, \bar{d} \rangle) = \exp(2\pi i(t - \bar{t})|z|^2).\]

Fixons $E$. The isomorphisms $r_E$ (2.1) permettent of considerer the $W_D$ ($D \not\supset E$) as formant a family of representations of $H$ operant in the space fixe $\mathcal{O}(E)$, the action of $H$ dependant holomorphiquement of $D$. Explicitons the dependance en $E$ of $r_E$. Prenons of the coordonnees as to the n\textsuperscript{o}~1 or $E$ let the axe of the $x$. Let $E'$ generated by $(1, u)$, $D$ generated by $(-t, 1)$. One prend $x$ as coordonnee on $E$ and $E'$. one has
\[(x', ux', 1) = (-tux', ux', 1)(x, 0, 1)(0, 0, \lambda)\]
for $(1 + tu)x' = x$ and $\lambda = \exp(\pi i uxx')$, of sorte that $r_{E'} \circ r_E^{-1}$ envoie $f_E \in \mathcal{O}(E)$ on $f_{E'} \in \mathcal{O}(E')$ with $f_{E'}(x') = \lambda f_E(x)$:

\end{equation}

\section*{5. Proposition}

Let $T > 0$ and considerons the conditions suivantes on a function integer $f$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)$_T$]
\item There exists a function holomorphic $g(z, t)$ ($z \in \mathbb{C}$, $|t| < T$) verifiant the equation of the chaleur $(\partial_t - \partial_z^2)g = 0$, with the condition initiale $g(z, 0) = f(z)$;
\item $|f(z)| < O(\exp(|z|^2/4T))$;
\item $\displaystyle\iint |f(z)\overline{f(z)}|\exp(-|z|^2/2T)\,|dz \wedge d\bar{z}| < \infty$.
\end{enumerate}

Then, si $T < T_1$, chacune of (i)$_T$, (ii)$_{T_1}$, (iii)$_{T_1}$ implique chacune of (i)$_{T_1}$, (ii)$_T$, (iii)$_T$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof.} (i)$_T$ $\Rightarrow$ (ii)$_T$. the equation of the chaleur reste verifiee by the derivees of $g$ and one has therefore
\[\partial_t^n(z^{2n}g) = \partial_z^{2n+1}g = \partial_t^n(z\partial_z^{2n}g).\]
Puisque $g(0, t)$ is holomorphic for $|t| < T_1$, si $T < T_2 < T_1$, one has en $(0, 0)$
\[|\partial_t^n g/n!| < O(1/T_2^n),\]
of or
\[|\partial_z^{2n}g| = O(n!/T_2^n)\]
and a estimation analogue for $\partial_z^{2n+1}g$. The serie of Taylor of $f$ given then
\[|f(z)| < \sum_n \binom{2n}{n} \frac{|z|^{2n}}{(2n)!} \frac{n!}{T_2^n}.\]
one has
\[\frac{1}{2n+1}\binom{2n}{n} \leq \frac{2^{2n}}{(n!)^2}\]
of or
\[|f(z)| < O\Bigl(\sum_n \frac{1}{n!}\Bigl(\frac{|z|^2}{4T}\Bigr)^n\Bigr)\]
and this sum is $\exp(|z|^2/4T)$.

(ii)$_T$ $\Rightarrow$ (i)$_T$. It is enough to prendre $g$ given by the kernel of the equation of the chaleur:
\[g(z, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\int_{i\mathbb{R}} f(z + x)\exp(-x^2/4t)\,dx.\]
Changer $x$ en $-ix$ change the orientation of the cycle $i\mathbb{R}$ and the signe of the integrande, of sorte that the integral ne change not. The condition (ii) assure the convergence for $|t| < T$.

The relation between (ii) and (iii) is clear: (ii)$_{T_1}$ $\Rightarrow$ (iii)$_T$ and that (iii)$_{T_1}$ $\Rightarrow$ (ii)$_T$ se satisfies en ecrivant $f(z)$ as moyenne of ses values on a disque of centre $z$ and of rayon $1/|z|$, and en evaluant this moyenne by Cauchy--Schwarz.

\section*{6}

En hommage to the theorem of Stone--von Neumann, the fiber $\mathscr{W}$ on $S$ is, projectivement, equipped with a connexion holomorphic for which the action of $H$ is horizontale.

Precisons: ``projectivement''.

On the projective line $S$, one dispose of the sheaf inversible ample $\mathcal{O}(1)$. Let $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ son dual. A racine carree $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$ of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$ is a sheaf inversible $L$ equipped with a isomorphism $L^{\otimes 2} \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}(-1)$. Locally, a telle racine carree exists and, locally, deux racines carrees are isomorphes, but the isomorphism n'is not unique: ambiguite $\pm 1$. This ambiguite obstrue the existence global of $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$. This n'is not on $\mathscr{V}$, but on
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.1}
\mathscr{V}(-\tfrac{1}{2}) := \mathscr{V} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\tfrac{1}{2})\tag{6.1}
\end{equation}
that one has a connexion:
\[\nabla \colon \mathscr{V}(-\tfrac{1}{2}) \to \mathscr{V}(-\tfrac{1}{2}) \otimes \Omega^1.\]

Noter that si $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})'$ and $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})''$ are deux racines carrees of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, and $\nabla$ a connexion on $\mathscr{V} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})'$, son transporte on $\mathscr{V} \otimes \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})''$ by $\alpha \colon \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})' \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})''$ (a isomorphism of racines carrees of $\mathcal{O}(-1)$) ne depend not of the choix of $\alpha$, that one ne can changer locally that by a constant $\pm 1$. The notion of ``connexion on $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$'' is therefore bien defined, independamment of the existence global or of the choix of $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$.

Let $D \in S$ and $t$ a vecteur tangent en $D$. For $s$ a section local of $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$, the point is to define $\nabla_t s$ in the fiber of $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$ en $D$. One procedera as suit: after avoir fait a choix auxiliaire $u$, one definira $\nabla_t^u s$ for $s$ a section local of $\mathscr{V}$, or of $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$. This derivation $\nabla_t^u$ depend of $u$, with a dependance en $u$ of the form
\[\nabla_t^{u+x}(s) = \nabla_t^u(s) + a \cdot s(D),\]
$a$ prenant of the values opposees for $\mathscr{V}$ and for $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$. For $s = s_1 \otimes s_2$, $s_1$ section local of $\mathscr{V}$ and $s_2$ of $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$,
\[\nabla_t(s) := \nabla_t^u(s_1)s_2(D) + s_1(D)\nabla_t^u(s_2)\]
is independant of the choix of $u$.

Let $D \in S$: $D$ is a line of $\mathbb{C}^2$. A vecteur tangent $t$ en $D \in S$ s'identifie a map lineaire $t \colon D \to \mathbb{C}^2/D$, i.e. a element of $(\mathbb{C}^2/D) \otimes D^*$: a $u' \otimes v' \in (\mathbb{C}^2/D) \otimes D^*$ attacher $t(d) = \langle u, d \rangle v'$ for $u$ a quelconque representant of $u'$. Notre datum auxiliaire will be celle of $t$ relevant $t$, i.e. of a lifting of $t$ a $\mathbb{C}^2/D \otimes \mathbb{C}^2$. We the ecrirons $t = u' \otimes u$, with $u \in \mathbb{C}^2$ of image $u'$ in $\mathbb{C}^2/D$.

the space tangent a the origine of $H$ is $\mathfrak{z} = \mathbb{C}^2 \times \mathbb{C}$. For $z$ in this space tangent, let $\partial_z$ the champ of vecteurs invariant a line corresponding. one has
\[[\partial_x, \partial_y] = -\langle x, y \rangle'\]
or figure a line the crochet in the algebra of Lie of $H$. For $x, y \in \mathbb{C}^2$, calculant $[x, y]$ as a commutateur infinitesimal, one deduces that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.2}
[\partial_x, \partial_y] = -2\pi i\langle x, y \rangle(\partial_0, \partial_0, 1).\tag{6.2}
\end{equation}

Let $\mathscr{W}_\bullet$ the quotient of the algebra enveloppante of the algebra of Lie of the champs of vecteurs invariants a line by the relation $\delta(0, 0, 1) = 1$. In $\mathscr{W}_\bullet$, one deduces of (6.2) that, for $d \in D$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.3}
[\partial_u^2, \partial_d] = 2 \cdot 2\pi i\langle u, d \rangle \partial_u = \langle u, d \rangle \partial_u = \langle \bar{t}(d) \rangle.\tag{6.3}
\end{equation}

Calculons to the prime ordre autour of $D$. For $\varepsilon^2 = 0$, and $\varphi_0(g)$ in $W_D$: $\partial_d\varphi_0 = 0$, one has
\[\partial_{d + \varepsilon t(d)}[\varphi_0(g) + \varepsilon\partial_u^2\varphi_0(g)/(4\pi i)] = \varepsilon\bigl([\partial_d, \partial_u^2]\varphi_0(g) + \partial_d\partial_u^2\varphi_0(g)\bigr) = 0\]
car $\partial_d(\partial_u^2\varphi_0/(4\pi i)) = -[-\partial_u^2/4\pi i, \partial_d]\varphi_0$: appliquer (6.3). To the prime ordre autour of $D$, the function of $g$ and $D'$ defined by $\varphi(g, D + \varepsilon t) = \varphi_0(g) + \varepsilon(\partial_u^2/4\pi i)\varphi_0(g)$ is therefore a section of the fiber $\mathscr{V}$. Si $\varphi(g, D')$ is a section local of $\mathscr{V}$, this permet of define the derivee $\nabla_t^u$ in $W_D$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.5}
\nabla_t^u = \partial_t\varphi((g, D)) + (\partial_u^2/4\pi i)\varphi(g, D).\tag{6.5}
\end{equation}

Calculons the dependance of (6.5) en $u$. Si $u$ is remplace by $u + x$ ($x \in D$), one has in $\mathscr{W}_\bullet$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.6}
\partial_{u+x}^2 = (\partial_u + \partial_x)^2 = \partial_u^2 + 2\partial_u\partial_x + [\partial_x, \partial_u] = \partial_u^2 + 2\partial_u\partial_x + 2\pi i\langle u, x \rangle.\tag{6.6}
\end{equation}
For $\varphi \in W_D$, one has $\partial_x\varphi = 0$ and (6.6) se simplifie en
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.7}
\partial_{u+x}^2\varphi = \partial_u^2\varphi + 2\pi i\langle u, x \rangle\varphi,\tag{6.7}
\end{equation}
of or
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.8}
\partial_{u+x}^2\varphi/(4\pi i) = \partial_u^2\varphi/(4\pi i) + \tfrac{1}{2}\langle u, x \rangle\varphi.\tag{6.8}
\end{equation}

The choix of $u$ permet also of define the derivee $\nabla_t^u$ with respect to $t$ of a section local of $\mathcal{O}(1)$. A section of $\mathcal{O}(1)$ on $U \subset S$ is a function homogene of degree $1$ on the inverse image of $U$ in $\mathbb{C}^2$, and one derive with respect to $\langle u, d \rangle\partial_u$ for obtenir a function homogene of degree $1$ on $D$. Quand one change $u$ en $u + x$,
\[\nabla_t^{u+x}(s) = \langle u + x, d \rangle\partial_{u+x}s = \nabla_t^u(s) + \langle u, x \rangle s.\]

This derivation en induces a on $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$, with
\[\nabla_t(s^{-1/2}) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_t(s) \cdot s^{-3/2}\]
and for dependance en $u$, $s$ being this fois a section local of $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:6.9}
\nabla_t^{u+x}(s) = \nabla_t^u(s) - \tfrac{1}{2}\langle u, x \rangle s.\tag{6.9}
\end{equation}

The dependances en $u$ of (6.8) and (6.9) se neutralisent for fournir a derivation $\nabla_t$ for $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$.

The connexion $\nabla$ being defined en terme of champs of vecteurs invariants a line on $H$, it commute a the action of $H$.

\section*{7}

Explicitons the connexion of the n\textsuperscript{o}~6 en coordonnees. Choisissons of the coordonnees as to the n\textsuperscript{o}~1 and let $E$ the axe of the $x$. By a telle trivialisation, the connexion cherchee se transporte en a connexion on the fiber $\mathscr{V}$. We shall the calculer.

Let $D_t$ the line generated by $(-t, 1)$. By $r_E$ (2.1), a section local of $\mathscr{V}$ s'identifie a function $f(z, t)$ holomorphic en $z$ and $t$. Calculons sa derivee with respect to $t$. In the n\textsuperscript{o}~6, one can prendre for $t$: $D_t \to \mathbb{C}^2 : (-tz, z) \mapsto (-1, 0)z$; this is $d \mapsto -\langle(1, 0), d\rangle \cdot (1, 0)$.

The derivation corresponding of $d^* \in \mathcal{O}(1)$ annule $s$, of sorte that one has simplement

Of the n\textsuperscript{o}~5, one deduces that by $s \in W_D^?(-\frac{1}{2})$ it passe a section local horizontale of $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$. Noter that en dimension infinie a connexion ne defines not necessarily a trivialisation local, same si by each point passe a section horizontale local.

The connexion $\nabla$ on $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$ en defines a on the fiber en spaces projective corresponding $\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{V}) = \mathbb{P}(\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2}))$. The sections local horizontales are the images of the sections horizontales not nulles of $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$. Bien that the sphere $S$ let simply connected, one has:

\section*{8. Surprise}

The fiber en spaces projective $\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{V})$ on $S$ n'a aucune section horizontale global.

the argument of the n\textsuperscript{o}~1, permettrait of the prevoir: the appliquer a the representation projective of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ given by the action of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ on the projective space of the sections horizontales globales of $\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{V})$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{1\textsuperscript{ere} proof.} Divisons $S$ en deux hemispheres $S_1$ and $S_2$ and choisissons $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$ on $S_i$. On $S_1 \cap S_2$, one dispose locally of $\alpha \colon \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})_2$, defined to the signe pres, but $\alpha$ ne can be defined globally that on the covering double of the cercle $S_1 \cap S_2$: monodromy $-1$. S'it existait of the sections projective globales horizontales of $\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{V})$, it existerait of the sections horizontales $f_1$, $f_2$ of $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})$ on $S_1$ and $S_2$ which, on $S_1 \cap S_2$ coincideraient to the signe pres. On $S_1 \cap S_2$, this permettrait of normaliser $\alpha \colon \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})_1 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})_2$ by $\alpha(f_1) = f_2$, contredisant the inexistence of $\alpha$ on $S_1 \cap S_2$.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{2\textsuperscript{eme} proof.} Choisissons of the coordonnees as to the n\textsuperscript{o}~1. Let $E$ the axe of the $x$ and $D$ celui of the $y$. As en n\textsuperscript{o}~2, identifions $W_D$ a the space of the functions entieres $f(z)$. Let $f \neq 0$ in $W_D$. By the proposition, for that there exists on $S - E$ a section horizontale of $\mathbb{P}(\mathscr{V})$ passant by $f$, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ satisfies the condition of croissance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:8.1}
\forall \varepsilon > 0 \quad |f(z)| < O(\exp(\varepsilon|z|)).\tag{8.1}
\end{equation}
Si $E'$ is a other line, of after (4.1), the existence of a section horizontale on $S - E'$ passant by $f$ se traduit by a condition of croissance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:8.2}
\forall \varepsilon > 0 \quad |f(z)\exp(az^2)| < O(\exp(\varepsilon|z|^2))\tag{8.2}
\end{equation}
with $a \neq 0$ suitable.

Posons $g(z) = f(z)\exp(az^2/2)$. By (8.1) and (8.2), $|g(z)|$ is domine by a multiple tant of $|\exp(\varepsilon|z|^2 + az^2/2)|$ that of $|\exp(\varepsilon|z|^2 - az^2/2)|$ of sorte that in all the directions, sauf celles for lesquelles $az^2$ is purement imaginaire, $g$ is a decroissance exponentielle quadratique. In all the directions, one has uniformement a croissance to the more exponentielle quadratique. By Phragmen--Lindelof, $g$ is a decroissance exponentielle quadratique in all the directions, therefore is zero, and $f = 0$.

\section*{9}

In the coordonnees of the n\textsuperscript{o}~1, let $D_t$ the line generated by $(-t, 1)$. Let $d = (-tz, z)$. one has for the commutateur of $d$ and $\bar{d}$
\[(d, \bar{d}) = \exp(-2\pi i(t - \bar{t})|z|^2) \in \mathbb{C}^*:\]
the commutateur is reel positive and
\[(d, \bar{d}) > 1 \iff \Impart(t) > 0.\]

The lines $D$ with for $d$ engendrant $D$ $(d, \bar{d}) > 1$ forment therefore a disque in $S$, dit attached to the structure reelle $H_\mathbb{R}$.

The structures reelles $L \subset \mathbb{C}^2$ for lesquelles $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ is reel on $L$ forment a space homogene under $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$, with $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$ the stabilisateur of $\mathbb{R}^2$. Chacune defines a structure reelle $H_L$ on $H$, therefore a conjugaison complex $\sigma_L$ of points fixes $L \cdot \mathrm{U}^1$.

The disques in $S$ forment similarly a space homogene under $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$, the stabilisateur of celui associated a $H_\mathbb{R}$ being $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{R})$. The correspondence
\[L \mapsto X(L) := \{D \mid \langle d, \sigma_L(d) \rangle > 1 \text{ pour } d \text{ un générateur de } D\}\]
is therefore a bijection of the space of the structures reelles considered with the space of the disques of $S$.

The passage of a disque to the disque complementaire se traduit on the structures reelles en $L \mapsto iL$.

\section*{10}

The calculs of the n\textsuperscript{o}~3 admettent the interpretation suivante. Let $X$ a disque (open) of $S$, corresponding a structure reelle $L$, of or of the forms reelles $H_L$ of $H$ and $\mathrm{SL}(L)$ of $\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$. Then $\Gamma(X, \mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})|_{V=0})$ admet a structure pre-hilbertienne $H_L$-invariante, the complete $\Gamma(X, \mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})|_{V=0})\hat{}$ being the representation unitaire irreducible of $H_L$. one has
\[\Gamma(X, \mathscr{V}(-\tfrac{1}{2})|_{V=0}) \subset \Gamma(X, \mathscr{V}|_{V=0}) \subset \Gamma(X, \mathscr{V}(-\tfrac{1}{2})|_{V=0})\hat{}.\]

The covering double $\mathrm{SL}(L)'$ of $\mathrm{SL}(L)$ agit on $\mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})|_X$, in such a way compatible a son action on $\mathcal{O}(-\frac{1}{2})$, of or a action of $\mathrm{SL}(L)'$ on $\Gamma(X, \mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})|_{V=0})$. C'is celle which normalise the action of $H_L$, and induces the action natural of $\mathrm{SL}(L)'$ on $H_L$.

The under-representation $\Gamma(X, \mathscr{V}(-\frac{1}{2})|_{V=0})$ can s'interpreter as the space of the vecteurs analytiques reels for the action of $\mathrm{SL}(L)'$.

This description shows that the representation projective of $\mathrm{SL}(L)$ se prolonge to the monoide of the $g \in \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{C})$ such that $gX \supset X$.

\[(x, \lambda)(y, \mu) = \bigl(x + y, \lambda\mu\exp(\pi i\langle x, y \rangle)\bigr),\]
