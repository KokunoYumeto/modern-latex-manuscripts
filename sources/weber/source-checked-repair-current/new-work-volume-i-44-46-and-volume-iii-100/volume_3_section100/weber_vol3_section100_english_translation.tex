\section*{\S\,100. Ideal classes according to the orders.}

The concept of equivalence of ideals and the theorems on class numbers can be presented most clearly if, according to Vol. II, \S\,169, one represents the ideals by means of the functionals discussed in Vol. II, \S\,153 ff., since in this way one may use the ordinary rules of multiplication and division.\footnote{Compare also the author's memoir ``Über Zahlengruppen in algebraischen Körpern'', first communication, \emph{Mathematische Annalen} 48, 438.}

We restrict ourselves here once and for all, in the body \(\Omega\), to numbers and ideals which are relatively prime to an arbitrarily assumed whole rational number \(Q\), the conductor of an order.

Under this supposition the whole and fractional numbers of the body \(\Omega\) form the group previously denoted by \(O\). We enlarge this group by adjoining the functionals, and denote the group so enlarged by \(\overline O\). To each element of \(\overline O\) there corresponds then a whole or fractional ideal, and to such elements whose quotient is a functional unit there corresponds the same ideal.

If \(\eta\) is a number in \(O\) and \(\varepsilon\) is any functional unit, then the product \(\varepsilon\eta\) corresponds to a principal ideal. Accordingly we denote
\[
 \overline E\quad\hbox{the group of functional units,}
\]
\[
 E\quad\hbox{the group of numerical units,}
\]
and by
\[
 \overline EO
\]
the principal class of the functionals.

If we take a system of representatives
\begin{equation}
 \varphi_1,\varphi_2,\ldots,\varphi_h
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
of non-equivalent whole functionals in \(\Omega\), then every whole or fractional functional \(\Phi\) can be represented in the form
\[
 \Phi=\varphi_i\omega,
\]
where \(\omega\) is a functional from \(\overline EO\); and the ideal classes in \(\Omega\) are the cosets of \(\overline EO\) in \(\overline O\). Hence the class number \(h\) is the index of the divisor \(\overline EO\) of \(\overline O\):
\begin{equation}
 h=(\overline O,\overline EO).
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
Here, in the sharper division into classes, one must in \(O\) restrict oneself to the numbers of positive norm.

If now we consider the division of the ideals into classes according to the order \([Q]\), we must regard the functional group \(\overline EO'\) as principal class, and the class number is
\begin{equation}
 h'=(\overline O,\overline EO').
\tag{3}
\end{equation}

In order to derive from this the relation between \(h\) and \(h'\), we make use of the general group theorems of Vol. II, \S\,2, which we formulate and supplement as follows.

12. \emph{Theorem.} If \(A\) is a group and \(B\) a divisor of \(A\) of finite index \((A,B)\), and furthermore \(C\) a divisor of \(B\) of finite index \((B,C)\), then, by Vol. II, \S\,2, (4),
\begin{equation}
 (A,C)=(A,B)(B,C).
\tag{4}
\end{equation}

Let \(A\) again be a group with the divisor \(B\) of index \(\mu\). We put
\begin{equation}
 A=a_1B+a_2B+\cdots+a_\mu B,
\tag{5}
\end{equation}
where \(a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_\mu\) is a complete system of representatives of \(A\) modulo \(B\).

Let now \(C\) be a group of elements which can be composed with the elements of \(A\) (for example, in our case simply by multiplication). Then from (5) there follows
\begin{equation}
 AC=a_1BC+a_2BC+\cdots+a_\mu BC.
\tag{6}
\end{equation}
Now one and the same element can occur in the two cosets \(a_1BC\), \(a_2BC\) only if \(a_1a_2^{-1}\) is contained in \(BC\), but not in \(B\).

From this we formulate the theorem:

13. \emph{Theorem.} If \(A\) is a group, \(B\) a divisor of \(A\) of finite index \((A,B)\), and \(C\) a group composable with \(A\), of such a kind that \(A\) and \(BC\) contain no common elements except those of \(B\), then
\begin{equation}
 (AC,BC)=(A,B).
\tag{7}
\end{equation}
The supposition of this theorem may also be expressed as follows:
\[
 B\hbox{ is the intersection of }A\hbox{ and }BC;
\]
and it is fulfilled, for example, when \(C\) is contained in \(B\), and hence \(B=BC\), and also when \(C\) has no element in common with \(A\).

14. \emph{Theorem.} If \(A\) is a group composed from the two groups \(A'\) and \(B\),
\begin{equation}
 A=A'B,
\tag{8}
\end{equation}
and if \(B'\) is the intersection of \(A'\) and \(B\), then
\begin{equation}
 (A,B)=(A',B'),
\tag{9}
\end{equation}
provided these indices are finite.

For by (8) there is contained, in every coset \(aB\) of \(B\) in \(A\), an element \(a'\) from \(A'\), and one can therefore also represent these cosets by \(a'B\), where \(a'\) is contained in \(A'\). If then \(a'_1B,a'_2B\) are different cosets modulo \(B\) in \(A\), then \(a'_1B',a'_2B'\) are different cosets modulo \(B'\) in \(A'\), and conversely; for \(a'_1a_2'^{-1}\) is contained in \(B'\) if and only if it is at the same time contained in \(B\), and from this formula (9) follows.

We now apply these theorems to the expressions (2) and (3) for \(h\) and \(h'\). By (4),
\begin{equation}
 (\overline O,\overline EO')=(\overline O,\overline EO)(\overline EO,\overline EO'),
\tag{10}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
 h'=h(\overline EO,\overline EO').
\tag{11}
\end{equation}

We now denote, as above, by \(E\) the group of numerical units in \(O\). Then \(\overline EE=\overline E\), since the numerical units are contained among the functional units; and every number which is contained in \(\overline EO'\) is contained in \(EO'\). Hence \(EO'\) is the intersection of \(O\) and \(\overline EO'\). If, therefore, we apply (7), putting \(O,\ EO',\ \overline E\) in place of \(A,B,C\), then \(B\) is the intersection of \(A\) and \(BC\), and we obtain
\begin{equation}
 (\overline EO,\overline EO')=(\overline EO,\overline EEO')=(O,EO').
\tag{12}
\end{equation}

Furthermore, by (4),
\begin{equation}
 (O,EO')(EO',O')=(O,O'),
\tag{13}
\end{equation}
and if by \(E'\) we denote the group of the numerical units in \(O'\), that is, the intersection of \(E\) with \(O'\), so that \(E'O'=O'\), then
\[
 (EO',O')=(EO',E'O')=(E,E')
\]
by (9), since \(E'\) is the intersection of \(E'O'\) and \(E\). Therefore from (13) we have
\[
 (O,EO')(E,E')=(O,O'),
\]
and from (12)
\[
 (\overline EO,\overline EO')(E,E')=(O,O'),
\]
and finally, from (11),
\begin{equation}
 (E,E')h'=(O,O')h.
\tag{14}
\end{equation}

15. \emph{Theorem.} Formula (14) remains unaltered if \(O'\) denotes not precisely the order group, but any number group contained in \(O\), of finite index \((O,O')\), and if we call two ideals \(\mathfrak a,\mathfrak a'\) equivalent according to \(O'\) when their quotient \(\mathfrak a'/\mathfrak a=\eta'\) is a number in \(O'\). If \(E'\) is the group of the units contained in \(O'\), and if \((E,E')\) has a finite value, then the class number \(h'\) is finite and is determined by formula (14).

For example, if \(O\) is the group of all numbers in \(\Omega\) (except \(0\)), and \(O'\) the group of numbers of positive norm, then, if there are any numbers of negative norm at all, \((O,O')=2\). If there are then units of negative norm, then also \((E,E')=2\). But if all units have positive norm, then \((E,E')=1\), and we obtain in the first case \(h'=h\), in the second case \(2h'=h\).

The equivalence according to \(O\) is then the general equivalence; that according to \(O'\) is the sharpened equivalence.

We return to the orders \([Q]\), and under \(O\) understand the numbers relatively prime to \(Q\) (if necessary only those of positive norm). Then \((O,O')\) has been determined in the preceding paragraph.

If the discriminant \(D\) and its root \(\Delta\) are negative, then in general both \(E\) and \(E'\) contain only the two units \(\pm1\). In the two exceptional cases \(\Delta=-3,-4\), \(E\) contains six and four units, respectively; but \(E'\), when \(Q>1\), contains only two; and therefore \((E,E')=1\) in general, \((E,E')=3\) in the case \(\Delta=-3\), \((E,E')=2\) in the case \(\Delta=-4\). Thus in these cases, by \S\,98, (10), we have
\begin{equation}
 \lambda h'=Q\prod_q\left(1-\frac{(\Delta,q)}{q}\right)h,
\tag{15}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \lambda=2\quad\hbox{for }\Delta=-4,\qquad
 \lambda=3\quad\hbox{for }\Delta=-3,\qquad
 \lambda=1\quad\hbox{for }\Delta<-4.
\tag{16}
\end{equation}
This is the relation which was first derived by Gauss and later confirmed by Dirichlet in another way.

If, for example, \(\Delta\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(Q=2\), then, according to Gauss's terminology, \(h\) is the class number for the forms of the second kind, \(h'\) that of the forms of the first kind. It is
\[
 Q\left(1-\frac{(\Delta,2)}{2}\right)=1,\quad \Delta\equiv1\pmod8,
\]
\[
 \hphantom{Q\left(1-\frac{(\Delta,2)}{2}\right)}=3,\quad \Delta\equiv5\pmod8.
\]
Thus
\[
 h'=h,\quad \Delta\equiv1\pmod8,
\]
\[
 h'=3h,\quad \Delta\equiv5\pmod8,
\]
and only in the exceptional case \(\Delta=-3\) does \(h'=h\) occur.

If \(D\) is positive, it remains to determine \((E,E')\). Let
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{t+u\sqrt D}{2}
\]
be the fundamental unit in \(O\), and let \(\lambda\) be the smallest positive exponent for which
\[
 \varepsilon^\lambda=\frac{t+u\sqrt D}{2}
\]
is contained in \(O'\). Then \(E\) consists of the powers \(\pm\varepsilon^\nu\), and \(E'\) of the powers \(\pm\varepsilon^{\lambda\nu}\), and it is
\[
 (E,E')=\lambda,
\]
and formula (15) gives the class number \(h'\). A further determination of \(\lambda\) cannot in general be given.

If again \(\Delta\equiv1\pmod4\) and \(Q=2\), then \(\lambda=1\) or \(=3\); namely \(=1\) when in (17) \(u\) is even, and \(=3\) when \(u\) is odd. The latter can occur only when \(\Delta\equiv5\pmod8\); consequently, for \(\Delta\equiv1\pmod8\) one always has \(\lambda=1\), while for \(\Delta\equiv1\pmod8\) \(\lambda\) can be both \(1\) and \(3\).
