\section*{Editor's Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Editor's Introduction (Cheikho)}

\begin{paracol}{2}
\sloppy

\begin{leftcolumn}
\subsection*{\ar{تَوْطِئَةُ الْمُحَقِّقِ}}
\vspace{0.3cm}
{\arabicfont\beginR
كِتَابُ طَبَقَاتِ الْأُمَمِ أَحَدُ الْكُتُبِ النَّادِرَةِ الَّتِي نَعَرَّضُ فِيهَا كَتَبَةُ الْعَرَبِ لِوَصْفِ الْعُلُومِ بَيْنَ الْأُمَمِ الَّتِي سَبَقَتْ عَهْدَهُمْ، وَإِنْ لَمْ يَبْلُغْ صَاحِبُهُ فِي ذَلِكَ شَأْوَ كِتَابِ الْفِهْرِسْتِ لِأَبِي الْفَرَجِ ابْنِ الدَّمِيمِ إِلَّا أَنَّهُ جَمَعَ عِدَّةَ فَوَائِدَ تَدُلُّ عَلَى نَشَاطٍ فِي الْبَحْثِ وَعَلَى رَغْبَةٍ فِي التَّحْصِيلِ وَدَقَّةِ نَظَرٍ فِي التَّنْوِينِ، وَكَانَ أَهْلُ الْأَنْدَلُسِ يَفْتَخِرُونَ بِهِ وَبِرُؤُوسِ أَهْلِ الشَّرْقِ.
\endR}
\end{leftcolumn}

\begin{rightcolumn}
\subsection*{[Editor's Introduction]}
\vspace{0.3cm}
\textit{Kitab Tabaqat al-Umam} (The Book of the Categories of Nations) is one of the rare works in which an Arab writer describes the sciences among the nations that preceded his era. Although its author did not reach the level of Abu al-Faraj ibn al-Damimi in his \textit{Fihrist}, he nevertheless collected numerous useful facts that demonstrate his research activity, his desire for knowledge, and his precision in observation. The people of al-Andalus used to boast of him and of the heads of the East.
\end{rightcolumn}

\switchcolumn

\begin{leftcolumn}
\vspace{0.2cm}
{\arabicfont\beginR
وَقَدْ ذَكَرَ ابْنُ الْأَبَّارِ فِي كِتَابِ التَّكْمِلَةِ لِكِتَابِ الصِّلَةِ (٢ : ٤٦٣ طَبْعَةُ بَجْرَيْطَ) عَنْ عَبْدِ اللهِ بْنِ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ مَرْزُوقٍ الْيَحْصَبِيِّ أَنَّهُ قَدَّمَ الْإِسْكَنْدَرِيَّةَ رَوَى هَذَا الْكِتَابَ لِأَبِي طَاهِرٍ السَّلَفِيِّ.
\endR}
\end{leftcolumn}

\begin{rightcolumn}
\vspace{0.2cm}
Ibn al-Abb\={a}r mentions in his \textit{Takmila} to the \textit{Silah} (2:463, Bajarid edition) on the authority of Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Marz\={u}q al-Yah\d{s}ib\'{i} that he transmitted this book to Abu Tahir al-Salaf\'{i}.
\end{rightcolumn}

\switchcolumn

\begin{leftcolumn}
\vspace{0.2cm}
{\arabicfont\beginR
وَمِمَّنْ عَرَّفُوا هَذَا الْكِتَابَ فِي الشَّرْقِ أَبُو الْفَرَجِ غْرِيغُورِيُوسَ ابْنُ الْعِبْرِيِّ فَإِنَّهُ نَقَلَ عَنْهُ فِي كِتَابِ تَارِيخِ مُخْتَصَرِ الدُّوَلِ نَبْذَتَيْنِ مُفِيدَتَيْنِ فِي الْعَرَبِ وَعُلُومِهِمْ، وَكَذَلِكَ عَرَفَهُ الْحَاجُّ خَلِيفَةُ فَذَكَرَهُ مِرَارًا فِي كِتَابِهِ كَشْفِ الظُّنُونِ فَدَعَا تَارَةً التَّعْرِيفُ بِطَبَقَاتِ الْأُمَمِ وَقَالَ فِي وَصْفِهِ إِنَّهُ كِتَابٌ صَغِيرُ الْحَجْمِ كَثِيرُ النَّفْعِ.
\endR}
\end{leftcolumn}

\begin{rightcolumn}
\vspace{0.2cm}
Among those who knew this book in the East was Abu al-Faraj Grigorius ibn al-\textquoteleft Ibr\={i}, for he quoted from it in his \textit{History of the Dynasties} two useful excerpts concerning the Arabs and their sciences. Likewise, al-H\={a}jj Khal\={i}fa knew it and mentioned it several times in his \textit{Kashf al-Zunun}: at one point calling it \textit{Ta\textquoteleft r\={i}f bi-Tabaqat al-Umam}, and describing it as a small book of great benefit.
\end{rightcolumn}

``Sa\textquoteleft id ibn Ahmad ibn \textquoteleft Abd al-Ra\d{h}m\={a}n ibn Mu\d{h}ammad ibn Sa\textquoteleft id al-Taghlab\={i}, the judge with the kunya Abu al-Q\={a}sim, originally from Cordoba, transmitted from Abu Mu\d{h}ammad ibn \d{H}azm, al-Fat\d{h} ibn Q\={a}sim, Abu al-Wal\={i}d al-Waqsh\={i}, and others. The amir Yahy\={a} ibn Dh\={i} al-N\={u}n appointed him as judge of Tulaytula (Toledo). He was a man of knowledge, intelligence, and transmission. \vspace{0.3cm}
{\arabicfont\beginR
\textbf{(الْأُمَّةُ الْأُولَى)} الْفُرْسُ وَكَانَ مَسْكَنُهَا فِي الْوَسَطِ الْمَعْمُورِ وَحِدٌ بِلَادِهَا مِنَ الْجِبَالِ الَّتِي فِي شِمَالِ الْعِرَاقِ بِعَقَبَةِ حُلْوَانَ وَالَّذِي فِيهِ الْأَنْجَهَاتُ وَالْكُرَجُ وَالدُّسْتُورُ وَهُمَدَانَ وَقُمَّ وَقَاشَانَ وَغَيْرِهَا مِنَ الْبِلَادِ إِلَى أَرْمِينِيَةَ وَالْبَابِ الْمُتَّصِلِ بِبَحْرِ أَذَرْبِيجَانَ وَطُبَرِسْتَانَ وَمُولْتَانَ وَالْبِيلَقَانَ وَأَرَزْنَ وَالشَّابَرَانَ وَالرِّيِّ وَالطَّالِقَانَ وَجُرْجَانَ إِلَى بِلَادِ خُرَاسَانِ.
\endR}
\end{leftcolumn}

\begin{rightcolumn}
\subsection*{[Chapter One: The Ancient Nations]}
\vspace{0.3cm}
\textit{In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate.}

\vspace{0.2cm}
\textit{O Lord, make it easy!}

\vspace{0.4cm}
The judge Abu al-Q\={a}sim Sa\textquoteleft \={i}d ibn A\d{h}mad ibn Sa\textquoteleft \={i}d---may God the Exalted have mercy on him---said: Know that all people in the East and West of the Earth, in its South and North, though they are one species, are distinguished by three things: by their moral character, their physical forms, and their languages.

\vspace{0.3cm}
Those who have concerned themselves with the reports of nations and investigated the various generations, and examined the categories of ages, say that in earliest times, before the branching of tribes and the divergence of languages, there were seven nations.

\vspace{0.3cm}
\textbf{The First Nation:} The Persians. Their dwelling was in the middle of the inhabited world. Their lands include the mountains in the north of Iraq, the region of Hulwan, and the area containing the Anjah\={a}t, Kuraj, Dust\={u}r, Hamad\={a}n, Qumm, Q\={a}sh\={a}n, and other lands extending to Armenia, the Gate connecting to the Sea of Azerbaijan, Tabarist\={a}n, Mult\={a}n, Bil\={a}q\={a}n, Arzan, Sh\={a}bor\={a}n, al-Rayy, T\={a}liq\={a}n, and Jurj\={a}n, to the lands of Khur\={a}s\={a}n.
\end{rightcolumn}

\end{paracol}

فَمِنْ مَذَاهِبِ الْهِنْدِ فِي عُلُومِ النُّجُومِ الْمَذَهَبُ الثَّلَاثَةُ الْمَشْهُورَةُ عَنْهُمْ وَهُوَ مَذْهَبُ السِّنْدِ هِنْدٌ وَمَذْهَبُ الْأَزْدِيجِ وَمَذْهَبُ الْأَرْكَنْدِ وَلَمْ يَصِلْ إِلَيْنَا مِنْهُمْ عَلَى التَّحْصِيلِ إِلَّا مَذْهَبُ السِّنْدِ هِنْدٌ وَهُوَ الْمَذْهَبُ الَّذِي تَقَلَّدَهُ جَمَاعَةٌ مِنَ الْإِسْلَامِ وَالْقُوَّامُ فِيهِ الْأَزْيَاجُ كَمُحَمَّدِ بْنِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ الْفَزَارِيِّ وَحَبْشِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللهِ الْبَغْدَادِيِّ وَمُحَمَّدِ بْنِ مُوسَى الْخَوَارِزْمِيِّ وَالْحُسَيْنِ بْنِ مُحَمَّدٍ الْمَعْرُوفِ بِابْنِ الْآدَمِيِّ وَغَيْرِهِمْ. وَتَفْسِيرُ السِّنْدِ هِنْدٍ <<الدَّهْرُ الدَّاهِرُ>> كَذَلِكَ حَكَى الْحُسَيْنُ بْنُ الْآدَمِيِّ فِي زِيجِهِ.
\endR}

\vspace{0.4cm}

\endR}
\end{leftcolumn}

\begin{rightcolumn}
\subsection*{[Science in India]}
\vspace{0.3cm}
Because of the distance of India from our lands and the obstacles between us and them, we have not become familiar with them. No portion of their sciences has reached us, nothing has come to us but fragments of their doctrines, and we have heard only a little of their knowledge.

\vspace{0.3cm}
Among the doctrines of the Indians in the science of astrology, three doctrines are famous among them: the doctrine of Sindhind, the doctrine of the \={A}z\={i}j, and the doctrine of Arkand. Only the doctrine of Sindhind has reached us in a complete form. It was followed by a group of Muslims, and those who established it were al-Azy\={a}j such as Mu\d{h}ammad ibn Ibr\={a}h\={i}m al-Faz\={a}r\={i}, \d{H}abash ibn \textquoteleft Abd All\={a}h al-Baghd\={a}d\={i}, Mu\d{h}ammad ibn M\={u}s\={a} al-Khw\={a}rizm\={i}, al-\d{H}usayn ibn Mu\d{h}ammad known as Ibn al-\={A}dam\={i}, and others. The interpretation of Sindhind is ``the Eternal Time'' (al-Dahr al-D\={a}hir), as al-\d{H}usayn ibn al-\={A}dam\={i} related in his \textit{z}\={i}\textit{j}.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\textbf{[Section]}

\vspace{0.3cm}
As for the followers of Sindhind, they agree that the duration of the world has no fixed number. Their doctrine which they mention is that the planets, their apogees, and their nodes were all gathered at the head of Aries. Since that event, the movements of the planets, apogees, and nodes began. The lower world remained desolate for a long time until the planets, apogees, and nodes dispersed through the zodiac signs. When that happened, the condition of the lower world returned to its first state, and this continues without end according to them. Each of the planets, apogees, and nodes has cycles during this period, which they consider the duration of the world, which I have mentioned.
\end{rightcolumn}

\end{paracol}

\subsection*{[Science among the Persians]}
\vspace{0.3cm}
As for the second nation, the Persians: they are a people of towering nobility and exalted glory, the most settled of nations and the noblest in region, and the most prosperous in kings. We know of no nation besides them whose sovereignty endured so long, who had kings that united them and leaders that defended them from those who opposed them, overcame with them those who envied them, and protected their oppressed from their oppressors, and who endured affairs in a way that secured their portion with continuity and permanence and the finest organization, so that the last of them took this over from the first, and the absent from the absent.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\textbf{[On the Arabs and the Non-Arabs]}

\vspace{0.3cm}
The most correct opinion on this is that from the beginning of the reign of Kay\={u}m\={a}rth ibn Am\={i}m ibn \={A}l\={a}dh ibn S\={a}m ibn N\={u}\d{h} (Noah) to the reign of Man\={u}shihr the Persian---who is, according to them, Adam the father of mankind, peace be upon him---to the beginning of the reign of Man\={u}shihr, the first king of the second class of Persian kings, was about a thousand complete years. From the reign of Man\={u}shihr to the beginning of the reign of Kaythub\={a}dh ibn Du\textquoteleft a, the first king of the third class of Persian kings, was about two hundred years. From the reign of Kaythub\={a}dh to the beginning of the reign of the Tribes (al-\-Taw\={a}\textquoteleft if), the fourth class of Persian kings, at the killing of Alexander (al-Iskandar) of D\={a}r\={a} ibn D\={a}r\={a}, the last king of the third class of Persian kings, was about a thousand years.

\vspace{0.3cm}
From the beginning of the reign of Azdash\={i}r ibn B\={a}bak the S\={a}s\={a}nian, the first king of the Ban\={u} Isf\={a}niy\={a}r (the fifth class of Persian kings), was five hundred and sixty-one years. From the beginning of the reign of Azdash\={i}r ibn B\={a}bak to the end of the Persian state on earth, at the killing of Yazdajird ibn Shahriy\={a}r ibn Kisr\={a}, was four years.

\subsection*{[Science in Greece]}
\vspace{0.3cm}
As for the Greeks, they are the people of Palestine, Rome, Constantinople, and the surrounding nations. Their kingdom extended from the shore of the Mediterranean Sea in the East to its shore in the West, and from the far north to the Syrian Sea. Their greatest city was the city of Alexandria. Their state was very ancient and their sciences were many and varied.

\vspace{0.3cm}
The first to discourse on the sciences and wisdom among them were Thales, Plato, Aristotle, Ptolemy, Galen, Hippocrates, Proclus, Euclid, Nicomachus, Archimedes, and all those attributed to these in the sciences and wisdom. They were the ones who established the principles of the sciences, elucidated them, arranged their parts, and codified their rules, and their compositions on this were in their own language. Then they were translated into Arabic after the Man\={a}dhirah disseminated them and the religious scholars among the Christians in the East adopted them.
\end{rightcolumn}

As for the Copts, they are the people of Upper Egypt. They took from the Copts their sciences and incorporated into them Greek wisdom, adding to them, refining them, and composing works on them. Among these were the science of astronomy, the science of the configuration of the planets, the science of causes and remedies, and other sciences.

وَأَمَّا الْيَهُودُ فَقَدْ كَانَتْ لَهُمْ عُلُومٌ فِي أَزْمِنَتِهِمُ الْأُولَى فِي زَمَنِ أَنْبِيَائِهِمْ وَمُلُوكِهِمْ. ثُمَّ إِنَّهُمْ لَمَّا خَرَجُوا مِنْ بِلَادِهِمْ وَتَفَرَّقُوا فِي بِلَادِ الْعَجَمِ وَالْعَرَبِ ضَاعَتْ عُلُومُهُمْ وَتَنَكَّرَتْ لِأَنَّهُمْ لَمْ يَكُونُوا أَهْلَ بَقَاءٍ وَلَا مُوَاظَبَةٍ عَلَى الْعِلْمِ. وَلَكِنَّهُمْ قَدْ حَفِظُوا مِنْ تَوَارِيثِ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ عُلُومًا فِي التَّعَبُّدِ وَالشَّرَائِعِ وَالتَّوْحِيدِ. وَكَانَ مِنْ أَشْهَرِ عُلَمَائِهِمْ مُوسَى بْنُ مَيْمُونٍ الْأَنْدَلُسِيُّ الَّذِي صَنَّفَ فِي عِلْمِ الْكَلَامِ وَالْفَلْسَفَةِ وَالطِّبِّ وَغَيْرِهَا.
\endR}
\end{leftcolumn}

\begin{rightcolumn}
\subsection*{[Science among the Jews]}
\vspace{0.3cm}
As for the Jews, they had sciences in their earliest times, in the era of their prophets and kings. But when they left their lands and dispersed into the lands of the non-Arabs and the Arabs, their sciences were lost and forgotten because they were not a people of endurance or perseverance in science. However, they did preserve from the inheritance of the prophets sciences concerning worship, religious laws, and monotheism. Among their most famous scholars was M\={u}s\={a} ibn Maym\={u}n (Maimonides) al-Andalus\={i}, who composed works on the science of theology, philosophy, medicine, and other subjects.
\end{rightcolumn}

\begin{rightcolumn}
\subsection*{[Science among the Arabs]}
\vspace{0.3cm}
As for the Arabs, they are two nations: a desert nation and a settled nation. The desert among them did not concern itself with science nor persevere in it due to the hardship of their livelihood and their movement from land to land. As for the settled people, they had sciences in the J\={a}hil\={i} period in medicine, astrology, magic, poetry, grammar, and other subjects. Then when Islam came and legislated the pursuit of knowledge, encouraged it, and made it a means of salvation and success, the Arabs strove in seeking the sciences, translating them from other nations, and rendering them into Arabic, until in a short time they became a people of sciences and arts.

\vspace{0.3cm}
The first [sciences] they were active in were theology (\textit{kal}\={a}\textit{m}), jurisprudence (\textit{fiqh}), \textit{had}\={i}\textit{th}, and [Qur\textquoteright \={a}nic] exegesis (\textit{taf}s\={i}r). Then they turned to the sciences of the ancients, translating them, composing works on them, and adding much to them. Among the most famous Arabs in the science of astrology were al-Fargh\={a}n\={i}, al-Batt\={a}n\={i}, and Abu Ma\textquoteleft shar the astronomer. In medicine: al-R\={a}z\={i}, Ibn S\={i}n\={a} (Avicenna), al-Maj\={u}s\={i}, and Ibn Rushd (Averroes). In astronomy: Ptolemy al-Majrit\={i}, al-Bitr\={u}j\={i} (Alpetragius), and Sharaf al-D\={i}n al-\d{T}\={u}s\={i}. In arithmetic: al-Khw\={a}rizm\={i} and al-K\={a}shsh\={i}. In philosophy: al-Kind\={i}, al-F\={a}r\={a}b\={i}, Ibn S\={i}n\={a}, and Ibn Rushd.
\end{rightcolumn}

\subsection*{On the Manuscript and Its Preservation}

The 1912 edition by Pere Louis Cheikho, S.J. (1859--1927) represents the first modern critical edition of this important work. Cheikho based his edition on a single manuscript preserved in the Oriental collections of Europe. The manuscript, as Cheikho describes it, is written in a clear hand resembling Persian script on thick yellowed paper, bound skillfully in gold-colored paper.

The \textit{Kitab Tabaqat al-Umam} has survived in a relatively small number of manuscripts compared to other major works of the Islamic scientific tradition. This may be attributed to the fact that it was composed in al-Andalus and did not circulate as widely in the eastern Islamic lands as works produced in Baghdad or other eastern centers.

\subsection*{On the Indian Section}

Al-Andalusi's chapter on India (Chapter Two above) is of particular historical importance. It contains one of the earliest extant Arabic descriptions of Indian astronomical doctrines, specifically the \textit{Sindhind}, \textit{Azij}, and \textit{Arkand} systems. The \textit{Sindhind}---interpreted as ``al-Dahr al-Dahir'' (the Eternal Time)---was the most influential of these, adopted by major Islamic astronomers including Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Fazari, Habash ibn Abd Allah al-Baghdadi, and Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi.

The description of Indian planetary theory, with its account of the great conjunction of all planets at the head of Aries at the beginning of the world-age, provides valuable evidence for the transmission of Indian astronomical ideas to the Islamic world. This doctrine of the \textit{Sindhind} became foundational for subsequent Islamic astronomical tables (\textit{zijat}).

\subsection*{On the Author}

Sa\textquoteleft id al-Andalusi (1029--1070 CE), whose full name is Abu al-Qasim Sa\textquoteleft id ibn Ahmad ibn Sa\textquoteleft id al-Taghlabi, was a jurist and scholar of Toledo. Born in Almeria (al-Mariyya) in 420 AH, he served as a judge (\textit{qadi}) in Toledo under the \d{H}ammudid amir Yahya ibn Dhi al-Nun. He died in Shawwal 462 AH (July 1070 CE).

\subsection*{On the Structure of the Work}

The \textit{Kitab Tabaqat al-Umam} is divided into two main parts:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The Ancient Nations} (\textit{al-Umam al-Qadima}): Seven nations that existed in earliest times---the Persians, the Chaldeans, the Greeks, the Egyptians, the Jews, the Arabs, and the Indians.
  \item \textbf{The Islamic Nations} (\textit{al-Umam al-Islamiyya}): A classification of the nations that embraced Islam, with special attention to their contributions to the sciences.
\end{enumerate}

The work is notable for being one of the earliest attempts at a systematic history of science organized by civilizations, predating similar European efforts by several centuries.

\subsection*{On the Scientific Content}

Al-Andalusi's work contains valuable information about:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The state of astronomy and astrology in various ancient civilizations
  \item The transmission of scientific knowledge between cultures
  \item The role of translation in preserving Greek scientific heritage
  \item Early Indian contributions to mathematics and astronomy (including the decimal system)
  \item The development of scientific institutions such as the House of Wisdom in Baghdad
\end{itemize}
