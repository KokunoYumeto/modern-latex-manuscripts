\section*{Chapter 23: \dev{त्रयोविंशो मानाध्यायः} --- The Measurement Chapter}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Chapter 23: Mānādhyāya (pp. 701--739)}
\markboth{Chapter 23: Mānādhyāya (pp. 701--739)}{}

\lettrine[lines=3,loversize=0.1]{T}{\textit{he twenty-third chapter}} of the \textit{Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta} treats of the measurement of time and the practical application of astronomical calculations. This chapter, like others in the work, is composed in the classical \textit{āryā} metre and presents Brahmagupta's rules for calendrical computation, planetary positions, and the reconciliation of different astronomical systems.

The chapter is divided into two principal sections:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Prayogavidhi} --- Practical application of the siddhānta
  \item \textbf{Caturviṃśa} --- The twenty-four fold division of time
\end{enumerate}

\wavydivider

\subsection*{\dev{प्रयोगविधिः} --- Practical Application (Verses 1--4)}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{सौरैराण्डं मासैस्तिथयश्चान्द्रैंस्तथा सावने दिवसाः ।}\\
\dev{दिनमासाब्दं पमध्यानत दिग्नाकेन्द्रं मानास्म्याम् ॥ १ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``The solar year, the lunar months and tithis, the civil days, days, months, years, the positions of the planets, the nakṣatras, and the zodiacal signs---these we call measures (\textit{māna}).''

\begin{commentary}
\textbf{Pada-vibhāga (Word Analysis):}

\dev{(१) सौरैराण्ड (ग) सौरैराण्ड (छ) for (सौरैराण्ड)} --- The term \textit{saura} refers to the solar reckoning. The solar year consists of 365 days 15\textonehalf\,ghatis according to Brahmagupta's system.

\dev{(२) मासैस्तिथय (ग) (छ) for (मासैस्तिथय)} --- The lunar months and tithis constitute the lunar measure.

\dev{(३) च for (पम्)} --- The particle indicating inclusion.

\dev{(४) तदिग्नाकेन्द्र for (तदिग्नाकेन्दु)} --- The digits (of the moon) and the ascendant.
\end{commentary}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{मानांति सौरचन्द्रार्क्षां सावनानि ग्रहानयनमेभिः ।}\\
\dev{मानेन पृथक् चतुर्मितिथ्यर्ब्बहारो त्रं लोकस्य ॥ २ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``The measures of solar, lunar, stellar, and civil reckoning, and the computation of planets by these measures separately---the fourfold manner of computation is in use in the world.''

\begin{shloka}
\dev{युगवर्षं विपुलब्दयनतद्वह्निनिशोद्विद्धिहानयः ।}\\
\dev{सौरास्तिथि करणाऽधिकमासौन राशिपर्व्विक्रियाश्चान्द्रात् ॥ ३ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``A yuga-year, abundant and deficient (days), fire (3), subtraction and addition---these (come) from the solar (system). Tithi, karaṇa, intercalary month, and the changes of zodiacal signs and parva (come) from the lunar (system).''

\begin{shloka}
\dev{यसस्य वन प्रमाणं ग्रहगतृपवासं सुतं चिकिर्सतः ।}\\
\dev{सावनं मानांतु शैया प्रायश्चित्त क्रियाश्चान्याः ॥ ४ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``For one desirous of performing the pramāṇa (measurement), the planetary motion, the rising and setting, and the creation (of almanacs), the civil measure (is useful), as well as the bed (auspicious moment), prāyaścitta (expiatory rites), and other ceremonies.''

\wavydivider

\subsection*{\dev{चतुर्विंशः} --- The Twenty-four Fold System (Verses 5--23)}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{यस्मात्तत्प्रति पश्तं संज्ञा संज्ञातो विना तस्मात् ।}\\
\dev{लोकप्रसिद्धसांख्यपादीनां दशांकाश्च ॥ ५ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``Since (computation) is impossible without names (saṃjñā) given to each object, the names and numerals well-known in the world (are given).''

\begin{commentary}
\textbf{Verse 5 analysis:} The commentator explains that Brahmagupta here gives a systematic nomenclature for the various measures of time and computation. The twenty-four items are enumerated in the following verses.
\end{commentary}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{युगपद्युगाद्यद् या याम्ययां भास्करस्य वा कन्याम् ।}\\
\dev{राश्यर्द्धसंख्यायां मस्तकया दिनदलाद्वेभ्राम् ॥ ६ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{प्रपमेवकृतः सूर्ये तु पुलिशा रोमक वाराही यवनाद्यैः ।}\\
\dev{यस्मात्तस्मादेकः सिद्धान्तो ग्रथ्य रचनात्मकः ॥ ७ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``The sun has been measured by Pulisha, Romaka, Vāraha, and Yavana (Greeks). Therefore, from that (measurement) one must construct a proper siddhānta.''

\begin{shloka}
\dev{यदि भिन्नाः सिद्धान्तभास्कर संस्कान्तयोभि भेदसमाः ।}\\
\dev{सरत्नः पूर्व्वस्यां विपुलत्याकार्द्दयो यस्य ॥ ८ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{तत्र पक्षागरिततं मध्यं गत्युत्तरादयः पञ्च ।}\\
\dev{कुडूाकारे बेधः छिन्द्धि चतुर्थां तरे गोलः ॥ ९ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``In it (the siddhānta) the five (methods): pakṣāgati, madhya, gati, uttara, etc.; kuṭūākāra (transformation), bheda (division), chid (cutting), caturtha (fourth proportional), and the sphere (gola).''

\wavydivider

\begin{shloka}
\dev{भट्ट ब्रह्माचार्येण जिज्ञासुतनयेन गणितगोलविदा ।}\\
\dev{प्रायःश्लेषसंहलं वा स्फुटसिद्धान्तं कृतो ब्रह्मा ॥ १० ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``By Brahmagupta, the son of Jiṣṇu, the knower of mathematics and the sphere, this true siddhānta was composed, (which is) Brahma (itself), adorned with the beauty of elegant composition.''

\begin{shloka}
\dev{मग्रहं युति वत्सांकुं विचित्रमलनाद्विव्रहोत्कसमः ।}\\
\dev{शकनिः कर्म्मबाहुत्वान्निर्भुतातो भास्करमग्रहं ॥ ११ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{प्राज्येयो नैकल्प्योर्द्धच्छिद्दिने स्थितस्य योग्केस्य ।}\\
\dev{शङ्कुश्ये कथयत्यब्दापि वत्सि सूर्य्यंश्च ॥ १२ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{प्रज मया यन्नोकं गोलादग्रं क्षयार्धसतो त्रं तनु ।}\\
\dev{प्रायःश्लोद्दशोऽयं संज्ञाध्यायचतुर्विंशः ॥ १३ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``As much as has not been spoken by me, the remainder of the sphere, thin as a sixth part of a ksaya (decrease), this (is) the prāyaśloka (resumé verse). This (is) the saṃjñā-adhyāya (chapter on nomenclature), the twenty-fourth.''

\begin{commentary}
\textbf{Note on verse numbering:} The original text numbers this as the 23rd adhyāya in the internal count (\dev{इति ब्रह्मगुप्ते मानाध्यायः (२३ अध्याय समाप्तः)}), though the final verse calls it the 24th in another reckoning. The manuscript tradition contains variations in chapter enumeration.
\end{commentary}

\wavydivider

\begin{shloka}
\dev{नष्टंघ्नसावनदिनात् सूर्यदिनां त्वसावन दिनानि ।}\\
\dev{यस्मात्तस्मादश्वं दुर्ल्लभगं मन्दबुद्धिनाम् ॥ ५ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{मानुष्यं पित्रदेवं बाहु यान्यष्टौ वसुर्कालस्य ।}\\
\dev{उत्कर्षि ज्ञानार्थं बाहु स्पष्टं नवममन्यत् ॥ ६ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{द्वौ द्वौ राशिं मकराहतवः षट् सूर्यगति वशाऽघोज्याः ।}\\
\dev{शकिरवसन्तं ग्रीष्मा वर्षा शरदौ सहैमन्ताः ॥ ७ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``Two-two (make) a zodiacal sign; multiplied by Makara (10, i.e.\ 60), these (are) the six (seasons); the solar motion (gives) the seasons: śiśira-vasanta (spring), grīṣma (summer), varṣā (rains), śarad (autumn), and hemanta (winter).''

\wavydivider

\begin{shloka}
\dev{मुख्यारः पूजोभक्तः काके व्यासान्तरेण रविकर्णं मुमुच्या ।}\\
\dev{द्वौ, क्षाया दिश्यन्त्वे चन्द्रकर्णेन शेषम् ॥ ८ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{पञ्चदशाहीनयुक्ताः षट् क्रान्तिकालाद्यरसगुणाः ॥ ६४ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{त्रिज्या विपुलछाया घातस्तच्छति विभाजिताक्षाप्तम् ।}\\
\dev{क्षयो नित्यं याम्यो गोलवशाद्विम्बेक्रान्तिः ॥ ६६ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{क्रान्त्यक्षायुति विभोगाक्षरापदैः शोधिते दिनदलमा ।}\\
\dev{भास्य तिकृतयोः कृतमनुयुतो नयोस्तत्पदे व्यस्ते ॥ ६७ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{पठगुणीतागतं शेषं नाडूच्चै दिवसाङ्कं विभाजितात् त्यात् ।}\\
\dev{दिनदलकर्णगुणाप्ता तया त्रिज्यया फलं कर्णः ॥ ६८ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\wavydivider

\begin{shloka}
\dev{इत्युक्तं क्रमेण सांख्याप्रकरणं प्रयोगविधिश्च ।}\\
\dev{व्यासगुणा दीर्घवृत्तं षड़ांशकक्षापयाम् ॥ ९ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{तस्य व्यास शकि करण्डूतद्विजयया गुणालिप्ता ॥ १० ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{रविकर्णं दूता त्रिजया काके व्यासान्तरं हृता शेषम् ।}\\
\dev{तिजयामुख्यास बधा क्षयि करणं हुतात्मो व्यासः ॥ १० ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{व्यासेन दुर्गति बधात् काके व्यासान्तरके मुक्ति बधम् ।}\\
\dev{प्रोक्ष् दुमध्यमुक्तथा तिथिगुणाप्तां तमो व्यासः ॥ ११ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{योऽधिकमासावमरात्रसम्भवनः सर्वैर्मानानि ।}\\
\dev{प्रायःश्लोकैर्द्दशाभिर्मानाध्यायश्चतुर्विंशः ॥ १२ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\section*{\dev{मध्यमाधिकारः} --- Mean Computation Chapter}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Madhyamādhikāra Commentary (pp.\ 752--838)}
\markboth{Madhyamadhikara Commentary}{}

\lettrine[lines=3,loversize=0.1]{T}{\textit{he Madhyamādhikāra}} constitutes the first and foundational chapter of the commentary (vāsanā-vijñāna-bhāṣya) on the \textit{Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta}. It treats the computation of mean positions of planets (\textit{madhyama-graha}), beginning with a discussion of the motion of the celestial sphere (\textit{bhacakra}), the definition of time (\textit{kāla-pravṛtti}), and the various yuga-systems employed in Indian astronomy.

\subsection*{Introductory Discussion (pp.\ 752--760)}

The commentary opens with a discussion of the \textit{bhacakra} (celestial sphere) and its motion:

\begin{shloka}
\dev{प्रदक्षिणागम् ( दक्षिणावस्स्क्ष्मेण चलायमानम् ) श्रादो ( सर्व्वप्रथमं ) ब्रह्मणा}\\
\dev{(जगद्गुरुपादकेन) सृष्टम् (रचितम्) ग्रयदिध्नयादौ स्थितेनक्षत्रादिमततुल्यचपा-}\\
\dev{तादिमित्र साकं भ्रुवयष्ट्याद्यारेण प्रमाणशील ज्योतिष्मत् प्रदायिनां नक्षत्रादीनां}\\
\dev{चक्रं (गोलं) ब्रह्मणा रचितं यथाप्याचार्ययोः कादीनां वचां न कियते तेषां स्वरूपा-}\\
\dev{भावात् किन्तु स्पष्टादिकाले तेषामपि स्थानाऽऽज्ञानरूपसर्जें कृतमिति ॥ १ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{commentary}
\textbf{Hindi commentary (pp.\ 752--753):} The commentator explains that the celestial sphere (\textit{bhacakra}) was first created by Brahmā (the creator). The sphere, along with the nakṣatras and other celestial bodies, revolves from west to east. The commentator notes that the ācāryas (teachers) do not discuss the nature (\textit{svārūpa}) of these bodies but have only determined their positions (\textit{sthāna}) for the purposes of computation.
\end{commentary}

\wavydivider

\subsection*{Key Verses from the Siddhānta (pp.\ 760--770)}

The commentary elucidates the following root text (\textit{mūla}) verses:

\begin{shloka}
\dev{इदानीमनाख्यानतस्य कालस्य प्रवृत्तिमाह ।}\\
\dev{चैनंसितादेर्द्दयाद्व मानोर्द्धनमासबर्षयुगकल्पः ।}\\
\dev{सृष्ट्यादौ लङ्कायां स्मं प्रवृतta दिनेस्कस्य ॥ ४ ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``Now (Brahmagupta) declares the commencement of that time whose beginning is unmentioned. Day, month, year, yuga, and kalpa began from the sun at Laṅkā at the beginning of creation.''

\begin{commentary}
\textbf{Hindi commentary (pp.\ 761--762):} \dev{चैत्यादि कल्पप्रतिपद्यात् लंकोपलक्षितांभृद्वेकोर्कोदयादिदिवा प्रवृत्ताः} --- From the first tithi of Caitra (the first month), at Laṅkā, the days etc.\ commenced from the sunrise. \dev{रविदिनम्बरः सृष्ट्यादौ कल्पादौ} --- The solar day (number) at the beginning of creation, at the beginning of the kalpa.

The commentator explains the concept of the \textit{kuldiņa} (civil day) in detail. The number of civil days elapsed since the beginning of the current kalpa is 1,58,22,23,78,50,000 according to Brahmagupta's system.
\end{commentary}

\wavydivider

\subsection*{On Time Computation and Planetary Motion (pp.\ 770--790)}

The commentary proceeds with detailed mathematical analysis:

\begin{shloka}
\dev{प्रथमं युगपदं चलनं द्वितीयं पश्चादिनपर्व्वैः}\\
\dev{तृतीयं तु त्रयोदशभिर्मासैः सावनेन वा चतुर्थम् ॥}
\end{shloka}

\begin{commentary}
The four types of time-reckoning are discussed: (1) \textit{yugapada} (yuga-epoch method), (2) \textit{parva} (lunisolar method), (3) \textit{trayodaśa-māsa} (thirteen-month intercalary method), and (4) \textit{sāvana} (civil reckoning). Each method is explained with its mathematical basis and astronomical application.
\end{commentary}

\wavydivider

\subsection*{Planetary Computations (pp.\ 790--820)}

The commentary provides detailed algorithms for computing mean planetary positions:

\textbf{Mean longitude formula:}
\begin{equation*}
\text{Mean longitude} = \frac{\text{ahargaṇa} \times \text{daily motion}}{\text{civil days in a kalpa}} \mod 360°
\end{equation*}

Where:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textit{ahargaṇa} = elapsed civil days since kalpa-beginning
  \item \textit{daily motion} = mean daily motion of the planet
  \item The result is expressed in degrees of the ecliptic
\end{itemize}

The commentary discusses the \textit{mandaphala} (equation of centre) and \textit{śīghraphala} (sighra equation) with detailed trigonometric formulas using the \textit{jyā} (sine) function.

\wavydivider

\subsection*{On the Computation of Weekdays (pp.\ 820--838)}

The final section of this portion treats the computation of weekdays (\textit{vāra-pravṛtti}):

\begin{shloka}
\dev{वारप्रवृत्तिं मनयो वदन्ति सूर्योदयाद्विष्णुराज्ञाध्याम् ।}\\
\dev{उर्वी तथाऽघोषपरत्र तस्याऽर्चराथं देशान्तरनाडिकाभिः ॥ १३६॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``The sages declare the commencement of the week (to be reckoned) from the sunrise (at Laṅkā); on the earth (at other places), it (is determined) by adding or subtracting the desāntara-nāḍikās (longitude difference in time).''

\begin{shloka}
\dev{लङ्कोदयाम्यसुजातप्रथममपरतः पूर्व्वदेशे च पश्चात्—}\\
\dev{दर्शवोत्थानिमष्टटीभिः सवितः क्षदयतो वासरैव प्रवृत्तिः ॥}\\
\dev{यं या सूर्योदयात् प्राक् चरराकलम्बवैश्चास्मियमियगोले ।}\\
\dev{पश्चात् सौम्यगोले युतिर्वियुतिर्वशाच्छोभयोः स्पष्टकालः इति ॥१३६॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``At Laṅkā, the weekday begins with the first nāḍī after sunrise; at a place to the west, (it begins) after (sunrise); with the six or eight nāḍīs (difference), the weekday is determined when the sun rises. For a place on the eastern or western side of the prime meridian, before or after sunrise (at Laṅkā), by addition or subtraction, the correct time (is found).''

\begin{commentary}
\textbf{Hindi commentary (pp.\ 836--838):} The commentator explains that the \textit{vāra} (weekday) begins at sunrise. By this definition, at a place east of the prime meridian (Laṅkā), the weekday begins after subtracting the longitude difference (in ghatīs); at a place west of the meridian, (the weekday begins) before sunrise by the same amount (longitude difference in ghatīs).

In the \textit{Siddhānta-śiromaṇi}, Bhāskarācārya states: ``\textit{arkodayād dvābhyāṃ bhyas tāniḥ prācyāṃ pratिच्यां दिनप्-}''---(the computation proceeds from sunrise with modifications for eastern and western positions).
\end{commentary}

\wavydivider

\subsection*{Concluding Verses (p.\ 838)}

The portion concludes with a verse from the \textit{Manusmṛti}:

\begin{shloka}
\dev{आसीदिदं तमोभूतमप्रज्ञातमलक्षणम् ।}\\
\dev{अप्रतर्क्यमनाऽऽगम्यं प्रसुप्तमिव सर्व्वतः ॥ इति ॥}
\end{shloka}

\textbf{Translation:} ``This (universe) existed as darkness, unknowable, without marks, not to be reasoned about, not to be apprehended, as if asleep everywhere.''

\begin{commentary}
The commentator explains that Manu has stated that at the time of the deluge (\textit{pralaya}), the perception of weekdays and other time measures is not possible. Therefore, the reckoning of weekdays is only possible during the manifest (\textit{sṛṣṭi}) period. Brahmagupta establishes that the weekday begins from sunrise at Laṅkā and is modified by the longitude difference (\textit{deśāntara}) for other locations.
\end{commentary}
