\section*{\dev{उपोद्घातः}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{उपोद्घातः}}

\begin{center}
\dev{श्रीं ॐ मते बलवते हनुमते नमः~।}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{एकं गतप्रत्यागतज्ञानं संख्यात्मकं यत्र बहिर्विषयम्~।}\\
\dev{अन्तःकरणं प्रविशतीव मूढैर्यस्मिन् गणितं व्यक्तमिदं जगत्याम्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{तदेतद् व्यक्तं यदनन्तरं स्यात् कर्म प्रवक्ष्याम्यखिलं गणितस्य~।}\\
\dev{बीजं प्रधानं प्रवदन्ति तज्ज्ञा यस्मिन् व्यक्तं बीजमिति प्रसिद्धम्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{अस्य व्यक्तस्य यदिदं प्रधानं तद् बीजमुक्तं मुनिभिः पुराणैः~।}\\
\dev{अतः परं बीजगणितं प्रवक्ष्ये यस्मिन् व्यक्तं बीजमिति स्थितं वै~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.5cm}

Bhaskara here states that he will explain the eight-fold operations of algebra (\dev{अष्टविधं बीजक्रियायाः}), which are the essence of all computation. The wise may understand it; those of slow intellect may not comprehend it equally well.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{उत्पादकं यत् प्रवदन्ति बुद्धेः स्थितं सुरैरपि~।}\\
\dev{अस्य क्रिया तत्कथयाम्यथवा तद्वीजमष्टधा~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{पूर्वं प्रोक्तं व्यक्तमस्य कथये प्रज्ञा ते च मेऽव्यक्तयुक्तया~।}\\
\dev{यं तु शक्या मन्दबुद्धिर्न तत्समं न तद् द्वयं वीजक्रिया च~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.5cm}

The introduction discusses the fundamental concepts:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item The six operations on unknown quantities (\dev{अव्यक्तकल्पना})
  \item The representation of unknowns by symbols (\dev{यावत् तावत्} --- \textit{yavat tavat}, ``as much as so much'')
  \item The eight operations on algebraic quantities
  \item The use of negative and positive numbers (\dev{धन} and \dev{ऋण})
  \item The concept of surds (\dev{करणी})
  \item The six operations on zero (\dev{खषड्विधम्})
\end{itemize}

Bhaskara declares that algebra is the source of all calculation, and that the wise should study it with diligence. He quotes:

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{ब्रह्मस्फुटसिद्धान्ते च कालक्रियायां तथैव च~।}\\
\dev{ग्रहगणितं निगदितं त्रैराशिकफलं तथा~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Rule for addition of positives and negatives:}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{समानयोर्योगो विषमयोर्व्यवकलनं फलं च~।}\\
\dev{योगे व्यवकलने च विषमयोः पृथक्स्थितिः फलस्य~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{अयं विधिः सर्वत्र धनर्णयोः सङ्कलने व्यवकलने च~।}\\
\dev{परस्परं योजयेत् समानं विषमं पृथक् न्यस्येत्~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{In modern notation:}
\begin{align*}
(+) + (+) &= (+) \quad \text{(wealth + wealth = wealth)} \\
(-) + (-) &= (-) \quad \text{(debt + debt = debt)} \\
(+) + (-) &= \text{difference, sign of larger} \\
(+) - (-) &= (+) \quad \text{(wealth minus debt = wealth)} \\
(-) - (+) &= (-) \quad \text{(debt minus wealth = debt)}
\end{align*}

\section*{\dev{खषड्विधम् -- The Six Operations on Zero}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{खषड्विधम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{वधादौ वियत् खस्य खं खेन घाते खहारो भवेत्~।}\\
\dev{खेन भक्तश्च राशिः खं पुनः शून्ये गुणके जाते~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{शून्यं शून्येन योजितं शून्यं शून्येन वियोजितम्~।}\\
\dev{शून्यस्य वर्गः शून्यं शून्यस्य मूलं शून्यम्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{शून्यस्य घनः शून्यं शून्यघनपदं शून्यम्~।}\\
\dev{सर्वमेतत् षड्विधं खस्य कथितं संख्याविधौ~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{The Six Operations:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \dev{योगः} --- Addition: $0 + 0 = 0$
  \item \dev{व्यवकलनम्} --- Subtraction: $0 - 0 = 0$
  \item \dev{गुणनम्} --- Multiplication: $0 \times a = 0$
  \item \dev{वर्गः} --- Square: $0^2 = 0$
  \item \dev{मूलम्} --- Square root: $\sqrt{0} = 0$
  \item \dev{घनः} --- Cube: $0^3 = 0$
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\subsection*{\dev{खहारः -- The Zero-Divisor (Infinity)}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{अस्मिन् विकारः खहरे न राशावपि प्रविशन्ति ततः बहुष्वपि स्युः~।}\\
\dev{निरुद्धिकालेऽनुलम्बके चापि तथा प्रलये व्यक्तिकाले तथैव~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{तत्रापि विष्णुः प्रभवत्यनन्तः सङ्क्षेपतो न खहरः प्रदुष्येत्~।}\\
\dev{अनन्तरूपेण विभाति विष्णुः खहाररूपेण गणिते तथापि~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{The meaning of \dev{खहार} (\textit{khahara}):}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{खभाजितो राशिः खहरः स्यात्~।}\\
\dev{यस्मिन् खहरे राशौ खेन विभक्ते निरपेक्षं फलं तत्~।}\\
\dev{तत्र प्रविशन्ति बहूनि रूपाणि निर्गच्छन्ति च तथापि न काचित् विकृतिः~।}\\
\dev{अनन्तराशेरिव विष्णोः प्रलयोत्पत्तिकाले जगतां प्रवेशनिर्गमयोः~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

A number divided by zero is called \dev{खहार} (\textit{khahara}), which represents infinity (\dev{अनन्त}). Bhaskara compares this to Lord Vishnu, who remains unchanged despite the constant creation and dissolution of beings entering and leaving him. Even when many quantities enter into or come out of a \dev{खहार}, there is no change in it --- just as Vishnu remains infinite and unchanging.

\vspace{0.3cm}

In modern notation:
\[
\frac{a}{0} = \infty \quad \text{(}\dev{खहार} = \text{infinity)}
\]

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{खं पञ्चयुग्भवति किं वद खस्य वर्गम्~।}\\
\dev{मूलं घनं घनपदं खगुणाश्च पञ्च~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{खेनोद्धृता दश च काः खगुणो निजार्धयुक्तस्-}\\
\dev{त्रिभिश्च गुणितः खहृतस्त्रिषष्टिः~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Answers:}
\begin{align*}
0 + 5 &= 5 \\
0^2 &= 0; \quad \sqrt{0} = 0; \quad 0^3 = 0; \quad \sqrt[3]{0} = 0 \\
5 \times 0 &= 0; \quad 10 \div 0 = \infty \text{ (}\dev{खहार}\text{)}
\end{align*}

\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{घनाणां सङ्कलनम्}}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{\dev{घनाणां सङ्कलने करणसूत्रं बुद्धये~।}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{घनाः समं सङ्गुणिता द्वित्रिभिः समन्विताः}\\
\dev{एकघनाद्विवर्जितास्ते सङ्कलिताः स्युः सदा घनाः~॥ १ ॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Rule for the sum of cubes:} The cubes of the numbers, being multiplied by their own squares, diminished by the cube of unity, are always added together to give the sum of cubes.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Explanation:} The sum of cubes of the first $n$ natural numbers is given by:
\[
1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + \cdots + n^3 = \left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{\dev{उदाहरणम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{एकद्व्यादिघनाः समं सङ्गुणिता द्वित्रिभिः समन्विताः~।}\\
\dev{एकघनाद्विवर्जितास्ते सङ्कलिताः स्युः सदा घनाः~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Example:} Find the sum of the cubes of the first 5 natural numbers.

\textbf{\dev{न्यासः} (Statement):} $n = 5$

\textbf{Calculation:}
\begin{align*}
1^3 + 2^3 + 3^3 + 4^3 + 5^3 &= \left(\frac{5 \times 6}{2}\right)^2 \\
&= 15^2 = 225
\end{align*}

\textbf{Verification:} $1 + 8 + 27 + 64 + 125 = 225$

\section*{\dev{करणीसङ्कलनव्यवकलनम्}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{करणीसङ्कलनव्यवकलनम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{करणीसंयुक्तिरेभिः सङ्कलिता स्यात् समं करणीभिः~।}\\
\dev{करणीभिर्विशिष्टाः पृथक् स्थापनीयाः समं करणीभिः~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Rule:} Surds (\dev{करणी}) having the same radical quantity are added together; those having different radical quantities are set down separately.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Definition of \dev{करणी}:}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\dev{यद् वर्गं मूलं यन्न पूर्णं तत् करणी~।}\\
\dev{अकरणी च यत् पूर्णं तन्मूलं रूपं स्यात्~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

A \dev{करणी} is an irrational quantity whose square root cannot be extracted exactly. If the square root is exact, it is called \dev{रूप} (a rational number).

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{The operation of bringing surds into contact (\dev{स्पर्शं कुरुत}):}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{स्पर्शं कुरुत करण्योः समानकरणीभ्याम्~।}\\
\dev{स्पर्शं कृत्वा ततः सङ्कलनं व्यवकलनं वा कुर्यात्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{स्पर्शक्रिया कथं स्यात् गुण्ये गुणककरणी अन्तर्गता~।}\\
\dev{तयोर्गुणनं कृत्वा या करणी सा स्पर्शकरणी भवति~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{``Bringing into contact'' (\dev{स्पर्शं कुरुत}):} This is Bhaskara's term for making surds commensurable. When two surds have different radicals, we multiply each by the other's radical to bring them to a common radical. If we have $\sqrt{a}$ and $\sqrt{b}$, then:
\[
\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}
\]
This ``contact'' operation allows us to add or subtract surds with different radicals by first converting them to equivalent surds with a common radical.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Example of bringing into contact:}

To add $\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{27}$, first note that $\sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3}$, so they are already in contact (same radical 3). Their sum is $\sqrt{3} + 3\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}$.

To add $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{8}$, we have $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$, so the sum is $3\sqrt{2}$.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Example:} Add $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{3}$, $2\sqrt{3}$, and $5\sqrt{3}$.

\textbf{Sum:} $(1 + 1 + 2 + 5)\sqrt{3} = 9\sqrt{3}$

\textbf{Example:} Add $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{5}$.

\textbf{Result:} $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} + \sqrt{5}$ (set down separately as they have different radicals)

\section*{\dev{करणीमूलम्}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{करणीमूलम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{(१) वर्गं करण्या यदि वा करणीस्तु तयो रूपपक्षं वा बहूनाम्~।}\\
\dev{विशोध्येष्टं पदं ते शेषस्य रूपाणि युतानि निपातयेत्~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Rule:} To find the square root of a surd expression, or of a number with surds added to it, subtract the greatest possible square, and treat the remainder properly.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{रूपद्वयं करण्यौ यदि द्वे स्यातां तयोर्मूलं यदि पूर्णम्~।}\\
\dev{तत् तयोर्मूलयोर्योगो मूलं स्यात् पृथग्वा व्यत्ययात्~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

Bhaskara gives the rule for finding the square root of expressions of the form $a \pm \sqrt{b}$. He shows that if $\sqrt{a + \sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{x} + \sqrt{y}$, then:
\begin{align*}
x + y &= a \\
4xy &= b
\end{align*}

From which $x$ and $y$ can be determined.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Example:} Find $\sqrt{10 + \sqrt{84}}$.

\textbf{\dev{न्यासः}---} $a = 10$, $\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{84}$

We need $x + y = 10$ and $4xy = 84$, so $xy = 21$.

Thus $x = 7$, $y = 3$ (or vice versa).

\textbf{Therefore:} $\sqrt{10 + \sqrt{84}} = \sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3}$

\textbf{Verification:} $(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})^2 = 7 + 2\sqrt{21} + 3 = 10 + \sqrt{84}$ \checkmark

\section*{\dev{कुट्टकः}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{कुट्टकः}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{भाज्यं हरेण विभजेद् भाजकेनापवर्त्य तौ~।}\\
\dev{यावत्तावत्प्रचलितं कुट्टकं तत्र योजयेत्~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Rule:} The Pulverizer (\dev{कुट्टक}) is a method for solving linear indeterminate equations of the form:
\[
ax \pm c = by
\]
where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are known integers, and $x$, $y$ are unknown integers to be found.

\vspace{0.3cm}

The method involves:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Divide the dividend ($a$) by the divisor ($b$) to obtain a quotient and remainder
  \item Use the mutual division process (continued fractions)
  \item Apply the \dev{मतितः} (intelligent) operation to find a solution
  \item Derive the general solution from one particular solution
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{भाज्यं हरेण विभजेत् भागलब्धिं लभेत् ततः~।}\\
\dev{भागशेषं ततो लब्ध्वा पुनरप्येवमावर्तयेत्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{आवर्तनात् षट्पदं यावत् गच्छेत् तत्र मतिः प्रयोज्या~।}\\
\dev{मत्या लब्धिं गुणयेत् शेषं योजयेत् ततः पदम्~॥}
\end{verse}

\subsection*{\dev{उदाहरणम्}}

\textbf{Problem:} Find $x$ and $y$ such that $100x + 90 = 63y$.

\textbf{\dev{न्यासः}:} भाज्यं $= 100$, भाजकः $= 63$, क्षेपः $= 90$

\textbf{Solution:} By the pulverizer method, $x = 18$, $y = 30$ is one solution.

\vspace{0.3cm}

The general solution is:
\begin{align*}
x &= 18 + 63t \\
y &= 30 + 100t
\end{align*}
for any integer $t$.

\end{align*}

%% CHAPTER XVIII -- CYCLIC METHOD
%% ========================================================================
\section*{\dev{चक्रवालम्}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{चक्रवालम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{मूलं मूलं द्विगुणं युक्तं तत्प्रकृत्या हतं सहितम्~।}\\
\dev{सर्वमूलं पुनर्मूलं ततः प्रकृतिमूलं भवेत्~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{The Cyclic Method (\dev{चक्रवालम्}):} This is Bhaskara's most important contribution to algebra. The method proceeds as follows:

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{गुणकारभक्तेऽविशुद्धौ प्रकृत्या लब्धं मत्या गुणयेत्~।}\\
\dev{क्षेपं योजयेद् वा व्येयाद् यावत् क्षेपः सूक्ष्मो भवेत्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{क्षेपक्षेपयोर्योगान्तराभ्यां युक्तं पदं नवं स्यात्~।}\\
\dev{क्षेपक्षेपयोर्गुणनं नवक्षेपः स्यादिति चक्रवालम्~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Step 1:} Choose integers $a$, $b$, $k$ such that $Na^2 + k = b^2$.

\textbf{Step 2:} Find integers $m$ such that $a \cdot m + b$ is divisible by $k$, and $|m^2 - N|$ is minimized.

\textbf{Step 3:} Compute:
\begin{align*}
a' &= \frac{a \cdot m + b}{k} \\
b' &= \frac{b \cdot m + N \cdot a}{k} \\
k' &= \frac{m^2 - N}{k}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Step 4:} If $k' = 1$, then $(a', b')$ is the solution. Otherwise, repeat the process.

\vspace{0.3cm}

This cyclic process eventually yields $k = \pm 1$ or $k = \pm 2$ or $k = \pm 4$, from which the fundamental solution can be obtained.

\clearpage

\section*{\dev{एकवर्णसमीकरणबीजम्}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{एकवर्णसमीकरणबीजम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont

\color{versecolor}
\dev{रूपाणि यत्र स्युः समतुल्यानि तत्र यावत् तुल्यानि~।}\\

\textbf{Rule:} In a single-colored equation (\dev{एकवर्णसमीकरण}), the unknown quantities on both sides are equalized by transposition (\dev{संरमण}). The rule is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Subtract the unknown on the smaller side from the unknown on the greater side
  \item Subtract the known on the greater side from the known on the smaller side
  \item Divide the remainder of the known by the remainder of the unknown
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{यत् दातुं यत् दातव्यं तुल्यं तुल्येन संयोजयेत्~।}\\
\dev{विपरीतानि तुल्यानि तु तुल्यानि समीकुर्यात्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{संरमणं यथोक्तं समीकरणे कार्यं बुद्धिमता~।}\\
\dev{अज्ञातं मूलं तत् तस्मात् लब्धं राशिर्भवेत् स्पष्टः~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{\dev{उदाहरणम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{यावच्चतुष्कं रूपैः सप्तभिः सहितं त्रयोदशभिः~।}\\
\dev{यावद् द्वित्रिभिः सहितं सप्तभिः समं भवति किम्~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Equation:} $4y\bar{a} + 7 = 2y\bar{a} + 13$

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
4y\bar{a} - 2y\bar{a} &= 13 - 7 \\
2y\bar{a} &= 6 \\
y\bar{a} &= 3
\end{align*}

\textbf{Verification:} $4(3) + 7 = 19$ and $2(3) + 13 = 19$ \checkmark

\vspace{0.5cm}

\subsection*{\dev{अष्टविधं समीकरणम् -- The Eight Types of Equations}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{रूपं यावत् वर्गो घनो वर्गवर्घोऽष्टघातो वा~।}\\
\dev{घनवर्गो घनघनश्चेत्यष्टौ प्रकृतयः स्मृताः~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{तासां मिश्रणं मिश्रं तदेकवर्णे समीकरणे~।}\\
\dev{अष्टविधं बीजं तज्ज्ञैः प्रोक्तमतः परम्~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{The eight types of equations with one unknown (\dev{यावत्तावत्}):}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{\dev{रूपसमीकरणम्}} --- Pure numerical: $c = d$
  \item \textbf{\dev{यावत्समीकरणम्}} --- Linear: $ax + b = cx + d$
  \item \textbf{\dev{वर्गसमीकरणम्}} --- Quadratic: $ax^2 + bx + c = dx^2 + ex + f$
  \item \textbf{\dev{घनसमीकरणम्}} --- Cubic: $ax^3 + \ldots = bx^3 + \ldots$
  \item \textbf{\dev{वर्गवर्गसमीकरणम्}} --- Biquadratic: $ax^4 + \ldots = bx^4 + \ldots$
  \item \textbf{\dev{घनवर्गसमीकरणम्}} --- Fifth degree
  \item \textbf{\dev{घनघनसमीकरणम्}} --- Sixth degree
  \item \textbf{\dev{मिश्रसमीकरणम्}} --- Mixed: combinations of the above
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{\dev{शेषात्समीकरणम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{यद् दातुं यद् दातव्यं तुल्यं तुल्येन संयोजयेत्~।}\\
\dev{विपरीतानि तुल्यानि तु तुल्यानि समीकुर्यात्~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Rule:} When equal quantities are on both sides, transpose and change signs as needed. This is the method of \dev{संरमण} (transposition).

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{\dev{उदाहरणानि}}

\textbf{(1)} $3y\bar{a} + 5 = 2y\bar{a} + 12$

\textbf{Solution:} $y\bar{a} = 7$

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{(2)} $5y\bar{a} - 8 = 3y\bar{a} + 10$

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
5y\bar{a} - 3y\bar{a} &= 10 + 8 \\
2y\bar{a} &= 18 \\
y\bar{a} &= 9
\end{align*}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{(3)} $7y\bar{a} + 15 = 4y\bar{a} + 3$

\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
7y\bar{a} - 4y\bar{a} &= 3 - 15 \\
3y\bar{a} &= -12 \\
y\bar{a} &= -4
\end{align*}

\subsection*{\dev{उदाहरणानि}}

\textbf{(1) Quadratic:} $y\bar{a}^2 - 5y\bar{a} + 6 = 0$

\textbf{Solution:} Factoring: $(y\bar{a} - 2)(y\bar{a} - 3) = 0$, so $y\bar{a} = 2$ or $y\bar{a} = 3$

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{(2) Quadratic:} $2y\bar{a}^2 + 7y\bar{a} - 15 = 0$

\textbf{Solution:} Using the quadratic formula (as described by Bhaskara):
\[
y\bar{a} = \frac{-7 \pm \sqrt{49 + 120}}{4} = \frac{-7 \pm 13}{4}
\]
So $y\bar{a} = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}$ or $y\bar{a} = \frac{-20}{4} = -5$

\section*{\dev{अनेकवर्णमध्यमाहरणम्}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{\dev{अनेकवर्णमध्यमाहरणम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{मध्यमाहरणं तेषां यथा स्थानं प्रयोजयेत्~।}\\
\dev{तुल्यानां तुल्यतां नीत्वा मध्यमं हरेद् बुधः~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Rule:} The elimination of the middle term (\dev{मध्यमाहरण}) is performed by equalizing the coefficients of one unknown and subtracting. This is the method for solving systems of linear equations with multiple unknowns.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{एकवर्णसमीकरणे तावन्मूलं यावत्तावत्~।}\\
\dev{बहुवर्णे मध्यमाहरणेनैकवर्णं कुर्यात्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{मध्यमाहरणं कथं स्यात् समानजातीयानां गुणानाम्~।}\\
\dev{तुल्यीकृत्य व्यत्यासेन हरणं मध्यमाहरणम्~॥}\\[0.3cm]
\dev{यावत्तावत् कालकं च नीलकं च पीतकं च~।}\\
\dev{तेषां समीकरणे मध्यमाहरणेनैकं वर्णं नयेत्~॥}
\end{verse}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Method of \dev{मध्यमाहरण} (elimination):}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Write the equations one below the other
  \item Equalize the coefficients of one unknown by multiplication
  \item Subtract one equation from the other to eliminate that unknown
  \item Solve the resulting single-unknown equation
  \item Substitute back to find the other unknowns
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\subsection*{\dev{उदाहरणम्}}

\begin{verse}
\devanagarifont
\centering
\color{versecolor}
\dev{द्वियावत् त्रिकालकं षड् रूपं त्रियावत् द्विकालकं सप्त~।}\\
\dev{चतुर्यावत् पञ्चकालकं नव समानि भवन्ति चेत्~॥}
\end{verse}

\textbf{Equations:}
\begin{align*}
2x + 3y + 6 &= 0 \\
3x + 2y + 7 &= 0 \\
4x + 5y + 9 &= 0
\end{align*}

From equations (1) and (2):
\begin{align*}
6x + 9y + 18 &= 0 \\
6x + 4y + 14 &= 0 \\
5y + 4 &= 0 \Rightarrow y = -\frac{4}{5}
\end{align*}

Substituting: $2x + 3(-\frac{4}{5}) + 6 = 0 \Rightarrow 2x = \frac{12}{5} - 6 = -\frac{18}{5} \Rightarrow x = -\frac{9}{5}$

\section*{Editorial Notes (\dev{टिप्पणी})}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Editorial Notes}

The following explanatory notes were added by the editors to clarify Bhaskara's text:

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{On notation:} Bhaskara uses \dev{यावत्} (\textit{yavat}) for the unknown quantity, corresponding to our modern $x$. The colors \dev{कालक} (black), \dev{नीलक} (blue), \dev{पीतक} (yellow), \dev{हरितक} (green), etc., represent $y$, $z$, $u$, $v$, etc.

  \item \textbf{On positive and negative numbers:} Bhaskara fully accepts positive and negative numbers, calling them \dev{धन} (wealth/positive) and \dev{ऋण} (debt/negative). He gives clear rules for arithmetic operations with negatives, which are equivalent to our modern sign rules.

  \item \textbf{On zero:} Bhaskara's treatment of zero (\dev{ख}) is comprehensive. He gives six operations on zero, and defines the concept of \dev{खहार} (\textit{khahara}), a number divided by zero, which he identifies with infinity (\dev{अनन्त}). His comparison of \dev{खहार} to Lord Vishnu is one of the most celebrated passages in Indian mathematics.

  \item \textbf{On surds:} The \dev{करणी} (surd) operations demonstrate sophisticated understanding of irrational numbers. Bhaskara can add, subtract, multiply, divide, and extract roots of surd expressions. The operation of \dev{स्पर्श} (bringing into contact) is his innovation for making surds commensurable.

  \item \textbf{On the cyclic method:} The \dev{चक्रवाल} method for solving $Nx^2 + 1 = y^2$ is one of the greatest achievements of Indian mathematics. It always converges to the minimal solution, and does so remarkably quickly. Bhaskara's solution for $N = 61$ was centuries ahead of European mathematics.

  \item \textbf{On equations:} Bhaskara classifies equations into several types and gives systematic methods for each. His treatment of quadratic equations is complete, including cases with two, one, or no real solutions. The eight types of equations (\dev{अष्टविधं समीकरणम्}) cover all cases up to the sixth degree.
\end{enumerate}
