\section{Book I: On Compound Ratios}
\label{sec:book1}

\subsection*{General Rule}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{General Rule}

\bilingualpair{\noindent\textcolor{arabicblue}{\textbf{\ar{الباب الأول: في أصول الأسباب المركبة}}}}{\noindent\textcolor{sectioncolor}{\textbf{Book I: On the Principles of Compound Ratios}}}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَنَقُولُ: إِنَّ أَصْلَ الْمَعْنَى فِي الْقَوْلِ إِنَّ هٰذَا النِّسْبَةَ مُرَكَّبَةٌ مِنْ هٰذَيْنِ النِّسْبَتَيْنِ أَوْ هٰذَا النِّسْبَةَ مُفَرَّقَةٌ إِلَى هٰذَيْنِ النِّسْبَتَيْنِ: أَنَّ النِّسْبَةَ الْمُرَكَّبَةَ نِسْبَتَانِ مَفْرُوقَتَانِ مِنْ نِسْبَةٍ، فَإِذَا وُضِعَتْ إِحْدَاهُمَا فِي الْأُخْرَى عَلَى التَّكْرَارِ حَصَلَ نِسْبَةٌ مَعْيُنَةٌ.}}{\noindent\textbf{We say:} The basis of the meaning in saying that ``this ratio is composed of these two ratios,'' or that ``this ratio is decomposed into these two ratios,'' is that the compound ratio consists of two ratios separated from one ratio, so that when one is placed in the other by repetition, a certain ratio is obtained.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَأَمَّا التَّفْرِيقُ: فَهُوَ أَنْ تَكُونَ النِّسْبَةُ مَفْرُوقَةً إِلَى نِسْبَتَيْنِ فَيَكُونُ كُلُّ وَاحِدَةٍ مِنْهُمَا بِمَنْزِلَةِ الْجُزْءِ مِنَ الْكُلِّ.}}{As for decomposition: it is that the ratio be divided into two ratios, so that each one of them is in the position of a part from the whole.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{فَإِذَا وُجِدَ هٰذَا فَلْنَقُلْ: أَيُّ نِسْبَةٍ كَانَتْ لِثَلَاثَةِ مُتَجَانِسَاتٍ إِلَى إِحْدَاهُنَّ فَهِيَ مُرَكَّبَةٌ مِنْ نِسْبَتِهِ إِلَى الثَّالِثَةِ وَنِسْبَةِ تِلْكَ الثَّالِثَةِ إِلَى الْأُولَى.}}{When this is established, let us say: Any ratio whatsoever of three homogeneous quantities to one of them is composed of its ratio to the third and the ratio of that third to the first.}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\noindent\textit{[Just as to give exact measure of discontinuous quantity we make use of certain properties essential to continuous magnitude, such as infinite divisibility, so also in the measure of continuous magnitude we bring in certain properties essential to discontinuous quantity. Such is the supposition according to which continuous magnitude is composed of discrete or discontinuous units which we imagine in order to arrive at the measure of continuous magnitude.]}

\ornament

%% ========================================================================
%% PROPOSITION I
%% ========================================================================
\subsection{First Proposition}
\label{prop:1}

\bilingualpair{\ar{\textbf{المسألة الأولى:} فَلْيَكُنْ ثَلَاثَةُ مُتَجَانِسَاتٍ أ وَ ب وَ ج، فَأَقُولُ: إِنَّ نِسْبَةَ أ إِلَى ب تُسَاوِي نِسْبَةَ أ إِلَى ج فِي نِسْبَةِ ج إِلَى ب.}}{\noindent\textbf{Proposition I.} Let there be three homogeneous quantities A, B, and C. I say that the ratio of A to B is equal to the ratio of A to C multiplied by the ratio of C to B.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{center}
\textbf{In modern notation:}
\[
\frac{A}{B} = \frac{A}{C} \times \frac{C}{B}, \quad
\frac{A}{C} = \frac{A}{B} \times \frac{B}{C}, \quad
\frac{B}{C} = \frac{B}{A} \times \frac{A}{C}
\]
\end{center}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَلْنَجْعَلْ وَاحِدَةً تَقِيسُ هٰذِهِ الْمُتَجَانِسَاتِ، وَلْتَكُنْ تِلْكَ الْوَاحِدَةُ تَقِيسُ د كَمَا تَقِيسُ أ ج، وَتَقِيسُ تَ كَمَا تَقِيسُ ج ب، وَتَقِيسُ هـ كَمَا تَقِيسُ أ ب. فَد هِيَ مِقْدَارُ نِسْبَةِ أ إِلَى ج، وَتَ هِيَ مِقْدَارُ نِسْبَةِ ج إِلَى ب، وَهـ هِيَ مِقْدَارُ نِسْبَةِ أ إِلَى ب.}}{Let us suppose that a unit measures these quantities, and let that unit measure D as A measures C; let it measure T as C measures B; and let it measure H as A measures B. Then D is the \textit{quantity} of the ratio $A:C$; T is the quantity of the ratio $C:B$; and H is the quantity of the ratio $A:B$.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَهٰذَا لِأَنَّ كُلَّ نِسْبَةٍ لَهَا اسْمٌ وَاحِدٌ مَعَ الْعَدَدِ الَّذِي تَقِيسُهُ الْوَاحِدَةُ بِقِيَاسِ مَا يَقِيسُهُ طَرَفُهَا الْأَوَّلُ مِنْ طَرَفِهَا الثَّانِي. وَالْعَدَدُ الَّذِي لَهُ اسْمُ النِّسْبَةِ هُوَ مِقْدَارُهَا.}}{This is because every ratio has one name with the number that the unit measures in the same manner as the first term of the ratio measures the second. And the number that has the name of the ratio is its quantity.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{فَإِذَا كَانَ هٰكَذَا فَقَدْ بَيَّنَّا أَنَّ التَّرْكِيبَ نَحْوُ التَّكْرَارِ، وَالتَّكْرَارُ بَيْنَ عَدَدَيْنِ هُوَ ضَرْبُ أَحَدِهِمَا فِي الْآخَرِ، فَهـ هُوَ أَيْضًا حَاصِلُ ضَرْبِ د فِي تَ.}}{When this is so, we have explained that composition is a kind of repetition, and repetition between two numbers is the multiplication of one of them by the other; therefore H is also the product of D multiplied by T.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textit{[Al-Tusi here establishes the fundamental property of compound ratios: that any ratio between two quantities can be decomposed through an intermediate third quantity. This is the foundation upon which all subsequent propositions rest. The proof proceeds by proportion theory:]}

\bilingualpair{\ar{إِنَّ أ:ج::وَاحِدَة:د، وَبِالْعَكْسِ ج:أ::د:وَاحِدَةٍ. وَأَيْضًا أ:ب::وَاحِدَة:هـ، فَبِالنِّسْبَةِ الْمُرَتَّبَةِ يَكُونُ ج:ب::د:هـ، وَج:ب::وَاحِدَة:تَ، فَوَاحِدَة:تَ::د:هـ، فَتَعْلَمُ أَنَّ تَ فِي د تُسَاوِي هـ فِي وَاحِدَةٍ، وَحَاصِلُ كُلِّ عَدَدٍ فِي وَاحِدَةٍ هُوَ ذٰلِكَ الْعَدَدُ، فَهـ تُسَاوِي تَ فِي د، فَنِسْبَةُ أ إِلَى ب تُسَاوِي نِسْبَةَ أ إِلَى ج فِي نِسْبَةِ ج إِلَى ب. وَهُوَ الْمَطْلُوبُ.}}{Indeed, $A:C::1:D$, and conversely $C:A::D:1$. Also $A:B::1:H$; therefore by the \textit{ordered proportion} we obtain $C:B::D:H$, and $C:B::1:T$, so $1:T::D:H$, which gives finally $T \times D = H \times 1$. Now the product of any number by the unit is that number itself; therefore $H = T \times D$, that is,
\[
\frac{A}{B} = \frac{A}{C} \times \frac{C}{B},
\]
\textit{quod erat demonstrandum.}}

\ornament

%% ========================================================================
%% PROPOSITION II
%% ========================================================================
\subsection{Second Proposition}
\label{prop:2}

\bilingualpair{\ar{\textbf{المسألة الثانية:} نِسْبَةُ أَحَدِ الثَّلَاثَةِ أ وَ ب وَ ج إِلَى آخَرَ تُسَاوِي نِسْبَةَ الثَّالِثِ إِلَى ذٰلِكَ الْآخَرِ فِي نِسْبَةِ هٰذَا إِلَى الْأَوَّلِ.}}{\noindent\textbf{Proposition II.} The ratio of any one of the three quantities A, B, C to any other is equal to the ratio of the third to that latter composed with that of that quantity to the first.}

\begin{center}
\[
\frac{A}{B} = \frac{A}{C} \times \frac{C}{B} = \frac{C}{B} \times \frac{A}{C}
\]
\textit{for the composition of ratios is like the multiplication of numbers; one may transpose the factors.}
\end{center}

\ornament

%% PROPOSITIONS III--XIV SUMMARY
%% ========================================================================
\subsection{Summary of Propositions III--XIV}
\label{sec:prop3-14}

\begin{center}
\textcolor{sectioncolor}{\textbf{Summary of Propositions III--XIV / \ar{ملخص المسائل من الثالثة إلى الرابعة عشر}}}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~III.} Every ratio composed of two arbitrary ratios is also composed of two other ratios that can be deduced from those by means of homogeneous quantities taken in the first and in the second ratio.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~IV.} If any quantity measures two others, the ratio of these two latter quantities between them is equal to the ratio composed of the inverse of the first ratio with the second ratio.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~V.} The ratio composed of two arbitrary ratios is also composed of the inverse of the first of these two ratios with the inverse of the second.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~VI.} Every composed ratio can be decomposed into simple ratios in two ways: either as fractions having unity for numerator, or as expressions having unity for denominator.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~VII.} If one has six quantities of which the composed ratios give rise to other composed ratios, then to decompose any composed ratio it suffices to know the quantities of a single column.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~VIII.} Every composed ratio gives rise to 9 other composed ratios and to 18 expressions of component ratios, or with their inverses 36 expressions.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~IX.} Two ways of decomposing a composed ratio: by making the two terms of the second ratio precede, or by making them follow the two terms of the first ratio.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~X.} Every composed ratio gives rise to 9 other composed ratios and to 18 expressions of component ratios, or with their inverses 36 expressions of composed ratios. This number can be carried to 72.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~XI.} If two of the six quantities taken each in one of the two members are equal, the 4 others are in proportion.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~XII.} If the two equal quantities belong to the same member, the other 4 are not proportional.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~XIII.} Every simple ratio can be considered as composed if one multiplies it by a ratio of identity, e.g.~if $\frac{A}{B} = \frac{C}{D}$, one also has $\frac{A}{B} = \frac{C}{D} \times \frac{D}{D}$.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Prop.~XIV.} Every simple ratio of identity is equivalent to any ratio whatsoever multiplied by its inverse: $\frac{A}{A} = \frac{X}{X} \times \frac{X}{X}$.

\section{Book II: On the Plane Quadrilateral Figure}
\label{sec:book2}

\bilingualpair{\noindent\textcolor{arabicblue}{\textbf{\ar{المقالة الثانية: في شكل القطاع والنسب الموجودة فيه}}}}{\noindent\textcolor{sectioncolor}{\textbf{Book II: On the Figure of Secants and the Ratios Found Therein}}}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَهٰذَا الشَّكْلُ يُسَمَّى شَكْلَ الْقِطَّاعِ لِأَنَّهُ مُنشَأٌ مِنْ أَرْبَعَةِ خُطُوطٍ مُقَاطِعَةٍ، وَيُسَمَّى أَيْضًا شَكْلَ الْقَطَائِعِ الْمُكَمَّلَةِ. وَقَدْ أَطْلَقَ عَلَيْهِ بَعْضُ الْمُتَأَخِّرِينَ اسْمَ الْمُخَمَّسِ لِأَنَّهُ يَحْتَوِي عَلَى خَمْسَةِ أَضْلَاعٍ فِي الْحَقِيقَةِ إِذَا أُضِيفَ إِلَيْهِ قُطْرٌ وَاحِدٌ.}}{\noindent This figure is called \textit{shakl al-qa\d{t}\d{t}a`} (the figure of secants) because it is generated from four intersecting lines, and is also called the figure of \textit{complete secants}. Some later scholars have given it the name of ``pentagonal figure'' because it contains five sides in reality when one diagonal is added.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَقَدْ أَخْطَأَ حُسَامُ الدِّينِ فِي قَوْلِهِ إِنَّ هٰذَا الشَّكْلَ إِنَّمَا يَكُونُ فِي تِسْعَةِ أَشْكَالٍ، وَهُوَ فِي اثْنَيْ عَشَرَ شَكْلًا. وَقَدْ بَيَّنَّا ذٰلِكَ فِي هٰذَا الْمَكَانِ وَأَثْبَتْنَاهُ بِالْبُرْهَانِ.}}{\noindent\textbf{Note.} Hussam al-Din erred in saying that this figure only occurs in nine configurations; it is in fact twelve figures. We have demonstrated this at this point and proved it rigorously.}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\subsection{The Twelve Figures of the Quadrilateral}
\label{sec:12figs}

\noindent\textit{[The quadrilateral figure (\textit{shakl al-qa\d{t}\d{t}a`}) is generated by the intersection of four straight lines. The twelve configurations are classified into three groups of four:]}

\begin{center}
\textbf{I.} The point of intersection of two lines falls between the two points of intersection on each of them.\\[0.3cm]
\textbf{II.} The point of intersection falls outside one or both segments.\\[0.3cm]
\textbf{III.} More complex configurations with all points of intersection external.\\[0.3cm]
\end{center}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَقَدْ بَيَّنَ الْمُتَقَدِّمُونَ أَنَّ نِسْبَةَ خَطِّ أ ب إِلَى خَطِّ ب د مُرَكَّبَةٌ مِنْ نِسْبَةِ أ هـ إِلَى هـ ج وَنِسْبَةِ ج و إِلَى و د. وَهٰذَا مَا يَتَضَمَّنُهُ كُلُّ وَاحِدٍ مِنَ الْأَشْكَالِ الثَّنَا عَشَرَ.}}{\noindent The ancients have demonstrated that the ratio of line AB to line BD is composed of the ratio of AE to EC and the ratio of CF to FD. And this is what is contained in each one of the twelve figures.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{فَمِنَ النُّقْطَةِ أ نَخْرُجُ خَطًّا أ هـ مُوَازِيًا لِخَطِّ ج د حَتَّى يُقَابِلَ خَطَّ ب هـ. فَنَحْصُلُ عَلَى أ هـ:ج و::أ هـ:هـ ج بِسَبَبِ الْمُثَلَّثَيْنِ الْمُتَشَابِهَيْنِ هـ أ هـ وَ هـ ج و.}}{From point A draw the line AH parallel to CD until it meets BE. You will have $AH : CF :: AE : EC$ because of the similar triangles EAH, ECF; and $AH : FD :: AB : BD$ because of the similar triangles AFH, DBF.}

\begin{center}
\[
\frac{AH}{FD} = \frac{AE}{EC} \times \frac{CF}{DF}
\]
\textit{(replacing AH by its value drawn from the first proportion,)} therefore also
\[
\frac{AB}{BD} = \frac{AE}{EC} \times \frac{CF}{FD}.
\]
\end{center}

\label{sec:book3}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\subsection{On Chords, Arcs, and Sines}
\label{sec:chords-arcs}

\begin{proposition}[Fundamental Theorem of Sines]
In any plane triangle, the ratio of the sides is equal to the ratio of the sines of the opposite angles:
\[
\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}
\]
\end{proposition}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{هٰذِهِ هِيَ الْقَاعِدَةُ الْأَصْلِيَّةُ الَّتِي بَنَى عَلَيْهَا جَمِيعُ مَا يَأْتِي فِي عِلْمِ الْمُثَلَّثَاتِ الْكُرَوِيَّةِ. وَقَدْ أَثْبَتَّهَا بِأَكْثَرَ مِنْ وَجْهٍ وَطَرِيقَةٍ.}}{This is the fundamental theorem upon which all that follows in the science of spherical triangles is built. I have proved it by more than one method and way.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَقَدْ طَبَّقَ الْمُتَقَدِّمُونَ هٰذَا عَلَى حِسَابِ فَلَكِيَّاتِهِمْ وَأَعْمَالِهِمْ فِي عِلْمِ الْهَيْئَةِ، وَلَمْ يُفَرِّقُوا بَيْنَ الْمُثَلَّثِ الْمُسْتَقِيمِ وَالْمُثَلَّثِ الْكُرَوِيِّ إِلَّا عَلَى وَجْهِ التَّقْرِيبِ.}}{The ancients applied this to their astronomical calculations and their works in the science of configuration (astronomy), and did not distinguish between the plane triangle and the spherical triangle except approximately.}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\subsection{The Fundamental Problem}
\label{sec:fundamental-problem}

\bilingualpair{\noindent\textcolor{arabicblue}{\textbf{\ar{المسألة الأساسية}}}}{\noindent\textcolor{sectioncolor}{\textbf{The Fundamental Problem}}}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{\textbf{الفصل الثالث:} الْمَسْألَةُ الْأَصْلِيَّةُ: مَعْرِفَةُ جَمْعِ قَوْسَيْنِ أَوْ تَفْرِيقِهِمَا مَعَ مَعْرِفَةِ نِسْبَةِ جِيْبَيْهِمَا، فَإِيجَادُ الْقَوْسَيْنِ. وَإِثْبَاتُ الْحِسَابِ لِلْحَالَتَيْنِ.}}{\noindent\textbf{Chapter III.} \textit{The fundamental problem:} knowing the sum of two arcs or their difference together with knowing the ratio of their sines, then finding the two arcs. Establishment of the calculation for the two cases.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textit{[This fundamental problem --- given $\alpha \pm \beta$ and $\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\beta}$, find $\alpha$ and $\beta$ --- is of central importance in spherical astronomy. Al-Tusi gives a complete algebraic solution, reducing it to the solution of a quadratic equation. Abu Nasr ibn `Iraq had previously solved it geometrically.]}

\section{Book IV: On the Spherical Quadrilateral}
\label{sec:book4}

\bilingualpair{\noindent\textcolor{arabicblue}{\textbf{\ar{المقالة الرابعة: في شكل القطاع الكروي والنسب الموجودة فيه}}}}{\noindent\textcolor{sectioncolor}{\textbf{Book IV: On the Spherical Quadrilateral and the Ratios Found Therein}}}

\subsection{Theorems of Ptolemy Applied to the Spherical Quadrilateral}
\label{sec:ptolemy}

\bilingualpair{\ar{\textbf{الفصل الثاني:} فِي تَطْبِيقِ أَحْكَامِ شَكْلِ الْقِطَّاعِ الْمُسَطَّحِ عَلَى جِيُوبِ شَكْلِ الْقِطَّاعِ الْكُرَوِيِّ. نِسْبَةُ بُطْلَمْيُوسَ الْمُعْلَنَةِ.}}{\noindent\textbf{Chapter II.} On the application of the theorems of the plane quadrilateral to the sines of the spherical quadrilateral. The explicit ratio of Ptolemy.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{\textbf{الفصل الثالث:} نِسْبَةُ بُطْلَمْيُوسَ الْمُضْمَرَةِ. بُرْهَانُهَا. وَلِمَاذَا هٰذَا الْبُرْهَانُ وَإِنْ كَانَ صَحِيحًا لَيْسَ بِكَافٍ فِي الْحَالَةِ الْحَاضِرَةِ. الْقَاعِدَةُ التَّيَقُّنِيَّةُ. تَرْكِيعُ الْحَالَاتِ السَّبْعِ وَالْعِشْرِينَ الَّتِي تَرَدَّدَ فِيهَا صَاحِبُنَا إِلَى ثَلَاثَةَ عَشَرَ.}}{\noindent\textbf{Chapter III.} The implicit ratio of Ptolemy. Its demonstration. Why this demonstration, however exact it may be, is not sufficient in the present case. The preliminary lemma. Reduction of the 27 cases in which our author hesitated to 13. Examination of each case in particular.}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{proposition}[The Sine Theorem for Spherical Triangles]
In any spherical triangle, the sines of the sides are in the same ratio as the sines of the opposite angles:
\[
\frac{\sin a}{\sin A} = \frac{\sin b}{\sin B} = \frac{\sin c}{\sin C}
\]
\end{proposition}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَمِنْ هٰذَا يَتَبَيَّنُ أَنَّ جِيبَ قَوْس أ ب إِلَى جِيبِ قَوْس أ ج كَنِسْبَةِ جِيبِ قَوْس أ د إِلَى جِيبِ قَوْس د ب، وَذٰلِكَ مَا أَرَدْنَا إِثْبَاتَهُ. وَإِنِ انْطَبَقَ أَحَدُ الْمُثَلَّثَيْنِ عَلَى الْآخَرِ فِي كُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْ هٰذِهِ الْأَشْكَالِ فَالْبَيَانُ عَلَى قِيَاسِ ذٰلِكَ.}}{From this it is clear that the sine of arc AB is to the sine of arc AC as the sine of arc AD is to the sine of arc DB, which is what we wanted to prove. And if one of the triangles coincides with the other in any one of these configurations, the explanation follows the same pattern.}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{\textbf{الفصل الرابع:} كَيْفَ يُؤَدِّي فَحْصُ النِّسْبَتَيْنِ الْمُضْمَرَةِ وَالْمُعْلَنَةِ إِلَى مِفْتَاحِ جَمِيعِ الْبُرْهَانَاتِ الْأُخْرَى. مُقَارَنَةُ مَسْلَكِ هٰذَا الْبُرْهَانِ مَعَ قَوَاعِدِ الْقِيَاسِ الِاسْتِدْلَالِيِّ.}}{\noindent\textbf{Chapter IV.} How the examination of the implicit and explicit ratios gives the key to all other demonstrations. \subsection{On Inclinations and the Sine Theorem}
\label{sec:inclinations}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَفِي هٰذِهِ الْمُثَلَّثَاتِ يُسَمَّى قَوْسُ ب ج \textit{مَيْلَ} قَوْسِ أ ج، وَهٰذَا الْمَيْلُ ب ج هُوَ نَصِيبُ قَوْسِ أ ج مِنَ الْمَيْلِ الْكُلِّيِّ لِدَائِرَةِ أ د عَلَى دَائِرَةِ أ هـ، وَهُوَ الَّذِي تَقِيسُهُ زَاوِيَةُ أ.}}{In these triangles, arc BC is called the \textit{inclination} of arc AC, which inclination BC is but the part of arc AC in the total inclination of circle AD to circle AE, which the angle A measures. And if arc BC is related to arc AB, then BC is called the second inclination.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَهٰذَا مَا يُسَمِّيهِ أَبُو رَيْحَانَ الْبِيرُونِيُّ \textit{الْعَرْضَ}، فَقَوْسُ ب ج يَكُونُ الْمَيْلَ الْأَوَّلَ بِالنِّسْبَةِ لِأ ج، وَالْمَيْلَ الثَّانِيَ بِالنِّسْبَةِ لِقَوْسِ أ ب.}}{This is what Abu Rayhan al-Biruni calls the \textit{width}; so arc BC would be the first inclination with respect to AC, and the second inclination with respect to arc AB.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{فَفِي هٰذَا الْمَعْنَى نَقُولُ: إِنَّ نِسَبَ جِيُوبِ الْمَيَالِي تُسَاوِي نِسَبَ جِيُوبِ أَقْوَاسِهَا، وَنِسْبَةُ جِيبِ كُلِّ مَيْلٍ إِلَى جِيبِ قَوْسِهِ تُسَاوِي نِسْبَةَ جِيبِ مَيْلٍ آخَرَ إِلَى جِيبِ قَوْسِهِ.}}{In this manner of speaking we say that the ratios of the sines of the inclinations are equal to the ratios of the sines of their arcs, and the ratio of the sine of any inclination to the sine of its arc is equal to the ratio of the sine of another inclination to the sine of its arc.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textit{[In modern notation:]}\\
If $BC$ is the inclination of $AC$, and $LM$ is the inclination of $AE$, then:
\[
\frac{\sin BC}{\sin LM} = \frac{\sin AC}{\sin AE}
\]

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَإِنْ لَمْ يَنْطَبِقْ مُثَلَّثُ أ ب ج عَلَى مُثَلَّثِ د أ هـ، لٰكِنْ كَانَتْ زَاوِيَةُ أ فِي هٰذَيْنِ الْمُثَلَّثَيْنِ مُتَسَاوِيَةً، وَكَانَتْ زَاوِيَتَا ب وَ هـ قَائِمَتَيْنِ، فَالْقَاعِدَةُ ثَابِتَةٌ.}}{If triangle ABC does not coincide with triangle DAE, but nevertheless angle A is equal in these two triangles and angles B and E are right, the theorem remains proved nonetheless.}

\subsection*{Note on the Edition}

This typeset edition reproduces the bilingual edition published in Constantinople (Istanbul) in 1891 under the authority of the Ottoman Ministry of Public Instruction. The original edition comprised:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{French Translation} (pp.~1--214): A complete translation by Alexandre Pacha Carath\'eodory (1833--1906), Ottoman diplomat and mathematician, with extensive notes and a biographical appendix on the mathematicians cited in the treatise.
\item \textbf{Arabic Original} (pp.~1--178): The original Arabic text, printed in lithographic form from the manuscript belonging to Edhem Pasha, with its own pagination in Arabic numerals.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The Significance of the Work}

Al-Tusi's \textit{Kitab Shakl al-Qa\d{t}\d{t}a`} represents one of the most important contributions to trigonometry in the medieval Islamic world. The work:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Systematized the theory of compound ratios} (Book I), providing a complete algebraic foundation for ratio theory that went beyond Euclid's \textit{Elements}.
\item \textbf{Analyzed the complete quadrilateral figure} (Book II), classifying all twelve configurations generated by four intersecting lines and deriving the fundamental proportional relationships.
\item \textbf{Established the foundations of spherical trigonometry} (Books III--IV), including the sine theorem for spherical triangles and its systematic application.
\item \textbf{Developed practical computational methods} (Book V) for solving spherical triangles, which were essential for astronomical calculations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Al-Tusi's Key Contributions to Trigonometry}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{First systematic treatment} of plane and spherical trigonometry as an independent science, separate from astronomy.
\item \textbf{Sine law for plane triangles:} $\dfrac{a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{c}{\sin C}$
\item \textbf{Sine law for spherical triangles:} $\dfrac{\sin a}{\sin A} = \dfrac{\sin b}{\sin B} = \dfrac{\sin c}{\sin C}$
\item \textbf{Polar triangle method:} The transformation of angle-given problems to side-given problems via the polar triangle.
\item \textbf{Complete classification} of spherical triangle cases (27 reduced to 13).
\item \textbf{Systematic use} of tangent and cotangent functions.
\end{itemize}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَأَمَّا التَّفْرِيقُ: فَهُوَ أَنْ تَكُونَ النِّسْبَةُ مَفْرُوقَةً إِلَى نِسْبَتَيْنِ فَيَكُونُ كُلُّ وَاحِدَةٍ مِنْهُمَا بِمَنْزِلَةِ الْجُزْءِ مِنَ الْكُلِّ.}}{As for decomposition: it is that the ratio be divided into two ratios, so that each one of them is in the position of a part from the whole.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{فَإِذَا وُجِدَ هٰذَا فَلْنَقُلْ: أَيُّ نِسْبَةٍ كَانَتْ لِثَلَاثَةِ مُتَجَانِسَاتٍ إِلَى إِحْدَاهُنَّ فَهِيَ مُرَكَّبَةٌ مِنْ نِسْبَتِهِ إِلَى الثَّالِثَةِ وَنِسْبَةِ تِلْكَ الثَّالِثَةِ إِلَى الْأُولَى.}}{When this is established, let us say: Any ratio whatsoever of three homogeneous quantities to one of them is composed of its ratio to the third and the ratio of that third to the first.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{فَلْيَكُنْ ثَلَاثَةُ مُتَجَانِسَاتٍ أ وَ ب وَ ج، فَأَقُولُ: إِنَّ نِسْبَةَ أ إِلَى ب تُسَاوِي نِسْبَةَ أ إِلَى ج فِي نِسْبَةِ ج إِلَى ب، وَنِسْبَةَ أ إِلَى ج تُسَاوِي نِسْبَةَ أ إِلَى ب فِي نِسْبَةِ ب إِلَى ج، وَنِسْبَةَ ب إِلَى ج تُسَاوِي نِسْبَةَ ب إِلَى أ فِي نِسْبَةِ أ إِلَى ج، وَهَكَذَا.}}{Let there be three homogeneous quantities A, B, and C. Then I say: the ratio of A to B is equal to the ratio of A to C multiplied by the ratio of C to B, and the ratio of A to C is equal to the ratio of A to B multiplied by the ratio of B to C, and the ratio of B to C is equal to the ratio of B to A multiplied by the ratio of A to C, and so on.}

\ornament

\subsection*{\ar{المقالة الثانية: في شكل القطاع والنسب الموجودة فيه}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Second Treatise: The Quadrilateral Figure (Arabic)}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَهٰذَا الشَّكْلُ يُسَمَّى شَكْلَ الْقِطَّاعِ لِأَنَّهُ مُنشَأٌ مِنْ أَرْبَعَةِ خُطُوطٍ مُقَاطِعَةٍ، وَيُسَمَّى أَيْضًا شَكْلَ الْقَطَائِعِ الْمُكَمَّلَةِ. وَقَدْ أَطْلَقَ عَلَيْهِ بَعْضُ الْمُتَأَخِّرِينَ اسْمَ الْمُخَمَّسِ لِأَنَّهُ يَحْتَوِي عَلَى خَمْسَةِ أَضْلَاعٍ فِي الْحَقِيقَةِ إِذَا أُضِيفَ إِلَيْهِ قُطْرٌ وَاحِدٌ.}}{This figure is called the figure of secants (\textit{shakl al-qa\d{t}\d{t}a`}) because it is generated from four intersecting lines, and is also called the figure of complete secants. Some later scholars have called it the ``pentagonal figure'' because it contains five sides in reality when one diagonal is added.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَقَدْ أَخْطَأَ حُسَامُ الدِّينِ فِي قَوْلِهِ إِنَّ هٰذَا الشَّكْلَ إِنَّمَا يَكُونُ فِي تِسْعَةِ أَشْكَالٍ، وَهُوَ فِي اثْنَيْ عَشَرَ شَكْلًا. وَقَدْ بَيَّنَّا ذٰلِكَ فِي هٰذَا الْمَكَانِ وَأَثْبَتْنَاهُ بِالْبُرْهَانِ.}}{Hussam al-Din erred in saying that this figure only occurs in nine configurations; it is in fact twelve figures. We have demonstrated this at this point and proved it rigorously.}

\ornament

\subsection*{\ar{المقالة الخامسة: في الضوابط البديلة لنظرية الشكل}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{Fifth Treatise: Methods Replacing the Theory (Arabic)}

\bilingualpair{\ar{الضَّرْبُ الْأَوَّلُ وَلْيَكُنِ الْمَعْلُومُ وَتَرُ الْقَاعِدَةِ وَوَتَرُ النَّهَايَةِ الْآخَرِ وَضَلْعٌ مُجَاوِرٌ لِإِحْدَاهُمَا فَلْنَفْرِضْ أَنَّهُ أ ج وَالْمَعْلُومُ أَيْضًا ضَلْعٌ آخَرُ مُجَاوِرٌ لِتِلْكَ الْقَاعِدَةِ فَلْيَكُنْ د أ، وَلْيَكُنِ الْمَجْهُولُ بَقِيَّةُ جَوَانِبِ هٰذَا الشَّكْلِ.}}{The first case: Let the known quantities be the base, the other extreme, and a side adjacent to one of them. Let us suppose that it is AC, and let the known quantity also be another side adjacent to that base, let it be DA, and let the unknown be the remaining sides of this figure.}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\bilingualpair{\ar{وَمِنْ هٰذَا يَتَبَيَّنُ أَنَّ جِيبَ قَوْس أ ب إِلَى جِيبِ قَوْس أ ج كَنِسْبَةِ جِيبِ قَوْس أ د إِلَى جِيبِ قَوْس د ب، وَذٰلِكَ مَا أَرَدْنَا إِثْبَاتَهُ وَإِنِ انْطَبَقَ أَحَدُ الْمُثَلَّثَيْنِ عَلَى الْآخَرِ فِي كُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْ هٰذِهِ الْأَشْكَالِ فَكَانَ الْبَيَانُ بِحَسَبِهِ عَلَى قِيَاسِ ذٰلِكَ.}}{From this it is clear that the sine of arc AB is to the sine of arc AC as the sine of arc AD is to the sine of arc DB, which is what we wanted to prove. And if one of the triangles coincides with the other in any one of these configurations, then the explanation follows accordingly.}
