\begin{german}

gelegt werden; f"ur die sp"ateren B"ucher war eine "ahnliche Reinschrift
vorhanden, welche bis zu dem Komma hinter vor Anmerkung~3 zu S.~280
reicht, ausserdem ein meist sehr sauberes, "uberall ohne Zweideutigkeit
lesbares Brouillon; ferner gew"ahrte neben wenigen Bl"attern mit einzelnen
Notizen der urspr"ungliche ziemlich knappe Entwurf der Anmerkungen,
welcher vollst"andig benutzt werden konnte, sp"arliche Ausbeute; nach
Vollendung des ersten Bandes wurden mir auch Fl"ugels Originalabschrift
des Textes sowie die Excerpte desselben aus Ibn al-Kifti und Ibn Abi
Useibi'a durch das dankbar anzuerkennende Vertrauen der K.
Universit"atsbibliothek zu Leipzig zug"anglich.

\smallskip

Dem mir gewordenen Auftrage gem"ass habe ich dies unfertige und auch,
soweit es von Fl"ugel in der Reinschrift hinterlassen worden ist, immerhin
der letzten Ueberarbeitung entbehrende Material, wo es noch n"othig

verst"andlich zu machen, "uberhaupt durch sorgf"altige und einheitliche
Behandlung aller Aeusserlichkeiten die Lesbarkeit des unvollendeten

Nur war es nach den von der Bearbeitung des ersten Bandes befolgten, mit

bezeichnet.

\end{german}

\begin{flushright}
\textit{Halle, 24. Mai 1872.}\\[0.3em]
\textbf{August M"uller.}

\section{The Work and Its Author}

\noindent\textbf{Ibn al-Nad\=im,} whose full name is Ab\=u al-Faraj Mu\d{h}ammad
ibn Is\d{h}\=aq ibn Ab\=i Ya\c{q}\=ub al-Nad\=im al-Warr\=aq al-Baghd\=ad\=i
(died c.~995/998 CE), was a Muslim scholar, bibliographer, and bookseller
who lived in Baghdad during the Buyid period. His
\textit{Kit\=ab al-Fihrist} (\ar{كتاب الفهرست}) is the most
important bibliographic work in Arabic literature and an indispensable
source for the history of Islamic civilization, especially for the period
down to the 10th century.

\subsection{The Author}

Ibn al-Nad\=im was a \textit{warr\=aq} (book copier and seller) by
profession, a trade that gave him unique access to the vast literary
output of his time. His \textit{nisba} ``al-Nad\=im'' (\ar{النَديم})
suggests he may have been a companion or boon-companion, while
``al-Warr\=aq'' (\ar{الوَرّاق}) refers to his profession as a
stationer. He moved in the intellectual circles of Baghdad and had
personal knowledge of many of the scholars and authors whose works he
catalogued.

The exact date of his death is uncertain. Internal evidence from the
\textit{Fihrist} suggests he was still working on it in 987/988 CE
(377 AH), and he is generally believed to have died around 995--998 CE.

\subsection{The \textit{Kit\=ab al-Fihrist}}

The \textit{Fihrist} is organized into ten \textit{discourses}
(\textit{maq\=al\=at}, \ar{مقالات}), covering the entirety of Arabic
book culture:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Languages, scripts, and calligraphy} ---
    \ar{الفن الأول في الكتب واللغات والخطوط}
  \item \textbf{Theology and sacred law} ---
    \ar{الفن الثاني في أصول الدين والفرق}
  \item \textbf{Law and government} ---
    \ar{الفن الثالث في الفقه والمذاهب والقضاة والحكام}
  \item \textbf{Scholarship and philology} ---
    \ar{الفن الرابع في النحو واللغة والتاريخ والعروض والأدب}
  \item \textbf{Poetry and poets} ---
    \ar{الفن الخامس في الشعراء وأخبارهم}
  \item \textbf{Religions, sects, and doctrines} ---
    \ar{الفن السادس في أصحاب الملل والنحل والأديان}
  \item \textbf{Philosophy, mathematics, and natural sciences} ---
    \ar{الفن السابع في الفلسفة والحكمة والعلوم الطبيعية والرياضية}
  \item \textbf{Stories, romances, and magic} ---
    \ar{الفن الثامن في الخرافات والأساطير والسحر}
  \item \textbf{Books of the non-Muslim peoples} ---
    \ar{الفن التاسع في كتب السابئين والمجوس والنصارى والمشركين}
  \item \textbf{History, biography, and genealogy} ---
    \ar{الفن العاشر في التاريخ والسير والأنساب}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Fl\"ugel Edition}

This edition was prepared by \textbf{Gustav Leberecht Fl\"ugel}
(1802--1870), the foremost German Arabist of his generation and
professor at Leipzig. Fl\"ugel worked on the edition for more than 25
years but died before its completion. The work was finished by
\textbf{Dr.~Johannes Roediger} (Volume I: Text, published 1871) and
\textbf{Dr.~August M\"uller} (Volume II: Notes and Indices, published 1872),
with the support of the \textit{Deutsche Morgenl\"andische Gesellschaft}.

The edition is based on multiple manuscripts designated by sigla:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C.} (= Codex) --- The primary manuscript
  \item \textbf{P.} --- Paris manuscript
  \item \textbf{H.} --- Oxford (Bodleian) manuscript
  \item \textbf{V.} --- Vienna manuscript
  \item \textbf{L.} --- Leipzig manuscript
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Chapter VII: Philosophy and the Sciences}

The seventh discourse of the \textit{Fihrist} is devoted to philosophy
(\ar{الفلسفة}), wisdom (\ar{الحكمة}), the natural sciences
(\ar{العلوم الطبيعية}), and the mathematical sciences
(\ar{العلوم الرياضية}). This section preserves invaluable
information about Greek, Persian, and Indian scientific works translated
into Arabic, as well as original contributions by Muslim scholars.

Ibn al-Nad\=im provides detailed accounts of the translators who rendered
Greek scientific works into Arabic during the 8th--10th centuries:

\section{Historical Significance of the \textit{Fihrist}}

The \textit{Kit\=ab al-Fihrist} is not merely a bibliography; it is a
window onto the intellectual world of medieval Islam. For historians of
mathematics and science, it provides:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The most complete record of the Greek-to-Arabic translation
    movement} (8th--10th centuries), listing hundreds of works translated
    from Greek, Syriac, Persian, Sanskrit, and other languages.

  \item \textbf{Detailed biographical information} about translators,
    scholars, and scientists, many of whom are not known from any other
    source.

  \item \textbf{Systematic catalogues of scientific works} organized by
    discipline, showing the scope and structure of each field.

  \item \textbf{Preservation of titles and incipits} of works now lost,
    including many Greek works that do not survive in the original.

  \item \textbf{Evidence for the circulation of scientific texts} in
    10th-century Baghdad, including information about which works were
    considered authoritative.
\end{enumerate}
