This chapter covers the fundamental operations of arithmetic: addition (saṅkalita), subtraction (vyavakalita), mul-
tiplication (guṇana), division (bhājana), squaring (varga), square roots (vargamūla), cubing (ghana), and cube
roots (ghanamūla).

3 िभन्नपिरकमर् — Operations with Fractions
3.1 जाितचतुष्टय — Four Classes of Fractions
Bhāskara defines four types of fractions (jāticatuṣṭaya):

   1. िभन्न (bhinna) — Simple fraction, e.g., 21

   2. प्रभाग (prabhāga) — Part of a part, e.g., 12 of 31

   3. भागानुबन्ध (bhāgānubandha) — Fraction combined with an integer, e.g., 2 + 13

   4. भागापवाह (bhāgāpavāha) — Fraction less than an integer, e.g., 2 − 13

3.2 िभन्नगुणन िभन्नभाग — Multiplication and Division of Fractions

4 त्रै रािशक — The Rule of Three
The trairāśika (Rule of Three) is one of the most important topics in Indian arithmetic. It solves problems of the
form:
                                                                             B×C
                             “If A gives B, what does C give?” Answer:
                                                                               A

4.1 इच्छाकमर्प्रकार — Direct Rule of Three

सूत्रम् : प्रमाणफलिमच्छाफलं चे त् कुयार्त्।                        Rule: Multiply the phala (fruit/result) by the icchā (de-

The Swallow Problem (हं ससमस्या)
Problem: Swallows are sitting on the branches of a tree. If each branch has 3 swallows, one swallow is left
over. If each branch has 4 swallows, one branch is left empty. How many swallows and branches are there?
Solution: Let s = number of swallows, b = number of branches.

                                                            s = 3b + 1
                                                            s = 4(b − 1) = 4b − 4

Equating: 3b + 1 = 4b − 4, so b = 5 and s = 16.
Answer: 16 swallows and 5 branches. [This is a classic example of a system of linear equations with a unique
solution. It is equivalent to finding x such that x ≡ 1 (mod 3) and x ≡ 0 (mod 4).]
