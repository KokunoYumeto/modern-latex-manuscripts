1     The Work and Its Author
Ibn al-Nadīm, whose full name is Abū al-Faraj Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq ibn Abī Yaq̧ūb al-Nadīm al-Warrāq
al-Baghdādī (died c. 995/998 CE), was a Muslim scholar, bibliographer, and bookseller who lived in
Baghdad during the Buyid period. His Kitāb al-Fihrist (‫ )ﻛﺘﺎﺏ ﺍﻟﻔﻬﺮﺳﺖ‬is the most important biblio-
graphic work in Arabic literature and an indispensable source for the history of Islamic civilization,
especially for the period down to the 10th century.

1.1    The Author
Ibn al-Nadīm was a warrāq (book copier and seller) by profession, a trade that gave him unique access
to the vast literary output of his time. His nisba “al-Nadīm” (‫ )ﺍﻟ َﻨﺪﻳﻢ‬suggests he may have been a
companion or boon-companion, while “al-Warrāq” (‫ﺍﻟﻮ ّﺭﺍﻕ‬ َ ) refers to his profession as a stationer. He
moved in the intellectual circles of Baghdad and had personal knowledge of many of the scholars and
authors whose works he catalogued.
    The exact date of his death is uncertain. Internal evidence from the Fihrist suggests he was still
working on it in 987/988 CE (377 AH), and he is generally believed to have died around 995–998 CE.

1.2    The Kitāb al-Fihrist
The Fihrist is organized into ten discourses (maqālāt, ‫)ﻣﻘﺎﻟﺎﺕ‬, covering the entirety of Arabic book
culture:

     1. Languages, scripts, and calligraphy — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺄﻭﻝ ﻓﻲ ﺍﻟﻜﺘﺐ ﻭﺍﻟﻠﻐﺎﺕ ﻭﺍﻟﺨﻄﻮﻁ‬

     2. Theology and sacred law — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺜﺎﻧﻲ ﻓﻲ ﺃﺻﻮﻝ ﺍﻟﺪﻳﻦ ﻭﺍﻟﻔﺮﻕ‬

     3. Law and government — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺜﺎﻟﺚ ﻓﻲ ﺍﻟﻔﻘﻪ ﻭﺍﻟﻤﺬﺍﻫﺐ ﻭﺍﻟﻘﻀﺎﺓ ﻭﺍﻟﺤﻜﺎﻡ‬

     4. Scholarship and philology — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺮﺍﺑﻊ ﻓﻲ ﺍﻟﻨﺤﻮ ﻭﺍﻟﻠﻐﺔ ﻭﺍﻟﺘﺎﺭﻳﺦ ﻭﺍﻟﻌﺮﻭﺽ ﻭﺍﻟﺄﺩﺏ‬

     5. Poetry and poets — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺨﺎﻣﺲ ﻓﻲ ﺍﻟﺸﻌﺮﺍﺀ ﻭﺃﺧﺒﺎﺭﻫﻢ‬

     6. Religions, sects, and doctrines — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺴﺎﺩﺱ ﻓﻲ ﺃﺻﺤﺎﺏ ﺍﻟﻤﻠﻞ ﻭﺍﻟﻨﺤﻞ ﻭﺍﻟﺄﺩﻳﺎﻥ‬

     7. Philosophy, mathematics, and natural sciences — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺴﺎﺑﻊ ﻓﻲ ﺍﻟﻔﻠﺴﻔﺔ ﻭﺍﻟﺤﻜﻤﺔ ﻭﺍﻟﻌﻠﻮﻡ ﺍﻟﻄﺒﻴﻌﻴﺔ‬
       ‫ﻭﺍﻟﺮﻳﺎﺿﻴﺔ‬
     8. Stories, romances, and magic — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺜﺎﻣﻦ ﻓﻲ ﺍﻟﺨﺮﺍﻓﺎﺕ ﻭﺍﻟﺄﺳﺎﻃﻴﺮ ﻭﺍﻟﺴﺤﺮ‬

     9. Books of the non-Muslim peoples — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﺘﺎﺳﻊ ﻓﻲ ﻛﺘﺐ ﺍﻟﺴﺎﺑﺌﻴﻦ ﻭﺍﻟﻤﺠﻮﺱ ﻭﺍﻟﻨﺼﺎﺭﻯ ﻭﺍﻟﻤﺸﺮﻛﻴﻦ‬

    10. History, biography, and genealogy — ‫ﺍﻟﻔﻦ ﺍﻟﻌﺎﺷﺮ ﻓﻲ ﺍﻟﺘﺎﺭﻳﺦ ﻭﺍﻟﺴﻴﺮ ﻭﺍﻟﺄﻧﺴﺎﺏ‬

1.3    The Flügel Edition
This edition was prepared by Gustav Leberecht Flügel (1802–1870), the foremost German Arabist of
his generation and professor at Leipzig. Flügel worked on the edition for more than 25 years but died
before its completion. The work was finished by Dr. Johannes Roediger (Volume I: Text, published
1871) and Dr. August Müller (Volume II: Notes and Indices, published 1872), with the support of the
Deutsche Morgenländische Gesellschaft.
    The edition is based on multiple manuscripts designated by sigla:

6     Historical Significance of the Fihrist
The Kitāb al-Fihrist is not merely a bibliography; it is a window onto the intellectual world of medieval
Islam. For historians of mathematics and science, it provides:

    1. The most complete record of the Greek-to-Arabic translation movement (8th–10th centuries),
       listing hundreds of works translated from Greek, Syriac, Persian, Sanskrit, and other languages.

    2. Detailed biographical information about translators, scholars, and scientists, many of whom are
       not known from any other source.

    3. Systematic catalogues of scientific works organized by discipline, showing the scope and struc-
       ture of each field.

    4. Preservation of titles and incipits of works now lost, including many Greek works that do not
       survive in the original.

    5. Evidence for the circulation of scientific texts in 10th-century Baghdad, including information
       about which works were considered authoritative.
