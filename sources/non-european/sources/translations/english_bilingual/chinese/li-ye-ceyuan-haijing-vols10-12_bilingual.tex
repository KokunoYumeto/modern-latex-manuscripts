\section*{Introduction / 引言}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction / 引言}

\bilingualprob{%
\textbf{測圓海鏡}（\textit{Ceyuan Haijing}，「Sea Mirror of Circle Measurements」）是中國中世紀數學的傑作之一。李冶（1192--1279）著，初版於1248年，全書十二卷共170題，皆圍繞一個幾何構型：直角三角形內切圓。

本文檔包含此書的最後三卷（第10--12卷）。這些卷繼續用\textbf{天元術}（tian yuan shu，「celestial element method」）系統研究各線段與內切圓直徑的關係——這種代數方法等價於求解多項式方程。

每題依古典結構：
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{問}（wen）—— 題目
  \item \textbf{答曰}（da yue）—— 數值答案
  \item \textbf{法曰}（fa yue）—— 方法（一般程序）
  \item \textbf{草曰}（cao yue）—— 天元術詳細演算
\end{itemize}%
}{%
The \textit{Ceyuan Haijing} (\textbf{測圓海鏡}, ``Sea Mirror of Circle Measurements'') is one of the masterpieces of medieval Chinese mathematics. Written by \textbf{Li Ye} (李冶, 1192--1279) and first published in 1248 CE, it consists of twelve volumes containing 170 problems, all devoted to a single geometric configuration: a \textit{circle inscribed in a right triangle}.

The present document contains the \textbf{final three volumes} (10--12) of this work. These volumes continue the systematic investigation of relationships between various line segments formed by the configuration and the diameter of the inscribed circle, using the \textit{tian yuan shu} (\textbf{天元術}, ``celestial element method'') --- an algebraic method equivalent to solving \textbf{polynomial equations}.

Each problem follows the classical structure:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=0pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Problem} (問 wen) --- The problem statement
  \item \textbf{Answer} (答曰 da yue) --- The numerical answer
  \item \textbf{Method} (法曰 fa yue) --- The general procedure
  \item \textbf{Working} (草曰 cao yue) --- Detailed celestial element method calculations
\end{itemize}%
}

The problems in this volume increasingly employ the celestial element method to derive \textbf{polynomial equations of higher degree} (degrees 5--6).

%% Problem 131
%% ========================================================================
\vspace{0.6cm}
\probnum{第百三十一問 \quad Problem 131}

\bilingualprob{%
\wenCN{假令有圓城一座，甲乙二人俱立於圓心。甲向東門直行，乙向南門直行。甲出東門，望見乙，乃斜行與乙相會。二人共行了三百八十步。又云乙出南門直行後，其不及斜行一百二十步。問圓徑幾何？}

\daCN{圓徑二百四十步。}

\fayueCN{以共步內減四之小差，復以自之於上。以十八個小差冪減於上為實。四之共步內減十六個小差於上，却以十八小差加上為益從。四步常法。開平方得中差。}

\caoyueCN{別得共步為三事和也。不及步即小差也。立天元一為中差，加二之小差得三事和也。倍三事得三千二百內入三事和得三千四百。又以前數加之得三千六百為三和也。內減三弦餘三較為三黃方。又以前數加之得三千八百為通弦也。倍之為通弦，三事減之亦得。}
}{%
\wenEN{Suppose there is a circular city. Two persons A and B stand together at the center. A walks straight toward the East Gate; B walks straight toward the South Gate. After exiting the East Gate, A sees B and walks diagonally to meet B. Together they walked 380 steps. It is also said that after exiting the South Gate, B's straight walk falls short of the diagonal walk by 120 steps. What is the diameter of the circle?}

\daEN{The diameter of the circle is 240 steps.}

\fayueEN{Subtract four times the small difference from the total walk, then square the result and place on top. Subtract eighteen times the square of the small difference from this to obtain the shi (constant term). Within four times the total walk, subtract sixteen times the small difference and place on top; then add eighteen times the small difference to obtain the negative cong (linear coefficient). Four is the chang fa (constant divisor). Extract the square root to obtain the middle difference.}

\caoyueEN{Let the celestial element unity (tian yuan yi) be the middle difference. Adding twice the small difference gives the three-item sum (san shi he). Doubling the three-item gives 3200; adding the three-item sum gives 3400. Adding the previous number gives 3600 as the triple sum. Subtracting three chords leaves three differences as three yellow squares. Adding the previous number gives 3800 as the贯通 chord. Doubling this gives the贯通 chord; subtracting the three items also gives the same.}
}

\vspace{0.6cm}
\probnum{第百三十二問 \quad Problem 132}

\bilingualprob{%
\wenCN{假令圓城一座，甲乙二人同立於圓心。甲向東門直行，乙向南門直行。甲出東門，回望見乙在城南，乃斜行與乙相會。只云甲行共二百步，乙行不及斜行五十步。問徑幾何？}

\daCN{圓徑一百二十步。}

\fayueCN{以共步自之為冪，又以減倍不及步數加上位為實。倍共步內減不及步為從。二步常法。開平方得半徑。}

\caoyueCN{立天元一為半徑，即為股弦差也。以二之減倍共步得二百步為大差，即弦較和也。以減倍共步得二百步為大差也。又三事和得三百步為股弦和也。以大差乘之得六萬步為冪。又以天元減共步得一百步為小差，以乘大差得二萬步。減上位餘四萬步為實。以大差得二百步為從。二步常法。開平方得一百二十步，即圓徑也。合問。}
}{%
\wenEN{Suppose a circular city. A and B stand together at the center. A walks straight toward the East Gate; B walks straight toward the South Gate. After exiting the East Gate, A looks back and sees B south of the city, then walks diagonally to meet B. Given that A walked 200 steps in total, and B's walk falls short of the diagonal by 50 steps. What is the diameter?}

\daEN{The diameter of the circle is 120 steps.}

\fayueEN{Square the total walk to obtain the power; subtract twice the shortfall and add to the upper position to obtain the shi. Subtract the shortfall from twice the total walk to obtain the cong. Two is the chang fa. Extract the square root to obtain the radius.}

\caoyueEN{Let the celestial element unity be the radius, which is the difference between the leg and the hypotenuse. Subtracting twice the total walk from twice this gives 200 steps as the large difference, i.e., the sum of the hypotenuse and the difference. The three-item sum gives 300 steps as the sum of the leg and hypotenuse. Multiplying by the large difference gives 60,000 steps as the power. Subtracting the celestial element from the total walk gives 100 steps as the small difference; multiplying by the large difference gives 20,000 steps. Subtracting from the upper position leaves 40,000 steps as the shi. The large difference of 200 steps is the cong. Two is the chang fa. Extracting the square root gives 120 steps, which is the diameter of the circle. This answers the question.}
}

\probnum{第百三十三問 \quad Problem 133}

\bilingualprob{%
\wenCN{假令圓城一座，甲直東行出東門，乙斜行出南門，在甲東南相會。只云甲乙共行四百二十步，又云乙斜行多於甲直行八十步。問徑幾何？}

\daCN{圓徑二百四十步。}

\fayueCN{以共步內減多步，餘為二勾。以多步加之為二股。以二勾自之，又以二股乘之，又以共步乘之為實。以二勾乘二股，又以共步乘之為從。以二勾冪加上位為益廉。二之共步為從隅。開三乘方得圓徑。}

\caoyueCN{立天元一為圓徑，以半之加多步得二百步為股。以半共步減之得十步為勾。以勾自之得一百步，又以股乘之得二萬步為直積。又以共步乘之得八百四十萬步為實。以勾乘股得二萬步，又以共步乘之得八百四十萬步為從。以勾冪一百步加上位為益廉。以共步四百二十步為從隅。開三乘方得二百四十步，即圓徑。合問。}
}{%
\wenEN{Suppose a circular city. A walks straight east out the East Gate; B walks diagonally out the South Gate, meeting A to the southeast. Given that A and B together walked 420 steps, and B's diagonal walk exceeds A's straight walk by 80 steps. What is the diameter?}

\daEN{The diameter of the circle is 240 steps.}

\fayueEN{Subtract the excess from the total walk; the remainder is twice the base. Add the excess to obtain twice the leg. Square twice the base, multiply by twice the leg, then multiply by the total walk to obtain the shi. Multiply twice the base by twice the leg, then multiply by the total walk to obtain the cong. Add the square of twice the base to the upper position as the negative lian (quadratic coefficient). Twice the total walk is the cong yu (cubic coefficient). Extract the cube root to obtain the diameter.}

\caoyueEN{Let the celestial element unity be the diameter. Halving it and adding the excess gives 200 steps as the leg. Subtracting from half the total walk gives 10 steps as the base. Squaring the base gives 100 steps; multiplying by the leg gives 20,000 steps as the direct product. Multiplying by the total walk gives 8,400,000 steps as the shi. Multiplying base by leg gives 20,000 steps; multiplying by the total walk gives 8,400,000 steps as the cong. Adding the square of the base, 100 steps, to the upper position gives the negative lian. The total walk of 420 steps is the cong yu. Extracting the cube root gives 240 steps, which is the diameter. This answers the question.}
}

\section{後序 Afterword / 跋}
\label{sec:afterword}

\longline

\bilingualprob{%
\textbf{原文：}

敬齋先生病且革，語其子克修曰：「吾平生著述，死後可盡燔去。獨測圓海鏡一書，雖九九小數，吾常精思致力焉，後世必有知者。庶可布廣垂永乎？」

先生於六藝百家，靡不貫串。文集近數百卷，常謙謙不自伐。惟於此書不忘稱異。予易簀之間，想有玄妙內得於心者。予以先生與先人同榜之故，素常兄事克修。克修兄命予重為序。予不敢說論艷藻，刻畫無鹽，唐突西子。直以所聞語之子弟，使知測圓海鏡之淵源云。
}{%
\textbf{Translation:}

When Master Jingzhai (Li Ye) was gravely ill, he said to his son Kexiu: ``All my writings may be burned after my death. \textbf{Only the \textit{Sea Mirror of Circle Measurements}}, though it concerns but the minor art of arithmetic, has been the object of my deep contemplation and dedicated effort. Posterity will surely have those who understand it. May it thus be spread abroad and transmitted forever?''

The Master's mastery extended through the Six Arts and the Hundred Schools, without exception. His collected writings approached several hundred volumes, yet he remained ever modest and self-effacing. \textbf{Only with this book} did he not forget to express his wonder. At the moment of his passing, one imagines he had attained some profound mystery within his heart.

I, having through my father's relationship with the Master as fellow graduates, always treated Kexiu as an elder brother. Elder Brother Kexiu has commanded me to write a preface anew. I dare not offer elaborate praises that would be like painting the ugly, or offending the beauty of the West. I simply recount what I have heard for the benefit of the younger generation, that they may know the \textbf{origins of the \textit{Sea Mirror of Circle Measurements}}.
}

\textbf{後世評價：}

測圓海鏡代表了中國數學史上的一個高峰。全書170題，以天元術為統一方法，系統地研究了直角三角形內切圓的各種性質。李冶在書中展現了多項式方程的建立與求解技術，其方法之精妙、結構之嚴謹，在當時世界上無與倫比。

天元術（設未知數為「天元一」，建立多項式方程）本質上等價於現代代數學中的方程式列法。李冶的工作比歐洲的類似發展（如韋達、笛卡爾的符號代數）早了約四百年。

\textbf{Historical Significance:}

The \textit{Ceyuan Haijing} represents a \textbf{pinnacle in the history of Chinese mathematics}. The complete work of 170 problems, unified by the celestial element method, systematically investigates the properties of circles inscribed in right triangles. Li Ye demonstrated techniques for establishing and solving polynomial equations with a sophistication and structural rigor \textbf{unmatched anywhere in the world at that time}.

The \textit{tian yuan shu} (setting the unknown as ``celestial element unity'' and establishing polynomial equations) is essentially equivalent to the formulation of equations in modern algebra. Li Ye's work preceded comparable European developments (such as the symbolic algebra of Viete and Descartes) by approximately \textbf{four centuries}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{1248 CE:} First publication of the \textit{Sea Mirror}
  \item \textbf{170 problems:} Complete system covering inscribed circle problems
  \item \textbf{Degree-6 equations:} Highest-level algebraic technique in the world at the time
  \item \textbf{Celestial Element Method:} Precursor to symbolic algebra
\end{itemize}
}
