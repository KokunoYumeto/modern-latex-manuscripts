\section*{Contents / \dev{विषयानि}}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.,leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Da\'sagitika} (\dev{दशगीतिका}) --- 33 verses\\ Numerical notation, planetary periods, sine table, astronomical constants
  \item \textbf{Ganitapada} (\dev{गणितपादः}) --- 33 verses\\ Mathematics: squares, cubes, areas, volumes, shadows, ku\d{t}taka
  \item \textbf{Kalakriyapada} (\dev{कालक्रियापादः}) --- 25 verses\\ Time reckoning: yugas, months, days, planetary motion
  \item \textbf{Golapada} (\dev{गोलपादः}) --- 50 verses\\ Spherical astronomy: Earth as sphere, eclipses, stellar positions
\end{enumerate}

Ku\d{t}taka (pulverizer) method & Verses 2.32--33\\
Earth's rotation on its axis & Golapada 9\\
\bottomrule

% PREFACE
\section*{Preface / \dev{प्रस्तावना}}

\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
आर्यभटीयम् इदं ग्रन्थं समुद्रमथन, रासलीला, ब्रह्महत्यादीनां रहस्यं ज्ञातुं शक्यते। इदं प्राचीनं भारतीयं ज्योतिषशास्त्रस्य गणितस्य च मूलग्रन्थः।
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
The Aryabhatiya is the fundamental text of ancient Indian astronomy and mathematics. Composed by Aryabhata in 499 CE when he was but 23 years old, this treatise consists of 121 verses divided into four chapters (p\=adas).
\end{englishcol}
\par\medskip

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
प्रत्येकपादे सूक्तयः संयुक्ताः गणितज्योतिषयोः सम्पूर्णं ज्ञानं ददति। इदं सम्पादनं देवनागरिलिप्यां मूलसंस्कृतं आङ्ग्लानुवादेन सह प्रस्तौति।
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
Each p\=ada contains verses giving complete knowledge of mathematics and astronomy. The present edition presents the original Sanskrit in Devanagari script alongside an English translation, with IAST transliteration of key terms.
\end{englishcol}
\par\medskip

\section*{First Chapter: Dasagitika / \dev{प्रथमः पादः -- दशगीतिका}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{I. Dasagitika (33 verses)}

\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
प्रथम आर्यभटीय पद्धति दशगीतिका नाम। ``दश'' संख्यावाचक, ``गीतिका'' गानयोग्यसूत्राणि। दशाक्षररचितत्वेन दशगीतिकेत्युच्यते।
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
The first method of the Aryabhatiya is called Da\'sag\=itik\=a. ``Da\'sa'' is a numerical term, and ``g\=itik\=a'' means verses fit for singing. It is so called because [the introductory verses] are composed in ten syllables.
\end{englishcol}
\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 1 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 1 --- Invocation / \dev{मङ्गलाचरण}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
ब्रह्मा नारायणो ब्रह्मा ब्रह्मा वेधाः सनातनः ।\\
परमेष्ठी च सप्तैते मुनयः स्युः पुरःसराः ॥ १ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Brahm\=a n\=ar\=aya\d no brahm\=a brahm\=a vedh\=a\d h san\=atana\d h.\\
Parame\d{s}\d{t}h\=i ca saptaite munaya\d h syu\d h pura\d hsar\=a\d h. [1]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
Brahma, Narayana, Brahma, Brahma, Vedhas the Eternal, and Parameshthin---these seven sages are the foremost leaders. [1]
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 2 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 2 --- Numerical Notation / \dev{संख्यास्थानकल्पना}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
वर्गाक्षराणि कादीनि नव ताण्डाक्षराणि च ।\\
नव धनानि चैतानि स्थानान्यष्टादश स्मृतम् ॥ २ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Varg\=ak\d{s}ar\=a\d{n}i k\=ad\=ini nava t\=a\d{n}\d{d}\=ak\d{s}ar\=a\d{n}i ca.\\
Nava dh\=an\=ani cait\=ani sth\=an\=nya\d{s}\d{t}\=ada\'sa sm\=rtam. [2]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The varga letters beginning with ka are nine in number; the avarga letters are also nine. Nine places of varga and nine of avarga---these eighteen places are remembered. [2]

\textit{[Translators note: Aryabhata's alphabetic numerical notation assigns values to consonants: ka-ta-pa-ya = 1-25, and to vowels representing powers of 10.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 3 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 3 --- Revolutions of the Sun / \dev{सूर्यस्य चतुर्युगे भगणाः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
सूर्यस्य भगणा यत्र चतुर्युगे यान्ति ते कति ।\\
चतुर्विंशतिसहस्राणि षट्शतानि च सप्ततिः ॥ ३ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{S\=uryasya bhag\=an\=a yatra catur-yuge y\=anti te kati.\\
Catur-vi\'msati-sahasr\=a\d{n}i \d{s}a\d{t}-\'sat\=ani ca saptati\d h. [3]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The revolutions of the Sun in a Caturyuga are: twenty-four thousand and six hundred and seventy, [that is,] \textbf{4,320,000}. [3]
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 4 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 4 --- Moon's Revolutions / \dev{शशिनो भगणाः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
शशिनो भगणा यत्र पञ्च सप्ततिरुत्तरम् ।\\
त्रिसहस्राणि चाष्टौ च लक्षं च द्वे च दक्षिणे ॥ ४ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{\'Sa\'sino bhag\=an\=a yatra pañca saptatir uttaram.\\
Tri-sahasr\=a\d{n}i c\=a\d{s}\d{t}au ca lak\=sa\d m ca dve ca dak\d si\d ne. [4]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The revolutions of the Moon: five and seventy above, three thousand and eight, and one hundred-thousand and two on the right [side], [that is,] \textbf{57,753,336}. [4]
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 5 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 5 --- Lunar Apogee and Nodes / \dev{चन्द्रोच्चराहुभगणौ}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
चन्द्रोच्चं शनिमन्दं च राहुकेतू तथैव च ।\\
भगणानां शतं द्वे तु सहस्राणि च सप्ततिः ॥ ५ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Candrocca\d m \'sani-manda\d m ca r\=ahu-ket\=u tathaiva ca.\\
Bhag\=an\=\=ana\d m \'sata\d m dve tu sahasr\=a\d{n}i ca saptati\d h. [5]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The [apogee] of the Moon, the [apogee] of Saturn, and Rahu and Ketu: two hundred and seventy-thousand [revolutions in a Caturyuga]. [5]

\textit{[Translators note: Rahu and Ketu are the ascending and descending nodes of the Moon, responsible for eclipses.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\clearpage

% ---- Verse 6 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 6 --- The Manus / \dev{मनुसङ्ख्या}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
चतुर्दश व्यतीताः स्युर्मनवोऽष्टौ तथापराः ।\\
प्रवर्तमानश्चैकस्तु कल्पे सप्तदश स्मृताः ॥ ६ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Caturda\'sa vyat\=it\=\=ah syur manavo '\d{s}\d{t}au tath\=apar\=a\d h.\\
Pravartam\=ana\'s caikas tu kalpe saptada\'sa sm\=rt\=a\d h. [6]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
Fourteen Manus have passed away; eight are yet to come; and one is current---seventeen Manus are remembered in a Kalpa. [6]

\textit{[Translators note: A Kalpa is one day of Brahma, equal to 1000 Caturyugas. Each Manu rules over 71 Caturyugas.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 7 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 7 --- Signs and Degrees / \dev{राश्यंशकल्पना}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
द्वादशा सूर्यजः सोऽयं त्रिंशता भागितो रविः ।\\
भागेन तेन राशिः स्यात्तनुं त्रिंशत् गुणीकृतम् ॥ ७ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Dv\=ada\'s\=a s\=uryaja\d h so'ya\d m tri\'m\'sat\=a bh\=agito ravi\d h.\\
Bh\=agena tena r\=a\'si\d h sy\=at tanu\d m tri\'m\'sat gu\d{n}\=ik\=rtam. [7]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The Sun, born of the twelve [signs], is divided by thirty. By that division a sign is produced; a degree is [a sign] multiplied by thirty. [7]

\textit{[Translators note: 12 signs (r\=asi) $\times$ 30 degrees = 360$^\circ$ in a circle.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 8 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 8 --- Measure of the Earth / \dev{योजनप्रमाणम्}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
योजनं त्रिभवं वृत्तं भूमेर्योजनलक्षणम् ।\\
निराकारः सदा व्यापी शून्यमध्यः सनातनः ॥ ८ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Yojana\d m tribhava\d m v\=rtta\d m bh\=umer yojana-lak\sa\nam.\\
Nir\=ak\=ara\d h sad\=a vy\=ap\=i \'s\=unyamadhya\d h san\=atana\d h. [8]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
A yojana: the circle of the Earth has a circumference measured in yojanas. [The Earth is] formless, always pervading, void at the centre, eternal. [8]

\textit{[Translators note: The circumference of the Earth is given as 4,967 yojanas. 1 yojana $\approx$ 7.5 miles.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 9 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 9 --- Planetary Orbits / \dev{ग्रहकक्ष्याः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
ग्रहाणां कक्ष्याविस्तारा योजनैस्तैः प्रकीर्तिताः ।\\
भूमेरर्धं त्रिभागश्च व्यासः सूर्यस्य कीर्तितः ॥ ९ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Grah\=a\d{n}\=a\d m kak\d sy\=a-vist\=ar\=a yojanais tai\d h prak\=irtit\=a\d h.\\
Bh\=umer ardha\d m tribh\=aga\'s ca vy\=asa\d h s\=uryasya k\=irtita\d h. [9]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The diameters of the orbits of the planets are proclaimed in yojanas. Half of the Earth plus a third: the diameter of the Sun is proclaimed. [9]

\textit{[Translators note: The Sun's diameter = 4410 yojanas; the Moon's = 315 yojanas; Earth's = 1050 yojanas.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% ---- Verse 10 ----
\noindent\textbf{Verse 10 --- The 24 Sines / \dev{चतुर्विंशत्यर्धज्याः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
त्रिभुजं षट्कोणं वृत्तं षट्त्रिंशदंशेन योजयेत् ।\\
चतुर्विंशतिजीवाः स्युः सप्तभिः सहिताः क्रमात् ॥ १० ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Tribhuja\d m \sa\tko\na\d m v\=rtta\d m \sa\t-tri\'m\'sad-a\'m\'sena yojayet.\\
Catur-vi\'msati-j\=iv\=a\d h syu\d h saptabhi\d h sahit\=a\d h kram\=at. [10]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
A triangle, a hexagon, and a circle are joined by thirty-six degrees. Twenty-four chords are produced, each increased successively by seven [and other differences]. [10]

\textit{[Translators note: Each sine-interval is $3^\circ 45'$ (225$'$). The 24 \textit{ardhajy\=a}s are computed at intervals of $3^\circ 45'$ from $3^\circ 45'$ to $90^\circ$.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

% Kuttaka detailed
\noindent\textbf{The Ku\d{t}taka Method --- Detailed Procedure}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{white}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\small
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
कूट्टकविधिः---भाजकभाज्ययोः परस्परभागदानं कृत्वा यावदवशेषं एकं न भवति। ततः पश्चादनुक्रमेण गुणकारभाजकौ लभ्येते।
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\textbf{The Ku\d{t}taka Method:}

Divide the divisor (bh\=ajaka) and dividend (bh\=ajya) mutually until the remainder becomes 1. Then by back-substitution, the multiplier (gu\d naka) and additive are obtained.
\end{englishcol}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textbf{Example:} Find $x$ such that $17x + 5 \equiv 0 \pmod{29}$

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
यदि १७x + ५ = ० (मो २९) तर्हि x = ?\\[0.3em]
हलः---\\
२९ = १ × १७ + १२\\
१७ = १ × १२ + ५\\
१२ = २ × ५ + २\\
५ = २ × २ + १\\[0.3em]
अतः गुणकारः x = १२, भाजकः y = ७
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
If $17x + 5 \equiv 0 \pmod{29}$, find $x$.\\[0.3em]
\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{align*}
29 &= 1 \times 17 + 12 \\
17 &= 1 \times 12 + 5 \\
12 &= 2 \times 5 + 2 \\
5 &= 2 \times 2 + 1
\end{align*}

Thus the multiplier $x = 12$, and the additive $y = 7$.

\textit{[Verification: $17 \times 12 - 29 \times 7 = 204 - 203 = 1$]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{\'Sa\d nkor nija-ch\=ayay\=a yukto d\d rgbh\=ga\d h s\=urya-ma\d{n}\d dalam.\\
\'Sa\d nku-ch\=ay\=agra-sa\d yukta\d m d\'s\'syate yadi tat phalam. [15]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The gnomon (\'sa\d nku), combined with its own shadow, [gives] the line of sight to the solar orb. Combined with the tip of the gnomon's shadow, it is seen---that is the result. [15]

\textit{[Translators note: The gnomon (\'sa\d nku) is a vertical stick of 12 a\d ngulas. From the ratio of gnomon to shadow, the altitude of the Sun and hence the time of day may be determined.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.5cm}

\begin{center}
\textcolor{rulecolor}{\rule{0.6\textwidth}{0.4pt}}\\[0.5em]
\textbf{\dev{॥ इति द्वितीयपादः समाप्तः ॥}}\\[0.3em]
\textit{End of the Second Chapter}\\[0.3em]
\textcolor{rulecolor}{\rule{0.6\textwidth}{0.4pt}}
\end{center}

% CHAPTER 3: KALAKRIYAPADA
% ====================================================================
\section*{Third Chapter: Kalakriyapada / \dev{तृतीयः पादः -- कालक्रियापादः}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{III. Kalakriyapada (25 verses)}

\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
कालक्रियापादे युगमासदिवसघटीपलादीनां परिकल्पना वर्तते।
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
In the Kalakriyapada is the reckoning of time: the definitions of yugas, months, days, gha\d t\=is, palas, and so forth.
\end{englishcol}
\par\medskip

\vspace{0.5cm}

% Verse 1
\noindent\textbf{Verse 1 --- Division of Time / \dev{कालविभागः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
नाडी तु दशपञ्चाशत्पलं षष्ट्या तु तैः पुनः ।\\
विपलैस्तैस्तथा कालो यावत् कालेन पश्यति ॥ १ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{N\=a\d{d}\=i tu da\'sa-pañc\=a\'sat-pala\d m \sa\d sthy\=a tu tai\d h puna\d h.\\
Vipalais tais tath\=a k\=alo y\=avat k\=alena pa\'syati. [1]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
A n\=a\d{d}\=i is fifty-five palas; again by sixty of those; and by vipalas of those---thus time proceeds until it is seen by [the smallest unit of] time. [1]

\textit{[Translators note: 1 n\=a\d{d}\=i = 60 palas; 1 pala = 60 vipalas. A n\=a\d{d}\=i equals 24 minutes of modern time.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 2
\noindent\textbf{Verse 2 --- Solar Division / \dev{सौरविभागः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
पूर्वं कालविभागः सैत्रविभागस्तथा भगणात् ।\\
द्वादशा सूर्यजः सोऽयं त्रिंशता भागितो रविः ॥ २ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{P\=urva\d m k\=ala-vibh\=aga\d h saitra-vibh\=agas tath\=a bhag\=a\d n\=at.\\
Dv\=ada\'s\=a s\=uryaja\d h so'ya\d m tri\'m\'sat\=a bh\=agito ravi\d h. [2]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
First, the division of time: the division of the [zodiacal] circle into signs, and the revolutions [of planets]. The Sun, born of the twelve [signs], is divided by thirty. [2]
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 3
\noindent\textbf{Verse 3 --- The Four Yugas / \dev{चतुर्युगम्}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
युगपादश्चतुर्धा स्यात् कृतं त्रेताद्वापरम् ।\\
कलिश्चेति चतुर्विंशतिर्घातिर्युगपदे स्मृता ॥ ३ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Yuga-p\=ada\'s caturdh\=a sy\=at k\=rta\d m tret\=a dv\=aparam.\\
Kali\'s ceti catur-vi\'msati\d r gh\=atir yuga-pade sm\=rt\=a. [3]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The four parts of a yuga are: K\=rta [Satya], Tret\=a, Dv\=apara, and Kali. Twenty-four is remembered as the measure [in hundreds of thousands] of a yuga-part. [3]

\textit{[Translators note: A Caturyuga = 4,320,000 years. K\=rta = 1,728,000; Tret\=a = 1,296,000; Dv\=apara = 864,000; Kali = 432,000. The ratio is 4:3:2:1.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 4
\noindent\textbf{Verse 4 --- The Kalpa / \dev{कल्पः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
मानयुगाद्ययुगपादस्य पञ्चदशयुतसहस्रकल्पः ।\\
युगपादैः सहस्रैस्तु कल्पः परिकीर्तितः ॥ ४ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{M\=ana-yug\=adya-yuga-p\=adasya pañcada\'sa-yuta-sahasra-kalpa\d h.\\
Yuga-p\=adai\d h sahasrais tu kalpa\d h parik\=irtita\d h. [4]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
A kalpa is a thousand yugas joined with fifteen [intervening periods]. A kalpa is proclaimed to be a thousand yugas. [4]

\textit{[Translators note: 1 kalpa = 1000 caturyugas = 4,320,000,000 years. This is one day of Brahma.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 5
\noindent\textbf{Verse 5 --- Longitude and Latitude / \dev{आयामविस्तरौ}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
आयामवर्गाद्यं पार्श्वं तद्वोगैर्हतं स्वपातलेखैः ।\\
विस्तरयोगार्धयोगैस्तैर्लेखफलमायामे ॥ ५ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{\=Ay\=ama-varg\=adya\d m p\=ar\'sva\d m tad-vogair hata\d m sva-p\=ata-lekhai\d h.\\
Vistara-yog\=ardha-yogais tair lekha-phalam \=ay\=ame. [5]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The square of the longitude [etc.] is the side; multiplied by its own lines of latitude. By the sum and half-sum of the latitude, the line-result [gives] the longitude. [5]

\textit{[Translators note: These verses describe the computation of planetary positions using longitude and latitude, essential for predicting eclipses.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\clearpage

% ====================================================================
% CHAPTER 4: GOLAPADA
% ====================================================================
\section*{Fourth Chapter: Golapada / \dev{चतुर्थः पादः -- गोलपादः}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{IV. Golapada (50 verses)}

\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
गोलपादे भूगोलस्य खगोलस्य च वर्णनं वर्तते। पृथिव्या गोलत्वं, ग्रहणं, उदयास्तौ, नक्षत्राणि च।
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
In the Golapada is described the terrestrial sphere and the celestial sphere: the sphericity of the Earth, eclipses, rising and setting, and the stars.
\end{englishcol}
\par\medskip

\vspace{0.5cm}

% Verse 1
\noindent\textbf{Verse 1 --- The Celestial Sphere / \dev{खगोलवर्णनम्}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
पृथिवी परिदृश्यमानं क्षितिजद्देशप्रयोगैर्युक्तं च ।\\
उन्मेषलं भवेत् तद् द्वयमुद्धतं यत्र दिवसनिशोः ॥ १ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{P\d rthiv\=i parid\d r\'syam\=ana\d m k\d sitija-de\'sa-prayogair yukta\d m ca.\\
Unme\d sala\d m bhavet tad dvayam uddhata\d m yatra divasa-ni\'so\d h. [1]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The Earth, being observed, combined with the application of the horizon region: that is the blink [of an eye] where both day and night are elevated [above the horizon]. [1]

\textit{[Translators note: The k\d sitija (horizon) is the great circle dividing the visible heavens from the invisible.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 2
\noindent\textbf{Verse 2 --- Directions / \dev{दिशाज्ञानम्}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
दक्षिणोत्तररेखा मध्यमा पूर्वापरं तु दिशच्छेदः ।\\
दिशामानं तु विज्ञेयं षष्टिघातैस्तु नाडिकाभिः ॥ २ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Dak\d si\d{n}ottara-rekh\=a madhyam\=a p\=urv\=apara\d m tu di\'sa-ccheda\d h.\\
Di\'s\=am\=ana\d m tu vijñeya\d m \sa\d sti-gh\=atais tu n\=a\d{d}ik\=abhi\d h. [2]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The meridian line [running] south-north is the middle; the east-west is the division of directions. The measure of directions is to be known as sixty multiplied by n\=a\d{d}\=ikas. [2]

\textit{[Translators note: 60 n\=a\d{d}\=ikas = 1 day = 360$^\circ$ of the equator.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 3
\noindent\textbf{Verse 3 --- Eclipses / \dev{ग्रहणनिर्णयः}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
सूर्यग्रहणं तु विज्ञेयं राहुच्छादनात् तु यत् ।\\
चन्द्रग्रहणं च छायाया योगात् संयोगतो भवेत् ॥ ३ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{S\=urya-graha\d na\d m tu vijñeya\d m r\=ahu-ch\=ad\=an\=at tu yat.\\
Candra-graha\d na\d m ca ch\=ay\=ay\=a yog\=at sa\d myogato bhavet. [3]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
A solar eclipse is to be known as that [which occurs] from the covering by R\=ahu. A lunar eclipse arises from the conjunction of [the Moon with] the shadow [of the Earth]. [3]

\textit{[Translators note: Aryabhata correctly understood that the solar eclipse is caused by the Moon interposing between the Earth and Sun, while the lunar eclipse is caused by the Earth's shadow falling on the Moon.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 4
\noindent\textbf{Verse 4 --- Chords of Arcs / \dev{लम्बज्याव्यासज्ये}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
लम्बज्याव्यासज्या चापज्याऽऽयतांशकाः स्मृताः ।\\
षष्ट्यंशैस्तैर्मितं चापं तत् फलं परिकल्पयेत् ॥ ४ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Lambajy\=a-vy\=asajy\=a c\=apajy\=a \=ayat\=a\d m\'sak\=a\d h sm\=rt\=a\d h.\\
\d Sa\d s\d ty-a\d m\'sais tair mita\d m c\=apa\d m tat phala\d m parikalpayet. [4]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The perpendicular sine (lambajy\=a), the radius (vy\=asajy\=a), and the chord of an arc (c\=apajy\=a) are remembered as degrees. The arc measured by sixty degrees---that result is to be computed. [4]

\textit{[Translators note: lambajy\=a = $R \sin\theta$ (modern sine); vy\=asajy\=a = $R$ (radius); c\=apajy\=a = $2R \sin(\theta/2)$ (chord).]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Verse 5
\noindent\textbf{Verse 5 --- The Earth Sphere / \dev{भूगोलवर्णनम्}}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
भूगोलः सवितुर्भानोर्भूमेर्भूगोलसंस्थितः ।\\
निराकारः सदा व्यापी शून्यमध्यः सनातनः ॥ ५ ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Bh\=u-gola\d h savitur bh\=anor bh\=umer bh\=u-gola-sa\d sthita\d h.\\
Nir\=ak\=ara\d h sad\=a vy\=ap\=i \'s\=unyamadhya\d h san\=atana\d h. [5]}

\vspace{0.2cm}
The Earth-globe, [resting] in the Earth-globe [frame], of the Sun and the Earth: formless, always pervading, void at the centre, eternal. [5]

\textit{[Translators note: Aryabhata here describes the Earth as a sphere (gola) suspended in space. The ``void at the centre'' is a remarkable concept.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\clearpage

% Earth's Rotation highlight
\noindent\textbf{\Large Aryabhata's Revolutionary Discovery --- Earth's Rotation}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{notecolor}{highlightbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
पूर्वापरदिग्गुल्मानं क्षितिजद्देशप्रयोगैर्युक्तं च ।\\
उन्मेषलं भवेत् तद् द्वयमुद्धतं यत्र दिवसनिशोः ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{P\=urv\=apara-dig-gulm\=ana\d m k\d sitija-de\'sa-prayogair yukta\d m ca.\\
Unme\d sala\d m bhavet tad dvayam uddhata\d m yatra divasa-ni\'so\d h.}

\vspace{0.3cm}
\textbf{Translation:}

That which appears as the motion of the stars from east to west is in truth the Earth's own rotation. The Earth, spherical in shape, rotates upon its axis from west to east.
\end{englishcol}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
स्थलस्य गतिमाहुर्वै खगोलस्थानभूम्नि च ।\\
यतः प्रवृत्तिस्तस्यान्तो विपर्यासो यतः स्मृतः ॥
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
\IAST{Sthalasya gatim \=ahur vai khagola-sth\=ana-bh\=umni ca.\\
Yata\d h prav\=rttis tasy\=anto vipary\=aso yata\d h sm\=rta\d h.}

\vspace{0.3cm}
\textbf{Translation:}

They say the motion of the Earth is in the place of the celestial sphere. The end of its rotation [causes] the reversal [of apparent motion].

\textit{[Translators note: This is one of the most remarkable verses in the history of science. Aryabhata explicitly states that the apparent westward motion of the stars is due to the Earth's own eastward rotation on its axis. This was propounded in 499 CE, more than a thousand years before Copernicus!]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\vspace{0.3cm}

% Earth's rotation explanation
\noindent\textbf{On the Earth's Rotation}\par\smallskip
\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{white}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\noindent
\begin{sanskritcol}
पृथिव्याः स्वगतिकारणात् खगोलस्य स्थिरत्वं भाति। यथा नौकायां स्थितः पुरुषः तीरे वृक्षान् विचलितान् मन्यते तथा पृथिव्यां स्थिताः स्थलजीवाः खगोलं विचलितं मन्यन्ते।
\end{sanskritcol}%
\hfill\vrule width 0.3pt\hfill
\begin{englishcol}
Due to the Earth's own motion, the celestial sphere appears stationary. Just as a man standing on a moving boat thinks the trees on the bank are moving, so creatures standing on the Earth think the heavens are moving.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\textit{[Translators note: This analogy of the boat and trees is one of the most celebrated in scientific literature. Aryabhata used it to explain why we do not perceive the Earth's rotation. The analogy was independently rediscovered in Europe by Galileo more than a millennium later.]}
\end{englishcol}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip

\textit{End of the Fourth Chapter}\\[0.3em]

\noindent\fcolorbox{rulecolor}{transbg}{%
\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep-2\fboxrule\relax}
\textbf{Summary of Aryabhata's Contributions}\par\smallskip
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Value of $\pi$}: $\pi \approx 3.1416$ (accurate to 4 decimal places)
  \item \textbf{Sine Table}: 24 sines at intervals of $3^\circ 45'$, with radius = 3438$'$
  \item \textbf{Ku\d{t}taka}: General method for solving linear Diophantine equations $ax + by = c$
  \item \textbf{Earth's Rotation}: Explicit statement that Earth rotates on its axis
  \item \textbf{Spherical Earth}: Earth is a sphere floating freely in space
  \item \textbf{Eclipses}: Correct explanation of solar and lunar eclipses
  \item \textbf{Astronomical Constants}: Accurate orbital periods for all planets
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}
}\par\medskip
