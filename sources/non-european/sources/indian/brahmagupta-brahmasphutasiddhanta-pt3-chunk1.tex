\section*{Preface to the Final Volume}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Preface to the Final Volume}

This final volume of the \textit{Br\=ahmasphu\d tasiddh\=anta} completes the monumental work begun in the first two volumes. Pages 1677--1816 cover the concluding chapters of Brahmagupta's masterpiece, including:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{Chapter XII (Ga\d nit\=adhy\=aya)}: Concluding sections on arithmetic operations, including circumference calculations (\textit{paridhi}), shadow computations (\textit{ch\=ay\=a vyavah\=ara}), and the application of these to gnomonic instruments.
  \item \textbf{Chapter XIII}: Algebraic operations, including the extraction of square roots of non-square numbers (\textit{vikalavarga}), surds, and indeterminate analysis.
  \item \textbf{Chapter XIV}: Astronomical problems (\textit{K\=alaj\~n\=ana}) --- calculations of time, planetary positions, and the determination of days and dates.
  \item \textbf{Chapters XV--XVI}: Lunar and solar calculations, including the computation of eclipses (\textit{graha\d na}), the \textit{mandasphu\d ta} (equation of centre), and \textit{\'s\=ighraphala} (second inequality).
  \item \textbf{Chapters XVII--XIX}: Planetary theory, epicyclic models, and the determination of planetary positions using geocentric models.
  \item \textbf{Chapters XX--XXI}: Advanced astronomical computations, including the \textit{bhogakal\=a} (daily motion), \textit{pr\=a\d nakal\=a}, and the final colophons.
\end{itemize}

The text preserves Brahmagupta's original verses in their authentic Sanskrit form, accompanied by detailed Hindi commentary, mathematical proofs (\textit{upapatti}), and where appropriate, modern mathematical notation to aid comprehension.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent\rule{0.3\textwidth}{0.4pt}\\
\textit{-- The Editors}

\section{Conclusion of Arithmetic (\dev{राशि व्यवहार}) --- \dev{परिधि} \& \dev{छाया व्यवहार}}

The present section begins with the final verses of the twelfth chapter (\dev{गणिताध्याय}), which treat the calculation of circumference (\dev{परिधि}), proportion (\dev{अनुपपत्ति}), and the computation of shadows (\dev{छाया व्यवहार}).

\subsection{\dev{परिधि} --- Circumference and Proportion}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{परिधौ क्रमशः प्राग्वत् स्वगुणात् भवेत् फलम्~|}\\
\dev{श्रीपत्युक्तमिदं चाचार्यैरनुक्रमेणावबोधित~||१७||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 12.37 / Vol.~II, p.~891)} This verse states that the circumference is obtained by multiplying the diameter successively by the prescribed factors, as taught by previous teachers including \'Sr\=ipati. Brahmagupta, Bh\=askar\=ac\=arya, and L\=il\=avat\=i have all taught this in their respective works.

\begin{shloka}
\dev{द्विवेद सचित्रभागे कनिष्ठान्तु परिधिः फलम्~|}\\
\dev{मिथ्यन्तबोधकोऽणस्थराशिः स्वगुणाभिजं तत्~||१७१||}
\end{shloka}

\verseno{171} The true circumference is that obtained by the rules taught by the two Dvivedis; the circumference otherwise (from crude approximation) is erroneous.

\subsection{\dev{अनुपपत्तिः} --- Mathematical Proof}

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{अनुपपत्तिः} --- \dev{मिथ्यान्तः संलग्न धान्यराशिः परिधिवास्तव परिधेर्बंधुतुल्यः~| क्रान्तः कोण स्थितस्य धान्यराशिः परिधिवास्तवपरिधिं चतुर्थांशतुल्यः~| बाहुकोणसंलग्न धान्यराशिः परिधिवास्तव परिधेः पादेनैक तुल्यस्तेनोत्परिधित्रयमानं द्विवेद-सचित्रभागेकेन क्रमशो गुणितां सघातं परिधिं भवेत्~| ततः पूर्वं वेधफलं ततु द्विवेद सचित्रभागेकेन क्रमशो भक्तं तदा वास्तवं तद्फलं भवतीत्येतावताऽऽचार्योक्त-मुपपन्नम्~||१७१||}
\end{hindicomm}

\subsection{End of Arithmetic Chapter --- \dev{इति राशि व्यवहार}}

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{इति राशि व्यवहारः} --- \dev{अब भत्ति के अन्तर्गत भत्ति के क्रान्तः कोणरूप तथा बाहुकोणरूप धान्यराशि प्रमाणा-नयन के लिये कहते हैं~|}
\end{hindicomm}

\subsection{\dev{छाया व्यवहार} --- Shadow Calculations}

\subsubsection*{BSS 12.41 \verseno{४१}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{तज्जादौ छायात् इष्टकालज्ञानार्थमिष्टकालतच्छायानयनार्थं चाहु~|}\\
\dev{छायानरसंक्रमहृतं शुद्धं प्रागपरयोर्दिगतदोषं~|}\\
\dev{दिनगतदोषांशहृतं धु दलं छाया नरखेखं~||४२||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 12.42)} The calculation of shadows for finding the time from a given shadow, or the shadow at a given time. The \textit{nara} (sine of latitude) multiplied by the shadow, divided by the \textit{\'sa\~nku} (gnomon), gives the \textit{\'suddha} (mean) value; considering the eastern or western direction, this is divided by the \textit{dinagata-do\d s\=a\d m\'sa} (ascensional difference) to obtain the accurate shadow.

\begin{sanskcomm}
\dev{सू. भा.---नरः शङ्कुः रभिमष्टः~| छायाया यो नरः शङ्कुं भागः स संक्रमे यस्तेन}\\
\dev{शुद्धं हृतं लब्धं प्राक् पश्चिमकपाले दुग्गतं पश्चिमकपाले दुग्गतं भवति~|}
\end{sanskcomm}

The \dev{छाया व्यवहार} (shadow computations) section continues with detailed derivations. The \textit{nara} is the sine of latitude, and the \textit{\'sa\~nku} is the gnomon (12 a\~ngulas). The shadow is computed from the relationship between these quantities.

\section{Algebraic Operations --- \dev{विकलवर्गानयन}}

\subsection*{BSS 12.46 \verseno{४६}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{इदानीं सकल विकलवर्गानयनार्थमाह~|}\\
\dev{स्वविकलपदं वर्गांशगुणाः सकलस्त्रिशद्द्वैतो विकलवर्गः~|}\\
\dev{प्रक्षेप्यः सकलकृतौ वर्गधनो द्वित्रितुल्यबधो~||४६||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 12.46)} Now (the author) teaches the method of finding the square root of a non-square number. The square of the \textit{sakala} (integer part) is subtracted from the number; the remainder, multiplied by the square of the denominator, becomes the \textit{vikalavarga} (square of the non-square part). This is to be added to the square of the \textit{sakala}, and the result gives the square root approximately.

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{सू. भा.---यत्र राशेर्कला विकला चेति द्वयं वर्तते तस्य वर्गार्थं कलाराशिः}\\
\dev{सकलसंज्ञो विकलाराशिश्च स्वविकलस्तत्कलापश्चात् शास्त्र कथये~|}
\end{hindicomm}

\subsubsection*{Upapatti (Mathematical Proof)}

\begin{sanskcomm}
\dev{उपपत्तिः} --- \dev{कल्पयते कलिमश्रवि राशौ कलाः = क~| विकलाः = वि~| तदा कलात्मकः}\\
\dev{स राशिः = क + वि/६०~|}
\end{sanskcomm}

The general formula for the square root of a compound number $k + \frac{vi}{60}$ is derived as:
\begin{align*}
\dtxt{वर्गः} &= k^2 + \frac{2k \cdot vi}{60} + \frac{vi^2}{60^2} = k^2 + \frac{k \cdot vi}{30} + \frac{vi^2}{60^2}
\end{align*}
Note: ``\dev{वर्गः}'' here means `square' in Sanskrit.

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{वि. भा.---यदिमन् राशौ कला-विकला चेति द्वयं वर्तते तस्य वर्गं करणार्थं}\\
\dev{कलाराशिः सकल संज्ञो विकलाराशिश्च स्वविकलस्तत्कलापश्चात् शास्त्र कथये~|}
\end{hindicomm}

\subsection*{BSS 12.47 \verseno{४७}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{छेदेनेष्टयुतोनेनार्धं भाज्यपदष्टष्टिगुणं स्यात्~|}\\
\dev{प्रकृतिरष्टच्छेदहृतं लब्धा युतं हैनिकमनघं~||४७||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 12.47)} This verse gives the method for division with fractions. The dividend, combined with the divisor (in the manner of adding fractions), is divided by half the divisor multiplied by the denominator of the divisor; the result, added to the \textit{prak\d rti}, gives the quotient.

\begin{sanskcomm}
\dev{उपपत्तिः} --- \dev{कल्पयते~| भाज्यः = भा~| छेदः = हा~| इष्टम् = ई~| तदा वास्तवा लब्धिः = भा/हा~|}
\end{sanskcomm}

The derivation continues with the algebraic manipulation of fractional quantities.

\subsection*{BSS 12.49 \verseno{४९}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{इदानीं भागहारे विरोपमाह~|}\\
\dev{छेदेनेष्टयुतोनेनार्धं भाजयपदष्टष्टिगुणं स्यात्~|}\\
\dev{प्रकृतिरष्टच्छेदहृतं लब्धा युतं हैनिकमनघं~||४९||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 12.49)} The section on division with fractions (\textit{bh\=agah\=ara}) is now explained. The rule involves the manipulation of fractional quantities through their denominators.

\section{Astronomical Problems --- \dev{कालज्ञान}}

The following sections deal with astronomical computations, including the determination of time, planetary positions, and the computation of various astronomical quantities.

\subsection{Mixed Astronomical Problems --- \dev{मिश्र प्रश्न}}

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{अब मिश्र प्रश्नों को कहते हैं~|}
\end{hindicomm}

\subsubsection*{BSS 13.6 \verseno{६}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{इदानीं मध्यान् प्रश्नानाह~|}\\
\dev{व्यतिपातं वैधृतिबृहस्पतिवर्षयोश्चर्चनीचरिवबस्तीन~|}\\
\dev{द्विप्रहयोगांश युगे यो वेति स कालतत्वज्ञः~||६||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 13.6)} He who knows the \textit{vyat\=ip\=ata}, \textit{vaidh\d rti}, \textit{b\d rhaspati-var\d sa}, and the motion of the nodes in a \textit{yuga} --- he is the knower of the essence of time.

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{सू. भा.---यो व्यतिपातं वेति~| वैधृतिं वेति बृहस्पतिवर्षं वेति,}\\
\dev{स्वोच्चनीचमार्गगान् वेति~| युगे द्विप्रहयोगांश इत्यादि~|}
\end{hindicomm}

\subsubsection*{BSS 13.10 \verseno{१०}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{सावनमासावधिप्रहरेक्काऽनिष्टमध्यसंयोगात्~|}\\
\dev{इष्टद्वयु संयु तोननिष्टानां यो वेति ग्रह्यकः सः~||१०||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 13.10)} He who knows the sum of the \textit{avama} (omitted tithis) and the \textit{praghara} (excess days), combined with the mean (longitudes) at the desired time --- he is the knower of the \textit{grahyaka} (the quantity to be known).

\subsection{Third Set of Problems --- \dev{तृतीय प्रश्नसमूह}}

\begin{shloka}

\section{Planetary Theory --- \dev{मन्दस्फुट} \& \dev{शीघ्रफल}}

\subsection{Epicyclic Model --- \dev{पर्याय} \& \dev{मन्दकर्ण}}

The computation of planetary positions involves the \textit{mandasphu\d ta} (equation of centre) and \textit{\'s\=ighraphala} (equation of anomaly, or second inequality). Brahmagupta gives the following method:

\begin{shloka}
\dev{मन्दस्फुटार्केन्द्रांशोनायां ग्रहस्यन्दं केन्द्रम्~|}\\
\dev{एव यदि तत् स्पष्टं त्रिकोणं तदानीं विशेषं नोद्येत्~|}\\
\dev{तद्यात् स्पष्टं त्रिकोणं तस्य स्पष्टं द्वितीयं केन्द्रं}\\
\dev{क्रमेण केन्द्रान्तरं फलमिति~| वा ज्ञानमन्दं स्पष्टं}\\
\dev{प्राप्यच्छी~स्साचार्य्योदिना शीघ्र-कर्त्तव्यं कार्यमिति सर्वं शुद्धम्~||३७८-५८१||}
\end{shloka}

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{वि. भा.---उदयमार्ग (विज्ञानान्) भुजयया गुणिगतं कर्णहृतं यत्फलं तस्य}\\
\dev{चापं स्पष्टद्वितीयोनयोरन्तरं भवेत् तत् स्पष्टमिति~||३७९-५८१||}
\end{hindicomm}

\subsection{\dev{उपपत्तिः} --- Proof of Epicyclic Correction}

\begin{sanskcomm}
\dev{उपपत्तिः} --- \dev{ज = गोच्चेभम्~| म = मन्दस्पष्टग्रहः~| मं३ = शीघ्रकेन्द्रम्~| मर = शीघ्र-}\\
\dev{केन्द्रज्या = गोच्चज्या~| स्प. = स्पष्टग्रहः~| स्प.३ = स्पष्ट केन्द्रम्~|}

\dev{केन्द्रराशिः = मन्दस्पष्टदोषयोरन्तरं~|}
\end{sanskcomm}

The \textit{mandasphu\d ta} (true planet via equation of centre) is computed as:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compute the \textit{mandakendra} (mean anomaly): difference between mean longitude and apogee (\dev{मन्दोच्च})
  \item Find the \textit{mandaphala} (equation of centre) from the sine table
  \item Apply to the mean longitude to get \textit{mandasphu\d ta}
  \item Compute \textit{\'s\=ighrakendra} from \textit{mandasphu\d ta}
  \item Find \textit{\'s\=ighraphala} and apply to get the true longitude
\end{enumerate}

\section{Advanced Astronomical Computations --- \dev{भोगकला} \& \dev{प्राणकला}}

\subsection*{BSS 21.25--26 \verseno{२५--२६}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{इष्टज्यां संगुणीताः पश्चकर्मणां कर्णच्चन्द्रम्~|}\\
\dev{इष्टज्यापाद्युत्थ्यासर्वं विभाजिता लब्धम्~||२५||}\\
\dev{नवति छते प्रोह पदे नवते मनोरथं शेषमगणत्कला~|}\\
\dev{एवं धनुरिष्टदया भवति ज्यया विना ज्यार्धिः~||२६||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 21.25--26)} The \textit{i\d stajy\=a} (desired sine) is multiplied by the results of the five operations and divided by the radius; the remainder, when 90 is subtracted from the \textit{nava} position, gives the \textit{\'se\d sa}; the \textit{manoratha} (desired arc) is obtained. The arc is thus found from the sine without the \textit{jy\=ardha} (half-chord).

\begin{sanskcomm}
\dev{सू. भा.---इष्ट ज्यायाः पादद्वयपरिलेखेन युक्त व्यासार्धं तद्द्वारा तेन}\\
\dev{विभाजिताः~| शेष स्पष्टम्~|}
\end{sanskcomm}

\subsubsection*{Upapatti --- Detailed Proof}

\begin{sanskcomm}
\dev{उपपत्तिः} --- \dev{पूर्वोक्तकोपपरित्य ज्या = (१८०-चा) चा.ज्र. / [१०१२५-(१८०-चा) चा/४]}\\
\dev{या ज्या / [१०१२५-या/४] = ज्या/१०१२५-या/४}
\end{sanskcomm}

The derivation proceeds:
\begin{align*}
\dtxt{ज्या} &= \frac{(180 - \dtxt{चा}) \cdot \dtxt{चा} \cdot \dtxt{ज्र}}{10125 - \frac{(180-\dtxt{चा})\dtxt{चा}}{4}} \\
\dtxt{या} &= (180 - \dtxt{चा}) \cdot \dtxt{चा}
\end{align*}

\begin{sanskcomm}
\dev{या = (१८०-चा) चा~| अतः शेषद्वारागमेन ज्या × ४०५०० = ज्या. या}\\
\dev{= ४ ज्या. या, ततो या = ज्या × ४०५०० = १०१२५. ज्या}\\
\dev{ज्या + ६ ज्या / ६}\\
= ल = (१८०-चा) चा = १८० चा - चा\(^2\)\\
समशोधनेन चा\(^2\) - १८० चा + ल = ०\\
\(\therefore\) चा = ९० \(\pm\) \(\sqrt{\mbox{\devanagarifont ९०}^2 - ल}\)\\
आचार्योक्तमर्क चापं गृहीतं तेन चा = ९० - \(\sqrt{\mbox{\devanagarifont ९०}^2 - ल}\)\\
\dev{अत उपपन्नं सर्वम्~||२५-२६||}
\end{sanskcomm}

\subsection*{BSS 21.50--52 \verseno{५०--५२}}

\begin{shloka}
\dev{षड्भिर्भोगकलानामेकयम् = ९ चग}\\
\dev{षड्भिर्भोगकलानामेकयम् = ३ चग}\\
\dev{पञ्चदशैर्भोगकलानामेकयम् = १५ चग = १५ चग}\\
\dev{सर्वयोगकलाः = २७ चग}\\
\dev{चक्रकलाभ्यः शुद्धा सर्वयोगकला जाता श्रभिज्ञुगकलास्तदिहनगतिः = चक्-२७ चग~|}\\
\dev{इय कल्पकृत् दिनगुणाः इष्टचक्रकलामका जाता~| कल्पे}\(^3\)\dev{भीजनो}\\
\dev{भगणाः = क्षु --- २७ क्षभं~| शेषोपपत्ति मोर्तकृत् मुहनक्षत्रायन विभिन्नकृत्~||५०-५२||}
\end{shloka}

\cnote{(BSS 21.50--52)} These verses compute the \textit{bhogakal\=a} (daily motion in minutes of arc). For the Sun, Moon, and planets, the daily motion is found by taking the difference in longitude between successive days and converting to \textit{kal\=as}.

\begin{hindicomm}
\dev{वि. भा.---रविः (चन्द्रस्य) मध्यगतिकला अथवा$^3$वैर्क (ई, ई, ई) गुणा-}\\
\dev{स्तदाऽऽध्यार्थ$^3$समनक्षत्राणां भोगकलाः स्युः~|}
\end{hindicomm}

The \textit{bhogakal\=a} is the daily motion of a planet in terms of \textit{kal\=as} (arc-minutes). The computation involves:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Finding the difference in longitude between successive days
  \item Converting this difference into minutes (\textit{kal\=as})
  \item For the Sun: about 1° per day (approximately 60 \textit{kal\=as})
  \item For the Moon: about 13° per day
\end{itemize}

\section*{Appendix: Summary of Mathematical Rules}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Shadow rule (\dev{छाया व्यवहार})}: The shadow is proportional to the \textit{nara} (sine of latitude) and inversely proportional to the \textit{\'sa\~nku} (gnomon height).

  \item \textbf{Square root of non-squares (\dev{विकलवर्गानयन})}: The method of successive approximation for extracting roots of non-square numbers using the formula:
  \[ \sqrt{N} = a + \frac{r}{2a + 1} \]
  where $a$ is the largest integer such that $a^2 \leq N$ and $r = N - a^2$.

  \item \textbf{Equation of centre (\dev{मन्दफल})}: The correction for the eccentricity of planetary orbits, computed using the \textit{jy\=a} (sine) of the \textit{kendra} (anomaly).

  \item \textbf{Second inequality (\dev{शीघ्रफल})}: The correction for the planet's position relative to the Sun-Earth line, computed from the \textit{\'s\=ighrakendra} (anomaly of conjunction).

  \item \textbf{Ku\d t\d taka (\dev{कुट्टक})}: The pulverizer method for solving linear Diophantine equations $ax + c = by$, essential for calendar calculations.

  \item \textbf{Bhogakal\=a (\dev{भोगकला})}: The daily motion of a planet in arc-minutes, computed from the difference in longitude between successive days.

  \item \textbf{Arc from sine (\dev{ज्याचापानयन})}: The inverse problem of finding the arc from its sine, solved by Brahmagupta's algebraic formula without using the \textit{jy\=ardha} (half-chord) table.
\end{enumerate}
