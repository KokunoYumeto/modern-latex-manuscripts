\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

This master index catalogs all typeset works produced as part of the Historical Mathematical Manuscripts Typesetting Project. The collection spans four major mathematical traditions of the medieval and early modern periods, encompassing foundational texts in algebra, arithmetic, geometry, and the history of science.

Each entry provides the title, author, file name for both the \LaTeX\ source and compiled PDF, and a brief description of the work's significance.

\subsection*{Organization}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Islamic/Arabic Mathematics} --- Seven works spanning from al-Khwarizmi (c.~820) to al-Kashi (c.~1427), foundational texts in algebra and arithmetic.
  \item \textbf{Indian Mathematics} --- Eight works from Aryabhata (c.~499) to Bhaskara~II (c.~1150), covering arithmetic and algebra.
  \item \textbf{Chinese Mathematics} --- Sixteen works from Sun Zi (c.~400) to Li Ye (1248), spanning nine centuries of mathematical development.
  \item \textbf{Central Asian References} --- Five historical and bibliographic sources documenting the transmission of mathematical knowledge.
\end{itemize}

\islamicsection{Islamic/Arabic Mathematics}
\label{sec:islamic}

\begin{center}
\textcolor{islamiccolor}{\rule{0.8\textwidth}{1pt}}
\end{center}

The Islamic/Arabic mathematical tradition, flourishing from approximately 750--1600 CE, produced foundational works in algebra, arithmetic, geometry, and trigonometry. The texts in this section represent the most influential mathematical works of this tradition, from the earliest systematic treatise on algebra by al-Khwarizmi to the encyclopedic computational handbook of al-Kashi.

\subsection*{Primary Texts}

\workitem
  {Kitab al-jabr wa-l-muqabala}
  {Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi (c.~820)}
  {islamic/al-khwarizmi-al-jabr-wa-l-muqabala.pdf}
  {islamic/al-khwarizmi-al-jabr-wa-l-muqabala.tex}
  {The foundational text of algebra. Systematic treatment of linear and quadratic equations, including the geometric proofs of solution methods. The word ``algebra'' derives from the title of this work.}

\workitem
  {Sina`at al-jabr}
  {Omar Khayyam (c.~1070)}
  {islamic/omar-khayyam-sinaat-al-jabr.pdf}
  {islamic/omar-khayyam-sinaat-al-jabr.tex}
  {Khayyam's treatise on algebra, containing the first systematic geometric solution of cubic equations using conic sections. Includes his famous proof that cubic equations cannot always be solved by compass and straightedge.}

\workitem
  {Kitab Shakl al-Qatta`}
  {Nasir al-Din al-Tusi (c.~1260)}
  {islamic/al-tusi-shakl-al-qatta.pdf}
  {islamic/al-tusi-shakl-al-qatta.tex}
  {Treatise on the complete quadrilateral (Shakl al-Qatta`), containing fundamental results in trigonometry and spherical geometry, including the sine law for plane and spherical triangles.}

\workitem
  {Miftah al-hisab}
  {Jamshid al-Kashi (c.~1427)}
  {islamic/al-kashi-miftah-al-hisab.pdf}
  {islamic/al-kashi-miftah-al-hisab.tex}
  {Comprehensive computational handbook covering arithmetic, algebra, mensuration, and astronomical calculations. Includes the earliest known statement of the binomial theorem for arbitrary integer exponents and extensive calculations with decimal fractions.}

\subsection*{Translations and Editions}

\workitem
  {Algebra of Mohammed ben Musa}
  {Frederic Rosen (translator), 1831}
  {islamic/rosen-algebra-of-mohammed-ben-musa-1831.pdf}
  {islamic/rosen-algebra-of-mohammed-ben-musa-1831.tex}
  {The first complete English translation of al-Khwarizmi's \textit{Kitab al-jabr wa-l-muqabala}, with extensive notes and commentary. Published in London in 1831.}

\workitem
  {Robert of Chester's Latin Translation}
  {Louis C.~Karpinski (editor), 1915}
  {islamic/karpinski-robert-of-chester-latin-translation-1915.pdf}
  {islamic/karpinski-robert-of-chester-latin-translation-1915.tex}
  {Critical edition of Robert of Chester's 12th-century Latin translation of al-Khwarizmi's algebra, the earliest Latin version that introduced algebra to medieval Europe.}

\workitem
  {Zur altesten arabischen Algebra und Rechenkunst}
  {Julius Ruska, 1917}
  {islamic/ruska-zur-aeltesten-arabischen-algebra-1917.pdf}
  {islamic/ruska-zur-aeltesten-arabischen-algebra-1917.tex}
  {German scholarly article on the earliest Arabic algebra and arithmetic, examining the origins and early development of algebraic methods in the Islamic world.}

\indiansection{Indian Mathematics}
\label{sec:indian}

\begin{center}
\textcolor{indiancolor}{\rule{0.8\textwidth}{1pt}}
\end{center}

The Indian mathematical tradition, flourishing from approximately 500--1500 CE, made profound contributions to arithmetic, algebra, trigonometry, and the development of the decimal place-value numeral system. The works in this section span from Aryabhata's astronomical-mathematical treatise to Bhaskara~II's masterworks on arithmetic and algebra.

\subsection*{Primary Texts}

\workitem
  {Aryabhatiya}
  {Aryabhata (c.~499)}
  {indian/aryabhata-aryabhatiya.pdf}
  {indian/aryabhata-aryabhatiya.tex}
  {Foundational astronomical-mathematical treatise in 121 verses, covering arithmetic, algebra, plane and spherical trigonometry, continued fractions, and a highly accurate approximation of $\pi$.}

\workitem
  {Lilavati}
  {Bhaskara~II (c.~1150)}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-lilavati.pdf}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-lilavati.tex}
  {Masterpiece of arithmetic and mensuration, named after Bhaskara's daughter. Covers arithmetic operations, fractions, progressions, compound interest, permutations, and Pythagorean geometry in 277 verses.}

\workitem
  {Bijaganita, Part 1}
  {Bhaskara~II (c.~1150)}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-bijaganita-part1.pdf}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-bijaganita-part1.tex}
  {First part of Bhaskara's algebra treatise, covering operations with positive and negative numbers, zero, unknown quantities, surds, and the kuttaka (pulverizer) method for solving linear indeterminate equations.}

\workitem
  {Bijaganita, Part 2}
  {Bhaskara~II (c.~1150)}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-bijaganita-part2.pdf}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-bijaganita-part2.tex}
  {Second part of the \textit{Bijaganita}, continuing with quadratic equations, equations with multiple unknowns, equations involving products of unknowns, and the cyclic method (cakravala) for solving Pell's equation.}

\workitem
  {Bijaganita, Part 3}
  {Bhaskara~II (c.~1150)}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-bijaganita-part3.pdf}
  {indian/bhaskara-ii-bijaganita-part3.tex}
  {Concluding part of the \textit{Bijaganita}, covering equations with one unknown, the madhyamaharana (elimination of the middle term) method, equations with multiple colors (unknowns), and concluding verses.}

\subsection*{The Brahmasphutasiddhanta}

The \textit{Brahmasphutasiddhanta} (c.~628) of Brahmagupta is one of the most important works in Indian mathematics, containing the first systematic treatment of zero as a number, rules for arithmetic with negative numbers, and solutions to various types of equations.

\workitem
  {Brahmasphutasiddhanta, Part 1 (Chunks 1--6)}
  {Brahmagupta (c.~628)}
  {indian/brahmagupta-brahmasphutasiddhanta-pt1-chunk1..6.pdf}
  {indian/brahmagupta-brahmasphutasiddhanta-pt1-chunk1..6.tex}
  {Part 1 of the \textit{Brahmasphutasiddhanta}, containing chapters on arithmetic (ganita), algebra (kuttaka), and planetary mean motions. Includes the famous rules for arithmetic operations with zero and negative numbers.}

\workitem
  {Brahmasphutasiddhanta, Part 2 (Chunks 1--6)}
  {Brahmagupta (c.~628)}
  {indian/brahmagupta-brahmasphutasiddhanta-pt2-chunk1..6.pdf}
  {indian/brahmagupta-brahmasphutasiddhanta-pt2-chunk1..6.tex}
  {Part 2, continuing with chapters on the lunar crescent, eclipses, conjunctions of planets, and the chapter on algebra. Contains the general solution to the quadratic equation and the adi-kuttaka method.}

\workitem
  {Brahmasphutasiddhanta, Part 3 (Chunks 1--3)}
  {Brahmagupta (c.~628)}
  {indian/brahmagupta-brahmasphutasiddhanta-pt3-chunk1..3.pdf}
  {indian/brahmagupta-brahmasphutasiddhanta-pt3-chunk1..3.tex}
  {Concluding part of the \textit{Brahmasphutasiddhanta}, covering instruments and observations, mathematical tables, and the diyagra (lamp-assumption) method for solving mixed equations.}

\chinesesection{Chinese Mathematics}
\label{sec:chinese}

\begin{center}
\textcolor{chinesecolor}{\rule{0.8\textwidth}{1pt}}
\end{center}

The Chinese mathematical tradition, with a continuous history spanning over two millennia, developed sophisticated methods for solving practical and theoretical problems. The \textit{Jiuzhang Suanshu} (Nine Chapters) stands as the most influential Chinese mathematical text, while the Song--Yuan period (960--1368) represents the golden age of Chinese algebra, with the development of the tian yuan shu (celestial element method) for solving polynomial equations.

\subsection*{Classical Texts}

\workitem
  {Sun Zi Suanjing (Master Sun's Mathematical Manual)}
  {Sun Zi (c.~400)}
  {chinese/sunzi-suanjing.pdf}
  {chinese/sunzi-suanjing.tex}
  {Early mathematical manual containing one of the earliest known formulations of the Chinese Remainder Theorem, along with problems in arithmetic, fractions, and linear algebra.}

\workitem
  {Jiuzhang Suanshu (Nine Chapters on Mathematical Art), Vols.~1--3}
  {Anonymous (compiled c.~200 BCE--200 CE)}
  {chinese/jiuzhang-suanshu-vols1-3.pdf}
  {chinese/jiuzhang-suanshu-vols1-3.tex}
  {Chapters 1--3 of the foundational Chinese mathematical text: Fangtian (rectangular fields), Sumi (millet and rice), and Cuifen (distributive proportions). Contains algorithms for area calculation, proportions, and rule of three.}

\workitem
  {Jiuzhang Suanshu (Nine Chapters on Mathematical Art), Vols.~4--6}
  {Anonymous (compiled c.~200 BCE--200 CE)}
  {chinese/jiuzhang-suanshu-vols4-6.pdf}
  {chinese/jiuzhang-suanshu-vols4-6.tex}
  {Chapters 4--6: Shaoguang (diminishing breadth), Shanggong (consultations on works), and Junshu (equitable taxation). Covers root extraction, volume calculations, and problems of fair distribution.}

\workitem
  {Jiuzhang Suanshu (Nine Chapters on Mathematical Art), Vols.~7--9}
  {Anonymous (compiled c.~200 BCE--200 CE)}
  {chinese/jiuzhang-suanshu-vols7-9.pdf}
  {chinese/jiuzhang-suanshu-vols7-9.tex}
  {Chapters 7--9: Yingbuzu (excess and deficit), Fangcheng (rectangular arrays), and Gougu (right triangles). Contains the double false position method, Gaussian elimination, and the Pythagorean theorem.}

\subsection*{Song--Yuan Mathematical Masters}

\workitem
  {Xiangjie Jiuzhang Suanfa (Detailed Analysis), Part 1}
  {Yang Hui (c.~1261)}
  {chinese/yang-hui-xiangjie-jiuzhang-part1.pdf}
  {chinese/yang-hui-xiangjie-jiuzhang-part1.tex}
  {Yang Hui's detailed commentary on the Nine Chapters, Part 1. Contains the earliest extant presentation of Jia Xian's triangle (Yang Hui's triangle) and explains the incremental method for root extraction.}

\workitem
  {Xiangjie Jiuzhang Suanfa (Detailed Analysis), Part 2}
  {Yang Hui (c.~1261)}
  {chinese/yang-hui-xiangjie-jiuzhang-part2.pdf}
  {chinese/yang-hui-xiangjie-jiuzhang-part2.tex}
  {Part 2 of Yang Hui's commentary, continuing with detailed worked examples from the Nine Chapters and alternative solution methods.}

\workitem
  {Xiangjie Jiuzhang Suanfa (Detailed Analysis), Part 3}
  {Yang Hui (c.~1261)}
  {chinese/yang-hui-xiangjie-jiuzhang-part3.pdf}
  {chinese/yang-hui-xiangjie-jiuzhang-part3.tex}
  {Concluding part of Yang Hui's commentary, completing the detailed analysis of all nine chapters with supplementary problems and methods.}

\workitem
  {Shuxue Jiuzhang (Mathematical Treatise in Nine Sections), Fascicle 1}
  {Qin Jiushao (1247)}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicle1.pdf}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicle1.tex}
  {Fascicle 1 (Dayan lei): Qin Jiushao's systematic treatment of the Dayan (Great Extension) method for solving systems of congruences, a generalization of the Chinese Remainder Theorem.}

\workitem
  {Shuxue Jiuzhang, Fascicles 2--4}
  {Qin Jiushao (1247)}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicle2.pdf}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicle2.tex}
  {Fascicles 2--4 covering astronomical calculations, land measurement, and survey problems. Includes sophisticated interpolation formulas and the complete solution of simultaneous congruences.}

\workitem
  {Shuxue Jiuzhang, Fascicles 5--6}
  {Qin Jiushao (1247)}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicles5-6.pdf}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicles5-6.tex}
  {Fascicles 5--6 on fiscal policy and military calculations. Contains problems on taxation, grain storage, fortification geometry, and troop mobilization.}

\workitem
  {Shuxue Jiuzhang, Fascicles 7--9}
  {Qin Jiushao (1247)}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicles7-9.pdf}
  {chinese/qin-jiushao-shuxue-jiuzhang-fascicles7-9.tex}
  {Concluding fascicles covering construction works, price and interest calculations, and the solution of polynomial equations up to degree 10 using Qin's method (Ruffini-Horner scheme).}

\workitem
  {Suanxue Qimeng (Introduction to Mathematical Studies), Part 1}
  {Zhu Shijie (1299)}
  {chinese/zhu-shijie-suanxue-qimeng-part1.pdf}
  {chinese/zhu-shijie-suanxue-qimeng-part1.tex}
  {Zhu Shijie's introductory textbook, Part 1. Covers fundamental operations, root extraction, and the tian yuan shu (celestial element) method for setting up polynomial equations from word problems.}

\workitem
  {Suanxue Qimeng, Part 2}
  {Zhu Shijie (1299)}
  {chinese/zhu-shijie-suanxue-qimeng-part2.pdf}
  {chinese/zhu-shijie-suanxue-qimeng-part2.tex}
  {Concluding part of the \textit{Suanxue Qimeng}, with advanced problems in the tian yuan shu method, systems of equations, and applications to commerce and geometry.}

\subsection*{Li Ye: The Ceyuan Haijing}

The \textit{Ceyuan Haijing} (Sea Mirror of Circle Measurements, 1248) is Li Ye's masterpiece, containing 170 problems on the properties of circles inscribed in or tangent to right triangles, all solved using the tian yuan shu method.

\workitem
  {Ceyuan Haijing, Vols.~1--3}
  {Li Ye (1248)}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols1-3.pdf}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols1-3.tex}
  {Volumes 1--3 of the \textit{Ceyuan Haijing}. Opens with the diagram of the round town and develops the systematic use of the tian yuan shu to derive polynomial equations for circle and triangle measurements.}

\workitem
  {Ceyuan Haijing, Vols.~4--6}
  {Li Ye (1248)}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols4-6.pdf}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols4-6.tex}
  {Volumes 4--6, continuing the series of problems on tangent circles and right triangles, with increasingly complex configurations solved by the tian yuan shu method.}

\workitem
  {Ceyuan Haijing, Vols.~7--9}
  {Li Ye (1248)}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols7-9.pdf}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols7-9.tex}
  {Volumes 7--9, with advanced problems involving multiple circles and complex geometric configurations, demonstrating the full power of the celestial element method.}

\workitem
  {Ceyuan Haijing, Vols.~10--12}
  {Li Ye (1248)}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols10-12.pdf}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-vols10-12.tex}
  {Concluding volumes of the \textit{Ceyuan Haijing}, containing the most challenging problems and culminating in the complete classification of circle-triangle configurations.}

\subsection*{Li Ye: The Ceyuan Haijing Fenlei Shishu}

The \textit{Ceyuan Haijing Fenlei Shishu} (Reclassified Methods of the Sea Mirror, 1259) is Li Ye's later work reorganizing the methods of the \textit{Ceyuan Haijing} into ten volumes classified by solution technique.

\workitem
  {Ceyuan Haijing Fenlei Shishu, Vols.~1--3}
  {Li Ye (1259)}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-fenlei-shishu-vols1-3.pdf}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-fenlei-shishu-vols1-3.tex}
  {Volumes 1--3 of the \textit{Fenlei Shishu}, reclassifying the circle measurement problems by algebraic method rather than geometric configuration.}

\workitem
  {Ceyuan Haijing Fenlei Shishu, Vols.~4--5}
  {Li Ye (1259)}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-fenlei-shishu-vols4-5.pdf}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-fenlei-shishu-vols4-5.tex}
  {Volumes 4--5, continuing the systematic reorganization with problems involving multiple unknowns and higher-degree equations.}

\workitem
  {Ceyuan Haijing Fenlei Shishu, Vols.~6--10}
  {Li Ye (1259)}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-fenlei-shishu-vols6-10.pdf}
  {chinese/li-ye-ceyuan-haijing-fenlei-shishu-vols6-10.tex}
  {Concluding volumes of the \textit{Fenlei Shishu}, completing the reclassification and presenting the most advanced techniques of the tian yuan shu and multiple unknown methods.}

\referencesection{Central Asian References}
\label{sec:references}

\begin{center}
\textcolor{refcolor}{\rule{0.8\textwidth}{1pt}}
\end{center}

This section contains historical, bibliographic, and geographical works from the Islamic world that document the lives of mathematicians, the transmission of scientific knowledge, and the organization of learning in the medieval period.

\workitem
  {Tabaqat al-Umam (Categories of Nations)}
  {Sa`id al-Andalusi (c.~1068)}
  {references/said-al-andalusi-tabaqat-al-umam.pdf}
  {references/said-al-andalusi-tabaqat-al-umam.tex}
  {Survey of the nations of the world and their contributions to science. Contains valuable biographical information on mathematicians, astronomers, and philosophers of the Islamic Golden Age.}

\workitem
  {Kitab al-Fihrist}
  {Ibn al-Nadim (c.~987)}
  {references/ibn-al-nadim-kitab-al-fihrist.pdf}
  {references/ibn-al-nadim-kitab-al-fihrist.tex}
  {Encyclopedic bio-bibliographical survey of all books in Arabic, organized by subject. The primary source for knowledge of lost works in Islamic mathematics, astronomy, and natural philosophy.}

\workitem
  {Tarikh al-Hukama (History of the Learned)}
  {Ibn al-Qifti (c.~1248)}
  {references/ibn-al-qifti-tarikh-al-hukama.pdf}
  {references/ibn-al-qifti-tarikh-al-hukama.tex}
  {Biographical dictionary of philosophers, mathematicians, astronomers, and physicians from antiquity through the Islamic period. Contains unique information on the lives and works of scholars.}
