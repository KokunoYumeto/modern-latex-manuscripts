# P43 full-paper closure on LocalCodex v26

## Scope and authority

Paper 43, *Idealdifferentiation und Differente*, has 21 printed pages. Earlier source work had complete-page dispositions for printed pp. 1-6 and 18-21, but its own ledger explicitly left pp. 7-17 open. LocalCodex v26 opened and checked every page in that eleven-page middle band against the best complete local article scan.

The staged PDF contains a 2048 x 3322 monochrome source mask for each printed page. `pdfimages` exposes that mask separately from a misleading color layer; inversion of the native mask gives the clearest available witness. The page mapping is direct: article PDF page 7 is printed p. 7, through PDF page 17 as printed p. 17.

## Confirmed repairs

1. Printed p. 9: restored the homomorphism clause linking `B` and the null ideal with `Q` and `M_o`. The stale TeX put `Q` and `O_o` in the wrong grammatical and mathematical roles.
2. Printed p. 11: restored both displays to `alpha = alpha e`; the stale TeX read `alpha = alpha e_i`.
3. Printed p. 12: restored `r e_i B_i congruent 0 (S_i)` before the subsequent inference of equality.
4. Printed p. 12: restored the heading `Komponentenzerlegung der Differente` in place of `Komponentendarstellung der Differente`.
5. Printed p. 17: restored the printed diplomatic wording `Basis; wo wird` in place of the normalized `Basis; so wird`.
6. Printed p. 17: restored source emphasis on the clause defining the ring generated by the conjugate orders.

Seven atomic TeX edits belong to these six correction groups. Three are hard-mathematical: the ideal-map clause, the two unit-factor displays, and the congruence relation.

## Reusable method lesson

A source PDF can contain a poor-looking base image plus a much sharper soft mask. Inspecting only the apparent color image can falsely classify the source as unusable. For this paper, extracting and inverting the native 2048 x 3322 mask exposed the actual glyph detail and made the `e`/`e_i`, equality/congruence, and blackletter ideal distinctions readable.
