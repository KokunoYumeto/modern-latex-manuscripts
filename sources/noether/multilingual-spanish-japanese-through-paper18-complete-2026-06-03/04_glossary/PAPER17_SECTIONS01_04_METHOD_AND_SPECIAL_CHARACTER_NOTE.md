# Paper 17 §§1-4 method and special-character note

Paper 17 starts a sustained noncommutative module-theoretic vocabulary. For Spanish/Japanese continuity, keep the following policy:

- `Restklasse`: Spanish `clase residual`; Japanese `剰余類`.
- `Restgruppe`: Spanish `grupo residual`; Japanese `剰余群`.
- `einseitiger Modul`: Spanish `módulo unilateral`; Japanese `一側加群`.
- `rechtsseitiger / linksseitiger Modul`: Spanish `módulo por la derecha / por la izquierda`; Japanese `右側加群 / 左側加群`.
- `kleinstes gemeinsames Vielfaches`: Spanish `mínimo común múltiplo`; Japanese `最小公倍式`.
- `größter gemeinsamer Teiler`: Spanish `máximo común divisor`; Japanese `最大公約数`.

Fraktur residue symbols are intentionally left as TeX macros:

`\mM, \mN, \mL, \mG, \mA, \mB, \ma, \mb, \mx, \my, \mz, \me, \mbarA, \mbarB, \bara`.

Do not replace the one-sided module computations by modern two-sided ideal shorthand; the source's point is precisely the restricted multiplication of residue classes by polynomials.
