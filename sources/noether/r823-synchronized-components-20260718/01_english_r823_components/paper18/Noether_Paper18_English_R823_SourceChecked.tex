\section*{18. On a Work by K. Hentzelt, Who Fell in the War, on Elimination Theory.}

\begin{center}
Jahresbericht der DMV 30 (1921), p. 101
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\textbf{11th Session, Friday, September 23, 1921, at 11 a.m.}\\
(Chair: Loewy.)
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\textbf{1. E. Noether: On a work by K. Hentzelt, who fell in the war, on elimination theory.}
\end{center}

Kurt Hentzelt has been missing near Dixmuiden since October 1914 and must be presumed dead. I intend shortly to publish in the \emph{Mathematische Annalen}, in a freely reworked form, the most essential parts of his Erlangen dissertation, which he left behind complete in structure but presented almost unintelligibly.

The issue is the \emph{general problem of elimination theory}: to determine the common zeros of all polynomials belonging to a polynomial ideal $\mathfrak M$. If the variables are first subjected to a linear transformation with indeterminate coefficients, the main result can be stated as follows:

A unique resultant form can be associated with the ideal $\mathfrak M$:
\par\smallskip
\noindent\makebox[\linewidth][c]{\(\displaystyle
R_{\mathfrak M}=R^{(1)}(x_1\ldots x_n)\cdots R^{(i)}(x_i\ldots x_n)\cdots R^{(n)}(x_n)\equiv 0(\mathfrak M),
\)}
\par\smallskip\noindent
in such a way that $R_{\mathfrak M}$ vanishes at all zeros of $\mathfrak M$ and at no others. If $\mathfrak N$ divides $\mathfrak M$ and $R_{\mathfrak N}$ and $R_{\mathfrak M}$ coincide, then $\mathfrak N$ and $\mathfrak M$ themselves coincide.

The second part of the theorem shows that the resultant form yields the zeros with their characteristic multiplicities. It is based on viewing $\mathfrak M$ as a module of linear forms in the power products of $x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1}$; once $x_{i+1},\ldots,x_n$ have been adjoined to the coefficient domain, the individual factors $R^{(i)}(x_i\ldots x_n)$ of $R_{\mathfrak M}$ become the norms, with respect to this module, of the corresponding ``fundamental module.'' Bringing in abstract ideal theory gives the following interpretation: if $\mathfrak M=[\mathfrak Q,\mathfrak Q_1,\ldots,\mathfrak Q_r]=[\mathfrak Q,\mathfrak L]$ is a decomposition of $\mathfrak M$ into primary ideals $\mathfrak Q_i$, with $\mathfrak L$ the complement of $\mathfrak Q$, then to the ideal $\mathfrak Q$ there corresponds a primary factor $Q^{(i)}(x_i\ldots x_n)$ of $R^{(i)}(x_i\ldots x_n)$ which, in the sense specified above, represents the norm of $\mathfrak L$ with respect to $\mathfrak Q$.
