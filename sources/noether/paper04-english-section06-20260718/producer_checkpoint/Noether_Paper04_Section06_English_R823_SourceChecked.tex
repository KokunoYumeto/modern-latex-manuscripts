\section*{\S\ 6. Explicit Representation of the Invariant Formations.\\Folding and Differentiation Processes.}

The results of the preceding sections allow us to complete the theorem on
symbolic representability stated in \S\ 2 (the first fundamental theorem) by
adding explicit representability; namely, we show:

\emph{Theorem IV.} All invariant formations of arbitrary ground forms can be
represented explicitly by matrix products; that is, aside from variable rows
$p_\rho$, only the rows $S^\sigma,\ldots,T^\tau$ of the original forms, or the
rows $P,Q,R$ derived from them by substitution (9) or (11), enter the final
expressions.\footnote{The current R823 German source separates the compound
\emph{Schlu\ss{} ausdr\"ucke} at R823 line 4048. The original print has the
single compound \emph{Schlu\ss ausdr\"ucke}. The printed compound is the
coherent reading and is followed in translation. This source defect has been
recorded for correction in the upstream German corpus.}

For the proof, suppose that an invariant formation depends, besides variable
rows, on the rows $S^\sigma,\ldots,T^\tau$, and suppose that these rows have
been resolved far enough into individual rows,
$S^\sigma=S^{\sigma_1}\cdots S^{\sigma_i},\ldots,
T^\tau=T^{\tau_1}\cdots T^{\tau_k}$, to permit a representation by
determinants. Let the rows be ordered in a sequence
$S^\sigma,\ldots,T^\tau$ that is arbitrary in itself but fixed once and for
all. We select an aggregate of determinants that depends on the first total
row $S^\sigma=S^{\sigma_1}\cdots S^{\sigma_i}$, not merely on individual
component rows. By \S\S\ 3--5 this dependence is a consequence of the general
identities. If we now resolve a variable row $p_\rho$ into the rows $y,v$, or
one of the later rows $T$ into the individual rows $t$, respectively $\tau$,
then the most general identities are composed of (20) and (21). These
identities always lead to an expression containing the row
$S^\sigma=S^{\sigma_1}\cdots S^{\sigma_i}$ explicitly, namely the left-hand
side of (20) and (21). Indeed, (19) shows that only the complete sum on the
right---not a part of it corresponding to a term of (20a)---has the property
of depending only on $S^\sigma$.

As the procedure continues, every row that has once entered explicitly
remains explicit; applying the identities can at most require several rows
to be combined into one, that is, substitution (9). If only a single row
$T^\tau$ remains to be brought to explicit form, two cases are possible.
There may still be a free variable row, one not connected with other rows by
substitution (9); resolving it into component rows $y$ or $v$ completes the
procedure and gives, as in (31), polars of one or more forms containing all
rows explicitly. If no free variable row remains, then, from the way they
arose, we recognize in the totality of the expressions that contain the
individual rows $t$ or $\tau$ but depend on $T^\tau$ the terms of a
decomposition identity. By \S\ 5 these always admit explicit representation
in all rows after substitution (11). This proves the theorem in full
generality. It should also be noted that when only two symbol rows occur,
substitution (11) never appears. Since
$\sigma,n-\sigma,\tau,n-\tau<n$, no variable rows can enter only when the two
symbol rows are united in a single determinant and thus occur explicitly;
as soon as variable rows enter, however, (34) and (33) show that substitution
(11) becomes unnecessary.

It follows that, in order to generate all invariant formations, it suffices
to unite the symbol rows, after adjoining variable rows, into all possible
matrix products. The most general matrix product is of the form
\begin{equation}
 \pair{P^{\mu_1}\cdots P^{\mu_i}}{Q_{\nu_1}\cdots Q_{\nu_k}},
 \qquad \sum\mu=\sum\nu,
\tag{40}
\end{equation}
where the $P$ and $Q$ may be rows of the original forms, may have arisen from
the original rows through a sequence of substitutions (9) or (11), or may be
variable rows. The expressions (40) can be generated by successively uniting
two symbol rows at a time into a matrix product, with the help of variable
rows. Indeed, from
\[
 \pair{q_{n-\mu-\lambda}P^\mu}
 {Q_{\nu_1}p_{n-\nu_1-\lambda}}
 =\bigl(R_\lambda Q_{\nu_1}p_{n-\nu_1-\lambda}\bigr)
\]
one obtains, by uniting with the row $Q_{\nu_2}$, an expression
\[
 \pair{[R_\lambda Q_{\nu_1}]^{n-\lambda-\nu_1}}
 {Q_{\nu_2}p_{n-\nu_1-\nu_2-\lambda}}
 =\pair{q_{n-\mu-\lambda}P^\mu}
 {Q_{\nu_1}Q_{\nu_2}p_{n-\nu_1-\nu_2-\lambda}}.
\]
Continuing the procedure therefore yields an expression (40). If $P$ and $Q$
are not rows of the original forms, (9) and (11), together with what has just
been shown, imply that they too arose by a sequence of unions of two symbol
rows. Thus:

\emph{Theorem V.} The process for generating all invariant formations---the
folding process---consists in successively uniting two symbol rows at a time
into a matrix product, with the help of variable rows.

From two symbol rows $S^\sigma,T^\tau$, where $\sigma\geq\tau$, the following
matrix products can be formed:
\begin{align}
&\pair{q_{n-\sigma-\lambda}S^\sigma}
 {T_{n-\tau}p_{\tau-\lambda}},
 &&(\lambda=1,2,\ldots,\min(\tau,n-\sigma)),\tag{41}\\
&\pair{q_{n-\tau-\mu}T^\tau}
 {S_{n-\sigma}p_{\sigma-\mu}},
 &&(\mu=\sigma-\tau+\lambda),\tag{42}\\
&\pair{q_{n-\tau-\nu}T^\tau}
 {S_{n-\sigma}p_{\sigma-\nu}},
 &&(\nu=1,2,\ldots,\sigma-\tau).
\tag{43}
\end{align}
From (31), however, the following values are obtained for (42) and (43):
\begin{align}
&\pair{q_{n-\sigma-\lambda}S^\sigma}
 {T_{n-\tau}p_{\tau-\lambda}}
 +\sum_{\alpha}\Pi\pair{q_{n-\sigma-\lambda-\alpha}S^\sigma}
 {T_{n-\tau}p_{\tau-\lambda-\alpha}},\tag{42a}\\
&\Pi(q_{n-\sigma}S^\sigma)(T_{n-\tau}p_\tau)
 +\sum_{\beta}\Pi\pair{q_{n-\sigma-\beta}S^\sigma}
 {T_{n-\tau}p_{\tau-\beta}}.
\tag{43a}
\end{align}
Thus the forms (42) can be expressed linearly through (41) and polars of
(41), and the forms (43) through polars of the ground form and of the forms
(41). Likewise, (31) gives the linear dependence of the forms (41) on (42)
and polars of (42). By Clebsch's definition,\footnote{Clebsch, loc. cit.,
\S\ 7.} the forms (42) are therefore equivalent to the forms (41), whereas
(43) leads back to the ground form. The generating process is thus given
equally well by (41) or (42). We shall define the process (41), which is
simpler with respect to the number $\lambda$, as the folding process. The
number $\lambda$, the defect of the folding (cf. \S\ 2), gives the number of
rows missing from the determinant product, namely in the matrix product that
contains the maximum number of rows for the given folding. Since $\lambda$
runs only up to the smaller of $\tau$ and $n-\sigma$, where
$\sigma\geq\tau$, we have
\begin{equation}
 \lambda\leq\frac n2\quad(n\text{ even});\qquad
 \lambda\leq\frac{n-1}{2}\quad(n\text{ odd}).
\tag{44}
\end{equation}

The reduction of (42) and (43) to (41) remains valid when further folding has
turned the rows $p,q$ into symbol rows; for by (36),
\[
 \pair{A^{n-\tau-\varkappa}T^\tau}
 {S_{n-\sigma}B_{\sigma-\varkappa}}
 =\sum_{\alpha=0,\varkappa-\sigma+\tau}^{\tau,n-\sigma}
 \eps\pair{R^{\tau-\alpha}B^{n-\sigma+\varkappa}}
 {A_{\tau+\varkappa}R_{n-\sigma-\alpha}},
\]
where the rows $R$ arose from $S$ and $T$ according to (33). Hence these
forms containing only symbol rows are obtained by folding
$\pair{q_\tau A^{n-\tau-\varkappa}}
{B_{\sigma-\varkappa}p_{n-\sigma}}$, respectively
$\pair{q_{\tau-\alpha}B^{n-\sigma+\varkappa}}
{A_{\tau+\varkappa}p_{n-\sigma-\alpha}}$---according as
$\sigma-\tau\gtreqless 2\varkappa$---with the ground form and the forms (41).

From the symbolic folding process one obtains the invariant differentiation
processes in the familiar way by replacing the symbol rows
$S^\sigma,T_{n-\tau}$ by the rows of differentiation symbols
$\frac{\partial}{\partial p_\sigma}$ and
$\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{n-\tau}}$. As usual, each product
$\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i_1\ldots i_\sigma}}
 \frac{\partial}{\partial q_{j_1\ldots j_{n-\tau}}}$ has the actual meaning
$\frac{\partial^2}{\partial p_{i_1\ldots i_\sigma}
 \partial q_{j_1\ldots j_{n-\tau}}}$. Since the rows
$\frac{\partial}{\partial p_\sigma}$ and
$\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{n-\tau}}$ are cogredient with
$S^\sigma,T_{n-\tau}$, they satisfy the same identities, in particular those
between (42) and (42a), and between (43) and (43a). In summary:

\emph{Theorem VI.} All invariant formations of arbitrary ground forms arise
from them through repeated application of the symbolic folding process (41),
or also of the invariant differentiation processes
\begin{equation}
 \left(q_{n-\sigma-\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_\sigma}
 \middle|\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{n-\tau}}p_{\tau-\lambda}\right),
 \qquad(\sigma\geq\tau,\ \lambda=1,2,\ldots,
 \min(\sigma,n-\tau)).
\tag{45}
\end{equation}
The original print and the current R823 source both give the
paired upper endpoints $\sigma,n-\tau$ in (45), i.e.
$\min(\sigma,n-\tau)$. The inherited English comparison witness changes them
to $\tau,n-\sigma$. The source reading is retained here.

It remains to note that at every folding (41) the total weight of the form,
that is, the sum of the weights of the individual variable rows (cf. \S\ 1),
decreases by the positive number
$2(\lambda^2+\lambda(\sigma-\tau))$. Independently of the general finiteness
proof, this implies the finiteness of the form row, that is, of the totality
of invariant formations of a given ground form that are linear in the
coefficients.\footnote{Cf. Clebsch, loc. cit., \S\S\ 11 and 12: finiteness
of the system of elementary covariants.}

It should further be noted that Theorem VI remains valid for forms that do
not satisfy conditions (12). Each factor in (3),
$\pair{S^\rho}{p_\rho}^{m_\rho}$, can be replaced by a product of $m_\rho$
linear factors
$\pair{A^\rho}{p_\rho}\pair{B^\rho}{p_\rho}\cdots
\pair{M^\rho}{p_\rho}$. The invariant formations thereby pass into those of
a system of linear forms, which need only afterwards be set equal to one
another. For every individual linear form, however, conditions (12), which
contain only second-order differential quotients when differential symbols
are introduced, are always satisfied.
