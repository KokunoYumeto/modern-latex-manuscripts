§ 8. Reduction of the Folding Process to Fundamental Foldings.
Following Theorem VII, we now carry out the reduction of the folding process to fundamental foldings
mentioned at the beginning of § 7. We call (cf. § 5) every folding of two cogredient rows S ρ , T ρ a “cogredient
folding,” and specifically the cogredient folding of defect 1 of two rows S ρ , T ρ a “fundamental folding of type
ρ.” We then prove, in completion of Theorem VI:
   Theorem VIII. The general folding process (41) can be composed from the (n − 1) fundamental foldings of
type ρ, where ρ = 1, 2, . . . , n − 1. In other words, the successive application of the n − 1 fundamental foldings
suffices to generate all invariant formations.
   In the binary domain the folding process (41) coincides directly with the only possible fundamental folding;
in the ternary domain I proved Theorem VIII in § 1 of my dissertation (cf. the Introduction). There, because
of (44), the general cogredient foldings are still identical with the fundamental foldings, and it is noteworthy
that Theorem VIII for n variables accomplishes the reduction to fundamental foldings, not merely the much
weaker reduction to cogredient foldings. The proof is modeled in principle on the ternary proof: the most
general foldings are constructed from the fundamental foldings by applying the symbolic identities. We shall
generate:
   1. arbitrary foldings of defect 1 by cogredient foldings of defect 1, that is, by fundamental foldings (the
      analogue of the ternary case),
   2. arbitrary foldings of defect λ by cogredient foldings of defect λ and arbitrary foldings of defect 1,
   3. cogredient foldings of defect λ by cogredient foldings of defect λ − 1 and arbitrary foldings of defect 1.
  As is essential, we assume by Theorem VII that the two forms S and T to be folded are normal forms.
Thus, by (46), the relations hold:

                    (qn−σ1 −λ S σ1 | Sn−σ2 pσ2 −λ ) = 0, (σ1 ≥ σ2 , λ = 1, 2, . . . , σ2 , n − σ1 ),

    2) Generation of general foldings from cogredient and fundamental foldings. The weight decrease for a
general folding (41) is 2(λ2 + λ(σ − τ )) = 2[λ2 + (σ − τ )(1 + (λ − 1))]. This gives the possibility of decomposing
it into a cogredient folding of defect λ (weight decrease 2λ2 ) and σ − τ foldings of defect 1 with difference of
weight indices λ − 1 (weight decrease 2{1 + (λ − 1)}). In the case λ = 1 this decomposition agrees with the
one given under 1); we show that it can indeed be realized.
    From the cogredient folding of defect λ

                                                  (qn−τ −λ S τ | Tn−τ pτ −λ )

we obtain, by the substitution Rλ ∼ Tn−τ pτ −λ , a form with the two rows1 of upper weight indices τ + λ and
τ + 1:
                                       (Rλ S τ | pτ +λ )(S τ +1 | pτ +1 ).                               (66)
The row with the lower weight index τ + 1 belongs to a normal form; hence (66) admits a folding of defect 1
composed of λ fundamental foldings, in the order of (65):

                                     (Rλ S τ )S τ +λ ,   S τ +λ−1 ,        S τ +λ−2 , . . . , S τ +1 .

Taking account of (31) and (62), this gives

                                 (qn−τ −λ−1 Rλ S τ | Sn−τ −1 pτ ) = ε(Rλ S τ +1 | pτ +λ+1 ),

that is, a form obtained from (66) by replacing τ by τ + 1, just as under 1) for (63). Repeating the process
σ − τ times leads to the desired folding:

                                      (Rλ S σ | pσ+λ ) = ε(qn−σ−λ S σ | Tn−τ pτ −λ ).

foldings. The weight decrease for cogredient foldings of defect λ is 2λ2 = 2((λ − 1)2 + 2λ − 1), giving the

This is most easily found by first putting n = 2λ. The only possible cogredient folding of defect λ is
(S n/2 | Tn/2 ) = (S λ | Tλ ); it contains one row u and one row x fewer than the folding of defect λ − 1 of the
same rows, (uS λ | Tλ x). Conversely, we compose the folding of defect λ from fundamental foldings that effect
the disappearance of a row u and a row x (weight decrease 2(n − 1) = 2(2λ − 1)) and at the same time
generate a new symbol row Rλ , together with a cogredient folding of defect λ − 1. The simplest folding that
does this is the following one of defect 1:

                                    (sT λ | σpλ ) = ε(S 2λ−1 | Rλ−1 pλ ) = ε(Rλ | pλ ),

                         (uS λ | σRλ−1 x) = ε(Rλ+1 | Sλ x) = ε(sT λ | S λ x) = ε(S λ | Tλ ).

   Adding the decomposition obtained under 2), we have indeed obtained a generation of the most general
foldings from fundamental foldings. It remains to show that this generation is unique apart from permutation
of the order; that is, that to each folding there corresponds one and only one number αρ of fundamental
foldings of type ρ, where ρ = 1, 2, . . . , n − 1.
   Let mρ be the degrees in the variable rows pρ of the initial form f , and lρ the degrees of the form produced
by folding. Each fundamental folding of type ρ transforms the degrees mρ−1 , mρ , mρ+1 respectively into
mρ−1 + 1, mρ − 2, mρ+1 + 1. Thus the α satisfy the system of equations

                                mρ − lρ = −αρ−1 + 2αρ − αρ+1 ,                  (ρ = 1, 2, . . . , n − 1),                             (70)

where α0 and αn are to be set equal to zero, and whose determinant has the value n.4 The α are thereby
uniquely determined and, by Theorem VIII, are positive integers; this gives conditions for the differences
mρ − lρ .
   The results of this paragraph hold by virtue of relations (62); however, if relations (62) do not hold, the
final expressions5 are by no means equivalent to the initial expressions. The definition of equivalence given
in § 6 also admits the formulation:6 “Two forms are equivalent if and only if they differ by forms of higher
folding, that is, by forms that exhibit a greater weight decrease.” This greater weight decrease always occurs
when the two rows qn−σ−λ , pτ −λ entering the folding are really variable rows, as happens in (41), (42), in the
foldings appearing in this paragraph as equivalent, and also in the equivalent forms of Theorem VII. But it
need not occur when these rows are derived from symbol rows by substitution, as was the case for the forms
of this paragraph that could be expressed in terms of one another by virtue of (62). One easily sees that some
of the vanishing forms even have a higher total weight.
