\par\indent
If the numbers $p$ and $a$ are both of the form $8n+1$, or both
of the form $8n+5$, one has $\leg{2}{a}=\leg{2}{p}$ and consequently also
$\leg{2^\mu}{a}=\leg{2^\mu}{p}$, and the equality $\leg{t}{p}\leg{2^\mu}{a}=\leg{t}{a}\leg{2^\mu}{p}$ changes into this one:
$\leg{t}{p}=\leg{t}{a}$. If, on the contrary, of the numbers $a$, $p$, one is of the form $8n+1$,
the other of the form $8n+5$, one has $\leg{2}{a}=-\leg{2}{p}$. On the other hand, the equation
$(\eta')$ shows that, in this case, the number $t$ is oddly even, so that
$\mu=1$. One therefore then has $\leg{t}{p}=-\leg{t}{a}$. These two results are comprised
in the formula:
\[
\leg{t}{p}=\leg{t}{a}(-1)^{\frac{a-1}{4}+\frac{p-1}{4}}.
\]

If we combine all that we have just proved, we shall obtain this statement:

\indent If $u$ is even, one has $\leg{t}{p}=\leg{t}{a}$, $\leg{u}{p}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}}$; if $u$ is odd,
one has $\leg{t}{p}=\leg{t}{a}(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{a-1}{4}}$, $\leg{u}{p}=1$.
\end{minipage}
