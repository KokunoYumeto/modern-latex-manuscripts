Let us decompose the numbers $t$ and $u$ into their simple factors, putting
$u=2^\nu kk'k''\ldots$, $t=2^\mu ll'l''\ldots$, $k$, $k'$, $k''\ldots$ and $l$, $l'$, $l''\ldots$ denoting odd
prime numbers, and one of the numbers $\mu$, $\nu$ being equal to zero.

\par\indent
The equation $(\eta')$ first gives:
\[
\leg{p}{k}=1,\quad\leg{p}{k'}=1,\quad\leg{p}{k''}=1,\quad\ldots
\]
whence one then concludes, on applying the law of reciprocity:
\[
\leg{k}{p}=1,\quad\leg{k'}{p}=1,\quad\leg{k''}{p}=1,\quad\ldots.
\]
Taking the product of the preceding equations and of the identical equation $\leg{2^\nu}{p}=\leg{2^\nu}{p}$,
one obtains:
\[
\leg{2^\nu kk'k''\ldots}{p}=\leg{u}{p}=\leg{2^\nu}{p}.
\]
% U:p092.u04 | continuation
It also follows from the equation $(\eta')$, that one has:
\[
\leg{a}{l}=\leg{p}{l},\quad\leg{a}{l'}=\leg{p}{l'},\quad
\leg{a}{l''}=\leg{p}{l''},\quad\ldots.
\]
Since the prime numbers $a$ and $p$ are both of the form $4n+1$,
one concludes from this, by the law of reciprocity:
\[
\leg{l}{a}=\leg{l}{p},\quad\leg{l'}{a}=\leg{l'}{p},\quad
\leg{l''}{a}=\leg{l''}{p},\quad\ldots.
\]
Multiplying all these relations together, one obtains the equation:
\[
\leg{ll'l''\ldots}{a}=\leg{ll'l''\ldots}{p},
\]
which, if one multiplies both its members by $\leg{2^\mu}{a}\leg{2^\mu}{p}$, changes into this one:
\[
\leg{t}{a}\leg{2^\mu}{p}=\leg{t}{p}\leg{2^\mu}{a}.
\]
