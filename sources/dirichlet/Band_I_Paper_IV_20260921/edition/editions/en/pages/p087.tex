Taking the product of all these expressions and of the identical formula
$\leg{2^\nu}{p}=\leg{2^\nu}{p}$, one obtains:
\[
\leg{2^\nu kk'k''\ldots}{p}=\leg{u}{p}=\leg{2^\nu}{p}.
\]
% U:p087.u02 | continuation
The number $p$ is of one of the two forms $8n+1$, $8n+5$. If $p$ is of the
first form, one has, by a known theorem, $\leg{2}{p}=1$, and consequently
also $\leg{2^\nu}{p}=1$. Thus $\leg{u}{p}=1$. If $p$ is of the form $8n+5$, the
number $u$ will be oddly even, so that $\nu=1$, and since one has, on the
other hand, for prime numbers $p=8n+5$, $\leg{2}{p}=-1$, one obtains
$\leg{u}{p}=-1$. The two cases just considered are comprised in
the formula:
\begin{equation*}\tag{$\beta'$}
\leg{u}{p}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}}.
\end{equation*}
% U:p087.u03 | continuation
Let us return to the equation $(\alpha')$ from which we immediately obtain:
\[
\leg{-b}{l}=\leg{p}{l},\quad\leg{-b}{l'}=\leg{p}{l'},\quad
\leg{-b}{l''}=\leg{p}{l''},\quad\ldots.
\]
Since $p$ is a prime number $4n+1$, and $b$ a prime number $4n+3$, one
will find, on applying the law of reciprocity:
\[
\leg{l}{b}=\leg{l}{p},\quad\leg{l'}{b}=\leg{l'}{p},\quad
\leg{l''}{b}=\leg{l''}{p},\quad\ldots.
\]
Multiplication will then give:
\[
\leg{ll'l''\ldots}{b}=\leg{ll'l''\ldots}{p},
\]
or, which comes to the same thing:
\begin{equation*}\tag{$\gamma'$}
\leg{t}{b}=\leg{t}{p}.
\end{equation*}
% U:p087.u04 | continuation
Putting the equation $(\alpha')$ into the form of a congruence, one has:
\[
t^2\equiv bu^2\md{p}.
\]
One concludes from this, by raising both members to the power $\dfrac{p-1}{4}$:
\[
t^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\equiv b^{\frac{p-1}{4}}u^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\md{p},
\]
or, which comes to the same thing, by replacing $u^{\frac{p-1}{2}}$ by its value $(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}}$ given
by the formula $(\beta')$:
\[
t^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\equiv(-b)^{\frac{p-1}{4}}\md{p}.
\]
