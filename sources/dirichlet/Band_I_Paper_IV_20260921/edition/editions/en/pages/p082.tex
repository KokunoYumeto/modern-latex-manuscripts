\par\indent
The difference of the odd numbers $t+\psi$ and $t-\psi$ being $2\psi$ and consequently
divisible by 4, one sees that these two numbers are either both
of the form $4n+1$, or both of the form $4n+3$. Since, on the other
hand, the numbers $E$ and $F$ are both of the form $4n+1$ (these numbers
being odd squares), it follows from the equations $t+\psi=EK$, $t-\psi=FL$, that
the numbers $K$ and $L$ are either both of the form $4n+1$ or both
of the form $4n+3$, and that the first or the second of these cases holds according
as the numbers $t+\psi$ and $t-\psi$ are both of the form $4n+1$, or both
of the form $4n+3$. Let us now distinguish two cases, according as $p$
is of the form $8n+1$ or of this one: $8n+5$. One sees, from the equation
$p^2=\varphi^2+\psi^2$, that, in the first case, $\psi$ is divisible by 4, whence it follows that
$t+\psi$ and $t-\psi$ are then both of the form $4n+1$ or both
of the form $4n+3$, according as $t$ is of the form $4n+1$, or of this one: $4n+3$.
The contrary holds in the case of $p=8n+5$, and the results relating to these
two cases can be comprised in this statement:
% U:p082.u02 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
The numbers $t+\psi$ and $t-\psi$, and consequently also the numbers $K$ and
$L$, are both of the form $4n+1$ or of this one: $4n+3$, according as
$(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{t-1}{2}}$ is equal to 1 or to $-1$.
% U:p082.u03 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
It follows from the equation $\varphi'^2+au'^2=KL$ that the numbers $K$ and $L$ are
divisors of the formula $\varphi'^2+au'^2$ (where $\varphi'$ and $au'$ are relatively prime and where
$a$ denotes a prime number $4n+1$). Now, one knows that, if $R$ denotes an
odd divisor of such a formula, one has:
\[
\leg{R}{a}=1,\quad\text{or}\quad\leg{R}{a}=-1,
\]
according as $R$ is of the form $4n+1$ or of the form $4n+3$. (\emph{Theory of
Numbers}, no. 196.)
% U:p082.u04 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
Applying this theorem to the numbers $K$ and $L$, one obtains:
\[
\leg{K}{a}=\leg{L}{a}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{t-1}{2}},
\]
whence one will then conclude, by multiplying by the expressions $\leg{E}{a}=1$,
$\leg{F}{a}=1$, found above:
\[
\leg{t+\psi}{a}=\leg{t-\psi}{a}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{t-1}{2}},
\]
or, which comes to the same thing, $a$ being a prime number $4n+1$:
\[
\leg{\psi+t}{a}=\leg{\psi-t}{a}=(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{4}+\frac{t-1}{2}}.
\]
