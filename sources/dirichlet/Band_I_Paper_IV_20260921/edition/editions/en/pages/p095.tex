\par\indent
Let us pass to the case where $u$ is odd. The number $u'=\dfrac{u}{m}$ will likewise be
odd in this case, and since the odd squares $(s\varphi')^2$, $u'^2$ are of the form
$8n+1$, one sees that the binomial $(s\varphi')^2-au'^2$ will be of the form $8n$ or of the form
$8n+4$, according as $a$ is of the form $8n+1$ or of this one: $8n+5$. It is easy
to ascertain that, if $a$ is of the form $8n+1$, one has $\leg{K}{a}=1$, $\leg{L}{a}=1$.
Indeed, if one puts $K=2^\beta K'$, $2^\beta$ being the highest power of 2 that
divides $K$, $K'$ will be an odd divisor of $(s\varphi')^2-au'^2$ and one has $\leg{K'}{a}=1$.
On the other hand, one knows, by a known theorem, that every prime number
$a=8n+1$, is such that $\leg{2}{a}=1$. One concludes from this that $\leg{2^\beta}{a}=1$, and then
multiplying by $\leg{K'}{a}=1$, one obtains $\leg{K}{a}=1$. In an entirely
similar manner one proves that one also has $\leg{L}{a}=1$. Multiplying these relations by the following:
$\leg{E}{a}=1$, $\leg{F}{a}=1$, obtained above, one obtains:
\[
\leg{EK}{a}=\leg{t+\psi s}{a}=1,\quad\leg{FL}{a}=\leg{t-\psi s}{a}=1.
\]
It is easy to see that these results, which hold when $u$ is odd and
at the same time $a$ is of the form $8n+1$, can be combined in the formula
$\leg{\psi s\pm t}{a}=1$, where the double sign is at will.
