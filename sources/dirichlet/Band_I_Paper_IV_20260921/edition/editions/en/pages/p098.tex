\par\indent
It has doubtless been observed that the statements of Theorems I and II are
such that only the root $\psi$ of the even square $\psi^2$ obtained by decomposing
$p$ into two squares enters into them. It would be easy to modify these statements so
that they contained only the root $\varphi$ of the odd square. One
would achieve this by following a procedure entirely similar to that which we
have set out in what precedes.
% U:p098.u05 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
The results $(\delta')$ and $(\vartheta')$, on which we have relied to
establish Theorems I and II, themselves contain infinitely many
interesting theorems analogous to the first of M. \textsc{Gauss}'s theorems. One deduces, for
example, from the statement $(\vartheta')$, on supposing $a=5$ in it:
% U:p098.u06 | quoted-statement
\par\indent
“$p$ denoting a prime number $20n+1$, if one puts $p=t^2+5u^2$,
5 will or will not be a biquadratic residue with respect to $p$, according as $u$ is
even or odd; on the contrary, if $p$ denotes a prime number $20n+9$ and one
likewise puts $p=t^2+5u^2$, 5 will or will not be a biquadratic residue with
respect to $p$, according as $u$ is odd or even.”
% U:p098.u07 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
The theorems $(\delta')$ and $(\vartheta')$ concern the equations:
\[
t^2-bu^2=ps^2,\quad t^2+au^2=ps^2.
\]
By considerations of the same kind one would find analogous results
concerning the equations $t^2+bu^2=ps^2$, $t^2-au^2=ps^2$.
% U:p098.u08 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
Treating the first of these equations and then putting, to give
an example, $b=3$, one would obtain this particular theorem:
% U:p098.u09 | quoted-statement
\par\indent
“$p$ denoting a prime number $12n+1$, if one puts $p=t^2+3u^2$, $t$
will be an odd number. This being so, I say that 3 will or will not be a biquadratic
residue with respect to $p$, according as $t$ is of the form $12n\pm1$ or of
this one: $12n\pm5$.”
