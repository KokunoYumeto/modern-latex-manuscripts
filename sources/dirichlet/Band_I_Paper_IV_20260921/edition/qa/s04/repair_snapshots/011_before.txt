One also has:
\[
\leg{K}{b}=1,\quad\leg{L}{b}=1,
\]
$K$ and $L$ being divisors of $s^2\varphi'^2+bu'^2$. One concludes from this, by multiplying:
\[
\leg{EK}{b}=1,\quad\leg{FL}{b}=1,
\]
or, which is the same thing:
\[
\leg{t+\psi s}{b}=1,\quad\leg{t-\psi s}{b}=1.
\]
Observing that $b$ is a prime number $4n+3$, one may present
the last of these relations in this other manner:
\[
\leg{\psi s-t}{b}=-1.
\]
This being so, one sees that both can be comprised in this formula:
\begin{equation*}\tag{$\varepsilon'$}
\leg{\psi s\pm t}{b}=\pm1,
\end{equation*}
where the signs are at will, but the same in both members.

Since one has $\leg{p}{b}=1$, the congruence $\chi^2\equiv p\md{b}$ is possible and
has, as one knows, two roots between the limits $-\frac12b$ and $\frac12b$. Let us denote by
$\chi$ one of these roots taken at random. The equation $(\alpha')$ gives this congruence:
$t^2\equiv ps^2\md{b}$. Comparing this with the preceding one, one has $t^2\equiv\chi^2s^2$
$(\mathrm{mod}.b)$. One concludes from this that $\pm t\equiv\chi s\md{b}$, the sign being sometimes positive,
sometimes negative and depending on the choice made between the two roots
of the congruence $\chi^2\equiv p\md{b}$. The signs being at will in the formula
$(\varepsilon')$, one may suppose that they are the same there as in this one: $\pm t\equiv\chi s$
$(\mathrm{mod}.b)$. Replacing $\pm t$ by the number $\chi s$, which differs from $\pm t$ only by a
multiple of $b$, one obtains:
\[
\leg{\psi s+\chi s}{b}=\pm1.
\]
