This last congruence shows that $-b$ is or is not a biquadratic residue
with respect to $p$, according as one has $\leg{t}{p}=1$, or $\leg{t}{p}=-1$. Comparing
this result with the formula $(\gamma')$, one obtains this statement:

\begin{equation*}\tag{$\delta'$}
\begin{minipage}{0.83\textwidth}
$-b$ is or is not a biquadratic residue with respect to $p$, according
as one has $\leg{t}{b}=1$ or $\leg{t}{b}=-1$.
\end{minipage}
\end{equation*}

\par\indent
Let us now decompose the prime number $p$ into two squares, putting
$p=\varphi^2+\psi^2$ (where $\psi$ is supposed even) and substitute this value of $p$ in
the equation $(\alpha')$. One thus obtains:
\[
t^2-bu^2=s^2\varphi^2+s^2\psi^2,
\]
and one will then have, on transposing:
\[
t^2-\psi^2s^2=(t+\psi s)(t-\psi s)=s^2\varphi^2+bu^2.
\]
% U:p088.u04 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
The numbers $s$, $t$, $u$ being relatively prime two at a time, one sees
from the equation $(\alpha')$ that $s$ cannot be divisible by $b$. We shall now
distinguish two cases according as $\varphi$ is or is not divisible by $b$, and we
shall first examine the latter of these two cases. If we denote by
$m$ the greatest common divisor of $\varphi$ and $u$, $m$ will be odd and not divisible
by $b$, like $\varphi$. Let us put $\varphi=m\varphi'$, $u=mu'$ and substitute these values in
the last equation. One thus obtains:
\[
(t+\psi s)(t-\psi s)=m^2(s^2\varphi'^2+bu'^2).
\]
Since $s\varphi'$ and $bu'$ are relatively prime, by what precedes, it follows
from a known theorem, a very simple consequence of the law of reciprocity (\emph{Theory
of Numbers} no. 197), that every divisor $R$ of the binomial factor of the second member,
which is evidently odd, is such that $\leg{R}{b}=1$.
% U:p088.u05 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
The last equation shows that each of the numbers $t+\psi s$, $t-\psi s$
is composed of two factors, one a divisor of $m^2$, the other a divisor of $s^2\varphi'^2+bu'^2$.
If we denote these 4 factors by $E$, $F$, $K$ and $L$, we shall have the equations:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
t+\psi s=EK,&m^2=EF,\\
t-\psi s=FL,&\varphi'^2s^2+bu'^2=KL.
\end{array}
\]
