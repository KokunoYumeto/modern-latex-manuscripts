The conclusion being the same in the case of $u$ even and in that of $u$ odd,

% U:p097.u02 | quoted-statement
\par\indent
“If $a$ is a biquadratic residue with respect to $p$, one has $\leg{t}{a}=\leg{\psi s\pm t}{a}$;
if, on the contrary, $a$ is not a biquadratic residue with respect to $p$, one has
$\leg{t}{a}=-\leg{\psi s\pm t}{a}$, the double sign being at will in both cases.”
% U:p097.u03 | paragraph-start
\par\indent
Since one has $\leg{p}{a}=1$, the congruence $\chi^2\equiv p\md{a}$ will be possible and
will have, as one knows, two roots between the limits $-\frac12a$ and $\frac12a$. Let us suppose
that $\chi$ denotes either of these roots indifferently. One immediately obtains
from the equation $(\eta')$: $t^2\equiv ps^2\md{a}$, a congruence whose comparison
with the preceding one gives $t^2\equiv\chi^2s^2$, $\pm t\equiv\chi s\md{a}$, the upper sign holding
for one and the lower sign for the other of the two roots of the congruence
$\chi^2\equiv p\md{a}$. If $a$ is a biquadratic residue with respect to $p$, one
has, as was seen just a moment ago, $\leg{t}{a}=\leg{\psi s\pm t}{a}$. The double sign
being at will in this formula, we may suppose it equal to that which
occurs in the congruence $\pm t\equiv\chi s\md{a}$. Replacing $\pm t$ by
$\chi s$, one obtains:
\[
\leg{t}{a}=\leg{\psi s+\chi s}{a}=\leg{\psi+\chi}{a}\leg{s}{a}.
\]
% U:p097.u04 | continuation
On the other hand, one obtains from the congruence $\pm t\equiv\chi s\md{a}$, recalling
that $a$ is of the form $4n+1$: $\leg{t}{a}=\leg{\chi}{a}\leg{s}{a}$. Multiplying now,
member by member, this equation and the preceding one, one has $\leg{t^2}{a}=\leg{\chi}{a}\leg{\chi+\psi}{a}\leg{s^2}{a}$
or, which comes to the same thing: $\leg{\chi(\chi+\psi)}{a}=1$, a result which holds when $a$ is
a biquadratic residue with respect to $p$. In the same manner one would find
that, if $a$ is not a biquadratic residue with respect to $p$, one has $\leg{\chi(\chi+\psi)}{a}=-1$.
