\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Proof of Bernoulli's Theorem\par}
\vspace{0.8em}
\end{center}

Let $l$ denote the sum of the terms in the expansion of $(p+q)^n$ (where $p+q=1$), from the term containing $q^h$ up to the term containing $q^{h'}$. It is easy to see that
\begin{equation}
 l=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}[p+q e^{z\sqrt{-1}}]^n e^{-(h+h')\frac z2\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\sin (h'-h+1)\frac z2}{\sin\frac z2}\,dz .
\tag{1}
\end{equation}
This expression can be put into the following other form, by setting $h=b-c$, $h'=b+c$:
\begin{equation}
 l=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi [p^2+q^2+2pq\cos z]^{\frac n2}\cos(n\psi-bz)\frac{\sin(c+\frac12)z}{\sin\frac z2}\,dz,
\tag{2}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \psi=\arctg\frac{q\sin z}{p+q\cos z}.
\]
From this one obtains
\begin{equation}
 \psi=qz-Qz^3+\cdots,
\tag{3}
\end{equation}
writing, for shortness,
\[
 Q=\frac13q^3-\frac12q^2+\frac16q.
\]
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
\left\{\begin{aligned}
\log(p^2+q^2+2pq\cos z)^{\frac n2}
 &= -\frac n2pqz^2+n\left(\frac{pq}{24}-\frac{p^2q^2}{4}\right)z^4+\cdots,\\
(p^2+q^2+2pq\cos z)^{\frac n2}
 &= e^{-\frac n2pqz^2}\left(1+n\left(\frac{pq}{24}-\frac{p^2q^2}{4}\right)z^4+\cdots\right),
\end{aligned}\right.
\tag{4}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{\sin\frac z2}=\frac2z\left(1+\frac{z^2}{24}+\cdots\right),
\tag{5}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
 l=\frac2\pi\int dz\,e^{-\frac n2pqz^2}\frac{\sin(c+\frac12)z}{z}\left(1+\frac{z^2}{24}+nkz^4+\cdots\right)\cos((nq-b)z-nQz^3+\cdots).
\tag{6}
\end{equation}

Let $nq=b$ and $c+\frac12=\gamma\sqrt n$, $z=\varphi/\sqrt n$. Then
\begin{equation}
 l=\frac2\pi\int d\varphi\,e^{-\frac{pq}{2}\varphi^2}\frac{\sin\gamma\varphi}{\varphi}\left(1+\frac{\varphi^2}{24n}+k\frac{\varphi^4}{n}+\cdots\right)\left(1-n^2\frac{Q\varphi^6}{n^3}+\cdots\right),
\tag{7}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 l=\frac2\pi\int_0^\infty d\varphi\,e^{-\frac{pq}{2}\varphi^2}\frac{\sin\gamma\varphi}{\varphi}
 =\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^{\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{2pq}}}e^{-u^2}\,du.
\tag{8}
\end{equation}
The probability that, in $n$ simple events, the event $B$ (whose probability is $q$) lies between
\[
 qn\pm\gamma\sqrt n
\]
is therefore
\[
 \frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^{\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{2pq}}}e^{-u^2}\,du.
\]
Equivalently, if $\gamma/\sqrt{2pq}=\beta$, then
\[
 \frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\beta e^{-u^2}\,du
\]
is the probability that $Q/n$ lies between
\[
 q\pm\frac{\beta\sqrt{2pq}}{\sqrt n}.
\]

\begin{center}\large\bfseries Remarks on the Preceding Memoir\end{center}

1) Formula (1) can be verified as follows. Rewrite the value of $l$ as
\[
 l=\sum_{\alpha=0}^n \binom n\alpha p^{n-\alpha}q^\alpha\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}e^{\alpha zi}e^{-(h+h')\frac{zi}{2}}\frac{\sin(h'-h+1)\frac z2}{\sin\frac z2}\,dz.
\]
Set
\[
 J_\alpha=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}e^{\alpha zi-(h+h')\frac{zi}{2}}\frac{\sin(h'-h+1)\frac z2}{\sin\frac z2}\,dz.
\]
With $e^{zi}=u$ this becomes
\[
 J_\alpha=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int u^{\alpha-1-h'}(1+u+u^2+\cdots+u^{h'-h})\,du,
\]
where the integration is over the circumference of the unit circle surrounding $u=0$. Hence
\[
 J_\alpha=0\quad(\alpha<h\text{ or }\alpha>h'),\qquad
 J_\alpha=1\quad(h\le\alpha\le h').
\]
Thus indeed
\[
 l=\binom nh p^{n-h}q^h+\binom n{h+1}p^{n-h-1}q^{h+1}+\cdots+\binom n{h'}p^{n-h'}q^{h'}.
\]

2) Substituting $h=b-c$, $h'=b+c$ in (1) gives formula (2), after writing $P$ for the modulus of $p+qe^{zi}$ and $\psi$ for its argument.

3) For (6), expansion of the two factors gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 l&=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi dz\,e^{-\frac n2pqz^2}\frac{\sin(c+\frac12)z}{z}
 \left[1+n\left(\frac{pq}{24}-\frac{p^2q^2}{4}\right)z^4+\cdots\right]\\
&\qquad\times\left[1+\frac{z^2}{24}+\frac{7z^4}{5760}+\cdots\right]
 \cos((nq-b)z-nQz^3+\cdots),
\end{aligned}
\]
so that in order to obtain the formula stated in the text one must put
\[
 nk=n\left(\frac{pq}{24}-\frac{p^2q^2}{4}\right)+\frac7{5760}.
\]

4) In (7), $Q^2/2$ must be put in place of $Q$; that is, the final factor must read
\[
 \left(1-\frac{n^2Q^2\varphi^6}{2n^3}+\cdots\right).
\]

5) In (8) it is first assumed that $n$ is very large. The second equality follows from the known formula
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\cos 2\alpha x\,dx=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}e^{-\alpha^2},
\]
whence
\[
 \frac d{d\alpha}\int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\frac{\sin 2\alpha x}{x}\,dx=\sqrt\pi e^{-\alpha^2}.
\]
Since the integral vanishes for $\alpha=0$, one obtains
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-x^2}\frac{\sin2\alpha x}{x}\,dx=\sqrt\pi\int_0^\alpha e^{-u^2}\,du.
\]
Putting $x=\varphi\sqrt{pq/2}$ and $\alpha=\gamma/\sqrt{2pq}$ gives (8).

6) In the third-to-last line on p. 355 the letter $Q$ occurs in a sense different from that used in equations (3), (6), and (7): it is the number of occurrences of the event $B$ among the $n$ trials. Thus $l$ is the probability that
\[
 h=b-c\le Q\le b+c=h',
\]
or, after $b=nq$ and $c+1/2=\gamma\sqrt n$ have been chosen, that $Q$ lies between the limits
\[
 qn\pm(\gamma\sqrt n-\tfrac12),
\]
or, rounded,
\[
 qn\pm\gamma\sqrt n.
\]
For this reason, in the penultimate line of the text $\sqrt n$ belongs not in the numerator but in the denominator: $l$ is the probability that the ratio $Q/n$ lies between
\[
 q\pm\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt n}=q\pm\frac{\beta\sqrt{2pq}}{\sqrt n}.
\]
