\subsection*{13.5. The relatively algebraic situation}

We now suppose that the analytic group $A$ comes from a relative group scheme over $S$, smooth and separated, whose fibres are extensions of abelian schemes by tori.  We denote this relative scheme again by $A$.  This abuse of notation is justified by the fact that the functor
\[
        G\longmapsto G^{\an}
\]
from relative group schemes of this type to analytic groups over $S$ is fully faithful.  This is a relative GAGA-type assertion.  For the notion of a relative scheme, and for the relation between relative schemes over $S$ and analytic spaces over $S$, the references are Hakim's book and the corresponding earlier expos\'es on relative analytic geometry.

Let $O$ be the local ring of $S$ at $s$.  Near $s$, the relative scheme $A$ is determined by the ordinary scheme $A_O$ over $\Spec O$ obtained by localizing $A$ at $s$.  The formal completion of $A_O$ along its special fibre, which is isomorphic to $A_s=A_0$, is the same as the formal completion of the analytic group $A$ along $A_s$, after identifying
\[
        A_n=A\times_S S_n
\]
with algebraic schemes over $S_n$.  In the favourable case where this formal group scheme, viewed over the completed local ring $\widehat O$, is algebraizable, one obtains a group scheme over $\Spec\widehat O$, extension of an abelian scheme $B_{\widehat O}$ by a torus.  To simplify the statement, assume that $B_{\widehat O}$ is projective over $\widehat O$; this is the case, for instance, when $S$ is normal at $s$.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 13.5.1}
Under the preceding hypotheses, the analytic group $A^{\natural}$ of (13.3.3) comes, in a neighbourhood of $s$, from a relative group scheme determined up to a unique isomorphism.  This group scheme is an extension of a projective relative abelian scheme over $S$ by a torus.  If $A^{\natural}_{\widehat O}$ denotes the group scheme over $\widehat O=\widehat O_{S,s}$ that it defines, then $A^{\natural}_{\widehat O}$, with its extension structure, is canonically isomorphic to the Raynaud extension introduced above.
\end{statement}

The last assertion follows at once from the first and from 7.2.1, since $A^{\natural}_{\widehat O}$ has, along its special fibre, the formal completion prescribed by (13.3.6).  To prove algebraicity of $A^{\natural}$ near $s$, one proceeds as in 7.2.1 and is reduced immediately to proving algebraicity of the abelian quotient $B$ of $A^{\natural}$.

\begin{statement}{Lemma 13.5.2}
Let $B$ be an analytic abeloid group over the analytic space $S$.  Let $s\in S$ be a point such that the formal completion $\widehat B$ along the fibre $B_s$ is algebraizable.  Thus the spaces $B_n=B\times_S S_n$ are algebraizable, and $\widehat B$, viewed as a formal scheme over $\widehat O_{S,s}$, comes from an abelian scheme $B_{\widehat O}$ over $\widehat O$.  Suppose moreover that $B_{\widehat O}$ is projective over $\widehat O$.  Then $B$ is projective over $S$ after replacing $S$ by a neighbourhood of $s$.
\end{statement}

The hypothesis means that the formal analytic space $\widehat B$ has a polarization, that is, a compatible system of polarizations $p_n$ on the $B_n$.  The conclusion means that there is a polarization $p$ of $B$ over a neighbourhood of $s$.  Let $P$ be the analytic space over $S$ of relative polarizations of $B$.  It represents the functor which assigns to $S'\to S$ the set of polarizations of $B_{S'}$ over $S'$.  This is an open subfunctor of the relative N\'eron-Severi functor $\mathrm{NS}_{B/S}$.  Standard arguments show that $\mathrm{NS}_{B/S}$, hence also $P$, is represented by an unramified analytic space over $S$.  The formal polarization gives a formal section of $P$ at $s$.  Since $P$ is unramified and locally quasi-finite over $S$, a formal section is induced by an analytic section over a neighbourhood of $s$.  This analytic section is the desired polarization of $B$.

\begin{remarktext}{Remarks 13.5.3}
(a)  If the projectivity hypothesis on $B_{\widehat O}$ is omitted, one can still conclude that $B$ is algebraic near $s$.  One uses an unpublished theorem, independently due to Artin and Deligne, saying that if $X$ is proper over $S$ and if the formal completion of $X$ along $X_s$ is algebraizable, then $X$ itself is algebraizable.  This reduces matters to the case where $S$ is reduced.  When $S$ is normal, an abelian scheme over $\widehat O$ is projective, and Lemma 13.5.2 applies.  The general reduced case is obtained by non-flat descent from the normalization of $S$, using the fact that a morphism for which $\cO_S\to f_*\cO_{S'}$ is injective is an effective descent morphism for relative abelian schemes.

(b)  The same method should also prove the analogous result for any analytic space $B$ proper over $S$.  The more delicate question is whether an analytic space $X$ proper over $S$, whose formal completion along $X_s$ is algebraizable, is itself algebraizable near $s$.  Here algebraizability must be understood in a wider sense than in Hakim's book: near $s$, the space should come from an algebraic space over the local base, in Artin's sense.
\end{remarktext}

Still under the hypotheses of Proposition 13.5.1, choose for every $n>0$ the standard isomorphism
\[
        \mu_n\simeq(\Z/n\Z)_S,
        \qquad m\longmapsto\exp(2\pi i m/n),
\]
and, by passing to inverse limits over powers of a fixed prime $\ell$, identify the usual Tate twist with the constant $\Z_\ell$-system.  Applying the exact sequence of $\Ext^1(\Z/n\Z,-)$ to the exact sequence (13.1.3) gives the corresponding canonical projective systems.  In the present algebraic situation the finite sheaves that occur are represented by relative finite group schemes, and the analytic symbols used earlier therefore acquire an algebraic meaning.

The resulting identifications are compatible with fixed parts and toric parts.  If $A'$ satisfies the same hypotheses and if $W$ is a biextension of $(A,A')$ by $\Gm$, defining a duality over the open set where $A$ and $A'$ are abelian schemes, then the induced pairings on Tate modules agree with the algebraic pairings.  After shrinking around $s$, the $\ell$-adic pairings glue to an integral pairing
\begin{equation}\tag{13.5.11}
        R\times R'\longrightarrow \Z .
\end{equation}
The algebraic orthogonality theorem can then be applied to $R$.  The pairing (13.5.11) is zero on the fixed and toric products prescribed by the algebraic theory.  If $S$ is nonsingular of dimension one and if, over $U=S-\{s\}$, the restrictions of $A$ and $A'$ are dual abelian schemes, the fixed and toric subobjects are mutual annihilators near $s$.  Since (13.5.11) is a perfect duality over $U$, it follows that $R/R^t$ is locally constant and that the local monodromy action on $R$ is unipotent of exponent at most two.  One also obtains a perfect duality between $R/R^f$ and the dual toric lattice.  Under this identification the homomorphism of (13.3.4) may be written
\begin{equation}\tag{13.5.13}
        i:M\longrightarrow A^{\natural}.
\end{equation}
This is the form in which it will reappear below.

\section*{14. Links with transcendental theory: the rigid-analytic case}
\subsection*{14.1. The canonical homomorphism $M_K\to G_K$}

We assume the foundations of rigid-analytic spaces.  Let $S$ be a complete trait and let $A_K$ be an abelian scheme over its fraction field $K$.  Section 8.1 constructs, by descent from the semi-stable case and from the Raynaud extension, an extension
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.1}
        0\longrightarrow T_K\longrightarrow G_K\longrightarrow B_K\longrightarrow0
\end{equation}
of an abelian scheme $B_K$ by a torus $T_K$.  For the dual abelian scheme $A'_K$ one has similarly
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.2}
        0\longrightarrow T'_K\longrightarrow G'_K\longrightarrow B'_K\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where $B'_K$ is dual to $B_K$.  Let
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.3}
        M_K=D(T'_K),\qquad M'_K=D(T_K)
\end{equation}
be the character lattices.  By Expos\'e VIII, the two extension structures are equivalently described by homomorphisms
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.4}
        M_K\xrightarrow{p_K}B'_K,
        \qquad
        M'_K\xrightarrow{p'_K}B_K.
\end{equation}
Let $W_K$ be the biextension of $(B_K,B'_K)$ by $\Gm$ that gives the canonical duality, and let $E_K$ be its inverse image on $(M_K,M'_K)$.  A trivialization of $E_K$ is canonically the same as a homomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.5}
        i:M_K\longrightarrow G_K
\end{equation}
lifting $p_K$, or, symmetrically, as a homomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.6}
        i':M'_K\longrightarrow G'_K
\end{equation}
lifting $p'_K$.

Writing a superscript $\an$ for the associated rigid-analytic groups, Raynaud defines a canonical exact sequence
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.7}
        0\longrightarrow M_K^{\an}
        \xrightarrow{i^{\an}}G_K^{\an}
        \xrightarrow{e}A_K^{\an}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The homomorphism $i^{\an}$ can also be read as an ordinary homomorphism $i:M_K\to G_K$, lifting $p_K$.  The sequence is the rigid-analytic analogue of (13.3.4), functorial in $A_K$ and compatible with change of traits.

By this compatibility one reduces to the semi-stable case.  There the groups $T_K,G_K,B_K,M_K,
\ldots$ are generic fibres of group schemes $T,G,B,M,\ldots$ over $S$, and $G$ is an extension of $B$ by $T$.  Let $\widehat G$ be the formal completion of $G$ along the special fibre.  There is a canonical isomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.8}
        \widehat G\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat A^0,
\end{equation}
and, on rigid generic fibres,
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.9}
        \widehat G^{\an}\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat A^{0,\an}.
\end{equation}
There are also canonical open immersions
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.10}
        \widehat G^{\an}\hookrightarrow G_K^{\an},
        \qquad
        \widehat A^{0,\an}\hookrightarrow A_K^{\an}.
\end{equation}
The map $e$ is characterized uniquely by the commutative square
\begin{equation}\tag{14.1.11}
\begin{tikzcd}
\widehat G^{\an}\arrow[r]\arrow[d]&\widehat A^{0,\an}\arrow[d]\\
G_K^{\an}\arrow[r,"e"']&A_K^{\an},
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
where the unnamed arrows are those of (14.1.9) and (14.1.10).

\begin{remarktext}{Remarks 14.1.12}
(a)  The uniqueness of $e$ is immediate from connectedness of $\widehat G^{\an}$; existence is the non-trivial part and is supplied by Raynaud's rigid-analytic theory.  The definition of $i^{\an}$ is likewise transcendental, although once $G$ is constructed by formal geometry the resulting $i$ is an algebraic datum of the form (14.1.5).  At this stage no purely algebraic-geometric description is known.  The arithmetic characterization below is obtained by reduction to a situation of finite type over $\Z$.

(b)  The homomorphism $i$ is canonically associated with a determined trivialization of $E_K$, or equivalently with the dual homomorphism $i'$.
\end{remarktext}

\subsection*{14.2. Relation with monodromy}

Let $n>0$.  Applying the exact sequence of $\Ext^1(\Z/n\Z,-)$ to (14.1.7) on the rigid-analytic site over $K$, and using that $M_K^{\an}$ is torsion-free and $G_K^{\an}$ is $n$-divisible, gives an exact sequence of finite group schemes
\begin{equation}\tag{14.2.1}
        0\longrightarrow G_K[n]\longrightarrow A_K[n]
        \longrightarrow M_K/nM_K\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Passing to inverse limits over powers of a prime $\ell$ yields
\begin{equation}\tag{14.2.2}
        0\longrightarrow T_\ell(G_K)\longrightarrow T_\ell(A_K)
        \longrightarrow M_K\otimes\Z_\ell\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The purely algebraic formulas of 8.1.2 and 8.1.7 give the same exact sequence:
\begin{equation}\tag{14.2.3}
        0\longrightarrow T_\ell(G_K)\longrightarrow T_\ell(A_K)
        \longrightarrow T_\ell(A_K)/T_\ell(A_K)^f
        \simeq M_K\otimes\Z_\ell\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
The compatibility follows from (14.1.11).  This compatibility, for a fixed $\ell$, already characterizes $i$ transcendently: the map $e$ identifies the quotients $T_\ell(A_K)/T_\ell(A_K)^f$ with the $\ell$-adic completions of a single lattice $\Ker e$, compatibly with the integral structure coming from the algebraic theory.  Thus
\begin{equation}\tag{14.2.3 bis}
        i:M_K\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ker e.
\end{equation}

The same homomorphism reconstructs the monodromy pairing
\begin{equation}\tag{14.2.4}
        u:M\times M'\longrightarrow\Z.
\end{equation}
In the semi-stable case, assume for simplicity that $M$ and $M'$ are constant lattices.  Pulling the Poincar\'e biextension on the abelian quotients back by the two homomorphisms from $M$ and $M'$ gives a biextension $E$ of $(M,M')$ by $\Gm$.  For $(m,m')\in M\times M'$, the fibre $E_{m,m'}$ corresponds to an invertible $V$-module.  Choosing a basis defines, independently of the choice, a valuation map
\[
        v:E_{m,m'}(K)\longrightarrow\Z.
\]
A trivialization $\varphi$ of $E$ gives
\begin{equation}\tag{14.2.5}
        (m,m')\longmapsto v(\varphi(m,m')),
\end{equation}
and the monodromy homomorphism is this pairing for the splitting attached to $i$.

\begin{remarktext}{Remarks 14.2.6}
Thus the rigid-analytic theory recovers integrality and independence of $\ell$ of the monodromy pairing.  Raynaud's theory also recovers its positivity.
\end{remarktext}

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 14.2.7}
Regard $M\otimes\Q$ as an extension of $(M\otimes\Q)/M$ by $M$.  Using $i:M\to G_K$ gives
\begin{equation}\tag{14.2.8}
        0\longrightarrow G_K\longrightarrow G_K^\natural
        \longrightarrow (M\otimes\Q)/M\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Since $G_K$ is divisible, this is equivalent to the family of extensions (14.2.2).  It is therefore defined by algebraic data and is canonically the group introduced in (2.2.3), after identifying its component group with $(M\otimes\Q)/M$.
\end{remarktext}

\subsection*{14.3. An arithmetic characterization of $i$}

For every prime $\ell$, the extension (14.2.2) is known algebraically.  Hence one knows the image of $i$ under
\begin{equation}\tag{14.3.1}
        \Hom(M_K,G_K)\longrightarrow
        \prod_\ell \Ext^1(M_K\otimes\Z_\ell,T_\ell(G_K)).
\end{equation}
When $M_K$ is constant with value $M$, this is the homomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{14.3.2}
        \Hom(M,G_K(K))\longrightarrow
        \prod_\ell \Hom(M,H^1(K,T_\ell(G_K)))
\end{equation}
obtained from the Kummer boundary maps
\begin{equation}\tag{14.3.3}
        G_K(K)\longrightarrow\prod_\ell H^1(K,T_\ell(G_K)).
\end{equation}
The kernel consists of those elements of $G_K(K)$ divisible by every integer $n>1$.

\begin{statement}{Lemma 14.3.4}
Let $V$ be a discrete valuation ring with residue field $k$ of characteristic $p>0$, of finite type over the prime field.  Let $G_K$ be a finite type group scheme over $K$, with zero unipotent rank if $\car K=0$.  Then the subgroup of infinitely divisible elements of $G_K(K)$ is zero.  Equivalently, when $G_K$ is divisible, (14.3.3) is injective.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
After a finite extension of $K$ and normalization of $V$, reduce to the case where $G_K$ has a composition series with quotients among finite constant groups, abelian varieties, $\Gm$, and additive groups.  The finite constant and multiplicative cases are immediate.  For an abelian variety, pass to the N\'eron model $G$ and reduce to proving that the infinitely divisible subgroup of $G(S)$ is zero.  This is true on the special fibre by the usual d\'evissage and the Mordell-Weil theorem over a field of finite type.  The kernel of $G(S)\to G(k)$ is filtered by vector groups over $k$, killed by $p$ on successive quotients; intersection over all infinitesimal levels is zero.  The additive case is handled similarly.
\end{prooftext}

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 14.3.4.1}
It is essential to require divisibility by all integers, not only by powers of one prime.  For a smooth group over a henselian trait, elements in the kernel of reduction are infinitely $\ell$-divisible for every $\ell\ne p$.  The preceding argument shows that the infinitely $p$-divisible subgroup is finite, and the full intersection is zero.
\end{remarktext}

When $k$ has characteristic $p>0$ and is of finite type in the absolute sense, the homomorphism $i$ is therefore determined by its image under (14.3.1), equivalently by the family of extensions (14.2.2).  To obtain a general characterization, let $S=\Spec V$, with $V$ a normal noetherian ring, separated and complete for an adic topology.  Let $A$ be a smooth quasi-projective group scheme over $S$, with connected fibres, such that the special fibre is an extension of an abelian scheme by an isotrivial torus and such that $A_U$ is an abelian scheme over a non-empty open $U\subset S-S_0$.  Section 7 constructs a group scheme $G$ over $S$, an extension of an abelian scheme $B$ by a torus $T$, characterized by an isomorphism of formal completions
\begin{equation}\tag{14.3.5.1}
        \widehat G\xrightarrow{\sim}\widehat A^0.
\end{equation}
For every prime $\ell$ one obtains on $U$ an exact sequence
\begin{equation}\tag{14.3.5.3}
        0\longrightarrow T_\ell(G_U)\longrightarrow T_\ell(A_U)
        \longrightarrow M\otimes\Z_\ell\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
If $A'$ satisfies the same hypotheses and $W$ is a biextension of $(A,A')$ by $\Gm$, inducing a duality over $U$, the resulting pairings on Tate modules give the orthogonality theorem and hence the identification
\begin{equation}\tag{14.3.5.5}
        T_\ell(A_U)/T_\ell(A_U)^f\simeq M\otimes\Z_\ell.
\end{equation}
Thus one obtains the extension
\begin{equation}\tag{14.3.5.6}
        0\longrightarrow T_\ell(G_U)\longrightarrow T_\ell(A_U)
        \longrightarrow M\otimes\Z_\ell\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}

\begin{statement}{Conjecture 14.4}
In the situation described in 14.3.5, there exists a homomorphism
\begin{equation}\tag{14.4.1}
        i:M_U\longrightarrow G_U
\end{equation}
such that, for every prime $\ell$, the extension (14.3.5.6) is isomorphic to the extension deduced from $i$ as in (14.2.2).  More precisely, there is one and only one way to assign such an $i$ to every situation of the type of 14.3.5, compatibly with all base changes $S'\to S$ arising from continuous homomorphisms $V\to V'$ of noetherian adic rings.
\end{statement}

The existence of $i$, compatibly with base change, should follow from Raynaud's rigid-analytic theory in its general adic form.  For uniqueness, one reduces to the case where $S_0$ is of finite type over $\Z$.  Indeed, $V$ is a filtered inductive limit of normal subrings of finite type over $\Z$, and the situation descends, for large index, to such a subring or to its separated completion for the induced adic topology.  Since $S-S_0$ is Jacobson, two possible homomorphisms are equal if they agree at all closed points of $U$.  Normalizing the closure of such a point gives a complete trait with finite residue field, and the uniqueness result of the preceding one-dimensional case applies.  This proves uniqueness, and then base-change compatibility follows from uniqueness, assuming existence.

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 14.5}
It remains to formulate and prove the expected compatibility between the rigid-analytic homomorphism $i$ and the analogous complex-analytic homomorphism (13.5.13), or better, to find a common generalization of both constructions.
\end{remarktext}
