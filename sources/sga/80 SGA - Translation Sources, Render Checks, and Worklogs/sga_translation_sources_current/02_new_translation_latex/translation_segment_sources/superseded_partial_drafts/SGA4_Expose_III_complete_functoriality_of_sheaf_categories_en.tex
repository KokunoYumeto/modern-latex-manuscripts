In Expos\'e~I, Section~5, the behavior of categories of presheaves with respect to functors between the categories of arguments was studied. In this expos\'e that study is extended to the case of sites and categories of sheaves (Sections~1 and~2). After introducing the induced topology (Section~3), we turn in Section~4 to the comparison lemma, which will play an important role in Expos\'e~IV. In Section~5, for the patient reader, certain commutative diagrams connected with localized categories of the type \(C/X\) are studied. In Theorem~5.1 of Expos\'e~I one imposes smallness hypotheses on the categories under consideration. These hypotheses are not, in general, satisfied by the sites occurring in practice (\(\U\)-sites). This forces some contortions in 4.2, 4.3, and 4.4.

\section{Continuous functors}

\begin{statement}{Definition 1.1}
Let \(C\) and \(C'\) be two \(\U\)-sites, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between the underlying categories. We say that \(u\) is \emph{continuous} if, for every sheaf of sets \(F\) on \(C'\), the presheaf
\[
X\longmapsto F\circ u(X)
\]
on \(C\) is a sheaf.
\end{statement}

This notion of continuity depends \emph{a priori} on the universe \(\U\) for which the two sites are \(\U\)-sites. Proposition~1.5 shows that in fact it does not.

\subsection*{1.1.1}
Let
\[
i:\widetilde C\longrightarrow \widehat C,
\qquad
i':\widetilde{C'}\longrightarrow \widehat{C'}
\]
denote the canonical inclusion functors of sheaves into presheaves. By Definition~1.1, the functor \(u\) is continuous if and only if there exists a functor
\[
u_s:\widetilde{C'}\longrightarrow \widetilde C
\]
such that
\[
i u_s = u^* i',\qquad u^*F=F\circ u
\]
(Expos\'e~I, 5.0).

\begin{statement}{Proposition 1.2}
Let \(C\) be a small site, let \(C'\) be a \(\U\)-site, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between the underlying categories. The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The functor \(u\) is continuous.

\item For every object \(X\) of \(C\) and every covering sieve \(R\hookrightarrow X\), the morphism
\[
u_!R\hookrightarrow u(X)
\]
is bicovering in \(\widehat{C'}\) (Expos\'e~II, 5.2 and Expos\'e~I, 5.1).\footnote{``Covering'' in place of ``bicovering'' is not necessarily sufficient; see Example~1.9.3 below.}

\item For every bicovering family \(H_i\to K\), \(i\in I\), in \(\widehat C\), the family
\[
u_!H_i\longrightarrow u_!K,
\qquad i\in I,
\]
is bicovering in \(\widehat{C'}\).

\item There exists a functor
\[
u^s:\widetilde C\longrightarrow \widetilde{C'}
\]
commuting with inductive limits and ``extending \(u\)'', i.e. such that the diagram of canonical functors
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
C & \xrightarrow{u} & C'\\
\downarrow\epsilon_C && \downarrow\epsilon_{C'}\\
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u^s} & \widetilde{C'}
\end{array}
\]
is commutative up to isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, when \(C\) is no longer assumed to be a small site, but is a \(\U\)-site, one still has \(\mathrm{i})\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{iv})\), and the functor \(u^s\) of \(\mathrm{iv})\) is necessarily left adjoint to
\[
u_s:\widetilde{C'}\longrightarrow\widetilde C;
\]
in particular, it is determined up to unique isomorphism.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The proof of \(\mathrm{i})\Leftrightarrow\mathrm{iv})\) when \(C\) is a \(\U\)-site will be given in 4.2. Suppose first that \(C\) is small. It is clear that \(\mathrm{iii})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\).

\emph{\(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{iii})\).} Let \(H_i\to K\), \(i\in I\), be a bicovering family in \(\widehat C\). For every sheaf \(F\) on \(C'\), the presheaf \(u^*F\) is a sheaf on \(C\). Hence, by Expos\'e~II, 5.3, the canonical map
\[
\Hom(K,u^*F)\longrightarrow\prod_i\Hom(H_i,u^*F)
\]
is bijective. By adjunction (Expos\'e~I, 5.1), it follows that
\[
\Hom(u_!K,F)\longrightarrow\prod_i\Hom(u_!H_i,F)
\]
is bijective. Thus, again by Expos\'e~II, 5.3, the family \(u_!H_i\to u_!K\), \(i\in I\), is bicovering.

\emph{\(\mathrm{ii})\Rightarrow\mathrm{i})\).} Let \(X\) be an object of \(C\), let \(R\hookrightarrow X\) be a covering sieve, and let \(F\) be a sheaf on \(C'\). By Expos\'e~II, 5.3, the map
\[
\Hom(u_!X,F)\longrightarrow\Hom(u_!R,F)
\]
is bijective. By adjunction, it follows that
\[
\Hom(X,u^*F)\longrightarrow\Hom(R,u^*F)
\]
is bijective. Hence \(u^*F\) is a sheaf.

\emph{\(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{iv})\).} Use the usual notation \(\underline a\) and \(i\) (respectively \(\underline a'\) and \(i'\)) for the associated-sheaf functor and the inclusion into presheaves. For a sheaf \(G\) on \(C\), put
\[
u^sG=\underline a' u_! iG.
\]
For every sheaf \(F\) on \(C'\), one has natural isomorphisms
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Hom(G,u_sF)
&\simeq \Hom(iG,iu_sF)
 \simeq \Hom(iG,u^*i'F) \\
&\simeq \Hom(u_!iG,i'F)
 \simeq \Hom(\underline a'u_!iG,F).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first isomorphism comes from the full faithfulness of \(i\); the second from the definition of \(u_s\); the third and fourth from adjunction. Thus \(u^s\) is left adjoint to \(u_s\). In particular, \(u^s\) commutes with inductive limits.

For every presheaf \(K\) on \(C\) and every sheaf \(F\) on \(C'\), the same adjunctions give natural isomorphisms
\[
\Hom(u^s\underline aK,F)
\simeq \Hom(\underline aK,u_sF)
\simeq \Hom(K,iu_sF)
\simeq \Hom(K,u^*i'F)
\simeq \Hom(u_!K,i'F)
\simeq \Hom(\underline a'u_!K,F).
\]
Hence there is a functorial isomorphism
\[
u^s\underline aK\simeq \underline a'u_!K.
\]
Taking \(K\) representable, and using Expos\'e~I, 5.4(3), one obtains a diagram commutative up to isomorphism:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
C & \xrightarrow{u} & C'\\
\downarrow\epsilon_C && \downarrow\epsilon_{C'}\\
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u^s} & \widetilde{C'}.
\end{array}
\]

\emph{\(\mathrm{iv})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\).} Let \(h:C\to\widehat C\) and \(h':C'\to\widehat{C'}\) be the Yoneda functors. Consider the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
C & \xrightarrow{u} & C'\\
\downarrow h && \downarrow h'\\
\widehat C & \xrightarrow{u_!} & \widehat{C'}\\
\downarrow\underline a && \downarrow\underline a'\\
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u^s} & \widetilde{C'}.
\end{array}
\]
The upper square is commutative (Remark~1.4(3)), and \(\underline ah=\epsilon_C\), \(\underline a'h'=\epsilon_{C'}\). For every object \(K\) of \(\widehat C\), one has the canonical isomorphism
\[
\varinjlim_{(h(X)\to K)\in\Ob(C/K)}h(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}K
\]
(Expos\'e~I, 3.4). Since \(u_!\), \(u^s\), \(\underline a\), and \(\underline a'\) commute with inductive limits, it follows that
\[
\varinjlim_{(h(X)\to K)} u^s\underline a h(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}u^s\underline aK,
\qquad
\varinjlim_{(h(X)\to K)}\underline a'u_!h(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline a'u_!K.
\]
Since \(u_!h=h'u\), and by hypothesis \(\underline a'h'u\simeq u^s\underline a h\), one obtains a functorial isomorphism
\[
u^s\underline aK\simeq \underline a'u_!K.
\]
The diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widehat C & \xrightarrow{u_!} & \widehat{C'}\\
\downarrow\underline a && \downarrow\underline a'\\
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u^s} & \widetilde{C'}
\end{array}
\]
is therefore commutative up to isomorphism. Since \(\underline ai\) is isomorphic to the identity functor, one has \(u^s\simeq\underline a'u_!i\), giving uniqueness of \(u^s\). If \(v:H\to K\) is a bicovering morphism in \(\widehat C\), then \(\underline a(v)\) is an isomorphism (Expos\'e~II, 5.3), and hence \(\underline a'u_!(v)\), isomorphic to \(u^s\underline a(v)\), is an isomorphism. Thus \(u_!(v)\) is transformed by \(\underline a'\) into an isomorphism, and consequently \(u_!(v)\) is bicovering. This proves \(\mathrm{ii})\). The additional assertion in the small case was proved along the way. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\subsubsection*{1.2.1}
The functor \(u^s:\widetilde C\to\widetilde{C'}\) of Proposition~1.2(iv) can often be interpreted as an ``inverse-image'' functor for a ``morphism of topoi'' \(\widetilde{C'}\to\widetilde C\); cf. Expos\'e~IV, 4.9. Its properties are summarized in the following proposition.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 1.3}
Let \(u:C\to C'\) be a continuous functor from a small site \(C\) to a \(\U\)-site \(C'\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item The functor \(u^s\) is left adjoint to \(u_s\).
\item There is a canonical isomorphism \(u^s\simeq\underline a'u_!i\).
\item There is a canonical isomorphism \(u^s\underline a\simeq\underline a'u_!\).
\item The functor \(u^s\) commutes with inductive limits.
\item When \(u_!\) is left exact (cf. Expos\'e~I, 5.4(4)), \(u^s\) is left exact as well. More generally, \(u^s\) commutes with the same finite projective-limit types with which \(u_!\) commutes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
Assertions (1)--(4) were proved during the proof of Proposition~1.2. Assertion (5) follows from the isomorphism in (2), since \(i\) commutes with projective limits and \(\underline a'\) commutes with finite projective limits (Expos\'e~II, 4.1). \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 1.4}
Combining Expos\'e~I, 5.4(3), with Proposition~1.3(3), one obtains a diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
C & \xrightarrow{u} & C'\\
\downarrow h && \downarrow h'\\
\widehat C & \xrightarrow{u_!} & \widehat{C'}\\
\downarrow\underline a && \downarrow\underline a'\\
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u^s} & \widetilde{C'}
\end{array}
\]
whose upper square is commutative and whose lower square is commutative up to isomorphism. But the functors \(\underline a\), \(\underline a'\), \(u_!\), and \(u^s\) are defined only up to isomorphism. The reader may check that the functors \(\underline a\), \(\underline a'\), and \(u^s\) can be chosen so that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item the composite functors \(\underline ah\) and \(\underline a'h'\) are injective on sets of objects;
\item the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
C & \xrightarrow{u} & C'\\
\downarrow\underline ah && \downarrow\underline a'h'\\
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u^s} & \widetilde{C'}
\end{array}
\]
is commutative.
\end{enumerate}
More precisely, one may first choose \(\underline a\) and \(\underline a'\) so as to satisfy (1). Once these have been chosen, one may choose \(u^s\) so as to satisfy (2).
\end{remarktext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 1.5}
Let \(\U\subset\V\) be two universes, let \(C\) and \(C'\) be two \(\U\)-sites, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between the underlying categories. Then \(u\) is continuous relative to \(\U\) if and only if it is continuous relative to \(\V\). Moreover, when \(u\) is continuous, let \(u^s_{\U}\) (respectively \(u^s_{\V}\)) denote the functor between categories of \(\U\)-sheaves (respectively \(\V\)-sheaves) introduced in Proposition~1.2(iv). Then the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde C_{\U} & \xrightarrow{u^s_{\U}} & \widetilde{C'}_{\U}\\
\downarrow && \downarrow\\
\widetilde C_{\V} & \xrightarrow{u^s_{\V}} & \widetilde{C'}_{\V}
\end{array}
\tag{1.5.1}
\]
where the vertical functors are the canonical inclusions, is commutative up to canonical isomorphism.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
We give the proof only when \(C\) is \(\U\)-small. The general case will be proved in 4.3. It is clear that if \(u^*\) sends every \(\V\)-sheaf on \(C'\) to a \(\V\)-sheaf on \(C\), then it sends every \(\U\)-sheaf on \(C'\) to a \(\U\)-sheaf on \(C\). Conversely, suppose \(u^*\) sends every \(\U\)-sheaf on \(C'\) to a \(\U\)-sheaf on \(C\). Write
\[
\widehat C_{\U}\hookrightarrow\widehat C_{\V},
\qquad
\widehat{C'}_{\U}\hookrightarrow\widehat{C'}_{\V}
\]
for the categories of \(\U\)-presheaves and \(\V\)-presheaves, and write \(u_{\U!}\) and \(u_{\V!}\) for the corresponding left Kan extension functors. By the explicit construction of \(u_!\) in Expos\'e~I, 5.1, one has a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widehat C_{\U} & \xrightarrow{u_{\U!}} & \widehat{C'}_{\U}\\
\downarrow && \downarrow\\
\widehat C_{\V} & \xrightarrow{u_{\V!}} & \widehat{C'}_{\V}.
\end{array}
\tag{*}
\]
The inclusion functors from \(\U\)-presheaves to \(\V\)-presheaves are fully faithful and commute with projective limits. Hence, by Expos\'e~II, 5.1(i), a bicovering morphism of \(\U\)-presheaves is a bicovering morphism of \(\V\)-presheaves. Now let \(R\hookrightarrow X\) be a covering sieve of an object \(X\) of \(C\). By Proposition~1.2(ii), \(u_{\U!}R\to u_{\U!}X\) is bicovering. Using \((*)\) and the preceding observation, we deduce that \(u_{\V!}R\to u_{\V!}X\) is bicovering. Hence, by Proposition~1.2, \(u^*\) sends \(\V\)-sheaves on \(C'\) to \(\V\)-sheaves on \(C\). The commutativity of (1.5.1) follows from the commutativity of \((*)\), Expos\'e~II, 3.6, and Proposition~1.3(2). \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 1.6}
Let \(C\) and \(C'\) be two \(\U\)-sites, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between the underlying categories. Consider the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item \(u\) is continuous (cf. Definition~1.1 and Proposition~1.2).
\item For every covering family \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) in \(C\), the family \((uX_i\to uX)_{i\in I}\) in \(C'\) is covering.
\end{enumerate}
Then \(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\).

Suppose that the topology of \(C\) can be defined by a pretopology \(T\) (Expos\'e~II, 1.3) and that \(u\) commutes with fiber products.\footnote{In fact it is enough that \(u\) commute with the fiber products occurring in base change for morphisms coming from covering families for \(T\).} Then \(\mathrm{i})\) and \(\mathrm{ii})\) are equivalent, and are also equivalent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*),resume]
\item The functor \(u\) sends covering families for \(T\) to covering families in \(C'\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, suppose that in \(C\) fiber products are representable and that \(u\) commutes with fiber products. Then conditions \(\mathrm{i})\) and \(\mathrm{ii})\) are equivalent.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
First prove \(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\). Let \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) be a covering family in \(C\). For every sheaf \(F\) on \(C'\), \(u^*F\) is a sheaf. Hence
\[
\Hom(X,u^*F)\longrightarrow\prod_i\Hom(X_i,u^*F)
\]
is injective (Expos\'e~II, 5.1). Therefore
\[
\Hom(u_!X,F)\longrightarrow\prod_i\Hom(u_!X_i,F)
\]
is injective. Thus the family \(u_!X_i\to u_!X\), \(i\in I\), is covering in \(C'\).

Every covering family for the pretopology \(T\) is a covering family in \(C\), so \(\mathrm{ii})\Rightarrow\mathrm{iii})\). We prove \(\mathrm{iii})\Rightarrow\mathrm{i})\). Let \(X\) be an object of \(C\), and let \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) be a covering family in \(\Cov(X)\) (Expos\'e~II, 1.3). The morphisms \(X_i\to X\) are base-changeable and \(u\) commutes with the relevant fiber products. Hence the fiber products \(u(X_i)\times_{u(X)}u(X_j)\) are representable and canonically isomorphic to \(u(X_i\times_XX_j)\). Let \(F\) be a sheaf on \(C'\). Then the diagram of sets
\[
F(u(X))\longrightarrow\prod_iF(u(X_i))\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}F(u(X_i\times_XX_j))
\]
is exact (Expos\'e~II, 2.1; Expos\'e~I, 3.5 and 2.12). Therefore
\[
u^*F(X)\longrightarrow\prod_i u^*F(X_i)\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}u^*F(X_i\times_XX_j)
\]
is exact. Hence \(u^*F\) is a sheaf (Expos\'e~II, 2.4), proving \(\mathrm{i})\). The final assertion follows from the preceding argument and from the fact that, when fiber products are representable in \(C\), all covering families of \(C\), indexed by sets in a suitable universe, define a pretopology whose associated topology is the topology of \(C\). \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 1.7}
Let \(C\) be a small site, let \(C'\) be a \(\U\)-site, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be continuous. Let \(\gamma\) be an algebraic structure defined by finite projective limits, such that in the category of \(\gamma\)-objects in \(\U\)-\(\Set\) the \(\U\)-inductive limits are representable.\footnote{In fact one can show that this condition is always satisfied.} We use the notation of Expos\'e~II, 6.4. The functor \(u_s\) commutes with projective limits and therefore defines a functor
\[
u_s^\gamma:\widetilde C_\gamma\longrightarrow \widetilde{C'}_\gamma.
\]
The functor \(u_s^\gamma\) has a left adjoint \(u^s_\gamma\), with the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item There is a canonical isomorphism
\[
u^s_\gamma\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline a'_\gamma u_{\gamma!}i_\gamma,
\]
and \(u^s_\gamma\) commutes with the finite projective limits with which \(u_{\gamma!}\) commutes.
\item There is a canonical isomorphism
\[
u^s_\gamma\underline a_\gamma\xrightarrow{\sim}\underline a'_\gamma u'_{\gamma!}.
\]
\item The functor \(u^s_\gamma\) commutes with inductive limits.
\item If \(u^s\) is left exact, the natural diagram comparing \(\gamma\)-objects with the underlying sheaves of sets,
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde C_\gamma & \xrightarrow{u^s_\gamma} & \widetilde{C'}_\gamma\\
\downarrow && \downarrow\\
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u^s} & \widetilde{C'}
\end{array}
\]
is commutative up to isomorphism, and \(u^s_\gamma\) is exact.
\item Suppose that the forgetful functor on \(\gamma\)-objects (respectively on \(C'\)) has a left adjoint \(\Lib^{\sim}\) (respectively \({\Lib'}^{\sim}\)); cf. Expos\'e~II, 6.5. Then there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
u^s_\gamma\Lib^{\sim}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\Lib'}^{\sim}u^s.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, when \(C\) is not necessarily small but is a \(\U\)-site, the functor \(u_s^\gamma\) still has a left adjoint \(u^s_\gamma\). Finally, if \(\V\) is a universe containing \(\U\), and if \(u^s_{\gamma\U}\) (respectively \(u^s_{\gamma\V}\)) denotes the left adjoint to \(u_s\) relative to the universe \(\U\) (respectively \(\V\)), the diagram analogous to (1.5.1)
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde C_{\gamma\U} & \xrightarrow{u^s_{\gamma\U}} & \widetilde{C'}_{\gamma\U}\\
\downarrow && \downarrow\\
\widetilde C_{\gamma\V} & \xrightarrow{u^s_{\gamma\V}} & \widetilde{C'}_{\gamma\V}
\end{array}
\tag{1.7.1}
\]
is commutative up to canonical isomorphism.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The proof in the case where \(C\) is a small site is left to the reader. It will be completed in 4.3 in the case where \(C\) is a \(\U\)-site. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{remarktext}{Notation 1.8}
From now on we shall use the notation \(u_s\) also for the functor \(u_s^\gamma\). This notation creates no risk of confusion, since the functor \(u_s\), defined on sheaves of sets, commutes with finite projective limits.
\end{remarktext}

Similarly, when \(u^s\) is left exact, we shall use the notation \(u^s\) for the functor \(u^s_\gamma\). This abuse of notation is justified by Proposition~1.7(4).

\begin{remarktext}{Example 1.9.1}
Let \(X\) be a small topological space. Denote by \(\Ouv(X)\) the category of open subsets of \(X\), whose morphisms are inclusions, endowed with the canonical topology. A family \((U_i\subset U)_{i\in I}\) is covering if and only if the open sets \(U_i\) cover \(U\) (Expos\'e~II, 2.6). The sheaves of sets on \(\Ouv(X)\) are therefore the sheaves of sets in the sense of Godement's \emph{Th\'eorie des faisceaux}. Let \(f:X\to Y\) be a continuous map. It gives a functor
\[
f^{-1}:\Ouv(Y)\longrightarrow\Ouv(X),
\]
which sends an open \(U\subset Y\) to its inverse image under \(f\). The functor \(f^{-1}\) is continuous (Proposition~1.6). The functor
\[
f^{-1}_s:\Ouv(X)^{\sim}\longrightarrow\Ouv(Y)^{\sim}
\]
is the direct-image functor for sheaves in the sense of Godement; the functor
\[
(f^{-1})^s:\Ouv(Y)^{\sim}\longrightarrow\Ouv(X)^{\sim}
\]
is the inverse-image functor for sheaves in that same sense. The functor \((f^{-1})^s\) commutes with finite projective limits by Proposition~1.3(5).
\end{remarktext}

\begin{remarktext}{Example 1.9.2}
Let \(X\) be a topological space and let \(Y\hookrightarrow X\) be an open subspace. Every open subset of \(Y\) is an open subset of \(X\), giving a functor
\[
\Phi:\Ouv(Y)\longrightarrow\Ouv(X).
\]
The functor \(\Phi\) is continuous (Proposition~1.6). The functor \(\Phi_s:\Ouv(X)^\sim\to\Ouv(Y)^\sim\) is the restriction-to-\(Y\) functor. The functor
\[
\Phi^s_{\Ab}:\Ouv(Y)^\sim_{\Ab}\longrightarrow\Ouv(X)^\sim_{\Ab}
\]
(Expos\'e~II, 6.3.3 and Expos\'e~I, 1.7) is the extension-by-zero-outside-\(Y\) functor of \emph{Th\'eorie des faisceaux}. The functor \(\Phi^s:\Ouv(Y)^\sim\to\Ouv(X)^\sim\) is analogous to extension by zero: for every sheaf \(H\) on \(Y\) and every point \(y\in Y\), the stalk at \(y\) of \(\Phi^sH\) is canonically isomorphic to the stalk at \(y\) of \(H\); for every point \(x\in X\), \(x\notin Y\), the stalk at \(x\) of \(\Phi^sH\) is empty. The functor \(\Phi^s\) is called \emph{extension by the empty set outside \(Y\)}. It commutes with fiber products and with finite products over nonempty index sets, but it does not carry the final object of \(\Ouv(Y)^\sim\) to the final object of \(\Ouv(X)^\sim\).
\end{remarktext}

\begin{remarktext}{Example 1.9.3}
Let \(C\) and \(C'\) be two small sites, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between the underlying categories which sends every covering family of \(C\) to a covering family of \(C'\). In general \(u\) is not continuous (cf. nevertheless Proposition~1.6). Here is a counterexample. Let \(S\) be an infinite set of the universe \(\U\), and let \(C\) be the full subcategory of the category of sets whose objects are the finite nonempty subsets of \(S\). Endow \(C\) with the canonical topology. Covering families in \(C\) are precisely the jointly surjective families of maps, and no covering family is empty. Moreover, up to isomorphism there are only two constant sheaves on \(C\): the sheaf with value the empty set, and the sheaf with value a singleton. Put \(C=C'\), and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a constant functor. The functor \(u\) sends covering families of \(C\) to covering families of \(C'\), but \(u\) is not continuous. Indeed, since the representable presheaves on \(C'\) are sheaves, for every object \(X\) of \(C'\) there is a sheaf \(F\) on \(C'\) such that \(\operatorname{card}F(X)\ge 2\); hence the presheaf \(u^*F\) is not a sheaf.
\end{remarktext}

\section{Cocontinuous functors}

\begin{statement}{Definition 2.1}
Let \(C\) and \(C'\) be two \(\U\)-sites, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between the underlying categories. We say that \(u\) is \emph{cocontinuous} if it has the following property:

\((\mathrm{COC})\) For every object \(Y\) of \(C\), and every covering sieve \(R\hookrightarrow u(Y)\), the sieve of \(Y\) generated by the arrows \(Z\to Y\) such that \(u(Z)\to u(Y)\) factors through \(R\), covers \(Y\).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 2.2}
Let \(C\) be a small site, let \(C'\) be a \(\U\)-site, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between the underlying categories. Let
\[
\widehat u_*:\widehat C\longrightarrow\widehat{C'}
\]
be the functor right adjoint to \(F\mapsto F\circ u=\widehat u^*F\) (Expos\'e~I, 5.1). The functor \(u\) is cocontinuous if and only if, for every sheaf \(G\) on \(C\), \(\widehat u_*G\) is a sheaf on \(C'\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
\emph{Sufficiency.} Suppose that for every sheaf \(G\) on \(C\), the presheaf \(\widehat u_*G\) is a sheaf. Let \(Y\) be an object of \(C\), and let \(R\hookrightarrow u(Y)\) be a covering sieve. One has an isomorphism
\[
\Hom(u(Y),\widehat u_*G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom(R,\widehat u_*G),
\]
and hence, by adjunction,
\[
\Hom(\widehat u^*u(Y),G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom(\widehat u^*R,G).
\]
Thus the morphism \(\widehat u^*R\to\widehat u^*u(Y)\) is bicovering (Expos\'e~II, 5.3). Since \(\widehat u^*\) commutes with projective limits, this morphism is also a monomorphism, hence a covering monomorphism. On the other hand, there is a canonical morphism
\[
Y\xrightarrow{p}\widehat u^*u(Y)
\]
deduced from the adjunction morphism \(\id_C\to\widehat u^*u_!\) (Expos\'e~I, 5.4(3)). Base-changing by \(p\), we obtain a covering sieve of \(Y\):
\[
\widehat u^*R\times_{\widehat u^*u(Y)}Y\longrightarrow Y.
\]
The arrows \(Z\to Y\) which factor through this sieve are exactly those described in (COC).

\emph{Necessity.} Let \(S\) be an object of \(C'\), and let \(R\hookrightarrow S\) be a covering sieve. We must prove that, for every sheaf \(G\) on \(C\), one has an isomorphism
\[
\Hom(S,\widehat u_*G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom(R,\widehat u_*G).
\]
Using Expos\'e~II, 5.3 and the adjunction isomorphisms, it is enough to prove that the monomorphism \(\widehat u^*R\to\widehat u^*S\) is covering. For this it is enough (Expos\'e~II, 5.1) to prove that, for every base change \(Y\to\widehat u^*S\), with \(Y\) an object of \(C\), the sieve
\[
\widehat u^*R\times_{\widehat u^*S}Y\longrightarrow Y
\]
is covering. By the properties of adjoint functors and by Expos\'e~I, 5.4(3), the morphism \(Y\to\widehat u^*S\) factors through
\[
Y\xrightarrow{p}\widehat u^*u(Y)\longrightarrow\widehat u^*S.
\]
Thus the sieve \(\widehat u^*R\times_{\widehat u^*S}Y\hookrightarrow Y\) is the base change, along \(p\), of the monomorphism
\[
\widehat u^*R\times_{\widehat u^*S}\widehat u^*u(Y)
\longrightarrow \widehat u^*u(Y).
\]
Since \(\widehat u^*\) commutes with projective limits, this monomorphism is obtained by applying \(\widehat u^*\) to the covering sieve \(R\times_Su(Y)\hookrightarrow u(Y)\). The hypothesis (COC) then says precisely that the sieve \(\widehat u^*R\times_{\widehat u^*S}Y\hookrightarrow Y\) is covering. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 2.3}
Let \(C\) and \(C'\) be two \(\U\)-sites, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be cocontinuous. Define
\[
u^*:\widetilde{C'}\longrightarrow\widetilde C,
\qquad
u^*=\underline a\,\widehat u^*i',
\]
where \(\underline a\) denotes sheafification for \(\widehat C\), \(\widehat u^*\) is the functor \(F\mapsto F\circ u\), and \(i'\) is the inclusion \(\widetilde{C'}\to\widehat{C'}\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item The functor \(u^*\) commutes with inductive limits and is exact.
\item There is a canonical isomorphism \(u^*\underline a'\simeq\underline a\widehat u^*\), where \(\underline a'\) denotes sheafification for \(\widehat{C'}\).
\item The functor \(u^*\) has a right adjoint
\[
u_*:\widetilde C\longrightarrow\widetilde{C'}.
\]
When \(C\) is small, the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde C & \xrightarrow{u_*} & \widetilde{C'}\\
\downarrow i && \downarrow i'\\
\widehat C & \xrightarrow{\widehat u_*} & \widehat{C'}
\end{array}
\tag{2.3.1}
\]
where the bottom functor is right adjoint to \(\widehat u^*\), is commutative up to canonical isomorphism.
\item Let \(\V\supset\U\) be a universe. If \(u_{*\U}:\widetilde C_{\U}\to\widetilde{C'}_{\U}\) and \(u_{*\V}:\widetilde C_{\V}\to\widetilde{C'}_{\V}\) denote the right adjoints relative to \(\U\) and \(\V\), then the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde C_{\U} & \xrightarrow{u_{*\U}} & \widetilde{C'}_{\U}\\
\downarrow && \downarrow\\
\widetilde C_{\V} & \xrightarrow{u_{*\V}} & \widetilde{C'}_{\V}
\end{array}
\tag{2.3.2}
\]
is commutative up to isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
Assertions (1) and (2) follow immediately from Expos\'e~II, 3.4. Assertion (3), when \(C\) is small, follows from Proposition~2.2; when \(C\) is a \(\U\)-site, it will be proved in 4.4. Assertion (4), when \(C\) is small, follows from the analogous commutativity assertion when the topologies on \(C\) and \(C'\) are chaotic, together with Expos\'e~I, 3.5. For chaotic topologies the commutativity of (2.3.2) is immediate from the explicit description of \(u_*\) in Expos\'e~I, 5.1. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\subsection*{2.4}
We shall not develop here the considerations concerning \(\gamma\)-objects in categories of sheaves. It will be enough to observe that the functors \(u^*\) and \(u_*\) introduced in this section always commute with finite projective limits (unlike the functor \(u^s\) in the preceding section). Consequently they extend naturally to functors defined on \(\gamma\)-objects; these functors are adjoint to one another and commute with the underlying-sheaf-of-sets functors. We shall use the abuses of notation indicated in 1.8, writing \(u^*\) and \(u_*\) also for the extensions to \(\gamma\)-objects.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 2.5}
Let \(C\) and \(C'\) be two \(\U\)-sites, and let
\[
C\underset{u}{\stackrel{v}{\rightleftarrows}} C'
\]
be a pair of functors, where \(v\) is left adjoint to \(u\). The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The functor \(u\) is continuous.
\item The functor \(v\) is cocontinuous.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, under these equivalent conditions, there are canonical isomorphisms
\[
v_*\simeq u_s,
\qquad
v^*\simeq u^s.
\]
In particular, \(u^s\) commutes with finite projective limits.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The property of a functor being continuous or cocontinuous does not depend on the universe \(\U\) for which \(C\) and \(C'\) are \(\U\)-sites. This is immediate for a cocontinuous functor, and follows from Proposition~1.5 for a continuous functor. Enlarging the universe if necessary, we may therefore assume \(C\) and \(C'\) small. The proposition then follows from Proposition~2.2 and Expos\'e~I, 5.6. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 2.6}
Let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor which is both continuous and cocontinuous between two \(\U\)-sites. Then the functor
\[
u^*:\widetilde{C'}\longrightarrow\widetilde C
\]
(Proposition~2.3) commutes with \(\U\)-inductive and projective limits. Let
\[
u_!:\widetilde C\longrightarrow\widetilde{C'},
\qquad
u_*:\widetilde C\longrightarrow\widetilde{C'}
\]
be the left and right adjoints to \(u^*\), respectively. The functor \(u_!\) is fully faithful if and only if \(u_*\) is fully faithful. When \(u\) is fully faithful, \(u_!\) is fully faithful; conversely, if the topologies of \(C\) and \(C'\) are coarser than the canonical topologies, full faithfulness of \(u_!\) implies full faithfulness of \(u\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The functor \(u^*\) has a right adjoint \(u_*\) (Proposition~2.3) and a left adjoint \(u_!\) (Proposition~1.2). Hence \(u^*\) commutes with inductive and projective limits (Expos\'e~I, 2.11). The assertion that \(u_!\) is fully faithful if and only if \(u_*\) is fully faithful is a general property of adjoint functors (Expos\'e~I, 5.7(1)). If \(u\) is fully faithful, then for a sufficiently large universe \(\V\), the functor
\[
\widehat u_*:\widehat C_{\V}\longrightarrow\widehat{C'}_{\V}
\]
on \(\V\)-presheaves is fully faithful (Expos\'e~I, 5.7). Hence \(u_*:\widetilde C\to\widetilde{C'}\) is fully faithful by the commutativity of (2.3.1) and (2.3.2), and therefore \(u_!\) is fully faithful by the preceding paragraph. The converse follows from the commutative diagram of Proposition~1.2(iv), together with the fact that the functors \(\epsilon_C\) and \(\epsilon_{C'}\) are fully faithful when the topologies are coarser than the canonical topologies. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{remarktext}{Example 2.7}
With the notation of Example~1.9.2, the functor
\[
\Phi:\Ouv(Y)\longrightarrow\Ouv(X)
\]
is continuous and cocontinuous. Moreover, let \(i:Y\to X\) be the canonical inclusion and let
\[
i^{-1}:\Ouv(X)\longrightarrow\Ouv(Y)
\]
be the functor it defines (Example~1.9.1). The functor \(i^{-1}\) is right adjoint to \(\Phi\). Thus one has a chain of three adjoint functors
\[
\Phi^s,
\qquad
\Phi_s=\Phi^*,
\qquad
\Phi_*,
\]
and canonical isomorphisms
\[
\Phi^*\simeq(i^{-1})^s,
\qquad
\Phi_*\simeq i^{-1}_s.
\]
\end{remarktext}

\section{Induced topology}

\subsection*{3.1}
Let \(C'\) be a site, let \(C\) be a category, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor. For every universe \(\U\) such that \(C'\) is a \(\U\)-site and \(C\) is a \(\U\)-small category, let \(\mathcal T_{\U}\) be the finest among the topologies \(T\) on \(C\) making \(u\) continuous (Definition~1.1). Such a topology exists by Expos\'e~II, 2.2. The topology \(\mathcal T_{\U}\) does not depend on the universe \(\U\): if \(\V\supset\U\), then \(\mathcal T_{\U}=\mathcal T_{\V}\), by Definition~1.1 and Proposition~1.5. This topology is called the \emph{topology induced on \(C\) by the topology of \(C'\) via the functor \(u\)}.\footnote{When there is no risk of confusion, it is simply called the topology induced on \(C\) by the topology of \(C'\).}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 3.2}
Let \(C\) be a small category, let \(C'\) be a \(\U\)-site, let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor, and let \(\mathcal T\) be the topology on \(C\) induced by \(u\). Let \(X\) be an object of \(C\), and let \(R\hookrightarrow X\) be a sieve on \(X\). The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The sieve \(R\hookrightarrow X\) is covering for \(\mathcal T\).
\item For every base change \(Y\to X\), where \(Y\) is an object of \(C\), the morphism
\[
u_!(R\times_XY)\longrightarrow u(Y)
\]
is bicovering in \(\widehat{C'}\) (Expos\'e~II, 5.2).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The implication \(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\) follows from axiom (T1) for topologies and from Proposition~1.2. Conversely, for every sheaf \(F\) on \(C'\), the map
\[
\Hom(Y,u^*F)\longrightarrow\Hom(R\times_XY,u^*F)
\]
is isomorphic by adjunction to
\[
\Hom(u(Y),F)\longrightarrow\Hom(u_!(R\times_XY),F),
\]
which is bijective by Expos\'e~II, 5.3. Hence, by Expos\'e~II, 2.2, the sieve \(R\hookrightarrow X\) is covering for \(\mathcal T\). \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 3.3}
Let \(C'\) be a site, let \(C\) be a category, let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor, and let \(\mathcal T\) be the topology on \(C\) induced by the topology of \(C'\). Let \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) be a family of base-changeable morphisms in \(C\), and suppose that \(u\) commutes with the relevant fiber products.\footnote{In fact it is enough that \(u\) commute with fiber products of the form \(X_i\times_XX'\).} The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The family \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) is covering for \(\mathcal T\).
\item The family \((u(X_i)\to u(X))_{i\in I}\) is covering in \(C'\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The implication \(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\) follows from Proposition~1.6. Conversely, choose a universe \(\U\) such that \(C\) is \(\U\)-small and \(C'\) is a \(\U\)-site. Let \(R\hookrightarrow X\) be the sieve generated by the \(X_i\to X\). The presheaf \(R\) is the coequalizer of
\[
\coprod_{i,j}X_i\times_XX_j\rightrightarrows\coprod_iX_i
\]
(the coproduct being taken in \(\widehat C\)); cf. Expos\'e~I, 2.12 and 3.5. Since \(u_!\) commutes with inductive limits, \(u_!R\) is the coequalizer of
\[
\coprod_{i,j}u(X_i\times_XX_j)\rightrightarrows\coprod_i u(X_i).
\]
As \(u\) commutes with fiber products, \(u_!R\) is the coequalizer of
\[
\coprod_{i,j}u(X_i)\times_{u(X)}u(X_j)\rightrightarrows\coprod_i u(X_i).
\]
Thus \(u_!R\to u(X)\) is the sieve on \(u(X)\) generated by the morphisms \(u(X_i)\to u(X)\). Using again that \(u\) commutes with fiber products, one proves that for every base change \(Y\to X\), with \(Y\) an object of \(C\), the morphism
\[
u_!(R\times_XY)\longrightarrow u(Y)
\]
is the sieve on \(u(Y)\) generated by the morphisms
\[
u(X_i)\times_{u(X)}u(Y)\longrightarrow u(Y).
\]
Since the family \(u(X_i)\to u(X)\) is covering, the family \(u(X_i)\times_{u(X)}u(Y)\to u(Y)\) is covering. Hence \(u_!(R\times_XY)\to u(Y)\) is a covering sieve, and by Proposition~3.2 the sieve \(R\to X\) is covering for \(\mathcal T\). \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 3.4}
Let \(C'\) be a \(\U\)-site, let \(C\) be a full subcategory of \(C'\), and let \(u:C\to C'\) be the inclusion functor. Suppose fiber products are representable in \(C\), and that \(u\) commutes with fiber products. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item For every object \(X\) of \(C\), every covering family \((Y_j\to X)_{j\in J}\) in \(C'\) is refined by a covering family \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) with \(X_i\) objects of \(C\).
\item There exists a small set \(G\) of objects of \(C\) such that every object of \(C\) is the target of a covering family in \(C'\) whose sources are in \(G\).
\end{enumerate}
\item The topology induced by the topology of \(C'\) is a \(\U\)-topology, and \(u\) is both continuous and cocontinuous for this topology.
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
This follows immediately from Corollary~3.3 and Definition~2.1. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 3.5}
Let \(C\) be a \(\U\)-site and let \(\epsilon_C:C\to\widetilde C\) be the canonical functor (Expos\'e~II, 4.4.0). Endow \(\widetilde C\) with the canonical topology. Then the topology of the site \(C\) is the topology induced by the topology of \(\widetilde C\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The covering families for the canonical topology on \(\widetilde C\) are the universally effective epimorphic families (Expos\'e~II, 2.5), i.e., by Expos\'e~II, 4.3, the epimorphic families. Let \(T\) be the topology of the site \(C\), and let \(\mathcal T_\U\) be the finest topology \(T'\) on \(C\) such that, for every sheaf \(F\) on \(\widetilde C\), the composite \(F\circ\epsilon_C\) is a sheaf for \(T'\). It is enough to show that \(T=\mathcal T_\U\), for then \(\mathcal T_\U\) is a \(\U\)-topology and hence is the induced topology.

\emph{A) The topology \(T\) is finer than \(\mathcal T_\U\).} Let \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) be a covering family for \(\mathcal T_\U\). Then for every sheaf \(F\) on \(\widetilde C\), the map
\[
F(\epsilon_CX)\longrightarrow\prod_iF(\epsilon_CX_i)
\]
is injective. Thus \((\epsilon_CX_i\to\epsilon_CX)_{i\in I}\) is covering for the canonical topology of \(\widetilde C\), and therefore \((X_i\to X)_{i\in I}\) is covering for \(T\).

\emph{B) The topology \(T\) is coarser than \(\mathcal T_\U\).} It is enough to prove that \(\epsilon_C:C\to\widetilde C\) is continuous. In Expos\'e~IV, Section~1, we shall prove that every sheaf on \(\widetilde C\) is representable; hence, for every sheaf \(F\) on \(\widetilde C\), the composite \(F\circ\epsilon_C\) is a sheaf on \(C\). Proposition~3.5 will not be used before Expos\'e~IV, Section~1.

We record two results that will be used in Expos\'e~VI, Section~7. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 3.6}
Let \((C_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of sites, let \(C\) be a category, and for each \(i\in I\) let \(u_i:C_i\to C\) be a functor. Let \(\U\) be a universe such that the categories \(C_i\) and \(C\) are \(\U\)-small. There exists a topology \(\mathcal T_\U\) on \(C\) which is the coarsest topology for which all the \(u_i\) are continuous. The topology \(\mathcal T_\U\) does not depend on the universe \(\U\) for which the categories under consideration are small.
\end{statement}

The last assertion of Proposition~3.6 follows from Proposition~1.5. Let \(T\) be a topology on \(C\). Then the functors \(u_i\) are continuous if and only if, for every \(i\in I\) and every covering sieve \(R\hookrightarrow X\) of an object \(X\) of \(C_i\), the morphism
\[
u_{i!}(R)\longrightarrow u_i(X)
\]
in \(\widehat C\) is bicovering (Proposition~1.2(ii)). Thus Proposition~3.6 follows from the following lemma.

\begin{statement}{Lemma 3.6.1}
Let \(C\) be a small category, and let \((u_i:F_i\to G_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of arrows in \(\widehat C\). Then there exists on \(C\) a coarsest topology among those which make the morphisms \(u_i\) covering (respectively bicovering).
\end{statement}

To say that \(u:F\to G\) is covering for a given topology \(T\) means that for every arrow \(X\to G\), with \(X\in\Ob C\), the corresponding arrow \(F\times_GX\to X\) is covering; equivalently, the family of arrows \(X'\to X\) in \(C\) which factor through this arrow is covering. The existence of a coarsest topology for which the \(u_i:F_i\to G_i\) are covering follows from Expos\'e~I, 1.1.6, proving the non-parenthetical assertion of Lemma~3.6.1. The bicovering assertion follows by recalling that a morphism \(u:F\to G\) is bicovering if and only if the two morphisms \(u:F\to G\) and \(\diag_u:F\to F\times_G F\) are covering.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 3.7}
Let \((C_i)_{i\in I}\) be a family of sites, let \(C\) be a category, for each \(i\in I\) let \(u_i:C_i\to C\) be a functor, and let \(\U\) be a universe such that the categories \(C_i\) and \(C\) are \(\U\)-small. There exists a topology \(\mathfrak T_\U\) which is the finest topology for which all the \(u_i\) are cocontinuous. The topology \(\mathfrak T_\U\) does not depend on the universe \(\U\) for which the categories under consideration are small.
\end{statement}

For a universe \(\U\) in which the categories under consideration are small, the functors \(u_i\) are cocontinuous for a topology \(\mathfrak T\) on \(C\) if and only if, for every \(i\in I\) and every sheaf \(F\) on \(C_i\), the presheaf \(\widehat u_{i*}F\) is a sheaf for \(\mathfrak T\) (Proposition~2.2). It follows that \(\mathfrak T_\U\) is the finest topology for which the presheaves \(\widehat u_{i*}F\), \(i\in I\), \(F\in\Ob C_i\), are sheaves (Expos\'e~II, 2.2). The last assertion follows from Proposition~2.2.

\section{The comparison lemma}

\begin{statement}{Theorem 4.1 (Comparison lemma)}
Let \(C\) be a small category, let \(C'\) be a site whose underlying category is a \(\U\)-category, and let \(u:C\to C'\) be a fully faithful functor. Endow \(C\) with the topology induced by \(u\) (3.1). Consider the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item Every object of \(C'\) can be covered by objects coming from \(C\).
\item The functor \(F\mapsto F\circ u\) induces an equivalence from the category of sheaves on \(C'\) to the category of sheaves on \(C\).
\end{enumerate}
One always has \(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\). If \(C'\) is a \(\U\)-site and the topology of \(C'\) is coarser than the canonical topology, then \(\mathrm{ii})\Rightarrow\mathrm{i})\).
\end{statement}

\subsubsection*{4.1.1}
We first prove \(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{ii})\). The proof has two steps.

\emph{First step.} For every presheaf \(H\) on \(C'\), the adjunction morphism
\[
\varphi:u_!u^*H\longrightarrow H
\]
(Expos\'e~I, 5.1) is bicovering (Expos\'e~II, 5.3), and the functor \(u_s:\widetilde{C'}\to\widetilde C\) is fully faithful.

Let \(C/H\) be the small category whose objects are pairs consisting of an object \(Y\) of \(C\) and a morphism \(u(Y)\to H\), and whose morphisms are the commutative triangles
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
u(Y)&\xrightarrow{u(m)}&u(Y')\\
&\searrow&\downarrow\\
&&H.
\end{array}
\]
One has
\[
u^*H=\varinjlim_{Y\in\Ob(C/H)}Y
\]
(Expos\'e~I, 3.4), and hence
\[
u_!u^*H=\varinjlim_{Y\in\Ob(C/H)}u(Y)
\]
(Expos\'e~I, 5.4). The adjunction morphism is the evident morphism resulting from this description of \(u_!u^*H\) as an inductive limit. It has the following property: \((*)\) for every object \(Y\) of \(C\), every morphism \(m:u(Y)\to H\) factors uniquely as the canonical morphism \(\alpha(m):u(Y)\to u_!u^*H\) followed by \(\varphi\).

It follows immediately from \(\mathrm{i})\) that \(\varphi\) is covering. We prove it is bicovering. Let \(p,q:Z\rightrightarrows u_!u^*H\) be two morphisms from an object \(Z\) of \(C'\) such that \(\varphi p=\varphi q\). For every object \(Y\) of \(C\) and every morphism \(n:u(Y)\to Z\), one has \(\varphi pn=\varphi qn\). Property \((*)\) then implies \(pn=qn\). Since the objects \(u(Y)\) cover \(Z\), the equalizer of \((p,q)\) is a covering sieve of \(Z\). Hence \(\varphi\) is bicovering.

For every sheaf \(H\) on \(C'\), \(u^*H\) is a sheaf on \(C\), denoted \(u_sH\). Moreover
\[
u^su_sH=\underline a'u_!u_sH
\]
by Proposition~1.3, and the adjunction morphism \(u^su_sH\to H\) is obtained by applying sheafification to \(\varphi:u_!u^*H\to H\). Therefore \(u^su_sH\to H\) is an isomorphism. Thus \(u_s:\widetilde{C'}\to\widetilde C\) is fully faithful.

\medskip
\noindent\emph{Second step.} The functor \(u\) is cocontinuous, and the functor \(u^s:\widetilde C\to\widetilde{C'}\) of Proposition~1.2 is fully faithful. Hence \(u_s:\widetilde{C'}\to\widetilde C\) is an equivalence.

Let \(Y\) be an object of \(C\), and let \(i:R\hookrightarrow u(Y)\) be a covering sieve. Since \(u^*\) commutes with projective limits (Expos\'e~I, 5.5), the morphism \(u^*(i):u^*(R)\hookrightarrow u^*u(Y)\) is a monomorphism. Since \(u\) is fully faithful, \(u^*u(Y)\simeq Y\), hence \(u^*(i)\) is a sieve of \(Y\). To prove that \(u\) is cocontinuous, it is enough to show that \(u^*(i):u^*(R)\hookrightarrow Y\) is covering for the induced topology on \(C\). By Proposition~3.2, it is enough to show that for every base change \(m:X\to Y\), the morphism
\[
u_!(u^*(R)\times_YX)\longrightarrow u(X)
\]
is bicovering. But \(u^*\) commutes with projective limits, so
\[
u^*(R)\times_YX=u^*(R\times_{u(Y)}u(X)),
\]
and \(R\times_{u(Y)}u(X)\) is a covering sieve of \(u(X)\). It is therefore enough to show that, for every object \(Y\) of \(C\) and every covering sieve \(i:R\hookrightarrow u(Y)\), the morphism in \(\widehat{C'}\)
\[
u_!u^*(R)\xrightarrow{u_!u^*(i)}u(Y)
\]
is bicovering. This morphism factors as the adjunction morphism \(u_!u^*(R)\to R\), which is bicovering by the first step, followed by the covering monomorphism \(R\hookrightarrow u(Y)\). It is therefore bicovering. This proves that \(u\) is cocontinuous. Since \(u\) is continuous and fully faithful, Proposition~2.6, used in the case where \(C\) is small, implies that \(u^s\) (denoted \(u_!\) in Proposition~2.6) is fully faithful. Since \(u^s\) and \(u_s\) are adjoint and both fully faithful, they are quasi-inverse functors; hence \(u_s\) is an equivalence.

\subsubsection*{4.1.2}
We now prove \(\mathrm{ii})\Rightarrow\mathrm{i})\). The objects \(Y\) of \(C\) form a generating family of \(\widetilde C\) (Expos\'e~II, 4.10). Hence, for every object \(X\) of \(C'\), there exists an epimorphic family in \(\widetilde C\)
\[
v_i:Y_i\longrightarrow u_sX,
\qquad i\in I.
\]
Since \(u_su(Y_i)=Y_i\), and since \(u_s\) is an equivalence of categories, one has \(v_i=u_s(w_i)\), where the morphisms \(w_i:u(Y_i)\to X\) form an epimorphic family in \(\widetilde{C'}\). It follows from Expos\'e~II, 5.1 that the family \(w_i:u(Y_i)\to X\), \(i\in I\), is covering.

\subsection{Completion of the proof of Proposition 1.2}
Let \(G\) be a full subcategory of \(C\) whose objects form a small topologically generating family of \(C\) (Expos\'e~II, 3.0.1). Endow \(G\) with the topology induced by the inclusion functor \(i:G\to C\). The functor
\[
(u\circ i)_s=i_s\circ u_s
\]
has a left adjoint by the first part of the proof of Proposition~1.2. Since \(i_s\) is an equivalence of categories by Theorem~4.1, the functor \(u_s\) has a left adjoint \(u^s\), and there is a functorial isomorphism
\[
u^s\simeq (u\circ i)^s\circ i_s.
\]
We prove that the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
C&\xrightarrow{u}&C'\\
\downarrow\epsilon_C&&\downarrow\epsilon_{C'}\\
\widetilde C&\xrightarrow{u^s}&\widetilde{C'}
\end{array}
\tag{4.2.1}
\]
is commutative up to isomorphism. For every sheaf \(H\) on \(C'\), one has
\[
\Hom_{\widetilde{C'}}(\epsilon_{C'}uX,H)\simeq u_sH(X)
\]
for every \(X\in\Ob C\). Moreover,
\[
\Hom_{\widetilde{C'}}(u^s\epsilon_CX,H)
\simeq \Hom_{\widetilde C}(\epsilon_CX,u_sH)
\simeq u_sH(X),
\]
first by adjunction and then by the definition of \(\epsilon_C\). Thus \(\epsilon_{C'}uX\simeq u^s\epsilon_CX\) for every \(X\in\Ob C\). This proves \(\mathrm{i})\Rightarrow\mathrm{iv})\). Conversely, assume \(\mathrm{iv})\). One has a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
G&\xrightarrow{i}&C&\xrightarrow{u}&C'\\
\downarrow\epsilon_G&&\downarrow\epsilon_C&&\downarrow\epsilon_{C'}\\
\widetilde G&\xrightarrow{i^s}&\widetilde C&\xrightarrow{u^s}&\widetilde{C'}.
\end{array}
\tag{4.2.2}
\]
It follows, by the first part of the proof, that \(u\circ i:G\to C'\) is continuous. Theorem~4.1 and Definition~1.1 then immediately imply that \(u\) is continuous. This proves \(\mathrm{iv})\Rightarrow\mathrm{i})\). The uniqueness of \(u^s\) follows as well, since it is already known in the small case.

\subsection{Completion of the proof of Propositions 1.5 and 1.7}
Let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between two \(\U\)-sites, and let \(G\) be a full subcategory of \(C\) whose set of objects is a small topologically generating family of \(C\) (Expos\'e~II, 3.0.1). Endow \(G\) with the topology induced by the inclusion functor \(i:G\to C\). It follows immediately from Theorem~4.1 and Definition~1.1 that \(u\) is continuous if and only if \(u\circ i\) is continuous. From this remark and from the first part of the proof of Proposition~1.5, the general case follows. The same remark also reduces the proof of Proposition~1.7 to the case where \(C\) is small.

\subsection{Completion of the proof of Proposition 2.3}
Let \(u:C\to C'\) be a functor between two \(\U\)-sites, and let \(G\) be a full subcategory of \(C\) whose set of objects is a small topologically generating family of \(C\) (Expos\'e~II, 3.0.1), endowed with the topology induced by the inclusion functor \(i:G\to C\). It follows from the proof of Theorem~4.1, specifically the second step of 4.1.1, that \(i\) is cocontinuous. Hence, if \(u\) is cocontinuous, the functor \(u\circ i:G\to C'\) is cocontinuous. Moreover
\[
i^*:\widetilde C\longrightarrow\widetilde G
\]
is an equivalence of categories by Theorem~4.1. Therefore the functor \(u^*:\widetilde{C'}\to\widetilde C\) has a right adjoint. This proves Proposition~2.3(3). To prove (4), it is enough to observe that
\[
(u\circ i)_*\simeq u_*\circ i_*,
\]
and that \(i_*\) is an equivalence (Theorem~4.1). The commutativity of (2.3.2) then follows from the commutativity of these diagrams when \(C\) is small.

\subsection*{5.1}
Let now \(C\) be a \(\U\)-site and let \(X\) be an object of \(\widehat C\). Unless explicitly stated otherwise, the category \(C/X\) will be endowed with the topology \(\mathcal T\) induced by the functor
\[
j_X:C/X\longrightarrow C.
\]
The notation \(C/X\) will denote the category \(C/X\) endowed with this topology. Proposition~5.11 of Expos\'e~I shows that the functor \(j_{X!}\) commutes with fiber products, hence sends monomorphisms to monomorphisms. In particular, for every object \((Y\to X)\) of \(C/X\), the functor \(j_{X!}\) establishes a one-to-one correspondence between sieves, in \(C/X\), of \((Y\to X)\), and sieves, in \(C\), of \(Y\).

\begin{statement}{Proposition 5.2}
Let \(C\) be a \(\U\)-site, let \(X\) be an object of \(\widehat C\), and let \(j_X:C/X\to C\) be the corresponding continuous functor.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item A sieve \(R\) of an object \((Z\to X)\) is covering in \(C/X\) if and only if the sieve \(j_{X!}(R)\hookrightarrow Z\) is covering in \(C\).
\item The functor \(j_X:C/X\to C\) is both cocontinuous and continuous.
\item Let \(m:Y\to X\) be a morphism in \(\widehat C\). Then one has a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
C/Y&\xrightarrow{j_m}&C/X\\
&\searrow j_Y&\downarrow j_X\\
&&C.
\end{array}
\]
The topology induced on \(C/Y\) by \(j_Y\) is equal to the topology induced on \(C/Y\) by \(j_m\).
\item The topology induced by \(j_X:C/X\to C\) is a \(\U\)-topology (Expos\'e~II, 3.0.2).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item If the sieve \(R\) of \((Z\to X)\) is covering, then \(j_{X!}(R)\hookrightarrow Z\) is covering by Proposition~1.6. Conversely, if the sieve \(j_{X!}(R)\to Z\) is covering in \(C\), then the same is true after every base change in \(C/X\). Hence \(R\) is covering by Proposition~3.2.
\item This follows immediately from (1), applying Definition~2.1.
\item This follows immediately from the description of covering sieves given in (1).
\item Let \((G_i)_{i\in I}\) be a small topologically generating family of \(C\). One checks immediately that the small family
\[
(u:G_i\to X),
\qquad
u\in\coprod_{i\in I}\Hom_{\widehat C}(G_i,X),
\]
is topologically generating in \(C/X\). \qedtext
\end{enumerate}
\end{prooftext}

\noindent\textbf{Terminology and notation 5.3.}
By the preceding proposition, the functor \(j_X\) is both continuous and cocontinuous. It therefore defines a sequence of three adjoint functors between categories of sheaves of sets (Propositions~1.3 and~2.3):
\[
j_X^s:(C/X)^\sim\longrightarrow\widetilde C,
\qquad
j_X^*=j_{X,s}:\widetilde C\longrightarrow(C/X)^\sim,
\qquad
j_{X*}:(C/X)^\sim\longrightarrow\widetilde C.
\]
In the particular situation of Proposition~5.2, we shall use the following terminology and notation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item The functor \(j_{X*}\) will be called the \emph{direct-image functor}.
\item The functor \(j_{X,s}=j_X^*\) will be denoted \(j_X^*\) and called the \emph{restriction-to-\(C/X\) functor}.
\item The functor \(j_X^s\) on sheaves of sets will be called the \emph{extension by the empty set to the category \(C\)} functor and will be denoted \(j_{X!}\) (cf. 2.9.2).
\end{enumerate}
Thus one has a sequence of three adjoint functors between \((C/X)^\sim\) and \(\widetilde C\):
\[
j_{X!},\qquad j_X^*,\qquad j_{X*}.
\]

\begin{statement}{Proposition 5.4}
The functor
\[
j_{X!}:(C/X)^\sim\longrightarrow\widetilde C
\]
factors through the category \(\widetilde C/\underline aX\), where \(\underline a\) is sheafification:
\[
(C/X)^\sim\xrightarrow{e_X^\sim}\widetilde C/\underline aX\longrightarrow\widetilde C.
\]
The functor
\[
e_X^\sim:(C/X)^\sim\longrightarrow\widetilde C/\underline aX
\]
is an equivalence of categories. The restriction-to-\(C/X\) functor, composed with the equivalence \(e_X^\sim\), is isomorphic to base change along \(\underline aX\to\epsilon\), where \(\epsilon\) is the final object of \(\widetilde C\):
\[
F\longmapsto(F\times\underline aX\xrightarrow{\pr_2}\underline aX).
\]
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The image under \(j_{X!}\) of the final object of \((C/X)^\sim\) is \(\underline aX\), whence the factorization. To prove that \(e_X^\sim\) is an equivalence, we give only a few indications. By Expos\'e~I, 5.11, a presheaf on \(C/X\) is defined by a presheaf \(F\) on \(C\) together with a morphism \(F\to X\). One then proves that the following two properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The presheaf on \(C/X\) defined by \(F\to X\) is a sheaf.
\item The following square is cartesian, where \(i\) and \(\underline a\) denote the inclusion into presheaves and sheafification:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
F&\longrightarrow&i\underline aF\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
X&\longrightarrow&i\underline aX.
\end{array}
\]
\end{enumerate}
It follows immediately that \(e_X^\sim\) is an equivalence. The last assertion is trivial. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 5.5}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*)]
\item Let \(C\) be a \(\U\)-site and let \(X\) be an object of \(\widehat C\). The following diagram of categories and functors is commutative up to canonical isomorphism:
\[
\begin{array}{ccccc}
C/X&\to&\widehat{C/X}&\xrightarrow{\underline a_X}&(C/X)^\sim\to\widehat{C/X}\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\quad\downarrow\\
C/X&\to&\widehat C/X&\xrightarrow{\underline a/X}&\widetilde C/\underline aX\to\widehat C/i\underline aX\to\widehat C/X.
\end{array}
\]
Here \(\underline a_X\) and \(i_X\) denote sheafification and inclusion into presheaves for the site \(C/X\). The notation \(\underline a/X\) denotes the natural extension of \(\underline a\), the sheafification functor for \(C\), to the category of arrows with target \(X\); similarly for \(i/\underline aX\). Finally, \(\pi\) denotes the base-change functor along the canonical arrow \(X\to i\underline aX\).

\item Let, moreover, \(m:Y\to X\) be a morphism of \(\widehat C\). The following diagram is commutative up to canonical isomorphism:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde C/\underline aX/\underline aY & \xleftarrow{\ f\ } &(C/X)^\sim/\underline a_XY\\
\big\Vert && \uparrow g\\
\widetilde C/\underline aY & \xleftarrow{\ e_Y^\sim\ } &(C/Y)^\sim \xleftarrow{\ e_m^\sim\ } (C/X/Y)^\sim .
\end{array}
\]
The arrow \(f\) is the natural extension of the equivalence \(e_X^\sim\) to the category of arrows with target \(\underline a_XY\), and is therefore an equivalence of categories. The arrow \(g\) is precisely the equivalence of Proposition~5.4 applied to the situation \((C/X)/[m]\).

\item Let
\[
j_{\underline aX!}:\widetilde C/\underline aX\longrightarrow\widetilde C
\]
be the functor ``forget \(\underline aX\)'', and let
\[
j_{\underline aX}^*:\widetilde C\longrightarrow\widetilde C/\underline aX
\]
be the functor ``product with \(\underline aX\)''. The diagrams
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
(C/X)^\sim&\xrightarrow{j_{X!}}&\widetilde C\\
\downarrow e_X^\sim&&\Vert\\
\widetilde C/\underline aX&\xrightarrow{j_{\underline aX!}}&\widetilde C
\end{array}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\begin{array}{ccc}
\widetilde C&\xrightarrow{j_X^*}&(C/X)^\sim\\
\Vert&&\downarrow e_X^\sim\\
\widetilde C&\xrightarrow{j_{\underline aX}^*}&\widetilde C/\underline aX
\end{array}
\]
are commutative up to canonical isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The proof of these assertions is immediate from the definition of the equivalences \(e_X^\sim\) of Proposition~5.4 and \(e_X^{\widehat{\ }}\) of Expos\'e~I, 5.11. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}
