\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

This exposé is devoted to the biduality theorem in étale cohomology. The theory developed here and in SGA A XVIII (SGA A = SGA 4), for the étale topology and constructible torsion sheaves on preschemes, is formally very analogous to the theory developed in Hartshorne [H] for the Zariski topology and coherent sheaves; the latter served, to a large extent, as its model. But whereas, in the case of ``continuous'' coefficients, the existence of dualizing complexes presents no difficulty, in the case of ``discrete'' coefficients it is much more delicate to prove, and it seems to require resolution of singularities in an essential way. See, however, Deligne's biduality theorem (SGA $4^{1/2}$, Finiteness Theorem 4.3).

Paragraph 1 contains the definition of dualizing complexes and the formal properties that follow from it, in particular the interpretation of the dualizing functors as ``interchangers of functors''.

In paragraph 2, one shows that, on a connected locally noetherian prescheme, a dualizing complex is determined uniquely, up to translation of degrees and tensoring by an invertible sheaf.

In paragraph 3, one comes to the central theorem of the exposé (3.4.1), which asserts that, on a ``good'' regular prescheme $X$, for example an excellent noetherian regular prescheme of characteristic zero,\footnote{This last restriction will become unnecessary once resolution of singularities à la Hironaka and the ``purity theorem'' are available for such preschemes.} the complex $(\Z/n\Z)_X$ is dualizing.

Paragraph 4 gives applications of the theory to the local duality theorem. The latter says that, on a ``good'' prescheme $X$ equipped with a closed point $x$ whose residue field is separably closed, and for a constructible sheaf $F$, there is a perfect pairing between $\underline{\mathrm H}^i_x(F')$ and $\underline{\mathrm H}^{-i}_x(F)$, where $F'$ is the dual of $F$, that is,
\[
F'=\mathrm{R}\,\underline{\Hom}(F,K_X),
\]
with $K_X$ a dualizing complex.

Finally, paragraph 5 proves directly that, on a regular prescheme of dimension $1$, the complex $(\Z/n\Z)_X$ is dualizing, where $n$ is prime to the residue characteristics.

\section*{1. Definition and formal properties of dualizing complexes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{1. Definition and formal properties of dualizing complexes}

Fix a coefficient ring $A=\Z/n\Z$. If $X$ is a prescheme, let $A_X$ denote the constant sheaf on $X_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}$ with value $A$, and let $\D(X)$ denote the derived category of the category of $A_X$-modules. We also write $\D_c(X)$ for the full triangulated subcategory of $\D(X)$ consisting of complexes $F$ such that $\mathrm H^i(F)$ is a constructible $A_X$-module for every $i$. Put
\[
\D_c^*(X)=\D^*(X)\cap \D_c(X),\qquad *=\varnothing,+,-,\text{ or }b.
\]

\begin{definitionstar}[1.1]
An $A_X$-module $I$ is called \emph{quasi-injective} if
\[
\uExt^i(F,I)=0
\]
for every constructible $A_X$-module $F$ and every $i>0$.
\end{definitionstar}

\begin{remarkstar}[1.2]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item Contrary to what happens in the case of ``continuous'' coefficients, cf. [H] II.7.17, a quasi-injective sheaf is not in general injective. For example, let $X=\Spec(\R)$ and $A=\Z/2\Z$. The sheaf $A_X$ is quasi-injective, but is not injective, nor even of finite injective dimension, since
\[
\mathrm H^*(X;A_X)=\mathrm H^*(\Z/2\Z;\Z/2\Z)
\]
is a polynomial algebra on one generator of dimension $1$.

\item Let $I$ be a quasi-injective $A_X$-module. It is false in general that the relation $\uExt^i(F,I)=0$ for $i>0$, valid for constructible $F$, is valid for every $F$. Indeed take $A=\F_\ell$. Let $k$ be a field, let $\ol{k}$ be a separable closure of $k$, let
\[
f:\ol{x}=\Spec(\ol{k})\longrightarrow X=\Spec(k)
\]
be the canonical morphism, and suppose that, for every connected étale covering $X'\to X$, there exists a connected étale covering $X''\to X'$ such that $[X'':X']$ is divisible by $\ell$; for instance, take $k=\mathbb Q$. Consider the exact sequence
\[
(*)\qquad 0\longrightarrow A_X\xrightarrow{\;\varepsilon\;} f_*f^*A_X\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow 0
\]
defined by the adjunction arrow $\varepsilon$. In view of the hypothesis, the section of $\Ext^1(F,A_X)$ defined by $(*)$ vanishes over no étale $U$ over $X$, and hence $\uExt^1(F,A_X)_{\ol{x}}\ne 0$. On the other hand, one checks trivially that $A_X$ is quasi-injective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarkstar}

\begin{propositionstar}[1.3, cf. Hartshorne 1.7.6]
Let $X$ be a prescheme and let $N\in\Z$. The following conditions on $K\in\ob\D^+(X)$ are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $K$ is isomorphic, in $\D(X)$, to a bounded complex $I$ such that each $I^i$ is quasi-injective and $I^i=0$ for $i>N$;
\item for every $F\in\ob\D_c^b(X)$ such that $\mathrm H^i(F)=0$ for $i<0$, one has $\uExt^i(F,K)=0$ for $i>N$;
\item for every constructible $A_X$-module $F$, one has $\uExt^i(F,K)=0$ for $i>N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{propositionstar}

\begin{proof}
$(i)\Rightarrow(ii)$. Let $F\in\ob\D_c^b(X)$ be such that $\mathrm H^i(F)=0$ for $i<0$, and let $I\in\ob\D^+(X)$ be bounded, with each $I^i$ quasi-injective and $I^i=0$ for $i>N$. We show that $\uExt^i(F,I)=0$ for $i>N$. Arguing by induction on the number of indices $i$ for which $\mathrm H^i(F)\ne0$, one reduces, by dévissage, to the case where $N=0$ and $I$ is concentrated in degree $0$. Then there is a biregular spectral sequence
\[
E_2^{pq}=\uExt^p(\mathrm H^{-q}(F),I)\Rightarrow \uExt^*(F,I).
\]
Since $I$ is quasi-injective, $E_2^{pq}=0$ for $p>0$ and all $q$, and therefore
\[
E_2^{0i}=\uHom(\mathrm H^{-i}(F),I)\xrightarrow{\sim}\uExt^i(F,I),
\]
which gives the conclusion.

$(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)$ is trivial.

$(iii)\Rightarrow(i)$. We may suppose that $K$ is bounded below and has injective components. Applying the hypothesis to $F=A_X$ gives $\mathrm H^i(K)=0$ for $i>N$. Thus $K$ may be replaced by an isomorphic complex $K'$, bounded, with $K'^i$ injective for $i<N$ and $K'^i=0$ for $i>N$. One then sees that $K'^N$ is quasi-injective, which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkstar}[1.4]
The redactor does not know whether (1.3) remains true when, in condition (ii), the hypothesis ``$F\in\ob\D_c^b(X)$'' is replaced by ``$F\in\ob\D_c^+(X)$''.
\end{remarkstar}

\begin{definitionstar}[1.5]
The \emph{quasi-injective dimension} of $K$, denoted $\qinjdim(K)$, is the infimum, in $\overline{\mathbb R}$, of the integers $N$ satisfying the equivalent conditions of (1.3).
\end{definitionstar}

If $K$ has finite quasi-injective dimension, then the functor
\[
\RHom(\,\cdot\,,K):\D(X)\longrightarrow \D(X)
\]
induces a functor $\D_c^b(X)\to\D^b(X)$.

We shall see below that if $X$ is smooth of dimension $d$ over a field $k$ and $n$ is prime to the characteristic of $k$, then
\[
\qinjdim(A_X)=2d.
\]

The example of (1.2 a) shows that a complex of finite quasi-injective dimension need not be of finite injective dimension. However, one has the following result.

\begin{propositionstar}[1.6]
Let $X$ be a locally noetherian prescheme and let $K\in\ob\D^+(X)$. Suppose that there exists an integer $N$ such that $\cd_{\mathbf n}(X)\leq N$, where $\mathbf n$ is the set of prime divisors of $n$ (notation of SGA A X 1). Then, for $N'\in\Z$,
\[
\qinjdim(K)\leq N'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \injdim(K)\leq N+N'.
\]
\end{propositionstar}

The proof will use the following lemma.

\begin{lemmastar}[1.6.1]
Let $C$ be an abelian category satisfying (AB5) and possessing a family of generators $(U_i)_{i\in I}$. Let $K\in\ob\D^+(C)$ and $N\in\Z$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\injdim(K)\leq N$;
\item for every $i\in I$ and every quotient $F$ of $U_i$, one has
\[
\Ext^n(F,K)=0\qquad\text{for }n>N.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemmastar}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to prove $(ii)\Rightarrow(i)$. By a well-known argument, cf. for example [H] 1.7.6, one is reduced to showing that if $K'\in\ob(C)$ is such that $\Ext^1(F,K')=0$ whenever $F$ is a quotient of some $U_i$, then $K'$ is injective. In other words, one must prove that if every morphism from a subobject $V$ of some $U_i$ to $K'$ extends to $U_i$, then $K'$ is injective. This follows immediately from the proof of Lemma 1, p. 136 of Tohoku.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (1.6)]
Suppose $\qinjdim(K)\leq N'$ and prove that $\injdim(K)\leq N+N'$. Since the category of $A_X$-modules has as generators the $A_{U,X}$, where $U$ is étale of finite type over $X$, it is enough, by (1.6.1), to prove that
\[
\Ext^i(X;F,K)=0\qquad\text{for }i>N+N'
\]
when $F$ is a quotient of such an $A_{U,X}$, hence constructible by SGA A IX 2.9. Consider the spectral sequence
\[
E_2^{pq}=\mathrm H^p(X;\uExt^q(F,K))\Rightarrow \Ext^*(X;F,K).
\]
The hypothesis $\cd_{\mathbf n}(X)\leq N$, respectively $\qinjdim(K)\leq N'$, implies $E_2^{pq}=0$ for $p>N$, respectively for $q>N'$. Thus $E_2^{pq}=0$ for $p+q>N+N'$, whence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkstar}[1.7]
The hypothesis of (1.6) is satisfied, with $N=2d$, if $X$ is a prescheme of finite type and dimension $d$ over a separably closed field $k$, with $n$ prime to $\car(k)$; see SGA A X 4.
\end{remarkstar}

\begin{propositionstar}[1.8]
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a quasi-finite separated morphism of noetherian preschemes, and let $K\in\ob\D^+(Y)$. Then, for $N\in\Z$,
\[
\qinjdim(K)\leq N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \qinjdim(\mathrm{R}f^!K)\leq N.
\]
\end{propositionstar}

\begin{proof}
By the Main Theorem (EGA IV 8.12.6), there exists a factorization $f=uf'$, where $f'$ is an open immersion and $u$ is finite. We have $\mathrm{R}f^!=\mathrm{R}f'^!\,\mathrm{R}u^!$, which reduces us to considering separately the case of an open immersion and the case of a finite morphism. If $f$ is an open immersion, the assertion is trivial, since every constructible sheaf on $X$ is then induced by a constructible sheaf on $Y$. Suppose then that $f$ is finite, and let $F$ be a constructible $A_X$-module. The duality isomorphisms (SGA A XVIII 3.1.9.6) give
\[
f_*\uExt^i(F,\mathrm{R}f^!K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\uExt^i(f_*F,K).
\]
Since $f_*F$ is constructible by SGA A IX 2.14, it follows that if $\qinjdim(K)\leq N$, then
\[
f_*\uExt^i(F,\mathrm{R}f^!K)=0\qquad(i>N),
\]
and hence $\uExt^i(F,\mathrm{R}f^!K)=0$ for $i>N$ by SGA A VIII 5.5. This proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

Let now $X$ be a prescheme and let $K\in\ob\D^+(X)$. Denote by $\Ddual_K$ the functor
\[
\RHom(\,\cdot\,,K).
\]
We shall define, for $F\in\ob\D(X)$, a functorial homomorphism
\[
F\longrightarrow \Ddual_K\Ddual_KF
\]
(cf. [H] V.1.2). The construction works more generally on a ringed topos. One may first suppose that $K$ is made up of injectives, which permits one to ``remove the $\mathrm R$''. The identity morphism
\[
\uHom(F,K)\longrightarrow \uHom(F,K)
\]
defines, by Cartan's beloved formula, a homomorphism
\[
F\otimes\uHom(F,K)\longrightarrow K,
\]
whence, by applying the same formula a second time, a homomorphism
\[
F\longrightarrow \uHom(\uHom(F,K),K).
\]
This is the announced homomorphism, and we call it the \emph{canonical homomorphism}. We say that the pair $(F,K)$ is \emph{bidualizing}, or \emph{satisfies biduality}, or also that $F$ is \emph{reflexive} relative to $K$, if the canonical homomorphism
\[
F\longrightarrow \Ddual_K\Ddual_KF
\]
is an isomorphism.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{From now on, all preschemes considered will, unless expressly stated otherwise, be assumed locally noetherian.}

\begin{definitionstar}[1.9]
Let $X$ be a prescheme. A complex $K\in\ob\D^+(X)$ is called \emph{dualizing} if the following conditions are satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $K$ has finite quasi-injective dimension;
\item for every $F\in\ob\D_c^-(X)$, one has $\Ddual_K(F)\in\ob\D_c^+(X)$;
\item every $F\in\ob\D_c^b(X)$ is reflexive relative to $K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definitionstar}

\begin{remarkstar}[1.10]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item By a standard dévissage, truncating $F$ and using the fact that $\D_c(X)$ is triangulated, one sees that condition (ii) is equivalent to:

\smallskip
\noindent (ii bis) for every constructible $A_X$-module $F$ and every $i\in\Z$, the sheaf $\uExt^i(F,K)$ is constructible.

\item Condition (ii bis) implies $K\in\ob\D_c^+(X)$. Contrary to the example of coherent sheaves (EGA $0_{\mathrm{III}}$ 12.3.3), the converse may not be automatic. It does hold, however, see number 3, in the ``good'' cases, for example when one has resolution of singularities and purity on $X$, in particular if $X$ is an excellent prescheme of characteristic zero.

\item By dévissage on $F$, one sees that condition (iii) is equivalent to:

\smallskip
\noindent (iii bis) every constructible $A_X$-module $F$ is reflexive relative to $K$.

\item If $K\in\ob\D^+(X)$ satisfies (i) and (ii), respectively is dualizing, the functor $\Ddual_K$ induces a functor, respectively an equivalence of categories,
\[
(\D_c^b(X))^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow \D_c^b(X).
\]

\item If $K\in\ob\D^+(X)$ has finite \emph{injective} dimension, cf. 1.6.1, and satisfies (ii), then for every $F\in\ob\D_c(X)$ one has $\Ddual_K(F)\in\D_c(X)$, and $\Ddual_K$ induces a functor
\[
(\D_c^+(X))^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow \D_c^-(X).
\]
If, moreover, $K$ is dualizing, every $F\in\ob\D_c(X)$ is reflexive relative to $K$, and $\Ddual_K$ induces equivalences of categories
\[
(\D_c(X))^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\approx}\D_c(X),\qquad
(\D_c^-(X))^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\approx}\D_c^+(X),\qquad
(\D_c^+(X))^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\approx}\D_c^-(X).
\]
It is not known whether this conclusion remains valid without the restriction that $K$ have finite injective dimension.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarkstar}

We now turn to the \\emph{exchange formulae}.

We shall not return to the notion of the Tor-dimension of a complex (SGA A XVII 4.1.9).\footnote{See also SGA 6 I 5.} Let us retain only that, if $X$ is a prescheme and $G\in\ob\D^-(X)$, the relation
\[
\uTor_i(F,G)=0\qquad (i>N)
\]
for every $A_X$-module $F$ is equivalent to the same relation with $F$ constructible, thanks to SGA A IX 2.9 and to the fact that $\otimes$ commutes with direct limits. Thus there is no need to introduce a notion of ``quasi-Tor-dimension''.

\begin{lemmastar}[1.11, cf. Hartshorne II 4.3]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item Let $X$ be a prescheme, let $F\in\ob\D_c^-(X)$ and $G\in\ob\D_c^-(X)$. Then
\[
F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G\in\ob\D_c(X)
\]
if $F\in\ob\D_c^-(X)$ or if $G$ has finite Tor-dimension.\footnote{The first condition is repeated as in the working TeX source. This point should be checked against the scan during proofing.}
\item Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes, and let $G\in\ob\D_c^-(Y)$. Then $f^*G\in\ob\D_c(X)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemmastar}

\begin{proof}
Assertion b) follows trivially from SGA A IX 2.4. Let us prove a). Either of the hypotheses on $(F,G)$ implies that the spectral sequence
\[
E_2^{pq}=\sum_{q_1+q_2=q}\uTor_{-p}\bigl(\mathrm H^{q_1}(F),\mathrm H^{q_2}(G)\bigr)
\Rightarrow \mathrm H^*(F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G)
\]
is biregular. We are therefore reduced to proving a) when $F$ and $G$ are concentrated in degree $0$, that is, are constructible $A_X$-modules. Since the question is local, we may suppose $X$ noetherian. Then $F$ admits, by SGA A IX 2.7, a left resolution $F'$, where the $F'^i$ are of the form $A_{U,X}$ with $U$ étale and of finite type over $X$. We have
\[
F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G\xleftarrow{\sim}F'\otimes G,
\]
and the conclusion follows because the $A_{U,X}\otimes G$ are constructible.
\end{proof}

\begin{notationstar}[1.11.1]
If $X$ is a prescheme, we shall denote by $\D^b(X)_{\mathrm{torf}}$ (respectively $\D^b(X)_{\mathrm{qinjf}}$) the full subcategory of $\D(X)$ consisting of objects of finite Tor-dimension (respectively finite quasi-injective dimension).
\end{notationstar}

\begin{propositionstar}[1.12, cf. Hartshorne V 2.6]
Let $X$ be a prescheme and let $K\in\ob\D^+(X)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item For $F\in\ob\D^-(X)$ and $G\in\ob\D^-(X)$, respectively for $F\in\ob\D(X)$ and $G\in\ob\D^b(X)_{\mathrm{torf}}$, there is a functorial isomorphism
\[
\Ddual_K(F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\RHom(F,\Ddual_KG).
\]
\item If $K$ satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) of (1.9), one has the implication
\[
G\in\ob\D_c^b(X)_{\mathrm{torf}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Ddual_KG\in\ob\D_c^b(X)_{\mathrm{qinjf}}\text{ and }\Ddual_KG\text{ satisfies (1.9)(ii).}
\]
\item If $K$ is dualizing, there is, for $F\in\ob\D_c^-(X)$ and $G\in\ob\D_c^b(X)$, respectively for $F\in\ob\D(X)$, $G\in\ob\D_c^b(X)$, and $\Ddual_KG\in\ob\D_c^b(X)_{\mathrm{torf}}$, a functorial isomorphism
\[
\Ddual_K\bigl(F\otimes^{\mathrm L}\Ddual_KG\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\RHom(F,G).
\]
Moreover one has the implication
\[
\Ddual_KG\in\ob\D_c^b(X)_{\mathrm{torf}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
G\in\ob\D_c^b(X)_{\mathrm{qinjf}}\text{ and }G\text{ satisfies (1.9)(ii).}
\]
\item If $K$ is dualizing and of finite injective dimension, the implications in b) and c) are equivalences.
\end{enumerate}
\end{propositionstar}

\begin{proof}
a) is none other than Cartan's adjunction formula (SGA 6 I 7.4).

b) Let $G\in\ob\D_c^b(X)_{\mathrm{torf}}$. As $F$ ranges over constructible $A_X$-modules, the cohomology objects of $F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G$ are constructible by (1.11) and vanish outside two fixed bounds independent of $F$. Therefore the same is true for the cohomology objects of $\Ddual_K(F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G)$, and the result follows from a).

c) The first assertion follows from formula a), applied to $\Ddual_KG$ in place of $G$, and from the reflexivity of $G$. The second is deduced by an argument analogous to b).

d) Let us prove, for example, the converse implication in b). Suppose that $\Ddual_KG\in\ob\D_c^b(X)_{\mathrm{qinjf}}$ and that $\Ddual_KG$ satisfies (1.9)(ii). Then, as $F$ ranges over constructible modules, the cohomology objects of $\Ddual_K(F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G)$ are constructible and vanish outside two fixed bounds. Thus the same is true for the cohomology objects of
\[
\Ddual_K\Ddual_K(F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G),
\]
but
\[
F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_K\Ddual_K(F\otimes^{\mathrm L}G)
\]
since $K$ is dualizing and of finite injective dimension, cf. (1.10 e). Hence $G$ has finite Tor-dimension. The converse implication in c) is proved similarly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkstar}[1.12.1]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item The redactor does not know whether assertion d) of (1.12) is valid under the sole hypothesis that $K$ is dualizing.
\item One expresses the preceding proposition figuratively by saying that the dualizing functor $\Ddual_K$ exchanges the functors $\otimes^{\mathrm L}$ and $\RHom$, and also the notions of finite Tor-dimension and finite quasi-injective dimension. This exchange is, moreover, fully satisfactory only when $K$ has finite injective dimension.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarkstar}

\begin{propositionstar}[1.13]
Let $Y$ be a noetherian prescheme, let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of finite type, and let $K_Y\in\ob\D^+(Y)$. Suppose that $n$ is prime to the residue characteristics of $Y$, and put
\[
K_X=\mathrm Rf^!K_Y\qquad\text{(SGA A XVIII 3.1)},\qquad
\Ddual_X=\Ddual_{K_X},\quad \Ddual_Y=\Ddual_{K_Y}.
\]
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item There is, for $F\in\ob\D^-(X)$, a functorial isomorphism
\[
\text{(i)}\qquad \mathrm Rf_*\Ddual_X(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_Y\,\mathrm Rf_*F.
\]
If, moreover, $K_X$ and $K_Y$ are dualizing,\footnote{We shall see below that, under fairly general conditions, $K_Y$ dualizing implies $K_X$ dualizing.} there is, for $F\in\ob\D_c^b(X)$, a functorial isomorphism
\[
\text{(ii)}\qquad \mathrm Rf_!\Ddual_X(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_Y\,\mathrm Rf_*F.
\]
\item There is, for $F\in\D_c^-(Y)$, a functorial isomorphism\footnote{If the functor $\mathrm Rf^!$ has finite cohomological dimension, as is the case for example when $Y$ is of finite type over a field (SGA A X 4.3 and XVIII 3.1.7), the isomorphism b)(i) is valid for $F\in\ob\D_c(Y)$.}
\[
\text{(i)}\qquad \mathrm Rf^!\Ddual_Y(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_X f^*F.
\]
If, moreover, $K_X$ and $K_Y$ are dualizing, there is, for $F\in\D_c^b(Y)$, a functorial isomorphism
\[
\text{(ii)}\qquad f^*\Ddual_YF\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_X\mathrm Rf^!F.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{propositionstar}

\begin{proof}
a)(i) is none other than the duality isomorphism (SGA A XVIII 3.1.9.6).

a)(ii) is obtained by applying $\Ddual_Y$ to the two sides of a)(i), written for the argument $\Ddual_XF$, and by using the finiteness theorem (SGA A XVII 5.3.6), which asserts that
\[
\mathrm Rf_*\Ddual_X(F)\in\ob\D_c(Y).
\]

b)(i) is none other than the ``induction formula'' (SGA A XVIII 3.1.12.2).

b)(ii) is obtained by applying $\Ddual_X$ to the two sides of b)(i), written for the argument $\Ddual_YF$, and using (1.12 b).
\end{proof}

\begin{remarkstar}[Scholium 1.13.1]
One may say, figuratively, that the dualizing functors $\Ddual_X$ and $\Ddual_Y$ exchange the usual and unusual direct-image functors, respectively inverse-image functors:
\[
\mathrm Rf_*\quad\text{and}\quad \mathrm Rf_!,
\qquad
f^*\quad\text{and}\quad \mathrm Rf^!.
\]
\end{remarkstar}

\begin{corollarystar}[1.14]
Under the hypotheses of 1.13, suppose further that $f$ is proper, and let $F\in\ob\D^-(X)$. If $(F,K_X)$ is bidualizing, then $(\mathrm Rf_*F,K_Y)$ is bidualizing. Conversely, if $f$ is finite and $(\mathrm Rf_*F,K_Y)$ is bidualizing, then $(F,K_X)$ is bidualizing.
\end{corollarystar}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $(F,K_X)$ is bidualizing. Applying $\mathrm Rf_*$ to the canonical isomorphism $F\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_X^2F$, one obtains
\begin{align*}
\mathrm Rf_*F
&\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm Rf_*\Ddual_X\Ddual_XF\\
&\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_Y\mathrm Rf_*\Ddual_XF \qquad\text{by a)(i)}\\
&\xrightarrow{\sim}\Ddual_Y\Ddual_Y\mathrm Rf_*F \qquad\text{by a)(i).}
\end{align*}
The courageous reader will convince himself that the isomorphism obtained is the canonical one; this proves the first assertion.

Now suppose $f$ finite and $(f_*F,K_Y)$ bidualizing, since $\mathrm Rf_*=f_*$ for $f$ finite. We prove that the canonical morphism
\[
F\longrightarrow \Ddual_X^2F
\]
is an isomorphism. By Lemma 1.14.1 a) below, it is enough to show that the induced morphism
\[
f_*F\longrightarrow f_*\Ddual_X^2F
\]
is an isomorphism. But, by applying a)(i) twice, one convinces oneself that this latter morphism is none other than the canonical isomorphism
\[
f_*F\longrightarrow \Ddual_Y^2f_*F,
\]
which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemmastar}[1.14.1]
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a finite morphism of preschemes.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item Let $u:F\to G$ be an arrow of $\D(X)$. Then $u$ is an isomorphism if and only if $f_*u$ is an isomorphism.
\item Let $F\in\ob\D(X)$. Then $F\in\ob\D_c(X)$ if and only if $f_*F\in\ob\D_c(Y)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemmastar}

\begin{proof}
a) Since the functor $f_*$ is exact, one may restrict to the case where $F$ and $G$ are concentrated in degree zero. The assertion then follows immediately from the computation of the fibers of $f_*F$, respectively $f_*G$, in SGA A VIII 5.5.

b) One may again restrict to the case where $F$ is concentrated in degree zero. Necessity is known by SGA A IX 2.14. Let us prove sufficiency, that is, that $f_*F$ constructible implies $F$ constructible. The question is local above, and hence a fortiori below, so we may suppose $Y$, and therefore $X$, affine. There then exists, by EGA IV 17.16.6, a finite family $(Y_i)$ of affine subpreschemes of $Y$, pairwise disjoint and with union $Y$, such that if $X_i=X\times_YY_i$, the induced morphism
\[
f_i:X_i\longrightarrow Y_i
\]
factors as
\[
X_i\xrightarrow{u_i}X'_i\xrightarrow{v_i}Y_i,
\]
where $u_i$ is finite, radicial, and surjective, and $v_i$ is finite étale. Put $F|_{X_i}=F_i$. By the base-change theorem for a finite morphism (SGA A VIII 5.5), one has
\[
f_{i*}F_i\xrightarrow{\sim}(f_*F)|_{Y_i},
\]
and one is reduced, by SGA A IX 2.8, to proving the assertion for $(f_i,F_i)$. Put $F'_i=u_{i*}F_i$, so that $f_{i*}F_i=v_{i*}F'_i$. It is clear from the definition of constructibility, respectively follows from SGA A VIII 1.1, that $F_i$, respectively $F'_i$, is constructible if and only if $F'_i$, respectively $v_{i*}F'_i$, is constructible. This gives the desired conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollarystar}[1.15]
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a quasi-finite separated morphism of noetherian preschemes. Let $K_Y\in\ob\D^+(Y)$, and put, as in 1.13,
\[
K_X=\mathrm Rf^!K_Y,
\qquad
\Ddual_X=\Ddual_{K_X},
\qquad
\Ddual_Y=\Ddual_{K_Y}.
\]
If $K_Y$ is dualizing, then $K_X$ is dualizing.
\end{corollarystar}

\begin{proof}
We already know from (1.8) that if $K_Y$ has finite quasi-injective dimension, then so does $K_X$. Let us show that, if $K_Y$ satisfies condition (ii), respectively (iii), of (1.9), then $K_X$ satisfies it as well. By the Main Theorem (EGA IV 8.12.6), $f$ factors as $f=f'i$, where $i$ is an open immersion and $f'$ is finite. Since
\[
\mathrm Rf^!\simeq \mathrm Ri^!\,\mathrm Rf'^!,
\]
one must therefore consider separately the case of an open immersion and that of a finite morphism. If $f$ is an open immersion, the assertion is trivial, since a constructible sheaf on $X$ is induced by a constructible sheaf on $Y$, and the formation of $\RHom$ commutes with restriction to an open. Suppose then that $f$ is finite. We know from (1.14) that if $K_Y$ satisfies (iii), then so does $K_X$, taking account of the finiteness theorem SGA A IX 2.14. It remains to prove that, if $K_Y$ satisfies (ii), then so does $K_X$. Let $F\in\ob\D_c^-(X)$; we must show that $\Ddual_XF\in\ob\D_c^+(X)$. By the finiteness theorem, $f_*F\in\ob\D_c^-(Y)$, hence by hypothesis $\Ddual_Yf_*F\in\ob\D_c^+(Y)$. But
\[
\Ddual_Yf_*F\xrightarrow{\sim}f_*\Ddual_XF
\]
by (1.13 a)(i), and the conclusion follows from (1.14.1 b).
\end{proof}
