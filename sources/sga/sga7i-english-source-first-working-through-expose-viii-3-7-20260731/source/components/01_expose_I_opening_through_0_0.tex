0. \underline{Preliminaries}

0.0. \underline{Terminology}. Recall the following definitions:

(0.0.1) \underline{trait}: the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring. The generic and closed points of a trait are often denoted by $\eta$ and $s$.

(0.0.2) \underline{strictly local}: for a ring, local henselian with separably closed residue field; for a scheme, the spectrum of such a ring.

(0.0.3) \underline{geometric point of $S$} (in this expos\'e): a morphism $\bar{x}:\operatorname{Spec}(\bar{k})\longrightarrow S$ with image $x\in S$, where $k(\bar{x})\underset{\mathrm{dfn}}{=}\bar{k}$ is a separable closure of $k(x)$. For a henselian trait $S=\operatorname{Spec}(V)$, a generic geometric point of $S$ (that is, one with image $\eta$) is often denoted by $\bar{\eta}$. The residue field of the valuation ring $\bar{V}$, the integral closure of $V$ in $k(\bar{\eta})$, is a separably closed algebraic extension of $k(s)$ and defines a geometric point $\bar{s}$ of $S$ centered at $s$.

(0.0.4) \underline{strict localization of $X$ at the geometric point $\bar{x}$}: SGA 4 VIII 4.4.

(0.0.5) \underline{essentially of finite type over $S$}: for a local (respectively, henselian local; respectively, strictly local) $S$-scheme, the spectrum of a local ring (respectively, the henselization of a local ring; respectively, a strict localization) of a scheme of finite type over $S$.

(0.0.6) $F\longmapsto F(n)$: Tate twist.

(0.0.7) An endomorphism $T$ of a finite-dimensional vector space is \underline{quasi-unipotent} if its eigenvalues are roots of unity, that is, if there exist $N\geqslant 1$ and $M\geqslant 1$ such that $(T^N-1)^M=0$.
