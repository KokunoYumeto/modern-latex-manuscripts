\section*{Exposé VII. Biextensions of sheaves of groups, continued}

\subsection*{2.3. Morphisms of biextensions}

Let \(E\) be a biextension of \((P,Q)\) by \(G\), and likewise let \(E'\) be a
biextension of \((P',Q')\) by \(G'\), where \(P',Q'\), and \(G'\) are again
commutative groups of the topos \(\cT\). A \emph{morphism of biextensions}
from \(E\) to \(E'\) is a system
\[
(u,v,w,f)
\]
where
\[
u:P\longrightarrow P',\qquad v:Q\longrightarrow Q',
        \qquad w:G\longrightarrow G'
\]
are homomorphisms of groups, and where
\[
f:E\longrightarrow E'
\]
is a morphism of the underlying sheaves of sets, such that \(f\) is at the same
time a homomorphism of extensions over \(P\), respectively over \(P'\), associated
with the corresponding homomorphisms of relative groups, and a homomorphism of
extensions over \(Q\), respectively over \(Q'\), associated with the corresponding
homomorphisms of relative groups. In other words the two group-extension
structures carried by a biextension must be respected simultaneously.

The maps \(u,v,w\) are already determined by \(f\). Passing to the quotient
recovers \(u\times v\), hence \(u\) and \(v\) by restriction to
\(P\times 1_Q\) and \(1_P\times Q\). Restricting \(f\) to the copy of \(G\)
through the neutral section recovers \(w\). Thus no ambiguity results from
identifying the morphism \((u,v,w,f)\) with the single map \(f\).

With the usual precaution of recording source and target, biextensions in
\(\cT\) are therefore the objects of a category. We denote it by
\(\BIEXT(\cT)\). The assignment
\[
(E;P,Q,G)\longmapsto (P,Q,G)
\]
defines a canonical functor
\[
\BIEXT(\cT)\longrightarrow
\GRAB(\cT)\times\GRAB(\cT)\times\GRAB(\cT),
\]
where \(\GRAB(\cT)\) denotes the category of abelian groups in \(\cT\). It is
often useful to decompose this functor into the two functors
\[
E\longmapsto (P,Q),\qquad E\longmapsto G.
\]

\subsection*{2.4. The category \(\BIEXT(P,Q;G)\) with \(P,Q,G\) variable}

The first of the two preceding functors is fibred, and the second is cofibrant.
Concretely, if \(E\) is a biextension of \((P,Q)\) by \(G\), and if
\[
u:P_1\to P,\qquad v:Q_1\to Q
\]
are homomorphisms of abelian groups, then there is a biextension \(E'\) of
\((P_1,Q_1)\) by \(G\) and a morphism of biextensions
\[
E'\longrightarrow E
\]
compatible with \(u,v,\id_G\), having the usual universal property. This
biextension is the inverse image of \(E\) by \((u,v)\).

Dually, if \(w:G\to G'\) is a homomorphism of abelian groups, there is a
biextension \(E'\) of \((P,Q)\) by \(G'\) and a morphism of biextensions
\[
E\longrightarrow E'
\]
compatible with \(\id_P,\id_Q,w\), again characterized by its universal property.
It is the push-out of \(E\) by \(w\). These constructions are obtained by
paraphrasing the corresponding constructions for ordinary extensions of groups.

For fixed \(P,Q,G\) we write
\[
\BIEXT(P,Q;G)
\]
for the fibre category over the triple \((P,Q,G)\). The preceding description
gives the usual functorialities:
\[
(u,v)^*:\BIEXT(P,Q;G)\to \BIEXT(P_1,Q_1;G),
\]
and
\[
w_*:\BIEXT(P,Q;G)\to \BIEXT(P,Q;G').
\]
These are compatible in the evident sense.

\subsection*{2.5. The structure of \(\BIEXT(P,Q;G)\)}

For fixed \(P,Q,G\), the category \(\BIEXT(P,Q;G)\) is a strictly commutative
Picard category. The sum of two biextensions is defined by taking the contracted
product over \(P\times Q\):
\[
E+E'=(E\times_{P\times Q}E')/G,
\]
where \(G\) is embedded diagonally with the usual opposite action on one factor.
The two extension laws on \(E\) and \(E'\) induce the two required extension laws
on \(E+E'\), and the compatibility axiom for a biextension is inherited from the
corresponding axioms for \(E\) and \(E'\).

The neutral object is the trivial biextension
\[
P\times Q\times G
\]
with its evident partial addition laws. The inverse of a biextension is obtained
by replacing the \(G\)-torsor \(E\) by its opposite torsor. Associativity and
commutativity are inherited from the corresponding facts for contracted products
of torsors. Thus the isomorphism classes form an abelian group, denoted
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;G).
\]

The automorphism group of the neutral biextension is also useful. An automorphism
of the trivial biextension over \(P\times Q\) is given by a morphism
\[
P\times Q\longrightarrow G.
\]
The condition that it respect both extension structures says precisely that this
morphism is additive separately in \(P\) and in \(Q\). Thus
\[
\operatorname{Aut}_{\BIEXT(P,Q;G)}(0)
        \simeq \operatorname{Bilin}(P,Q;G),
\]
the group of biadditive morphisms \(P\times Q\to G\). We denote this group by
\[
\Biext^0(P,Q;G).
\]

The two groups \(\Biext^0\) and \(\Biext^1\) behave functorially in all three
arguments. Pulling back in \(P\) and \(Q\), and pushing out in \(G\), gives the
corresponding homomorphisms of Picard categories and hence of their homotopy
groups.

\subsection*{2.6. Opposite and transposed biextensions}

Let \(E\) be a biextension of \((P,Q)\) by \(G\). One may form the opposite
biextension, obtained by replacing the \(G\)-torsor by its inverse. It represents
the negative of the class of \(E\) in \(\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\).

One may also transpose the two variables. The transposed biextension
\[
{}^tE
\]
is a biextension of \((Q,P)\) by \(G\), obtained by pulling \(E\) back along the
symmetry \(Q\times P\simeq P\times Q\) and interchanging the two extension
structures. This operation is involutive and is compatible with inverse images
and direct images.

\subsection*{2.7. Symmetric biextension associated with a given biextension}

The sum of \(E\) with its transpose, when the two variables are identified, gives
the natural symmetric construction. More generally, whenever a symmetry condition
is imposed on \(P\) and \(Q\), a biextension may be equipped with an isomorphism
to its transpose satisfying the usual cocycle condition. This gives the category
of symmetric biextensions. In the applications to pairings and to duality, this
is the construction which records the symmetry or alternating behaviour of the
pairing arising from a biextension.

\subsection*{2.8. Change of topos. Localization}

Let \(u:\cT'\to\cT\) be a morphism of topoi. If \(E\) is a biextension of
\((P,Q)\) by \(G\) in \(\cT\), then \(u^*E\) is a biextension of
\((u^*P,u^*Q)\) by \(u^*G\) in \(\cT'\). This construction is functorial and
respects sums, inverses, and transposition.

If \(X\) is an object of \(\cT\), the localization \(\cT/X\) gives a special
case. The restriction of \(E\) to \(X\) is a biextension of the restrictions of
\(P\) and \(Q\) by the restriction of \(G\). Conversely, assertions about
biextensions which are local for the topology of \(\cT\) may be checked after
such localizations. This is used repeatedly below when questions of existence or
uniqueness are reduced to the case of a torsor admitting a section.

The formation of \(\Biext^i(P,Q;G)\) is therefore compatible with change of
topos in the expected way:
\[
u^*\Biext^i(P,Q;G)\longrightarrow
\Biext^i(u^*P,u^*Q;u^*G).
\]
For \(i=0\) this is the usual pullback of biadditive morphisms; for \(i=1\) it is
obtained by pulling back representatives.

\subsection*{2.9. Relation with bitrivialized torsors}

A biextension may be described equivalently as a torsor over \(P\times Q\) under
\(G\), together with two compatible partial group laws. If one chooses
trivializations along the two axes \(P\times 1_Q\) and \(1_P\times Q\), agreeing
at the neutral section, the remaining datum is often encoded by a torsor equipped
with two rigidifications. This is the bitrivialized-torsor description.

This point of view is especially useful for explicit computations. Given a
\(G\)-torsor \(E\) over \(P\times Q\), a rigidification along \(P\times 1_Q\)
and one along \(1_P\times Q\) allow one to speak of the defect of additivity in
each variable. The compatibility condition for a biextension says exactly that
these defects are themselves compatible; in a Čech description it is the usual
two-variable cocycle condition.

The equivalence between the two descriptions is natural in \(P,Q,G\), compatible
with change of topos, and preserves the Picard structure.

\subsection*{2.10. Variants of the notion of biextension}

The definition admits evident variants. One may replace abelian groups by group
objects subject to commutativity restrictions sufficient for the two partial
extension structures to commute. One may also introduce rigidified, symmetrized,
or alternating biextensions, according to whether additional trivializations or
symmetry constraints are imposed.

There are also relative variants, in which \(P,Q,G\) are objects over a base
object of the topos. They are obtained by applying the preceding definitions in
the localized topos. All these variants are stable under pullback and push-out
and will be used without further comment when the context requires them.

\section*{3. Complements of homological algebra}

\subsection*{3.1. The additive cofibration \(\cF_{L_\bullet}\)}

Let \(\cC\) be an additive category in which the extensions under consideration
are defined, and let
\[
L_\bullet:\quad \cdots\longrightarrow L_2\longrightarrow L_1
        \longrightarrow L_0
\]
be a chain complex of objects of \(\cC\). To \(L_\bullet\) one associates a
cofibred additive category
\[
\cF_{L_\bullet}
\]
over \(\cC\). The fibre over an object \(A\) is the category of pairs consisting
of an extension
\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow L_0\longrightarrow 0
\]
together with a lifting of the differential \(L_1\to L_0\) to \(X\), subject to
the condition imposed by the next differential \(L_2\to L_1\).

More explicitly, an object of the fibre over \(A\) is a pair \((X,\alpha)\),
where \(X\) is an extension of \(L_0\) by \(A\) and
\[
\alpha:L_1\longrightarrow X
\]
satisfies
\[
j\alpha=d_1,\qquad \alpha d_2=0
\]
with \(j:X\to L_0\) the quotient morphism. Morphisms are morphisms of extensions
compatible with \(\alpha\). The resulting category is additive and cofibred in
\(A\), by push-out of extensions.

There is a canonical interpretation of its connected components and automorphism
groups in terms of Ext groups:
\[
\pi_0\cF_{L_\bullet}(A)\simeq \Ext^1(L_\bullet,A),\qquad
\pi_1\cF_{L_\bullet}(A)\simeq \Ext^0(L_\bullet,A),
\]
where the Ext groups are computed from the complex \(L_\bullet\).

\subsection*{3.2. Determination of \(\cF^1_{L_\bullet}(A)\)}

The group \(\cF^1_{L_\bullet}(A)\) is the group of isomorphism classes of objects
of the fibre over \(A\). The preceding description shows that it is exactly the
group of extensions of the complex \(L_\bullet\) by \(A\), modulo the usual
equivalence relation. In formulas,
\[
\cF^1_{L_\bullet}(A)\simeq \Ext^1(L_\bullet,A).
\]
Under this correspondence, the Baer sum of extensions agrees with the addition
in \(\Ext^1\). Likewise, automorphisms of the zero object correspond to
\[
\Hom(H_0(L_\bullet),A)
\]
or, more generally, to \(\Ext^0(L_\bullet,A)\) in the derived sense.

The construction is compatible with morphisms of complexes. A morphism
\(L'_\bullet\to L_\bullet\) gives an inverse-image functor
\[
\cF_{L_\bullet}\longrightarrow \cF_{L'_\bullet},
\]
and the induced maps on \(\cF^i\) are the usual maps on Ext groups.

\subsection*{3.3. Exactness properties of \(\cF_{L_\bullet}\)}

Suppose a morphism of complexes
\[
L'_\bullet\longrightarrow L_\bullet
\]
induces isomorphisms on \(H_0\) and \(H_1\). Then for every \(A\) the induced
functor
\[
\cF_{L_\bullet}(A)\longrightarrow \cF_{L'_\bullet}(A)
\]
is an equivalence of categories. This is the categorical form of the fact that
extensions of degree \(0\) and \(1\) depend only on the truncation of the complex
through \(H_1\).

For a short exact sequence of complexes
\[
0\longrightarrow L'_\bullet\longrightarrow L_\bullet
        \longrightarrow L''_\bullet\longrightarrow 0,
\]
one obtains the exact sequence
\[
0\to \Ext^0(L''_\bullet,A)\to \Ext^0(L_\bullet,A)
\to \Ext^0(L'_\bullet,A)
\to \Ext^1(L''_\bullet,A)\to \Ext^1(L_\bullet,A)\to\cdots .
\]
The connecting homomorphism admits a direct categorical description: an element
of \(\Ext^0(L'_\bullet,A)\), viewed as an automorphism of the neutral object in
\(\cF_{L'_\bullet}(A)\), measures the obstruction to descending the corresponding
trivialized object along the exact sequence of complexes. This obstruction is the
image in \(\Ext^1(L''_\bullet,A)\).

The sign conventions are those of the derived category. Since only the resulting
Picard categories and exactness assertions will be used later, the possible
ambiguities in signs do not affect the applications that follow.

\subsection*{3.4. Variance of \(\cF_{L_\bullet}\) with respect to \(L_\bullet\)}

Let
\[
0\to L'_\bullet\to L_\bullet\to L''_\bullet\to 0
\]
be an exact sequence of complexes. The functor
\[
\cF_{L_\bullet}\longrightarrow \cF_{[L'_\bullet\to L''_\bullet]}
\]
which sends an extension of \(L_\bullet\) to its restriction to \(L'_\bullet\),
equipped with the induced trivialization after pullback to \(L''_\bullet\), is an
equivalence of categories. Here
\(\cF_{[L'_\bullet\to L''_\bullet]}\) denotes the category of objects of
\(\cF_{L'_\bullet}\) together with a trivialization of their inverse image.

Indeed, an object in the target consists of an extension
\[
0\to A\to X'\to L'_0\to 0
\]
together with the auxiliary maps encoding the liftings from \(L'_1\) and
\(L''_1\), satisfying the evident relations. These data combine uniquely into an
object of \(\cF_{L_\bullet}\), and the construction is inverse to restriction.
This proves the asserted equivalence and gives the left-exactness in the
complex-variable which complements the left-exactness in \(A\).

The preceding equivalence identifies the connecting homomorphism in the long
exact Ext sequence with the geometric operation of taking the object whose
trivialization fails to descend. This is the compatibility needed below.

\subsection*{3.5. A canonical partial flat resolution of an arbitrary \(\bZ\)-module}

We apply the preceding results to the category of commutative groups of the topos
\(\cT\), that is, to the category of \(\bZ\)-modules of \(\cT\). Let \(P\) be
such a group. Define a chain complex \(L_\bullet(P)\), augmented toward \(P\), by
\[
0\longrightarrow \bZ[P\times P\times P]
\xrightarrow{d_2}\bZ[P\times P]
\xrightarrow{d_1}\bZ[P]
\xrightarrow{\epsilon}P\longrightarrow 0.
\]
For a section \(p\) of \(P\), write \([p]\) for the corresponding section of the
free module \(\bZ[P]\), and use analogous notation \([p,q]\) and \([p,q,r]\).
Then
\[
\epsilon[p]=p,
\]
\[
d_1[p,q]=[p+q]-[p]-[q],
\]
and
\[
d_2[p,q,r]=[p+q,r]-[p,q+r]+[p,r]-[q,r].
\]
These formulas define a complex:
\[
\epsilon d_1=0,\qquad d_1d_2=0.
\]
The first identity is immediate. The second decomposes into the identities
expressing commutativity and associativity of the group law on \(P\).

The construction is functorial in \(P\). Each component \(L_i(P)\) is a free
\(\bZ\)-module, hence flat.

\begin{proposition}
One has \(H_1(L_\bullet(P))=0\), and the augmentation induces an isomorphism
\[
H_0(L_\bullet(P))\xrightarrow{\sim}P.
\]
\end{proposition}

It is enough to prove that for every commutative group \(G\) of \(\cT\), the
functor
\[
\cF_{L_\bullet(P)}(G)\longrightarrow \cF_P(G)
\]
is an equivalence. The left hand side identifies with the category of extensions
of the \(\bZ\)-module \(P\) by \(G\). Interpreting extensions of the free modules
\(\bZ[P]\), \(\bZ[P\times P]\), and \(\bZ[P\times P\times P]\) as torsors over
\(P\), \(P\times P\), and \(P\times P\times P\), respectively, an object of the
right hand side is a \(G_P\)-torsor on \(P\) together with a trivialization of its
pullback to \(P\times P\), satisfying the compatibility imposed by \(d_1d_2=0\).
This is exactly the classical description of an extension of the group object
\(P\) by \(G\). The two constructions are inverse to one another.

\subsection*{3.6. Application to the cofibred category of biextensions of a fixed pair}

Let \(P,Q,G\) be commutative groups in \(\cT\). Apply the preceding construction
to both \(P\) and \(Q\), and set
\[
L_\bullet(P,Q)=L_\bullet(P)\otimes L_\bullet(Q).
\]
The complex \(L_\bullet(P,Q)\) is a flat resolution, in the relevant low degrees,
of \(P\otimes^{\bL}Q\). The general formalism yields a Picard category
\[
\cF_{L_\bullet(P,Q)}(G).
\]

The category \(\BIEXT(P,Q;G)\) is canonically equivalent to this Picard category.
Under this equivalence, the class of a biextension corresponds to the class of the
associated extension datum for the complex \(L_\bullet(P,Q)\). Therefore
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\simeq
\Ext^1(P\otimes^{\bL}Q,G),
\]
and
\[
\Biext^0(P,Q;G)\simeq
\Ext^0(P\otimes^{\bL}Q,G).
\]
The latter is precisely the group of biadditive maps \(P\times Q\to G\).

This description is functorial in \(G\), by push-out, and in \(P,Q\), by
pullback. It is compatible with the Picard structure and with change of topos.

\subsection*{3.7. Various compatibilities}

The equivalence just described is compatible with the usual exactness properties
in each variable. For an exact sequence
\[
0\to P'\to P\to P''\to 0
\]
and fixed \(Q,G\), the sequence of Picard categories of biextensions is
left-exact. On isomorphism classes and automorphism groups it gives the beginning
of the long exact Ext sequence associated with
\[
0\to P'\otimes^{\bL}Q\to P\otimes^{\bL}Q\to
P''\otimes^{\bL}Q\to 0.
\]
Equivalently, a biextension of \((P,Q)\) whose inverse image to \((P',Q)\) is
trivial has an obstruction class in the group of biextensions of
\((P'',Q)\).

Similarly, exactness in \(Q\) is obtained by symmetry, and exactness in \(G\) is
the exactness of push-outs of extensions. The connecting morphisms are the
boundary maps in the corresponding Ext sequences.

Thus the geometrically defined group \(\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\) is expressed as the
simple homological invariant \(\Ext^1(P\otimes^{\bL}Q,G)\), and all the natural
operations on biextensions are the operations induced on Ext.

\subsection*{3.8. Biextensions of \((P,Q)\) trivialized on extensions of \(P\) and \(Q\)}

Consider two exact sequences of abelian groups
\[
0\to L_1\xrightarrow{\partial} L_0\xrightarrow{u}P\to 0,\qquad
0\to M_1\xrightarrow{\partial} M_0\xrightarrow{v}Q\to 0.
\]
We describe the category of biextensions \(E\) of \((P,Q)\) by an abelian group
\(G\) whose pullback \((u,v)^*E\) is trivial. A trivialization is a section
\[
s:L_0\times M_0\longrightarrow (u,v)^*E
\]
additive in each variable.

Once \(s\) is chosen, the two partial restrictions of the trivial biextension
give homomorphisms
\[
f:L_1\otimes M_0\to G,\qquad
g:L_0\otimes M_1\to G.
\]
They must agree on \(L_1\otimes M_1\), in the sense that
\[
f(x_1\otimes \partial y_1)=g(\partial x_1\otimes y_1).
\]
Conversely, such a pair \((f,g)\) reconstructs the required descent datum for the
trivialized biextension.

Changing the trivialization by a bilinear form
\[
h:L_0\otimes M_0\to G
\]
changes \(f\) and \(g\) by the restrictions of \(h\):
\[
f'(x_1\otimes y_0)=f(x_1\otimes y_0)+h(\partial x_1\otimes y_0),
\]
\[
g'(x_0\otimes y_1)=g(x_0\otimes y_1)+h(x_0\otimes \partial y_1).
\]

\begin{proposition}
The subgroup of \(\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\) consisting of classes whose pullback to
\((L_0,M_0)\) is trivial is canonically isomorphic to the quotient of the group
of pairs \((f,g)\), with
\[
f:L_1\otimes M_0\to G,\qquad g:L_0\otimes M_1\to G,
\]
satisfying the compatibility condition on \(L_1\otimes M_1\), by the subgroup of
pairs obtained by restricting a form \(h:L_0\otimes M_0\to G\).
\end{proposition}

Interpreting the two exact sequences as complexes
\[
L_\bullet:\;0\to L_1\to L_0\to 0,\qquad
M_\bullet:\;0\to M_1\to M_0\to 0,
\]
their tensor product is
\[
0\to L_1\otimes M_1\to
L_1\otimes M_0\oplus L_0\otimes M_1
\to L_0\otimes M_0\to 0.
\]
The preceding quotient is precisely
\[
H^1\bigl(\Hom^\bullet(L_\bullet\otimes M_\bullet,G)\bigr).
\]
The canonical morphism
\[
L_\bullet\otimes M_\bullet\longrightarrow P\otimes^{\bL}Q
\]
therefore induces
\[
H^1\bigl(\Hom^\bullet(L_\bullet\otimes M_\bullet,G)\bigr)
\longrightarrow
\Ext^1(P\otimes^{\bL}Q,G),
\]
and the isomorphism with \(\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\) sends the class described by
\((f,g)\) to this image.

In the important special case
\[
L_0=\bZ[P],\qquad M_0=\bZ[Q],
\]
with \(L_1\) and \(M_1\) the kernels of the augmentations to \(P\) and \(Q\),
the data \((f,g)\) can be written explicitly as functions
\[
P\times P\times Q\to G,\qquad P\times Q\times Q\to G.
\]
The five compatibility conditions are exactly the five axioms which define the
two partial group laws of a biextension. Thus the explicit cocycle description
and the intrinsic definition agree.

\subsection*{0. Introduction}

We continue the general study of biextensions begun in the preceding exposé. We
first add several complements in the case of an arbitrary base topos, developing
in particular the formalism of pairings associated with biextensions. This
formalism is well known to be important in the theory of abelian schemes. We then
begin the study of biextensions of general group schemes. The case of greatest
interest for the sequel is that of biextensions by the multiplicative group
\(\Gm\); nevertheless, as technical intermediates, we shall also study
biextensions by other types of group schemes.

The most useful result for what follows is the criterion of Section 7: under
suitable hypotheses, if \(P\) and \(Q\) are smooth over a trait with generic point
\(\eta\), a biextension of \((P_\eta,Q_\eta)\) by \(\Gm\) extends to a
biextension of \((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\), uniquely up to a unique isomorphism. This
will be convenient in the following exposé, which is devoted to the Néron model
of abelian varieties. It is likely that one could proceed there by a shorter
route; nevertheless the developments of Exposés VII and VIII are expected to be
useful elsewhere as well.

When working with group schemes over a scheme \(S\), the ambient topos is the
fppf topos of \(S\), associated with the site of schemes locally of finite
presentation over \(S\), endowed with the faithfully flat topology. The word
``local'' without further qualification means local for this topology.

Most statements about extensions or biextensions by \(\Gm\) remain valid with
\(\Gm\) replaced by a torus \(G\). Since such a torus is locally isomorphic, for
the fppf topology and even for the étale topology, to a power \(\Gm^r\), the
proofs are obtained by descent.

\subsection*{1. Various special cases over an arbitrary topos}

In this section, as in Exposé VII, \(T\) is a topos and \(P,Q,G\) are abelian
groups in \(T\). We use the canonical isomorphism
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\simeq
\Ext^1(P\otimes^{\bL}Q,G).
\]
It gives immediate vanishing and comparison results in a number of special
situations.

If
\[
\Ext^1(P\otimes Q,G)=0
\]
and
\[
\Hom(\Tor_1(P,Q),G)=0,
\]
then every biextension of \((P,Q)\) by \(G\) is trivial. More precisely, the
spectral sequence for the derived tensor product gives the exact sequence
\[
0\to \Ext^1(P\otimes Q,G)\to \Biext^1(P,Q;G)\to
\Hom(\Tor_1(P,Q),G)\to \Ext^2(P\otimes Q,G),
\]
and the assertion follows under the corresponding vanishing hypotheses.

If \(P\) is a flat \(\bZ\)-module, then
\[
P\otimes^{\bL}Q\simeq P\otimes Q,
\]
hence
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\simeq \Ext^1(P\otimes Q,G).
\]
The same conclusion holds, by symmetry, if \(Q\) is flat. In particular, if both
\(P\) and \(Q\) are locally free abelian sheaves, biextensions are ordinary
extensions of \(P\otimes Q\) by \(G\).

Suppose \(P\) is killed by an integer \(n>0\). The exact triangle associated with
multiplication by \(n\) on \(Q\) relates \(P\otimes^{\bL}Q\) to
\(\Tor_1(P,Q)\) and to \(P\otimes Q\). This provides the exact sequences used
below to extract torsion pairings from a biextension.

When both \(P\) and \(Q\) are \(n\)-torsion, the derived tensor product is
controlled by \(\Tor_1(P,Q)\) and \(P\otimes Q\), and the biextension group is
described by the same Ext sequence. These elementary cases will be applied to
finite flat group schemes and to the finite layers of Tate modules.

\subsection*{2. Pairings defined by a biextension}

Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be abelian groups of \(T\), and let \(n>0\) be an integer.
Write \({}_nP\) for the kernel of multiplication by \(n\) on \(P\), and similarly
for \(Q\). There is a canonical homomorphism
\[
{}_nP\otimes {}_nQ\longrightarrow \Tor_1(P,Q).
\]
It is obtained from the exact sequence
\[
0\to {}_nQ\to Q\xrightarrow{n}Q
\]
by applying \(P\otimes^{\bL}-\). Equivalently, a pair
\((p,q)\), with \(np=0\) and \(nq=0\), defines the standard Tor class represented
by the relation \(nq=0\) pulled against \(p\).

One may describe this homomorphism explicitly. For a section \(q\) of
\({}_nQ\), lift it in the two-term resolution
\[
Q\xrightarrow{n}Q.
\]
Tensoring with \(P\), a section \(p\in {}_nP\) yields a cycle. Its class in
\(\Tor_1(P,Q)\) is the image of \(p\otimes q\). The construction is functorial
in both variables and is bilinear.

Interchanging \(P\) and \(Q\) gives a second construction
\[
{}_nQ\otimes {}_nP\longrightarrow \Tor_1(Q,P).
\]
Under the canonical symmetry
\[
\Tor_1(P,Q)\simeq \Tor_1(Q,P),
\]
the two constructions differ by the usual sign. This is the standard Koszul sign
arising from the symmetry of the derived tensor product. Thus the induced pairing
is alternating in the expected graded sense.

Now let \(G\) be a further abelian group and let
\[
\xi\in \Biext^1(P,Q;G).
\]
Through the isomorphism
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\simeq \Ext^1(P\otimes^{\bL}Q,G),
\]
and the canonical map
\[
{}_nP\otimes {}_nQ\to \Tor_1(P,Q),
\]
the biextension \(\xi\) defines a pairing
\[
{}_nP\times {}_nQ\longrightarrow G/nG
\]
whenever the target is interpreted through the connecting morphism determined by
multiplication by \(n\) on \(G\). More invariantly, it defines a morphism
\[
{}_nP\otimes {}_nQ\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Coker}(G\xrightarrow{n}G).
\]
If \(G=\Gm\), this is the familiar Weil-type pairing attached to a biextension.

When \(n\) varies through powers of a prime \(\ell\), the preceding pairings form
a compatible projective system. For any commutative group \(E\), write
\[
T_\ell(E)=\varprojlim_\nu {}_{\ell^\nu}E.
\]
A biextension \(\xi\) then defines an \(\ell\)-adic pairing
\[
T_\ell(P)\times T_\ell(Q)\longrightarrow T_\ell(G)
\]
or, after the standard shift appropriate to the multiplicative group, a pairing
with values in the Tate module of \(\Gm\). These pairings are functorial in all
variables and compatible with pullback, push-out, and change of topos.

In concrete terms, take local sections \(p\in {}_nP\) and \(q\in {}_nQ\).
Restrict the biextension \(E\) to the subgroup generated by \(p\) and \(q\). The
relations \(np=0\) and \(nq=0\) provide two ways of comparing the neutral section
with itself after \(n\) successive additions. Their quotient is an element of
\(G/nG\). This element is the value of the pairing at \((p,q)\). The
biextension axiom is precisely what ensures that the value is independent of the
choices and is bilinear.

\subsection*{2.3. Relation with Cartier duality}

The description just given, through the isomorphism
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;G)\simeq \Ext^1(P\otimes^{\bL}Q,G),
\]
is not the original definition of the pairing attached to a biextension. In the
classical setting of finite locally free commutative group schemes and
\(G=\Gm\), the pairing is usually described by Cartier duality.

Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be finite locally free commutative group schemes over a base
\(S\), killed by \(n\), and let \(E\) be a biextension of \((P,Q)\) by
\(\Gm\). Fixing a section \(p\) of \(P\), the restriction of \(E\) to
\(\{p\}\times Q\) is an extension of \(Q\) by \(\Gm\), hence determines a section
of the Cartier dual \(Q^\vee\). In this way \(E\) gives a morphism
\[
P\longrightarrow Q^\vee.
\]
Equivalently it gives a bilinear pairing
\[
P\times Q\longrightarrow \Gm.
\]
The homological construction above recovers the same morphism. The verification
is local on the base and reduces to the explicit description of the Tor class
associated with \(p\) and \(q\), together with the concrete computation of the
commutator or monodromy element in the restricted biextension.

Thus the two viewpoints agree: the pairing obtained by Cartier duality is the
pairing obtained from the Ext-class of the biextension by applying the canonical
map from torsion to \(\Tor_1\). This compatibility is the bridge used below
between the formal theory of biextensions and the duality theory of finite flat
group schemes.


\subsection*{2.4. Pairings attached to torsion and passage to limits}

Let \(n\) and \(m\) be positive integers. The constructions of the previous
section are compatible with the transition morphisms
\[
{}_{mn}P\longrightarrow {}_nP,\qquad {}_{mn}Q\longrightarrow {}_nQ
\]
and with the corresponding transition maps on roots of unity or on the
cokernels of multiplication. Thus an element
\(\xi\in\Biext^1(P,Q;\Gm)\) gives, for every \(n\), a pairing
\[
{}_nP\times {}_nQ\longrightarrow \mu_n
\]
whenever the relevant finite flatness hypotheses hold. These pairings are
compatible in \(n\), and hence for every prime \(\ell\) yield a pairing of Tate
modules
\[
T_\ell(P)\times T_\ell(Q)\longrightarrow T_\ell(\Gm)=\bZ_\ell(1).
\]
Equivalently, after tensoring with \(\bQ_\ell\), one obtains
\[
V_\ell(P)\times V_\ell(Q)\longrightarrow \bQ_\ell(1).
\]

When \(P\) and \(Q\) are dual abelian schemes, and \(\xi\) is the Poincaré
biextension, this is the usual Weil pairing. The preceding construction shows
that it is not an additional structure but the image, under the formalism of
derived tensor products, of the universal biextension class.

\section*{3. Biextensions of group schemes \((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\): generalities}

\subsection*{3.1. The basic example}

Recall first the most important example. If \(A\) is an abelian scheme over
\(S\), and \(A'\) is its dual, then the Poincaré invertible sheaf on
\(A\times_S A'\), rigidified along the two zero sections, defines a biextension
of \((A,A')\) by \(\Gm\). Its universal property identifies \(A'\) with the
functor which classifies extensions of \(A\) by \(\Gm\). Thus the Poincaré
biextension is the geometric representative of the identity morphism
\[
A'\longrightarrow A'.
\]

The partial extension structures are the theorem of the square in each variable.
Their compatibility is the theorem of the cube. This example is the model for the
general discussion.

\subsection*{3.2. The case in which one variable is an abelian scheme}

Suppose \(Q\) is an abelian scheme. For a commutative group scheme \(P\), a
biextension of \((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\) may be viewed, by fixing the first variable,
as a morphism from \(P\) to the sheaf of extensions of \(Q\) by \(\Gm\). Since
this sheaf is represented by the dual abelian scheme \(Q'\), one obtains a
description
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;\Gm)\simeq \Hom(P,Q')
\]
under the hypotheses which ensure the usual representability and rigidity. In
the reverse direction, a morphism \(P\to Q'\) pulls the Poincaré biextension on
\(Q\times Q'\) back to a biextension of \((P,Q)\).

This construction is compatible with base change, since the dual abelian scheme
and the Poincaré biextension commute with base change. It is also compatible with
the pairings on torsion defined above.

\subsection*{3.3. The case in which one variable is finite locally free}

Assume now that \(Q\) is finite locally free over \(S\). A biextension of
\((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\) is equivalently an extension of \(P\) by the Cartier dual
\(Q^\vee\), under the standard hypotheses. Indeed, restricting the biextension to
\(P\times \{q\}\) for sections \(q\) of \(Q\) gives a system of extensions of
\(P\) by \(\Gm\), additive in \(q\). By Cartier duality this is the same as an
extension by \(Q^\vee\).

Similarly, if \(P\) is finite locally free, biextensions of \((P,Q)\) by
\(\Gm\) correspond to extensions of \(Q\) by \(P^\vee\). The two descriptions are
identified by the symmetry of biextensions and the symmetry of Cartier duality.

This gives a useful bridge between the theory of biextensions and the duality
theory of finite flat group schemes. In particular, for finite flat
\(n\)-torsion group schemes the pairing of Section 2 is exactly the pairing
obtained from the corresponding Cartier-dual extension.

\subsection*{3.4. The unipotent case}

If one of \(P\) or \(Q\) is unipotent and the other is of the type considered
above, many biextension groups vanish. The reason is that homomorphisms from a
unipotent group to a torus, or from an abelian variety to an affine unipotent
group, vanish under the usual hypotheses. Consequently, the Ext descriptions
obtained above collapse to zero in the relevant degrees.

These vanishings will be used in reduction arguments: after decomposing a smooth
connected group scheme into its abelian, toric, and unipotent parts, only the
abelian and toric components contribute to biextensions by \(\Gm\) in the
situations needed later.

\subsection*{3.5. Summary of the preceding cases}

The preceding discussion may be summarized as follows. If \(P\) and \(Q\) are
group schemes over \(S\) belonging locally to the standard classes of abelian
schemes, tori, finite locally free groups, and unipotent groups, then
\(\Biext^1(P,Q;\Gm)\) is computed by the corresponding duality objects:
dual abelian schemes, character groups of tori, and Cartier duals of finite flat
groups. The comparison is functorial and compatible with the pairings on torsion.

\subsection*{3.6. Two abelian schemes}

Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be abelian schemes over \(S\), and let \(Q'\) be the dual of
\(Q\). The preceding construction gives a canonical isomorphism
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;\Gm)\simeq \Hom(P,Q').
\]
By symmetry, this is also canonically
\[
\Hom(Q,P').
\]
The Poincaré biextension corresponds to the identity in the case \(P=Q'\), and
pullback of the Poincaré biextension gives the inverse map.

In particular, a biextension of \((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\) determines, and is
determined by, the bilinear correspondence between \(P\) and \(Q\) expressed by
the associated homomorphism to the dual abelian scheme.

\subsection*{3.7. Semi-abelian variants}

Suppose \(P\) is an extension of an abelian scheme by a torus, and \(Q\) is of a
similar type. The computation of \(\Biext^1(P,Q;\Gm)\) is obtained by devissage
from the abelian and toric parts. The abelian--abelian part is governed by the
dual abelian scheme; the toric part is governed by character groups; and the
mixed parts are the evident homomorphism groups between the corresponding
lattices and dual abelian schemes.

This form of the result is the one needed for Néron models. It is stable under
smooth base change and local for the fppf topology.

\subsection*{3.8. Principal consequences}

For smooth group schemes over \(S\) whose geometric fibres are built from
abelian varieties, tori, and additive groups, the category of biextensions by
\(\Gm\) is controlled by the duality data of those pieces. In particular, if one
of the relevant Hom or Ext groups vanishes for structural reasons, the
biextension is uniquely trivial. If an abelian part remains, the biextension is
the pullback of the Poincaré biextension by the corresponding morphism to the
dual.

\section*{4. Biextensions and the theorem of the cube}

\subsection*{4.1. The cube condition}

Let \(P,Q,G\) be sheaves of commutative groups on a topos. A biextension of
\((P,Q)\) by \(G\) can be characterized as a \(G\)-torsor on \(P\times Q\),
rigidified along the two axes, for which the two partial difference torsors
satisfy the cocycle conditions in both variables. This is an abstract form of the
theorem of the cube.

For group schemes, especially for abelian schemes, the theorem of the cube says
that an invertible sheaf on a product which is rigidified on the coordinate axes
and satisfies the usual compatibility on triples carries a unique biextension
structure. Thus biextensions by \(\Gm\) may often be recognized purely in terms
of rigidified line bundles.

\subsection*{4.2. Smooth connected groups}

If \(P\) and \(Q\) are smooth connected group schemes over a base \(S\), the cube
condition is checked fibrewise and then descended. The connectedness ensures that
a morphism to a discrete group is determined by its value on the identity
component, hence often vanishes. This is the mechanism behind the full
faithfulness results used later.

\subsection*{4.3. Abelian varieties over a field}

Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be abelian varieties over a field \(k\). A rigidified line
bundle on \(P\times Q\) satisfying the cube condition defines a biextension of
\((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\). Conversely, every such biextension has this form. The
theorem of the cube gives the necessary associativity and compatibility of the
two partial group laws.

For abelian varieties this recovers the classical description:
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;\Gm)\simeq \Hom(P,Q').
\]

\subsection*{4.4. On the automatic nature of the cube condition}

It is natural to ask whether the cube condition imposed above follows
automatically from the two rigidifications and from weaker additivity
conditions. The answer depends on the class of group schemes. For abelian
varieties the theorem of the cube gives the desired uniqueness. In more general
situations, especially when disconnected fibres or affine parts intervene, one
must retain the cube condition explicitly or verify it by devissage.

\subsection*{4.5--4.8. Devissage}

The computations for smooth connected groups are obtained by filtering the group
schemes by affine, toric, unipotent, and abelian parts. The biextension functor
is exact enough in each variable to allow passage from the pieces to the whole.
Vanishing for unipotent pieces and the standard duality for tori and abelian
schemes give the required formulas.

\subsection*{4.9--4.13. Invertible sheaves and ampleness}

Let \(P\) be a smooth connected group scheme over \(k\), extension of an abelian
variety by a torus or by a unipotent group. Rigidified invertible sheaves on
\(P\) are controlled by the corresponding Picard functor. If an invertible sheaf
\(\mathcal L\) on \(P\) is ample, the associated morphism to the dual is an
isogeny on the abelian quotient. The biextension attached to \(\mathcal L\) is
obtained by the usual formula
\[
m^*\mathcal L\otimes p_1^*\mathcal L^{-1}\otimes p_2^*\mathcal L^{-1},
\]
where \(m\) is the group law. The cube theorem identifies the compatibility
needed for this line bundle to define a biextension.

\section*{5. Extensions and biextensions by constant truncated group schemes}

Let \(S\) be a scheme, \(T\subset S\) a closed subscheme, and \(Z\) a set or a
group. Denote by \(Z_S\) the corresponding constant sheaf or constant group
scheme on \(S\), and by \(Z_T\) its restriction to \(T\).

When \(Z\) is a group, the sheaf \(Z_S\) is an étale group scheme. The basic
computations of \(\Hom\), \(\Ext\), and \(\Biext\) with coefficients in such
constant groups reduce to computations on connected components. If \(P\) is
proper over \(S\), then morphisms \(P\to Z_S\) are locally constant and hence
factor through the component group. For an abelian scheme \(P\), this gives
\[
\Hom(P,Z_S)=0
\]
when \(Z\) is torsion-free and the fibres of \(P\) are connected.

More generally, if \(P\) is an abelian scheme over \(S\), the Ext groups
\(\Ext^i(P,Z_S)\) are controlled by the component data on the boundary. Analogous
formulas hold for \(\Tor_i\), and all these constructions commute with base
change. These facts will be applied with \(Z=\bZ\), supported on a closed
subscheme, in the study of the Néron model of \(\Gm\).

\begin{proposition}
Let \(S\) be a scheme, \(T\subset S\) a closed subscheme, \(Z\) a torsion-free
constant group, and \(P,Q\) abelian schemes over \(S\). Under the standard
connectedness hypotheses, the groups of extensions and biextensions by the
constant group supported on \(T\) are described by the corresponding Hom groups
from the component groups of the special fibres to \(Z\), and by their derived
functors.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is a direct consequence of the exact sequences for Ext and Biext
and the fact that proper connected fibres admit no non-constant maps to a
discrete group.

\section*{6. Extensions and biextensions by the Néron model of \(\Gm\)}

Let \(S\) be a scheme, \(T\subset S\) a closed subscheme, and \(U=S-T\). Let
\(\mathcal N\) be the Néron--Raynaud model of \(\Gm\) relative to \(U\). It fits,
under the hypotheses used here, into an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow \Gm\longrightarrow \mathcal N\longrightarrow
i_*\bZ_T\longrightarrow 0,
\]
where \(i:T\hookrightarrow S\).

For a commutative group scheme \(P\) over \(S\), the extensions of \(P\) by
\(\mathcal N\) are easier to control than extensions by \(\Gm\), because
\(\mathcal N\) has the Néron mapping property. If \(P\) is an abelian scheme, one
gets
\[
\Ext^1(P,\mathcal N)\simeq \Ext^1(P_U,\Gm)
\]
by restriction to \(U\). Similarly, for two abelian schemes \(P,Q\),
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;\mathcal N)\simeq \Biext^1(P_U,Q_U;\Gm).
\]
The obstruction to replacing \(\mathcal N\) by \(\Gm\) lies in the
corresponding groups with coefficients in \(i_*\bZ_T\).

If \(L\) is an invertible sheaf on \(P\times Q\), rigidified along the axes, its
associated biextension by \(\Gm\) extends to a biextension by \(\mathcal N\) when
restricted to the open \(U\). The failure of this \(\mathcal N\)-biextension to
come from a \(\Gm\)-biextension is measured by the component group term
\(i_*\bZ_T\).

\begin{proposition}
Let \(S\) be regular and let \(T\) be a divisor with suitable normal-crossing or
unibranch hypotheses. Then the quotient sheaf
\[
j_*(\Gm)_U/(\Gm)_S
\]
is represented, étale locally, by the constant group supported on \(T\), and its
formation is compatible with smooth base change.
\end{proposition}

In the case where \(T\) is geometrically unibranch, this quotient is simply
\[
i_*\bZ_T.
\]
Consequently the exact sequence above gives explicit obstruction groups for the
extension of \(\Gm\)-extensions and \(\Gm\)-biextensions.

\begin{theorem}
Let \(S\) be regular, let \(T\) be a divisor of the preceding type, and let
\(P,Q\) be abelian schemes over \(S\). The obstruction to extending a
biextension of \((P_U,Q_U)\) by \(\Gm\) to a biextension of \((P,Q)\) by
\(\Gm\) is the image of its canonical extension by \(\mathcal N\) in
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;i_*\bZ_T).
\]
\end{theorem}

The corollary used later is the corresponding criterion for vanishing of this
obstruction in terms of component groups.

\section*{7. Canonical prolongations of extensions and biextensions by \(\Gm\)}

Let \(S=\Spec V\) be a trait, with generic point \(\eta\), closed point \(s\),
and residue field \(k\). Let \(P\) be a smooth commutative group scheme over
\(S\), and write
\[
\Phi=P_s/P_s^0
\]
for the group of connected components of the special fibre.

\begin{theorem}
Assume \(\Phi\) is a torsion group, for instance \(P\) is of finite type over
\(S\). Then the restriction functor from extensions of \(P\) by \(\Gm\) over
\(S\) to extensions of \(P_\eta\) by \(\Gm\) over \(\eta\) is fully faithful.
If \(n\Phi=0\), then to every extension \(E_\eta\) of \(P_\eta\) by
\(\Gm\) there is associated a canonical homomorphism
\[
d(E_\eta):\Phi\longrightarrow (\bQ/\bZ)_k
\]
whose vanishing is necessary and sufficient for \(E_\eta\) to be the generic
fibre of an extension of \(P\) by \(\Gm\). In particular, if \(P_s\) is
connected, restriction is an equivalence.
\end{theorem}

For biextensions the statement is analogous. Let \(P,Q\) be smooth commutative
group schemes over \(S\), and put
\[
\Phi=P_s/P_s^0,\qquad \Psi=Q_s/Q_s^0.
\]

\begin{theorem}
Assume that \(\Phi\) or \(\Psi\) is torsion. Then restriction from biextensions
of \((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\) over \(S\) to biextensions of
\((P_\eta,Q_\eta)\) by \(\Gm\) over \(\eta\) is fully faithful. If \(n\Phi=0\)
or \(n\Psi=0\), then every generic biextension \(E_\eta\) has a canonical
obstruction pairing
\[
d(E_\eta):\Phi\otimes\Psi\longrightarrow (\bQ/\bZ)_k,
\]
and this pairing vanishes if and only if \(E_\eta\) extends to a biextension of
\((P,Q)\) by \(\Gm\) over \(S\). If \(P_s\) or \(Q_s\) is connected, restriction
is an equivalence.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses the Néron--Raynaud model \(\mathcal N\) of \((\Gm)_\eta\).
Restriction for biextensions by \(\mathcal N\) is an equivalence by the Néron
mapping property. Thus the question is reduced to passing from
\(\mathcal N\) to \(\Gm\), using the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow \Gm\longrightarrow \mathcal N\longrightarrow \bZ_s
\longrightarrow 0.
\]
For extensions, full faithfulness is equivalent to
\[
\Hom(P,\bZ_s)=0,
\]
which identifies with \(\Hom(\Phi,\bZ)\) and therefore vanishes when \(\Phi\) is
torsion. The obstruction group is
\[
\Ext^1(P,\bZ_s)\simeq \Hom(\Phi,\bQ/\bZ).
\]
For biextensions the same argument, with the exact sequence of \(\Biext\)'s,
gives the obstruction group
\[
\Biext^1(P,Q;\bZ_s)\simeq
\Hom(\Phi\otimes\Psi,\bQ/\bZ).
\]

\subsection*{7.3. Complements}

In the situation of the extension theorem, with \(n\Phi=0\), the obstruction
\(d(E_\eta)\) admits a torsion interpretation. The restriction of \(E_\eta\) to
\({}_nP_\eta\) gives an extension of \({}_nP_\eta\) by \(\mu_n\). Passing to
component groups gives
\[
{}_n\Phi\longrightarrow (\bZ/n\bZ)_k,
\]
which is the restriction of \(d(E_\eta)\).

In the biextension case, a generic biextension \(E_\eta\) defines the torsion
pairings discussed in Section 2. These recover the obstruction pairing
\[
d(E_\eta):\Phi\otimes\Psi\to(\bQ/\bZ)_k.
\]
If \(q\in Q(S)\) is a section and \(q_s\) its image in \(\Psi(k)\), then the
extension of \(P_\eta\) obtained from \(E_\eta\) by restriction along \(q_\eta\)
has obstruction
\[
d_q(x)=d(E_\eta)(x,q_s).
\]
Thus the biextension obstruction is characterized by the extension obstructions
obtained by fixing one variable.

The obstruction is symmetric under transposition. If \({}^tE_\eta\) denotes the
transposed biextension of \((Q_\eta,P_\eta)\), then the diagram
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Phi\otimes\Psi & \xrightarrow{d(E_\eta)} & (\bQ/\bZ)_k\\
\downarrow & & \Vert\\
\Psi\otimes\Phi & \xrightarrow{d({}^tE_\eta)} & (\bQ/\bZ)_k
\end{array}
\]
commutes. In particular, if \(P=Q\) and \(E_\eta\) is symmetric, then the
obstruction form on \(\Phi\) is symmetric.

Finally, let \(S'\to S\) be a dominant local morphism of traits and let
\[
e(S'/S)
\]
be the ramification index. Under base change one has
\[
d(E_{\eta'})=e(S'/S)\, d(E_\eta)\otimes_k k'.
\]
When \(e(S'/S)=1\), for example for étale extensions, henselizations, strict
henselizations, or completions, formation of the obstruction commutes with base
change. In general, after a suitable finite extension of traits, the obstruction
is killed and the extension or biextension prolongs.

\begin{proposition}
Let \(S\) be reduced. If \(P\) is a smooth group scheme over \(S\) whose fibres
at the maximal points are connected, then the functor from extensions of \(P\)
by \(\Gm\) to rigidified \(\Gm\)-torsors on \(P\) is fully faithful. If \(S\) is
normal and \(P\) has connected fibres, a rigidified torsor lies in the essential
image if and only if this is true over every maximal point of \(S\).
\end{proposition}

The analogous statement holds for biextensions of \((P,Q)\): the functor to
bitrivialized \(\Gm\)-torsors on \(P\times_S Q\), rigidified along the two unit
sections, is fully faithful under the same connectedness assumptions, and over a
normal base its essential image is detected at the maximal points.

\begin{corollary}
Let \(S\) be normal and let \(P,Q\) be smooth group schemes over \(S\), with
connected maximal fibres and with one of \(P,Q\) having connected fibres. Assume
that over every maximal point one of \(P_\eta,Q_\eta\) is an extension of an
abelian variety by a torus, or more generally has a composition series whose
factors are abelian varieties, tori, or additive groups. Then every bitrivialized
torsor of the preceding kind comes from a unique biextension.
\end{corollary}

\section*{Exposé IX. Néron models of abelian schemes and monodromy}

\subsection*{0. Introduction}

Let \(S\) be a trait with generic point \(\eta=\Spec K\), closed point \(s\),
and residue field \(k\). Let \(A_K\) be an abelian variety over \(K\), and let
\(A\) be its Néron model over \(S\). The object of this exposé is to express the
structure of the special fibre \(A_s\), and in particular its group of connected
components, in terms of the action of inertia on the Tate module of \(A_K\).

The dual abelian variety is denoted \(A'_K\), and its Néron model by \(A'\).
The Poincaré biextension of \((A_K,A'_K)\) by \(\Gm\) has a canonical
prolongation to a biextension
\[
W
\]
of \((A^0,A'^0)\) by \(\Gm\). From it one obtains a canonical pairing of
component groups
\[
\Phi\times\Phi'\longrightarrow (\bQ/\bZ)_k,
\]
where
\[
\Phi=A_s/A_s^0,\qquad \Phi'=A'_s/A_s'^0.
\]
This pairing will be related to the monodromy pairing on Tate modules.

Fix a prime \(\ell\) invertible on \(S\). The \(\ell\)-adic Tate module
\(T_\ell(A_K(\overline K))\) is equipped with an action of
\[
\pi=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K),
\]
and in particular of the inertia group \(I\). The main constructions below
single out two canonical submodules: the fixed part and the toric part. The
orthogonality theorem identifies the toric part as the orthogonal complement of
the fixed part for the Weil pairing attached to the canonical biextension.

\section*{1. Néron model of an abelian scheme}

\subsection*{1.0. Notation and the canonical pairing}

Let \(A_K\) be an abelian variety over \(K\), \(A'_K\) its dual, and \(A,A'\)
their Néron models over \(S\). Write
\[
\Phi=A_s/A_s^0,\qquad \Phi'=A'_s/A_s'^0.
\]
The canonical biextension \(W_K\) of \((A_K,A'_K)\) by \(\Gm\) extends
canonically to a biextension
\[
W
\]
of \((A^0,A'^0)\) by \(\Gm\). Applying the obstruction formalism of Exposé VIII
to this biextension gives a pairing
\[
\Phi\times\Phi'\longrightarrow(\bQ/\bZ)_k.
\]
This is the component pairing associated with the Poincaré biextension. It is
functorial in \(A_K\) and compatible with duality.

The restriction of \(W\) to the generic fibre is \(W_K\). The uniqueness part of
the canonical prolongation theorem ensures that \(W\) is uniquely determined by
\(W_K\), and hence that the pairing on component groups is canonical.

\subsection*{1.1. Non-degeneracy properties}

The pairing
\[
\Phi\times\Phi'\to(\bQ/\bZ)_k
\]
is expected, and in the situations used below is known, to be non-degenerate in
the appropriate sense. More precisely, after restricting to the finite component
groups which arise for Néron models of abelian varieties over a trait, it induces
homomorphisms
\[
\Phi\longrightarrow \Hom(\Phi',\bQ/\bZ),\qquad
\Phi'\longrightarrow \Hom(\Phi,\bQ/\bZ).
\]
Raynaud's results identify these maps as isomorphisms under the standard
hypotheses. This is one of the principal inputs in the orthogonality theorem.

\subsection*{1.2. Behaviour under isogenies}

Let \(C_K\) be another abelian variety over \(K\), with Néron model \(C\), and
let
\[
u_K:C_K\to A_K
\]
be a homomorphism. By the Néron mapping property it extends uniquely to
\[
u:C\to A.
\]
If \(u_K\) is an isogeny, then \(u\) induces isogenies on the connected
components of the special fibres and on the corresponding toric and abelian
parts. In particular the numerical invariants attached to the reduction of
\(A_K\) are invariant under isogeny. The same applies to \(A_K\) and its dual.

\subsection*{1.3. The invariants \(\lambda,\mu,\alpha\)}

Let \(A_s^0\) be the identity component of the special fibre. Its Chevalley
decomposition has an affine part whose maximal torus has rank \(\mu\), an
abelian quotient of dimension \(\alpha\), and a unipotent part of rank
\(\lambda\). These integers satisfy
\[
\dim A_K=\lambda+\mu+\alpha.
\]
The dual abelian variety has the same invariants. This follows from the
isogeny-invariance just mentioned, together with the duality between the toric
and component data.

\subsection*{1.4. The canonical biextension on the identity components}

The canonical biextension \(W\) on \((A^0,A'^0)\) restricts on the special fibre
to a biextension
\[
W_s
\]
of \((A_s^0,A_s'^0)\) by \(\Gm\). This biextension is the object through which
the \(\ell\)-adic pairings on the fixed parts of the Tate modules are compared
with the geometry of the special fibre. Its non-degeneracy on the abelian
quotients and its vanishing on toric directions are the essential facts used
below.

\section*{2. Fixed and toric parts of \(T_\ell(A)\). Criteria for good reduction}

\subsection*{2.1. The Tate module and inertia}

Let \(\ell\) be a prime distinct from the residue characteristic \(p\). The Tate
module
\[
T_\ell(A_K)=T_\ell(A_K(\overline K))
\]
is a free \(\bZ_\ell\)-module of rank \(2\dim A_K\), equipped with a continuous
action of \(\pi=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K)\). Let \(I\subset \pi\) be the
inertia group.

If \(S\) is henselian, the finite étale group schemes \({}_{\ell^\nu}A\) over
\(S\) identify their sections over the maximal unramified extension with the
inertia invariants in \({}_{\ell^\nu}A_K(\overline K)\). Passing to the inverse
limit gives the fixed part:
\[
T_\ell(A_K)^f=T_\ell(A_K)^I.
\]

\subsection*{2.2. Definition of the fixed part}

The fixed part is the \(\ell\)-adic sheaf on \(S\) whose generic fibre is the
inertia-invariant submodule of \(T_\ell(A_K)\). Equivalently, when \(S\) is
henselian, it is
\[
T_\ell(A_K)^f=\varprojlim_\nu
{}_{\ell^\nu}A(K^{\operatorname{nr}}),
\]
where \(K^{\operatorname{nr}}\) is the maximal unramified extension of \(K\) in
\(\overline K\).

The specialization map identifies this module with the Tate module of the
identity component of the special fibre:
\[
T_\ell(A_K)^f\simeq T_\ell(A_s^0)
\]
after the standard passage through the Néron model. More precisely, the fixed
part is characterized as the lisse \(\bZ_\ell\)-sheaf whose special fibre is
\(T_\ell(A_s^0)\).

\begin{proposition}
Assume \(S\) henselian and \(\ell\neq p\). The submodule of
\(T_\ell(A_K(\overline K))\) corresponding to the fixed part is the submodule of
invariants:
\[
T_\ell(A_K)^f=T_\ell(A_K(\overline K))^I.
\]
\end{proposition}

For every integer \(m>0\) prime to \(p\) one has
\[
{}_mA(K^{\operatorname{nr}})
=({}_mA(\overline K))^I.
\]
This is because \({}_mA\) is finite étale over \(S\). Passing to the inverse
limit over \(m=\ell^\nu\) proves the proposition.

\subsection*{2.2.6. Isogenies and the fixed part}

Let \(C_K\) be another abelian variety with Néron model \(C\), and let
\(u_K:C_K\to A_K\) be an isogeny. Then
\[
T_\ell(u_K):T_\ell(C_K)\to T_\ell(A_K)
\]
induces an isogeny on fixed parts
\[
T_\ell(C_K)^f\longrightarrow T_\ell(A_K)^f.
\]
The induced morphism on the identity components of special fibres gives an
isogeny
\[
T_\ell(C_s^0)\longrightarrow T_\ell(A_s^0).
\]
It also induces an isogeny on maximal tori and, after a harmless extension of
the residue field, on the abelian quotients of the identity components.

Consequently the invariants
\[
\lambda,\quad \mu,\quad \alpha
\]
of the reduction are invariant under isogeny. In particular \(A_K\) and its dual
\(A'_K\) have the same invariants.

\subsection*{2.2.9. Good reduction}

Let \(A_K\) be an abelian variety over \(K\), \(A\) its Néron model, and
\(\ell\neq p\). The following conditions are equivalent.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item There exists an abelian scheme over \(S\) with generic fibre \(A_K\).
\item The Néron model \(A\) is an abelian scheme.
\item The identity component \(A^0\) is an abelian scheme.
\item The special fibre \(A_s\) is an abelian variety.
\item The identity component \(A_s^0\) is an abelian variety, equivalently
\(\lambda=\mu=0\).
\item The Néron model \(A\) is proper over \(S\).
\item The inertia group \(I\) acts trivially on
\(T_\ell(A_K(\overline K))\).
\item Equivalently, for every \(\nu\), \(I\) acts trivially on
\({}_{\ell^\nu}A_K(\overline K)\).
\end{enumerate}

This is the Néron--Ogg--Shafarevich criterion for good reduction. An abelian
scheme satisfying (i) is the Néron model of its generic fibre and is therefore
unique up to unique isomorphism.

The proof uses the Néron mapping property for abelian schemes over a regular
base, the structure of \(A_s^0\), and the equality between the fixed part of the
Tate module and the inertia invariants. Trivial inertia action forces the fixed
part to be the whole Tate module, which is equivalent to the absence of toric and
unipotent contribution in the special fibre.

\subsection*{2.3. The toric part}

Assume again that \(S\) is henselian and \(\ell\neq p\). Let \(T_0\) be the
maximal torus of \(A_s^0\). The inclusion
\[
T_\ell(T_0)\subset T_\ell(A_s^0)
\]
defines a canonical lisse \(\bZ_\ell\)-subsheaf of \(T_\ell(A^0)\), called the
toric part:
\[
T_\ell(A^0)^t\subset T_\ell(A^0)\subset T_\ell(A).
\]
Its special fibre is
\[
T_\ell(A^0)^t\times_S s=T_\ell(T_0).
\]
On the generic fibre this gives a \(\pi\)-stable submodule
\[
T_\ell(A_K)^t\subset T_\ell(A_K(\overline K)),
\]
the toric submodule of the Tate module. Thus
\[
T_\ell(A_K)^t\subset T_\ell(A_K)^f\subset T_\ell(A_K)
\]
is a canonical three-step filtration. The same construction applies to the dual
abelian variety.

\subsection*{2.4. The orthogonality theorem}

Let \(A'_K\) be the dual abelian variety. The Poincaré biextension gives the
Weil pairing
\[
e_\ell:T_\ell(A_K)\times T_\ell(A'_K)\longrightarrow \bZ_\ell(1).
\]

\begin{theorem}
Assume \(S\) henselian and \(\ell\neq p\). With respect to the pairing
\(e_\ell\), the toric part of \(T_\ell(A_K)\) is the orthogonal complement of
the fixed part of \(T_\ell(A'_K)\) inside the fixed part of \(T_\ell(A_K)\):
\[
T_\ell(A_K)^t
=
T_\ell(A_K)^f\cap\bigl(T_\ell(A'_K)^f\bigr)^\perp.
\]
The symmetric statement with \(A\) and \(A'\) interchanged also holds.
\end{theorem}

The first inclusion follows by extending the Poincaré biextension to
\((A^0,A'^0)\). On special fibres, it is enough to prove that the pairing
\[
T_\ell(T_0)\times T_\ell(A_s'^0)\to \bZ_\ell(1)
\]
is zero; this is a consequence of the vanishing of biextensions between a torus
and the relevant connected part. For the reverse inclusion, one reduces to the
special fibre and uses the non-degeneracy, due to Raynaud, of the biextension
\(W_s\) on the abelian quotient.

\subsection*{2.5. Polarized form}

If \(\xi\) is a polarization of \(A_K\), it identifies \(T_\ell(A_K)\) with a
submodule of \(T_\ell(A'_K)\) up to isogeny and gives an alternating form
\[
e_\ell^\xi:T_\ell(A_K)\times T_\ell(A_K)\to \bZ_\ell(1).
\]
Let
\[
V=T_\ell(A_K)^f,\qquad W=T_\ell(A_K)^t.
\]
The orthogonality theorem becomes the simple formula
\[
V^\perp=W.
\]
The successive ranks in the induced filtration recover the numerical invariants
\(\lambda,\mu,\alpha\) of the special fibre.

\section*{3. The case of semi-stable reduction}

The semi-stable case is characterized by the absence of unipotent part in the
identity component of the special fibre. Equivalently, the identity component is
an extension of an abelian variety by a torus. The Tate module filtration then
has only the fixed and toric contributions described above.

We first record a general result in the style of SGA 3. Let \(G\) be a
commutative \(S\)-group scheme, flat, separated, and of finite presentation, let
\(H\) be a quasi-separated group scheme locally of finite type over \(S\), and
let
\[
u:G\to H
\]
be a homomorphism with kernel \(K\). For \(s\in S\), and for \(n\) prime to the
residue characteristic, the group scheme \({}_nK\) is étale of finite type near
\(s\), and its geometric ranks specialize divisibly:
\[
\operatorname{rank}({}_nK_{\bar s})\mid
\operatorname{rank}({}_nK_{\bar t}).
\]
This gives inequalities among the toric, abelian, and unipotent ranks of the
fibres. In particular, if the generic connected kernel is unipotent, then so is
the special connected kernel. If \(G_s\) has no unipotent part, \(H\) is smooth,
and \(u_s\) is an isogeny, then \(u\) is flat and quasi-finite near \(s\).

These facts are used to prove the semi-stable reduction theorem: after a finite
separable extension of \(K\), an abelian variety acquires semi-stable reduction.
In the language of Tate modules this is reflected in the quasi-unipotence of the
inertia action and in the existence, after finite extension, of the canonical
filtration whose graded pieces are the toric and abelian parts.

\noindent\textbf{4.5.} We shall give an application of 4.3 a) to the theory
of local conductor of abelian schemes. Suppose $k$ algebraically closed
(*). Recall (SGA 5 X 6.2 or [20]) that for every commutative \'etale
$\ell$-group scheme $F_K$ over $K$, ($\ell \neq p$), one defines an
integer
\begin{equation}
a_s(F_K) = a_s(i_*(F_K))
\end{equation}
($i \colon \eta \to S$ denoting the inclusion), in the following way : one
chooses a finite Galois extension $K'$ of $K$, with group $\Gamma$, which
splits $F_K$, so that $F_K$ is defined by a finite $\ell$-group $M$ on
which $\Gamma$ acts. Moreover, one associates to the extension $K'$ of
$K$ a certain \emph{projective} $\ZZ_{\ell}[\Gamma]$-\emph{module}
(defined up to isomorphism)
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Sw} = \operatorname{Sw}(K'/K, \ZZ_{\ell}) \; ,
\end{equation}
and one sets
\begin{equation}
a_s(F_K) = \longueur \Hom_{\ZZ_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(\operatorname{Sw}, M) \; .
\end{equation}
One verifies (loc. cit.) that this expression does not depend on the
choice of the extension $K'/K$. Besides, the fact that
$\operatorname{Sw}$ is a projective $\ZZ_{\ell}[\Gamma]$-module implies
that $a_s(F_K)$ behaves additively for exact sequences.

We shall apply this definition to the case of the $\ell$-adic sheaf
$\sA_K$, where $\sA_K$ is our abelian scheme over $K$. One has thus, in
this case, an integer
\begin{equation}
a_s(\sA_K; \ell) = \longueur
\Hom_{\ZZ_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(\operatorname{Sw}, U_{\ell} \otimes_{\ZZ_{\ell}}
\FF_{\ell}) \; , \quad U_{\ell} = \Tl(A(\overline{K})) \; ,
\end{equation}
$\Gamma$ being chosen as was said (choice depending a priori on $\ell$).
The integer (4.5.4) is called the \emph{wild conductor exponent} (or
``sauvage'') (*) of the abelian scheme $\sA_K$ with respect to the
discrete valuation ring $V \subset K$; by passing to the henselisation, it
is of course defined without supposing $V$ henselian, and it is
immediately reduced to the case where $S$ is \emph{strictly local}. We
propose to show the

\begin{corollaire}[4.6]
The wild conductor exponent (4.5.4) does not depend on the choice of
the prime number $\ell \neq p$.
\end{corollaire}

We may assume $S$ strictly local. We shall give the calculation of
(4.5.4), replacing $\operatorname{Sw}$ by any other projective
$\ZZ_{\ell}[\Gamma]$-module $L$. Note that the filtration (4.1.1) of
$U_{\ell}$ is formed by $\ZZ_{\ell}$-submodules which are manifestly
direct factors, so this filtration induces an analogous filtration of
$U(\ell) = U_{\ell} \otimes_{\ZZ_{\ell}} \FF_{\ell}$, whose successive
factors are deduced from the factors $V_i$ $(i = 1, 2, 3)$
\[
U^{\et} \; , \quad U^{\et}/U^{\et} \; , \quad U/U^{\et}
\]
appearing in the filtration (4.1.1) by tensorisation with $\FF_{\ell}$.
Of course, this filtration is stable under $\Gamma$, and gives by
additivity a decomposition of
$\longueur \Hom_{\ZZ_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(L, U(\ell))$ into a sum of three
terms, lengths of the groups
\begin{equation}
\Hom_{\ZZ_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(L, V_i \otimes_{\ZZ_{\ell}} \FF_{\ell})
\simeq (\Hom_{\ZZ_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(L, V_i)) \otimes_{\ZZ_{\ell}}
\FF_{\ell} \; ,
\end{equation}
the isomorphism written in (*) resulting again from the projectivity
hypothesis made on $L$; one only assumes $K'$ chosen large enough for
$\Gamma$ to operate on the $V_i$. Now
$\Hom_{\ZZ_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(L, V_i)$ is evidently a torsion-free
$\ZZ_{\ell}$-module, hence its \emph{rank} is equal to the right hand
side of (*). If $\varphi_i$ is the character of the representation of
$\Gamma$ in $V_i$, and $\psi$ the character of the representation of
$\Gamma$ in $\check{L}$, one therefore finds
\begin{equation}
\longueur \Hom_{\ZZ_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(L, U(\ell)) = \sum_{1 \leq i
\leq 3} N^{-1} \sum_{g \in \Gamma} \psi(g) \varphi_i(g)
\end{equation}
\[
= N^{-1} \sum_{g \in \Gamma} \psi(g) \varphi(g) \; , \quad
\text{with } N = \card \Gamma \; ,
\]
where $\varphi(g) = \sum \varphi_i(g)$ is also the function on $\Gamma$
deduced by passage to the quotient from the function on the inertia
group $I$, character of the monodromy representation of $I$ on $U$. Now
by virtue of 4.3 a) this character is a function with \emph{rational
integer values independent} of $\ell$. Taking $L = \operatorname{Sw}$,
it is the same for the character $\psi(g)$ of $L$, which is equal to
$a - N\delta + 1$, where $\delta$ is the ``\emph{Dirac function}'' of
$\Gamma$, and $a$ the ``\emph{Artin conductor}'' of $K'/K$ [26]. This
therefore proves 4.6, while exhibiting at the same time the following
expression, independent of $\ell$, for the wild conductor exponent :
\begin{equation}
a_s(\sA_K) = N^{-1} \sum_{g \in \Gamma} (a(g) - N\delta(g) + 1) \cdot
\varphi(g) \; ,
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma$ is the Galois group of a finite Galois extension $K'$ of
$K$ splitting $U^{\et}$ (for example the smallest such extension, cf.
4.1), $a(g)$ the Artin conductor on $\Gamma$, and $\varphi(g)$ the trace
function on $\Gamma$ made explicit in (4.3.0). In the case of
semi-stable reduction, one can take $K' = K$, hence $\Gamma = e$, and one
finds
\begin{equation}
a_s(\sA_K) = 0 \quad \text{(semi-stable reduction case).}
\end{equation}

\noindent (*) It would in fact suffice that $k$ be \emph{perfect}, but
by passing to the strict henselisation of $S$, one immediately reduces
to the case under consideration.

\noindent\textbf{4.7.} To conclude, let us make explicit a criterion of
semi-stable reduction which follows from the general reflections of 4.2 :

\begin{proposition}[4.7]
\emph{(M.~RAYNAUD's criterion for semi-stable reduction).} Let $S$ be a
trait, $A_K$ an abelian scheme defined over its function field $K$, $n$
an integer $\geq 1$ prime to the residual characteristic $p$ (*), and
such that ${}_n A_K$ is unramified with respect to $S$, i.e. such that
the inertia group acts trivially on ${}_n A(\overline{K})$. Then, either
$A_K$ has semi-stable reduction over $S$, or $n = 2$, and if $S$ is
strictly local, $A_K$ acquires semi-stable reduction over a finite
Galois extension $K'$ of $K$ with Galois group isomorphic to
$(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^r$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We may obviously assume $S$ strictly local. With the notation of 4.1 and
4.2, let $K'$ be the smallest possible, i.e. assume that $\Gamma$ acts
\emph{faithfully} on $A_0^{\ast 0}$. By hypothesis, $\Gamma$ acts
trivially on ${}_n A(\overline{K})$, and a fortiori on
${}_n A(\overline{K})^{\et} \simeq {}_n A_0^{\ast}(k) \supset
{}_n A_0^{\ast 0}(k)$. We conclude by means of the two following lemmas :

\begin{lemme}[4.7.1]
\emph{(J.P.~SERRE).} Let $G$ be a commutative group scheme over a field
$k$, extension of an abelian scheme by a torus, $n$ an integer $\geq 2$.
Then every automorphism of finite order of $G$ inducing the identity on
${}_n G$ is reduced to the identity if $n \neq 2$, and is the identity
or of order two if $n = 2$.
\end{lemme}

\begin{lemme}[4.7.2]
A finite group in which every element $\neq 1$ is of order $2$ is
isomorphic to $(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^r$, for a suitable $r \geq 0$.
\end{lemme}

\noindent\textbf{4.7.3.} Let us recall the proof of 4.7.1, which is well
known. One notes that thanks to the fact that $n \cdot \id_G$ is an
epimorphism, the hypothesis that $u$ induce the identity on ${}_n G$,
i.e. that $1 - u$ induce $0$ on ${}_n G$, is expressed by the condition
that one has $1 - u = nv$ in the ring of endomorphisms of $G$. To prove
the announced conclusion, one is reduced (decomposing the finite cyclic
group generated by $u$ into its $p$-primary components, for the various
prime numbers $p$ dividing the order of $u$) to the case where $u$ is of
order $p^N$, a power of a prime number. Let $E$ be the subring of
$\End(G)$ generated by $v$, which is a quotient of $\ZZ[T]/(T^{p^N} - 1)$,
hence of finite type over $\ZZ$, and which moreover is torsion-free
(this is a formal consequence of the fact that $G$ is divisible). It
thus embeds into $E_{\QQ} = E \otimes_{\ZZ} \QQ$, which appears as a
quotient of $\QQ[T]/(T^{p^N} - 1)$, which is a direct sum of finite field
extensions of $\QQ$. It is thus the same for $E_{\QQ}$. Considering the
images of $E, u, v$ in the component fields $K_i$ of $E_{\QQ}$, one is
thus reduced to seeing that if $K = \QQ(\mu)$ is a field generated by a
primitive $p^n$-th root of unity $\mu$, and if $n$ is an integer
$\geq 2$ such that $\mu - 1 \equiv 0 \mod n$ (in the ring of integers of
$K$), then one has $\mu = 1$, or $\mu = -1$ and $n = 2$. Now this
follows immediately from the well-known fact that if $\mu \neq 1$ i.e.
$m \geq 1$, the ideal $(\mu - 1)$ is prime and the ideal $(p)$ is a
power thereof, whose exponent is $> 1$ except if $p = 2, m = 1$ i.e.
$\mu = -1$. From the fact that $(\mu - 1)$ is prime and divides $p$ it
follows indeed that $n \geq 2$ can divide $\mu - 1$ only if $n = p$, and
$p$ divides $\mu - 1$ only if $(\mu - 1) = (p)$ i.e. $\mu = -1$, hence
$n = 2$.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{4.7.4.} Let us prove 4.7.2. It suffices to prove that
$\Gamma$ is commutative. Now if $x, y \in \Gamma$, one will have
$(xy)(xy) = 1$, which can also be written, since $x^2 = 1$, $y^2 = 1$,
\[
xy = (xy)^{-1} = y^{-1}x^{-1} = yx \; , \quad \text{qed.}
\]

\end{proof}

\begin{remarque}[4.7.5]
The proof given of 4.7.3, together with 4.7.2, shows us that if $G$ is
a divisible commutative group of a topos, $n \geq 2$ an integer, and
$\Gamma$ a finite group, acting faithfully on $G$ while inducing the
identity on ${}_n G$, then $\Gamma$ is reduced to the identity if $n
\geq 3$, and is isomorphic to a group $(\ZZ/2\ZZ)^r$ if $n = 2$.
\end{remarque}

\noindent\textbf{\large \S\,5. The orthogonality theorem in the case
$\ell = p$ (semi-stable case). Duality of abelian schemes $B_0$ and
$B'_0$. Characterisation of the fixed part. Criteria for good
reduction, essentially good reduction, and semi-stable reduction.}

\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{5.1.} Place ourselves in the case 2.2.3 where $S$ is
henselian and $A^0$ is $\ell$-divisible, so that the \emph{fixed part}
\[
\Tl(A^0)^f \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow \Tl(A)
\]
is defined as the Barsotti--Tate pro-$\ell$-group formed by the
$({}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!A^0)^f$. We shall now give a definition of the toric
part (2.3.2) of $\Tl(A^0)$, independent of the hypothesis $\ell \neq p$
made in 2.3, but assuming on the other hand that $S$ is \emph{complete}
(*). For this, introduce for every integer $n \geq 0$ the local artinian
scheme
\begin{equation}
S_n = \Spec(V/\frakm^{n+1}) \; ,
\end{equation}
$n$-th infinitesimal neighbourhood of $s$ in $S$, and for every
$S$-scheme $X$ let $X_n = X \times_S S_n$. As $(A^0)_n = (A_n)^0$ is
smooth over $S_n$, it is known (SGA 3 IX 3.6 bis) that $T_0$ is the
special fibre of a unique subtorus
\begin{equation}
T_n \subset A_n^0 \; .
\end{equation}
According to the uniqueness of the $T_n$, these are deduced from one
another by the base changes $S_{n'} \to S_n$. In other terms, they define
a \emph{formal torus} $\hat{T}$ of the formal group $\hat{A}^0$ (in the
category of formal schemes over $S$) formal completion of $A^0$ along
the special fibre :
\begin{equation}
\hat{T} \subset \hat{A}^0 \; .
\end{equation}

As the functor $X \mapsto \hat{X}$ (formal completion along the special
fibre) induces an equivalence between the category of finite schemes
over $S$, and that of formal finite schemes over $S$ (EGA III 4.8), we
shall simply identify a finite scheme over $S$ with the corresponding
formal scheme. With this identification, the definition of the ``fixed
parts'' immediately gives us for every $\nu \geq 0$ :
\begin{equation}
({}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!A^0)^f = {}_{\ell^{\nu}}(\hat{A}^0) \; ,
\end{equation}
which can also be written as an identity of projective systems
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A^0)^f = \Tl(\hat{A}^0) \; .
\end{equation}
One thus obtains a sub-pro-Barsotti--Tate group of the fixed part of
$\Tl(A^0)$ by taking $\Tl(\hat{T}) \subset \Tl(\hat{A}^0)$. We then
define the \emph{toric part} of $\Tl(A^0)$ as
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A^0)^t \overset{\mathrm{dfn}}{=} \Tl(\hat{T}) \subset
\Tl(\hat{A}^0) = \Tl(A^0)^f \; .
\end{equation}
So by definition, one will have
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A^0)^t = ({}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!\hat{T})_{\nu \geq 0} \; ,
\end{equation}
the finite and flat $S$-group ${}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!\hat{T}$ itself being
described as the inductive limit of the finite and flat $S_n$-groups
\begin{equation}
{}_{\ell^{\nu}}(T_n) \; , \quad n \text{ variable} \; ,
\end{equation}
i.e. its affine algebra is described as the projective limit of the
affine algebras of the groups (5.1.8). Of course, one will have
\begin{equation}
(\Tl(A^0)^t)_n = \Tl(T_n) \; ,
\end{equation}
which for $n = 0$ is none other than formula (2.3.3) by which we had
characterised the toric part (as pro-finite-\'etale group) in the case
$\ell \neq p$. This shows that the definition (5.1.6) is compatible
with that given in 2.3 when $\ell \neq p$.

\smallskip

Passing to the generic fibres, the fixed and toric parts of
$\Tl(A^0)$ define fixed and toric parts in $\Tl(A_K)$ :
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^t \subset \Tl(A_K)^f \subset \Tl(A_K) \; .
\end{equation}
These are inclusions of pro-$\ell$-Barsotti--Tate groups. Consider the
analogous filtration, corresponding to the dual abelian scheme
$A'_K$ :
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A'_K)^t \subset \Tl(A'_K)^f \subset \Tl(A'_K) \; .
\end{equation}


\begin{theoreme}[5.2]
Suppose that $S$ is a complete trait, and let $A_K$ be an abelian scheme
over $K$, $A'_K$ the dual abelian scheme, $\ell$ a prime number such that
the connected N\'eron model $A^0$ is $\ell$-divisible (cf.~2.2.1), so
that we then dispose of the filtrations (5.1.10) and (5.1.11) of the
pro-groups $\Tl(A_K)$ and $\Tl(A'_K)$. This being posited, the toric
parts are reconstructed in terms of the fixed parts by the formula
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^t = \Tl(A_K)^f \;\cap\; (\Tl(A'_K)^f)^{\perp}
\end{equation}
and the analogous formula obtained by exchanging the roles of $A_K$ and
$A'_K$, where the sign $\perp$ denotes the \emph{orthogonal} with respect
to the \emph{canonical pairing} (1.0.2).
\end{theoreme}

Finally, when $A_K$ has semi-stable reduction over $S$ (which results
from the other hypotheses in the case $\ell = p$), then one even has the
formula
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^t = (\Tl(A'_K)^f)^{\perp} \; .
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{5.2.3.} The meaning of formula (5.2.1) being quite
clear if $\ell$ is prime to the characteristic of $K$ (where it can be
interpreted by passing to ordinary $\ZZ_{\ell}$-modules, with action of
$\pi = \Gal(\overline{K}/K)$, formed of the $\overline{K}$-points of the
pro-groups in play, as in 2.4), let us make it explicit in the general
case, using the notion of dual
\begin{equation}
D(G) = \Hom(G, \Tl(\GG_m))
\end{equation}
of a pro-$\ell$-Barsotti--Tate group $G = (G(\nu))$, which by definition
is the projective system of Cartier duals $D(G(\nu))$ (SGA 3 VII
3.3.1 and VIII 1), where the transition morphisms are defined in an
evident way (by transposition from the morphisms of \emph{inclusion}
between the $G(\nu)$, for example). The duality theory of abelian
varieties teaches us that the pairing mentioned in 5.2 in fact defines
an \emph{isomorphism}
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A'_K) \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow} D(\Tl(A_K)) \; ,
\end{equation}
whence by composition a morphism
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^f \longrightarrow D(\Tl(A'_K)^f) \; ,
\end{equation}
and formula (5.2.1) asserts that the kernel of the preceding homomorphism
(which a priori is a sub-pro-group of the first term) is none other than
the toric part $\Tl(A_K)^t$. We shall note that \emph{it is an
isomorphism of pro-objects, and not of projective systems} : we do not
assert (and it would be false in general) that for every integer $\nu$,
$\Tl(A_K)^t(\nu)$ is equal to the kernel of
$\Tl(A_K)^f(\nu) \to D(\Tl(A'_K)^f)(\nu)$; the latter could a priori be
larger. (We shall see however below that it is not so if $A_K$ has
semi-stable reduction, which is in particular the case, according to the
hypotheses made in 5.2, when $\ell = p$.)

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{5.2.4.} The proof of (5.2.1) merely repeats, practically
without change, that of 2.4. One notes that the induced pairing
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^f \times \Tl(A'_K)^f \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_{mK})
\end{equation}
giving rise to (5.2.3.3) is induced by a pairing
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A^0)^f \times \Tl(A'^0)^f \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_{mS})
\end{equation}
of Barsotti--Tate pro-groups over $S$, itself induced by the pairing of
pro-groups
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A^0) \times \Tl(A'^0) \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_{mS})
\end{equation}
deduced from the biextension $W$ of $(A^0, A'^0)$ by $\GG_{mS}$. Hence,
the pairing
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_0^0) \times \Tl(A_0'^0) \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_{mk})
\; ,
\end{equation}
deduced from (5.2.4.2) by passage to the special fibres, is none other
than that deduced from the biextension $W_0$ of $(A_0^0, A_0'^0)$ by
$\GG_{mk}$. By virtue of VIII 4.11 and of 1.5, the left annihilator of
the pairing (5.2.4.4) is therefore equal to $\Tl(T_0)$, which is none
other than the special fibre of the toric part $\Tl(A^0)^t$. One thus
concludes, returning to the definition of the left annihilators made
explicit in 5.2.3, by means of the

\begin{lemme}[5.2.5]
Let $u \colon G \to H$ be a homomorphism of pro-$\ell$-Barsotti--Tate
groups over $S$. If $u_0 \colon G_0 \to H_0$ is zero, then $u$ is zero.
If $u_0 \colon G_0 \to H_0$ is an isogeny \emph{(resp.~an isomorphism)},
$u$ is an isogeny \emph{(resp.~an isomorphism)}.
\end{lemme}

We indeed apply the first assertion of 5.2.5 to the composed morphism
\[
\Tl(A^0)^t \longrightarrow \Tl(A^0)^f \longrightarrow
D(\Tl(A'^0)^f) \; ,
\]
where the second morphism is that deduced from the pairing (5.2.4.2);
one finds that this composition is zero, hence that the pairing under
consideration vanishes on the toric part on the left. By symmetry, it
therefore vanishes on the toric part on the right, hence it comes from a
pairing on the quotients, or ``abelian pieces''
\[
\Tl(A^0)^{\ab} \times \Tl(A'^0)^{\ab} \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_m)
\; ,
\]
whose quotients are again Barsotti--Tate pro-groups, as one easily
verifies. It remains to prove that the morphism corresponding to this
pairing
\[
\Tl(A^0)^{\ab} \longrightarrow D(\Tl(A'^0)^{\ab})
\]
is an isogeny, which again results from 5.2.5.

We shall leave to the reader the (easy) proof of 5.2.5, which is a
result valid over every noetherian connected base. We shall find
moreover below (7.4.7), by a somewhat more precise analysis of the
biextensions $W_n$ of the $(A_n^0, A_n'^0)$, a fairly obvious direct
proof of the two particular cases where we had to use 5.2.5.


\medskip

\noindent\textbf{5.2.6.} Suppose now that $A_K$ has semi-stable reduction
over $S$, so that we may take for $\ell$ any prime number (distinct or
not from $p$). Moreover, by virtue of the Galois criterion for
semi-stable reduction 3.5, one has for $\ell \neq p$
\begin{equation}
(\Tl(A'_K)^f)^{\perp} \;(= \Tl(A_K)^t)\; \subset \Tl(A_K)^f \; .
\end{equation}
It remains to prove, to finish the proof of 5.2, that this inclusion is
valid without restriction on $\ell$.

Indeed, the left hand side is canonically isomorphic to
$D(\Tl(A'_K)/\Tl(A'_K)^f)$, and is thus of ``rank'' (or ``height'')
equal to $2n - 2\alpha - \mu = \mu$ (notation of (2.1.9), where by
hypothesis $\lambda = 0$), which is also that of $\Tl(A_K)^t$. As the
inclusion
\[
\Tl(A_K)^t \subset \Tl(A_K)
\]
is \emph{strict}, in the sense that it already induces a monomorphism
for all terms of the projective systems under consideration, it follows
easily that the first member of (*) is maximal among the
sub-pro-Barsotti--Tate groups of $\Tl(A_K)$ having the same rank as it,
so that one indeed has the equality of (5.2.7), which is equivalent to
the inclusion between the extreme terms of 5.2.7 by the orthogonality
theorem.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{5.3.} Using the correspondence by orthogonality between
strict sub-pro-Barsotti--Tate groups (in the precise sense explained
above) of the dual pro-groups $\Tl(A_K)$ and $\Tl(A'_K)$, one deduces
formally from (5.2.2) that the pairing (1.0.2) between these pro-groups
induces a pairing
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^{\ab} \times \Tl(A'_K)^{\ab} \longrightarrow
\Tl(\GG_{mk})
\end{equation}
which is a \emph{perfect duality}, i.e. identifying each of the two
factors of the first member with the dual of the other (in the sense of
5.2.2). (NB the exponents $\ab$ denote the ``abelian parts'', quotient
of the fixed part by the toric part). It is clear moreover that (5.3.2)
is induced by the pairing
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A^0)^{\ab} \times \Tl(A'^0)^{\ab} \longrightarrow
\Tl(\GG_{mS})
\end{equation}
deduced by passage to the quotients from the pairing (5.2.4.2). This
being a perfect duality on the generic fibres, i.e. the corresponding
homomorphism
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A^0)^{\ab} \longrightarrow D(\Tl(A'^0)^{\ab})
\end{equation}
being an isomorphism on the generic fibres, we obviously want to deduce
from this that it is the same over all of $S$, or equivalently, over
the special fibres. This deduction is valid, trivially, if $\ell \neq p$;
it is also valid, at least in the case of unequal characteristics, if
$\ell = p$, thanks to a deep theorem of TATE [33, th.~4]. We shall
however be able to establish this \emph{directly}, without the help of
Tate's theorem, below (7.4.6).

To make precise the geometric meaning of the result we desire to
establish, note that the pairing deduced from (5.3.3) by passage to the
special fibres is none other than the pairing
\begin{equation}
\Tl(B_0) \times \Tl(B'_0) \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_{mk})
\end{equation}
deduced from the biextension $W_0$ of $(A_0^0, A_0'^0)$ by passage to
the quotient, where $B_0$ and $B'_0$ denote respectively the abelian
parts of $A_0^0$ and $A_0'^0$. As, by virtue of VIII 4.8, the
biextension $W_0$ comes from a biextension $W_0^{\ab}$ of $(B_0, B'_0)$
by $\GG_{mk}$, determined up to unique isomorphism, the pairing (5.3.5)
can also be interpreted as deduced from the divisor correspondence
$W_0^{\ab}$, and the corresponding homomorphism
\begin{equation}
\Tl(B_0) \longrightarrow D(\Tl(B'_0)) \overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}
\Tl(D(B'_0))
\end{equation}
(where in the last term, the letter $D$ denotes the dual abelian scheme)
is none other than that deduced by functoriality of $\Tl$ from the
homomorphism
\begin{equation}
B_0 \longrightarrow D(B'_0)
\end{equation}
defined by $W_0^{\ab}$. Hence the fact that (5.3.5) is a perfect duality
also means, by the well-known dictionary (cf.~[5]) that the kernel of
(5.3.7), which is a finite group over $k$ by virtue of 1.5, has a null
$\ell$-primary component. Saying that this is so for all $\ell$ therefore
means that the isogeny (5.3.7) is an \emph{isomorphism}, i.e. that the
divisor correspondence $W_0^{\ab}$ is a perfect duality between $B_0$ and
$B'_0$. The preceding considerations only show that the kernel of (5.3.7)
is a $p$-group. Let us therefore state the more complete result :

\begin{theoreme}[5.4]
Let $S$ be a trait, and $A_K$ an abelian scheme over $S$ having
semi-stable reduction over $S$. Denote by $B_0$, $B'_0$ the abelian
parts of the N\'eron models of $A_K$ and of the dual abelian scheme
$A'_K$ respectively. Then the canonical divisor correspondence
$W_0^{\ab}$ on $(B_0, B'_0)$, deduced by VIII~4.8 from the canonical
biextension $W_0$ of $(A_0^0, A_0'^0)$ (special fibre of the biextension
$W$ of 1.4), is a perfect duality between $B_0$ and $B'_0$, i.e. defines
an isomorphism of each of these abelian schemes with the dual of the
other.
\end{theoreme}

The proof of 5.4 in the general case will be given in No.~7, as a
consequence of a very natural and important construction due to RAYNAUD.
Let us only note here that to prove 5.4, one may assume $S$ complete, in
view of 3.3.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{5.5.} To express conveniently the extreme factors in
the filtrations (5.1.10) and (5.1.11), in the case of semi-stable
reduction, let us introduce the twisted constant group schemes over $S$
\begin{equation}
\underline{M}' = D(T_0) \; , \quad \underline{M} = D(T'_0) \; ,
\end{equation}
duals of the maximal tori $T_0$, $T'_0$ of the special fibres of $A, A'$
(SGA 3 X 5.7). As $S$ is local complete, these group schemes may be
considered as the special fibres of twisted constant group schemes
\begin{equation}
\underline{M}' \; , \quad \underline{M} \quad \text{on } S \; ;
\end{equation}
thus, if the torus $T_0$ is split (for example if $k$ is separably
closed) i.e. if $\underline{M}'_0$ is the constant group defined by the
ordinary group
\begin{equation}
M' = \Hom_{k\text{-gr}}(T_0, \GG_{mk}) \; ,
\end{equation}
then $\underline{M}'$ is the constant group defined by $M'$ :
\[
\underline{M}' = \underline{M}'_S \; .
\]
One may also note that, as $T_0$ and $T'_0$ are isogenous (2.2.6), $T'_0$
is split if $T_0$ is, so that there is reason to also introduce in this
case
\begin{equation}
M = \Hom_{k\text{-gr}}(T'_0, \GG_{mk}) \; ,
\end{equation}
and one will then have
\[
\underline{M} = \underline{M}_S \; .
\]
This being posited, one will evidently have, for every integer $n$,
isomorphisms
\begin{equation}
\underline{M}'_n \simeq D(T_n) \; , \quad \underline{M}_n \simeq
D(T'_n) \; ,
\end{equation}
where $T'_n$ is defined in terms of $A'$ as $T_n$ in terms of $A$
(5.1.2), whence one deduces canonical isomorphisms (where one sets
$\ZZ_{\ell}(1) = \Tl(\GG_{mS})$) :
\begin{equation}
\Tl(T_n) \simeq \check{\underline{M}}'_n \otimes_{\ZZ} \ZZ_{\ell}(1)
\; , \quad \Tl(T'_n) \simeq \check{\underline{M}}_n \otimes_{\ZZ}
\ZZ_{\ell}(1) \; ,
\end{equation}
and by passage to the limit on $n$ canonical isomorphisms :
\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
\Tl(A^0)^t = \Tl(\hat{T}) \simeq \check{\underline{M}}' \otimes
\ZZ_{\ell}(1) \\[1ex]
\Tl(A'^0)^t = \Tl(\hat{T}') \simeq \check{\underline{M}} \otimes
\ZZ_{\ell}(1) \; .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the semi-stable reduction case, one concludes by duality, using the
orthogonality theorem 5.2 and relation (5.2.2), canonical isomorphisms :
\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f \simeq \underline{M} \otimes_{\ZZ} \ZZ_{\ell}
\\[1ex]
\Tl(A'_K)/\Tl(A'_K)^f \simeq \underline{M}' \otimes_{\ZZ} \ZZ_{\ell}
\; .
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
One finds likewise, thanks to the same formula (5.2.2), canonical
isomorphisms :
\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^t \simeq D(\Tl(A'_K)^f) \\[1ex]
\Tl(A'_K)/\Tl(A'_K)^t \simeq D(\Tl(A_K)^f) \; ,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where one will recall that the groups $\Tl(A_K)^f$ and $\Tl(A'_K)^f$
appearing in the second member are the restrictions to $\eta$ of
Barsotti--Tate pro-$\ell$-groups defined over $S$, $\Tl(A^0)^f$ and
$\Tl(A'^0)^f$ respectively; the letter $D$ denotes here the duals in
the sense of Barsotti--Tate pro-groups, namely $\Hom(-, \ZZ_{\ell}(1))$.

\begin{proposition}[5.6]
Suppose $S$ complete and that $A_K$ has semi-stable reduction over $S$.
Then for every prime number $\ell$ (which may be equal to the residual
characteristic of $S$), the pro-$\ell$-Barsotti--Tate group
$\Tl(A_K)^t$ is the generic fibre of a Barsotti--Tate group over $S$,
of ``toric type'', given by (5.5.7), while $\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f$ is the
generic fibre of a Barsotti--Tate group over $S$ of pro-\'etale type,
given by (5.5.8). Finally the groups $\Tl(A_K)^f$ and
$\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^t$ are the generic fibres of Barsotti--Tate groups
defined over $S$, equal respectively to $\Tl(A^0)^f$ and to
$D(\Tl(A'^0)^f) = \Hom(\Tl(A'^0)^f, \ZZ_{\ell}(1))$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textbf{5.6.1.} One will note that it amounts to the same for the
\'etale covering $S'$ to split $\Tl(A_K)^t$ or to split
$\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f$, for this means respectively that $S'$ splits the
twisted constant groups $\underline{M}$ and $\underline{M}'$, which are
isogenous, since $T_0$ and $T'_0$ are so. One sees at the same time that
there exists a smallest \'etale extension $S'$ of $S$ which does the job.

Let us also mention the following easy consequence of the definitions of
2.2.3 :

\begin{proposition}[5.7]
Suppose $A^0$ is $\ell$-divisible, $S$ henselian. Let $F_K$ be a
constructible $\ell$-adic sheaf on $K$ which is ``unramified'' i.e.
which extends to a constant twisted $\ell$-adic sheaf $F$ on $S$, and
let $u \colon F_K \to \Tl(A_K)$ be a homomorphism of pro-groups, then
$u$ factorises into a homomorphism from $F$ into $\Tl(A_K)^f$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, one will have
\begin{equation}
u = (u(\nu))_{\nu \geq 0} \; , \quad
\text{with } u(\nu) \colon F_K(\nu) \longrightarrow
\Tl(A_K)(\nu) = {}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!A_K \; .
\end{equation}
By the universal property of $A$, as $F(\nu)$ is \'etale hence smooth
over $S$, the morphism $u(\nu)$ comes from a homomorphism
\[
v(\nu) \colon F(\nu) \longrightarrow {}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!A \; ,
\]
and by definition of the fixed part, this homomorphism factorises as
\[
v(\nu) \colon F(\nu) \longrightarrow ({}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!A)^f \; .
\]
As the image of the induced morphism on the special fibres falls into
the infinitely $\ell$-divisible part of $A_0$, one concludes that
$v(\nu)$ even factorises through $({}_{\ell^{\nu}}\!A^0)^f$. By passage
to the limit on $\nu$, one finds a homomorphism
\[
v \colon F \longrightarrow \Tl(A^0)^f \; ,
\]
and the morphism induced on the generic fibres evidently gives the
sought factorisation.
\end{proof}


\noindent\textbf{5.8.} Using the orthogonality theorem, we shall now
obtain the main variants of the N\'eron--Ogg--Shafarevich criterion :

\begin{theoreme}[5.8]
Let $A_K$ be an abelian scheme over $K$. Suppose that $S$ is strictly
local, and let $\ell$ be a prime number $\neq p$. The following
conditions are equivalent :
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
\item $A_K$ has \emph{good reduction} over $S$, i.e. $A_K$ extends to an
abelian scheme over $S$.
\item The specialisation homomorphism $I \to \Aut(\Tl(A_K))$ is
\emph{trivial}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoreme}

\begin{theoreme}[5.9]
With the notation of \emph{5.8}, suppose $A_K$ has semi-stable
reduction over $S$. Then the following equivalent conditions :
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
\item $A_K$ has \emph{good reduction} over $S$.
\item $\Tl(A_K)^t = 0$.
\item $\Tl(A_K)^t = 0$ for \emph{one} prime number $\ell \neq p$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoreme}

\begin{theoreme}[5.10]
With the notation of \emph{5.8} (but without necessarily supposing $A_K$
to have semi-stable reduction), the following equivalent conditions :
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
\item $A_K$ has \emph{good reduction} over $S$.
\item $\Tl(A_K)^f = 0$ for \emph{one} prime number $\ell \neq p$.
\item $\Tl(A_K)^f = 0$ for \emph{every} prime number $\ell \neq p$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoreme}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) is clear, for if $A_K$ has good reduction, then
$A^0 = A$ and $\Tl(A^0)^f = \Tl(A)$, hence $\Tl(A_K)^f = 0$.

(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (iii) is trivial.

(iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) : If $\Tl(A_K)^f = 0$, then $A_0^0$ is
unipotent (2.2.5). But for it to be zero, it is necessary and
sufficient that the euclidean dual of $\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f$ be zero,
which still means that the maximal torus of $A_0'^0$ is zero, i.e.
$A_K'$ has good reduction. As $A_K'' = A_K$, this means that $A_K$ has
good reduction.
\end{proof}

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{5.11.} One also finds criteria for good reduction as
well as for semi-stable reduction in terms of finite groups :

\begin{theoreme}[5.12]
Suppose $S$ strictly local, and let $n$ be an integer $\geq 1$ prime to
$p$. The following conditions are equivalent :
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
\item $A_K$ has \emph{good reduction} over $S$.
\item The group ${}_n A_K$ is unramified over $S$, i.e. $I$ acts
trivially on ${}_n A(\overline{K})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoreme}

\begin{proof}
If $A_K$ has good reduction, ${}_n A$ is finite and smooth over $S$, hence
${}_n A_K$ is unramified. Conversely, suppose that ${}_n A_K$ is
unramified. Then ${}_n A$ is a finite and flat scheme over $S$ (SGA 3 X
5.9), and as ${}_n A_K$ is unramified, the special fibre ${}_n A_0$ is
\'etale over $k$. Hence ${}_n A$ is \'etale over $S$. For $p > 1$, this
implies that $A_0$ is an abelian scheme, hence $A_K$ has good
reduction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theoreme}[5.13]
With the notation of \emph{5.12} (but without supposing $A_K$ to have
semi-stable reduction), the following equivalent conditions :
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
\item $A_K$ has \emph{semi-stable reduction} over $S$.
\item For every element $g \in I$, the multiplicity of $1$ as a root
of the characteristic polynomial of the automorphism of
$V_{\ell}(A_K)$ defined by $g$ is equal to $2\alpha$, where $\alpha$
denotes the rank of the abelian scheme $B_0^{A_K}$ appearing in the
decomposition (2.2.2) of the special fibre of the N\'eron model of
$A_K$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoreme}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii) : If $A_K$ has semi-stable reduction, then the
characteristic polynomial of $g \in I$ is of the form
$(t-1)^{2\alpha} P(t)$, where $P(1) \neq 0$, whence (ii).

(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) : This is a criterion of M.~RAYNAUD, whose
proof will be given elsewhere.
\end{proof}

\begin{theoreme}[5.14]
Suppose $S$ strictly local. Let $\ell$ be a prime number $\neq p$, $A_K$
an abelian scheme over $K$, and suppose that $A_K$ has semi-stable
reduction. Consider the canonical injection
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^f \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow \Tl(A_K) \; ,
\end{equation}
and let $\mu$ be the rank of the toric factor of the special fibre of
the N\'eron model of $A_K$. Then the following equivalent conditions :
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
\item $\mu = 0$, i.e. $A_K$ has \emph{good reduction} over $S$.
\item $I$ acts trivially on $\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f$, and the
characteristic polynomial of the automorphism defined by an element $g
\in I$ on $\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f$ has rational integer coefficients
independent of $\ell$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoreme}

\begin{theoreme}[5.15]
With the notation of \emph{5.14}, suppose $k$ finite. Then the
following conditions are equivalent :
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{(\roman*)}]
\item $A_K$ has \emph{semi-stable reduction} over $S$.
\item $\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f$ and $\Tl(A_K)^t$ are defined over $K$
(i.e. $I$ acts trivially on these groups).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theoreme}

\noindent\textbf{\large \S\,6. Remarks on the construction of the toric
and fixed parts in the non-semi-stable case.}

\smallskip

\noindent\textbf{6.1.} Let us now return to the general situation of
\S\,2, that is, we do not suppose $A_K$ to have semi-stable reduction.
One then has, for every prime number $\ell \neq p$, the filtrations
(1.3.2) and (2.3.0) :
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^t \;\subset\; \Tl(A_K)^f \;\subset\; \Tl(A_K) \; .
\end{equation}
The fundamental result of 3.6 teaches us that the toric part
$\Tl(A_K)^t$ is zero for $\ell$ sufficiently large, i.e. if $K'$ is a
finite extension of $K$ such that $A_{K'}$ has semi-stable reduction
over $S'$, then $I' = \Gal(\overline{K}/K')$ acts trivially on
$\Tl(A_K)^t$, so that $\Tl(A_K)^t$ is the generic fibre of a
Barsotti--Tate pro-group \emph{of toric type} over $S$. Likewise, the
fixed part $\Tl(A_K)^f$ is defined for all $\ell \neq p$, and we have
seen in 2.2 that $\Tl(A_K)^f$ is the generic fibre of a Barsotti--Tate
pro-group over $S$, restriction to $\eta$ of $\Tl(A^0)^f$, when $S$ is
strictly local and $A^0$ is $\ell$-divisible.

\smallskip

We now propose to show how the results of \S\,\S\,4, 5 allow in their
turn to simplify and make precise the structure of the filtration
(6.1.1) in the general case, and to construct the toric and fixed
parts in an intrinsic way, without passing through the semi-stable
reduction.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{6.2.} Let $S$ be strictly local. Recall (1.4) that we
have constructed a \emph{biextension} $W$ of $(A^0, A'^0)$ by
$\GG_{mS}$, where $A'$ is the N\'eron model of the dual $A'_K$ of
$A_K$. This biextension reduces on the special fibre to a biextension
$W_0$ of $(A_0^0, A_0'^0)$ by $\GG_{mk}$, and this biextension defines a
divisor correspondence, identifying $B'_0$ with the dual of $B_0$ (and
reciprocally), where $B_0$ and $B'_0$ denote the ``abelian parts'' of
$A_0^0$ and $A_0'^0$ respectively (2.2.2).

Let $T_0$ be the maximal torus of $A_0^0$, and $T'_0$ the maximal torus
of $A_0'^0$. According to 2.2.6, the twisted constant group schemes
$M = D(T'_0)$ and $M' = D(T_0)$ are isogenous. The fact that $W_0$
vanishes on $T_0 \times T'_0$ (VIII 4.10) means that the pairing
\begin{equation}
\Tl(T_0) \times \Tl(T'_0) \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_{mk})
\end{equation}
is trivial, which is evident since $\Tl(T_0)$ and $\Tl(T'_0)$ are
Barsotti--Tate pro-groups of toric type, and every homomorphism from
such a group to $\ZZ_{\ell}(1) = \Tl(\GG_{mS})$ is trivial. The
vanishing of (6.2.1) allows one to pass to the quotient and obtain a
pairing :
\begin{equation}
\Tl(B_0) \times \Tl(B'_0) \longrightarrow \Tl(\GG_{mk}) \; ,
\end{equation}
which is a \emph{perfect duality} according to 5.4.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{6.3.} Let $\ell$ be a prime number $\neq p$. We shall
now show that the factors of the filtration (6.1.1) can be
reconstructed in terms of the filtration (1.3.2) and of the biextension
pairing. Indeed, consider the exact sequence of Galois cohomology
\begin{equation}
0 \longrightarrow \Tl(A_K)^t \longrightarrow \Tl(A_K)^f
\longrightarrow \Tl(A_K)^{\ab} \longrightarrow 0 \; ,
\end{equation}
where $\Tl(A_K)^{\ab}$ denotes the quotient
$\Tl(A_K)^f/\Tl(A_K)^t$, and the dual exact sequence
\begin{equation}
0 \longrightarrow \Tl(A'_K)^t \longrightarrow \Tl(A'_K)^f
\longrightarrow \Tl(A'_K)^{\ab} \longrightarrow 0 \; .
\end{equation}
The biextension pairing defines a perfect duality
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^{\ab} \times \Tl(A'_K)^{\ab} \longrightarrow
\ZZ_{\ell}(1) \; ,
\end{equation}
so that $\Tl(A_K)^{\ab}$ and $\Tl(A'_K)^{\ab}$ determine one another by
duality.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{6.4.} We have seen in 2.2.6 that the ranks of the tori
$T_0$ and $T'_0$ are equal. More precisely, the constant twisted groups
$M = D(T'_0)$ and $M' = D(T_0)$ are isogenous. In fact, the divisor
correspondence $W_0^{\ab}$ allows us to define a \emph{canonical
isomorphism} between the $\QQ_{\ell}$-vector spaces
\begin{equation}
V_{\ell}(A_K)^t \otimes_{\ZZ_{\ell}} \QQ_{\ell} \simeq
V_{\ell}(A'_K)^t \otimes_{\ZZ_{\ell}} \QQ_{\ell} \; ,
\end{equation}
and the structure of the factors of the filtration (6.1.1) is completely
determined by the biextension pairing.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{6.5.} To conclude, let us point out that the
construction of the toric part in the case where $\ell = p$ (hence in
residual characteristic $p$) is done by the same method as in 5.1 :
one defines the toric part by
\begin{equation}
\Tp(A^0)^t = \Tp(\hat{T}) \subset \Tp(A^0)^f \; ,
\end{equation}
where $\hat{T}$ is the formal torus defined in (5.1.3). This
construction is valid under the hypothesis that $A^0$ is
$p$-divisible, which is the case when $A_K$ has semi-stable reduction
over $S$ (3.5). In the general case, one can always define
$\Tp(A^0)^t$ as the largest sub-pro-Barsotti--Tate group of toric type
contained in $\Tp(A^0)^f$.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{6.6.} Let us summarise the main properties of the
monodromy filtration in the general case : to every abelian scheme
$A_K$ over $K$, one has associated a canonical filtration :
\begin{equation}
\Tl(A_K)^t \;\subset\; \Tl(A_K)^f \;\subset\; \Tl(A_K) \; ,
\quad (\ell \neq p) \; ,
\end{equation}
whose successive factors are :
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Tl(A_K)^t$ : toric part, of rank $\mu = \dim T_0$, defined
over a finite extension of $K$ (semi-stable reduction) ;
\item $\Tl(A_K)^{\ab} = \Tl(A_K)^f/\Tl(A_K)^t$ : abelian part, of
rank $2\alpha = 2\dim B_0$, in perfect duality with the abelian part
of the dual ;
\item $\Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f$ : \'etale part, of rank $\mu$, isomorphic
to $M' \otimes \ZZ_{\ell}$ for the semi-stable reduction.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip

The ranks of these groups are related to the invariants of the
decomposition (2.2.2) by :
\begin{equation}
\dim A_K = \alpha + \lambda + \mu \; ,
\end{equation}
where $\alpha = \dim B_0$, $\lambda = \dim U_0$, $\mu = \dim T_0$.

In the semi-stable reduction case ($\lambda = 0$), one has
$\dim A_K = \alpha + \mu$, and the monodromy filtration reduces to :
\begin{equation}
0 \;\subset\; \Tl(A_K)^t \;\subset\; \Tl(A_K)^f \;\subset\;
\Tl(A_K) \; ,
\end{equation}
with :
\begin{itemize}
\item $\rang \Tl(A_K)^t = \mu$,
\item $\rang \Tl(A_K)^f = 2\alpha + \mu$,
\item $\rang \Tl(A_K)/\Tl(A_K)^f = \mu$.
\end{itemize}
