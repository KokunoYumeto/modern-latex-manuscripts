\section*{1. Introduction}
Throughout this expos\'e, unless the contrary is expressly stated, the word ``sheaf'' means ``abelian sheaf.''

Let \(X\) be a scheme and let \(\ell\) be a prime number. Recall that the \emph{\(\ell\)-cohomological dimension} of \(X\), denoted \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\), is the largest integer \(n\), or \(\infty\) if no such integer exists, such that \(H^n(X,F)\ne0\) for at least one \(\ell\)-torsion sheaf \(F\) on \(X\). If \(X=\Spec A\), we write \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell A=\operatorname{cd}_\ell\Spec A\). Taking the dictionary of VIII, 2 into account, this recovers the classical notion of Galois cohomological dimension in the case where \(A=k\) is a field. Since plainly
\[
\operatorname{cd}_\ell\Bigl(\coprod_iX_i\Bigr)=\sup_i\operatorname{cd}_\ell X_i,
\]
the classical theory extends immediately to arbitrary artinian rings. This theory is set out in Serre's \emph{Cohomologie galoisienne}, cited below as CG; we shall add a few refinements in Section 2.

For instance, let \(X\) be an irreducible algebraic variety of dimension \(n\) over a separably closed field \(k\), and fix \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\). The theorem of Section 4 gives the bound \(2n\), the ``true'' dimension suggested by the topological analogy when \(k=\Cc\). This result is quite elementary from the theory for fields. In fact the bound \(2n\) can be improved except in the complete case: there is a bound \(2n-1\) whenever at least one point is missing from \(X\). This is the stable bound in the sense that every \(X\) of dimension \(n\) contains an open subset of cohomological dimension \(2n-1\). For affine varieties one obtains the still sharper bound \(n\), a generalization of a Lefschetz theorem for hyperplane sections. These two latter assertions are deeper and will be proved only later, in XIV.

Cohomological dimension is not a local notion, unlike what happens for paracompact spaces. A henselian local ring with separably closed residue field has cohomological dimension zero, but after removing its closed point one may obtain arbitrarily large cohomological dimension, namely \(2n-1\) in the most important cases when \(n=\dim A\). This again agrees with the topological analogy supplied by the cohomology of \(U-\{x\}\), where \(U\) is a small open neighbourhood of a point of a complex analytic space.

\section*{2. Auxiliary results on fields}

\begin{statement}{Theorem 2.1}
Let \(k'/k\) be a finitely generated extension of a field \(k\), and let \(\ell\) be a prime. Then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_\ell k'\leq \operatorname{cd}_\ell k+\operatorname{trdeg}(k'/k).
\]
If the inequality is strict and \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\), then \(\ell=2\), the field \(k\) is orderable, and \(k'\) is not orderable, \ie \(-1\) is a sum of squares in \(k'\).
\end{statement}

When \(\ell=\operatorname{char}k\), one has \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k'\leq1\) (CG II, 2.2), so the question of equality in Theorem 2.1 scarcely arises. Also \(\operatorname{cd}_2k=\infty\) if \(k\) is orderable: an algebraic closure \(\bar k\) contains a subextension \(\widetilde k\), a maximal ordered subfield, with \([\bar k:\widetilde k]=2\); plainly \(\operatorname{cd}_2\widetilde k=\infty\), and \(\operatorname{cd}_2\widetilde k\leq\operatorname{cd}_2k\) by CG I, Proposition 14.

\begin{prooftext}
The inequality, and equality when \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k<\infty\), are due to Tate (CG II, 4.1.2). It remains to show that \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k'<\infty\) and \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k=\infty\) imply that \(k\) is orderable and \(\ell=2\). It is enough to treat (a) finite extensions and (b) \(k'=k(x)\) with \(x\) transcendental over \(k\). In case (a), after assuming the extension separable, the absolute Galois group of \(k'\) is an open subgroup of that of \(k\). A theorem of Serre gives equality of \(\ell\)-dimensions except when the larger group contains an element of order \(\ell\). An algebraically closed field has no finite-index subfield except in the case \(\ell=2\) and a real closed situation (Artin--Schreier), giving the claim. In case (b), take the henselization \(R\) of \(\Spec k[x]\) at \(x=0\), with fraction field \(K\). If \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k(x)<\infty\), then \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell K<\infty\); the result follows from the next theorem applied to \(R\).
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Theorem 2.2}
Let \(R\) be a henselian discrete valuation ring, with fraction field \(K\) and residue field \(k\). Let \(\ell\) be a prime. Then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_\ell K=\operatorname{cd}_\ell k+1
\]
in the following two cases:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\);
\item \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}K\), the field \(k\) is perfect, and \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k<\infty\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

This theorem was recently proved by Ax. If \(\ell=\operatorname{char}K\), one always has \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell K\leq1\) (CG II, 2.2). What remains in unequal characteristic, when \(\operatorname{char}k=\ell\) and \(k\) is imperfect, should be related to the absolute module of differentials \(\Omega^1_k\) (EGA IV, 20.4.3).

\begin{statement}{Lemma 2.2.1}
Let \(R\) be a henselian discrete valuation ring with fraction field \(K\), and let \(\widehat K\) be the fraction field of the completion \(\widehat R\). Then
\[
G(\overline{\widehat K}/\widehat K)\simeq G(\bar K/K).
\]
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
Equivalently, the functor \(L\mapsto L\otimes_K\widehat K\) from finite \(\etale\) \(K\)-algebras to finite \(\etale\) \(\widehat K\)-algebras is an equivalence. Essential surjectivity is obtained by approximating a separable irreducible equation over \(\widehat R\) by one over \(R\), using the usual Newton method. For full faithfulness it is enough to see that if \(K'\) is a field, then \(K'\otimes_K\widehat K\) is a field. If \(R'\) is the normalization of \(R\) in \(K'\), then \(R'\) is finite over \(R\), a discrete valuation ring, and its completion is \(R'\otimes_R\widehat R\); the corresponding total ring of fractions is therefore again a field.
\end{prooftext}

The proof of Theorem 2.2 is then the standard argument of Tate, together with the preceding lemma. In case (i), when \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k<\infty\), one reduces to the complete case in CG II, Proposition 12. To see that \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell K<\infty\) implies \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k<\infty\), let \(\widetilde K\) be the maximal unramified extension of \(K\), with residue field separably closed, and put \(G=G(\bar K/K)\), \(H=G(\bar K/\widetilde K)\), \(\bar G=G/H=G(\bar k/k)\). Passing to an \(\ell\)-Sylow subgroup of \(\bar G\) and using the structure of inertia gives the required finiteness. Case (ii) follows similarly from CG II, Proposition 12 after completion.

\begin{statement}{Theorem 2.3}
Let \(A\) be a strictly local ring, let \(k\) be its residue field, and let \(K\) be the total ring of fractions of \(A\). If \(K\) is noetherian and if \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\), then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_\ell K\leq \dim A.
\]
\end{statement}

The proof proceeds by noetherian induction and reduction to the case of a domain. One uses normalization, passage to finite extensions, and Theorem 2.2 applied to henselian localizations at codimension-one points. This is the same dévissage as in the classical proof for excellent local rings; the strict locality ensures that no residue-field contribution remains. For example, a strictly henselian discrete valuation ring has fraction field of \(\ell\)-cohomological dimension at most one for \(\ell\) prime to the residue characteristic.

\begin{statement}{Corollary 2.4}
Let \(A\) be a strictly local noetherian ring, let \(K\) be its total ring of fractions, and let \(\ell\) be invertible in \(A\). Then \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell K\leq\dim A\).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 2.5}
Let \(X\) be a noetherian scheme, let \(x\) be a point, and let \(X_{\bar x}\) be a strict localization at a geometric point over \(x\). If \(\ell\) is invertible at \(x\), the total ring of fractions of \(X_{\bar x}\) has \(\ell\)-cohomological dimension at most \(\operatorname{codim}x\).
\end{statement}

\section*{3. Fraction fields of a strictly local ring}

\subsection*{3.0}
Let \(A\) be strictly local with closed point \(s\), and let \(U=\Spec A-\{s\}\). The preceding estimates for fraction fields will be applied to the fibres of direct images from \(U\) to \(\Spec A\). The basic point is that the strict localization at a geometric point sees only the corresponding henselian local algebra, so that the cohomology of the punctured local scheme is controlled by the cohomological dimension of its total fraction ring.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 3.2}
Let \(A\) be strictly local noetherian of dimension \(n\), and let \(\ell\) be invertible in \(A\). Then for every \(\ell\)-torsion sheaf \(F\) on \(U=\Spec A-\{s\}\), one has
\[
H^q(U,F)=0\qquad(q>2n-1).
\]
If \(F\) is the restriction of a sheaf from \(\Spec A\), the bound improves in the expected low-dimensional cases.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The proof uses the Leray spectral sequence for the immersion \(j:U\to\Spec A\). Its higher direct images are computed on strict localizations, and Corollary 2.5 gives the necessary vanishing of their fibres. Induction on the dimension of the support then gives the bound.
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Lemma 3.3}
Let \(A\) be strictly local noetherian and let \(j:U\to\Spec A\) be the complement of the closed point. If \(\ell\) is invertible in \(A\), then the sheaves \(R^qj_*F\) have support constrained by codimension: their stalks vanish at a point once \(q\) exceeds the codimension of that point in the relevant strict localization.
\end{statement}

This is just the stalkwise form of Corollary 2.5 and is the technical input for the estimates in the next section.

\section*{4. Cohomological dimension when \(\ell\) is invertible in \(\Oo_X\)}

\begin{statement}{Theorem 4.1}
Let \(X\) be a noetherian scheme of dimension \(n\), and let \(\ell\) be invertible on \(X\). Suppose that the \(\ell\)-cohomological dimension of every residue field of \(X\) is bounded by \(N\). Then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\leq N+2n.
\]
More precisely, if an \(\ell\)-torsion sheaf \(F\) is supported in dimension \(\leq d\), then its cohomology vanishes above the corresponding bound \(N+2d\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
It is enough, by the constructible filtration theorem of IX, to treat constructible sheaves, then to proceed by induction on the support. On a dense open subset of each irreducible component the sheaf becomes locally constant and isotrivial; after a finite \(\etale\) cover and using the trace method of IX, 5, one reduces to constant sheaves. The local terms are controlled by Proposition 3.2 and Lemma 3.3. The spectral sequence for the inclusion of the generic points gives the bound.
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 4.2}
Let \(X\) be of finite type over a field \(k\), with \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\). Then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\leq \operatorname{cd}_\ell k+2\dim X.
\]
In particular, if \(k\) is separably closed, \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\leq2\dim X\).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 4.3}
Let \(X\) be of finite type over a separably closed field, and let \(\ell\) be invertible on \(X\). Then \(H^q(X,F)=0\) for every \(\ell\)-torsion sheaf \(F\) and every \(q>2\dim X\).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 4.4}
Let \(X\) be an algebraic curve over a separably closed field, with \(\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k\). Then \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\leq2\); if \(X\) is affine, the sharper bound \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\leq1\) is supplied later by XIV.
\end{statement}

\begin{remarktext}{Example 4.5}
One cannot hope for an analogous local formula in terms only of the local rings of \(X\). There are discrete valuation rings with algebraically closed residue field whose fraction fields have arbitrarily large cohomological dimension; they arise by taking formal curve arcs through a rational point of a smooth variety whose function field is prescribed.
\end{remarktext}

\subsection*{4.6}
These estimates will be used repeatedly in the sequel. Their force is that, for \(\ell\) invertible, the relevant cohomological dimension is twice the geometric dimension, plus the cohomological dimension of the ground field.

\section*{5. Cohomological dimension in the case \(\ell=p\)}

\subsection*{5.0}
Using Artin--Schreier theory (IX, 3.5), one obtains a better bound for the \(p\)-cohomological dimension of a scheme of characteristic \(p\). Let \(\operatorname{cd}_{\mathrm{qc}}X\) be the largest integer \(n\) such that \(H^n(X,F)\ne0\) for some quasi-coherent \(\Oo_X\)-module \(F\) in the Zariski sense.

\begin{statement}{Theorem 5.1}
Let \(X\) be a noetherian scheme of characteristic \(p>0\). Then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_pX\leq \operatorname{cd}_{\mathrm{qc}}X+1.
\]
In particular, a noetherian affine scheme of characteristic \(p>0\) has \(p\)-cohomological dimension at most one.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
Apply IX, 5.5. Since higher direct images under finite morphisms vanish for abelian sheaves, and likewise for quasi-coherent modules, one reduces to proving \(H^q(X,i_!(\Zz/p\Zz)_U)=0\) for an open immersion \(i:U\to X\) and \(q>\operatorname{cd}_{\mathrm{qc}}X+1\). Let \(Y\) be a closed subscheme with support \(X-U\), defined by a coherent ideal \(J\). The Artin--Schreier map \(f\mapsto f^p-f\) induces a morphism \(J\to J\), and the Artin--Schreier sequence gives
\[
0\longrightarrow i_!(\Zz/p\Zz)_U\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(J\) is coherent, its cohomology vanishes above \(\operatorname{cd}_{\mathrm{qc}}X\), giving the assertion.
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 5.2}
Let \(X\) be of finite type over a field \(k\) of characteristic \(p>0\). Then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_pX\leq \dim X+1.
\]
If \(k\) is separably closed and \(X\) is quasi-projective, then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_pX\leq \dim X.
\]
\end{statement}

The first assertion is clear from \(\operatorname{cd}_{\mathrm{qc}}X\leq\dim X\). For the second, the top coherent cohomology groups are finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector spaces. In the exact Artin--Schreier sequence above, the induced endomorphism of the top cohomology is of the form \(\epsilon-\operatorname{id}\), with \(\epsilon(rv)=r^p\epsilon(v)\). The corresponding additive morphism of affine space has Jacobian \(-I\), hence is \(\etale\); over a separably closed field it is surjective. The top obstruction vanishes.

\section*{6. Cohomological dimension for schemes of finite type over \(\Spec\Zz\)}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 6.1}
Let \(X\) be quasi-finite over \(\Spec\Zz\). Suppose either \(\ell\ne2\), or every residue field of \(X\) of characteristic zero is totally imaginary. Then \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\leq3\).
\end{statement}

This follows from Theorem 2.2(ii), Corollary 4.2, and the fact that a number field, totally imaginary when \(\ell=2\), has \(\ell\)-cohomological dimension at most two (CG II, 4.4). Class field theory shows that equality holds if \(X\to\Spec\Zz\) is proper and surjective.

The results of Section 4 do not apply unchanged when \(X\) is merely of finite type over \(\Spec\Zz\), because the hypotheses at points of residual characteristic \(\ell\) fail in general. The estimate of Theorem 5.1 must be used in that characteristic.

\begin{statement}{Theorem 6.2}
Let \(X\) be of finite type over \(\Spec\Zz\), and let \(\ell\) be a prime. If \(\ell=2\), suppose that no residue field of \(X\) is orderable. Then
\[
\operatorname{cd}_\ell X\leq2\dim X+1.
\]
More precisely, let \(F\) be an \(\ell\)-torsion sheaf such that, for every point \(x\) with \(F_{\bar x}\ne0\),
\[
\dim\overline{\{x\}}\leq
\begin{cases}
(N-1)/2,&\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k(x),\\
N-1,&\ell=\operatorname{char}k(x).
\end{cases}
\]
Then \(H^p(X,F)=0\) for \(p>N\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
Proceed by induction on \(X\), and reduce to constructible \(F\) by writing any sheaf as the filtered limit of its constructible subsheaves. The set of points where \(F_{\bar x}\ne0\) is then constructible, and induction handles the case where it is not dense. If the support is in characteristic \(\ell\), Theorem 5.1 gives the bound; if it is in characteristic \(p\ne\ell\), Corollary 4.3 and the fact \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell\mathbb{F}_p=1\) give it.

For a reduced \(X\), write \(X=X_0\cup X_1\), where \(X_1\) is the union of the irreducible components of characteristic \(\ell\), and \(X_0\) the union of the others. The exact sequence
\[
0\to F\to j_{0*}j_0^*F\oplus j_{1*}j_1^*F\to C\to0
\]
has \(C\) supported on \(X_0\cap X_1\), which is of characteristic \(\ell\) and dimension at most \(\dim X_1-1\). The induction and Theorem 5.1 reduce the assertion to the cases \(X=X_0\) and \(X=X_1\); the latter was already handled.

It remains to treat the case in which every maximal point has characteristic different from \(\ell\). Let \(i:x\to X\) be the generic point of an irreducible component. Using the exact sequence comparing \(F\) with the sum of its generic-point extensions, induction reduces to \(F=i_*G\). The spectral sequence
\[
H^p(X,R^qi_*G)\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}(\Spec k(x),G)
\]
finishes the proof. If \(s=\dim X\), then \(k(x)[i]\) has transcendence degree \(s-1\) over \(\Qq[i]\), a field of cohomological dimension two; Theorem 2.1 gives \(\operatorname{cd}_\ell k(x)=s+1\), under the non-orderability hypothesis when \(\ell=2\). The stalks of \(R^qi_*G\) vanish when \(q\) exceeds the appropriate codimension, by Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 2.2. Applying induction to these higher direct images gives the required vanishing.
\end{prooftext}
