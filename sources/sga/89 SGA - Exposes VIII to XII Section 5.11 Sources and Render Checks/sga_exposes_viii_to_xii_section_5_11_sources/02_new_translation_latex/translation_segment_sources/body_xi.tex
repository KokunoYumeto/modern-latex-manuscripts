\section*{1. Introduction}
In Section 4 we shall prove that \(\etale\) cohomology with values in a locally constant torsion sheaf agrees with classical cohomology for a scheme \(X\) smooth over \(\Spec\Cc\). The proof, essentially the one given in Giraud's Bourbaki expos\'e, is elementary once one uses Grauert's theorem recalled below (4.3(iii)), namely that every finite covering of \(X(\Cc)\) comes from an \(\etale\) covering of \(X\). We give it here even though a more complete result will be proved later using Hironaka's resolution of singularities. The good neighbourhoods constructed in Section 3 may also be useful in other situations.

It is not true that \(\etale\) cohomology and classical cohomology agree for non-torsion sheaves. For example, \(H^1(X_{\et},\Zz)=0\) for every normal scheme \(X\) (IX, 3.6(ii)), whereas the corresponding classical cohomology need not vanish.

\section*{2. Existence of sufficiently general hyperplane sections}

We shall use the following classical result, for which the original notes lacked a convenient reference.

\begin{statement}{Theorem 2.1}
Let \(k\) be a field, and let \(V\subset \mathbf{P}^n_k\) be a projective algebraic scheme purely of dimension \(r\). Let \(V'\) be the open subscheme of points of \(V\) smooth over \(\Spec k\), and let \(P\) be a rational point of \(\mathbf{P}^n\). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item A sufficiently general linear subspace \(L\) of prescribed dimension \(d\) in \(\mathbf{P}^n\) meets \(V\) transversally at every point of \(L\cap V'\).
\item Let \(\{H_1,\ldots,H_s\}\), \(s\leq r\), be hypersurfaces of degree \(d\geq2\) through \(P\), and put \(Y=H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_s\). If \(\{H_1,\ldots,H_s\}\) is sufficiently general, then \(Y\) meets \(V\) transversally at every point of \(Y\cap V'\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

Here ``sufficiently general'' means that the relevant parameter space, a Grassmannian in (i) or a product of projective spaces in (ii), contains a dense open subset over which the assertion holds. Thus it does not assert the existence of a rationally defined member when the ground field is finite.

\begin{prooftext}
For (i), induction reduces immediately to the case of a hyperplane. After covering \(V'\) by finitely many opens, one may assume an affine situation \(V\subset\mathbb A^n_k=\Spec k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\). The hyperplane has equation
\[
\ell(x)=a_0+\sum_{i=1}^n a_ix_i.
\]
Since a smooth scheme is locally a complete intersection, after shrinking one may suppose that \(V\) is cut out near \(V'\) by equations \(f_1,
\ldots,f_{n-r}\) with an invertible Jacobian minor. The condition that \(L\) fail to meet \(V\) transversally is then expressed by the existence of a point \(x\in V'\) satisfying an explicit finite system of equations in the parameters \(a_i\). The image of this closed condition is constructible by EGA IV, 9.5.1. It is enough to check the generic parameter, where the equations plainly have no solution. This proves (i).

For (ii), use the rational map \(\mathbf{P}^n\dashrightarrow\mathbf{P}^N\) defined by the linear system of degree \(d\) hypersurfaces through \(P\). For \(d\geq2\) it is the blow-up of \(P\) followed by a locally closed immersion off \(P\). Away from \(P\) the assertion reduces to (i) applied to the image. At \(P\) the conclusion is immediate for sufficiently general hypersurfaces.
\end{prooftext}

\section*{3. Construction of good neighbourhoods}

\begin{statement}{Definition 3.1}
An \emph{elementary fibration} is a morphism of schemes \(f:X\to S\) which can be embedded in a commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix@=2cm{
X\ar[r]^j\ar[rd]_f&\bar X\ar[d]^{\bar f}&Y\ar[l]_i\ar[ld]^g\\
&S&
}
\]
satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(j\) is an open immersion, dense in every fibre, and \(X=\bar X-Y\);
\item \(\bar f\) is smooth and projective, with geometrically irreducible fibres of dimension one;
\item \(g\) is an \(\etale\) covering, and every fibre of \(g\) is non-empty.
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

Such an embedding of \(X\), if it exists, is easily checked to be unique up to canonical isomorphism.

\begin{statement}{Definition 3.2}
A \emph{good neighbourhood relative to \(S\)} is an \(S\)-scheme \(X\) for which there exist \(S\)-schemes
\[
X=X_n,\ldots,X_0=S
\]
and elementary fibrations \(f_i:X_i\to X_{i-1}\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 3.3}
Let \(k\) be algebraically closed, let \(X/\Spec k\) be smooth, and let \(x\in X\) be a rational point. Then there is an open neighbourhood of \(x\) in \(X\) which is a good neighbourhood relative to \(\Spec k\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
Assume \(X\) irreducible. By induction on \(n=\dim X\), it is enough to find a neighbourhood \(U\) of \(x\) and an elementary fibration \(f:U\to V\), with \(V\) smooth of dimension \(n-1\). Indeed, a good neighbourhood of \(f(x)\) in \(V\) then pulls back to one of \(x\) in \(U\).

Embed \(X\) as an affine open in \(\mathbb A^r\), let \(X_0\) be its closure in \(\mathbf P^r\), let \(\bar X\) be the normalization of \(X_0\), and let \(Y=\bar X-X\) with reduced structure. Let \(S\subset\bar X\) be the singular locus; then \(S\subset Y\), \(\dim\bar X=n\), \(\dim Y=n-1\), and \(\dim S\leq n-2\).

Choose a projective embedding of \(\bar X\) by an invertible sheaf which is a sufficiently high power of a very ample sheaf. By Theorem 2.1 there exist hyperplanes \(H_1,\ldots,H_{n-1}\) through \(x\) such that \(L=H_1\cap\cdots\cap H_{n-1}\) meets \(\bar X\) and \(Y\) transversally. Then \(\bar X\cap L\) is a smooth connected curve, and \(Y\cap L\) is zero-dimensional. Choose an additional hyperplane \(H_0\) such that \(H_0\) meets \(\bar X\cap L\) transversally and avoids \(Y\cap L\).

Projection from the center \(C=H_0\cap\cdots\cap H_{n-1}\) gives a rational map \(\mathbf P^N\dashrightarrow\mathbf P^{n-1}\). Blow up \(C\), take the proper transform \(\bar X'\), put \(X'=X-\bar X\cap C\), and let \(Y'=\bar X'-X'\). We get a diagram
\[
\xymatrix@=2cm{
X'\ar[r]^j\ar[rd]_{f'}&\bar X'\ar[d]^{\bar f'}&Y'\ar[l]_i\ar[ld]^{g'}\\
&\mathbf P^{n-1}&
}
\]
Near the point \(v=f'(x)\), the fibre of \(\bar f'\) is identified with the smooth curve \(\bar X\cap L\); by Hironaka's lemma, smoothness near that fibre follows from smoothness at its generic point. The morphism \(g'\) is \(\etale\) near \(v\) because \(L\) meets \(Y\) transversally, and each fibre is non-empty. After replacing \(\mathbf P^{n-1}\) by a neighbourhood of \(v\), the diagram gives an elementary fibration.
\end{prooftext}

\section*{4. The comparison theorem}

\subsection*{4.0}
Let \(X\) be a scheme locally of finite type over \(\Spec\Cc\), and put on \(X(\Cc)\) its usual topology. Denote by \(X_{\mathrm{cl}}\) the site whose objects are local isomorphisms \(U\to X(\Cc)\), that is, maps of topological spaces which are locally homeomorphisms onto open subsets of \(X(\Cc)\). A family \(\{U_\nu\to U\}\) is covering if the images cover \(U\). Since open immersions are local isomorphisms, the site \(X_{\mathrm{cl}}\) has the same associated topos as the usual topological space \(X(\Cc)\). Thus one may compute ordinary cohomology using \(X_{\mathrm{cl}}\).

If \(f:X'\to X\) is \(\etale\), the analytic map \(f(\Cc):X'(\Cc)\to X(\Cc)\) is a local isomorphism, by the Jacobian criterion and the implicit function theorem. Therefore the functor \(X'\mapsto X'(\Cc)\) defines a morphism of sites
\[
\epsilon:X_{\mathrm{cl}}\longrightarrow X_{\et}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

\begin{statement}{Theorem 4.3}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(X\) is connected and non-empty if and only if \(X(\Cc)\) is connected and non-empty.
\item The functor \(\epsilon\) is fully faithful.
\item (Grauert--Remmert theorem.) The functor \(\epsilon\) induces an equivalence between finite coverings of \(X(\Cc)\), with its usual locally compact topology, and \(\etale\) coverings of \(X\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

We accept (i) as known; it follows, for example, from GAGA. Assertion (ii) follows easily from (i): for a morphism \(\varphi:X'(\Cc)\to X''(\Cc)\) over \(X(\Cc)\), after reducing to the connected affine separated case, its graph is a connected component of \((X'\times_XX'')(\Cc)\), hence comes from a connected component of \(X'\times_XX''\). This component is the graph of a morphism \(X'\to X''\). For (iii), by descent one reduces to \(X\) connected normal and affine. Taking a finite morphism \(X\to\mathbb A^n\), any finite connected covering of \(X(\Cc)\) becomes an analytic covering of \(\mathbb A^n(\Cc)\), extends to an analytic covering of \(\mathbf P^n(\Cc)\) by Grauert--Remmert, and the resulting coherent analytic algebra is algebraic by GAGA. Taking \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}A\) gives the desired \(\etale\) covering.

\begin{statement}{Theorem 4.4}
Let \(X\) be smooth over \(\Spec\Cc\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The category of constructible locally constant torsion sheaves on \(X_{\et}\) is equivalent to the category of locally constant torsion sheaves with finite fibres on \(X_{\mathrm{cl}}\); the quasi-inverse functors are \(\epsilon_*\) and \(\epsilon^*\).
\item If \(F\) is a locally constant torsion sheaf with finite fibres on \(X_{\mathrm{cl}}\), and if the induced sheaf on \(X_{\et}\) is again denoted \(F\), then
\[
R^q\epsilon_*F=0\quad(q>0).
\]
\item With the notation of (i), the canonical map
\[
H^q(X_{\et},F)\longrightarrow H^q(X_{\mathrm{cl}},F)
\]
is bijective for every \(q\). In particular,
\[
H^q(X_{\et},\Zz/n\Zz)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^q(X_{\mathrm{cl}},\Zz/n\Zz)
\]
for every \(q\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
For (i), the sheaves in question are precisely those represented by finite \(\etale\) coverings of \(X\), respectively finite coverings of \(X(\Cc)\). The equivalence of coverings is Theorem 4.3(iii). Assertion (iii) follows from (ii) and the Leray spectral sequence for \(\epsilon\). It remains to prove (ii).

The sheaf \(R^q\epsilon_*F\) is associated with the presheaf \(X'\mapsto H^q(X'_{\mathrm{cl}},F)\) on \(X_{\et}\). We must therefore show that every cohomology class becomes zero after passing to an \(\etale\) neighbourhood of any prescribed point.
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Lemma 4.5}
Let \(\xi\in H^q(X_{\mathrm{cl}},F)\), and let \(x\in X(\Cc)\). There exists an \(\etale\) morphism \(X'\to X\), whose image contains \(x\), such that the image of \(\xi\) in \(H^q(X'_{\mathrm{cl}},F)\) is zero.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The proof is by induction on \(n=\dim X\). The problem is local for the \(\etale\) topology, so one may assume \(F\) constant, and by dévissage even \(F=\Zz/n\Zz\). After replacing \(X\) by a Zariski neighbourhood of \(x\), Proposition 3.3 allows us to assume that \(X\) admits an elementary fibration \(f:X\to S\). With the notation of Definition 3.1, a direct calculation gives \(R^qj_{\mathrm{cl}*}F=0\) for \(q>1\), the sheaf \(R^1j_{\mathrm{cl}*}F\) is extension by zero of a constant sheaf on \(Y_{\mathrm{cl}}\), and \(j_{\mathrm{cl}*}F\) is the constant sheaf \(\Zz/n\Zz\) on \(\bar X\). Moreover \(R^q\bar f_{\mathrm{cl}*}(j_{\mathrm{cl}*}F)\) is computed fibrewise. The Leray spectral sequence implies that
\[
R^0f_{\mathrm{cl}*}\Zz/n\Zz=\Zz/n\Zz,
\quad R^1f_{\mathrm{cl}*}\Zz/n\Zz\text{ is locally constant torsion},
\quad R^qf_{\mathrm{cl}*}\Zz/n\Zz=0\ (q>1).
\]
Thus the Leray sequence reduces to
\[
\cdots\to H^q(S_{\mathrm{cl}},f_{\mathrm{cl}*}F)\to H^q(X_{\mathrm{cl}},F)\to H^{q-1}(S_{\mathrm{cl}},R^1f_{\mathrm{cl}*}F)\to\cdots.
\tag{*}
\]
Let \(s=f(x)\). By the induction hypothesis and (i), every class on \(S\) with finite locally constant torsion coefficients dies after an \(\etale\) neighbourhood \(S'\to S\) of \(s\). Since the functors \(R^qf_{\mathrm{cl}*}\) commute with \(\etale\) base change, the pullback \(X\times_SS'\to X\) is an \(\etale\) neighbourhood of \(x\), and the exact sequence (*) kills first the boundary term and then the remaining term. The case \(q=1\) also follows directly from the classification of principal finite coverings and Theorem 4.3(iii).
\end{prooftext}

\begin{remarktext}{Variant 4.6}
Serre gave a more elegant proof of Lemma 4.5. A connected good neighbourhood \(X\) satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(\pi_n(X(\Cc))=0\) for \(n>1\);
\item \(\pi_1(X(\Cc))\) is an iterated extension of finitely generated free groups.
\end{enumerate}
Indeed, for an elementary fibration \(X\to S\), the map \(X(\Cc)\to S(\Cc)\) is a locally trivial fibration whose fibre is a non-complete smooth connected curve; such a fibre has vanishing higher homotopy and free fundamental group. The long exact homotopy sequence gives the assertions by induction. Hence \(X(\Cc)\) is a \(K(\pi,1)\). A cohomology class becomes zero on the universal cover; it remains to show that it becomes zero on a finite cover. This is equivalent to the group \(\pi_1\) being good in Serre's sense, \ie
\[
H^q(\widehat\pi_1,M)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^q(\pi_1,M)
\]
for every finite continuous \(\pi_1\)-module \(M\). Iterated extensions of finitely generated free groups are good (CG I, pp. 15--16, Exercises 1, 2).
\end{remarktext}
