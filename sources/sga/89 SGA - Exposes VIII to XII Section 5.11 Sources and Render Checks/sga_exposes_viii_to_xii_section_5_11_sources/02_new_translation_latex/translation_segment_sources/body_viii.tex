\section*{1. Topological invariance of the \'etale topos}

\begin{statement}{Theorem 1.1}
Let \(f:X'\to X\) be an \emph{integral, surjective, radicial} morphism. Then the base-change functor
\[
f^*:X_{\et}\longrightarrow X'_{\et}
\]
is an equivalence of sites, that is, an equivalence of categories which is continuous, as is every quasi-inverse functor. Consequently the functors
\[
\begin{aligned}
f_*:\widetilde{X'_{\et}}&\longrightarrow \widetilde{X_{\et}},\\
f^*:\widetilde{X_{\et}}&\longrightarrow \widetilde{X'_{\et}}
\end{aligned}
\]
are quasi-inverse equivalences of categories.
\end{statement}

The first assertion is well known when \(f\) is of finite presentation or when \(f\) is flat (\SGA{} 1, IX, 4.10 and 4.11); the general case reduces to the finite-presentation case. Indeed, it is already known that the functor \(f^*\) is fully faithful (\SGA{} 1, IX, 3.4). It remains to prove that \(f^*\) is essentially surjective, \ie that every \(Z'\) \'etale over \(X'\) ``comes from'' a \(Z\) \'etale over \(X\). Using the fact that \(f\) is a universal homeomorphism, together with the full faithfulness of \(f^*\), one is reduced to the case where \(X\), \(X'\), and \(Z'\) are affine, say the spectra of rings \(A\), \(A'\), and \(B'\). Writing \(A'\) as the filtered inductive limit of its finite-type \(A\)-subalgebras \(A'_i\), the algebra \(B'\) comes from an \'etale algebra \(B'_i\) over some \(A'_i\) (\cf \EGA{} IV, 8). Thus one is reduced to the case where \(A'\) is integral and of \emph{finite type} over \(A\), hence finite over \(A\). Then \(A'\) is \(A\)-isomorphic to \(A''/J\), where \(A''\simeq A[t_1,\ldots,t_n]\) and \(J\) is an ideal of \(A''\). Writing \(J\) as the inductive limit of its finite-type subideals \(J_i\), and putting \(A'_i=A''/J_i\), one again has \(A'=\varinjlim_i A'_i\), so that \(B'\) comes from an \'etale algebra \(B'_i\) over some \(A'_i\) (loc. cit.). On the other hand, since \(\Spec A'\to\Spec A\) is surjective, the same is true of \(\Spec A'_i\to\Spec A\); furthermore one checks easily that, since \(\Spec A'\to\Spec A\) is radicial, the same is true of \(\Spec A'_i\to\Spec A\) for \(i\) sufficiently large. For example, apply \EGA{} IV, 1.9.9 to \(S=\Spec A\): if \(S_i\subset S\) denotes the set of points \(s\) for which the fibre of \(X_i=\Spec A'_i\) at \(s\) has separable rank one, then the \(S_i\) are constructible subsets of \(S\) (\EGA{} IV, 9.7.9), form an increasing family, and their union is \(S\), by the hypothesis on \(S'=\Spec A'\to S\) and the fact that the fibres of the \(S'_i\) are noetherian schemes. Hence \(S=S_i\) for \(i\) sufficiently large. This reduces us to the case where \(A'\) is one of the \(A'_i\), hence of finite presentation over \(A\).

This proves the first assertion of Theorem 1.1. The fact that \(f_*\) and \(f^*\) are quasi-inverse equivalences follows immediately.

\begin{statement}{Corollary 1.2}
Let \(F\) be an abelian sheaf on \(X\), and let \(F'\) be its inverse image on \(X'\). Then the canonical homomorphisms
\[
H^i(X,F)\longrightarrow H^i(X',F')
\tag{*}
\]
are isomorphisms. Likewise, if \(F'\) is an abelian sheaf on \(X'\) and \(F\) is its direct image on \(X\), the canonical homomorphisms \((*)\) are isomorphisms.
\end{statement}

\begin{remarktext}{Examples 1.3}
Here are some frequent applications of Theorem 1.1.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(X'\) is a subscheme of \(X\) with the same underlying set as \(X\), \ie it is defined by a nil-ideal \(I\).
\item \(X\) is a scheme over a separably closed field \(k\), \(k'\) is an algebraic closure of \(k\), and \(X'=X\otimes_k k'\).
\item Let \(X\) be a geometrically unibranch scheme, for example an algebraic curve over a field \(k\) having only ``cuspidal'' singularities, excluding in particular ordinary double points. If \(X'\) is the normalization of \(X_{\red}\), then by definition \(X'\to X\) is radicial, so that Theorem 1.1 applies.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarktext}

\begin{remarktext}{Remarks 1.4}
A morphism \(f\) as in Theorem 1.1 is a \emph{universal homeomorphism}, \ie it is a homeomorphism and remains one after every base extension. Conversely, if \(f\) is a universal homeomorphism, then \(f\) satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 1.1 (\cf \EGA{} IV, 8.11.6 when \(f\) is of finite presentation, and \EGA{} IV, 18.12.11 in general). This explains the title of the present section.

Concerning Example 1.3(c), note explicitly that if \(X\) is an algebraic curve, say over an algebraically closed field \(k\), having at least one singular point which is not unibranch, for instance an ordinary double point, and if \(X'\) denotes the normalization of \(X_{\red}\), then the conclusion of Corollary 1.2 already fails for \(H^1\) with constant coefficients; compare \SGA{} 1, I, 11(a), and \SGA{} 1, IX, 5.
\end{remarktext}

\section*{2. Sheaves on the spectrum of a field}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 2.1}
Let \(k\) be a field, let \(\bar{k}\) be a separable closure of \(k\), let \(\pi=\Gal(\bar{k}/k)\) be its topological Galois group, and put \(X=\Spec k\), \(\bar X=\Spec\bar k\), on which \(\pi\) acts on the left. Consider the canonical functor
\[
i:X_{\et}\longrightarrow \widetilde{X_{\et}}
\]
(defined by \(i(X')(X'')=\Hom_X(X',X'')\)), and the canonical functor
\[
j:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\longrightarrow \Bb_\pi
\]
from the category of sheaves on \(X_{\et}\) to the category \(\Bb_\pi\) of discrete sets endowed with a continuous left action of \(\pi\) (\cf IV, 2.7), defined by
\[
j(F)=\varinjlim_\alpha F(\Spec k_\alpha),
\]
where \(k_\alpha\) runs through the finite subextensions of \(\bar k\). Then \(i\) and \(j\) are equivalences of categories. Consequently the topos \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) is equivalent (IV, 3.4) to the classifying topos \(\Bb_{\pi^{\bullet}}\).
\end{statement}

The composite functor
\[
ji:X_{\et}\longrightarrow \widetilde{X_{\et}}\longrightarrow (\pi\text{-}\mathbf{Set})
\]
is an equivalence of categories by the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, in the form of \SGA{} 1, V. Since \(\Bb_\pi\) is plainly a topos (II, 4.14), the same is true of \(X_{\et}\). Moreover a family \((X_i\to X)\) of morphisms in \(X_{\et}\) is covering if and only if it is surjective, \ie if and only if its image in \(\Bb_\pi\) is surjective, or equivalently covering for the canonical topology of \(\Bb_\pi\). This shows that the topology of \(X_{\et}\) is indeed its canonical topology. Hence \(i\) is an equivalence, and therefore so is \(j\).

\begin{statement}{Corollary 2.2}
The functor \(j\) of Proposition 2.1 induces an equivalence between the category of abelian sheaves on \(X=\Spec k\) and the category of Galois \(\pi\)-modules.
\end{statement}

Indeed, the latter are precisely the abelian-group objects of the topos \(\Bb_\pi\). Similarly, by transport of structure one obtains the following.

\begin{statement}{Corollary 2.3}
Let \(F\) be an abelian sheaf on \(X=\Spec k\), and let \(M=j(F)\) be the associated Galois \(\pi\)-module. Then there is a canonical isomorphism of \(\delta\)-functors in \(F\):
\[
H^*(X,F)\simeq H^*(\pi,M),
\]
where the right hand side denotes Galois cohomology, studied for example in Serre's course \emph{Cohomologie galoisienne}.
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 2.4}
Assume that \(k\) is separably closed. Then the functor
\[
\Gamma:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\longrightarrow (\mathbf{Set})
\]
is an equivalence of categories. If \(F\) is an abelian sheaf on \(X=\Spec k\), then \(H^i(X,F)=0\) for \(i\ne 0\). In other words, the topos \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) is a punctual topos (IV, 2.2).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 2.5}
Let \(k'\) be a separably closed extension of a separably closed field \(k\), put \(X=\Spec k\) and \(X'=\Spec k'\), and let \(u:X'\to X\) be the canonical morphism. Then the functor
\[
u^*:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\longrightarrow \widetilde{X'_{\et}}
\]
is an equivalence of categories.
\end{statement}

\section*{3. Fiber functors associated with geometric points of a scheme}

\subsection*{3.1}
Let \(X\) be a scheme. We shall call a \emph{geometric point} of \(X\) any \(X\)-scheme \(\xi\) which is the spectrum of a separably closed field \(\Omega\). Giving such a point is therefore equivalent to giving an ordinary point \(x\in X\) and a separably closed extension \(\Omega\) of the residue field \(k(x)\). The most frequent case for us will be that where \(\Omega\) is a separable closure of \(k(x)\), which we shall generally denote by \(\bar{k(x)}\), and the corresponding geometric point of \(X\) by \(\bar x\). For fixed \(x\in X\), the fields \(\bar{k(x)}\), respectively the geometric points \(\bar x\), are determined only up to non-unique \(k(x)\)-isomorphism, respectively non-unique \(X\)-isomorphism.

\begin{remarktext}{Remarks 3.2}
In most geometric questions it is more natural to restrict to algebraically closed \(\Omega\). The different convention used here is special to the study of the \'etale topology. It is the convention adopted in the definition of the fundamental group in \SGA{} 1, V, 7. Note however that the developments of loc. cit. are equally valid with the convention adopted here, since the property of \(\Omega\) used there is that every \'etale covering of \(\Spec\Omega\) is trivial, precisely the assertion that \(\Omega\) is separably closed.
\end{remarktext}

\begin{statement}{Definition 3.3}
Let \(X\) be a scheme, let \(\xi\) be a geometric point of \(X\), and let \(u:\xi\to X\) be its structural morphism. The \emph{geometric fiber functor} associated with \(\xi\), denoted \(F\mapsto F_\xi\), is the composite functor
\[
\widetilde{X_{\et}}\xrightarrow{u^*}\widetilde{\xi_{\et}}\xrightarrow{\Gamma_\xi}(\mathbf{Set}).
\]
\end{statement}

Equivalently, by Corollary 2.4, a geometric point \(\xi\) of \(X\) defines a point of the topos \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) (IV, 6.1), and one takes the associated fiber functor.

\subsection*{3.4}
Since the functor \(\Gamma_\xi\) of sections over \(\xi\) is an equivalence of categories (2.4), knowing the fiber functors \(F\mapsto F_\xi\) is essentially equivalent to knowing the inverse-image functors \(u^*\). Moreover it follows immediately from Corollary 2.5 that if \(\xi\) is a geometric point of \(X\) and there exists an \(X\)-morphism \(\xi'\to\xi\), \ie if \(\xi'\) corresponds to a separably closed extension \(\Omega'\) of \(\Omega\), then the canonical homomorphism \(F_\xi\to F_{\xi'}\) is a functorial isomorphism
\[
F_\xi\simeq F_{\xi'}.
\]
Thus, for the study of fiber functors, one may, after replacing \(\Omega\) by the separable algebraic closure of \(k(x)\) in \(\Omega\), restrict to the case where \(k(\xi)\) is a separable closure \(\bar{k(x)}\) of \(k(x)\). The fiber functors corresponding to geometric points of \(X\) localized at the same point \(x\in X\) are isomorphic, non-canonically.

In accordance with the conventions of 3.1, we shall denote by \(F_{\bar x}\) a fiber functor corresponding to a chosen separable closure \(\bar{k(x)}\) of \(k(x)\).

We also record an evident transitivity property, which explains the technical usefulness of not restricting exclusively to geometric points defined by separable closures of residue fields. Let \(f:X'\to X\) be a morphism of schemes, and let \(u':\xi'\to X'\) be a geometric point of \(X'\). Then \(u=fu':\xi'\to X\) is a geometric point of \(X\), which we denote by \(\xi\). For these geometric points one has a functorial isomorphism in \(F\in\Ob\widetilde{X_{\et}}\):
\[
f^*(F)_{\xi'}\simeq F_\xi.
\]
This follows from the transitivity formula for inverse-image functors,
\[
(fu)^*\simeq u^*f^*.
\]

\begin{statement}{Theorem 3.5}
Let \(X\) be a scheme.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item For every geometric point \(\xi\) of \(X\), the fiber functor \(F\mapsto F_\xi\), from \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) to \((\mathbf{Set})\), commutes with arbitrary inductive limits, indexed by categories in \(\U\), and with finite projective limits. In particular it sends sheaves of groups, respectively abelian sheaves, and so on, to groups, respectively abelian groups, and so on.
\item As \(x\) runs over the points of \(X\), the fiber functors \(F_{\bar x}\) form a conservative family: a morphism \(u:F\to G\) of sheaves on \(X\) is an isomorphism if and only if, for every \(x\in X\), the corresponding morphism \(u_{\bar x}:F_{\bar x}\to G_{\bar x}\) is an isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

Taking account of the exactness properties in (a), we immediately record the following formal consequences of (b).

\begin{statement}{Corollary 3.6}
Let \(u:F\to G\) be a morphism of sheaves. Then \(u\) is a monomorphism, respectively an epimorphism, if and only if for every \(x\in X\), the map \(u_{\bar x}:F_{\bar x}\to G_{\bar x}\) is injective, respectively surjective.
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 3.7}
Let \(u,v:F\to G\) be two morphisms of sheaves on \(X\). Then \(u=v\) if and only if, for every \(x\in X\), one has \(u_{\bar x}=v_{\bar x}:F_{\bar x}\to G_{\bar x}\). In particular, if \(u,v\) are two sections of \(F\), then \(u=v\) if and only if \(u_{\bar x}=v_{\bar x}\) in \(F_{\bar x}\) for every \(x\in X\).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 3.8}
Let \(F\to G\to H\) be a sequence of two morphisms of sheaves on \(X\). The sequence is exact if and only if, for every \(x\in X\), the corresponding sequence \(F_{\bar x}\to G_{\bar x}\to H_{\bar x}\) is exact.
\end{statement}

We leave to the reader the deduction of these corollaries (\cf IV, 6.5.2), although they are also proved in part in the proof below.

Assertion (a) follows from the exactness properties already noted in Expos\'e VII, 1.4 for every inverse-image functor, such as \(u^*:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\to\widetilde{\xi_{\et}}\), and from the fact that \(\widetilde{\xi_{\et}}\to(\mathbf{Set})\) is an equivalence. To prove (b), note the following formula, which gives a way to \emph{compute} fiber functors.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 3.9}
Let \(\xi\xrightarrow{u}X\) be a geometric point of \(X\), and let \(C_\xi\) be the category of ``\(\xi\)-pointed \'etale \(X\)-schemes'', \ie pairs \((X',u')\), where \(X'\in\Ob X_{\et}\) and \(u':\xi\to X'\) is an \(X\)-morphism. Then the opposite category \(C_\xi^\circ\) is filtering, and for a variable sheaf \(F\), respectively presheaf \(P\), on \(X\), there is a canonical functorial isomorphism
\[
F_\xi\simeq \varinjlim_{C_\xi^\circ}F(X'),\qquad
(aP)_\xi\simeq \varinjlim_{C_\xi^\circ}P(X'),
\]
where \(aP\) denotes the sheaf associated with \(P\).
\end{statement}

Indeed, first note that for every presheaf \(P\) on \(\xi_{\et}\), if \(aP\) is its associated sheaf, the natural homomorphism
\[
P(\xi)\longrightarrow aP(\xi)
\]
is an isomorphism; this follows immediately, for example, from the explicit computation of \(aP\) as \(L(LP)\) (II, 3.19). Let \(\varphi:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\to\widetilde{\xi_{\et}}\) be the inverse-image functor. Then \(u^*=\varphi^*\) is, by III, 2.3(d), the composite \(a\varphi^\bullet i\), where \(i:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\to\widehat{X_{\et}}\) is the inclusion functor and \(a:\widehat{\xi_{\et}}\to\widetilde{\xi_{\et}}\) is the associated-sheaf functor. By the preceding remark,
\[
u^*(F)(\xi)=(\varphi^\bullet i(F))(\xi).
\]
Thus it remains to apply the explicit expression for \(\varphi^\bullet\) given in the proof of the corresponding general formula. The analogous assertion for a presheaf \(P\) is proved in the same way, using the functorial isomorphism \(\varphi^*a(P)\simeq a\varphi^*(P)\). The fact that \(C_\xi\) is filtering, which remains true for \emph{any} \(X\)-scheme \(\xi\), not necessarily a geometric point, follows immediately from the existence of finite projective limits in \(C_\xi\); this in turn follows from the fact that \(X_{\et}\) is a subcategory of \((\Sch)_{/X}\) stable under finite projective limits.

Now let \(u:F\to G\) be a morphism of sheaves on \(X\) such that, for every \(x\in X\), the map \(u_{\bar x}:F_{\bar x}\to G_{\bar x}\) is a monomorphism. We prove that \(u\) is a monomorphism. It suffices to show that if \(X'\in\Ob X_{\et}\) and \(\varphi,\psi\in F(X')\) have the same image under \(u\), then \(\varphi=\psi\). Replacing \(X\) by \(X'\) and using the transitivity of fiber functors, we may suppose \(X=X'\). For every \(x\in X\), we have \(u(\varphi)_{\bar x}=u(\psi)_{\bar x}\), hence \(u_{\bar x}(\varphi_{\bar x})=u_{\bar x}(\psi_{\bar x})\), and therefore \(\varphi_{\bar x}=\psi_{\bar x}\). By Proposition 3.9, there exists \(X'_x\in\Ob X_{\et}\), whose image contains \(x\), such that \(\varphi\) and \(\psi\) have the same inverse image on \(X'_x\). As \(x\) varies over \(X\), the \(X'_x\) form a covering family of \(X\); hence \(\varphi=\psi\).

This proves that if all \(u_{\bar x}\) are monomorphisms, then so is \(u\). Suppose in addition that all \(u_{\bar x}\) are epimorphisms. We prove that \(u\) is an epimorphism, hence an isomorphism. It remains to show that for every \(X'\in\Ob X_{\et}\) and every \(\psi\in G(X')\), there exists \(\varphi\in F(X')\) with \(u(X')(\varphi)=\psi\). Again we may suppose \(X'=X\). By Proposition 3.9, for every \(x\in X\) there exists \(X'_x\in\Ob X_{\et}\), with image containing \(x\), and an element \(\varphi_x\in F(X'_x)\) whose image in \(G(X'_x)\) is the inverse image of \(\psi\). Since \(u\) is a monomorphism, the \(\varphi_x\) agree on the fibre products \(X'_x\times_X X'_y\), and hence come from a well-determined element \(\varphi\in F(X)\). Then \(u(\varphi)=\psi\), since the inverse images on the \(X'_x\) agree. \qedtext

\begin{remarktext}{Scholium 3.10}
Theorem 3.5 implies that the collection of fiber functors for the \'etale topology has the same essential properties as in the usual theory of sheaves on a topological space. We shall use Theorem 3.5 and its corollaries very frequently, and for this reason we shall usually refrain from referring to them explicitly.
\end{remarktext}

\subsection*{3.11}
For every \(x\in X\) we shall also, by abuse of language, denote by \(x\) the \(X\)-scheme \(\Spec k(x)\), with its usual structural morphism
\[
i_x:x=\Spec k(x)\longrightarrow X.
\]
It gives rise to a canonical functor
\[
i_x^*:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\longrightarrow \widetilde{x_{\et}}.
\]
If \(F\) is a sheaf on \(X\), put \(F_x=i_x^*(F)\). Thus \(F_x\) is a sheaf on \(x=\Spec k(x)\), and as such can also be identified with an \'etale scheme over \(x\), by Proposition 2.1. It depends functorially on \(F\), and the functor \(i_x^*:F\mapsto F_x\) again commutes with arbitrary inductive limits and finite projective limits, by Expos\'e VII, 1.4.

If \(\bar x=\Spec\bar{k(x)}\), the fiber functor \(F\mapsto F_{\bar x}\) is canonically isomorphic to the composite of \(F\mapsto F_x\) with the functor \(j\) of Proposition 2.1, taking into account that the latter is isomorphic to the fiber functor associated with the geometric point \(\Spec\bar k\) of \(\Spec k\). It follows immediately from this and Theorem 3.5 that the family of functors \((F\mapsto F_x)\), for \(x\in X\), is still conservative. If \(k(x)\) is separably closed, \ie \(x=\bar x\), then the notations \(F_x\) and \(F_{\bar x}\) are strictly contradictory, the first denoting an object of \(\widetilde{x_{\et}}\), the second a set; but by Proposition 2.1 this is an inessential contradiction.

\subsection*{3.12}
The preceding remarks show that, if \(\bar x=\Spec\bar{k(x)}\), then for every sheaf \(F\) on \(X\) the group \(\pi_x=\Gal(\bar{k(x)}/k(x))\) acts naturally on the left on the fiber set \(F_{\bar x}\). Thus \(F\mapsto F_{\bar x}\) may in fact be regarded as a functor
\[
\widetilde{X_{\et}}\longrightarrow \Bb_{\pi_x},
\]
whose knowledge is essentially equivalent, by Proposition 2.1, to that of the functor \(F\mapsto F_x\).

This shows, in an important special case, that contrary to what happens for sheaves on topological spaces, fiber functors generally have nontrivial automorphisms. In general, Theorem 7.9 below will determine all homomorphisms from one fiber functor to another.

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 3.13}
The following is a sometimes useful generalization of Theorem 3.5(b). Let \(E\) be a subset of \(X\) such that, for every \'etale morphism \(f:X'\to X\), the inverse image \(E'=f^{-1}(E)\) is very dense in \(X'\) (\EGA{} IV, 10.1.3), \ie every open subset \(U\) of \(X'\) containing \(E'\) is equal to \(X'\). Then the fiber functors \(F_{\bar x}\) on \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\), for \(x\in E\), already form a conservative family. Consequently Corollaries 3.6, 3.7, and 3.8 remain valid when one restricts to \(x\in E\). This is seen immediately by repeating the proof of Theorem 3.5(b). The preceding result applies in particular in the following situations.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(X\) is a Jacobson scheme (\EGA{} IV, 10.4.1), for example locally of finite type over a field or over \(\Spec\mathbb Z\), and \(E\) is the set of closed points of \(X\).
\item \(X\) is locally of finite type over a scheme \(S\), and \(E\) is the set of points of \(X\) which are closed in their fibre.
\item \(X\) is locally noetherian, and \(E\) is the set of \(x\in X\) such that \(\overline{\{x\}}\) is a finite set, \ie a semi-local scheme of dimension \(\leq 1\); compare \EGA{} IV, 10.5.3 and 10.5.5.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarktext}

\section*{4. Strictly local rings and schemes}

\subsection*{4.1}
Recall that, following Azumaya, a local ring \(A\) is called \emph{henselian} if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Every finite \(A\)-algebra \(B\) is a direct product \(\prod_i B_i\) of local rings. The \(B_i\) are then necessarily isomorphic to the localizations \(B_{\mathfrak m_i}\), where the \(\mathfrak m_i\) run through the maximal ideals of \(B\).
\item Every \(A\)-algebra \(B\) which is the localization of a finite-type \(A\)-algebra \(C\) at a prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) over the maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\) of \(A\), and such that \(B/\mathfrak mB\) is finite over \(k=A/\mathfrak m\), is finite over \(A\).
\item As in (ii), but assuming \(C\) to be \'etale over \(A\) at \(\mathfrak p\), and \(k\to B/\mathfrak mB\) to be an isomorphism.
\item Hensel's lemma, in its classical form, holds for \(A\): for every monic polynomial \(F\in A[T]\), denoting by \(\dot F\in k[T]\) its reduction, and for every factorization
\[
\dot F=\dot F_1\dot F_2
\]
of \(\dot F\) as a product of two coprime monic polynomials, \(\dot F_1\) and \(\dot F_2\) lift, necessarily uniquely, to \(F_1,F_2\in A[T]\) such that \(F=F_1F_2\).
\end{enumerate}
For the proof of these equivalences and the general properties of henselian rings, see \EGA{} IV, 18.5.11. Recall that if \(A\) is any local ring, one proves, following Nagata, that there is a local homomorphism
\[
A\longrightarrow A^h
\]
with \(A^h\) henselian, universal for local homomorphisms from \(A\) to henselian local rings. The ring \(A^h\) is called the henselization of \(A\). The construction in \EGA{} IV, 18.6, different from Nagata's, consists in putting
\[
A^h=\varinjlim_i A_i,
\]
where \(A_i\) runs through the \(A\)-algebras essentially of finite type and essentially \'etale over \(A\), \ie localizations of an \'etale \(A\)-algebra, such that \(A\to A_i\) is local and the residue extension \(k(A_i)/k(A)\) is trivial. This construction shows in particular that \(A^h\) is flat over \(A\), and that if \(A\) is noetherian then so is its henselian closure.

\begin{statement}{Definition 4.2}
A local ring \(A\) is called \emph{strictly local} if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(A\) is henselian and its residue field is separably closed.
\item Every local homomorphism \(A\to B\), where \(B\) is the localization of an \'etale \(A\)-algebra, is an isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
A scheme is called a \emph{strictly local scheme} if it is isomorphic to the spectrum of a strictly local ring.
\end{statement}

The equivalence of (i) and (ii) follows immediately from 4.1. In geometric form, 4.2(ii), respectively 4.1(iii), says that for every scheme \(X\) \'etale over \(Y=\Spec A\), and every point \(x\) of the fibre \(X_y\), respectively every point of \(X_y\) rational over \(k(y)\), where \(y\) is the closed point of \(Y\), there exists a section of \(X\) over \(Y\) passing through \(y\), necessarily unique.

\begin{statement}{Definition 4.3}
Let \(X\) be a scheme, let \(\Oo_{X_{\et}}\) be the sheaf on \(X_{\et}\) defined by
\[
\Oo_{X_{\et}}(X')=\Gamma(X',\Oo_{X'}),
\]
compare Expos\'e VII, 2(c), and let \(u:\xi=\Spec\Omega\to X\) be a geometric point of \(X\). The \emph{strictly local ring of \(X\) at \(\xi\)}, denoted \(\Oo_{X,\xi}\), or simply \(\Oo_\xi\), is the fibre of the sheaf \(\Oo_{X_{\et}}\) at the point \(\xi\). Its spectrum is called the \emph{strict localization of \(X\) at \(\xi\)}.
\end{statement}

By Proposition 3.9, the fibre \(\Oo_{X,\xi}\) has the description
\[
\Oo_{X,\xi}=\varinjlim \Gamma(X',\Oo_{X'}),
\tag{*}
\]
where \(X'\) runs through the filtering opposite category of \'etale \(X\)-schemes pointed by \(\xi\), \ie endowed with an \(X\)-morphism \(\xi\to X'\). By transitivity of inductive limits, one may replace \(\Gamma(X',\Oo_{X'})\) on the right by \(\Oo_{X',x'}\), where \(x'\) is the image of \(\xi\) in \(X'\), and obtains
\[
\Oo_{X,\xi}=\varinjlim_i A_i,
\tag{**}
\]
where \(A_i\) runs through the algebras essentially of finite type and \'etale over \(A=\Oo_{X,x}\), endowed with a \(k\)-homomorphism \(k(A_i)\to\Omega\). Here \(x\) denotes the image of \(\xi\) in \(X\). The transition homomorphisms among the \(A_i\) are the local homomorphisms of \(A\)-algebras \(A_i\to A_j\) making the corresponding triangle commute,
\[
\xymatrix@R=1.0cm@C=1.6cm{
k(A_i)\ar[r]\ar[rd]&k(A_j)\ar[d]\\
&\Omega,
}
\]
which implies that if there exists an admissible homomorphism from \(A_i\) to \(A_j\), it is unique. Thus the inductive limit \((**)\) is a limit over an increasing filtered ordered set.

\subsection*{4.4}
The description \((**)\) shows that the strictly local ring of \(X\) at \(\xi\) depends essentially only on the usual local ring \(A=\Oo_{X,x}\), where \(x=u(\xi)\), and on the extension \(\Omega\) of \(k=k(A)\). It is also called the \emph{strict henselization} of \(A\), relative to the chosen separably closed extension \(\Omega\) of \(k\), and is denoted \(A^{hs}\). We refer to \EGA{} IV, 18.8 for the general properties of \(A^{hs}\). We merely point out that the construction above again shows that \(A^{hs}\) is flat over \(A\), and noetherian if \(A\) is noetherian. On the other hand, one easily proves (loc. cit.) that the local homomorphism
\[
A\longrightarrow A^{hs}
\]
endowed with the \(k\)-homomorphism
\[
k(A^{hs})\longrightarrow \Omega
\]
solves the universal problem, relative to the data \(A\) and the extension \(\Omega\) of \(k\), of finding local homomorphisms
\[
A\longrightarrow A'
\]
with \(A'\) strictly local, endowed with a \(k\)-homomorphism
\[
k(A')\longrightarrow \Omega.
\]
In particular, \(A^{hs}\) \emph{is} a strictly local ring, justifying the terminology in Definition 4.3.

\subsection*{4.5}
Keep the notation of Definition 4.3 and choose an affine open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(x\). In Proposition 3.9 one may plainly restrict to those \(X'\) which are affine over \(U\). These then form a pseudo-filtered projective system of affine schemes, and we are in the general situation of Expos\'e VII, 5 (\cf VI, 5.12(b)). By the expression \((*)\) for \(\Oo_{X,\xi}\), one has an \(X\)-isomorphism
\[
\varprojlim_{\substack{X'\ \text{\'etale}\\ \text{affine over }U\\ \xi\text{-pointed}}}X'
\simeq \Spec \Oo_{X,\xi},
\tag{***}
\]
which makes precise the intuitive geometric meaning of \(\Spec\Oo_{X,\xi}\) as the \emph{limit of the \'etale neighbourhoods of \(\xi\)}.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 4.6}
Let \(X\) be a strictly local scheme, and let \(x\) be its closed point, hence a geometric point of \(X\). Then the functor \(F\mapsto\Gamma(X,F):\widetilde{X_{\et}}\to(\mathbf{Set})\) is canonically isomorphic to the fiber functor \(F\mapsto F_x\), and therefore commutes with arbitrary inductive limits, not necessarily filtered.
\end{statement}

Indeed, by Definition 4.2(ii), the final object \(X\) of the category of \(x\)-pointed \'etale \(X\)-schemes considered in Proposition 3.9 dominates all the other objects. Hence \(\varinjlim F(X')\) is canonically isomorphic to \(F(X)\). \qedtext

In particular, the induced functor \(\Gamma\) on the category of abelian sheaves is exact. Therefore:

\begin{statement}{Corollary 4.7}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition 4.6, for every abelian sheaf \(F\) on \(X\),
\[
H^q(X,F)=0\qquad\text{for }q\ne 0.
\]
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 4.8}
Let \(X\) be a scheme, let \(\xi\) be a geometric point of \(X\), let \(\bar X=\Spec\Oo_{X,\xi}\) be the corresponding strictly localized scheme, let \(F\) be a variable sheaf on \(X\), and let \(\bar F\) be its inverse image on \(\bar X\). Then there is a functorial isomorphism
\[
F_\xi\simeq \Gamma(\bar X,\bar F).
\]
\end{statement}

Indeed, \(\xi\) may also be regarded as a geometric point of \(\bar X\), and it suffices to apply Proposition 4.6 and the transitivity of fibres (3.4).

\section*{5. Application to computing the fibres of \(R^qf_*\)}

\subsection*{5.1}
Let
\[
f:X\to Y
\]
be a morphism of schemes, and let \(F\) be an abelian sheaf on \(X\). We want to determine \(R^qf_*(F)\). By Theorem 3.5, this amounts, up to minor qualifications, to knowing the geometric fibres \(R^qf_*(F)_{\bar y}\), for \(y\in Y\). Now by Expos\'e V, 5.1, \(R^qf_*(F)\) is the sheaf associated with the presheaf
\[
\Hh^q:Y'\longmapsto H^q(X\times_YY',F)
\]
on \(Y\). Therefore, by Proposition 3.9,
\[
R^qf_*(F)_{\bar y}\simeq \varinjlim H^q(X\times_YY',F),
\tag{5.1.1}
\]
where the inductive limit is taken over the filtering opposite category of the \(Y'\) \'etale over \(Y\) pointed by \(\bar y\). Choosing an affine open neighbourhood \(U\) of \(y\), one may restrict in the limit to the cofinal category of the \(Y'\) which are affine and lie over \(U\).

Introduce
\[
\bar Y=\Spec(\Oo_{Y,\bar y})=\varprojlim Y'
\]
(\cf 4.5), and
\[
\bar X=X\times_Y\bar Y.
\]
In the projective system of the \(X\times_YY'\), obtained from that of the \(Y'\) by base change along \(X\to Y\), the transition morphisms are affine. Thus one is under the general hypotheses of Expos\'e VII, 5.1, and by the construction of \(\varprojlim\) in loc. cit. there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
\bar X=\varprojlim (X\times_YY').
\]
Let \(\bar F\) be the inverse image of \(F\) on \(\bar X\). One obtains a canonical homomorphism
\[
\varinjlim_{Y'} H^q(X\times_YY',F)\longrightarrow H^q(\bar X,\bar F),
\]
and hence, by comparison with (5.1.1), a canonical homomorphism, functorial in \(F\),
\[
R^qf_*(F)_{\bar y}\longrightarrow H^q(\bar X,\bar F).
\tag{5.1.2}
\]

Assume now that \(f:X\to Y\) is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Then the same is true of \(X\times_YY'\to Y'\), and since \(Y'\) is affine the schemes \(X\times_YY'\) are quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Using (5.1.1) and the limit-passage theorem of Expos\'e VII, 5.8, we obtain the following.

\begin{statement}{Theorem 5.2}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated morphism of schemes, let \(F\) be an abelian sheaf on \(X\), let \(y\) be a point of \(Y\), let \(\bar y\) be the geometric point above \(y\) associated with a separable closure \(k(\bar y)\) of \(k(y)\), let \(\bar Y=\Spec\Oo_{Y,\bar y}\) be the corresponding strict localization, put \(\bar X=X\times_Y\bar Y\), and let \(\bar F\) be the inverse image of \(F\) on \(\bar X\). Then the canonical homomorphism (5.1.2) is an isomorphism:
\[
R^qf_*(F)_{\bar y}\simeq H^q(\bar X,\bar F).
\]
\end{statement}

This statement practically reduces the determination of the fibres of a sheaf \(R^qf_*(F)\) to the determination of the cohomology of a prescheme over a strictly local scheme, and will be used constantly below. Technically, Theorem 5.2 explains the important role of henselian rings and strictly local rings in the study of \'etale cohomology.

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 5.3}
The statement remains valid for \(R^0f_*(F)=f_*(F)\), with \(F\) an arbitrary sheaf of sets. Also, Theorem 5.2 remains valid for \(R^1f_*(F)\) when \(F\) is a sheaf of groups, not necessarily commutative, using the usual definition of \(R^1f_*(F)\) (\cf Giraud's thesis).
\end{remarktext}

\subsection*{5.4}
Assume now that \(f\) is a \emph{finite} morphism. Then \(\bar f:\bar X\to\bar Y\) is finite, and since \(\bar Y\) is strictly local it follows that \(\bar X\) is a finite sum of strictly local schemes \(\bar X_i\). Therefore, using Corollary 4.7,
\[
H^q(\bar X,\bar F)=0\qquad\text{for }q\ne 0.
\tag{5.4.1}
\]
On the other hand, the components \(\bar X_i\) of \(\bar X\) correspond to the points \(\bar x_i\) of \(\bar X\) above the closed point \(\bar y\) of \(\bar Y\), \ie to the points of
\[
\bar X_{\bar y}=X_y\otimes_{k(y)}k(\bar y).
\]
These points may also be regarded as geometric points of \(X\), and \(\bar X_i\) is then nothing other than the strict localization of \(X\) at \(\bar x_i\). Thus, by Corollary 4.8,
\[
H^0(\bar X_i,\bar F)\simeq F_{\bar x_i},
\]
and hence
\[
H^0(\bar X,\bar F)=\prod_i F_{\bar x_i},
\tag{5.4.2}
\]
a functorial isomorphism in the sheaf of sets \(F\); here it is not necessary to restrict to abelian sheaves. Taking Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.3 into account, formulas (5.4.1) and (5.4.2) give the following.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 5.5}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be a finite morphism of preschemes, and let \(y\) be a point of \(Y\). For every sheaf \(F\) on \(X\), there is a functorial isomorphism
\[
f_*(F)_{\bar y}\simeq \prod_{\bar x\in X_y\otimes_{k(y)}k(\bar y)}F_{\bar x}.
\]
Consequently formation of \(f_*(F)\) commutes with every base change \(y'\to Y\). If \(F\) is an abelian sheaf, then
\[
R^qf_*(F)=0\qquad\text{for }q>0.
\]
\end{statement}

The first formula in Proposition 5.5 is in fact independent of Theorem 5.2 and of the general limit-passage theorem of Expos\'e VII, 5.7. It implies, by Theorem 3.5, that \(F\mapsto f_*(F)\) is an exact functor on the category of abelian sheaves, again giving \(R^qf_*(F)=0\) for \(q\ne 0\). Here is a slight variant of Proposition 5.5.

\begin{statement}{Corollary 5.6}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be an integral morphism. Then for every abelian sheaf \(F\) on \(X\),
\[
R^qf_*(F)=0\qquad\text{for }q\ne 0.
\]
Moreover, the functor \(f_*\) on sheaves of sets commutes with every base change \(Y'\to Y\).
\end{statement}

Indeed, by Theorem 5.2 we are reduced to proving that, when \(Y\) is strictly local, \(H^q(X,F)=0\) for every abelian sheaf \(F\) on \(X\). Write \(Y=\Spec A\), \(X=\Spec B\), with \(B\) an integral \(A\)-algebra. Writing \(B=\varinjlim B_i\), where the \(B_i\) run through the finite-type subalgebras of \(B\), which are even finite over \(A\), we have \(X=\varprojlim_i X_i\), where \(X_i=\Spec B_i\). By Expos\'e VII, 5.13, it is enough to prove that \(R^qf_*(F_i)=0\) for \(q\ne 0\) with \(F_i\) an abelian sheaf on \(X_i\), and this follows from Proposition 5.5. The final assertion of Corollary 5.6 is proved by the same reduction to Proposition 5.5.

\begin{statement}{Corollary 5.7}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be an immersion. Then for every sheaf \(F\) on \(X\), the canonical morphism
\[
f^*f_*(F)\longrightarrow F
\]
is an isomorphism.
\end{statement}

Since \(f\) factors as the product of an open immersion and a closed immersion, and Corollary 5.7 is trivial for an open immersion (IV, No. 3), one is reduced to the case where \(f\) is a closed immersion. It is enough to prove that for every \(x\in X\), the corresponding homomorphism on geometric fibres
\[
(f^*f_*(F))_{\bar x}\longrightarrow F_{\bar x}
\]
is bijective. By transitivity of fibres (3.4), the left hand side is just the fibre \(f_*(F)_{\bar x}\), so it remains to check that the canonical homomorphism
\[
f_*(F)_{\bar x}\longrightarrow F_{\bar x}
\]
is bijective. This follows immediately from Proposition 5.5.

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 5.8}
Using Proposition 5.5 and proceeding as in Corollary 5.6, one easily proves that if \(f:X\to Y\) is integral and \(F\) is a sheaf of groups on \(X\), not necessarily commutative, then \(R^1f_*(F)\) is the final sheaf on \(Y\); compare Remark 5.3.
\end{remarktext}

\section*{6. Supports}

Let \(U\) be a Zariski open subset of the scheme \(X\). Then \(U\in\Ob X_{\et}\), and in fact \(U\) is a subobject of the final object \(X\) of \(X_{\et}\). Hence it defines a subobject \(\widetilde U\) of the final object of \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\), \ie an open subtopos of the \'etale topos of \(X\) (IV, 8.3).

\begin{statement}{Proposition 6.1}
The preceding map \(U\mapsto \widetilde U\) is an isomorphism from the ordered set of open subsets of \(X\), in the Zariski sense, onto the set of open subobjects of the \'etale topos \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\).
\end{statement}

Since \(X_{\et}\to\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) is fully faithful, the map \(U\mapsto\widetilde U\) plainly preserves order, \ie \((U\subset V)\Leftrightarrow(\widetilde U\subset\widetilde V)\), so in particular it is injective. To prove surjectivity, let \(F\) be a subsheaf of the final sheaf \(\widetilde X\), and consider the objects of \(X_{\et}/F\), \ie the \'etale schemes \(X'\) over \(X\) such that \(F(X')\ne\varnothing\). Since \(X'\to X\) is \'etale, it is an open map (\EGA{} IV, 2.4.6); in particular its image \(\operatorname{Im}(X')\) is open. Let \(U\) be the union of the opens \(\operatorname{Im}(X')\) for \(X'\in\Ob X_{\et}/F\). Since the family \(X'\to U\), for \(X'\in\Ob X_{\et}/F\), is surjective, hence covering, one concludes that \(U\in\Ob X_{\et}/F\), hence \(X_{\et}/F=X_{\et}/U\), and therefore \(F=U\). \qedtext

Taking Proposition 6.1 into account, we may therefore speak without ambiguity of an open of \(X\), without specifying whether we mean this in the usual Zariski sense or in the sense of the \'etale topology.

\begin{statement}{Corollary 6.2}
Let \(U\) be an open subset of \(X\), and let \(j:U\to X\) be the canonical immersion. Then the functor
\[
j^*:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\longrightarrow \widetilde{U_{\et}}
\]
induces an equivalence of categories
\[
\widetilde{X_{\et}}/\widetilde U\longrightarrow \widetilde{U_{\et}}.
\]
\end{statement}

Indeed, for every site \(C\) in which finite projective limits exist, and for every subobject \(U\) of the final object \(e\) of \(C\), the functor \(j:C\to C_{/U}\) defined by \(j(S)=S\times U\) is a morphism of sites and \(j^*: \widetilde C\to\widetilde{C/U}\) induces an equivalence \(\widetilde C/\widetilde U\to\widetilde{C/U}\). It remains only to combine this general fact with the canonical isomorphism \(U_{\et}\simeq X_{\et}/U\).

Pictorially, Corollary 6.2 says that the operations of restricting to an open, in the usual sense for schemes on the one hand and in the sense of topoi on the other, are compatible. The following is the analogous compatibility for restriction to a closed subset.

\begin{statement}{Theorem 6.3}
Let \(X\) be a scheme, let \(Y\) be a closed subscheme of \(X\), let \(U=X-Y\) with its induced structure, and let \(i:Y\to X\) and \(j:U\to X\) be the canonical immersions. Then the functor
\[
i_*:\widetilde{Y_{\et}}\longrightarrow \widetilde{X_{\et}}
\]
is fully faithful. Moreover, for \(F\in\Ob\widetilde{X_{\et}}\), the sheaf \(F\) is isomorphic to one of the form \(i_*(G)\) if and only if \(j^*(F)\) is isomorphic to the final sheaf on \(U\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
Since \(i_*\) and \(i^*\) are adjoint, the full faithfulness of \(i_*\) is equivalent to the assertion that the functorial homomorphism
\[
i^*(i_*(G))\longrightarrow G
\]
is an isomorphism; this is just Corollary 5.7. On the other hand, if \(G\in\Ob\widetilde{Y_{\et}}\), then by Corollary 6.2 one checks trivially that \(j^*(i_*(G))\) is the final sheaf on \(U\).

Conversely, suppose \(F\in\Ob\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) is such that \(j^*(F)\) is the final sheaf. We prove that \(F\) is isomorphic to a sheaf of the form \(i_*(G)\), or equivalently that the canonical homomorphism
\[
F\longrightarrow i_*i^*F
\]
is an isomorphism. It is enough to check this on all geometric fibres. If \(x\in U\), this is precisely the hypothesis on \(F\). If \(x\in Y\), transitivity of fibres reduces us to checking that the homomorphism on fibres induced by
\[
i^*(F)\longrightarrow i^*(i_*i^*(F))
\]
is an isomorphism. But this homomorphism is an isomorphism by Corollary 5.7, applied to \(i\) and to \(i^*F\). This completes the proof.
\end{prooftext}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 6.4}
The functor \(i_*\) induces an equivalence between the category of abelian sheaves on \(Y\) and the category of abelian sheaves on \(X\) whose restriction to \(U=X-Y\) is zero.
\end{statement}

In accordance with common usage, we shall therefore often identify an abelian sheaf on \(Y\) with an abelian sheaf on \(X\) which is zero on \(U\).

\subsection*{6.5}
By Proposition 6.1 we know how to interpret the opens of the topos \(\E=\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) as ordinary opens \(U\) of \(X\). By Theorem 6.3, with this identification, the residual topos \(\E_{\complement U}\) of IV, 3 is canonically equivalent to \(\widetilde{Y_{\et}}\), where \(Y=X-U\) is endowed with any induced scheme structure. We may therefore apply to this situation the results of IV, 3, especially the description of sheaves \(F\) on \(X\) in terms of triples \((F',F'',u)\), where \(F'\) is a sheaf on \(Y\), \(F''\) a sheaf on \(U\), and \(u\) a homomorphism from \(F'\) to \(i^*j_*(F'')\). We shall freely use below the notation \(i^!\), \(j_!\) of IV, 3, denoting functors
\[
\begin{aligned}
j_!:\widetilde{(U_{\et})}_{\ab}&\longrightarrow \widetilde{(X_{\et})}_{\ab},\\
i^!:\widetilde{(X_{\et})}_{\ab}&\longrightarrow \widetilde{(Y_{\et})}_{\ab},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the subscript \(\ab\) denotes the category of abelian sheaves. These functors give rise to the two exact sequences of IV, 3.7.

\subsection*{6.6}
In view of the preceding developments and of the general terminology introduced in IV, 8.5, it is natural to introduce, for an abelian sheaf \(F\) on \(X\), or a section \(\varphi\) of such a sheaf, the notion of the \emph{support} of \(F\), respectively of \(\varphi\), as the closed subset, in the usual Zariski sense, complementary to the largest open over which the object in question vanishes.

In the present situation it is also appropriate to replace the general notation of Expos\'e V, 4.3, \(H^q(\E_{\complement U},F)\), \(\mathcal H^q_{\complement U}(F)\), by the notation \(H^q_Y(X,F)\), \(\mathcal H^q_Y(F)\). The first denotes an abelian group, and the second an abelian sheaf on \(Y\), or equivalently an abelian sheaf on \(X\) which is zero on \(U\). Thus \(\mathcal H^q_Y=R^q i^!\), and so forth. We refer to Expos\'e V, 6 for the general properties of these functors.

\section*{7. Specialization morphisms of fiber functors}

\subsection*{7.1}
We saw in No. 4 how to associate to every geometric point \(\xi\) of the scheme \(X\) a strictly local \(X\)-scheme
\[
\bar X(\xi)=\Spec\Oo_{X,\xi},
\]
which in fact depends only on the geometric point above the image \(x\) of \(\xi\) defined by the separable closure \(\bar{k(x)}\) of \(k(x)\) in \(\Omega=k(\xi)\). We shall often restrict below to geometric points \(\xi=\Spec\Omega\) which are \emph{separable algebraic} over \(X\), \ie such that \(\Omega\) is a separable closure of \(k(x)\), in other words \(\xi=\bar x\). Call an \(X\)-scheme \(Z\) a \emph{strict localization of \(X\)} if it is \(X\)-isomorphic to a scheme of the form \(\Spec\Oo_{X,\bar x}\). Then
\[
\xi\longmapsto \Spec\Oo_{X,\xi}=\bar X(\xi)
\]
is an equivalence from the category of separable algebraic geometric points over \(X\), with only isomorphisms as morphisms, to the category of \(X\)-schemes which are strict localizations of \(X\), again with only isomorphisms as morphisms.

The latter assertion is no longer true if one allows all \(X\)-morphisms, because in the second category there may be \(X\)-morphisms which are not isomorphisms. We therefore make the following definition.

\begin{statement}{Definition 7.2}
Let \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\) be two geometric points of the scheme \(X\). A \emph{specialization arrow} from \(\xi'\) to \(\xi\) is an \(X\)-morphism between the corresponding strict localizations
\[
\bar X(\xi')\longrightarrow \bar X(\xi).
\]
We say that \(\xi\) is a specialization of \(\xi'\), or that \(\xi'\) is a generization of \(\xi\), if there exists a specialization arrow from \(\xi'\) to \(\xi\).
\end{statement}

Specialization arrows compose in the evident way. Thus, taking specialization arrows as morphisms, the geometric points of \(X\) form a category, equivalent, as is the full subcategory of separable algebraic geometric points over \(X\), to the full subcategory of \((\Sch)_{/X}\) consisting of the strict localizations of \(X\).

\begin{statement}{Lemma 7.3}
Let \(X\) be a scheme, let \(Z\) be an \(X\)-scheme isomorphic to a pseudo-filtered projective limit of \'etale \(X\)-schemes \(X_i\) with affine transition morphisms (Expos\'e VII, 5.1), let \(\xi'\) be a geometric point of \(X\), and let \(Z'=\bar X(\xi')\) be the corresponding strict localization. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item the restriction map
\[
\Hom_X(Z',Z)\longrightarrow \Hom_X(\xi',Z)
\]
is bijective;
\item the two sides are nonempty if and only if the image \(x'\) of \(\xi'\) in \(X\) lies in the image of \(Z\);
\item if \(T\) is a \(Z\)-scheme, then \(T\) is a strict localization of \(Z\) if and only if it is a strict localization of \(X\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
For (a), one immediately reduces to the case where \(Z\) is one of the \(X_i\), \ie where \(Z\) is \'etale over \(X\); after the base change \(Z'\to X\) one may suppose \(Z'\simeq X\), and the conclusion follows from Definition 4.2(ii). For (b), note that if \(x'\) is the image of a point \(z\) of \(Z\), then \(k(z)\) is necessarily a separable algebraic extension of \(k(x')\), hence admits a \(k(x')\)-homomorphism into \(k(\xi')\). Assertion (c) is an easy exercise left to the reader.
\end{prooftext}

Definitions 7.2 and Lemma 7.3 imply the following.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 7.4}
Let \(\xi\) and \(\xi'\) be two geometric points of the scheme \(X\). Restriction defines a bijection from the set \(\Hom_X(\bar X(\xi'),\bar X(\xi))\) of specialization arrows from \(\xi'\) to \(\xi\), to the set of \(X\)-morphisms from \(\xi'\) to \(\bar X(\xi)\).
\end{statement}

\begin{statement}{Corollary 7.5}
The geometric point \(\xi\) is a specialization of \(\xi'\) if and only if the same is true for their images \(x\), \(x'\) in \(X\), \ie if and only if \(x\) belongs to the closure of \(\{x'\}\).
\end{statement}

Since \(\Spec\Oo_{X,\xi}\to\Spec\Oo_{X,x}\) is faithfully flat (4.4), it is surjective, and one only has to apply the second assertion of Lemma 7.3.

\begin{statement}{Corollary 7.6}
For every scheme \(X\), let \(\Pt(X)\) be the category of geometric points over \(X\), or equivalently of separable algebraic geometric points over \(X\), with specialization arrows as morphisms. Let \(\xi\) be a geometric point of a scheme \(X\), and let \(\bar X(\xi)\) be the corresponding strict localization. Then there is an equivalence of categories
\[
\Pt(\bar X(\xi))\xrightarrow{\sim}\Pt(X)_{/\xi},
\]
which associates to every geometric point \(\xi'\) of \(\bar X(\xi)\) the corresponding geometric point over \(X\), together with the specialization morphism to \(\xi\) deduced from the structural morphism \(\xi'\to\bar X(\xi)\) by Proposition 7.4.
\end{statement}

In other words, giving a generization \(\xi'\) of the geometric point \(\xi\) is essentially equivalent to giving a geometric point of \(\bar X(\xi)\). By Lemma 7.3(c), the corresponding homomorphism \(\bar X(\xi')\to\bar X(\xi)\) makes \(\bar X(\xi')\) the strict localization of \(\bar X(\xi)\) relative to \(\xi'\).

\subsection*{7.7}
For every geometric point \(\xi\) of \(X\) and every sheaf \(F\) on \(X\), interpret the fibre \(F_\xi\) as \(\Gamma(\bar X(\xi),\bar F(\xi))\), where \(\bar F(\xi)\) is the inverse image of \(F\) on \(\bar X(\xi)\) (Corollary 4.8). Then every specialization arrow
\[
u:\xi'\longrightarrow \xi
\]
induces a homomorphism, functorial in \(F\),
\[
u^*:F_\xi\longrightarrow F_{\xi'},
\]
called the \emph{specialization homomorphism} associated with \(u\). It is clear from the transitivity of inverse images of sheaves that for a composite specialization arrow one has
\[
(wu)^*=u^*w^*.
\]

\subsection*{7.8}
Recall that if \(\E\) is a topos, a \emph{fiber functor}, or by abuse of language a point of \(\E\), is a morphism from the final topos \((\mathbf{Set})\) to \(\E\), \ie a functor
\[
\varphi:\E\longrightarrow (\mathbf{Set})
\]
which commutes with arbitrary inductive limits and finite projective limits. The set of fiber functors of \(\E\) is regarded as the set of objects of a full subcategory of \(\Hom(\E,(\mathbf{Set}))\), called the \emph{category of fiber functors} of the topos \(\E\); its opposite is called the \emph{category of points of \(\E\)}, denoted \(\Pt(\E)\), \cf IV, 6.1.

When \(\E=\widetilde{X_{\et}}\), where \(X\) is a scheme, we defined in 3.3 and 7.7 a functor
\[
\Pt(X)\longrightarrow \Pt(\widetilde{X_{\et}}).
\tag{*}
\]
Here the left hand side is defined in Corollary 7.6. With this notation we have:

\begin{statement}{Theorem 7.9}
Let \(X\) be a scheme. The functor \((*)\) is an equivalence from the category of geometric points over \(X\), with specialization arrows as morphisms, to the category of points of the \'etale topos \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\), the latter being the opposite of the category of fiber functors on \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\).
\end{statement}

Since this theorem will not be used later in the seminar, we give only a sketch of proof, in which we take some liberties with questions of universes.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \emph{The functor in question is fully faithful.} For every \(X\in\Ob X_{\et}\), let \(X^\vee:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\to(\mathbf{Set})\) be defined by
\[
X^\vee(F)=\Hom(X,F)=F(X).
\]
Note that the fiber functor
\[
\mathcal E_\xi:F\longmapsto F_\xi
\]
may be written as
\[
\mathcal E_\xi\simeq \varinjlim_i X_i^\vee,
\]
where the \(X_i\) are affine schemes \'etale over \(X\), indexed by a certain filtering category (\cf 4.3 and 4.5). Thus for every functor \(\varphi:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\to(\mathbf{Set})\),
\[
\Hom(\mathcal E_\xi,\varphi)\simeq \varprojlim_i\Hom(X_i^\vee,\varphi).
\]
On the other hand, one has the well-known bifunctorial isomorphism
\[
\Hom(X_i^\vee,\varphi)\simeq\varphi(X_i).
\]

When \(\varphi\) is of the form \(\mathcal E_{\xi'}\), hence
\[
\varphi\simeq\varinjlim_j (X'_j)^\vee,
\]
there is a natural bijection
\[
\Hom(\mathcal E_\xi,\mathcal E_{\xi'})\simeq
\varprojlim_i\varinjlim_j\Hom_X(X'_j,X_i).
\tag{*}
\]
On the other hand, in the category of schemes,
\[
\bar X(\xi)=\varprojlim_i X_i,
\qquad
\bar X(\xi')=\varprojlim_j X'_j,
\]
so that
\[
\Hom_X(\bar X(\xi'),\bar X(\xi))\simeq
\varprojlim_i\Hom_X(\bar X(\xi'),X_i).
\]
Since \(X_i\) is locally of finite presentation over \(X\),
\[
\Hom_X(\bar X(\xi'),X_i)\simeq
\varinjlim_j\Hom_X(X'_j,X_i),
\]
and therefore
\[
\Hom_X(\bar X(\xi'),\bar X(\xi))\simeq
\varprojlim_i\varinjlim_j\Hom_X(X'_j,X_i).
\tag{**}
\]
Comparing \((*)\) and \((**)\) gives the desired conclusion, up to a compatibility check left to the reader.

\item \emph{The functor in question is essentially surjective.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{7.9.1}
First observe that if \(C\) is a site in which finite projective limits are representable, and \(\widetilde C=\E\) is the corresponding topos, then every fiber functor \(\varphi\) on \(\E\) can be represented as a filtered inductive limit of functors of the form
\[
\check Y(F)=F(Y)=\Hom(\widetilde Y,F),\qquad Y\in\Ob C,
\]
where \(\widetilde Y\) is the sheaf associated with \(Y\), as follows. Consider the category \(C_{/\varphi}\) of pairs \((Y,\xi)\), with \(Y\in\Ob C\) and \(\xi\in\varphi(\widetilde Y)\), morphisms being defined in the evident way. There is an evident homomorphism
\[
\varinjlim_{(Y,\xi)\in C_{/\varphi}^\circ}\check Y\longrightarrow\varphi.
\tag{*}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\Hom\left(\varinjlim_{C_{/\varphi}}\check Y,\varphi\right)
\simeq \varprojlim_{C_{/\varphi}}\Hom(\check Y,\varphi)
\simeq \varprojlim_{C_{/\varphi}}\varphi(\widetilde Y).
\]
There is a canonical element of \(\varprojlim_{C_{/\varphi}}\varphi(\widetilde Y)\), assigning to each \((Y,\xi)\) the element \(\xi\in\varphi(Y)\). Since finite projective limits exist in \(C\), and the functor \(Y\mapsto\varphi(\widetilde Y)\) commutes with them, finite projective limits exist in \(C_{/\varphi}\). A fortiori \(C_{/\varphi}^\circ\) is filtering. Using the fact that every sheaf \(F\) is an inductive limit of sheaves of the form \(\widetilde Y\), and that \(\varphi\) commutes with inductive limits, one easily concludes that \((*)\) is bijective, giving the asserted representation.

\subsubsection*{7.9.2}
Also note that if \(\E\) is a topos and \(Y\) an object of \(\E\), giving a fiber functor \(\psi\) for \(\E_{/Y}\) is equivalent to giving a pair \((\varphi,\xi)\), where \(\varphi\) is a fiber functor for \(\E\) and \(\xi\in\varphi(Y)\). To \(\psi:\E_{/Y}\to(\mathbf{Set})\) one associates \((\varphi,\xi)\), where \(\varphi\) is the composite \(Z\mapsto\varphi(Z)=\psi(Z\times Y)\), and where \(\xi\in\varphi(Y)=\psi(Y\times Y)\) is the image of the unique element of \(\psi(Y)\) by the diagonal morphism of \(Y\times Y\).

\subsubsection*{7.9.3}
Return now to the site \(C=X_{\et}\), and let \(\varphi:\widetilde{X_{\et}}\to(\mathbf{Set})\) be a fiber functor on the \'etale topos of \(X\). Restricting \(\varphi\) to the full subcategory of \(\widetilde{X_{\et}}\) formed by the opens of this topos, or equivalently by Proposition 6.1 by the opens \(U\) of \(X\), gives a map
\[
\varphi_0:\mathcal U(X)\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
\]
commuting with arbitrary suprema and finite infima. Here \(\varphi(U)\) is either empty or reduced to one point, and \(\varphi_0(U)\) is defined to be \(0\) or \(1\) accordingly.

Using the fact that every irreducible closed subset of \(X\) has a unique generic point, one sees easily that \(\varphi_0\) is defined by a unique point \(x\in X\), by the condition
\[
\varphi_0(U)=1\quad\text{if and only if}\quad x\in U,
\]
\ie
\[
\varphi(U)=\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\notin U.
\]
Let \(F\in\Ob\widetilde{X_{\et}}\). The image of \(F\) in the final sheaf \(X\) is an open \(U\), and since
\[
\varphi(F)\longrightarrow\varphi(U)
\]
is surjective, \(\varphi(F)\ne\varnothing\) if and only if \(\varphi(U)\ne\varnothing\), \ie if and only if \(x\in U\). Let \(C_{/\varphi}\) be the category of pairs \((X',\xi)\), where \(X'\in\Ob X_{\et}\) and \(\xi\in\varphi(X')\). We noted in 7.9.1 that \(C_{/\varphi}^\circ\) is connected and filtering. Moreover, by 7.9.2 and the preceding discussion applied to \(X'\) in place of \(X\), to an object \((X',\xi)\) there is associated a unique point \(x'\in X'\) such that, for an \'etale morphism \(X''\to X'\), \(\xi\) lies in the image of \(\varphi(X'')\to\varphi(X')\) if and only if \(x'\) lies in the image of \(X''\). It follows that the scheme \(\varprojlim X'\), taken over the category \(C\) of the \((X',\xi)\), exists and is a strict localization \(Z\) of \(X\), corresponding to a geometric point \(\xi\) and to a fiber functor \(\mathcal E_\xi\). For every sheaf \(F\),
\[
\mathcal E_\xi(F)\simeq \varinjlim_{C_\varphi}F(X').
\]
By 7.9.1, \(\varphi\) is isomorphic to \(\mathcal E_\xi\), completing the proof of Theorem 7.9.

\section*{8. Two spectral sequences for integral morphisms}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 8.1}
Let \(f:X\to Y\) be an integral surjective morphism, and let \(F\) be an abelian sheaf on \(Y\). For every integer \(i\), let \(\Hh^i(F)\) be the presheaf on \((\Sch)_{/Y}\) defined by
\[
\Hh^i(F)(Z)=H^i(Z,F_Z),
\]
where \(F_Z\) is the inverse image of \(F\) on \(Z\). Then there is a spectral sequence, functorial in \(F\),
\[
H^*(X,F)\Longleftarrow E_2^{pq}=H^p(X/Y,\Hh^q(F)),
\]
the \emph{descent spectral sequence}.
\end{statement}

Of course, for a presheaf \(G\) on \((\Sch)_{/Y}\), the symbol \(H^p(X/Y,G)\) denotes the \(p\)-th relative Cech cohomology group, defined by the complex \(C^n(X/Y,G)=G(X^{n+1})\), where \(X^m\) denotes the \(m\)-fold power in \((\Sch)_{/Y}\). To prove Proposition 8.1, let \(p_n:X^{n+1}\to Y\) be the projection, and put
\[
A^n=p_{n*}(\Zz_{X^{n+1}}),
\]
where \(\Zz_{X^{n+1}}\) denotes the constant sheaf \(\Zz\) on \(X^{n+1}\). The \(A^n\) are the components of a simplicial abelian sheaf on \(Y\), hence of a complex \(A^*\) of abelian sheaves on \(Y\). There is an evident homomorphism
\[
\Zz_Y\longrightarrow A^0.
\tag{*}
\]

\begin{statement}{Lemma 8.2}
The complex \(A^*\), endowed with the homomorphism \((*)\), is a resolution of \(\Zz_Y\). More generally, for every abelian sheaf \(F\) on \(Y\), the complex \(A^*\otimes_{\Zz}F\) is a resolution of \(F\).
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
We may assume that \(Y\) is affine, say \(Y=\Spec A\), and \(X=\Spec B\), with \(B\) an integral \(A\)-algebra. Write \(B=\varinjlim_iB_i\), where the \(B_i\) run over the finite-type, hence finite, \(A\)-subalgebras of \(B\). Then \(X=\varprojlim X_i\), with \(X_i=\Spec B_i\). Using Expos\'e VII, 5.11, the augmented complex \(A^*(X/Y)\) is the inductive limit of the augmented complexes \(A^*(X_i/Y)\). This reduces us to the case where \(f\) is finite. It is enough to prove that for every geometric point \(\bar y\) of \(Y\), the complex \(A^*_{\bar y}\) is a resolution of \(\Zz_{\bar y}\), and remains so after tensoring with \(F\). If \(X_{\bar y}\) is the fibre of \(X\) at \(\bar y\), Proposition 5.5 shows that \(A^*_{\bar y}\) is just the analogous complex \(A^*(X_{\bar y}/\bar y)\). This reduces us to the case where \(Y\) is the spectrum of a separably closed field \(k\). Using Examples 1.3(a) and (b), we may even assume \(k\) algebraically closed and \(X\) reduced; thus \(X\) is of the form \(I_Y\), where \(I\) is a finite set. Then \(A^*\otimes F\) identifies with the trivial cochain complex of the indexing set \(I\), with coefficients in \(F_{\bar y}\), and is indeed a resolution of \(F_{\bar y}\).
\end{prooftext}

As a consequence of Lemma 8.2 we obtain a spectral sequence
\[
H^*(Y,F)\Longleftarrow E_2^{pq}=H^p(H^q(Y,A^*\otimes F)),
\]
and it remains to identify the initial term. There is a canonical homomorphism
\[
A^n\otimes F\longrightarrow p_{n*}p_n^*(F),
\]
which is an isomorphism, again by reduction to the finite case and passage to fibres. Using Proposition 5.5, it follows that
\[
H^q(Y,A^n\otimes F)\simeq H^q(X^{n+1},p_n^*F)=\Hh^q(F)(X^{n+1}),
\]
giving the announced initial term in Proposition 8.1.

\begin{remarktext}{Remark 8.3}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item If \(Y\) is covered by a locally finite family of closed subsets \(Y_i\), and if \(X=\coprod_iY_i\), then the canonical morphism \(f:X\to Y\) is finite, and the initial term of the spectral sequence in Proposition 8.1 is described as the cohomology of the complex defined by the groups \(H^q(Y_{i_0\ldots i_p},F|Y_{i_0\ldots i_p})\), where \(Y_{i_0\ldots i_p}=Y_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap Y_{i_p}\). This is the analogue of Leray's spectral sequence for a locally finite closed covering of an ordinary topological space (TF, Chapter II, 5.2.4). The latter can also be generalized to a spectral sequence relative to a ``finite'' morphism, \ie a proper morphism with finite fibres, by proceeding as in Proposition 8.1.
\item Note the analogy between the spectral sequence of Proposition 8.1 and Leray's spectral sequence for a covering \((X_i)\) of \(Y\), here by \(X_i\) \'etale over \(Y\). The latter would be obtained formally by writing the spectral sequence of Proposition 8.1 for \(X=\coprod_iX_i\). It is likely that these two spectral sequences admit a common generalization, valid whenever one has a family of morphisms \((X_i\to Y)\) which is a universal effective descent family for the fibred category of \'etale sheaves over a variable base scheme; compare No. 9 below. The analogous question also arises in ordinary topology, concerning a common generalization of the two kinds of Leray spectral sequences for coverings, assumed either open or locally finite closed. Since these lines were written, P. Deligne has developed a general theory of descent spectral sequences; see Expos\'e V bis.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarktext}

\begin{statement}{Proposition 8.4}
Let \(Y\) be a scheme, let \(\pi\) be a profinite group, let \((\pi_i)_i\) be the projective system of its finite discrete quotient groups, let \((X_i)_{i\in I}\) be a projective system of principal coverings of \(Y\), with groups \(\pi_i\), the transition homomorphisms \(X_j\to X_i\) being compatible with the homomorphisms \(\pi_j\to\pi_i\) on the operator groups, and put \(X=\varprojlim X_i\) (\cf Expos\'e VII, 5.1), so that \(\pi\) acts on the \(Y\)-scheme \(X\). Let \(F\) be an abelian sheaf on \(Y\). Then there is a Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence, functorial in \(F\),
\[
H^*(Y,F)\Longleftarrow E_2^{pq}=H^p\bigl(\pi,\varinjlim_iH^q(X_i,F_{X_i})\bigr),
\]
where \(F_{X_i}\) is the inverse image of \(F\) on \(X_i\), and \(H^p(\pi,-)\) denotes Galois cohomology.
\end{statement}

The term \(E_2^{pq}\) displayed here is also, by definition of \(H^p(\pi,-)\) and by transitivity of inductive limits, isomorphic to
\[
E_2^{pq}=\varinjlim_i H^p(\pi_i,H^q(X_i,F_{X_i})).
\]
It is therefore enough to find an inductive system of spectral sequences, depending on \(i\),
\[
H^*(Y,F)\Longleftarrow {}^iE_2^{pq}=H^p(\pi_i,H^q(X_i,F_{X_i})).
\]
This reduces us to defining the spectral sequence when \(\pi\) is finite. Then \(f:X\to Y\) is a covering morphism in \(Y_{\et}\), so we may write the Leray spectral sequence of this morphism. Taking into account the canonical isomorphisms
\[
X^n\simeq X\times^G G^n,
\]
a well-known calculation shows that for every presheaf \(\Hh\) on \(X_{\et}\) which transforms sums into products, \(C^*(X/Y,\Hh)\) is precisely the complex of homogeneous cochains of \(G\) with coefficients in \(\Hh(X)\). This gives the asserted form of the initial term.

A variant of this proof, avoiding any calculation, consists in viewing \(E\mapsto X\times^\pi E\) as a morphism from the site of finite \(\pi\)-sets to the \'etale site of \(Y\), and in writing the Leray spectral sequence of this morphism. Finally, when \(\pi\) is finite, the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence can also be regarded as a special case of Proposition 8.1 relative to the morphism \(f:X\to Y\).

\begin{statement}{Corollary 8.5}
Assume \(Y\) quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Then the spectral sequence of Proposition 8.4 is written
\[
H^*(Y,F)\Longleftarrow E_2^{pq}=H^p(\pi,H^q(X,F_X)).
\]
\end{statement}

Indeed, by Expos\'e VII, 5.8, there are canonical isomorphisms
\[
\varinjlim_iH^q(X_i,F_{X_i})\simeq H^q(X,F_X).
\]

\begin{statement}{Corollary 8.6}
Let \(Y\) be a henselian local scheme with closed point \(y\), let \(F\) be an abelian sheaf on \(Y\), and let \(F_0\) be the induced sheaf on \(Y_0=\Spec k(y)\). Then the canonical homomorphisms
\[
H^n(Y,F)\longrightarrow H^n(Y_0,F_0)
\]
are isomorphisms.
\end{statement}

Indeed, let \(\bar y\) be a geometric point over \(y\), corresponding to a separable closure \(k(\bar y)\) of \(k(y)\). Since \(Y\) is henselian, the strict localization \(X\) of \(Y\) at \(\bar y\) is the projective limit of the connected pointed Galois \'etale coverings \(X_i\). Thus the hypotheses of Corollary 8.5 apply. Since \(H^q(X,F_X)=0\) for \(q>0\) by Corollary 4.7, one obtains isomorphisms
\[
H^n(Y,F)\xleftarrow{\sim}H^n(\pi,F(X)),
\]
where \(\pi\) is the Galois group of \(X\) over \(Y\), isomorphic to that of \(\bar{k(y)}\) over \(k=k(y)\). Similarly, or by Corollary 2.3,
\[
H^n(Y_0,F_0)\xleftarrow{\sim}H^n(\pi,F_0(X_0)),
\]
where \(X_0=X\times_YY_0\simeq\Spec k(\bar y)\). The restriction homomorphism \(F(X)\to F_0(X_0)\) is an isomorphism by Corollary 4.8, and the conclusion follows.

\section*{9. Descent of \'etale sheaves}

This section will not be used later in the seminar and may be omitted on a first reading.

\begin{statement}{Proposition 9.1}
Let \(f:X'\to X\) be a surjective, respectively universally submersive (\SGA{} 1, IX, 2.1), morphism of schemes. Then the functor \(f^*:X_{\et}\to X'_{\et}\) is faithful and conservative (\cf Corollary 3.6), respectively induces a fully faithful functor from the category of sheaves on \(X_{\et}\) to the category of sheaves on \(X'_{\et}\) endowed with descent data relative to \(f:X'\to X\).
\end{statement}

The first point follows immediately from the corresponding assertions on the fibres (3.6, 3.7). The second, which can also be stated by saying that \(f\) is a descent morphism relative to the fibred category of \'etale sheaves over variable schemes, means that for two sheaves \(F\), \(G\) on \(X\), the natural diagram
\[
\Hom(F,G)\longrightarrow\Hom(F',G')\rightrightarrows\Hom(F'',G'')
\tag{*}
\]
is exact, where \(F'\) and \(G'\), respectively \(F''\) and \(G''\), are the inverse images of \(F\) and \(G\) on \(X'\), respectively on \(X''=X'\times_XX'\). Taking \(F\) to be the final sheaf gives:

\begin{statement}{Corollary 9.2}
Let \(f:X'\to X\) be universally submersive. Then for every sheaf \(F\) on \(X\), the diagram
\[
\Gamma(X,F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(X',F')\rightrightarrows\Gamma(X'',F'')
\]
is exact, where \(X''=X'\times_XX'\), and \(F'\), \(F''\) are the inverse images of \(F\) on \(X'\), \(X''\).
\end{statement}

We prove Proposition 9.1 using the following lemma.

\begin{statement}{Lemma 9.3}
Assume \(f\) universally submersive. Let \(F\) be a sheaf on \(X\), denote by \(S(F)\) the set of subsheaves of \(F\), and define \(S(F')\), \(S(F'')\) similarly. Then the natural diagram of maps
\[
S(F)\longrightarrow S(F')\rightrightarrows S(F'')
\]
is exact.
\end{statement}

\begin{prooftext}
The injectivity of \(S(F)\to S(F')\) follows immediately from the fact that \(f^*\) is conservative. Indeed, if \(F_i\), \(i=1,2\), are two subsheaves of \(F\) such that \(f^*(F_1)=f^*(F_2)\), then the inclusions \(F_3=F_1\cap F_2\to F_i\) are isomorphisms after applying \(f^*\), hence are isomorphisms, and so \(F_1=F_2\).

It remains to prove that if \(G'\) is a subsheaf of \(F'\) such that \(\pr_1^*(G')=\pr_2^*(G')\), then \(G'\) is the inverse image of a subsheaf of \(F\). Choose an epimorphism \(F_1\to F\) in \(X_{\et}\), with \(F_1\) representable by an \'etale scheme over \(X\). Let \(F_2=F_1\times_FF_1\), and similarly define \(F'_1\), \(F''_1\), \(F'_2\), \(F''_2\). These sheaves give a diagram of sets
\[
\xymatrix@R=.75cm@C=1.2cm{
S(F)\ar[r]\ar[d]&S(F')\ar[r]\ar[d]&S(F'')\ar[d]\\
S(F_1)\ar[r]\ar@<3pt>[d]\ar@<-3pt>[d]&S(F'_1)\ar@<3pt>[r]\ar@<-3pt>[r]\ar@<3pt>[d]\ar@<-3pt>[d]&S(F''_1)\ar@<3pt>[d]\ar@<-3pt>[d]\\
S(F_2)\ar[r]&S(F'_2)\ar@<3pt>[r]\ar@<-3pt>[r]&S(F''_2),
}
\]
in which the columns are exact by the exactness properties in the categories of sheaves on \(X\), \(X'\), and \(X''\), respectively. A standard diagram chase shows that, to prove exactness of the first row, it is enough to prove it for the second and third rows. For the second row this follows from the fact that \(F_1\) is representable, \(X'\to X\) is universally submersive, and \SGA{} 1, IX, 2.3. The sheaf \(F_2\) is also representable: it is a subsheaf of \(F_1\times_XF_1\), which is representable, and one applies Proposition 6.1. Thus the third row is exact as well. \qedtext
\end{prooftext}

We now prove Proposition 9.1, namely that every morphism \(u':F'\to G'\), compatible with the descent data on \(F'\) and \(G'\), comes from a morphism \(u:F\to G\). Put \(H=F\times G\), so \(H'=F'\times G'\) and \(H''=F''\times G''\). The graph of \(u'\) is a subsheaf \(\Gamma'\) of \(H'\), whose two inverse images are equal to the graph of the same morphism \(u'':F''\to G''\). Hence, by Lemma 9.3, \(\Gamma'\) comes from a subsheaf \(\Gamma\) of \(H\). I claim that \(\Gamma\) is the graph of a morphism \(u:F\to G\), \ie that the morphism \(p:\Gamma\to F\) induced by \(\pr_1\) is an isomorphism. Indeed, it becomes an isomorphism after base change by \(X'\to X\), and we apply the part of Proposition 9.1 already proved. The morphism \(u:F\to G\) then has the desired property. \qedtext

\begin{statement}{Theorem 9.4}
Let \(f:X'\to X\) be a surjective morphism of schemes. Assume that one of the following hypotheses holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\item \(f\) is integral;
\item \(f\) is proper;
\item \(f\) is flat and locally of finite presentation;
\item \(X\) is discrete, for example the spectrum of a field.
\end{enumerate}
Then \(f\) is an \emph{effective descent morphism} for the fibred category \(\F\) over \((\Sch)\) of \'etale sheaves on variable schemes. That is, \(f^*\) induces an equivalence from the category of sheaves on \(X\) to the category of sheaves on \(X'\) endowed with descent data relative to \(f:X'\to X\).
\end{statement}

\subsubsection*{9.4.1}
Let \(F'\) be a sheaf on \(X'\), endowed with descent data relative to \(f:X'\to X\). Define a functor
\[
G:X_{\et}^\circ\longrightarrow(\mathbf{Set})
\]
by
\[
G(Y)=\Ker\bigl(F'(Y')\rightrightarrows F''(Y'')\bigr).
\]
One immediately checks that \(G\) is a sheaf for the \'etale topology. Moreover there is an injective canonical homomorphism \(G\to f_*(F')\), hence a homomorphism \(f^*(G)\to F'\), and the latter is evidently compatible with descent data. It remains to examine whether this homomorphism is an isomorphism.

Note that \(G\) is also defined by exactness of
\[
G\longrightarrow f_*(F')\rightrightarrows g_*(F''),
\]
so for every base change \(Y\xrightarrow{h}X\) which commutes with the formation of \(f_*(F')\) and \(g_*(F'')\), the sheaf \(h^*(G)=G_Y\) identifies with the sheaf descended from \(F'_{Y'}\) by \(Y'\to Y\); and of course the homomorphism \(f_Y^*(G_Y)\to (F_Y)'\) is obtained from \(f^*(G)\to F'\) by base change. Now assume that the base change \(Y=X'\to X\) commutes with the formation of \(f_*(F')\) and \(g_*(F'')\). Since \(Y'=X'\times_XX'\) has a section over \(Y=X'\), the morphism \(Y'\to Y\) is an effective descent morphism for every fibred category, in particular for \(\F\). It follows that \(f_Y^*(G_Y)\to(F_Y)'\) is an isomorphism, and therefore \(f^*(G)\to F'\) becomes an isomorphism after base change by \(Y'=X'\times_XX'\to X'\). As the latter morphism has a section, \(f^*(G)\to F'\) is itself an isomorphism. Thus the descent datum on \(F'\) is effective.

\subsubsection*{9.4.2}
This proves Theorem 9.4 in case (a), by Corollary 5.6. Case (b) follows similarly from the fact that if \(f\) is proper then \(f_*\) commutes with every base change \(Y'\to Y\); this will be proved in a later expos\'e, XII, 5.1(i).

\subsubsection*{9.4.3}
In case (c), the question is local on \(X\). We may assume \(X\) affine and that there exists an affine scheme \(X'_1\), of finite presentation, quasi-finite and faithfully flat over \(X\), together with an \(X\)-morphism \(X'_1\to X'\). This reduces us to the case where \(f\) is quasi-finite. After further localization on \(X\), this time in the \'etale topology, there exists an open \(X'_1\) of \(X'\) such that \(X'_1\to X\) is finite and surjective, reducing us to the finite surjective case already treated in (a).

\subsubsection*{9.4.4}
In case (d), after localizing on \(X\), we may suppose that \(X\) is a single point, and, taking Theorem 1.1 into account, even that it is the spectrum of a field \(k\). Moreover \(f\) is universally open (\EGA{} IV, 2.4.9), so the hypotheses of Proposition 9.1 apply, and the argument of 9.4.1 still applies, with a base change by \(Y=\Spec k(x)\), for \(x\in X\). In that case it is still true that \(f_*\) commutes with the base change \(Y\to X\), as will be seen later (XVI, 1.4 and 1.5).

This completes the proof of Theorem 9.4.
