\section*{1. Generalities on regular immersions}

The classical notion of regular immersion from EGA IV, 16.9.2, is not adequate in the non-noetherian case. We shall use a weaker notion, which nevertheless has the required properties and which agrees with the usual definition in the noetherian case.

\paragraph{Definition 1.1.} Let $A$ be a commutative ring, let $E$ be a finite projective $A$-module, and let $u:E\to A$ be an $A$-linear homomorphism. We say that $u$ is \emph{regular} if the Koszul complex $K_\bullet(u)$ defined by $u$ is acyclic in positive degrees; equivalently, it is a resolution of $B=A/u(E)$.

Recall that the Koszul complex attached to $u$ is the exterior algebra $\bigwedge(E)$ as a graded $A$-module, with boundary the interior multiplication by $u$, considered as an element of $\check E$. This is the antiderivation of degree $-1$ of $\bigwedge(E)$ which is equal to $u$ on $E$.

\paragraph{Lemma 1.2.} Let $u:E\to A$ be regular. Let $A'$ be an $A$-algebra, let $u':E\otimes_AA'\to A'$ be obtained from $u$ by base change, and suppose that $u'$ is regular. Put $J=u(E)$ and $J'=JA'=u'(E\otimes_AA')$. Then, for every integer $n\geq0$, one has
\begin{align*}
J^n/J^{n+1}\otimes_AA'&\iso J'^n/J'^{n+1},\\
\Tor_i^A(J^n/J^{n+1},A')&=0 \qquad (i\geq1),\\
J^n\otimes_AA'&\iso J'^n,\\
\Tor_i^A(J^n,A')&=0 \qquad (i\geq1).
\end{align*}

\emph{Proof.} First suppose that the quotients $J^n/J^{n+1}$ are projective over $B=A/J$. Since $J$ is of finite type, they are locally free of finite type over $B$. The assertions are local on $\Spec A$, so we may assume that $A$ is local and that $u(E)$ is contained in the maximal ideal. The quotients $J^n/J^{n+1}$ are then free over $B$, and the vanishing of their higher Tors after tensoring with $A'$ is reduced to the vanishing of $\Tor_i^A(B,A')$. These groups are the homology groups of $K_\bullet(u)\otimes_AA'=K_\bullet(u')$, and vanish in positive degrees by the regularity of $u'$.

It follows by induction on $n$, using
\[
0\to J^n/J^{n+1}\to A/J^{n+1}\to A/J^n\to0,
\]
that $\Tor_i^A(A/J^n,A')=0$ for $i\geq1$. The identifications of $J^n/J^{n+1}$ after base change then give the identifications of $J^n$ by using
\[
0\to J^n\to A\to A/J^n\to0,
\]
and the remaining Tor-vanishing follows from
\[
0\to J^{n+1}\to J^n\to J^n/J^{n+1}\to0.
\]
It remains to know that $J^n/J^{n+1}$ is projective over $B$. This is local; assume $E$ is free with basis mapping to $f_1,\ldots,f_d$. Let $A_0=\ZZ[T_1,\ldots,T_d]$ and map $T_i$ to $f_i$. The corresponding universal homomorphism is regular, and the preceding part applies to the base change $A_0\to A$. Since $J_0^n/J_0^{n+1}$ is free of finite type in the universal case, $J^n/J^{n+1}\simeq J_0^n/J_0^{n+1}\otimes_{A_0}A$ is locally free of finite type. This proves the lemma.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.3.} With the hypotheses of 1.2, the ideal $J=u(E)$ is quasi-regular in the sense of EGA IV, 16.9.7. Thus $J$ is of finite type, $J/J^2$ is a finite projective $A/J$-module, and the canonical homomorphism
\[
\Sym_{A/J}(J/J^2)\longrightarrow \gr_J(A)=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}J^n/J^{n+1}
\]
is an isomorphism.

\emph{Proof.} We have just proved that $J/J^2$ is finite projective. Locally, using the same universal polynomial algebra as in the proof of 1.2, the displayed morphism is obtained by base change from the corresponding morphism for the ideal generated by the variables. In that case it is an isomorphism. Hence it is an isomorphism in general.

\paragraph{Definition 1.4.} Let $(X,\cA)$ be a commutatively locally ringed site, let $\uE$ be an $\cA$-module locally free of finite type, and let $u:\uE\to\cA$ be an $\cA$-linear homomorphism. We say that $u$ is regular if its Koszul complex is acyclic in positive degrees. An ideal $\cJ$ of $\cA$ is called regular if it is the image of some surjective regular homomorphism $\uE\to\cJ$.

\paragraph{Definition 1.5.} Let $i:Y\to X$ be an immersion of schemes. Let $U$ be an open subset of $X$ containing $i(Y)$ such that $i$ is a closed immersion into $U$. We say that $i$ is \emph{regular} if the ideal of $\cO_U$ defining $i(Y)$ is regular; this condition does not depend on $U$. The immersion is regular at $y\in Y$ if this is true after restricting to some neighbourhood of $i(y)$.

This definition replaces the one of EGA IV, 16.9.2. In the noetherian case the two definitions are equivalent, by EGA IV, 19.5.1. Any immersion which is locally defined by a regular sequence is regular, and every regular immersion is quasi-regular.

\paragraph{1.4.1--1.4.3.} Let $\cJ$ be a finite type ideal of $\cO_X$, and let $x\in\Supp(\cO_X/\cJ)$. A family of sections $f_i$ of $\cJ$ near $x$ is a minimal system of generators near $x$ when its images form a basis of $\cJ\otimes k(x)$. If $\cJ/\cJ^2$ is locally free, a basis of $\cJ/\cJ^2$ near $x$ can be lifted to a minimal system of generators of $\cJ$ near $x$, by Nakayama's lemma. Thus regularity may be checked on any minimal system of local generators: the corresponding Koszul complex must be acyclic near the point.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.5.} A regular immersion remains regular after flat base change. Conversely, regularity may be checked locally for the quasi-compact faithfully flat topology.

\emph{Proof.} Flat base change carries a Koszul resolution to the base-changed Koszul complex and preserves acyclicity. Conversely, if after a faithfully flat quasi-compact base change the relevant Koszul complex is acyclic, then its positive homology sheaves vanish after a faithful flat pullback and therefore vanish already before base change.

Let $i:Y\to X$ be a regular closed immersion defined by $\cJ$. The \emph{conormal sheaf} of $Y$ in $X$ is
\[
\underline N_{X/Y}=\cJ/\cJ^2.
\]
It is locally free of finite type. Its rank at a point is the codimension of the immersion at that point. We write it as $\codim(i)$ when it is constant.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.6.} Let $i:Y\to X$ and $j:Z\to Y$ be two immersions. Then there is a natural exact sequence
\[
j^*(\underline N_{X/Y})\longrightarrow \underline N_{X/Z}\longrightarrow \underline N_{Y/Z}\longrightarrow0.
\]

\emph{Proof.} Let $\cI$ be the ideal of $Y$ in $X$, $\cJ$ the ideal of $Z$ in $X$, and $\cK$ the ideal of $Z$ in $Y$. From
\[
0\to\cI\to\cJ\to\cK\to0
\]
and after tensoring first by $\cO_Y$ and then by $\cO_Z$, one obtains
\[
j^*(\cI/\cI^2)\to\cJ/\cJ^2\to\cK/\cK^2\to0,
\]
which is the asserted sequence.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.7.} Let $i:Y\to X$ and $j:Z\to Y$ be immersions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item If $i$ and $j$ are regular, then $i\circ j$ is regular, and
\[
0\to j^*(\underline N_{X/Y})\to \underline N_{X/Z}\to \underline N_{Y/Z}\to0
\]
is exact. In particular $\codim(i\circ j)=\codim(i)+\codim(j)$.
\item If $i\circ j$ and $i$ are regular, and the preceding conormal sequence is exact and locally split, then $j$ is regular.
\item If $X$ is noetherian, and if $i\circ j$ and $j$ are regular, then $i$ is regular at the points of $Z$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} The assertion is local, so write $X=\Spec A$, $Y=\Spec B$, $Z=\Spec C$, with $B=A/I$ and $C=A/J=B/K$. Choose minimal systems of generators $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ for $I$ and $\bar g_1,\ldots,\bar g_m$ for $K$, lifted to elements $g_j$ of $A$. The situation is obtained from the standard diagram
\[
\ZZ[T_1,\ldots,T_{n+m}]\to \ZZ[T_1,\ldots,T_m]\to \ZZ
\]
by base change. The regularity conditions are expressed by the vanishing of the corresponding Tor groups. The transitivity spectral sequence
\[
E^2_{pq}=\Tor_p^{B_0}\bigl(C_0,\Tor_q^{A_0}(B_0,A)\bigr)\Longrightarrow \Tor_{p+q}^{A_0}(C_0,A)
\]
then gives (i) and (ii). The rank computation for the conormal sheaves gives exactness on the left. Part (iii) is the noetherian statement of EGA IV, 19.1.5, using the equivalence of definitions in the noetherian case.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.8.} Let $X$ be a scheme, let $\uE$ be a locally free $\cO_X$-module of finite type, and let $u:\uE\to\cO_X$ be regular with image $\cJ$. Let $Y$ be the closed subscheme defined by $\cJ$, let $X'$ be the blow-up of $X$ along $Y$, let $P=\bbP(\uE)$ with structural map $p:P\to X$, and consider on $P$ the exact sequence
\[
0\to\uF\to p^*\uE\xto{v}\cO_P(1)\to0.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The formation of $X'$ commutes with every base change $T\to X$ for which the pullback of $u$ is regular.
\item The closed immersion $j:X'\to P$ defined by the surjection $S(\uE)\to\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\cJ^n$ is regular. Its ideal is the image of the regular morphism $v$; if $\codim(Y,X)$ is constant, then $\codim(j)=\codim(i)-1$.
\item The graded $S(\uE)$-module $\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\cJ^n$ is perfect as a graded module: locally it has a finite graded resolution by graded free modules of finite type.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} The first assertion follows from $X'=\Proj(\bigoplus J^n)$ and the base-change formulas of 1.2. For (ii), the question is local. In the standard case $A=\ZZ[X_1,\ldots,X_d]$, with $u(e_i)=X_i$, one works on the standard open $D^+(X_1)$ of the projective bundle. The kernel of $p^*\uE\to\cO_P(1)$ is generated by $e_i-(X_i/X_1)e_1$, $i>1$, and the functions defining the image of $v$ form a regular sequence; they generate exactly the ideal of the blow-up in the projective bundle. The general case is obtained by base change from this standard case, and acyclicity follows from the Tor-vanishing in 1.2. For (iii), construct in the standard noetherian case a finite graded free resolution, and tensor it with the given ring. The homology in positive degree is computed by the Tor groups $\Tor_i(A,J_0^n)$, which vanish by 1.2; hence the tensor complex is the desired resolution.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.9.} Let $i:Y\to X$ be a regular closed immersion, let $X'$ be the blow-up of $X$ along $Y$, and let $f:X'\to X$ be the structural morphism. Then $i$ and $f$ are perfect morphisms.

\emph{Proof.} Locally the structure sheaf of $Y$ has a finite locally free resolution given by a Koszul complex, hence $i$ is perfect. By 1.8, $f$ factors as a regular immersion into a projective bundle followed by the smooth structural morphism of the projective bundle. Both factors are perfect.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.10.} If $X$ and $Y$ are smooth schemes over $S$, then every immersion $X\to Y$ is regular. In particular every section of a smooth morphism is a regular immersion.

This follows at once from EGA IV, 17.12.1, because in the noetherian smooth case the EGA notion of regular immersion agrees with the present one.

\section*{2. Computations for regular immersions}

\paragraph{2.1--2.3.} Let $A$ be a ring and let $B,C$ be $A$-algebras. The graded module
\[
\bigoplus_{i\geq0}\Tor_i^A(B,C)
\]
has a natural structure of an associative graded algebra over $B\otimes_A C$. Indeed, take a projective resolution $P_\bullet$ of $B$. The product is obtained by composing the canonical homology product
\[
H_i(P_\bullet\otimes_A C)\otimes_AH_j(P_\bullet\otimes_A C)	o H_{i+j}((P_\bullet\otimes_A C)\otimes_A(P_\bullet\otimes_A C))
\]
with a lifting $P_\bullet\otimes_A P_\bullet\to P_\bullet$ of the multiplication $B\otimes_AB\to B$, and then with the multiplication of $C$. The result is independent of choices and is functorial in $B$ and $C$.

If the resolution $P_\bullet$ is itself a differential graded algebra, then this product is computed directly by the product of $P_\bullet$. In particular, for a quotient by a regular ideal one may use the Koszul resolution; hence the resulting Tor-algebra is alternating. These constructions globalize: for two $X$-schemes $X'$ and $X''$, the sheaf
\[
\bigoplus_{i\geq0}\Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cO_{X'},\cO_{X''})
\]
is a graded associative $\cO_{X'\times_X X''}$-algebra.

\paragraph{Lemma 2.4.} Let $X'$ and $X''$ be two $X$-schemes, and let $\cM$ be a flat $\cO_{X'}$-module. Then there is a canonical isomorphism
\[
\Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cO_{X'},\cO_{X''})\otimes_{\cO_{X'}}\cM\iso \Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cM,\cO_{X''}).
\]

\emph{Proof.} This is affine-local. If $X=\Spec A$, $X'=\Spec A'$, $X''=\Spec A''$, and $M$ is a flat $A'$-module, a projective $A$-resolution of $A''$ gives
\[
M\otimes_{A'}(A'\otimes_AP_\bullet)\simeq M\otimes_AP_\bullet,
\]
and passage to homology gives the assertion.

\paragraph{Proposition 2.5.} Let $Y$ and $Z$ be closed subschemes of $X$, and put $T=Y\times_X Z$. Assume that locally near every point of $T$ the schemes $Y$, $Z$, and $T$ are defined by Koszul resolutions associated to systems of sections $f_1,\ldots,f_n$, $g_1,\ldots,g_m$, and their union. Then
\[
\Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cO_Y,\cO_Z)\simeq \bigwedge^i\Tor_1^{\cO_X}(\cO_Y,\cO_Z)
\]
for all $i\geq0$, and
\[
\Tor_1^{\cO_X}(\cO_Y,\cO_Z)
\simeq \Ker\bigl(i^*\underline N_{X/Y}\oplus j^*\underline N_{X/Z}\to\underline N_{X/T}\bigr),
\]
where $i:T\to Y$ and $j:T\to Z$ are the induced immersions. In particular, if $Y\to X$ is a regular closed immersion, then
\[
\Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cO_Y,\cO_Z)\simeq \bigwedge^i\Tor_1^{\cO_X}(\cO_Y,\cO_Z),
\qquad
\Tor_1^{\cO_X}(\cO_Y,\cO_Z)\simeq i^*\underline N_{X/Y}.
\]

\emph{Proof.} The Tor-algebra is alternating by the Koszul calculation of 2.2, hence there is a unique algebra homomorphism from the exterior algebra on degree one. Locally, the tensor product of the two Koszul complexes is the Koszul complex for the union of the two systems. The spectral sequence of the associated bicomplex degenerates, and the homology is exactly the exterior algebra on the degree-one homology. The description of degree one follows from the exact sequence
\[
0\to\cI\cap\cJ\to\cI\oplus\cJ\to\cI+\cJ\to0
\]
and the Tor exact sequence; it identifies the degree-one Tor with the kernel of the difference map between the two conormal sheaves and the conormal sheaf of $T$.

\paragraph{2.6.} Suppose that $X$ has an ample sheaf. If $i:Y\to X$ is a regular closed immersion, then $i$ is perfect and the additive morphism
\[
i_*:K'(Y)\to K'(X)
\]
is defined by sending a perfect complex on $Y$ to its direct image on $X$.

\paragraph{Proposition 2.7.} Let $X$ have an ample sheaf, let $i:Y\to X$ be a regular closed immersion, and let $\uN$ be the conormal sheaf of $Y$ in $X$. Then, for every $x\in\Kdot(Y)$,
\[
i^!i_*(x)=x\cdot\lambda_{-1}(\uN),
\qquad
\lambda_{-1}(\uN)=\sum_{i\geq0}(-1)^i\lambda^i(\uN).
\]

\emph{Proof.} By additivity it is enough to take $x$ to be the class of a locally free $\cO_Y$-module $\cM$ of finite type. The homology sheaves of $\LL i^*\RR i_*(\cM)$ are
\[
\Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cM,\cO_Y)\simeq \cM\otimes_{\cO_Y}\bigwedge^i\uN
\]
by 2.4 and 2.5. Their alternating sum in $K'(Y)$ is precisely $x\lambda_{-1}(\uN)$.

\paragraph{Corollary 2.8.} Under the hypotheses of 2.7, the self-intersection formula is
\[
i_*(y\cdot x\cdot\lambda_{-1}(\uN))=i_*(y)\cdot i_*(x)
\]
for $x,y\in\Kdot(Y)$. This follows from 2.7 and the projection formula.

\section*{3. Computation of $K'$ of a blow-up}

Let $i:Y\to X$ be a regular closed immersion. Let $X'$ be the blow-up of $X$ along $Y$, put $Y'=Y\times_XX'$, and write $f:X'\to X$ and $g:Y'\to Y$ for the structural morphisms. The immersion $j:Y'\to X'$ is a regular closed immersion of codimension one, defined by an invertible ideal. If $\cJ$ is the ideal of $Y$ in $X$, then $\cJ\cO_{X'}=\cO_{X'}(1)$.

Since
\[
Y'=\Proj\bigl(\bigoplus_{n\geq0}\cJ^n/\cJ^{n+1}\bigr)
=\Proj(\Sym(\uN))=\bbP(\uN),
\]
where $\uN=\cJ/\cJ^2$, there is on $Y'$ an exact sequence
\[
0\to\uF\to g^*\uN\to\cO_{Y'}(1)\to0.
\]
The sheaf $\cO_{Y'}(1)$ is also the conormal sheaf of $j$.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.2.} With these notations,
\[
\Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cO_{X'},\cO_Y)\simeq \bigwedge^i\Tor_1^{\cO_X}(\cO_{X'},\cO_Y),
\qquad
\Tor_1^{\cO_X}(\cO_{X'},\cO_Y)\simeq \uF.
\]

\emph{Proof.} Work locally where $\cJ$ is defined by a regular sequence $f_1,\ldots,f_d$. On the standard open of the blow-up on which $f_1^{(1)}$ is invertible, change the basis of the free module defining the Koszul complex by setting $e'_1=e_1$ and $e'_i=e_i-(f_i/f_1)e_1$ for $i>1$. The differential sends $e'_1$ to $f_1$ and the other $e'_i$ to zero. Since $f_1$ is a non-zero-divisor on that open, the homology is the exterior algebra on the image of the module spanned by $e'_2,\ldots,e'_d$, which is $\uF$. These local identifications are compatible with the algebra structure and glue.

\paragraph{3.3.} If $X$ has an ample sheaf, then so do $X'$, $Y$, and $Y'$. The immersions $i$ and $j$, the morphism $g$ (a projective bundle), and the morphism $f$ are perfect. Hence we have additive maps
\[
i_*:K'(Y)\to K'(X),\quad j_*:K'(Y')\to K'(X'),\quad
f_*:K'(X')\to K'(X),\quad g_*:K'(Y')\to K'(Y).
\]

\paragraph{Proposition 3.4.} For every $x\in K'(Y)$,
\[
f^*i_*(x)=j_*\bigl(g^*(x)\lambda_{-1}(\uF)\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, after applying $f_*$ to the two sides, this is the usual compatibility of the Gysin construction with the exceptional divisor.

\emph{Proof.} It is enough by additivity to take $x$ to be a locally free sheaf $\cM$ on $Y$. The homology of $\LL f^*\RR i_*(\cM)$ is $\Tor_i^{\cO_X}(\cM,\cO_{X'})$, and 2.4 together with Lemma 3.2 identifies this with $g^*\cM\otimes\bigwedge^i\uF$. Taking the alternating class gives the displayed formula.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.5.} For the blow-up $f:X'\to X$,
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item $R^if_*\cO_{X'}(n)=0$ for $i\geq1$ and $n\geq0$;
\item $f_*\cO_{X'}(n)=\cJ^n$ for $n\geq0$.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Proof.} The assertion is local on $X$. For $n\gg0$ the vanishing follows from the standard acyclicity theorem for projective perfect morphisms. The exact sequences
\[
0\to\cO_{X'}(n+1)\to\cO_{X'}(n)\to\cO_{Y'}(n)\to0
\]
and the known cohomology of $\cO(n)$ on the projective bundle $Y'=\bbP(\uN)$ permit descending induction to all $n\geq0$. The identification of the direct image with $\cJ^n$ is first obtained for large $n$ from a finite graded presentation of $\bigoplus\cJ^n$, then descended by the same exact sequences.

\paragraph{Proposition 3.6.} With the above notation,
\[
f_*f^*=\Id_{K'(X)}.
\]

\emph{Proof.} The projection formula gives $f_*f^*(x)=x\cdot f_*(1)$. By Lemma 3.5, $\RR f_*\cO_{X'}$ has no higher cohomology and degree-zero cohomology $\cO_X$, so $f_*(1)=1$.

\paragraph{Theorem 3.7.} Suppose $X$ is quasi-compact and has an ample sheaf. Then the sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow K'(Y)\xto{u}K'(Y')\times K'(X)\xto{v}K'(X')
\]
is exact, where
\[
u(y)=\bigl(g^*(y)\lambda_{-1}(\uF),-i_*(y)\bigr),
\qquad
v(y',x)=j_*(y')+f^*(x).
\]
Moreover $u$ admits the left inverse $u'(y',x)=g_*(y')$. If, in addition, $X$ is noetherian and $Y$ is regular, then $v$ is surjective, and hence
\[
0\to K'(Y)\to K'(Y')\times K'(X)\to K'(X')\to0
\]
is exact.

\emph{Proof.} The identity $g_*\lambda_{-1}(\uF)=1$, together with the projection formula, gives the left inverse of $u$. The equality $v\circ u=0$ is exactly Proposition 3.4. Conversely, if $j_*(y')+f^*(x)=0$, applying $f_*$ and using 3.6 gives $x=-i_*g_*(y')$. Put $y=g_*(y')$. It remains to prove $y'=g^*(y)\lambda_{-1}(\uF)$. The difference $z=y'-g^*(y)\lambda_{-1}(\uF)$ has $g_*(z)=0$ and $j_*(z)=0$. The self-intersection formula gives $j^*j_*(z)=z(1-L)$, where $L$ is the conormal line of $j$. The projective-bundle computation implies that the simultaneous kernel of $j_*$ and $g_*$ is zero; hence $z=0$.

If $X$ is noetherian and $Y$ regular, then $Y'$ and $X'$ are regular along the exceptional divisor, and the usual devissage and localization argument gives the surjectivity of $v$.

\paragraph{Remark 3.8.} In the noetherian regular case, Theorem 3.7 determines the abelian group $K'(X')$ from the $\lambda$-rings $K'(X)$ and $K'(Y)$, the maps $i_*$ and $i^*$, and the class of the conormal sheaf $N$ in $K'(Y)$. The projective-bundle formula computes $K'(Y')$ from $K'(Y)$ and $N$. The next results describe the multiplicative and rational $\lambda$-structures from the same data.

\paragraph{Proposition 3.9.} Let $X$ be noetherian and $Y$ regular. In the notation of 3.7, the exterior-power operations on the class represented by $f^*(x)=j_*(y')+x'$ are computed by the same universal formulas as in the projective-bundle case: if $y=g_*(y')$, then
\[
f^*(\lambda^p(x))=j_*\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(-1)^i\lambda^{p-1-i}(N)g^*(\lambda^i(y))\Bigr)+\lambda^p(x')
\]
whenever the displayed decomposition is defined. The proof reduces to the case where $i$ is a section of a projective bundle, by using the blow-up square and functoriality of the operations $\lambda^p$.

\section*{4. Self-intersection formulas for the operations $\lambda^p$ and $\gamma^p$}

\paragraph{Proposition 4.1.} Let $i:Y\to X$ be a regular immersion of codimension $d$, and suppose the ideal of $Y$ is generated by a regular sequence of $d$ sections. If $N$ is the conormal class and $y\in K'(Y)$, then for every $p\geq1$,
\[
i^*i_*(\lambda^p(Ny))=\lambda^p(N)\lambda^p(y).
\]
The proof reduces to the zero section of a projective space bundle.

\paragraph{Propositions 4.2--4.3.} The preceding formula holds for every regular immersion. One embeds the situation into successive projective bundles so that the composite immersion is given by a regular sequence, proves the formula for the composite, and descends step by step.

Consequently, for every regular immersion $i:Y\to X$ and every $y\in K'(Y)$,
\[
i_*(\lambda^p(Ny))=\sum_{k=0}^{p}(-1)^k\lambda^{p-k}(N)\lambda^k(i_*(y))
\quad\text{modulo torsion}.
\]

\paragraph{Theorem 4.3.} Let $i:Y\to X$ be a regular immersion with conormal class $N$. For every $y\in K'(Y)$ and every $p\geq1$,
\[
i_*(\gamma^p(Ny))=\gamma^p(i_*(y))
\quad\text{modulo torsion}.
\]

\emph{Proof.} Apply the previous exterior-power formula to the generating functions defining the Adams and Grothendieck operations. If
\[
u(t)=1+\sum_{k\geq1}i_*(\lambda^k(Ny))t^k,
\qquad
v(t)=\sum_{k\geq0}\lambda^k(i_*(y))t^k,
\]
then the logarithmic-derivative relation implies $d(u(t)/v(t))/dt\equiv0$ modulo torsion. Since the constant term is $1$, integration gives $u(t)\equiv v(t)$, which is equivalent to the stated congruences for the $\gamma^p$.

\paragraph{Corollary 4.4.} The same congruence follows directly for the operations $\gamma^p$, since they are integral linear combinations of the exterior-power operations in the universal formulas.

\paragraph{Remark 4.5.} The proof suggests that the congruence should in fact be an equality, not merely an equality modulo torsion. In characteristic zero this was proved in Riemann-Roch references by a geometric characterization of the classes $\lambda^p(Nx)$. Such a method should extend to all characteristics once exterior powers are available for perfect complexes.

\section*{4.6. Application to the multiplicative structure}

Keep the notation of 3.1 and 3.3, and suppose $X$ noetherian and $Y$ regular. Theorem 3.7 describes $K'(X')$ as an abelian group. Its multiplication is determined by the rings $K'(X)$ and $K'(Y)$, the maps $i_*$ and $i^*$, and the conormal class $N$.

More explicitly, for $y'_1,y'_2\in K'(Y')$ and $x_1,x_2\in K'(X)$,
\begin{multline*}
\bigl(j_*(y'_1)+f^*(x_1)\bigr)\bigl(j_*(y'_2)+f^*(x_2)\bigr)\\
= j_*\bigl(y'_1g^*(g_*(y'_2))\lambda_{-1}(\uF)\bigr)
+j_*\bigl(y'_1f^*i^*(x_2)\bigr)+j_*\bigl(y'_2f^*i^*(x_1)\bigr)+f^*(x_1x_2).
\end{multline*}
This is just the projection formula, the self-intersection formula for $j$, and Theorem 3.7.

\section*{4.7. Rational $\lambda$-ring structure of $K'(X')$}

After tensoring with $\QQ$, the $\lambda$-ring structure of $K'(X')$ is determined by the same data. Recall that over a $\QQ$-algebra the datum of a binomial-ring structure is equivalent to a $\lambda$-ring structure. If $y'\in K'(Y')$ and $y=g_*(y')$, then
\[
j^*j_*(y')=y'\,g^*(N)
\]
and the Adams operations satisfy
\[
\psi^k(j_*(y'))=j_*\bigl(g^*(\psi^k(y))\lambda_{-1}(\uF)^{k-1}\bigr).
\]
Together with the formulas of 3.9 and the known $\lambda$-structures on $K'(X)_\QQ$ and $K'(Y)_\QQ$, these identities determine the operations $\lambda^p$ on $K'(X')_\QQ$.

\section*{5. Complements and applications}

\subsection*{5.1. Complete intersections}

\paragraph{Proposition 5.1.} Let $X$ be a regular scheme with an ample sheaf, and let $Y$ be a closed subscheme which is a complete intersection of order $r$. Then the ideal of $Y$ is locally generated by an $\cO_X$-regular sequence of $r$ sections.

\paragraph{Theorem 5.2.} Let $X$ be a regular scheme with an ample sheaf, let $Z$ be an irreducible closed subscheme of $X$, and let $z$ be its generic point. Suppose the maximal ideal of $\cO_{X,z}$ can be generated by an $\cO_{X,z}$-regular sequence. Then there is an open neighbourhood $U$ of $z$ such that $Z\cap U\to U$ is a regular immersion.

\emph{Proof.} The ideal of $Z$ at its generic point is the maximal ideal of the regular local ring $\cO_{X,z}$, and by hypothesis it has a regular system of generators. After shrinking $X$ near $z$, these generators extend to sections defining a regular ideal. Hence $Z\cap U\to U$ is regular, of the expected codimension.

\subsection*{5.3. Filtrations and Gysin morphisms}

For the filtration by codimension of support, a regular immersion $i:Y\to X$ of codimension $d$ satisfies
\[
i_*(\Fil^kK'(Y))\subset\Fil^{k+d}K'(X).
\]
For the blow-up $f:X'\to X$ along $Y$,
\[
f^*(\Fil^kK'(X))\subset\Fil^kK'(X').
\]
The exact sequence of Theorem 3.7 is compatible with these filtrations in the noetherian regular case.

\subsection*{5.6. Surjectivity after tensoring with $\QQ$}

Suppose $X$ is noetherian and $Y$ regular. The surjectivity in Theorem 3.7 remains true after tensoring with $\QQ$. If $x'\in\Fil^kK'(X')_\QQ$, one may choose $(y',x)\in K'(Y')_\QQ\times K'(X)_\QQ$ with
\[
x'=v(y',x),\qquad x\in\Fil^kK'(X)_\QQ.
\]
Filtering the product by
\[
\Fil^k(K'(Y')_\QQ\times K'(X)_\QQ)=\Fil^{k-1}K'(Y')_\QQ\times\Fil^kK'(X)_\QQ,
\]
one obtains a surjection
\[
\Fil^k(K'(Y')_\QQ\times K'(X)_\QQ)\longrightarrow\Fil^kK'(X')_\QQ.
\]
Indeed, if $x'=\gamma^k(x'_1)$ with augmentation zero, lift $x'_1$ to a pair $(y'_1,x_1)$ with $x_1$ of filtration at least one; the formula for the $\gamma$-operation in the product ring gives a lift of $x'$ in the required filtered degree.
