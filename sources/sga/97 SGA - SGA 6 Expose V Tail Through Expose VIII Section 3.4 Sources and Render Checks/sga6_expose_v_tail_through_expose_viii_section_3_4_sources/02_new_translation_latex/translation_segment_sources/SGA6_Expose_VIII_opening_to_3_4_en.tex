\section*{1. Complete-intersection morphisms}

\paragraph{Definition 1.1.} Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of schemes. We say that $f$ is a \emph{complete-intersection morphism} if, for every point $x\in X$, there exist an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ and a smooth $Y$-scheme $V$ such that the restriction $f|_U$ factors as
\[
U\xto{i}V\to Y,
\]
where $i$ is a regular immersion in the sense of Expose VII. After replacing $V$ by an open subscheme, one may suppose that $i$ is a regular closed immersion.

In this definition one may replace $V$ by an affine space $\bbA^n_Y$. Indeed, locally $V$ embeds as a closed subscheme of an affine space over an affine open of $Y$; this immersion is regular because $V$ is smooth, and the composite of regular immersions is regular.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.2.} Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism. Suppose that two factorizations of $f$ through smooth $Y$-schemes are related by a smooth morphism between the intermediate schemes. Then the immersion in one factorization is regular if and only if the immersion in the other factorization is regular.

\emph{Proof.} If $i:X\to V$ is regular and $V\to V'$ is smooth, form $X'=V\times_{V'}X$. The induced section $X\to X'$ is a regular immersion, being a section of a smooth morphism, and the base change of $i$ is regular. Hence the composite immersion is regular. Conversely, when the smooth morphism is etale the comparison is immediate because the induced section is open; the general smooth case is reduced etale-locally to this situation.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.3.} Suppose $X=\Spec B$, $Y=\Spec A$, and $f:X\to Y$ factors through a regular closed immersion $j:X\to\bbA^n_Y$. Then one may find an analogous factorization through $\bbA^{n+1}_Y$ in which a section of the projection is a regular immersion and the remaining immersion is regular.

\emph{Proof.} Choose lifts in $A$ of the coordinate functions defining the original factorization. They define a section of the larger affine space. Since a section of a smooth morphism is a regular immersion, and since the conormal sequence for the two immersions is exact and locally split, the remaining immersion is regular by Expose VII, 1.7.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.4.} The composite of two complete-intersection morphisms is a complete-intersection morphism.

\emph{Proof.} Factor each morphism locally as a regular immersion followed by a smooth morphism. After restricting to suitable opens, the composite factors through the fiber product of the two smooth intermediates; the new intermediate is smooth and the induced immersion is regular by Expose VII, 1.7.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.5.} Complete-intersection morphisms are stable under arbitrary base change.

\emph{Proof.} This follows from the definition, from the stability of smooth morphisms under base change, and from the flat-base-change stability of regular immersions.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.6.} A morphism which locally factors as a regular immersion followed by a smooth morphism is perfect. In particular every complete-intersection morphism is perfect.

\emph{Proof.} A regular immersion is perfect by Expose VII, 1.9, and a smooth morphism is perfect. The composite of perfect morphisms is perfect.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.7.} If a complete-intersection morphism is flat and of finite presentation, then locally it is a regular immersion followed by a smooth morphism of fixed pure relative dimension.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.8.} Let $f:X\to Y$ be a complete-intersection morphism, and let
\[
U\xto{i}V\xto{g}Y
\]
be a local factorization near $x$, with $i$ regular and $g$ smooth. If $\cJ$ is the ideal of $i$, the integer
\[
d_x=\rank(\Omega^1_{V/Y})_{i(x)}-\rank(\cJ/\cJ^2)_x
\]
does not depend on the chosen factorization.

\emph{Proof.} Given two factorizations, compare them through the fiber product of the two smooth intermediate schemes over $Y$. The conormal sheaf for the induced immersion is obtained by adding the pullback of the relative differentials of the smooth comparison morphism. Hence the displayed difference of ranks is unchanged.

\paragraph{Definition 1.9.} The integer $d_x$ is called the \emph{virtual relative dimension} of $f$ at $x$. If it is independent of $x$, for instance when $X$ is connected, it is called the virtual relative dimension of $f$ and is denoted $d(f)$ or $d_{X/Y}$.

\paragraph{Proposition 1.10.} If $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to Z$ are complete-intersection morphisms, then
\[
d_{g\circ f}=d_f+d_g.
\]

\paragraph{Proposition 1.11.} In a cartesian square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
X'&\longrightarrow&X\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow f\\
Y'&\longrightarrow&Y,
\end{array}
\]
if $f$ is a complete-intersection morphism, then so is its base change $f'$, and $d_{f'}=d_f$.

\section*{2. Projective morphisms and complete-intersection morphisms}

\paragraph{Proposition 2.1.} A projective smooth morphism is a complete-intersection morphism, and its virtual relative dimension is its relative dimension.

\paragraph{Proposition 2.2.} A projective morphism which is a regular closed immersion is a complete-intersection morphism of virtual relative dimension $-\codim(X,Y)$.

\paragraph{Theorem 2.3.} Let $f:X\to Y$ be projective. Suppose $f$ factors as
\[
X\xto{i}\bbP(E)\xto{p}Y,
\]
where $E$ is a vector bundle on $Y$, $p$ is the canonical projection, and $i$ is a regular immersion. Then $f$ is a complete-intersection morphism and
\[
d_f=\rank(E)-1-\codim(X,\bbP(E)).
\]

\emph{Proof.} The projection $p$ is smooth of relative dimension $\rank(E)-1$, and the immersion $i$ has virtual relative dimension equal to the negative of its codimension. The formula follows from the additivity of virtual dimensions.

\paragraph{Corollary 2.4.} If $f:X\to Y$ is projective and a complete-intersection morphism, then after choosing a sufficiently ample vector bundle on $Y$, $f$ may be factored as a regular immersion into a projective bundle followed by the projection.

\paragraph{Proposition 2.5.} Let $f:X\to Y$ be projective and a complete-intersection morphism.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item If $f$ is finite, then $d_f\leq0$.
\item If $f$ is surjective, then $d_f\geq0$.
\item If $f$ is finite and surjective, then $d_f=0$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Remark 2.6.} If $Y$ is connected and $f:X\to Y$ is projective and a complete-intersection morphism, then $d_f$ is constant on $Y$; for a dominant morphism it is $\dim X-\dim Y$.

\section*{3. Complete-intersection morphisms and the Chern character}

\paragraph{Proposition 3.1.} Let $f:X\to Y$ be a projective complete-intersection morphism of virtual relative dimension $d$. Suppose $Y$ is quasi-compact and has an ample sheaf. Then, for every integer $k$,
\[
f_*(\Fil^kK'(X)_\QQ)\subset\Fil^{k-d}K'(Y)_\QQ.
\]

\emph{Proof.} Factor $f$ as $X\xto{i}P\xto{p}Y$, with $p$ smooth of relative dimension $n$ and $i$ a regular immersion of codimension $n-d$. The Gysin map for $i$ raises the support-codimension filtration by $n-d$, while the smooth projective projection $p$ lowers it by $n$. The result follows.

\paragraph{Proposition 3.2.} The preceding inclusion defines graded homomorphisms
\[
f_{\mathrm{gr}}:\Gr^kK'(X)_\QQ\longrightarrow\Gr^{k-d}K'(Y)_\QQ.
\]

\subsection*{3.3. Reminder on the Chern character and the Todd class}

For a graded $K$-algebra $A$, with $K$ a binomial ring, Expose V defined
\[
\Ch(A)=K\times(1+\widehat A^+).
\]
For every augmented $K$-$\lambda$-algebra $\Lambda$ there is a $K$-$\lambda$-homomorphism, the completed Chern class,
\[
\widetilde c=(\varepsilon,c):\Lambda\longrightarrow\Ch(\Gr_\Lambda).
\]
There is also a functorial ring homomorphism, the Chern character,
\[
\ch:\Ch(A)\longrightarrow K\otimes_\ZZ\QQ,
\]
and a multiplicative endomorphism, the Todd character,
\[
\Todd:1+\widehat A^+\longrightarrow1+\widehat A^+.
\]
Both are multiplicative in the expected sense: $\ch(xy)=\ch(x)\ch(y)$ and $\Todd(x\oplus y)=\Todd(x)\Todd(y)$.

\subsection*{3.4. The Todd character of a morphism}

\paragraph{Definition 3.4.} Let $f:X\to Y$ be a complete-intersection morphism of virtual relative dimension $d$. Assume $Y$ is quasi-compact and has an ample sheaf. The \emph{Todd character} of $f$, denoted $\tau(f)$ or $\tau_{X/Y}$, is the element of $\Gr K'(X)_\QQ$ defined by
\[
\tau(f)=\Todd(N_f)^{-1},
\]
where $N_f$ is the virtual conormal class of $f$ in $K'(X)$.

\paragraph{Proposition 3.4.} If $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to Z$ are complete-intersection morphisms, then
\[
\tau(g\circ f)=\tau(f)\cdot f^*\tau(g).
\]

\emph{Proof.} The virtual conormal class satisfies $N_{g\circ f}=N_f\oplus f^*N_g$. The multiplicativity of the Todd character gives
\[
\Todd(N_{g\circ f})=\Todd(N_f)\,f^*\Todd(N_g),
\]
and taking inverses gives the formula.
