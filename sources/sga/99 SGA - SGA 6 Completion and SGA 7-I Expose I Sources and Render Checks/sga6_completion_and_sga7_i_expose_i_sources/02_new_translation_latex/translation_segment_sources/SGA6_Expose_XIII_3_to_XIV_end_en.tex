\section*{Exposé XIII -- Quotients of Picard schemes by subschemes of Pic, continuation}

\subsection*{3. General finiteness theorems}

\paragraph{Lemma 3.1.}
Let $S$ be a scheme and let $X$ be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated $S$-scheme. Let $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ be the Picard functor localized for the f.p.p.f. topology. Then, for every filtering projective system $(Z_\alpha)$ of quasi-compact and quasi-separated $S$-schemes whose transition morphisms are affine, the canonical map
\[
\varinjlim_\alpha \underline{\Pic}_{X/S}(Z_\alpha)
   \longto
\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}\bigl(\varprojlim_\alpha Z_\alpha\bigr)
\tag{3.1.1}
\]
is bijective. Here one uses the definition of $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ of SGA 3, IV, 6.3 and FGA, no. 232, with the f.p.p.f. topology in place of the fpqc topology.

Consequently, by EGA IV, 8.14.2, if the Picard functor is represented by an $S$-scheme $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$, then this representing scheme is locally of finite presentation over $S$.

Indeed, an element $\lambda_\alpha$ of $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}(Z_\alpha)$ is represented by an invertible sheaf $L'_\alpha$ on $X_{Z'_\alpha}$, where $Z'_\alpha\to Z_\alpha$ is f.p.p.f. Put $Z=\varprojlim Z_\alpha$. The image $\lambda$ of $\lambda_\alpha$ in $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}(Z)$ is represented by the invertible sheaf $L'$ on $X_{Z'}$ obtained by pullback from $L'_\alpha$, where $Z'=Z'_\alpha\times_{Z_\alpha}Z$.

If $\lambda$ is the neutral element of $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}(Z)$, then there exists an f.p.p.f. morphism $Z''\to Z'$ such that the pullback of $L'$ to $X_{Z''}$ is isomorphic to $\cO_{X_{Z''}}$. By the standard limit theorems of EGA IV, there exist $\beta\geq\alpha$ and an f.p.p.f. morphism
\[
Z''_\beta \longto Z'_\beta=Z'_\alpha\times_{Z_\alpha}Z_\beta
\]
such that the pullback of $L'_\alpha$ to $X_{Z''_\beta}$ is $\cO_{X_{Z''_\beta}}$. Thus the image of $\lambda_\alpha$ is already neutral in $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}(Z_\beta)$, and (3.1.1) is injective. Surjectivity is proved in the same way.

\paragraph{Proposition 3.2.}
Let $S$ be noetherian and let $X$ be proper over $S$. Assume that the Picard functor is represented by an $S$-scheme $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ is locally of finite type over $S$.
\item Subsets $\Lambda$ of $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ correspond bijectively to families $\cL$ of classes of invertible sheaves on the fibres of $X/S$.
\item Such a subset $\Lambda$ is quasi-compact if and only if the corresponding family $\cL$ is bounded.
\end{enumerate}

Here (i) follows from (3.1), and (ii) from the fact that if $s\in S$ and $K$ is an algebraically closed extension of $k(s)$, then the $K$-points of $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ are in bijection with isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves on $X_K$.

For (iii), suppose first that $\Lambda$ is quasi-compact. Then $\Lambda$ is contained in an open subscheme $U$ of finite type, and the inclusion $U\to \underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ corresponds to an invertible sheaf $L$ on some $X_T$, where $T\to U$ is a surjective flat morphism of finite type. Since $T\to S$ is of finite type, $L$ bounds $\cL$.

Conversely, suppose that $\cL$ is bounded by a coherent sheaf $L$ on $X_T$, with $T$ of finite type over $S$. By 1.13 (i), we may assume $L$ invertible, so that it defines a morphism
\[
f:T\longto \underline{\Pic}_{X/S}.
\]
The image of $f$ contains $\Lambda$, and is quasi-compact; it is therefore noetherian by (i). Hence $\Lambda$ is quasi-compact.

\paragraph{Definition 3.3.}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be proper over a noetherian scheme $S$. A morphism of Picard functors
\[
\underline{\Pic}_{Y/S}\longto \underline{\Pic}_{X/S}
\]
is said to be \emph{of finite type} if the inverse image of every bounded family of classes of invertible sheaves on the fibres of $X/S$ is a bounded family of classes of sheaves on the fibres of $Y/S$. This extends the usual definition for morphisms between Picard schemes.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.4.}
Let $X,Y,X',Y'$ be proper over a noetherian scheme $S$, and let $T\to S$ be surjective of finite type. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item A morphism $\Phi:\underline{\Pic}_{Y/S}\to\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ is of finite type if and only if the base changed morphism
\[
\Phi_T:\underline{\Pic}_{Y/T}\to\underline{\Pic}_{X/T}
\]
is of finite type.
\item In a commutative square
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\underline{\Pic}_{Y'/S} \arrow[r]\arrow[d] &
\underline{\Pic}_{Y/S}\arrow[d,"\Phi"]\\
\underline{\Pic}_{X'/S} \arrow[r] &
\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}
\end{tikzcd}
\]
if the corresponding upper and lower auxiliary morphisms are of finite type, then so is $\Phi$.
\end{enumerate}

If $\Phi_T$ is of finite type, let $\cL$ be a family on the fibres of $X/S$, bounded by a sheaf $L$ on $X_P$. Then the base changed family $\cL_T$ on $X_T/T$ is bounded by the pullback of $L$ to $X_{P\times_S T}$. If $\Phi_T(\cL_T)$ is bounded by a sheaf $M$ on some $Y_Q$, then $M$ also bounds $\Phi(\cL)$. The converse and assertion (ii) are similar.

\paragraph{Theorem 3.5.}
Let $S$ be noetherian, let $X$ and $Y$ be proper $S$-schemes, and let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism. If $f$ is surjective, then the induced morphism
\[
f^*:\underline{\Pic}_{Y/S}\longto\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}
\]
is of finite type.

It is enough, by (3.4) (i), to assume $S$ reduced and irreducible. Then there is a non-empty open $U\subset S$ over which the Picard functors of $Y/S$ and $X/S$ are representable and the restriction of $f^*$ is quasi-affine, by XII, 1.1; hence it is of finite type by 3.2 (i). Giving $T=S-U$ the induced reduced structure, noetherian induction permits one to assume that the corresponding morphism over $T$ is of finite type. The assertion then follows from 3.4 (i), applied to $T\hookrightarrow S$.

\paragraph{Theorem 3.6.}
Let $S$ be noetherian, let $X$ be proper over $S$, and let $\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ be the Picard functor. For every non-zero integer $n$, the $n$th power homomorphism
\[
n:\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}\longto \underline{\Pic}_{X/S}
\]
is a morphism of finite type.

Let $f:X'\to X$ be a Chow presentation: $X'$ is projective over $S$ and $f$ is surjective. If the theorem is known for $X'$, then it follows for $X$ from 3.5 and 3.4 (ii). Thus we may suppose $X/S$ projective.

There exists a non-empty open $U\subset S$, an integral scheme $S'$, and a finite surjective morphism $S'\to U$ such that every irreducible component $X'_j$ of $X_{S'}$, with its induced reduced structure, is integral over $S'$. Giving $T=S-U$ the induced reduced structure, noetherian induction allows the theorem for $X_T/T$. If it is known for all $X'_j/S'$, it follows for $\coprod_j X'_j/S'$ from the following lemma, hence for $X_{S'}/S'$ by 3.5 and 3.4 (ii), and finally for $X/S$ by 3.4 (i).

\paragraph{Lemma 3.7.}
Let $X=\coprod_i X_i$ be of finite type over a noetherian scheme $S$, and let $\cF$ be a family of classes of coherent sheaves on the fibres of $X/S$. Then $\cF$ is bounded if and only if, for every $i$, the induced family $\cF_i$ on the fibres of $X_i/S$ is bounded.

Returning to the proof of 3.6, we have reduced to the case where $X$ is projective and integral over $S$. Equip $X/S$ with a very ample sheaf $\cO_X(1)$. Let $\cL$ be a bounded family of classes of invertible sheaves on the fibres of $X/S$, and let $L$ be an invertible sheaf on $X_T$, with $T$ of finite type over $S$, bounding $\cL$. After cutting $T$ into pieces, we may assume $L$ flat over $T$, and after replacing $T$ by its connected components, we may assume $T$ connected. Then the polynomial in $q$ and $m$
\[
P(q,m)=\chi(L_t^{\otimes q}(m))
\]
is independent of $t\in T$ by EGA III, 7.9.4.

Let $\cL_n$ be the family of those invertible sheaves $M_t$ for which the class of $M_t^{\otimes n}$ lies in $\cL$. If
\[
\chi(M_t^{\otimes q}(m))=\sum_i a_i(q)\binom{m+i}{i},
\]
then $a_i(q)$ is a polynomial in $q$ of degree at most $r-i$. Since, as a polynomial in $q,m$, one has $\chi(M_t^{\otimes q}(m))=P(q,m)$, the polynomials $a_i(q)$ do not depend on the chosen $M_t$. Consequently the family $\cL_n$ has a single Hilbert polynomial $\chi(M_t(m))$, and is bounded by 2.11.

\paragraph{Theorem 3.8.}
Let $S$ be noetherian. Let $Y$ be projective over $S$, with relatively ample invertible sheaf $\cO_Y(1)$, and let $X$ be the zero-scheme of a section $\sigma$ of $\cO_Y(1)$. Let $f:X\hookrightarrow Y$ be the inclusion. Suppose that, for every $s\in S$ and every irreducible component $Y_s^j$ of $Y_s$, all irreducible components of
\[
X_s\cap Y_s^j=V(\sigma|Y_s^j)
\]
have dimension at least $2$; this is automatic if the irreducible components of the fibres of $Y$ have dimension at least $3$. Then
\[
f^*:\underline{\Pic}_{Y/S}\longto \underline{\Pic}_{X/S}
\]
is of finite type.

Moreover, if $S$ is integral, there is a non-empty open $U\subset S$ such that the restricted Picard functors over $U$ are representable and the induced morphism
\[
\underline{\Pic}_{Y/S}|_U\longto\underline{\Pic}_{X/S}|_U
\]
is quasi-affine, and hence of finite type.

For the first assertion, by noetherian induction as in the proof of 3.6 one may replace $S$ by any non-empty open. One reduces to the case where $S$ is integral and $Y$ is integral over $S$; when $Y$ is replaced by $Y'$, $X$ is replaced by $X\times_Y Y'$. One then reduces similarly to the case where $Y$ is normal over $S$, using the following lemma in place of XII, 1.3.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.9.}
Let $S$ be an integral noetherian scheme and let $X$ be an $S$-scheme, integral over $S$. Then there exist a non-empty open $U\subset S$, an integral scheme $S'$ finite and faithfully flat over $U$, an $S'$-scheme $X'$ normal and integral over $S'$, and a finite surjective $S'$-morphism
\[
X'\longto X_{S'}.
\]

Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $S$ and let $K$ be an algebraic closure of $k(\eta)$. The scheme $\Spec(K)$ is the limit of the filtering projective system of affine integral schemes $S_\alpha$ equipped with finite faithfully flat morphisms $S_\alpha\to U_\alpha$, where $U_\alpha$ ranges over non-empty open subsets of $S$. The normalization of $X_\eta$ therefore descends to an $S_\alpha$-scheme $X'$ for some $\alpha$, and the finite surjective morphism $X'_K\to X_\eta$ descends to a finite surjective $S_\alpha$-morphism $X'\to X_{S_\alpha}$. Since $X'_K$ is normal and integral, after replacing $S_\alpha$ by an open $S'$, the scheme $X'_{S'}$ is normal and integral over $S'$.

\paragraph{Remark 3.10.}
Under the notation and hypotheses of 3.9, suppose that there exists a resolving morphism $X'_K\to X_\eta$ in the sense of EGA IV, 7.9.1; this holds, for example, when $\dim(X_\eta)\leq 2$, by Abhyankar. Then the same argument gives a finite faithfully flat morphism $S'\to U$, a scheme $X'$ smooth over $S'$, and a proper surjective birational morphism $X'\to X_{S'}$.

In the proof of the first part of 3.8, we have reduced to the case where $S$ is integral and $Y$ is normal and integral over $S$, hence $Y$ is integral. If $\sigma=0$, then $X=Y$ and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, after shrinking $S$, we may assume that $\sigma_s\neq 0$ for all $s\in S$; then $\sigma_s$ is $\cO_{Y_s}$-regular and the fibre dimension $r$ of $Y_s$ is at least $3$.

Let $\cL$ be a bounded family of classes of invertible sheaves on the fibres of $X/S$. Let $M_s$ be an invertible sheaf on a geometric fibre $Y_s$ of $Y/S$, and write its Hilbert polynomial
\[
\chi(M_s(n))=\sum_i a_i\binom{n+i}{i}.
\]
The Hilbert polynomial of $M_s|_{X_s}$ is
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}a_{i+1}\binom{n+i}{i}.
\]
When the class of $M_s$ ranges over $(f^*)^{-1}(\cL)$, the three coefficients $a_{r-1},a_{r-2},a_{r-3}$ remain bounded, by 1.13. If $H=\cO_Y(m)$ is a very ample multiple of $\cO_Y(1)$, then in the Hilbert polynomials, relative to $H$, of the $M_s$ in $(f^*)^{-1}(\cL)$,
\[
\chi(M_s^{\otimes q}(n))=\sum_i a_i^{(m)}\binom{n+i}{i},
\]
the coefficients $a^{(m)}_{r-1},a^{(m)}_{r-2},a^{(m)}_{r-3}$ remain bounded by 2.10. Thus $\cL$ is bounded by 2.11, proving the first part of 3.8.

For the second part, the generic representability and separation of the Picard functors were established in XII, 1.2. By an argument parallel to that of XII, 2, using 3.9 and XII, 1.1, one reduces to the case where $Y$ is normal over $S$. For the morphism $\underline{\Pic}_{Y/S}\to \underline{\Pic}_{X/S}$ to be quasi-affine, it is enough that it be separated and quasi-finite; this can be checked on geometric fibres and follows from the next lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma 3.11.}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed. Let $Y$ be a normal projective $k$-scheme with ample sheaf $\cO_Y(1)$, and let $X$ be the zero-scheme of a section $\sigma$ of $\cO_Y(1)$. If $\dim Y\geq 3$, then $\Pic_Y\to \Pic_X$ is of finite type and has finite kernel. If $\dim Y\geq 2$, the same conclusion holds for
\[
\Pic^0_Y\longto \Pic^0_X.
\]
The finite kernel need not be reduced.

We may assume $\sigma\neq 0$; since $Y$ is integral, $\sigma$ is regular. The morphism considered is of finite type by the first part of 3.8, respectively by 4.7 (ii). Its kernel $N$ is therefore an algebraic group. It is contained in $\Pic^0_Y$, and the same kernel occurs in both cases. Since $\Pic^0_Y$ is proper, $Y$ being normal, and since a subgroup is necessarily closed, it is enough, in order to prove $N$ finite, to show that it contains no subgroup of type $\bmu_\ell=\ZZ/\ell\ZZ$ with $(\ell,\car(k))=1$.

Consider Kummer theory, namely the exact sequence of étale sheaves on $Y$ and on $X$
\[
0\longto \bmu_\ell\longto \Gm \xrightarrow{\ell}\Gm\longto 0,
\]
and the corresponding commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\HH^1(Y,\bmu_\ell)\arrow[r]\arrow[d] &
\HH^1(Y,\Gm)\arrow[r,"\ell"]\arrow[d] &
\HH^1(Y,\Gm)\arrow[d]\\
\HH^1(X,\bmu_\ell)\arrow[r] &
\HH^1(X,\Gm)\arrow[r,"\ell"'] &
\HH^1(X,\Gm).
\end{tikzcd}
\]
By Hilbert's Theorem 90, $\HH^1(Y,\Gm)=\Pic_Y(k)$ and similarly for $X$. Since $X$ is proper and integral, $\HH^0(X,\Gm)=k^*$, and the map $\HH^1(Y,\bmu_\ell)\to\HH^1(Y,\Gm)$ is injective.

It remains to see that
\[
\HH^1(Y,\ZZ/\ell\ZZ)\longto\HH^1(X,\ZZ/\ell\ZZ)
\]
is injective. This follows from the surjectivity of $\pi_1(X)\to\pi_1(Y)$, or equivalently, by SGA 1, V, 6.9, from the fact that every connected étale cover $\widetilde Y$ of $Y$ with group $\ZZ/\ell\ZZ$ has connected restriction
\[
\widetilde X=X\times_Y\widetilde Y,
\]
a well-known consequence of Bertini's theorem and Zariski's connectedness theorem.

\paragraph{Remark 3.12.}
With the hypotheses of 3.11:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The kernel $N$ is a finite unipotent group. Indeed, it contains no subgroup of multiplicative type $\Gm$ for any $n$; the proof is the same, using in place of Kummer theory the following fact due to Grothendieck: if $X$ is proper over a field $k$ and $\HH^0(X,\cO_X)=k$, then there is an isomorphism of $k$-group schemes
\[
\Hom_k(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\Pic^0_{X/k})\simeq \HH^1(X,\ZZ/n\ZZ).
\]
Thus, if $k$ is algebraically closed,
\[
\Hom_k(\ZZ/n\ZZ,\Pic^0_{Y/k})\simeq \HH^1(Y,\ZZ/n\ZZ).
\]
\item If $k$ has characteristic zero, then $N=0$, since every finite unipotent group is then zero. In this form one recovers, essentially, Kodaira's vanishing theorem in Mumford's formulation.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Theorem 3.13.}
Let $S$ be noetherian, and let $X$ be projective over $S$, with relatively ample invertible sheaf $H=\cO_X(1)$. Assume that the geometric fibres of $X/S$ are integral and of dimension $r$. For a family $\cL$ of classes of invertible sheaves on the fibres of $X/S$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item $\cL$ is bounded.
\item In the Hilbert polynomials
\[
\chi(L_s(n))=\sum_{i=0}^r a_i(s)\binom{n+i}{i},\qquad L_s\in\cL,
\]
the coefficient $a_{r-1}$ remains bounded and the coefficient $a_{r-2}$ remains bounded below.
\item As $L_s$ runs through $\cL$, the degree
\[
d(L_s)=\langle c_1(L_s)c_1(H_s)^{r-1}\rangle
\]
remains bounded, and the intersection number
\[
\langle c_1(L_s)^2c_1(H_s)^{r-2}\rangle
\]
remains bounded below.
\item There is an integer $N$ such that
\[
\cL^{\otimes N}<\cL<\cL^{\otimes -N}
\]
in the order relation defined by ampleness; that is, for every $L_s\in\cL$, the two sheaves
\[
L_s^{\otimes N}\otimes H_s^{-1},\qquad L_s^{\otimes -N}\otimes H_s
\]
are ample.
\end{enumerate}

If $\cL$ is bounded by an invertible sheaf over $X_T$, then EGA II, 4.6.12 gives (iv). If that sheaf is flat over $T$, the polynomial $\chi(L_t^{\otimes m}(n))$ in $m,n$ depends only on the connected component of $T$, by EGA III, 7.9.4. This gives (ii), and, using 2.1, (iii).

The family consisting of the classes of $H_K$ is bounded, as is the family consisting of $\cO_{X_K}$; hence these satisfy (iii) and (ii), respectively. If (iv) holds, then
\[
\langle N c_1(H_K)+c_1(L_K)\rangle c_1(H_K)^{r-2}>0,
\]
and so $d(L_K)>-N\,d(H_K)$; similarly $d(L_K)<N\,d(H_K)$. Also
\[
\langle (N c_1(H_K)+c_1(L_K))^2c_1(H_K)^{r-2}\rangle>0,
\]
which gives
\[
\langle c_1(L_K)^2c_1(H_K)^{r-2}\rangle
  > -2N\,d(L_K)-N^2d(H_K).
\]
Thus (iv) implies (iii).

It remains to prove that (ii), respectively (iii), implies boundedness. By 2.10 and 2.3 one may replace $H$ by a multiple; assume $H$ very ample. After cutting $S$ into pieces, one may suppose that there is a linear section $Y$ of $X$, integral over $S$, whose fibres have dimension $2$. By 3.8, one may replace $X,\cL$ by $Y,\cL|_Y$, using 1.7, respectively the projection formula, to see that (ii), respectively (iii), is preserved. Thus one reduces to $r=2$. If
\[
\delta=\sup\{d(L_K)\}>0,
\]
replace $\cL$ by the family of the classes of $L_K(-\delta)$, using 2.3 and 2.10; hence assume $\delta\leq 0$. If the Hilbert polynomials
\[
\sum_{i=0}^2 a_i\binom{n+i}{i}
\]
have $a_1$ bounded and $a_0$ bounded below, then by 2.3 and 2.7 all three coefficients are bounded; hence $\cL$ is bounded by 2.11. Thus (ii) implies (i). Finally (iii) implies (ii) by 2.3 and 2.8.

\subsection*{4. Finiteness theorems for $\Pic^\tau_{X/S}$}

\paragraph{4.1.}
Let $S$ be a scheme and let $G$ be a functor in commutative groups on $S$. We denote by $G^0$, respectively $G^\tau$, the subgroup functor whose $T$-points $f:T\to G$ are those $T$-points of $G$ such that for every algebraically closed field $K$ and every $K$-point $t$ of $T$, there exist a connected $K$-scheme $Z$, two $K$-points $z,z_0$ of $Z$, and a morphism $g:Z\to G$ with
\[
g(z)=f(t),\qquad g(z_0)=0
\]
in $G(K)$; respectively, there exists a non-zero integer $n$ such that $n f(t)\in G^0(K)$.

Formation of $G^0$ and $G^\tau$ plainly commutes with arbitrary base change. A morphism $G\to G'$ of group functors induces $G^0\to G'^0$, respectively $G^\tau\to G'^\tau$. If $S$ is a field and $G$ is represented by a locally finite type group scheme, then $G^0$ is represented by the connected component of the identity, an open and closed subgroup scheme of finite type, geometrically irreducible.

\paragraph{Lemma 4.2.}
Let $\Phi:G\to G'$ be a morphism of commutative group functors on a scheme $S$. Suppose that for every algebraically closed field $K$ and every morphism $\Spec(K)\to S$, the functors $G_K$ and $G'_K$ are represented by locally finite type group schemes, and that $\Phi_K$ is representable by a morphism of finite type, equivalently quasi-compact. Then
\[
\Phi(G^\tau)\subset G'^\tau.
\]

The inclusion $\Phi(G^0)\subset G'^0$ has already been noted. Moreover, for $g\in G(K)$, membership of $g$ in $G^\tau(K)$ is equivalent to quasi-compactness of the sequence $(ng)_{n\in\NN}$. Since $\Phi$ is quasi-compact, the same property for the sequence $\Phi(ng)=n\Phi(g)$ follows; this proves the assertion.

\paragraph{Lemma 4.3.}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be proper over a scheme $S$, and let $f:X\to Y$ be either a surjective morphism or the inclusion of the zero-scheme of a section of an ample sheaf $\cO_Y(1)$. Then
\[
(f^*)^{-1}(\Pic^\tau_{X/S})=\Pic^\tau_{Y/S}.
\]
This is a formal consequence of 4.2, XII, 1.2, 3.1, and 3.5, respectively 3.8.

\paragraph{4.4.}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $X$ be proper over $k$, and let $L$ be an invertible sheaf on $X$. We say that $L$ is \emph{algebraically equivalent to zero}, respectively \emph{$\tau$-equivalent to zero}, if there exist a connected finite type $k$-scheme $T$, an invertible sheaf $M$ on $X_T=X\times_kT$, and two $k$-points $t,t_0$ of $T$ such that
\[
L\simeq M_t,\qquad \cO_X\simeq M_{t_0},
\]
respectively if some non-zero tensor power $L^{\otimes n}$ is algebraically equivalent to zero. Equivalently, the $k$-point $\lambda$ of $\Pic_{X/k}$ corresponding to $L$ lies in $\Pic^0_{X/k}(k)$, respectively in $\Pic^\tau_{X/k}(k)$.

We also recall that $L$ is \emph{numerically equivalent to zero} if, for every closed integral curve $Y$ in $X$, the intersection number
\[
\langle c_1(L)\cdot Y\rangle
\]
is zero.

\paragraph{Proposition 4.5 (Functorial behaviour).}
Let $f:X'\to X$ be a morphism between proper $k$-schemes, and let $L$ be an invertible sheaf on $X$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item If $L$ is $\tau$-equivalent to zero, respectively numerically equivalent to zero, then $f^*L$ is so.
\item Conversely, if $f$ is surjective and $f^*L$ is $\tau$-equivalent to zero, respectively numerically equivalent to zero, then $L$ is so.
\end{enumerate}

For $\tau$-equivalence, (i) and (ii) follow immediately from 4.1 and 4.3. For numerical equivalence, let $Y'$ be a closed integral curve in $X'$, and let $Y=f(Y')$, with the induced reduced structure. If $\dim Y=1$, the projection formula gives
\[
\langle c_1(f^*L)\cdot Y'\rangle
   =[k(Y'):k(Y)]\,\langle c_1(L)\cdot Y\rangle.
\tag{4.5.1}
\]
If $\dim Y=0$, then $(f^*L)|_{Y'}\simeq\cO_{Y'}$. Hence numerical triviality of $L$ implies that of $f^*L$.

Conversely, when $f$ is surjective and $Y$ is a closed integral curve in $X$, choose a point $y'$ in the fibre over the generic point $y$ of $Y$, algebraic over $k(y)$, and let $Y'$ be the closure of $y'$. Then $f(Y')=Y$, and (4.5.1) shows that
\[
\langle c_1(f^*L)\cdot Y'\rangle=0
\]
implies $\langle c_1(L)\cdot Y\rangle=0$.

\paragraph{Theorem 4.6 (Numerical characterization of $\Pic^\tau_{X/k}$).}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $X$ be proper over $k$, and let $L$ be an invertible sheaf on $X$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item $L$ is $\tau$-equivalent to zero.
\item For every coherent sheaf $F$ on $X$, one has $\chi(F\otimes L)=\chi(F)$.
\item $L$ is numerically equivalent to zero.
\end{enumerate}
If $X$ is projective over $k$, with ample invertible sheaf $H=\cO_X(1)$, these are also equivalent to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*),resume]
\item For every integer $m$, positive or negative, $L^{\otimes m}\otimes H$ is ample.
\item If $X$ is irreducible, then for all integers $m,n$,
\[
\chi(L^{\otimes m}(n))=\chi(\cO_X(n)).
\]
\item If $X$ is irreducible of dimension $r\geq 2$, then
\[
\langle c_1(L)H^{r-1}\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle c_1(L)^2H^{r-2}\rangle=0.
\]
\end{enumerate}

To prove (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii), dévissage reduces, using 4.5 (i), to the case where $X$ is integral and $F=\cO_X$. It is enough to show that $\chi(L^{\otimes m})$ is constant. Replacing $L$ by a non-zero tensor power, one may suppose $L$ algebraically equivalent to zero. Then $L$ is deformed to $\cO_X$ by an algebraic family $M$ on $X\times T$, with $T$ connected of finite type; the polynomial $\chi(M_t)$ is independent of $t$ by EGA III, 7.9.5.

If $Y$ is a closed integral curve in $X$, apply (ii) to $F=\cO_Y$. Then
\[
\chi(\cO_Y)=\chi(L|_Y),
\]
and Riemann's theorem on curves gives
\[
\chi(L|_Y)=\langle c_1(L)\cdot Y\rangle+\chi(\cO_Y).
\]
Thus (ii) implies (iii).

Let $f:X'\to X$ be a Chow presentation, with $X'$ projective and $f$ surjective. If (iii) $\Rightarrow$ (i) is known for $X'$, it follows for $X$ by 4.5. We may therefore suppose $X$ projective with ample sheaf $H$. Passing to the disjoint union of the reduced irreducible components reduces, by 4.5 and EGA III, 2.6.2, to the case where $X$ is integral.

If $X$ is irreducible, then (ii) plainly implies (v), (iii) implies (vi), and by 2.1, (v) implies (vi); moreover (vi) is unchanged by replacing $X$ by $X_{\red}$. It remains to prove that (vi) implies (i) and (iv), and that (iv) implies (i), for $X$ integral. Under either (vi) or (iv), the family of classes
\[
\{L^{\otimes m}\}_{m\in\NN}
\]
satisfies 3.13 (iii), respectively 3.13 (iv), and is therefore bounded. Hence there is an invertible sheaf on some $X_T$, with $T$ of finite type over $k$, which bounds this family. Choose two distinct integers $p,q$ such that $L^{\otimes p}$ and $L^{\otimes q}$ correspond to the same connected component of $T$; then $L^{\otimes(p-q)}$ is algebraically equivalent to zero. This gives (i). Similarly, (vi) implies that for some $N$, and every $m$, the sheaf $L^{\otimes m}\otimes H^{\otimes N}$ is ample; hence $(L^{\otimes m}\otimes H)^{\otimes N}$ is ample for every $m$, proving (iv).

\paragraph{Theorem 4.7.}
Let $f:X\to S$ be proper and flat, with $S$ noetherian. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item The subfunctor $\Pic^\tau_{X/S}$ of $\Pic_{X/S}$ consisting of classes of invertible sheaves numerically equivalent to zero on the fibres is represented by an open subscheme of $\Pic_{X/S}$.
\item If, in addition, $f$ is Cohen--Macaulay and its geometric fibres are integral, then the subfunctor $\Pic^{\num}_{X/S}$ of $\Pic_{X/S}$ consisting of classes of invertible sheaves algebraically equivalent to zero on the fibres is also represented by a closed subscheme.
\item $\Pic^\tau_{X/S}$ is of finite type over $S$; equivalently, the family of classes of invertible sheaves $\tau$-equivalent to zero on the fibres of $X/S$ is bounded.
\end{enumerate}

For (i), respectively (ii), it is enough to treat a morphism $T\to \Pic_{X/S}$, where $T$ is an arbitrary $S$-scheme, and show that the inverse image of $\Pic^\tau_{X/S}$, respectively $\Pic^{\num}_{X/S}$, is represented by an open, respectively by an open and closed, subscheme of $T$. Taking $T=\Spec(B)$ over an affine open $\Spec(A)\subset S$, write $T$ as the limit of the filtering projective system of affine $S$-schemes
\[
T_i=\Spec(B_i),
\]
where $B_i$ runs through the finite type $A$-subalgebras of $B$. By 3.1, the morphism in question descends to some $T_i\to \Pic_{X/S}$. Thus one may assume $T$ of finite type over $S$.

The morphism $T\to \Pic_{X/S}$ corresponds to an f.p.p.f. morphism $R\to T$ and an invertible sheaf $L$ on $X_R$. By f.p.p.f. descent, $T$ may be replaced by $R$. After the base change $R\to S$, and using compatibility of formation of $\Pic^\tau_{X/S}$ with base change, (i) and (ii) reduce to the case of a section $S\to \Pic_{X/S}$ defined by an invertible sheaf $L$ on $X$.

Let $S_0$ be the set of $s\in S$ such that $L_s$ is $\tau$-equivalent to zero. One must prove that $S_0$ is open, and closed under the supplementary assumptions of (ii). Since $S$ is noetherian, this amounts to:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item every generalization of a point of $S_0$ remains in $S_0$;
\item every specialization of a point of $S_0$, sufficiently close to it, remains in $S_0$;
\item under the hypotheses of (ii), every specialization of a point of $S_0$ remains in $S_0$.
\end{enumerate}

Let $f:X'\to X$ be a Chow presentation: $X'$ is projective over $S$ and $f$ is surjective. If (a), (b), (c), and (iii) are known for $X'$, then they follow for $X$ by 4.3 and 3.5. Thus one may suppose $X$ projective over $S$.

Let $H$ be ample for $X/S$. If, for some $s\in S$, the sheaf $(L\otimes H)_s$ is ample, then $(L\otimes H)_t$ is ample for every generalization $t$ of $s$. Using the equivalence in 4.6 between (i) and (iv), this proves (a).

For (b), (c), and 4.7 (iii), one may assume $S$ integral, with generic point $s$ in (b) and (c). There exists an integral scheme $S'$ and an f.p.p.f. morphism $S'\to S$ such that every irreducible component $X_j$ of $X_{S'}$, with the induced reduced structure, is integral over $S'$. It is enough to prove (b) and (iii) after this base change: for (b) by descent, and for (iii) by noetherian induction and 3.4 (i). Considering the canonical surjective morphism
\[
\coprod X'_j\longto X_{S'},
\]
one reduces, by 4.3, 3.5, and 3.7, to the case where $S$ is integral with generic point $s$, and $X$ is projective and integral over $S$. Let $\cO_X(1)$ be very ample for $X/S$. Then for all $m,n$, the polynomials
\[
\chi(L^m(n)),\qquad \chi(\cO_X(n))
\]
are independent of $t\in S$. Thus (c) follows from the equivalence 4.6 (i) $\Leftrightarrow$ (v), and the same condition shows that the family of classes of invertible sheaves $\tau$-equivalent to zero on the fibres of $X/S$ has a single Hilbert polynomial. Hence (iii) follows from 2.11.

\paragraph{Remark 4.8.}
Let $X$ be proper and integral over a noetherian scheme $S$. Without assuming $X/S$ projective, it is not known whether $\Pic^\tau_{X/S}\hookrightarrow \Pic_{X/S}$ is still a closed immersion, except when $X$ is normal over $S$ and $\Pic_{X/S}$ is representable.

\subsection*{5. Finiteness theorems of Néron--Severi type}

\paragraph{Theorem 5.1.}
Let $X$ be proper over a noetherian scheme $S$. Then the Néron--Severi groups
\[
\NS(X_{\ov K})=
\Pic_{X_{\ov K}/\ov K}(\ov K)/\Pic^\tau_{X_{\ov K}/\ov K}(\ov K)
\]
of the geometric fibres $X_{\ov K}$ of $X/S$ are finitely generated, and their ranks and the orders of their torsion subgroups remain bounded.

By noetherian induction, it is enough to prove the theorem after replacing $S$ by an arbitrary non-empty open of $S_{\red}$. Thus one may suppose $\Pic^\tau_{X/S}$ represented by an $S$-scheme of finite type. Then the order of $\Tors(\NS(X_{\ov K}))$ is the number of connected components of $(\Pic^\tau_{X/S})_{\ov K}$, and this number is constant over a non-empty open of $S$.

It remains to bound the rank of $\NS(X_{\ov K})$. As in the proof of 4.7 (iii), one reduces to $S$ integral and $X$ projective and integral over $S$. Let $r$ be the common dimension of the fibres. For $r=0$, respectively $r=1$, the rank is plainly $0$, respectively $1$. If $r\geq 2$, one reduces to the case $r=2$ and $X$ smooth over $S$: after shrinking $S$, choose a linear section $Y$ of $X$ which is integral over $S$ and has fibres of dimension $2$, and replace $X$ by $Y$ using 4.3. Then, after a flat cover of a non-empty open of $S$, 4.3 and 3.10, using resolution of singularities for surfaces, allow one to replace $X$ by a smooth $S$-scheme.

Now the theorem follows from 4.6 (i) $\Rightarrow$ (iii), the finiteness and specialization theorem for étale cohomology groups, and the following proposition.

\paragraph{Proposition 5.2.}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $X$ be proper and smooth over $k$. Then the group $C^p(X)$ of algebraic cycles of codimension $p$, modulo numerical equivalence, is free of rank at most the Betti number $b^{2p}$.

Ignoring the action of roots of unity, étale cohomology
\[
H^i_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\QQ_\ell)=
\left[\varprojlim H^i_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\ZZ/\ell^n\ZZ)\right]\otimes_{\ZZ_\ell}\QQ_\ell
\qquad (\ell\neq\car(k))
\]
is a Weil cohomology: it satisfies Poincaré duality and the Künneth formula, and receives a non-zero functorial homomorphism from the ring of algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence,
\[
C^p(X)\longto H^{2p}_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\QQ_\ell(p)).
\]
Let $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in C^{r-p}(X)$, where $r=\dim X$, be classes whose images form a basis of the $\QQ_\ell$-vector subspace of
\[
H^{2(r-p)}_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\QQ_\ell(r-p))
\]
spanned by algebraic cycles. Consider
\[
\alpha:C^p(X)\longto \QQ_\ell^m,\qquad
\alpha(b)=\bigl(\langle b\cdot a_1\rangle,\ldots,\langle b\cdot a_m\rangle\bigr).
\]
Then $\alpha$ is injective on numerical classes, and the bound on the rank follows.

\paragraph{Remark 5.3.}
If $k$ is algebraically closed and $X$ is a proper smooth surface over $k$, the second Betti number can be written
\[
b^2(X)=c_2(X)-2+2b^1(X),
\]
where $b^1(X)=2\dim(\Pic^0_{X/k})$ and $c_2(X)$ is the second Chern class, equivalently the Euler--Poincaré characteristic or the self-intersection number of the diagonal in $X\times X$. Thus $b^2(X)$ has an interpretation independent of cohomology. In characteristic $>0$, the inequality
\[
\rank C^1(X)\leq b^2(X)
\]
in this form is due to Igusa, before étale cohomology, using deep results on the structure of the tame fundamental group of an algebraic curve minus finitely many points.

\paragraph{Theorem 5.4.}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $X$ be proper over $k$. Then there exist finitely many closed integral curves $Y_1,\ldots,Y_p$ in $X$ such that a subset $A$ of the scheme $\Pic^\tau_{X/k}$ is quasi-compact if and only if the intersection numbers
\[
\langle L,Y_i\rangle,\qquad L\in A,
\]
remain bounded. Moreover $p$ may be taken to be the rank of the Néron--Severi group
\[
\Pic_{X/k}(k)/\Pic^0_{X/k}(k).
\]

Since $\Pic^0_{X/k}$ is an open quasi-compact subgroup of $\Pic^\tau_{X/k}$, a subset $A$ is quasi-compact if and only if the corresponding set of points in
\[
\left(\Pic^\tau_{X/k}(k)/\Pic^0_{X/k}(k)\right)\otimes\QQ
\]
is finite. But $\tau$-equivalence is the same as numerical equivalence, by 4.6, so one may choose curves in $X$ whose numerical classes form a basis of the dual vector space.

\subsection*{6. Appendix: $(b)$-sheaves on $P=\bP^r_k$}

Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $P=\bP^r_k$. Let $\cO_P(1)$ be the canonical invertible sheaf on $P$, and set
\[
S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_r].
\]
Recall that a coherent sheaf $F$ on $P$ is called \emph{$(b)$-regular}, or simply $b$-regular, if
\[
H^i(P,F(b-i))=0\qquad (i>0).
\]

\paragraph{Theorem 6.2.}
Let $F$ be a non-zero coherent sheaf on $P$, $b$-regular. Let
\[
s\in H^0(P,F(b))
\]
be a section whose zero subscheme $Z(s)$ has codimension $>0$, i.e. contains no irreducible component of $\Supp(F)$. Then
\[
F'=\Ker\bigl(F\xrightarrow{s}F(b)\bigr)
\]
is $(b+1)$-regular.

The proof uses the exact sequence
\[
0\longto F'\longto F\xrightarrow{s}F(b)\longto G\longto 0,
\]
where $G$ has finite length, together with the vanishing implied by $b$-regularity. If $m=P_F(b)$, then
\[
h^0(G)=m,\qquad h^i(G)=0\quad (i>0).
\]
Since $s\neq 0$, the quotient $G$ is non-zero; in particular $F'$ is not contained in $F(-1)$, i.e. the section is not divisible by the equation of a hyperplane. The regularity assertion follows by the standard Castelnuovo--Mumford argument.

\paragraph{6.3.}
The converse of 6.2 is also useful. Suppose there is an exact sequence
\[
0\longto F'\longto F\xrightarrow{s}F(b)\longto G\longto 0
\]
with $F'$ $(b+1)$-regular and $G$ of finite length. Then $F$ is $b$-regular. In the case $q=1$, the support of the relevant module $N_*$ has dimension $0$, and one has
\[
h^1(N_*(b-1))=0,\qquad h^0(N_*(b-1))\leq h^0(N_*(b)).
\]
Moreover, if $G=0$ then
\[
h^0(F'(b))=h^0(F(b))-m.
\]

\paragraph{Corollary 6.8 (Grothendieck).}
Let $X$ be projective over a noetherian scheme $S$, let $\cO_X(1)$ be very ample for $X/S$, and let $\cF$ be a bounded family of classes of coherent sheaves on the fibres of $X/S$. Then there exists an integer $m$ such that, for every class $\xi\in\cF$ and every coherent sheaf $F$ representing $\xi$, the sheaf $F$ is $m$-regular on the corresponding fibre.

After replacing the base field by the algebraic closure of a purely transcendental extension, one may assume the ground field infinite. Then the assertion follows from the previous regularity estimates, from 6.10 and 1.8 (i), and from 6.2 applied to $F(n)$ for $n\geq m$.

\paragraph{Corollary 6.14 (L. Hermann).}
For every $N\geq 0$ there is a polynomial $R_N(x)$ such that for every algebraically closed field $k$, every projective $k$-scheme $X$ with very ample sheaf $\cO_X(1)$, and every coherent sheaf $F$ on $X$ satisfying $\chi(F(n))=N$ for all $n$, one has
\[
h^0(F)\leq R_N(\deg X).
\]

\subsection*{7. Appendix: The Hodge index theorem}

We recover and complement here some earlier results by a direct argument.

\paragraph{Theorem 7.1.}
Let $X$ be an irreducible surface, proper over an algebraically closed field. Suppose that there exists an invertible sheaf $H$ on $X$ such that
\[
\langle c_1(H)^2\rangle>0
\]
(for example $H$ ample). Then an invertible sheaf $L$ on $X$ satisfying
\[
\langle c_1(L),c_1(H)\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle c_1(L)^2\rangle\geq 0
\]
is numerically equivalent to zero. Consequently, by 4.6, the intersection form on
\[
\NS(X)\otimes\QQ=\bigl[\Pic(X)/\Pic^0(X)\bigr]\otimes\QQ
\]
is non-degenerate of type $(1,\rho-1)$.

First suppose that $X$ is reduced, hence integral, that $H$ is ample, that
\[
\langle c_1(L),c_1(H)\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle c_1(L)^2\rangle>0.
\]
Arguing by contradiction, choose $m>0$ such that $H_1=L\otimes H^{\otimes m}$ is ample, and choose $n$ so that, with
\[
N=L^{\otimes n}\otimes H^{-1},
\]
one has
\[
\langle c_1(N),c_1(H_1)\rangle
 =n\langle c_1(L)^2\rangle-m\langle c_1(H)^2\rangle>0.
\]
At the same time
\[
\langle c_1(N),c_1(H)\rangle=-\langle c_1(H)^2\rangle<0,\qquad
\langle c_1(N)^2\rangle=n^2\langle c_1(L)^2\rangle+\langle c_1(H)^2\rangle>0,
\]
contradicting the next lemma.

\paragraph{Lemma 7.1.2.}
Let $X$ be an integral surface, projective over an algebraically closed field. For an invertible sheaf $N$ on $X$ with $\langle c_1(N)^2\rangle>0$, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item For every ample sheaf $H$, one has $\langle c_1(N),c_1(H)\rangle>0$.
\item[\textup{i'}] There exists an ample sheaf $H$ with $\langle c_1(N),c_1(H)\rangle>0$.
\item There exists an integer $n>0$ such that $H^0(X,N^{\otimes n})\neq 0$.
\end{enumerate}

If (ii) holds, there is an effective divisor $D$ such that $N\simeq\cO_X(D)$, and $\langle c_1(N)^2\rangle>0$ forces $D>0$, giving (i). Conversely, to see that (i') implies (ii), assume $H=\cO_X(1)$ very ample. There is a closed integral locally principal curve $Y$ with $H=\cO_X(Y)$. Riemann's theorem gives vanishing of the relevant cohomology for $N^{\otimes n}$ for $n$ large. The exact sequence
\[
H^0(N^{\otimes n}(m))\to H^0(N^{\otimes n}(m)|_Y)\to
H^1(N^{\otimes n}(m-1))\to H^1(N^{\otimes n}(m))\to 0
\]
then implies $H^1(N^{\otimes n})=0$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Hence
\[
h^0(N^{\otimes n})=\chi(N^{\otimes n})
  =\frac12\langle c_1(N)^2\rangle n^2+\cdots>0
\]
for $n\gg0$.

Now suppose in 7.1 that there is an invertible sheaf $M$ on $X$ with
\[
\langle c_1(L),c_1(M)\rangle\neq 0.
\]
Arguing by contradiction, replace $X$ by the underlying reduced scheme of a Chow presentation; this is allowed by the projection formula. Let $\xi$ be ample on $X$. Choose $m>0$ such that $H_1=\xi^{\otimes m}$ is ample and
\[
\langle c_1(L),c_1(H_1)\rangle\neq 0.
\]
Then choose integers $p,q$ with $q\neq0$ and set
\[
L_1=L^{\otimes p}\otimes H_1^{\otimes q}
\]
so that
\[
\langle c_1(L_1),c_1(H)\rangle>0,\qquad
\langle c_1(L_1)^2\rangle>0.
\]
This reduces to the preceding case.

Finally, in the general case, assume again by contradiction that $L$ is not numerically equivalent to zero. Replace $X$ by the normalization $\widetilde X$ of $X_{\red}$; then $\widetilde X$ has only finitely many singular points, and the replacement is justified by the projection formula and 4.5 (ii). There is a closed integral curve $Y$ on $\widetilde X$ with
\[
\langle c_1(L),Y\rangle\neq 0.
\]
The curve $Y$ is locally principal outside a finite set $Z$. Let $f:X'\to \widetilde X$ be the blow-up of the ideal $I$ defining the defect. The ideal
\[
M=I\cO_{X'}
\]
is invertible and defines a subscheme of $X'$ which is the union of two subschemes $Y'$ and $Y''$ with $f(Y')=Y$ and $f(Y'')\subset Z$. By the projection formula,
\[
\langle c_1(f^*L),c_1(M)\rangle
  =-\langle c_1(f^*L),Y'\rangle-\langle c_1(f^*L),Y''\rangle
  =-\langle c_1(L),Y\rangle\neq 0.
\]
Replacing $X,L,H$ by $X',f^*L,f^*H$ brings us back to the preceding case and completes the proof of 7.1.

\paragraph{Remark 7.2.}
It is not known whether every irreducible proper surface over an algebraically closed field which carries an invertible sheaf $H$ with $\langle c_1(H)^2\rangle>0$ must be projective. Nor is it known whether, for every irreducible proper surface, the intersection form on $\NS(X)\otimes\QQ$ is non-degenerate. It follows from 7.1 that, when it is degenerate, it must be negative, not indefinite.

\paragraph{Corollary 7.3.}
Under the hypotheses of 7.1, let $M$ be an invertible sheaf on $X$ not numerically equivalent to zero, and set
\[
a=\langle c_1(M),c_1(H)\rangle,\qquad h=\langle c_1(H)^2\rangle.
\]
Then
\[
\langle c_1(M)^2\rangle < a^2/h.
\]
Indeed, take $L=M^{\otimes h}\otimes H^{-a}$. Since $\langle c_1(L),c_1(H)\rangle=0$, Theorem 7.1 gives $\langle c_1(L)^2\rangle<0$.

\paragraph{Corollary 7.4.}
Let $X$ be an irreducible projective scheme of dimension $r\geq2$ over an algebraically closed field, and let $H$ be ample on $X$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\roman*)]
\item An invertible sheaf $L$ on $X$ is numerically equivalent to zero if and only if
\[
\langle c_1(L)c_1(H)^{r-1}\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle c_1(L)^2c_1(H)^{r-2}\rangle\geq0.
\]
\item For every invertible sheaf $M$ on $X$ not numerically equivalent to zero,
\[
\langle c_1(M)^2c_1(H)^{r-2}\rangle\,
\langle c_1(H)^r\rangle
<
\langle c_1(M)c_1(H)^{r-1}\rangle^2.
\]
\end{enumerate}

Assertion (ii) follows immediately from (i) by the argument of 7.3. To prove (i), let $Y$ be a closed integral curve in $X$; it is enough to show $\langle c_1(L),Y\rangle=0$. Embed $X$ in projective space by $H^{\otimes m}$, $m\gg0$. Let $I=\bigoplus I_\nu$ be the homogeneous ideal of $Y$, and choose $\nu_0$ so that $I_\nu$ generates $I$ for $\nu\geq\nu_0$. Hypersurfaces $Z$ of degree $\nu$ containing $Y$ have no common point outside $Y$. By induction on $r$ and Bertini's theorem, choose $r-2$ such hypersurfaces $Z_1,\ldots,Z_{r-2}$ so that
\[
X'=Z_1\cap\cdots\cap Z_{r-2}\cap X
\]
is irreducible. Since $\langle c_1(L),Y\rangle=\langle c_1(L|_{X'}),Y\rangle$, it remains to prove that $L|_{X'}$ is numerically equivalent to zero on the surface $X'$. This follows from 7.1 by the projection formula.

\paragraph{Corollary 7.5.}
Let $k$ be algebraically closed. Let $Y$ be projective over $k$, with ample sheaf $H$, and let $X$ be the zero-scheme of a section of $H$. Suppose that, for every irreducible component $Y^*$ of $Y$, all irreducible components of
\[
X\cap Y^*=V(\omega|_{Y^*})
\]
have dimension $>2$. Then
\[
\NS(Y)\longto \NS(X)
\]
is injective. It is enough to assume $Y$ integral, and the assertion follows from 7.4 (i) and the projection formula.

\section*{Exposé XIV -- Open problems in intersection theory}
\begin{center}
\large\textbf{OPEN PROBLEMS IN INTERSECTION THEORY}\\[0.25em]
\textit{by A. Grothendieck}
\end{center}

In this exposé we review several problems connected with questions encountered in this seminar and deserving further study. We leave aside the problems connected with the local intersection theory of Serre, which were not used in the seminar, and refer to Serre's book for the two fundamental conjectures still open in that direction. We also omit the very important problems, especially for arithmetic applications, concerning the structure of groups of cycle classes on projective smooth algebraic schemes; for these questions see S. Kleiman, \emph{Algebraic cycles and the Weil conjectures}, in \emph{Dix exposés sur la cohomologie des schémas}. In no. 4 below we also give complements to the seminar, mainly concerning the Chow ring, which had not found a place in the preceding exposés.

\subsection*{1. $\lambda$-operations in the derived category $D(X)$}

Let $X$ be a locally ringed topos, and let $K^\bullet(X)$ be the ring of classes of perfect complexes on $X$ (Exposé IV). One would like to define on $K^\bullet(X)$ a $\lambda$-ring structure, functorial in $X$, such that the natural homomorphism from the ring of classes of locally free modules on $X$, endowed with the $\lambda$-structure of Exposé V, to $K^\bullet(X)$ is a $\lambda$-homomorphism. For this it would be necessary to define functors, generally non-additive, of exterior powers
\[
\Lambda^i:D(X)\longto D(X),
\]
sending pseudo-coherent, respectively perfect, complexes to pseudo-coherent, respectively perfect, complexes, and having the formal properties with respect to exact triangles needed to define
\[
\lambda^i(\cl(L_\bullet))=\cl(\Lambda^i(L_\bullet)).
\tag{1.1}
\]

When $X$ has characteristic zero, i.e. when its structure sheaf is a sheaf of $\QQ$-algebras, the problem has a simple solution:
\[
\Lambda^i(L_\bullet)=\bigl(L_\bullet^{\otimes i}\bigr)^{\mathrm{alt}}.
\tag{1.2}
\]
Here the $i$th tensor power is taken in the derived category and regarded as an object of $D(\cO_X[\mathfrak S_i])$, and ``alt'' denotes passage to the alternating part for the action of the symmetric group. This is meaningful because of the characteristic-zero hypothesis. A formula of this type was already used in RRR, in the first proof of the Riemann--Roch theorem.

Without a characteristic hypothesis, if $L_\bullet$ is zero in positive degrees, then by Dold--Puppe it is canonically homotopic to the complex associated to a semi-simplicial module $L'_\bullet$ canonically associated with it. For flat $L_\bullet$, say, a natural candidate for $\Lambda^i(L_\bullet)$ is the complex associated with the semi-simplicial module $\Lambda^iL'_\bullet$. To extend this to complexes not assumed zero in positive degrees, one would have to study the behaviour of this operation under translation $L_\bullet\mapsto L_\bullet[1]$, or suspension. Unfortunately there seems to be no isomorphism
\[
\Lambda^i(L_\bullet[1])\simeq \Lambda^i(L_\bullet)[i]
\]
that would make the desired extension formal.

\subsection*{2. The Riemann--Roch formula without projectivity assumptions}

\paragraph{2.1.}
Once a natural $\lambda$-structure on $K^\bullet(X)$ is available, the Riemann--Roch theorem in the form considered in Exposé VIII can be formulated for any proper complete-intersection morphism
\[
f:X\to Y.
\]
More generally, it should apply to a relative scheme $X$ over a locally ringed topos $Y$, subject to the corresponding relative conditions. The Todd class of $f$ is defined from the class in $K^\bullet(X)$ of the relative cotangent complex $\mathbb L_\bullet$ of $f$, constructed in Exposé 0, 4.4; this complex is perfect by the local hypothesis on $f$. A proof of Riemann--Roch in this generality will probably require essentially new ideas. These same ideas may also give the key to the analogous formula in the complex analytic, or rigid analytic, case. In complex analytic geometry, only the case of a locally free sheaf $F$ on a compact nonsingular variety $X$, over a point $Y$, is presently known, by Atiyah--Singer's index theorem; the case of a merely coherent $F$ still seems beyond the known methods.

\paragraph{2.2.}
As another essential ingredient in Riemann--Roch, recall the finiteness problem raised in Exposé III: if $f:X\to Y$ is proper and $L_\bullet$ is a complex on $X$ pseudo-coherent relative to $Y$, is $\RR f_*(L_\bullet)$ pseudo-coherent? A general proof should probably use algebraic ideas also capable of proving Grauert's finiteness theorem under hypotheses substantially more general than the classical analytic ones, with base spaces or topoi topologically ringed in $\CC$-algebras: locally compact spaces with the sheaf of continuous complex-valued functions, $C^\infty$ manifolds with the sheaf of complex $C^\infty$ functions, real analytic spaces, and so on.

\paragraph{2.3.}
Finally, in these questions the properness assumption on $f$ should be replaced by the hypothesis that the cohomology sheaves $H^i(L_\bullet)$ of the complexes considered on $X$ have support proper over $Y$.

\subsection*{3. A ``denominator-free'' Riemann--Roch formula for an immersion}

\paragraph{3.1.}
Let $f:Y\to X$ be a regular closed immersion, with conormal sheaf $N$. For simplicity suppose that $X$ admits an ample invertible sheaf. The Riemann--Roch formula for $y\in K^\bullet(Y)$,
\[
\ch(f_*(y))=f_*\bigl(\ch(y)\Td(-N)\bigr),
\]
together with the general formula
\[
f_*(y)\,\xi=f_*(y\,f^*\xi)\qquad(\xi\in K^\bullet(X)),
\]
allows one to express the Chern classes of $f_*(y)$ in $\Gr^i(X)$ in the form
\[
c_i(f_*(y))=P_i\bigl(c_j(y),c_k(N)\bigr),
\tag{3.1}
\]
where $P_i$ is a universal polynomial, a priori with rational coefficients. In fact, RRR shows that these polynomials have integral coefficients. This raises the question of the validity of a denominator-free Riemann--Roch formula.

Such a formula cannot in general be sought in $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ itself, since $f_!:K^\bullet(Y)\to K^\bullet(X)$ is not compatible with the $\gamma$-filtrations, shifted by $d$, except up to torsion. Thus there is generally no homomorphism
\[
f_*:\Gr^\bullet(Y)\to\Gr^{\bullet+d}(X)
\]
realizing the rational map $f_!$ and making (3.1) a formula in $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ rather than merely in $\Gr^\bullet(X)_\QQ$.

If $X$ is regular, so that $K^\bullet(X)\simeq K_\bullet(X)$, one may endow $K^\bullet(X)$ with the topological filtration by codimension of supports. It is plausible that this filtration is compatible with the ring structure; this can be proved at least when $X$ is smooth over a field, by using the Chow ring. Then the associated graded
\[
\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)
\]
is a graded ring, and, if $Y$ is regular, there is a natural homomorphism of graded groups
\[
f_*:\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(Y)\longto \Gr^{\bullet+d}_{\topf}(X).
\]
Since the $\gamma$-filtration is finer than the topological filtration, there is a canonical homomorphism of graded groups, indeed of graded rings,
\[
\Gr^\bullet(X)\longto \Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X),
\]
which is an isomorphism modulo torsion. This gives a theory of Chern classes with values in $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$, and hence a meaning to (3.1) as a formula in $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$. In this form the formula is proved in RRR when $X$ has characteristic zero, at least when $X$ is smooth over a field. The characteristic-zero hypothesis is used essentially in order to have formulas of the type (1.1), (1.2); essentially the same proof should work without a characteristic hypothesis once the question raised in no. 1 is answered positively.

\paragraph{3.2.}
When $X$ is smooth over a field, there is also the Chow ring $A(X)$ and a theory of Chern classes with values in $A(X)$, finer than the theory with values in $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$. The latter Chern classes are images of the Chow Chern classes under the canonical surjection
\[
\phi:A(X)\longto \Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X).
\]
This map is an isomorphism modulo torsion, but not an isomorphism in general. If $X$ and $Y$ are smooth, the inclusion $f:Y\hookrightarrow X$ still defines a degree $d$ homomorphism of graded groups
\[
f_*:A^\bullet(Y)\longto A^{\bullet+d}(X),
\]
and (3.1) can be interpreted as a formula in $A(X)$. The formula is trivial for $i<d$ and interesting only for $i>d$. Even for $i=d=2$, neither this formula nor the weaker one obtained by replacing rational equivalence by algebraic equivalence is presently proved. Unlike the formula in $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$, there is no compelling reason to expect the denominator-free formula to hold in $A(X)$ itself. One practical way to test it, given our ignorance about the groups $A^i(X)$ for $i>2$, is to push the two sides of (3.1) forward to groups $A^2(Z)=\Pic(Z)$ for suitable proper morphisms $g:X\to Z$.

\paragraph{3.3.}
One may also interpret (3.1) cohomologically whenever Gysin homomorphisms
\[
f_*:H^{\bullet-2d}(Y,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet-d))
   \longto H^\bullet(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))
\]
are available. This is the case at least when both $X$ and $Y$ are smooth over a scheme $S$, by relative cohomological purity, and conjecturally after absolute purity in the expected generality. When $X$ and $Y$ are smooth over $\CC$, one can also use the integral Chern class on the locally compact space of complex points. In that case, Atiyah and Hirzebruch proved the formula for analytic embeddings, using naive $K$-theory. By Lefschetz's principle and SGA 4, the $\ell$-adic formula follows for $X$ and $Y$ smooth over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Its validity is therefore very plausible, but it is not currently proved even over a non-algebraically-closed field of characteristic zero.

\paragraph{3.4.}
One may likewise use De Rham or Hodge cohomological Chern classes. The problem of proving (3.1) in this setting only arises outside characteristic zero, since the formula is known in any case modulo torsion. For smooth quasi-projective schemes over a field, the De Rham or Hodge version would follow from the formula for cycle classes modulo algebraic equivalence, and so it gives another accessible way of testing the cycle-class version.

\paragraph{3.5.}
The proof of the transcendental cohomological version of (3.1) is essentially transcendental and uses tubular neighbourhoods of $Y$ in $X$. This suggests that a proof of a formula of type (3.1) may be tied to a systematic use of $K$-groups and cohomology groups with support in $Y$, a technical means of localizing around $Y$.

\subsection*{4. Relations between $K^\bullet(X)$ and the Chow ring $A(X)$}

\paragraph{4.1.}
For simplicity, suppose that $X$ is smooth and quasi-projective over a field $k$. Then one has the Chow ring $A(X)$ of cycle classes modulo rational equivalence. There are also the graded rings
\[
\Gr^\bullet(X)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X),
\]
the first associated with the $\gamma$-filtration on $K^\bullet(X)$, the second with the filtration on $K^\bullet(X)=K_\bullet(X)$ by codimension of supports. There are Chern-class theories with values in each of these rings, and also an $\ell$-adic cohomological theory
\[
c_i(E)\in H^{2i}(X,\ZZ_\ell(i)),
\]
for $\ell\neq\car(k)$.

These theories are related by the canonical homomorphisms
\[
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large,row sep=large]
& A^\bullet(X)\arrow[dd,"\phi"]\arrow[rdd,"\psi"] &\\
\Gr^\bullet(X)\arrow[rd,"j"]\arrow[rrd,"\chi"'] &&\\
& \Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X) & H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet)).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Here
\[
H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))=\bigoplus_i H^{2i}(X,\ZZ_\ell(i)).
\]
The map $j$ comes from the fact that the $\gamma$-filtration is finer than the topological filtration, and is an isomorphism modulo torsion. The map $\phi$ sends the class of a codimension $i$ closed subscheme $Y\subset X$ to the class of $\cO_Y$ in
\[
\Fil^i_{\topf}K_\bullet(X)/\Fil^{i+1}_{\topf}K_\bullet(X).
\]
It is surjective, and, as in 4.2, is an isomorphism modulo torsion. Hence, modulo torsion, the three rings
\[
A(X),\qquad \Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X),\qquad \Gr^\bullet(X)
\]
are canonically isomorphic, and Riemann--Roch formulas expressed in any one of them after tensoring with $\QQ$ are equivalent. The ring $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ has the advantage of being defined for every locally ringed topos, hence for every scheme, without regularity or ampleness assumptions, and of acquiring covariance with respect to proper morphisms. Its disadvantage is that this covariance only appears after tensoring with $\QQ$.

The Chow ring has another advantage: one has a homomorphism
\[
A(X)\to H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))
\]
which interprets cohomological Chern classes as images of Chow-valued Chern classes. In general this homomorphism does not factor through $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$, and it is at least doubtful that there is in general a homomorphism
\[
\Gr^\bullet(X)\to H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))
\]
carrying Chern classes to Chern classes. All these difficulties disappear after tensoring with $\QQ$:
\[
\Gr^\bullet(X)_\QQ\simeq
\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)_\QQ\simeq
A^\bullet(X)_\QQ
\longto H^{2\bullet}(X,\QQ_\ell(\bullet)).
\tag{4.2}
\]

\paragraph{4.2.}
To prove that the surjection $\phi$ of (4.1) is an isomorphism modulo torsion, one uses the Chern homomorphism
\[
K_\bullet(X)\longto \Ch(A(X)),
\]
where the target is the Chern ring of $A(X)$. This is compatible with the topological filtration of $K^\bullet(X)$ and the natural filtration of $\Ch(A(X))$, hence gives a homomorphism of graded groups
\[
\psi:\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)\longto A(X).
\tag{4.3}
\]
One proves
\[
\psi\circ\phi(x)=(-1)^{i-1}(i-1)!\,x
\quad \bmod\ \text{torsion},
\qquad x\in A^i(X),
\tag{4.4}
\]
and similarly
\[
\phi\circ\psi(x)=(-1)^{i-1}(i-1)!\,x
\quad \bmod\ \text{torsion},
\qquad x\in \Gr^i_{\topf}(X).
\]
The first formula implies the second, since $\phi$ is surjective. Thus $\phi$ and $\psi$ are isomorphisms modulo torsion, inverse to each other up to the factor $(-1)^{i-1}(i-1)!$ in degree $i$.

To prove (4.4), reduce to $x$ represented by an irreducible closed subscheme $Y$ of codimension $i$. The formula is equivalent to
\[
\cl(\cO_Y)=(-1)^{i-1}(i-1)!\,\cl(Y)
\quad\bmod\ \text{torsion}.
\tag{4.5}
\]
Removing a closed subset of codimension $>i$, one reduces to the case where $Y$ is regular, hence smooth over $k$ if $k$ is perfect. In that case (4.5) is precisely the Riemann--Roch formula for the inclusion $Y\hookrightarrow X$ and the element $1\in K^\bullet(Y)$, written with Chern classes in $A(X)$. Borel--Serre prove the needed formula in the circumstances considered here. This proves, at least for $k$ perfect, that $\phi$ is an isomorphism modulo torsion. The general case reduces to the algebraically closed one using the trace map for a finite extension $k'/k$.

\paragraph{4.3.}
It is plausible that (4.4), equivalently (4.5), holds exactly and not merely modulo torsion. This is a special case of the denominator-free Riemann--Roch formula in $A(X)$ for an immersion. In characteristic zero, the denominator-free formula is known at least in $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$, which implies that the two sides of (4.5) have the same image in $\Gr^i_{\topf}(X)$.

There is a simple support argument for (4.5) in $A(X)$ itself. Since the restriction of the left side to $X-Y$ is zero, and $Y$ is irreducible, the left side must be an integral multiple of $\cl(Y)$:
\[
\cl(\cO_Y)=a(Y)\cl(Y).
\]
This proves the exact formula whenever $\cl(Y)$ is not torsion. For example, if $X$ is projective over $k$ and $\cl(Y)\cdot \xi^{r-i}>0$ for a polarization $\xi$, the formula follows. Thus the formula holds whenever $X$ is an open subscheme of a smooth projective scheme over $k$, as happens whenever an adequate resolution of singularities theorem is available; in particular it holds in characteristic zero by Hironaka.

If exact (4.5) holds, then the kernel of
\[
\phi:A^i(X)\to \Gr^i_{\topf}(X)
\]
is killed by $(i-1)!$, and in particular $\phi:A^2(X)\to \Gr^2_{\topf}(X)$ is an isomorphism. Apart from $\phi^1$, which is already known to be an isomorphism, this does not seem to have been proved in general, even when $\dim X=3$. The earlier assertion of the validity of (4.4) and (4.5) in the cited notes was therefore premature.

\paragraph{4.4.}
The main present advantage of $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$, or $\Gr^\bullet(X)$, over the Chow ring $A(X)$ is that it is better suited to explicit computations of certain intersection formulas in cases of excess intersection. The moving-lemma definition of intersection classes in $A(X)$ is poorly adapted to computation. A striking example is the fundamental formula, for a closed immersion $f:Y\to X$ of smooth quasi-projective schemes over $k$,
\[
f_*(y)=y\cdot c_d(N),
\tag{4.6}
\]
valid in $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$, where $y\in \Gr^\bullet(Y)$ and $N$ is the conormal sheaf of $Y$ in $X$, assumed everywhere of rank $d$. In Exposé VII this formula follows from a simple computation of Tor sheaves. By 4.2, it implies the same formula in $A(Y)$ only modulo torsion. It is remarkable that (4.6) in the Chow ring itself, and even the particular self-intersection formula
\[
f_*(1)=c_d(N),
\tag{4.7}
\]
has not at this point been proved in general, even over the complex numbers. The same problem appears in the computation of the Chow ring of the blow-up $X'$ of $X$ along $Y$, where the key formula is
\[
c_i(\xi)=j_*\bigl(\pi^*c_{i-d}(F)\bigr).
\tag{4.8}
\]
It is known in $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X')$ by a Tor computation, but its validity in $A(X')$ remains to be established. Even the $\ell$-adic analogues of (4.6), (4.7), (4.8) are not presently proved in full generality; when $y$ is the cohomology class of an algebraic cycle, the formulas are at least true modulo torsion.

\paragraph{4.5.}
We next give examples where, in diagram (4.1), either $j$ or $\phi$ is not an isomorphism. The image of
\[
j:\Gr^\bullet(X)\to\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)
\]
is the subring of $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$ generated by Chern classes of locally free sheaves. If $K^\bullet(X)$ is generated as a $\ZZ$-algebra by classes of invertible sheaves, this is the subring generated by $\Gr^1_{\topf}(X)$.

Take $X$ to be a flag variety
\[
X=G/B,
\]
where $G$ is a simply connected semisimple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field $k$, and $B$ is a Borel subgroup. Over $k=\CC$, the Bruhat cell decomposition implies that the canonical map
\[
K^\bullet(X)\longto K^\bullet(X(\CC))
\]
is an isomorphism, and the same is true for the corresponding Chow/cohomology comparison. Hodgkin proved that $K^\bullet(X(\CC))$ is generated by classes of line bundles; hence the same is true for $K^\bullet(X)$, and by specialization also over any algebraically closed field. The cell decomposition also implies that $A(X)$ is torsion-free, hence $\phi:A(X)\to\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$ is an isomorphism. Therefore $j$ is an isomorphism if and only if $A(X)$ is generated by its degree-one elements, or, over $\CC$, if and only if the integral cohomology of $X(\CC)$ is generated in degree $2$, equivalently if $BG$ is torsion-free. This occurs exactly when $G$ is a product of groups $SL(n)$ and $Sp(m)$. Thus $G=SO(n)$ with $n\geq7$, or a simple exceptional group, gives examples where $j$ is not an isomorphism.

\paragraph{4.6.}
In this example the good ring is plainly the torsion-free ring
\[
A(X)=\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X),
\]
with basis given by Bruhat cells and, over $\CC$, identified with integral cohomology. By contrast, $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ has torsion of a rather pathological kind. This confirms the view that $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ is mainly useful modulo torsion, i.e. after tensoring with $\QQ$.

The same example shows that only after tensoring with $\QQ$ does $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ have a covariant character for proper morphisms, even for closed immersions of smooth quasi-projective schemes. If $j$ is not an isomorphism, there is an integral closed subscheme $Y\subset X$ such that
\[
c_i^\gamma(Y)\in K^\bullet(X)
\]
does not lie in $\Fil^i_{\topf}(X)$, where $i=\codim(Y,X)$. Choosing $Y$ of minimal dimension and removing its singular locus, one obtains a closed immersion
\[
f:Y'\hookrightarrow X'
\]
of codimension $i$ between smooth quasi-projective schemes such that
\[
f_*:K^\bullet(Y')\to K^\bullet(X')
\]
is not compatible with the $\gamma$-filtrations shifted by $i$, since $f_*(1)$ is not in $\Fil^i_{\topf}(X')$.

\paragraph{4.7.}
With the notation of 4.5, Exposé IX shows that Hodgkin's theorem is equivalent to
\[
K^\bullet(G)=\ZZ.
\]
Thus $\Gr^\bullet(G)=\ZZ$. The map $K^\bullet(G/B)\to K^\bullet(G)$ is a priori surjective, with kernel generated by the $c_1(L)-1$, where $L$ runs through the line bundles on $G/B$ associated with characters $B\to \GG_m$. Similarly,
\[
A(G/B)\to A(G)
\]
is surjective with kernel generated by $A^1(G/B)$. It follows that $A(G)=\ZZ$ if and only if $G$ is torsion-free, i.e. a product of groups $SL(n)$ and $Sp(m)$. Thus, when $G$ has torsion, the underlying scheme of $G$ gives an example where
\[
\phi:A(G)\to\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(G)
\]
is not bijective.

\paragraph{4.8.}
Similarly, one gets examples where
\[
\psi:A(X)\to H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))
\tag{4.9}
\]
does not factor through $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$. It is enough, after specialization, to consider $k=\CC$, and hence the homomorphism
\[
A(G)\to H^{2\bullet}(G(\CC),\ZZ),
\tag{4.10}
\]
induced from
\[
A(G/B)=H^{2\bullet}(G/B(\CC),\ZZ)\to H^{2\bullet}(G(\CC),\ZZ).
\]
It is tempting to conjecture that (4.10) is injective. If so, whenever $G$ has torsion the map (4.10) would not factor through $\phi$, and the same would hold for (4.9) for a suitable $\ell$. At least for the group $G_2$ over $\CC$, the map (4.10) is non-zero in positive degree: there is an element of $A^3(G)$ whose image in $H^6(G(\CC),\ZZ)$ has order $2$. This shows that (4.9) does not factor through $\phi$ for $\ell=2$. The fact about $G_2$ follows from the corresponding statement in the topology of compact Lie groups, communicated by J.-P. Serre.

\subsection*{5. Complements on Chern homomorphisms}

\paragraph{5.1.}
The example in 4.5 does not rule out the possible existence, for an arbitrary scheme $X$, of a ring homomorphism
\[
g^*:\Gr^\bullet(X)\longto H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))
\]
which sends each Chern class $c_i$ of a locally free sheaf $E$ to the cohomological Chern class of SGA 5, VII. Since $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ is generated by the $c_i(E)$, such a $g^*$ would be uniquely determined by compatibility with Chern classes.

\paragraph{5.2.}
Using the Chern homomorphism
\[
K^\bullet(X)\longto H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet)),
\]
and passing to associated gradeds for the filtration on the source and the degree filtration on the target, one obtains a homomorphism of graded groups, but not of rings,
\[
\gamma^*:\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)\longto H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))
\]
satisfying
\[
\gamma^*(\cl(E))=c_i^{(\ell)}(E)\quad\bmod\ \text{torsion}.
\]
After the same divisions as before, this gives a ring homomorphism
\[
g^*:\Gr^\bullet(X)\longto
H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))\otimes \ZZ[1/(i-1)!],
\]
carrying Chern classes to Chern classes. Thus the existence question has an affirmative answer after replacing cohomology by its quotient modulo torsion. The first doubtful case for defining the components $g^i$ is $g^2$, where a priori one reaches only
\[
2c_2^{(\ell)}=\gamma^*\bigl(\cl(\Lambda^2E-\Sym^2E)\bigr).
\]

A negative answer for $g^2$ would follow, for example, from the existence of three invertible sheaves $L_1,L_2,L_3$ on $X$ such that
\[
(L_1-1)(L_2-1)(L_3-1)=0\quad\text{in }K^\bullet(X),
\]
but
\[
c_1^{(\ell)}(L_1)c_1^{(\ell)}(L_2)c_1^{(\ell)}(L_3)\neq0
\quad\text{in }H^6(X,\ZZ_\ell(3)).
\]

\paragraph{5.3.}
When $X$ is smooth and quasi-projective over a field $k$, one can similarly ask whether there exists a ring homomorphism
\[
g:\Gr^\bullet(X)\longto A(X)
\]
with values in the Chow ring and carrying Chern classes to Chern classes. The preceding discussion applies, and in particular shows how to construct directly
\[
(-1)^{i-1}(i-1)!\,g^i
\]
when $g^i$ itself is not available.

\paragraph{5.4.}
The same question also arises for a compact topological space $X$, since one may form the graded ring $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ of $K(X)$ with its $\lambda$-filtration and ask for a ring homomorphism
\[
\Gr^\bullet(X)\longto H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ)
\]
carrying Chern classes to Chern classes.

\subsection*{6. Cohomological Riemann--Roch and the Gysin homomorphism}

\paragraph{6.1.}
For simplicity, restrict to schemes $X$ admitting an ample invertible sheaf, so that naive and non-naive $K^\bullet(X)$ need not be distinguished. Consider first Riemann--Roch with denominators for a proper complete-intersection morphism $f:X\to Y$. For a prime $\ell$ invertible on $Y$, one has $\ell$-adic Chern classes
\[
c_i^{(\ell)}(E)\in H^{2i}(X,\ZZ_\ell(i)),
\]
and hence a homomorphism
\[
K^\bullet(X)\longto H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet)).
\tag{6.1}
\]
The missing ingredient for a cohomological Riemann--Roch formula is a Gysin homomorphism
\[
f_*:H^{\bullet-2d}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet-d))
\longto H^\bullet(Y,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet)),
\tag{6.2}
\]
where $d$ is the virtual relative dimension of $f$. Assuming such a homomorphism is defined, Riemann--Roch for $f$ in the form of Exposé VIII, with values in $\Gr^\bullet(Y)$, is equivalent to commutativity of
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
\Gr^\bullet(X)\arrow[r]\arrow[d,"f_*"']&
H^{2\bullet}(X,\QQ_\ell(\bullet))\arrow[d,"f_*"]\\
\Gr^{\bullet-d}(Y)\arrow[r]&
H^{2(\bullet-d)}(Y,\QQ_\ell(\bullet-d)).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{6.3}
\]
The horizontal arrows are obtained from (6.1) by passing to associated gradeds and dividing so that Chern classes go to Chern classes.

\paragraph{6.2.}
The Gysin homomorphism is defined at least when $Y$ is smooth over a field, using relative cohomological purity. The same construction applies when $Y$ is excellent, regular, and of characteristic zero, by the absolute purity theorem of SGA 4, XIX, and it will apply for every excellent regular $Y$ once absolute purity is known in full generality. It also applies when $X$ and $Y$ are both smooth over a common scheme $S$ and $f$ is an $S$-morphism, by relative purity. In each case one should conjecture that (6.3) is commutative. The only case presently verified is the case in which $X$ and $Y$ are both smooth over a field; it follows from commutativity of the analogous diagram with Chow rings:
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
A^\bullet(X)\arrow[r]\arrow[d,"f_*"']&
H^{2\bullet}(X,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet))\arrow[d,"f_*"]\\
A^{\bullet-d}(Y)\arrow[r]&
H^{2(\bullet-d)}(Y,\ZZ_\ell(\bullet-d)).
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{6.4}
\]

\paragraph{6.3.}
In each case where a Gysin homomorphism can be constructed, the construction is integral, with coefficients in $\ZZ_\ell(i)$, not only with coefficients in $\QQ_\ell(i)$. When $f$ is an immersion, this permits the denominator-free Riemann--Roch question of no. 3. If $X$ and $Y$ are both smooth over a field $k$, then commutativity of (6.4) shows that it would be enough to prove the denominator-free formula in the Chow ring.

\paragraph{6.4.}
Without regularity on $Y$, even when $X$ and $Y$ are of finite type over $\CC$, there is no evident definition of a cohomological Gysin morphism (6.2), even for a regular immersion of codimension one. Can one nevertheless define one with reasonable properties, especially making (6.3) commute? This is closely related to defining, for $X$ a complete intersection of codimension $i$ in $Y$, its cohomology class
\[
\cl(X)\in H^{2i}(Y,\ZZ_\ell(i))
\]
so that proper intersections correspond to cup-products. This latter question has an affirmative answer at least modulo torsion, by setting
\[
\cl(X)=\frac{c_i(\cO_X)}{(-1)^{i-1}(i-1)!}\in H^{2i}(Y,\QQ_\ell(i)).
\]
It is not known whether $c_i(\cO_X)$ is divisible by $(i-1)!$ in $H^{2i}(Y,\ZZ_\ell(i))$. For instance, if $Y$ is a projective threefold over $\CC$ and $X$ is a singular point of $Y$, is $c_3(\cO_X)$ divisible by $2$ in $H^6(Y,\ZZ_\ell(3))$? Equivalently, is the mod-$2$ Chern class zero? Similarly, if $Y$ is a projective fourfold and $X$ a one-dimensional closed subscheme that is globally the intersection of three positive divisors $D_1,D_2,D_3$, is
\[
c_1(D_1)c_1(D_2)c_1(D_3)\in H^6(Y,\ZZ/2\ZZ)
\]
independent of the chosen $D_i$ cutting out $X$?

\subsection*{7. Chern classes of perfect complexes}

\paragraph{7.1.}
To develop a cohomological Riemann--Roch formula, with or without denominators, outside the artificial assumption that the schemes admit ample line bundles, one is forced to define, for a perfect complex $L_\bullet$ on a scheme $X$, Chern classes
\[
c_i(L_\bullet)\in H^{2i}(X,\ZZ_\ell(i))
\]
generalizing those of locally free modules. The same question arises for general locally ringed topoi, and for perfect complexes on paracompact spaces ringed by continuous complex-valued functions, using ordinary integral cohomology. For compact spaces, the $K^\bullet(X)$ built from perfect complexes agrees with naive $K$-theory built from locally free modules, so the problem becomes classical. Already for locally compact spaces, however, the problem is non-trivial, since integral cohomology is not always the inverse limit of the cohomologies of compact subsets.

\paragraph{7.2.}
More precisely, if $Y$ is a closed subset of $X$ such that $L_\bullet|_{X-Y}$ is zero in the derived category, i.e. all cohomology sheaves of $L_\bullet$ are supported on $Y$, one would like to define Chern classes with support in $Y$,
\[
c_i^Y(L_\bullet)\in H_Y^{2i}(X,\ZZ_\ell(i)),
\]
whose image under
\[
H_Y^{2i}(X,\ZZ_\ell(i))\longto H^{2i}(X,\ZZ_\ell(i))
\]
is the ordinary class of 7.1. Even when ordinary Chern classes reduce to those of locally free modules, such supported Chern classes have not yet been constructed. They will probably have to be constructed together with the general Chern classes postulated in 7.1. These supported classes should play a role in proving the denominator-free cohomological Riemann--Roch formula and should also yield an improved Riemann--Roch formula involving families of supports.

\subsection*{8. The Chow ring of regular schemes}

The problem is to define rational equivalence of cycles, and to study the resulting ring of classes and its functorial properties, for a noetherian regular scheme $X$ admitting an ample invertible sheaf, generalizing the theory known when $X$ is smooth over a field. The key technical result should again be a moving lemma, ensuring that a cycle can be varied within its class so as to meet a given cycle in the proper dimension.

This problem is natural, especially because 4.5--4.8 show that the substitutes $\Gr^\bullet(X)$ and $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$ are unsatisfactory when one wants more than a theory modulo torsion. If $X$ is obtained from a projective smooth algebraic scheme $Y$ over a field $k$ by taking the local scheme at the vertex of the projective cone over $Y$ and deleting the vertex, then, assuming the expected limiting property for Chow rings, one finds that $A(X)$ is canonically isomorphic to
\[
A(Y)/\xi A(Y),
\]
where $\xi\in A^1(Y)$ is induced by the chosen projective embedding. This quotient may be called the primitive part of $A(Y)$, in a sense suggested by Lefschetz--Hodge theory. Thus local Chow rings of local rings with isolated singularity are non-trivial geometric invariants and should play a role in the arithmetic study of such local rings.

Once a reasonable notion of Chow ring is available for a noetherian regular scheme with an ample line bundle, the relations established and conjectured in no. 4 between that ring and $\Gr^\bullet_{\topf}(X)$ should be generalized, and the denominator-free Riemann--Roch question of no. 3 should be formulated in terms of that ring.
