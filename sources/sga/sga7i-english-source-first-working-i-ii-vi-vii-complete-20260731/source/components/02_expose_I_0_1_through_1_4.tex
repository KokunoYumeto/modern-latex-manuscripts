0.1. \underline{Review of $\ell$-adic cohomology}

Let $X$ be a scheme of finite type over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic exponent $p$, and let $\ell$ be a prime different from $p$.

The $\ell$-adic cohomology groups of $X$ were defined in SGA 4 and SGA 5.

a) $H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$ is the $i$-th cohomology group of the \`etale site $X_{\mathrm{et}}$ of $X$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n$;

b) by definition,
\[
H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}_\ell)=\varprojlim_n H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n);
\]

c) by definition,
\[
H^i(X,\mathbb{Q}_\ell)=H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}_\ell)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}\mathbb{Q}_\ell.
\]

The $\ell$-adic cohomology groups of $X$ with proper supports are defined in the same way from the groups $H^i_c(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$ of SGA 4 XVII.

In each of the following cases, one knows how to prove that these groups $H^i(X)$ have reasonable finiteness properties:

(0.1.1) for cohomology with proper supports;

(0.1.2) when $X$ is proper over $k$;

(0.1.3) when $X$ is smooth over $k$ (by (0.1.1) and Poincar\'e duality, SGA 4 XVIII);

(0.1.4) when Hironaka's resolution of singularities is available for schemes of finite type over $k$ of dimension at most $\dim(X)$;

(0.1.5) when $i=0,1$.

%%%%%%%%%%  scan idx 14  |  folio 3  %%%%%%%%%%

In all these cases, the groups $H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)$ are finite, the groups $H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ are finitely generated, the groups $H^i(X,\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ are finite-dimensional, and (except perhaps in case (0.1.5)) there are split short exact sequences

\begin{equation*}
0 \longrightarrow H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}_\ell)\otimes \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n
\longrightarrow H^i(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)
\longrightarrow \Tor_1(H^{i+1}(X,\mathbb{Z}_\ell),\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n)
\longrightarrow 0.
\end{equation*}
Moreover, in each case the proof of the finiteness theorem also proves a generic constructibility theorem: if $k$ is the field of fractions of an integral noetherian scheme $S$, and $X$ is the generic fibre of a morphism $f_1:X_1\longrightarrow S$ of finite type, then there exists a nonempty open subset $U\subset S$ such that over $U$ the sheaves $R^i f_*\mathbb{Z}/\ell^n$ are locally constant and their formation commutes with every base change.

0.2. We no longer suppose that $k$ is algebraically closed. Let $\bar{k}$ be an algebraic closure of $k$ (or a separable closure, which amounts to the same thing here). We consider the ``geometric'' cohomology groups $H^i(X_{\bar{k}})$, where $X_{\bar{k}}$ is obtained from $X$ by extension of scalars from $k$ to $\bar{k}$. By transport of structure, the Galois group $\Gal(\bar{k}/k)$ acts on these groups. In $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-cohomology, in any of the cases (0.1.1)--(0.1.5), this gives continuous representations of $\Gal(\bar{k}/k)$ in a linear group $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$.

0.3. \underline{Review of traits} (0.0.1)

For the proofs, we refer to [4]. Let $S$ be a henselian trait, and let $\eta$, $s$, $V$, $\bar V$, $\bar\eta$, and $\bar s$ be as in (0.0.1) and (0.0.3). Let $p$ denote the characteristic exponent of $k(s)$, and let $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)$ (respectively, $\Gal(\bar s/s)$) denote the Galois group $\Gal(k(\bar\eta)/k(\eta))$ (respectively, the corresponding group for $\bar s/s$). By transport of structure, $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)$ acts on $k(\bar s)$; hence there is a homomorphism from $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)$ onto $\Gal(\bar s/s)$ whose kernel is the \underline{inertia group} $I$. The subfield of $k(\bar\eta)$ fixed by $I$ is a maximal unramified extension of $k(\eta)$.

The profinite group $I$ has a largest pro-$p$ subgroup $P$. The quotient $I/P$ is the \underline{tame inertia group}. Canonically,

\begin{equation*}
I/P\simeq \varprojlim_n\mu_n(k(\bar s))
\underset{\mathrm{dfn}}{=}\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)(k(\bar s)).
\end{equation*}

%%%%%%%%%%  scan idx 15  |  folio 4  %%%%%%%%%%

If $\phi_n$ is the canonical map from $I/P$ to $\mu_n(\bar k)$ and $u$ is an element of $\bar V$ of valuation $1/n$, then, for $\sigma\in I$,

\begin{equation*}
\sigma(u)\equiv\phi_n(\sigma)\,u
\qquad (\text{modulo elements of valuation greater than }1/n).
\end{equation*}
% Printed source: \phi_n(u)u. The argument must be \sigma because \phi_n is
% a character of I/P; the English text transparently corrects this source typo.
In summary,

\begin{equation}\tag{0.3.1}
\begin{aligned}
\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)&\supset I\supset P,\\
\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)/I&\simeq\Gal(\bar s/s),\\
I/P&\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)(k(\bar s)),\\
P&:\ \text{a pro-$p$ group}\quad(\{e\}\text{ if }p=1),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and the action (by inner automorphisms in $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)$) of $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)/I$ on $I/P$ is identified with the natural action of $\Gal(\bar s/s)$ on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)(k(\bar s))$.

0.4. More generally, let $S$ be strictly local and regular, let $D$ be a regular divisor in $S$, and let $p'$ be the characteristic exponent of the generic point of $D$. Let $\bar\eta$ be a geometric generic point of $S$, and let $\bar s$ be the resulting geometric point centered at the closed point $s$. By Abhyankar's lemma, the fundamental group $\pi_1(S-D,\bar\eta)$ has a filtration analogous to (0.3.1):

\begin{equation}\tag{0.4.1}
\begin{aligned}
\pi_1(S-D,\bar\eta)&\supset I\supset P,\\
\pi_1(S-D,\bar\eta)/I&\simeq\Gal(\bar s/s),\\
I/P&\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)(k(\bar s)),\\
P&:\ \text{with no quotient of order prime to }p'.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The action by inner automorphisms of $\pi_1(S-D,\bar\eta)/I$ on $I/P$ is identified with the natural action of $\Gal(\bar s/s)$ on $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}(1)(k(\bar s))$.

0.5. \underline{N\'eron desingularization}

The method of N\'eron desingularization gives the following result.

\underline{Lemma} 0.5.1. \underline{Let $S\longrightarrow S_o$ be a morphism of henselian traits, with $S=\Spec(V)$ and $S_o=\Spec(V_o)$, and let $\pi$ be a uniformizer of $V_o$. Suppose that $\pi$ is a uniformizer of $V$, and that the extensions $k(s)/k(s_o)$ and $k(\eta)/k(\eta_o)$ are separable. Then $V$ is a filtered direct limit of smooth henselian $V_o$-algebras $B_i$ essentially of finite type over $V_o$.}

%%%%%%%%%%  scan idx 16  |  folio 5  %%%%%%%%%%

To construct the desired $V_o$-algebras, one takes $V_o\subset B\subset V$, with $B$ of finite type, and desingularizes $\Spec(B)$ by N\'eron's method ([1], \S~4).

Let $S=\Spec(V)$ be a henselian trait.

(0.5.2) If $S$ is of equal characteristic, with uniformizer $\pi$, one may apply (0.5.1) with $S_o=\mathbf{F}_p[\pi]_{(\pi)}$. The extension $s/s_o$ is separable because $\mathbf{F}_p$ is perfect; the extension $\eta/\eta_o$ is separable because $\pi$, being a uniformizer, is not a $p$-th power (if $p\neq1$), and hence belongs to a $p$-basis. It follows that $S$ is a limit of spectra of smooth henselian $\mathbf{F}_p[\pi]_{(\pi)}$-algebras essentially of finite type.

(0.5.3) If $S$ is complete and of mixed characteristic, with perfect residue field $k$, then $V$ is an Eisenstein extension of the ring of Witt vectors (that is, the Cohen ring) $W(k)$:

\begin{equation*}
V\simeq W(k)[\pi]/\left(\pi^n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_i\pi^i\right).
\end{equation*}
If the $a_i$ are perturbed slightly, one obtains an isomorphic extension. We may therefore suppose that the $a_i$ belong to $W(k')$, where $k'$ is of finite type over $\mathbf{F}_p$. Let $k_o$ be the perfect closure of $k'$ in $k$, and set

\begin{equation*}
V_o=W(k_o)[\pi]/\left(\pi^n+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_i\pi^i\right).
\end{equation*}
Lemma 0.5.1 applies to $V/V_o$, and the residue field of $V_o$ is a radicial extension of a field of finite type over $\mathbf{F}_p$.

1. \underline{Arithmetic proof of the monodromy theorem}

The reader will find in [5], Appendix, a proof of the following result of A. Grothendieck.

\underline{Proposition} 1.1. \underline{Let $S$ be a henselian trait, let $\ell$ be a prime different from the characteristic exponent $p$ of $k(s)$, and let $\rho$ be a continuous representation of $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)$ in $\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{Q}_\ell)$. Suppose that the following condition holds:}

$(*_\ell)$ \quad \underline{No finite extension of $k(s)$ contains all roots of unity of $\ell$-power order.}

%%%%%%%%%%  scan idx 17  |  folio 6  %%%%%%%%%%

\underline{Then there exists an open subgroup $I_1$ of the inertia group $I$ such that $\rho(\sigma)$ is unipotent for every $\sigma\in I_1$.}

Condition $(*_\ell)$ is automatically satisfied when $k(s)$ is of finite type over the prime field (or is radicial over such a field).

The following theorem follows immediately from 1.1.

\underline{Monodromy theorem} 1.2. \underline{With the preceding notation, let $X$ be a scheme of finite type over $\eta$, and let $H$ be a cohomology space of $X_{\bar\eta}$ with coefficients in $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, of one of the types (0.1.1)--(0.1.5). If condition $(*_\ell)$ is satisfied, the action on $H$ of every element of $I$ is quasi-unipotent.}

% reference is the list (0.1.1)--(0.1.5) immediately above.

The limit arguments of 0.5 allow us to dispense with hypothesis $(*_\ell)$.

\underline{Variant} 1.3. \underline{Condition $(*_\ell)$ in 1.2 is superfluous.}

Let $H'_o$ be a $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-cohomology space of $X_{\bar\eta}$ giving rise to $H$, and put $H_o=H'_o/\text{torsion}$. By hypothesis,
$H=H_o\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, and the action of $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)$ is induced by

\begin{equation*}
\rho:\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(H_o).
\end{equation*}
Let $I_{\ell'}$ be the largest prime-to-$\ell$ subgroup of $I$. Its image in $\mathrm{GL}(H_o)$ is an $\ell$-adic Lie group of order prime to $\ell$, and is therefore finite.

Make an extension of traits $S'\longrightarrow S$. This does not change $H_o$ (SGA 4 XVI 6.6), and $\rho(I')$ has finite index in $\rho(I)$, because $I'/P'$ has finite index in $I/P$, by what precedes. It therefore suffices to prove the theorem over $S'$; we may suppose that

a) $\Gal(\bar\eta/\eta)$ acts trivially on $H_o/\ell H_o$;

b) if $S$ is of mixed characteristic, then $S$ is complete with perfect residue field (EGA $\mathrm{O}_{III}$ 10.3.1).

Put $S=\Spec(V)$, define $V_o$ as in (0.5.2) and (0.5.3), and apply (0.5.1). We have

\begin{equation*}
V=\varinjlim_i B_i.
\end{equation*}
