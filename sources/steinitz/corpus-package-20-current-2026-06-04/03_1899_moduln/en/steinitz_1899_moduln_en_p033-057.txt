The recursion formulae Ia and IIa show that the functions γ̄ and δ̄ can be put together from
t-functions and v-functions respectively by addition, subtraction, and multiplication. This
representation is, however, substantially more complicated than the representation of t and v by
ψ-functions. In the latter representation the number of representing ψ-functions is bounded by
the degree n of the classes under consideration, whereas the number of t- and v-functions used
in representing γ̄(Γ; A1 , . . . , As ) and δ̄(∆; A1 , . . . , As ) is finite in each individual case but has no
upper bound even when n and s are fixed. Moreover the subtraction occurring in these formulae
makes the question of the vanishing of the γ̄- and δ̄-functions very difficult. These difficulties
are removed by transforming the expressions obtained for γ̄ and δ̄ into products of functions
which are easier to survey.
