§ 4. Modules
22. From now on the degree of a rectangle means its number of columns. This applies in
particular when the rectangle consists of only one row; its degree is then the number of its
elements. We use small Greek letters for rows. The equation σ = 0 means that all elements of σ
are zero. A factor of a row σ is any ideal which divides every element of σ; the greatest common
divisor of the elements is called the greatest factor of σ. By aσ we mean the row obtained from
σ by multiplying its entries by the number a. If σ and τ have the same degree, then σ + τ and
σ − τ denote the rows obtained by componentwise addition and subtraction. If integral rows
σ, σ1 , σ2 , . . . , σm satisfy a relation
σ = a1 σ1 + · · · + am σm
with integral coefficients ai , then σ is said to be composable from σ1 , . . . , σm .
23. Modules. A system S of integral rows of degree n is called a module of degree n if it has
the following two properties:
1) if σ ∈ S, then aσ ∈ S for every integral
number a of K;
2) if σ, τ ∈ S, then σ + τ ∈ S.
Modules are denoted by capital German letters; a lower index gives the degree. The row σ = 0
by itself forms a module, the zero module.
Two rows σ, τ are congruent modulo a module An , written
σ≡τ

(mod An ),

if σ − τ belongs to the module. The module An is said to be divisible by the module Bn if every
row of An is also contained in Bn ; then Bn is a divisor of An , and An is a multiple of Bn . A
congruence which holds modulo An also holds modulo every divisor of An .
