 22. Transcendental Extensions. Algebraic Dependence Among Elements of an Extension Field.

Every non-algebraic extension of a field will be called transcendental. -- In the following investigations, which are meant to lead us to a clear classification of all fields, we fix a field $$ and refer to it expressions such as ``extension field,'' ``transcendental,'' and so on, unless something else is expressly stated. If $ S$ is a system of elements of an extension field $$, and $a$ is an element of $$, we call $a$ algebraically dependent on $ S$ (with respect to $$) if $a$ is algebraic over the field $( S)$. We call a system of elements $ S'$ algebraically dependent on $ S$ if every element of $ S'$ is algebraically dependent on $ S$.

1. If the element $a$ is algebraically dependent on the system $ S$, then there is a finite subsystem $ S'$ of $ S$ on which $a$ is algebraically dependent.

For our assumption implies that $a$ is a root of an integral function $(x)$ of the field $( S)$. Since $(x)$ has only finitely many coefficients, there is a finite subsystem $ S'$ of $ S$ such that these coefficients already occur in $( S')$. Then $a$ is algebraically dependent on $ S'$.

2. If $ S_3$ is algebraically dependent on $ S_2$, and $ S_2$ on $ S_1$, then $ S_3$ is algebraically dependent on $ S_1$.

Indeed, put $( S_1)=_1$, $( S_2)=_2$. It follows from our assumption that $_1( S_2)$ is algebraic over $_1$, and $_2( S_3)$ is algebraic over $_2$ ( 7, 7.). Since $_2$ is a subfield of $_1( S_2)$, the elements of $_2( S_3)$ are algebraic over $_1( S_2)$, hence also over $_1=( S_1)$ ( 7, 9.). Therefore the elements of $ S_3$ are algebraic over $( S_1)$, as required.

Accordingly, if we call two systems $ S_1, S_2$ algebraically equivalent (with respect to $$), or simply equivalent, when each is algebraically dependent on the other, then any two systems equivalent to a third are equivalent to each other, and so on.

We call a system $ S$ of elements of an extension field algebraically reducible (with respect to $$), or simply reducible, if it is equivalent to a proper part of itself or consists of a single element algebraic over $$; otherwise we call $ S$ (algebraically) irreducible. It is immediately clear from this definition that in a reducible system containing more than one element, at least one element occurs which is algebraically dependent on the system of the remaining elements. In conjunction with 1. this gives at once:

3. Every subsystem of an irreducible system is irreducible.

4. Every reducible system contains a finite reducible subsystem.

Let $u_1,,u_m$ be the elements of $ U$, and $v_1,,v_n$ those of $ B$. Since $v_1$ is transcendental over $$ and algebraically dependent on $ U$, Theorem 7 gives at least one element of $ U$ such that replacing it by $v_1$ changes $ U$ into an equivalent irreducible system. This proves the assertion for $n=1$. For $n>1$ the proof is by complete induction. Let $ B'$ be the system consisting of $v_1,,v_n-1$. Since it is irreducible and algebraically dependent on $ U$, we may regard it as proved that $ U$ is equivalent to an irreducible system $ U'$ consisting of $ B'$ and $m-n+1$ elements from $ U$. The element $v_n$ is algebraically dependent on $ U'$, but not on $ B'$. Hence, if $ T$ is the smallest subsystem of $ U'$ on which $v_n$ is algebraically dependent, then $ T$ contains at least one of the $m-n+1$ elements from $ U$. Replacing such an element by $v_n$ turns $ U'$ into an irreducible system equivalent to $ U$, consisting of $ B$ and $m-n$ elements of $ U$. In the case $m=n$, therefore, $ U$ and $ B$ are equivalent.

We also see from this that an irreducible system $ B$ algebraically dependent on $ U$ cannot contain more than $m$ elements. Otherwise a proper part $ B'$ of $ B$ consisting of $m$ elements would, by what has just been proved, be equivalent to $ U$. Then $ B$ would be algebraically dependent on $ B'$, hence reducible. Thus:

9. An irreducible system $ B$ algebraically dependent on a finite system $ U$ cannot contain more elements than that system; in the case of equal numbers of elements the two systems are equivalent.
