On the Theory of Abelian Groups
By E. Steinitz in Charlottenburg.
Among finite groups, those whose operations are interchangeable, the so-called commutative
or Abelian groups, are distinguished by particular simplicity and transparency. This transparency
rests on the fact that we are able to characterize the different commutative groups by definite
numbers standing in very simple relation to them, their “invariants.” From every Abelian group
one can, namely, select a system of generating operations
A1 , A2 , . . . , An
which has the following properties: 1. Every operation can be represented, and in only one way, as
a product of powers of the operations A1 , . . . , An . 2. Of the orders a1 , a2 , . . . , an of the operations
A1 , A2 , . . . , An , each is divisible by the following one. The numbers a are thereby completely
determined by the group, and in turn determine the group (if holohedrically isomorphic groups
are not regarded as different). They are called the “invariants” of the group. Naturally the
system of invariants of the group may be prolonged arbitrarily by adding invariants of value 1,
since one may add the identity as often as desired to the system of generating operations. The
number of invariants different from 1 is called the “rank” of the group and indicates how many
operations are required to generate it.

If, therefore, the coefficient of pm is denoted by h(γ; α, β), then h(γ; α, β) is independent of p
and cannot be negative, because otherwise for large values of the prime number p the function
t(γ; α, β) would take negative values, contrary to its nature as a number. – The form, however,
in which the function t(γ; α, β) presents itself is not suited to decide in general when it vanishes
identically (that is, with the exponents ai , bi , ci fixed, for every value of p), and when it does
not. According to the investigations up to now, the following theorems may be regarded as
very probable: 1. If the function t(γ; α, β) does not vanish identically, then it vanishes for
no value of the prime number p. 2. The function t(γ; α, β) either actually rises to degree m,
or else it vanishes identically altogether. Accordingly, the vanishing and non-vanishing of the
function t(γ; α, β) would be quite independent of p and conditioned solely by the vanishing or
non-vanishing of h(γ; α, β).
