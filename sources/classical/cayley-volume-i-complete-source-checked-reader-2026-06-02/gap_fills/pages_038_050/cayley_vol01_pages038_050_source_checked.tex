Hence the function
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\dd\phi\cdot
\nabla\frac{1}{(\xi+\phi)\sqrt{\{(\xi+h^{2}+\phi)\ldots\}}}
\]
(observing that differentiation with respect to $\xi$ is the same as differentiation with

value is
\[
-\,\frac{2\chi_{0}'\xi\,\Sigma\frac{1}{\xi+h^{2}}+4\chi_{0}''\xi}

\]
where
\[
\chi_{0}\xi=\frac{1}{\xi\sqrt{\{(\xi+h^{2})(\xi+h_{1}^{2})\ldots\}}}.
\]
We have immediately
\[
\frac{\chi_{0}'\xi}{\chi_{0}\xi}
=-\half\left(\frac{2}{\xi}+\Sigma\frac{1}{\xi+h^{2}}\right);
\]
or
\[
2\chi_{0}\xi\,\Sigma\left(\frac{1}{\xi+h^{2}}\right)+4\chi_{0}'\xi
=-\,4\frac{\chi_{0}\xi}{\xi};
\]
whence
\[
\int_{0}^{\infty}\dd\phi\cdot
\nabla\frac{1}{(\xi+\phi)\sqrt{\{(\xi+h^{2}+\phi)\ldots\}}}
=
\frac{4}{\xi^{2}\sqrt{\{(\xi+h^{2})(\xi+h_{1}^{2})\ldots\}}
\left\{\dfrac{a^{2}}{(\xi+h^{2})^{2}}+\dfrac{b^{2}}{(\xi+h_{1}^{2})^{2}}+\ldots\right\}}.
\]

Hence restoring the value of $\nabla$, and of the first side of the equation,
\[
\int\!\!\int\cdots(\n\ \text{times})\,
\frac{\dd x\,\dd y\ldots}
{\left\{(a-x)^{2}+(b-y)^{2}\ldots\right\}^{\frac12\n+q}}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
={}&
\frac{hh_{1}\ldots\pi^{\frac12\n}}{2^{2q-2}\cdot 1.2\ldots q\cdot
\Gamma(\frac12\n+q)}
\left(\frac{d^{2}}{da^{2}}+\frac{d^{2}}{db^{2}}+\ldots\right)^{q-1}\\
&{}\times
\frac{1}
{\xi^{2}\sqrt{\{(\xi+h^{2})(\xi+h_{1}^{2})\ldots\}}

\end{aligned}
\]

\[
\frac{a^{2}}{\xi+h^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}}{\xi+h_{1}^{2}}+\ldots=1;
\]

\noindent In general if $u$ be any function of $\xi,a,b\ldots$
\[
\left(\frac{d^{2}}{da^{2}}+\frac{d^{2}}{db^{2}}+\ldots\right)u
=
\frac{
4\dfrac{d^{2}u}{d\xi^{2}}
+2\dfrac{du}{d\xi}\Sigma\dfrac{1}{\xi+h^{2}}
+4\dfrac{d^{2}u}{d\xi\,da}\Sigma\dfrac{a}{\xi+h^{2}}
}
{\Sigma\dfrac{a^{2}}{(\xi+h^{2})^{2}}}
+\Sigma\frac{d^{2}u}{da^{2}},
\]
from which the values of the second side for $q=1$, $q=2$, \&c.\ may be successively
calculated.

The performance of the operation
$\left(\dfrac{d}{da}\right)^{p}\left(\dfrac{d}{db}\right)^{q}
\left(\dfrac{d}{dc}\right)^{r}\ldots$, upon the integral $V'$, leads in like manner to a very
great number of integrals, all of them expressible algebraically, for a single differentiation
renders the integration with respect to $\phi$ possible. But this is a subject which need not
be further considered at present.

We shall consider, lastly, the definite integral
\[
U=\int\!\!\int\cdots(\n\ \text{times})
\frac{(a-x)f\left(\dfrac{x^{2}}{h^{2}}+\dfrac{y^{2}}{h_{1}^{2}}+\ldots\right)\dd x\dd y\ldots}
{\left\{(a-x)^{2}+(b-y)^{2}\ldots\right\}^{\frac12\n}},
\]
limits, \&c.\ as before. This is readily deduced from the less general one
\[
\int\!\!\int\cdots(\n\ \text{times})
\frac{(a-x)\dd x\dd y\ldots}
{\left\{(a-x)^{2}+(b-y)^{2}\ldots\right\}^{\frac12\n}}.
\]
For representing this quantity by $F(h,h_{1},\ldots)$, it may be seen that
\[
U=\int_{0}^{1}f(m^{2})\,\frac{d}{dm}F(mh,mh_{1},\ldots)\dd m;
\]
but in the value of $F(h,h_{1},\ldots)$, changing $h,h_{1},\ldots$ into $mh,mh_{1},\ldots$
also writing $m^{2}\phi$ instead of $\phi$, and $m^{2}\xi'$ for $\xi$, we have
\[
F(mh,mh_{1},\ldots)=
\frac{hh_{1}\ldots\pi^{\frac12\n}}{\Gamma(\frac12\n)}\,a
\int_{0}^{\infty}
\frac{\dd\phi}{(\xi'+h^{2}+\phi)\sqrt{\Phi'}},
\]
where
\[
\Phi'=(\xi'+h^{2}+\phi)(\xi'+h_{1}^{2}+\phi)\ldots
\]
and
\[
\frac{a^{2}}{\xi'+h^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}}{\xi'+h_{1}^{2}}+\ldots=m^{2}.
\]
Hence
\[
\frac{d}{dm}F(mh,mh_{1},\ldots)
=
\frac{hh_{1}\ldots\pi^{\frac12\n}}{\Gamma(\frac12\n)}\,a
\frac{d\xi'}{dm}
\int_{0}^{\infty}\dd\phi\,
\frac{d}{d\xi'}
\frac{1}{(\xi'+h^{2}+\phi)\sqrt{\Phi'}},
\]
or, observing that $\dfrac{d}{d\xi'}$ is equivalent to $\dfrac{d}{d\phi}$, and effecting the
integration between the proper limits,
\[
\frac{d}{dm}F(mh,mh_{1},\ldots)
=-\,\frac{hh_{1}\ldots\pi^{\frac12\n}}{\Gamma(\frac12\n)}\,a
\frac{d\xi'}{dm}\,
\frac{1}{(\xi'+h^{2})\sqrt{\{(\xi'+h^{2})(\xi'+h_{1}^{2})\ldots\}}}.
\]

Substituting this value, also
$f\left\{\dfrac{a^{2}}{\xi'+h^{2}}+\dfrac{b^{2}}{\xi'+h_{1}^{2}}+\ldots\right\}$

$\xi'=\infty$, $m=1$ gives $\xi'=\xi$, where $\xi$ is a quantity determined as before by

\[
\frac{a^{2}}{\xi+h^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}}{\xi+h_{1}^{2}}+\ldots=1,
\]

\[
U=-\,\frac{hh_{1}\ldots\pi^{\frac12\n}}{\Gamma(\frac12\n)}\,a
\int_{\infty}^{\xi}
\frac{
f\left\{\dfrac{a^{2}}{\xi'+h^{2}}+\ldots\right\}\dd\xi'}

\]

or, inverting the limits and omitting the negative sign,
\[
U=\frac{hh_{1}\ldots\pi^{\frac12\n}}{\Gamma(\frac12\n)}\,a
\int_{0}^{\infty}
\frac{
f\left\{\dfrac{a^{2}}{\xi+h^{2}+\phi}+
\dfrac{b^{2}}{\xi+h_{1}^{2}+\phi}+\ldots\right\}\dd\phi}
{(\xi+h^{2}+\phi)\sqrt{\{(\xi+h^{2}+\phi)(\xi+h_{1}^{2}+\phi)\ldots\}}};

\[
\int\!\!\int\cdots(\n\ \text{times})
\frac{
f\left(\dfrac{x^{2}}{h^{2}}+\dfrac{y^{2}}{h_{1}^{2}}+\ldots\right)\dd x\dd y\ldots}
{\left\{(a-x)^{2}+(b-y)^{2}\ldots\right\}^{\frac12\n}}
\]

\begin{center}
{\Large\textbf{4.}}\par
\bigskip
{\Large ON CERTAIN EXPANSIONS, IN SERIES OF MULTIPLE SINES\\
AND COSINES.}
\end{center}

\medskip
\begin{center}
\textit{[From the Cambridge Mathematical Journal, vol.\ \textsc{iii}.\ (1842), pp.~162--167.]}
\end{center}

\bigskip

\noindent\textsc{In} the following paper we shall suppose $e$ the base of the hyperbolic system of
logarithms; $\epsilon$ a constant, such that its modulus, and also the modulus of
$\dfrac{1}{e}\{1-\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\}$, are each of them less than unity;
$\chi\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}$ a function of $u$, which, as $u$ increases from $0$ to $\pi$,
passes continuously from the former of these values to the latter, without becoming a
maximum in the interval, $f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}$ any function of $u$ which remains finite
and continuous for values of $u$ included between the above limits. Hence, writing
\[
\chi\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}=m \tag{1},
\]
and considering the quantity
\[
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\,f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}}
{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}(1-e\cos u)} \tag{2}
\]
as a function of $m$, for values of $m$ or $u$ included between the limits $0$ and $\pi$,
we have
\[
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\,f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}}
{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}(1-e\cos u)}
=\frac{2}{\pi}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos rm
\int_{0}^{\pi}
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\,f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}\cos rm\,\dd m}
{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}(1-e\cos u)}
\tag{3}
\]
(Poisson, \textit{M\'ec.} tom.~I. p.~650); which may also be written
\[
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\,f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}}
{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}(1-e\cos u)}
=\frac{2}{\pi}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos rm
\int_{0}^{\pi}
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\,f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}\cos r\chi\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}\dd u}
{1-e\cos u} \tag{4};
\]
and if the first side of the equation be generally expansible in a series of multiple
cosines of $m$, instead of being so in particular cases only, its expanded value will
always be the one given by the second side of the preceding equation.

\clearpage

Now, between the limits $0$ and $\pi$, the function
\[
f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}\cos r\chi\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}
\]
will always be expansible in a series of multiple cosines of $u$; and if by any
algebraical process the function $f\rho\cos r\chi\rho$ can be expanded in the form
\[
f\rho\cos r\chi\rho=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}a_{s}\rho^{s},
\qquad (a_s=a_{-s}) \tag{5};
\]
we have, in a convergent series,
\[
f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}\cos r\chi\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}
=a_{0}+2\Sigma_{1}^{\infty}a_{s}\cos su \tag{6}.
\]
Again, putting
\[
\frac{1}{e}\{1-\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\}=\lambda \tag{7},
\]
we have
\[
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}{1-e\cos u}=1+2\Sigma_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{p}\cos pu \tag{8}.
\]
Multiplying these two series, and effecting the integration, we obtain
\[
\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\,f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}\cos r\chi\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}\dd u}
{1-e\cos u}
=2\left\{\half a_{0}+\Sigma_{1}^{\infty}(a_s\lambda^{s})\right\} \tag{9}.
\]
And the second side of this equation being obviously derived from the expansion of
$f\lambda\cos r\chi\lambda$ by rejecting negative powers of $\lambda$ and dividing by
2, the term independent of $\lambda$ may conveniently be represented by the notation
\[
\br{2f\lambda\cos r\chi\lambda} \tag{10};
\]
where in general, if $\Gamma\lambda$ can be expanded in the form
\[
\Gamma\lambda=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}(A_s\lambda^{s}),\qquad [A_{-s}=A_s] \tag{11},
\]
we have
\[
\br{\Gamma\lambda}=\half A_{0}+\Sigma_{1}^{\infty}A_s\lambda^{s}. \tag{12}
\]
(By what has preceded, the expansion of $\Gamma\lambda$ in the above form is always
possible in a certain sense; however, in the remainder of the present paper,
$\Gamma\lambda$ will always be of a form to satisfy the equation
$\Gamma(\frac{1}{\lambda})=\Gamma\lambda$, except in cases which will afterwards be
considered, where the condition $A_{-s}=A_s$ is unnecessary.)

Hence, observing the equations (4), (9), (10),
\[
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\,f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}}
{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}(1-e\cos u)}
=
\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos rm\,\br{2\cos r\chi\lambda\,f\lambda} \tag{13};
\]
from which, assuming a system of equations analogous to (1), and representing by
$\Pi(\Phi)$ the product $\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2}\ldots$, it is easy to deduce
\[
\Pi\left\{\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}
{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}(1-e\cos u)}\right\}
f\{e^{u_1\sqrt{-1}},e^{u_2\sqrt{-1}}\ldots\}
\]
\[
=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots
\Pi\cos r m\,
\br{\Pi(2\cos r\chi\lambda)f(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ldots)}. \tag{14}
\]

\clearpage

where $\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\ldots)$ being expansible in the form
\[
\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\ldots)
=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots
A_{s_1,s_2,\ldots}\lambda_1^{s_1}\lambda_2^{s_2}\ldots,
\quad [A_{s_1,s_2,\ldots}=A_{-s_1,-s_2,\ldots}], \tag{15}
\]
\[
\br{\Gamma(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\ldots)}
=\Sigma_{0}^{\infty}\Sigma_{0}^{\infty}\ldots
\frac{1}{2^{N}}A_{s_1,s_2,\ldots}\lambda_1^{s_1}\lambda_2^{s_2}\ldots,
\tag{16}
\]
$N$ being the number of exponents which vanish.

The equations (13) and (14) may also be written in the forms
\[
f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}
=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos rm\,
\br{
2\cos r\chi\lambda\,
\frac{\sqrt{-1}\chi'\lambda\{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\}}{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}
f\lambda
} \tag{17},
\]
\[
f\{e^{u_1\sqrt{-1}},e^{u_2\sqrt{-1}}\ldots\}
=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots
\Pi(\cos rm)\,
\br{\Pi\left\{
2\cos r\chi\lambda\,
\frac{\sqrt{-1}\chi'\lambda\{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\}}
{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}
\right\}f(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ldots)}. \tag{18}
\]

As examples of these formulae, we may assume
\[
\chi\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}=m=u-e\sin u \tag{19}.
\]
Hence, putting
\[
\lambda^{r}e^{-\frac{re}{2}(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})}
\lambda^{-r}e^{\frac{re}{2}(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})}=A_r, \tag{20}
\]
and observing the equation
\[
\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}=1-e\cos u, \tag{21}
\]
the equation (17) becomes
\[
f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}
=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos rm\,\br{
A_r\frac{\{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\}^{2}}{\sqrt{(1-e^{2})}}f\lambda
}. \tag{22}
\]
Thus, if
\[
\theta-\varpi=\cos^{-1}\frac{\cos u-e}{1-e\cos u} \tag{23},
\]
assuming
\[
f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}=\frac{\cos u-e}{1-e\cos u}, \tag{24}
\]
\[
\cos(\theta-\varpi)=
\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}\cos rm\,
\br{\{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\}\{\half(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})-e\}A_r}. \tag{25}
\]
the term corresponding to $r=0$ being
\[
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}\{2\lambda-2e-e(\lambda^{2}+1)+2e\lambda\}=-e. \tag{26}
\]
Again, assuming
\[
f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}=\frac{d\theta}{dm}
=\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}{(1-e\cos u)^{2}} \tag{27},
\]

\clearpage

and integrating the resulting equation with respect to $m$,
\[
\theta-\varpi=
\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin rm}{r}\br{A_r}
=m+2\Sigma_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin rm}{r}\br{A_r}, \tag{28}
\]
a formula given in the fifth No.\ of the \textit{Mathematical Journal}, and which suggested the
present paper.

As another example, let
\[
f\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}=\cos(\theta-\varpi)\frac{d\theta}{dm}
=\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}(\cos u-e)}{(1-e\cos u)^{3}}. \tag{29}
\]
Then integrating with respect to $m$, there is a term
\[
2m\br{\frac{\half(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})-e}{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})}} \tag{30},
\]
which it is evident, \textit{\`a priori}, must vanish. Equating it to zero, and reducing, we obtain
\[
\frac{e}{1-e^{2}}=
\br{\frac{\lambda+\lambda^{-1}}{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})}} \tag{31},
\]
that is
\[
\frac{e}{1-e^{2}}=
\lambda+\frac{e}{2}(\lambda^{2}+1)+\frac{e^{2}}{4}(\lambda^{3}+3\lambda)
+\frac{e^{3}}{8}(\lambda^{4}+4\lambda^{2}+3)+\ldots \tag{32},
\]
a singular formula, which may be verified by substituting for $\lambda$ its value: we then obtain
\[
\sin(\theta-\varpi)=
2\Sigma_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin rm}{r}
\br{A_r\frac{\half(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})-e}
{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})}}. \tag{33}
\]

The expansions of $\sin k(\theta-\varpi)$, $\cos k(\theta-\varpi)$, are in like manner
given by the formulae
\[
\cos k(\theta-\varpi)=
\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\br{A_rL' \cos kL}\cos rm, \tag{34}
\]
\[
\sin k(\theta-\varpi)=
\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\br{A_r\frac{1}{kr}\cos kL}\,\frac{\sin rm}{r}, \tag{35}
\]
where, to abbreviate, we have written
\[
\cos^{-1}\left\{
\frac{\half(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})-e}
{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})}
\right\}=L \tag{36},
\]
\[
\frac{\{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\}^{2}}{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}=L'. \tag{37}
\]
Forming the analogous expressions for
\[
\cos k(\theta'-\varpi'), \qquad \sin k(\theta'-\varpi'),
\]

\clearpage

substituting in
\[
\begin{aligned}
\cos k(\theta-\theta')={}&
\cos k(\varpi-\varpi')\{\cos k(\theta-\varpi)\cos k(\theta'-\varpi')
+\sin k(\theta-\varpi)\sin k(\theta'-\varpi')\}\\
&-\sin k(\varpi-\varpi')\{\sin k(\theta-\varpi)\cos k(\theta'-\varpi')
-\sin k(\theta'-\varpi')\cos k(\theta-\varpi)\},
\end{aligned}
\]
and reducing the whole to multiple cosines, the final result takes the very simple form
\[
\cos k(\theta-\theta')=
\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cos\{r'm'-rm+k(\varpi-\varpi')\}
\br{A_rA'_{r'}\cos kL\cos kL'\left(L-\frac1{kr}\right)
\left(L'-\frac1{kr'}\right)}. \tag{38}
\]

Again, formulae analogous to (14), (18), may be deduced from the equation
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(m_1,m_2\ldots)
={}&\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots\Bigg\{
\cos(r_1m_1+r_2m_2\ldots)
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\dd m_1}{2\pi}
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\dd m_2}{2\pi}\ldots\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\times
\cos(r_1m_1+r_2m_2\ldots)\Gamma(m_1,m_2\ldots)\\
&+\sin(r_1m_1+r_2m_2\ldots)
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\dd m_1}{2\pi}
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\dd m_2}{2\pi}\ldots\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\times
\sin(r_1m_1+r_2m_2\ldots)\Gamma(m_1,m_2\ldots)\Bigg\},
\end{aligned}\tag{39}
\end{equation}
which holds from $m_i=0$ to $m_i=2\pi$, \&c., but in many cases universally. In this
case, writing for $\Gamma(m_1,m_2\ldots)$ the function
\[
\Pi\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}}
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}-e\sin u\sqrt{-1}}{1-e\cos u}\right\}
f\{e^{u_1\sqrt{-1}},e^{u_2\sqrt{-1}}\ldots\} \tag{40};
\]
and observing
\[
\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}-e\sin u\sqrt{-1}}{1-e\cos u}
=\frac{1+\lambda e^{-u\sqrt{-1}}}{1-\lambda e^{-u\sqrt{-1}}}
=1+2\Sigma_{1}^{\infty}\{\cos su-\sqrt{-1}\sin su\}\lambda^{s} \tag{41},
\]
an exactly similar analysis, (except that in the expansion
\[
\Gamma(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ldots)=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots
A_{s_1,s_2,\ldots}\lambda_1^{s_1}\lambda_2^{s_2}\ldots,
\]
the supposition is not made that $A_{s_1,s_2,\ldots}=A_{-s_1,-s_2,\ldots}$), leads to the result
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&f\{e^{u_1\sqrt{-1}},e^{u_2\sqrt{-1}}\ldots\}
\Pi\left\{\frac{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}-e\sin u\sqrt{-1}}
{\sqrt{-1}\,e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\chi'\{e^{u\sqrt{-1}}\}(1-e\cos u)}\right\}\\
&=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots\left\{
\cos(r_1m_1+r_2m_2\ldots)\br{2^{n}\cos(r_1\chi\lambda_1+r_2\chi\lambda_2\ldots)f(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ldots)}\right.\\
&\qquad\left.
+\sin(r_1m_1+r_2m_2\ldots)\br{2^{n}\sin(r_1\chi\lambda_1+r_2\chi\lambda_2\ldots)f(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ldots)}
\right\},
\end{aligned}\tag{42}
\end{equation}
$(n)$ being the number of variables $u_1,u_2\ldots$. Hence also
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&f\{e^{u_1\sqrt{-1}},e^{u_2\sqrt{-1}}\ldots\}\\
&=\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Sigma_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ldots
\left\{
\cos(r_1m_1+\ldots)\cos(r_1\chi\lambda_1+\ldots)
\br{\Pi\left\{
\frac{2\sqrt{-1}\chi'\lambda\{1-\half e(\lambda+\lambda^{-1})\}}
{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}-\half e(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})}\right\}f(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ldots)}
\right.\\
&\qquad\left.

\begin{center}
{\Large\textbf{5.}}\par
\bigskip
{\Large ON THE INTERSECTION OF CURVES.}
\end{center}

\medskip
\begin{center}
\textit{[From the Cambridge Mathematical Journal, vol.\ \textsc{iii}.\ (1843), pp.~211--213.]}
\end{center}

\bigskip

\noindent\textsc{The} following theorem is quoted in a note of Chasles' Aper\c{c}u Historique \&c.,
\textit{M\'emoires de Bruxelles}, tom.~XI. p.~149, where M. Chasles employs it in the
demonstration of Pascal's theorem: ``If a curve of the third order pass through eight
of the points of intersection of two curves of the third order, it passes through the
ninth point of intersection.'' The application in question is so elegant, that it deserves
to be generally known. Consider a hexagon inscribed in a conic section. The aggregate
of three alternate sides may be looked upon as forming a curve of the third order, and
that of the remaining sides, a second curve of the same order. These two intersect in
nine points, viz.\ the six angular points of the hexagon, and the three points which are
the intersections of pairs of opposite sides. Suppose a curve of the third order passing
through eight of these points, viz.\ the aggregate of the conic section passing through
the angular points of the hexagon, and of the line joining two of the three intersections
of pairs of opposite sides. This passes through the ninth point, by the theorem of
Chasles, i.e.\ the three intersections of pairs of opposite sides lie in the same straight
line, (since obviously the third intersection does not lie in the conic section); which is
Pascal's theorem.

The demonstration of the above property of curves of the third order is one of
extreme simplicity. Let $U=0$, $V=0$, be the equations of two curves of the third
order, the curve of the same order which passes through eight of their points of
intersection (which may be considered as eight perfectly arbitrary points), and a ninth
arbitrary point, will be perfectly determinate. Let $U_0$, $V_0$, be the values of $U,V$,
when the coordinates of this last point are written in place of $x,y$. Then
$UV_0-U_0V=0$, satisfies the above conditions, or it is the equation to the curve
required; but it is an equation which is satisfied by all the nine points of intersection
of the two curves, i.e.\ any curve that passes through eight of these points of
intersection, passes also through the ninth.

\clearpage

Consider generally two curves, $U_m=0$, $V_n=0$, of the orders $m$ and $n$
respectively, and a curve of the $r^{\text{th}}$ order ($r$ not less than $m$ or $n$)
passing through the $mn$ points of intersection. The equation to such a curve will be
of the form
\[
U=u_{r-m}U_m+v_{r-n}V_n=0,
\]
$u_{r-m}$, $v_{r-n}$, denoting two polynomes of the orders $r-m$, $r-n$, with all
their coefficients complete. It would at first sight appear that the curve $U=0$ might
be made to pass through as many as
$\{1+2\ldots+(r-m+1)\}+\{1+2\ldots(r-n+1)\}-1$, arbitrary points, i.e.
\[
\half(r-m+1)(r-m+2)+\half(r-n+1)(r-n+2)-1;
\]
or, what is the same thing,
\[
\half r(r+3)-mn+\half(r-m-n+1)(r-m-n+2)
\]
arbitrary points, such being apparently the number of disposable constants. This is in
fact the case as long as $r$ is not greater than $m+n-1$; but when $r$ exceeds this,
there arise, between the polynomes which multiply the disposable coefficients, certain
linear relations which cause them to group themselves into a smaller number of
disposable quantities. Thus, if $r$ be not less than $m+n$, forming different
polynomes of the form $x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}V_n$ [$\alpha+\beta=$ or $<m$], and
multiplying by the coefficients of $x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}$ in $U_m$ and adding, we obtain
a sum $U_mV_n$, which might have been obtained by taking the different polynomes
of the form $x^{\gamma}y^{\delta}U_m$ [$\gamma+\delta=$ or $<n$], multiplying by
the coefficients of $x^{\gamma}y^{\delta}$ in $V_n$, and adding: or we have a linear
relation between the different polynomes of the forms $x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}V_n$, and
$x^{\gamma}y^{\delta}U_m$. In the case where $r$ is not less than $m+n+1$, there
are two more such relations, viz.\ those obtained in the same way from the different
polynomes $x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}\cdot xV_n$, $x^{\gamma}y^{\delta}\cdot xU_m$, and
$x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}\cdot yV_n$, $x^{\gamma}y^{\delta}\cdot yU_m$, \&c.; and in
general, whatever be the excess of $r$ above $m+n-1$, the number of these linear
relations is
\[
1+2\ldots(r-m-n+1)=\half(r-m-n+1)(r-m-n+2).
\]
Hence, if $r$ be not less than $m+n$, the number of points through which a curve of
the $r^{\text{th}}$ order may be made to pass, in addition to the $mn$ points which are
the intersections of $U_m=0$, $V_n=0$, is simply $\half r(r+3)-mn$. In the case of
$r=m+n-1$, or $r=m+n-2$, the two formulae coincide. Hence we may enunciate the
theorem

``A curve of the $r^{\text{th}}$ order, passing through the $mn$ points of intersection
of two curves of the $m^{\text{th}}$ and $n^{\text{th}}$ orders respectively, may be
made to pass through
\[
\half r(r+3)-mn+\half(m+n-r-1)(m+n-r-2)
\]
arbitrary points, if $r$ be not greater than $m+n-3$: if $r$ be greater than this
value, it may be made to pass through $\half r(r+3)-mn$ points only.''

Suppose $r$ not greater than $m+n-3$, and a curve of the $r^{\text{th}}$ order made
to pass through
\[
\half r(r+3)-mn+\half(m+n-r-1)(m+n-r-2)
\]
arbitrary points, and
\[
mn-\half(m+n-r-1)(m+n-r-2)
\]

\clearpage

\noindent of the $mn$ points of intersection above. Such a curve passes through
$\half r(r+3)$ given points, and though the
$mn-\half(m+n-r-1)(m+n-r-2)$ latter points are not perfectly arbitrary, there appears
to be no reason why the relation between the positions of these points should be such
as to prevent the curve from being completely determined by these conditions. But if
it be so, then the curve must pass through the remaining
$\half(m+n-r-1)(m+n-r-2)$ points of intersection, or we have the theorem

``If a curve of the $r^{\text{th}}$ order ($r$ not less than $m$ or $n$, not greater
than $m+n-3$) pass through
\[
mn-\half(m+n-r-1)(m+n-r-2)
\]
of the points of intersection of two curves of the $m^{\text{th}}$ and $n^{\text{th}}$
orders respectively, it passes through the remaining
\[
\half(m+n-r-1)(m+n-r-2)
\]
points of intersection.''
