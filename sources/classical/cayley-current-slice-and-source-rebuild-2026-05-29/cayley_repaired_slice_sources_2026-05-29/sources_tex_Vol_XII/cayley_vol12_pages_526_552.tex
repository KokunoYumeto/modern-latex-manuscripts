\section*{869 (continued). On the Transformation of Elliptic Functions.}

\noindent (Continuation, beginning of memoir on printed page 506. The opening pages of paper 869 occupy PDF pp.\ 526--552. The introductory matter of the memoir---the announcement of the $p\alpha\beta$-form, the comparison with Jacobi's results, and the survey of the eight sections to come---fills the present page.)

\medskip

\noindent where $\Pi_1,\Pi_2,\Pi_3,\ldots$ denote given functions of $\rho$, $\alpha$, $\beta$. Taking $n$ odd and $=2s+1$, we assume for $y$ an expression
\[
y \;=\; \frac{x(A_1 + A_{s-1}x^2 + \cdots + A_1 x^{2s-2} + x^{2s})}{1 + A_1 x^2 + \cdots + A_{s-1} x^{2s-2} + A_s x^{2s}}\,,
\]
where the last coefficient $A_s$ is at once seen to be $=\rho$. Comparing with the series-value $y = \rho x(1 + \Pi_1 x^2 + \Pi_2 x^4 + \cdots)$, we have an infinite series of equations. The first of these is, in fact, $A_s = \rho$; the next equations give linearly $A_{s-1}, A_{s-2}, \ldots, A_1$ in terms of the coefficients $\Pi$; that is, of $\rho$, $\alpha$, $\beta$: the two which follow serve in effect to determine $\rho$, $\beta$ as functions of $\alpha$: and then, $\rho$ and $\beta$ having these values, all the remaining equations will be satisfied identically.

\medskip

\noindent The process is an eminently practical one, so far as regards the determination of the coefficients $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{s-1}$ as functions of $\rho$, $\alpha$, $\beta$; it is less so, and requires eliminations more or less complicated, as regards the determination of the relations between $\rho$, $\alpha$, $\beta$. As to this, it may be remarked that the problem is not so much the determination of the equation between $\rho$ and $\alpha$ (or say the $p\alpha$-multiplier equation, or simply the $p\alpha$-equation), and of the equation between $\beta$, $\alpha$ (or say the $\alpha\beta$-modular equation, or simply the $\alpha\beta$-equation), as it is to determine the complete system of relations between $\rho$, $\alpha$, $\beta$; treating these as coordinates, we have what may be called the multiplier-modular-curve, or say the MM-curve, and the relations in question are those which determine this curve.

\medskip

\noindent In the absence of special exceptions, it follows from general principles that the coefficients $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{s-1}$, qua rational functions of $\rho$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, must also be rational functions of $\alpha$, $\beta$ or of $\alpha$, $\rho$; and I think it may be assumed that this is the case; the method, however, affords but little assistance towards thus expressing them.

\medskip

\noindent In connexion with the foregoing theory, I consider the solutions of the problem of transformation given by Jacobi's partial differential equation (``Suite de Notices sur les Fonctions elliptiques,'' \textit{Crelle}, t.~\textsc{iv}.\ (1829), pp.~185--193), and by what I call the Jacobi--Brioschi differential equations. The first and third of these were obtained by Jacobi in the memoir\footnote{\textit{Ges. Werke}, bd.~i., pp.~295--318; in particular, p.~303.}, ``De functionibus ellipticis Commentatio,'' \textit{Crelle}, t.~\textsc{iv}.\ (1829), pp.~371--390 (see p.~376); but the second equation, which completes the system, was, I believe, first given by Brioschi in the second appendix to his translation of my \textit{Elliptic Functions: Trattato elementare delle Funzioni ellittiche}: Milan, 1880. I had, strangely enough, overlooked the great importance of these equations. I shall have occasion also to refer to results, and further develop the theory contained in my memoir, ``On the Transformation of Elliptic Functions,'' \textit{Phil. Trans.}, t.~\textsc{clxiv}.\ (1874), pp.~397--456, [578], and the addition thereto, \textit{Phil. Trans.}, t.~\textsc{clxxxix}.\ (1878), pp.~419--424, [692].

\medskip

\noindent I remark that, while I have only worked out the formul\ae{} for the cases $n=3$ and $n=5$, and a few formul\ae{} for the case $n=7$, the memoir is intended to be a contribution to the general theory of the $p\alpha\beta$-transformation; I hope to be able to complete the theory for the case $n=7$.

\section*{Comparison of the $Mk\lambda$- and $p\alpha\beta$-Forms. The Modular and Multiplier Equations. Art.\ Nos.\ 1 to 12.}

\noindent 1. The equation
\[
\frac{M\,dy}{\sqrt{1-y^2}\cdot\sqrt{1-\lambda^2 y^2}} \;=\; \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}\cdot\sqrt{1-k^2 x^2}}\,,
\]
if we write therein
\[
x = \frac{x}{\sqrt{k}}\,,\quad y = \frac{y}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\,,\quad M = \frac{M}{\rho}\,,
\]
becomes
\[
\frac{M\,dy}{\sqrt{1-(\lambda^{-1}+\lambda)y^2 + y^4}} \;=\; \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-(k^{-1}+k)x^2 + x^4}}\,;
\]
viz., this is
\[
\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1 - 2\beta y^2 + y^4}} \;=\; \frac{\rho\,dx}{\sqrt{1 - 2\alpha x^2 + x^4}}\,,
\]
if only
\[
2\alpha = k^{-1} + k\,,\quad 2\beta = \lambda^{-1} + \lambda\,,\quad \rho = \frac{1}{M}\,.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 2. We have a $u v$-modular equation, see ante my Transformation Memoir*, p.\ 450, this may be converted into a $uw$-modular equation; in particular, $n=3$, the equation is
\[
y^4 + 6x^2 y^2 + x^4 - 4xy\,(4x^2y^2 - 3x^2 - 3y^2 + 4) = 0,
\]
where $x$, $y$ denote $u^2$, $v^2$ respectively; say the equation is
\[
F(x,\,y) \;=\; x^4 + x^2(-16y^2 + 12y) + x^2\,(6y^2 + x(12y^2 - 16y)) + y^4 = 0.
\]
From the equation $F(x,\,y) = 0$, we derive
\[
x^{-2} F(x,\,y)\,,\quad x^{-2} F(x,\,y^{-1}) = 0\,;
\]
say this is
\[
(Ax^2 + Bx + C + Dx^{-1} + Ex^{-2})\,(A'x^2 + Bx + C' + D'x^{-1} + E'x^{-2}) = 0,
\]
viz.\ the equation is
\[
AA'x^4 + (AB' + A'B)x^3 + \cdots + EE'x^{-4} = 0\,;
\]
where, by reason of the symmetry of $F(x,\,y)$, the coefficients $AA'$, $EE'$ of $x^4$, $x^{-4}$, those of $x^3$, $x^{-3}$, \&c., have equal values; the form thus is
\[
\mathfrak{A}\,(x^4 + x^{-4}) + \mathfrak{B}\,(x^3 + x^{-3}) + \mathfrak{C}\,(x^2 + x^{-2}) + \mathfrak{D}\,(x + x^{-1}) + \mathfrak{E} \;=\; 0,
\]
where $x + x^{-1} = 2\alpha$,
\[
x^2 + x^{-2} = 2\alpha\,,
\]
\[
x^3 + x^{-3} = 8\alpha^3 - 6\alpha\,,
\]
\[
x^4 + x^{-4} = 16\alpha^4 - 16\alpha^2 + 2.
\]

\]
or, finally, $\mathfrak{B} = -128\beta^3 + 120\beta$; and so for the other coefficients. The numerical coefficients contain, all of them, the factor $16$; and, throwing this out, we obtain, for $n=3$, the $\alpha\beta$-modular equation in the form

\medskip

\noindent 4. We have a $M u$-multiplier equation of the form $F\!\bigl(\frac{1}{M},\,2\alpha^2 - 1\bigr) = 0$ (see Memoir*, pp.\ 420--422), but we cannot, by the preceding formul\ae{}, deduce thence a $p\alpha$-multiplier equation; in fact, writing therein $\frac{1}{M} = \frac{\alpha^2\rho}{x^2}$, the resulting equation is
\[
F\!\left(\frac{\alpha^2\rho}{x^2},\;1 - 2x^2\right) = 0,
\]
which is a $\rho\alpha$-multiplier equation only on the assumption that $1 - 2x^2$, $\alpha^2$ and $x^2$ are therein regarded as given functions of $\alpha$. But it is very

\hfill {\footnotesize [* This Collection, vol.~\textsc{x}., pp.\ 384, 385.]}

\newpage

\noindent remarkable that the $\rho\alpha$-equation, in fact, is $F(\rho,\,\alpha) = 0$. To prove this, assume that the equation
\[
\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1-2\beta y^2 + y^4}} \;=\; \frac{\rho\,dx}{\sqrt{1-2\alpha x^2 + x^4}}
\]
has a $\rho\alpha$-multiplier equation $F(\rho,\,\alpha) = 0$. Starting from the equation
\[
\frac{M\,dy}{\sqrt{1-y^2}\cdot\sqrt{1-\lambda^2 y^2}} \;=\; \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}\cdot\sqrt{1-k^2 x^2}},
\]
we may, by effecting on each side a quadric transformation, convert this into
\[
\frac{M^{-1}dy}{\sqrt{1 - 2(2\beta^2 - 1)y^2 + y^4}} \;=\; \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - 2(2\alpha^2 - 1)x^2 + x^4}}\,;
\]
and this being so, we have, between $M^{-1}$ and $2\alpha^2 - 1$, the relation
\[
F\!\left(\frac{1}{M},\;2\alpha^2 - 1\right) = 0;
\]
or, conversely, if this be the form of the $Mu$-multiplier equation, then the $\rho\alpha$-multiplier equation is $F(\rho,\,\alpha) = 0$.

\medskip

\noindent 5. The quadric transformations are
\[
x = \frac{\sqrt{1-x^2}}{x\sqrt{1-k^2 x^2}}\,,\qquad y = \frac{\sqrt{1-y^2}}{y\sqrt{1-\lambda^2 y^2}}\,.
\]
We have then only to show that
\[
\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - 2(2\alpha^2 - 1)x^2 + x^4}} \;=\; \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}\cdot\sqrt{1-k^2 x^2}}\,,
\]
for then, in like manner,
\[
\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1 - 2(2\beta^2 - 1)y^2 + y^4}} \;=\; \frac{dy}{\sqrt{1-y^2}\cdot\sqrt{1-\lambda^2 y^2}}\,,
\]
and we pass from the assumed differential relation between $x$, $y$ to the above-mentioned differential equation between $x$, $y$.

\medskip

\noindent 6. For the quadric transformation between $x$, $x$, write
\[
\theta^k = k - ik'\,,\qquad \theta^{-k} = k + ik'\,,
\]
\Big(whence also $\theta = \dfrac{k-ik'}{k+ik'}$\Big), and therefore
\[
\theta^k + \theta^{-k} = 2k\,,\qquad \theta + \theta^{-1} = 2k^2 - 2k'^2;
\]
we have
\[
1 - \theta^k x^2 = 1 - \theta\,\frac{1 - x^2}{x^2(1 - k^2 x^2)} \;=\; \frac{1}{x^2(1 - k^2 x^2)}\bigl(\theta + (\theta+1)x^2 - k^2 x^4\bigr),
\]
\[
\;=\; \frac{-\theta}{x^2(1 - k^2 x^2)}\,(1 - \theta^{-k} k^2 x^2);
\]

\newpage

\noindent and similarly,
\[
1 - \theta^{-k} x^2 \;=\; \frac{-\theta^{-1}}{x^2(1 - k^2 x^2)}\,(1 - \theta^k k^2 x^2).
\]
Consequently,
\[
(1 - \theta^k x^2)(1 - \theta^{-k} x^2) \;=\; 1 - 2(2k^2 - 1)x^2 + x^4 \;=\; \frac{1}{x^4(1 - k^2 x^2)^2}\,(1 - 2k^2 x^2 + k^2 x^4)^2\,;
\]
or say
\[
\sqrt{1 - 2(2k^2 - 1)x^2 + x^4} \;=\; \frac{1}{x^2(1 - k^2 x^2)}\,(1 - 2k^2 x^2 + k^2 x^4).
\]
Moreover,
\[
dx \;=\; \frac{dx}{x^2(1-x^2)^{1/2}(1-k^2 x^2)^{1/2}}\,(1 - 2k^2 x^2 + k^2 x^4),
\]
and thence the required equation
\[
\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - 2(1 - 2k^2)x^2 + x^4}} \;=\; \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}\cdot\sqrt{1-k^2 x^2}}\,;
\]
this completes the proof.

\medskip

\noindent 7. Thus, referring to the $Mu$-equations given in the place referred to, we obtain the following $\rho\alpha$-multiplier equations. When $n=3$, we have
\[
\rho^4 - 6\rho^2 - 8\alpha\rho - 3 = 0.
\]
This may be written in the forms
\[
8\alpha\rho \;=\; \rho^4 - 6\rho^2 - 3,
\]
\[
8(\alpha+1)\rho \;=\; (\rho-1)^3(\rho+3),
\]
\[
8(\alpha-1)\rho \;=\; (\rho+1)^3(\rho-3).
\]

\noindent Next, for $n=5$, we have $\rho^6 - 10\rho^5 + 35\rho^4 - 60\rho^3 + 55\rho^2 + (38 - 64\alpha^2)\rho + 5 = 0$.

\noindent This may be written in the two forms
\[
64\alpha\rho \;=\; (\rho^2 - 4\rho - 1)(\rho^2 - 2\rho + 5)^2
\]
and
\[
64(\alpha^2 - 1)\rho \;=\; (\rho - 1)^5(\rho - 5).
\]

\noindent And, for $n=7$, we have
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\rho^8 - 28\rho^6 - 112\alpha\rho^5 - 210\rho^4 - 224\alpha\rho^3 + (-1484 + 1344\alpha^2)\rho^2 + (-560\alpha + 512\alpha^3)\rho + 7 = 0.
$}
\]

\medskip

\noindent 8. The relation between $\rho$ and $\beta$, or say the $\rho\beta$-multiplier equation, may be obtained by a known property of elliptic functions; viz.\ writing $\rho\sigma = \pm n$ (the sign is $-$ for $n=3$, $n=7$, or generally for any prime value $4p+3$: and it is $+$ for $n=5$ and generally for any prime value $4p+1$), then we have between $\sigma$, $\beta$ the same relation as between $\rho$, $\alpha$. Thus, if $n=3$, $\sigma = -\dfrac{3}{\rho}$, for $\rho$, $\alpha$ writing $\sigma$, $\beta$, the equation is
\[
\sigma^4 - 6\sigma^2 - 8\beta\sigma - 3 = 0\,;
\]
or, as this may be written,
\[
\rho^4 + 8\beta\rho^3 + 18\rho^2 - 27 = 0\,;
\]
and so for the other cases; but it is perhaps more convenient to retain the $\sigma$; thus, if $n=5$, $\sigma = \dfrac{5}{\rho}$, we have
\[
\sigma^6 - 10\sigma^5 + 35\sigma^4 - 60\sigma^3 + 55\sigma^2 + (38 - 64\beta^2)\sigma + 5 = 0.
\]

\newpage

\noindent 9. We are hence able to express $\beta$ as a rational function of $\rho$, $\alpha$. We, in fact, have
\[
\sqrt{\rho}\,8\beta \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}(\rho^2 - 4\rho - 1)\sqrt{\rho^2 - 2\rho + 5}\,,\qquad 8\beta \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}(\sigma^2 - 4\sigma - 1)\sqrt{\sigma^2 - 2\sigma + 5}\,,
\]
(the signs must be opposite), and then for $\sigma$, substituting its value $=\frac{5}{\rho}$, and observing that $\sigma^2 - 2\sigma + 5$ is thus $=\frac{5}{\rho^2}(\rho^2 - 2\rho + 5)$, we find
\[
\frac{\beta}{\alpha} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2 + 20\rho - 25}{\rho^2(\rho^2 - 4\rho - 1)},
\]
which is the required formula.

\medskip

\noindent Observe that, for $\rho = \sigma = \sqrt{5}$, the formul\ae{} with the sign $-$, as above, give $\beta = -\alpha$, whereas with the sign $+$ they would have given $\beta = \alpha$. For the value in question, $\rho = \sqrt{5}$, the equation
\[
64\alpha^2 \;=\; -\,(\rho^2 - 4\rho - 1)^2(\rho^2 - 2\rho + 5),
\]
gives
\[
64\alpha^2 \;=\; -\,16\,(1 - \sqrt{5})^2(10 - 2\sqrt{5})\,;
\]
that is,
\[
\alpha^2 \;=\; -\,\tfrac{1}{4}\,(1 - \sqrt{5})^2(5 - \sqrt{5})\,,\;=\;(3 - \sqrt{5})(\sqrt{5} - 1)\,;
\]
that is, $\alpha^2 = -8 + 4\sqrt{5}$, or $\alpha^4 + 16\alpha^2 - 16 = 0$; it appears, \textit{ante} No.~3, that this value belongs to the case $\beta = -\alpha$ and not to $\beta = \alpha$.

\medskip

\noindent 10. But there is another way of arriving at a formula containing $\beta$. Starting from Jacobi's equation
\[
n M^k \;=\; \frac{\lambda \lambda'^2}{k k'^2}\cdot\frac{d k}{d\lambda},
\]
and introducing for $\lambda$, $\lambda'$, $k$, $k'$, $M$ their values in terms of $u$, $v$, we have
\[
\frac{n v^k}{u^k\rho^k} \;=\; \frac{v^4 (1 - v^8)}{u^4 (1 - u^8) v^4 \rho^4}\,\frac{d v}{d u},
\]
that is,
\[
\frac{d v}{d u} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2 u^3 (1 - v^8)}{n v^3 (1 - u^8)}\,;
\]
but, from the values of $\alpha$, $\beta$, we find
\[
\frac{d v}{d u} \;=\; \frac{v^5 (1 - u^8)}{u^5 (1 - v^8)}\,\frac{d\beta}{d\alpha},
\]
and, combining these results,
\[
\frac{d\beta}{d\alpha} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2 u^8}{n v^8}\cdot\frac{(1 - v^8)^2}{(1 - u^8)^2} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2}{n}\cdot\frac{(v^4 - v^{-4})^2}{(u^4 - u^{-4})^2}\,;
\]

\newpage

\noindent that is,
\[
\frac{d\beta}{d\alpha} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2}{n}\cdot\frac{\beta^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 - 1}.
\]
We have, consequently,
\[
\frac{d\beta}{\beta^2 - 1} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2\,d\alpha}{n(\alpha^2 - 1)},
\]
and therefore
\[
\tfrac{1}{2}\log\frac{\beta - 1}{\beta + 1} \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\int\frac{\rho^2\,d\alpha}{\alpha^2 - 1}\,,
\]
where $\rho^2$ must be regarded as a function of $\alpha$, or $\alpha$ of $\rho$; and from the form of the equation, it appears that the integral must be expressible as the logarithm of an algebraic function of $\rho$, $\alpha$.

\medskip

\noindent 11. Thus, when $n=3$, we have
\[
8\alpha \;=\; \rho^3 - 6\rho - \frac{3}{\rho}\,;
\]
\[
8\,\frac{d\alpha}{d\rho} \;=\; 3\rho^2 - 6 + \frac{3}{\rho^2} \;=\; \frac{3}{\rho^2}\,(\rho^2 - 1)^2\,,
\]
and thence easily
\[
\tfrac{1}{2}\log\frac{\beta - 1}{\beta + 1} \;=\; \int\frac{8\rho\,d\rho}{\rho^2 - 1\cdot\rho^2 - 9} \;=\; -\int\frac{d\rho}{\rho^2 - 1} + 9\int\frac{d\rho}{\rho^2 - 9}
\]
\[
\;=\; -\tfrac{1}{2}\log\frac{\rho - 1}{\rho + 1} + \tfrac{3}{2}\log\frac{\rho - 3}{\rho + 3}\,;
\]
that is,
\[
\frac{\beta - 1}{\beta + 1} \;=\; \frac{(\rho - 3)^2(\rho + 1)}{(\rho + 3)^2(\rho - 1)}\,,
\]
as may be at once verified.

\medskip

\noindent 12. In the case $n=5$, I verify the equation under the form
\[
\frac{d\beta}{\beta^2 - 1} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2}{5}\cdot\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^2 - 1}.
\]
From the equations
\[
64(\alpha^2 - 1) \;=\; -(\rho - 1)^5(\rho - 5)\,,\quad\text{and}\quad 8\alpha \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\,(\rho^2 - 4\rho - 1)\sqrt{\rho^2 - 2\rho + 5}\,,
\]
we have
\[
\frac{128\alpha\,d\alpha}{\alpha^2 - 1} \;=\; \frac{5\,(\rho^2 - 4\rho - 1)\,d\rho}{\rho\,(\rho - 1)(\rho - 5)}\,,
\]
and thence
\[
\frac{16\,d\alpha}{\alpha^2 - 1} \;=\; \frac{5\,d\rho}{(\rho - 1)(\rho - 5)\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\rho^2 - 2\rho + 5}}\,.
\]
Similarly, observing the $-$ sign of $8\beta$, we have
\[
\frac{16\,d\beta}{\beta^2 - 1} \;=\; \frac{-5\,d\sigma}{(\sigma - 1)(\sigma - 5)\sqrt{\sigma}\sqrt{\sigma^2 - 2\sigma + 5}}\,,
\]

\newpage

\noindent whence, substituting for $\sigma$ its value $=\dfrac{5}{\rho}$, we have
\[

\]
which is right.

\section*{Connexion of the $Mk\lambda$- and $p\alpha\beta$-Theories. Order of Modular Equation. Art.\ Nos.\ 13 to 18.}

\noindent 13. In the Transformation Memoir [578], starting from the equation

where $s = \tfrac{1}{2}(n-1)$, as before. For instance, when $n=7$, $P = \alpha + 7\chi^2$, $Q = \beta + \delta\chi^2$.

\medskip

\noindent If, solving for $y$, we then for $x$, $y$ write $\dfrac{1}{kx}$, $\dfrac{1}{\lambda y}$, we find

\noindent 15. with the like equations between $S_1$, $\alpha$, $\rho$. It will be recollected that
\[
R_1 \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha\,,\quad \alpha \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\bigl(u^2 + \dfrac{1}{u^2}\bigr)\,;
\]
hence
\[
(R_1 + 1) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\Bigl(u + \dfrac{1}{u}\Bigr)^2\,,\quad (R_1 - 1) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\Bigl(u - \dfrac{1}{u}\Bigr)^2\,.
\]
The formul\ae{} just obtained are useful for obtaining the $u v$-modular equation from the foregoing equations; or say
\[
4\,\Bigl(v^4 + \frac{1}{v^4}\Bigr)\sigma \;=\; \sigma^4 - 6\sigma^2 - 3,
\]
where $\rho\sigma = -3$, and we have to eliminate $\rho$ and $\sigma$; the elimination gives
\[
\frac{u^2}{v^2} - \frac{v^2}{u^2} + 2 v u - \frac{2}{u v} \;=\; 0,
\]
that is,
\[
v^4 + 2 v^3 u^3 - 2 v u^4 - u^4 \;=\; 0.
\]

\section*{The Quintic Transformation, $n=5$. Art.\ Nos.\ 29 to 32.}

\noindent 29. We have here
\[
\frac{\rho + A_1 x^2 + x^4}{1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4} \;=\; \rho\,(1 + \Pi_1 x^2 + \Pi_2 x^4 + \cdots),
\]
and multiplying by $1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4$, we obtain an infinite series of equations, the first three of which are
\[
A_1 \;=\; \rho\,\Pi_1 + A_1\rho,
\]
\[
1 \;=\; \rho\,\Pi_2 + A_1\rho\,\Pi_1,
\]
\[
0 \;=\; \rho\,\Pi_3 + A_1\rho\,\Pi_2 + \rho^2\Pi_1,
\]
\[
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots
\]

\newpage

\noindent The first of these gives
\[
A_1 \;=\; \frac{\rho\,\Pi_1}{-\rho + 1}\,,\quad =\;\frac{\Theta_1 - \rho^2 + 1}{-\rho + 1}\,;
\]
and the other two equations then determine the MM-curve. These being satisfied, the remaining equations will be satisfied identically. It is proper to introduce $\Theta_1, \Theta_2, \Theta_3$ into the equations instead of $\Pi_1, \Pi_2, \Pi_3$. We have first
\[
1 \;=\; \rho\,\Pi_2 + \rho\,\Pi_1\cdot\frac{\Theta_1 - \rho^2 + 1}{-\rho + 1} + \rho^2,
\]
that is,
\[
0 \;=\; (\Theta_1 - \rho^2 + 1)\,\Theta_1 + (-\rho + 1)\,(\rho\,\Pi_2 + \rho^2 - 1)\,;
\]
viz.\ this is
\[
0 \;=\; (\Theta_1 - \rho^2 + 1)\,\Theta_1 + (-\rho + 1)\,\Theta_2 + \rho^2 - 1\,;
\]
or, finally, this is
\[
\rho\,(\rho - 1)\,\Theta_1 - \Theta_1^2 - \Theta_2 + 2\Theta_1\,(\rho - 1) \;=\; 0.
\]

\medskip

\noindent Secondly, we have
\[
0 \;=\; \rho\,\Pi_3 + A_1\rho\,\Pi_2 + \rho^2\Pi_1,
\]
that is,
\[
0 \;=\; \Theta_3\,(-\rho + 1) + \Theta_1^2 + \Theta_1\,(\rho^2 - \rho) - \Theta_2\,(\rho^2 - 1) \;=\; 0,
\]
viz.\ this is
\[
\Theta_3\,(-\rho + 1) + \Theta_1^2 + \Theta_1\,(\rho^2 - \rho) - \Theta_2\,(\rho^2 - 1) \;=\; 0;
\]
or, finally, it is
\[
\Theta_3\,(-\rho + 1) + \Theta_1^2 + \Theta_1\,(\rho^2 - \rho) - \Theta_2\,(\rho^2 - 1) \;=\; 0.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 30. We have thus the two equations
\[
(\rho - \rho)\Theta_1 - \Theta_1^2 + 2\Theta_1\,(\rho - 1) \;=\; 0,
\]
\[
\Theta_3\,(-\rho + 1) + \Theta_1^2 + \Theta_1\,(\rho^2 - \rho) - \Theta_2\,(\rho^2 - 1) \;=\; 0\,;
\]
and recollecting that $\Theta_3$ is of the form $L\,\Theta_1 + M\,\Theta_2$, we see that each of these equations is satisfied if only $\Theta_1 = 0$, $\Theta_2 = 0$ (the formul\ae{} belonging to the cubic transformation). This ought to be the case, for the expression $\dfrac{x(\rho + A_1 x^2 + x^4)}{1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4}$, by writing $A_1 = \rho + 1$, reduce the expression to $\dfrac{x(\rho + x^2)}{1 + \rho x^2}$, which belongs to the cubic transformation (see \textit{ante} No.~17). The equations may be written
\[
\rho^2 \;=\; -\,(2\rho + 2)\,\Theta_1 + \cdots,
\]

\newpage

\noindent 31. The investigation may be presented in a slightly different form by introducing the functions $\Theta$ at an earlier stage; viz.\ writing
\[
\frac{\rho + A_1 x^2 + x^4}{1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4} \;=\; \rho\,(1 + \Pi_1 x^2 + \Pi_2 x^4 + \cdots),
\]
we have
\[
\frac{\rho + A_1 x^2 + x^4}{1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4} \;=\; \rho + \frac{\Theta_1}{1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4}.
\]
Transposing, reducing, and dividing by $x^2$, we have
\[
\Theta_1\,(\rho - 1 + A_1\rho x^2 + \rho x^4) \;=\; \Theta_1\,(1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4),
\]
whence clearly $\rho - 1 + A_1\,(- \rho + 1) = \Theta_1$, giving for $A_1$ the before-mentioned value; and we then have
\[
1 + A_1 x^2 + \rho x^4 \;=\; 1 + (\rho + 1)x^2 + \rho x^4 - \frac{\Theta_1}{\rho - 1}\,x^2 \;=\; (1 + x^2)(1 + \rho x^2) - \frac{\Theta_1}{\rho - 1}\,x^2.
\]
The equation thus becomes
\[
(1 - x^2)(1 + \rho x^2)\,\Theta_1 + \cdots
\]
and expanding the left-hand side, first in the form
\[
x_0 + \frac{\Theta_2 x^2}{(\rho - 1)(1 + x^2)} + \frac{\Theta_2^2 x^4}{(\rho - 1)^2(1 + x^2)^2(1 + \rho x^2)} + \frac{\Theta_2^3 x^6}{(\rho - 1)^3(1 + x^2)^3(1 + \rho x^2)^2} + \cdots,
\]
and then each of these terms separately in powers of $x$, and comparing with $\Pi_1 x^2 + \Pi_2 x^4 + \Pi_3 x^6 + \cdots$, we have the two equations in the last-mentioned form, and an infinite series of other equations, which will be satisfied identically.

\medskip

\noindent 32. The successive coefficients might be called $\Phi_2, \Phi_3, \ldots$; say
\[
\Phi_2 \;=\; (\rho^2 - \rho)\,\Theta_1 - \Theta_1^2 + 2\,(\rho^2 - 1)(\Pi)_1,
\]
\[
\Phi_3 \;=\; (-\rho + 1)\,\Theta_3 + \Theta_1\Theta_2 + (2\Theta_1 + \Theta_1^2)\,\rho,
\]
and similarly for $\Phi_4, \ldots$; and it would then be proper to show \textit{\`a posteriori} that each of the equations $\Phi_4 = 0$, $\Phi_5 = 0$, \ldots is satisfied identically in virtue of the two equations $\Phi_2 = 0$, $\Phi_3 = 0$, or, what is the same thing, that the functions $\Phi_4, \Phi_5, \ldots$ are each of them a linear function (with coefficients which are functions of $\rho$) of the two functions $\Phi_2$ and $\Phi_3$. I do not attempt to do this, nor even to discuss the MM-curve by means of the equations $\Phi_2 = 0$, $\Phi_3 = 0$; but I will obtain equivalent results, and complete the solution by means of the Jacobi--Brioschi equations, in effect reproducing the investigation contained in the third appendix of the \textit{Funzioni ellittiche}.

\section*{The Jacobi--Brioschi Equations. Art.\ Nos.\ 34 to 42.}

\noindent 34. These were obtained for the differential equation
\[
\frac{dx}{\sqrt{a x^4 + b x^3 + c x^2 + d x + e}} \;=\; \frac{dy}{\sqrt{a y^4 + b y^3 + c y^2 + d y + e}}\,,
\]
viz.\ if this be satisfied by $y = U \div V$, where $U$, $V$ are rational and integral functions of $x$ of the degrees $n$ and $n-1$ respectively, then, writing for shortness
\[
\phi \;=\; a x^4 + b x^3 + c x^2 + d x + e,
\]
and using accents to denote differentiation in regard to $x$, the numerator and denominator $U$, $V$ satisfy the equations
\[
(V V'' - V'^2)\,\phi + \tfrac{1}{2}\,V V'\,\phi' + a U^4 + \rho V^2 = 0,
\]
\[
-(V U'' - U'V')\,\phi - \tfrac{1}{2}\,(V U' + UV')\,\phi' + b U^3 + \rho UV = 0,
\]
\[
(U U'' - U'^2)\,\phi + \tfrac{1}{2}\,U U'\,\phi' + e U^2 + \rho U^2 = 0,
\]
where $\rho$ is a function $= \alpha x^2 + b x + c$, with coefficients $\alpha$, $b$, $c$, the values of which have to be determined. By way of verification, observe that, multiplying by $U^2$, $UV$, $V^2$, and adding, we obtain
\[
\frac{V}{V}\,(\cdots\cdots) \;=\;
\]
that is,
\[
-\,(V U' - V'U)^2(\phi) + a x^4 + b x^3 + c x^2 + d x + e + a y^4 + b y^3 + c y^2 + d y + e \;=\; 0,
\]
the result obtained by substituting for $y$ its value $= U \div V$ in the differential equation.

\medskip

\noindent 35. Considering the foregoing special form
\[
\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - 2\alpha x^2 + x^4}} \;=\; \frac{dy}{\rho\sqrt{1 - 2\beta y^2 + y^4}}\,,
\]
so that $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $e$ have the values $\rho^2$, $0$, $-2\beta\rho^2$, $0$, $\rho^2$ and $\phi$ is $= 1 - 2\alpha x^2 + x^4$, the equations are
\[
(V V'' - V'^2)\,\phi + \tfrac{1}{2}\,V V'\,\phi' + \rho^2 U^4 + \rho V^2 = 0,
\]
\[
\cdots\cdots\cdots = 0,
\]

\newpage

\noindent where, writing as before, $n = 2s + 1$, and assuming that the last coefficient, $A_{(2s+1)/2}$ or $A_s$, is $= \rho$, we have
\[
U \;=\; x\,(\rho + A_{s-1}x^2 + A_{s-2}x^4 + \cdots + A_1 x^{2s-2} + x^{2s}),
\]
\[
V \;=\; 1 + A_1 x^2 + A_2 x^4 + \cdots + A_{s-1}x^{2s-2} + \rho x^{2s},
\]
and where, as is easily shown, $\rho$ has the value $=-\bigl[2 A_1 + (2s + 1)\,x^2\bigr]$. In comparing with Brioschi, it will be recollected that $2\alpha$, $2\beta$ are written in place of his $\alpha$, $\beta$.

\medskip

\noindent 36. The equations contain $n$, and they are not satisfied by values of $U$, $V$ belonging to any inferior value of $n$; $U$, $V$ may each of them be multiplied by any common constant factor at pleasure, but not by a common variable factor $P$; viz.\ it is assumed that the fraction $U \div V$ is in its least terms, and consequently that (save as to a constant factor) $U$, $V$ are determinate functions. It is easy to verify that the equations (being verified by $U$, $V$) are not verified by $PU$, $PV$, but it is interesting to show \textit{\`a priori} why this is so. The equations are obtained as follows. Consider the differential equation in the form $\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt{X}} = \dfrac{dy}{\sqrt{Y}}$, and suppose that an integral equation is given in the form $F = 0$ ($F$ a rational and integral function of $x$, $y$); we thence deduce a relation $L\,dx + M\,dy = 0$ between the differentials, and this must agree with the given differential equation; that is, we have $L\sqrt{X} + M\sqrt{Y} = 0$, or, rationalizing, $L^2 X - M^2 Y = 0$; viz.\ this last equation must agree with the equation $F = 0$, or, what is the same thing, $L^2 X - M^2 Y$ must contain $F$ as a factor; say we have
\[
L^2 X - M^2 Y \;=\; F\cdot G,
\]
where $G$ is a function of $x$, $y$. In the particular case where the integral is of the form
\[
y \;=\; U + V,
\]
we have
\[
F \;=\; V y - U,
\]
and we have therefore
\[
L^2 X - M^2 Y \;=\; G\,(V y - U);
\]
and it is by means of this identity that the equations are obtained. But suppose that there is a common factor $P$, or that we have $y = PU \div PV$; then, if we write $F = P V y - P V U$, $= P\,(V y - U)$, there is no necessity that $L^2 X - M^2 Y$ should contain as a factor this expression of $F$, and it will not in fact contain it; all that is necessary is that $L^2 X - M^2 Y$ shall contain the factor $V y - U$; and thus the equations obtained for $U$, $V$ do not apply to $PU$, $PV$. We might, of course, introduce an arbitrary constant factor $\Theta$; contrast herewith the solution by means of the Jacobi partial differential equation, \textit{post} No.~43, where $\Theta$ is not arbitrary but has a determinate value.

\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
-6\rho^2 + 30\rho - 2 A_1 A_3 - 2 A_1\,(2 A_1\rho + A_2^2) - 1\,(2\rho + 2 A_1 A_2) - 2\alpha\,(8 A_1\rho - 4 A_2^2 + 4\,(2 A_1\rho + A_2^2)) + \rho^2\,(2\rho + 2 A_1 A_2) \;=\; 0,
$}
\]
\[
-6\rho^2 + 8 A_1\rho - 4 A_2^2 - 2 A_1\,(2 A_2\rho) + 1\,(2 A_2\rho + A_1^2) - 2\alpha\,(-6\rho^2 + 5\,(2 A_2\rho)) + \rho^2\,(2 A_1\rho + A_2^2) \;=\; 0,
\]
\[
-6\rho^2 - 2 A_1\cdot\rho^2 + 3\,(2 A_2\rho) - 2\alpha\,(-6\rho^2 + 6\cdot\rho^2 + \rho^2\cdot 2 A_1) + \rho^2\cdot 2 A_1 \;=\; 0,
\]
\[
-6\rho^2 + 5\cdot\rho^2 - 2\alpha\,(0 + 7\cdot 0) + \rho^2\cdot 1 \;=\; 0\,;
\]
viz.\ the first, seventh and eighth equations are satisfied identically, and there remain five equations connecting $\rho$, $\alpha$, $A_1$, $A_2$.

\medskip

\noindent These equations\footnote{[See this volume, p.~535.]} should lead to the before-mentioned $\alpha\beta$-modular equation
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\rho^8 - 28\rho^6 - 112\alpha\rho^5 - 210\rho^4 - 224\alpha\rho^3 + (-1484 + 1344\alpha^2)\rho^2 + (-560\alpha + 512\alpha^3)\rho + 7 = 0,
$}
\]
and to expressions for $A_1, A_2$ as rational functions of $\alpha$, $\rho$: and they should be, all five of them, satisfied by these results; but I do not see how the results are to be worked out; there is, so far as appears, no clue to the discovery of the rational functions of $\alpha$, $\rho$.

\section*{The Jacobi Partial Differential Equation. Art.\ Nos.\ 43 to 48.}

\noindent 43. Writing, as above, $2\alpha$ in place of Jacobi's $\alpha$, this is
\[
(1 - 2\alpha x^2 + x^4)\,\frac{d^2 y}{dx^2} + (n - 1)\,(2\alpha x - 2 x^3)\,\frac{d y}{dx} + n\,(n - 1)\,x^2 y - 4 n\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,x\,\frac{d x}{d\alpha} \;=\; 0,
\]
satisfied by the numerator and denominator $U$, $V$, each of them taken with the same proper value of the coefficient $A_1$; or, what is the same thing, by the values
\[
U \;=\; \Theta x\,(A_s + A_{s-1}x^2 + A_{s-2}x^4 + \cdots + A_1 x^{2s-2} + x^{2s}),
\]
\[
V \;=\; \Theta\,(1 + A_1 x^2 + A_2 x^4 + \cdots + A_{s-1}x^{2s-2} + A_s x^{2s}),
\]

\hfill {\footnotesize [* See this volume, p.~535.]}

\newpage

\noindent where $A_s = \rho$ as before: $\Theta$ has its proper value; viz.\ disregarding an arbitrary merely numerical factor which might of course be introduced, the value is
\[
\Theta \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{1}{M}\cdot\frac{\lambda'}{k'}}\,,\quad =\;\sqrt{\frac{v^2\rho}{u^2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{1 - v^4}}{\sqrt{1 - u^4}}}\,,\quad =\;\sqrt{\rho}\cdot\frac{\sqrt[4]{v^4 - v^{-4}}}{\sqrt[4]{u^4 - u^{-4}}}\,,
\]
or, what is the same thing,
\[
\Theta \;=\; \sqrt{\rho}\cdot\sqrt[4]{\frac{\beta^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 - 1}}.
\]
If for $x$ we write $\Theta x$, then the equation becomes
\[
(1 - 2\alpha x^2 + x^4)\,\frac{d^2 V}{dx^2} + (n - 1)\,(2\alpha x - 2 x^3)\,\frac{d V}{dx} + (n - 1)\,x^2 V - 4 n\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\bigl(\frac{d V}{d\alpha} + \frac{1}{\Theta}\,\frac{d\Theta}{d\alpha}\,V\bigr) \;=\; 0,
\]
satisfied by the foregoing values without the factor $\Theta$; or, attending only to the denominator, say by the value
\[
V' \;=\; 1 + A_1 x^2 + A_2 x^4 + \cdots + A_{s-1}x^{2s-2} + \rho x^{2s}.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 44. To calculate the value of $\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\,\dfrac{d\Theta}{d\alpha}$, we have
\[
\frac{1}{\Theta}\,\frac{d\Theta}{d\alpha} \;=\; \frac{1}{\rho}\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} + \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d}{d\alpha}\!\log\,\frac{\beta^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 - 1};
\]
but it has been seen (No.~10) that we have
\[
\frac{d\beta}{d\alpha} \;=\; \frac{\rho^2}{n}\cdot\frac{\beta^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 - 1},
\]
and the formula thus becomes
\[
\frac{1}{\Theta}\,\frac{d\Theta}{d\alpha} \;=\; \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\,\dfrac{d\rho}{d\alpha}\,(\beta\rho^2 - n\alpha)}{\alpha^2 - 1}.
\]
We have, as the first of the equations obtained by substituting in the partial differential equation,
\[
2 A_1 - 4 n\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{1}{\Theta}\,\frac{d\Theta}{d\alpha} \;=\; 0,
\]
and we have hence the value of the first coefficient,
\[
A_1 \;=\; n\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{1}{\rho}\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,(\beta\rho - n\alpha)\,;
\]
or we may, by means of this result, get rid of the term $\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\,\dfrac{d\Theta}{d\alpha}$ from the partial differential equation; viz.\ the equation may be written
\[
(1 - 2\alpha x^2 + x^4)\,\frac{d^2 V}{dx^2} + (n - 1)\,(2\alpha x - 2 x^3)\,\frac{d V}{dx} + \bigl[n\,(n - 1)\,x^2 - 2 A_1\bigr]\,\zeta - 4 n\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{d V}{d\alpha} \;=\; 0.
\]

\newpage

\noindent Before going further, I remark that the last of the equations obtained by the substitution gives the coefficient $A_{s-1}$: but this is also given in terms of $A_1$ by the formula No.~33, $A_{s-1} - \rho A_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\,(\alpha\rho - \beta\rho^3)$; combining the two formulae, we have
\[
A_1 \;=\; \frac{1}{\rho}\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} - \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\rho^2,
\]
\[
A_{s-1} \;=\; n\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} + \bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr)\,\rho + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\rho^3.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 45. In the case $n = 3$, we have $A_{s-1} = A_1 = 1$, $A_s = \rho$; the two equations become
\[
3\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} - \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha\rho - \rho^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\rho^3 \;=\; 0,
\]
\[
3\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} - 1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha\rho + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\rho^3 \;=\; 0,
\]
each of which is easily verified.

\medskip

\noindent I remark also that, in the same case, $(n = 3)$, we have
\[
\rho^4\,\frac{\beta^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 - 1} \;=\; \Bigl(\frac{\rho^2 - 9}{\rho^2 - 1}\Bigr)^2,\quad\text{and thence}\quad \Theta = \sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\frac{\beta^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 - 1}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{\rho^2 - 9}{\rho^2 - 1}}\,;
\]
hence
\[
\frac{1}{\Theta}\,\frac{d\Theta}{d\alpha} \;=\; \frac{4\rho}{(\rho^2 - 1)(\rho^2 - 9)}\,;
\]
and writing the equation $A_1 - 2 n\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\,\dfrac{d\Theta}{d\alpha} = 0$ in the form
\[
\rho - \alpha^2\,n\,\frac{(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\dfrac{1}{\Theta}\,\dfrac{d\Theta}{d\rho}\,\dfrac{d\rho}{d\alpha}}{1} \;=\; 0,
\]
we can verify this equation.

\medskip

\noindent 46. In the case $n = 5$, we have for $A_1$ two equations, each ultimately giving the foregoing value
\[
A_1 \;=\; \frac{1}{8\alpha}\,(\rho^2 - 2\rho + 5)(\rho^2 - 4\rho - 1).
\]
Moreover, the equation $\Theta = \sqrt{\rho}\sqrt[4]{\dfrac{\beta^2 - 1}{\alpha^2 - 1}}$ gives, without difficulty, $\Theta \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\cdot\dfrac{\rho - 5}{\rho - 1}$.

\medskip

\noindent 47. In the case $n = 7$, the formulas give the two coefficients $A_1, A_2$; viz.\ we have
\[
A_1 \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,7\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} - \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\rho^2,
\]
\[
A_2 \;=\; 7\,(\alpha^2 - 1)\,\frac{d\rho}{d\alpha} - \tfrac{1}{2}\alpha\rho + \tfrac{1}{2}\beta\rho^3,
\]
where the value of $\dfrac{d\rho}{d\alpha}$ must of course be obtained from the before-mentioned $\rho\alpha$-equation (given in No.~7). I have not considered these results nor endeavoured to compare them with the results for this case, obtained in the Transformation Memoir and the addition thereto, [578, 692].
