      38. Jacobi s note of 1837, On &quot; the Integration of the Differential Equations of

Dynamics.&quot; Jacobi remarks that it is possible to derive from Lagrange s form of the
equations of motion an important profit for the integration of these equations, and he

refers to his communication of the 29th of November 1839 to the Academy of Berlin,

and to his former note to the Academy of Paris. He proceeds to say, that whenever

the condition of vis viva holds good, he had found that it was possible in the integration
of the equations of motion to follow a course such that each of the given integrals
successively lowers by two unities the order of the system ; and that the like theorem
holds good when the condition of vis viva is not satisfied, that is, when the force
function involves the time (this seems to be a restatement, in a more general form,
of the theorems referred to in the note of the 29th of November 1836 to the

Academy of Berlin) ; and he mentions that he had been, by his researches on the
theory of numbers, led away from composing an extended memoir on the subject. The
note then passes on to other subjects, and it concludes with two theorems, which are
given without demonstration as extracts from the intended work he had before spoken

of. These theorems are in effect as follows :
