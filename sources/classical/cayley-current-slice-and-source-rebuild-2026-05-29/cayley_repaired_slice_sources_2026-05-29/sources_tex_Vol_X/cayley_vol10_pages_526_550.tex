\noindent\textbf{82.}\ \ The product-theorem was
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\alpha,\beta\\ \gamma,\delta\end{pmatrix}\!(u+u')\,.\,\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\alpha',\beta'\\ \gamma',\delta'\end{pmatrix}\!(u-u') = \Sigma\,\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha+\alpha')+p,\ \tfrac{1}{2}(\beta+\beta')+q\\ \gamma+\gamma',\ \delta+\delta'\end{pmatrix}\!(2u)\,.\,\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha-\alpha')+p,\ \tfrac{1}{2}(\beta-\beta')+q\\ \gamma-\gamma',\ \delta-\delta'\end{pmatrix}\!(2u'),$}
\]
where only one argument is exhibited, viz. $u+u'$, $u-u'$, $2u$, $2u'$ are written in place of $(u+u',\ v+v')$, $(u-u',\ v-v')$, $(2u,\ 2v)$, $(2u',\ 2v')$ respectively. The expression on the right-hand side is always a sum of four terms, corresponding to the values $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(1,1)$ of $(p,q)$. \[

\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}\\ \gamma\end{pmatrix} + 2 = i\,\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}\\ \gamma\end{pmatrix},\quad

\[

\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}\\ \gamma\end{pmatrix} - 2 = -i\,\Theta\!\begin{pmatrix}\tfrac{1}{2}\\ \gamma\end{pmatrix},\quad
