\begin{center}\textsc{No.~4. The mechanical construction of conformable figures.}\end{center}

\begin{center}\small [From the \textit{American Journal of Mathematics}, t.~ii. (1879), p.~186.]\end{center}

Is it possible to devise an apparatus for the mechanical construction of conformable figures; that is, figures which are similar as regards corresponding infinitesimal areas? The problem is to connect mechanically two points $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$ in such wise that $P_{1}$ (1)~shall have two degrees of freedom (or be capable of moving over a plane area) its position always determining that of $P_{2}$: (2)~that if $P_{1}$, $P_{2}$ describe the infinitesimal lengths $P_{1}Q_{1}$, $P_{2}Q_{2}$, then the ratio of these lengths, and their mutual inclination, shall depend upon the position of $P_{1}$, but be independent of the direction of $P_{1}Q_{1}$: or what is the same thing, that if $P_{1}$ describe uniformly an indefinitely small circle, then $P_{2}$ shall also describe uniformly an indefinitely small circle, the ratio of the radii, and the relative position of the starting points in the two circles respectively, depending on the position of $P_{1}$.

Of course a pentagraph is a solution, but the two figures are in this case similar; and this is not what is wanted. Any unadjustable apparatus would give one solution only: the complete solution would be by an apparatus containing, suppose, a flexible lamina, so that $P_{1}$ moving in a given right line, the path of $P_{2}$ could be made to be any given curve whatever.

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Cambridge, July 9th, 1879.}

\begin{center}\textsc{A Link-Work for $x^{2}$: Extract from a Letter to\\ Mr.~Sylvester.}\end{center}

\begin{center}\small [From the \textit{American Journal of Mathematics}, t.~i. (1878), p.~386.]\end{center}

I \textsc{suppose} the following is substantially your link-work for $x^{2}$. I use a slot to make $D$ move in the line $OA$; but this could be replaced by proper link-work. Supposing $O$ and $A$ fixed; the line $OB$ is movable, and I wanted to have the distance $OB$ measured in a fixed direction. This can be done by a hexagon $OABQB'A'$ with equal sides, and two other equal links $B'R$, $BR$: then of course, if $O$, $R$, $Q$ are in line, the hexagon will be symmetrical as to $OQ$, and $OB'$ will be equal to $OB$, and $B'$ may be made to move in the fixed line $OB'$. If
\[ \angle BOA = \tfrac{1}{2}\theta, \quad OA = AB = a, \quad AC = CD = \tfrac{1}{2}a, \]
then
\[ OB = 2a\cos\tfrac{1}{2}\theta, \quad OD = a(1 + \cos\theta) = 2a\cos^{2}\tfrac{1}{2}\theta, \]
or
\[ 2a \cdot OD = (OB)^{2}. \]

\begin{center}\textsc{Calculation of the Minimum N.G.F. of the Binary\\ Seventhic.}\end{center}

\begin{center}\small [From the \textit{American Journal of Mathematics}, t.~ii. (1879), pp.~71--84.]\end{center}

For the binary seventhic $(a, \dots \mid x, y)^{7}$ the number of the asyzygetic covariants $(\alpha, \dots)^{\theta}(x, y)^{\mu}$, or say of the deg-order $(\theta \cdot \mu)$, is given as the coefficient of $a^{\theta} x^{\mu}$ in the function
\[ \frac{1-x^{-1}}{1-ax^{7}\cdot 1-ax^{5}\cdot 1-ax^{3}\cdot 1-ax\cdot 1-ax^{-1}\cdot 1-ax^{-3}\cdot 1-ax^{-5}\cdot 1-ax^{-7}} \]
developed in ascending powers of $a$. See my ``Ninth Memoir on Quantics,'' \textit{Phil. Trans.}, t.~CLXI. (1871), pp.~17--50, [462].

This function is in fact
\[ = A(x) - \frac{1}{x^{2}}\,A\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right), \]
where, developing in ascending powers of $a$, the second term $-\dfrac{1}{x^{2}} A\!\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$ contains only negative powers of $x$, and it may consequently be disregarded: the number of asyzygetic covariants of the deg-order $(\theta\cdot\mu)$ is thus equal to the coefficient of $a^{\theta} x^{\mu}$ in the function $A(x)$, which function is for this reason called the Numerical Generating Function (N.G.F.) of the binary seventhic; and the function $A(x)$ expressed as a fraction in its least terms is said to be the minimum N.G.F.

According to a theorem of Professor Sylvester's (\textit{Proc. Royal Soc.}, t.~XXVIII. (1878), pp.~11--13), this minimum N.G.F. is of the form
\[ \frac{Z_{0} + a Z_{1} + a^{2} Z_{2} + \dots + a^{36} Z_{36}}{1-ax\cdot 1-ax^{3}\cdot 1-ax^{5}\cdot 1-ax^{7}\cdot 1-a^{4}\cdot 1-a^{8}\cdot 1-a^{12}\cdot 1-a^{20}}, \]

\bigskip
\begin{center}\textsc{On the Double $\vartheta$-Functions.}\end{center}

\begin{center}\small [From the \textit{Journal f\"{u}r die reine und angewandte Mathematik} (Crelle), t.~LXXXVII. (1878), pp.~74--81.]\end{center}

I \textsc{have} sought to obtain, in forms which may be useful in regard to the theory of the double $\vartheta$-functions, the integral of the elliptic differential equation
\[ \frac{dx}{\sqrt{a-x \cdot b-x \cdot c-x \cdot d-x}} + \frac{dy}{\sqrt{a-y \cdot b-y \cdot c-y \cdot d-y}} = 0: \]
the present paper has immediate reference only to this differential equation; but, on account of the design of the investigation, I have entitled it as above.

We may for the general integral of the above equation take a particular integral of the equation
\[ \frac{dx}{\sqrt{a-x \cdot b-x \cdot c-x \cdot d-x}} + \frac{dy}{\sqrt{a-y \cdot b-y \cdot c-y \cdot d-y}} \pm \frac{dz}{\sqrt{a-z \cdot b-z \cdot c-z \cdot d-z}} = 0; \]
viz. this particular integral, regarding therein $z$ as an arbitrary constant, will be the general integral of the first mentioned equation. And we may further assume that $z$ is the value of $y$ corresponding to the value $a$ of $x$.

I write for shortness
\begin{align*}
a-x,\ b-x,\ c-x,\ d-x &= \mathfrak{a},\ \mathfrak{b},\ \mathfrak{c},\ \mathfrak{d},\\
a-y,\ b-y,\ c-y,\ d-y &= \mathfrak{a}_{1},\ \mathfrak{b}_{1},\ \mathfrak{c}_{1},\ \mathfrak{d}_{1};
\end{align*}
and I write also $(xy,\ bc,\ ad)$, or more shortly $(bc,\ ad)$ to denote the determinant
\[ \begin{vmatrix} 1, & x+y, & xy\\ 1, & b+c, & bc\\ 1, & a+d, & ad \end{vmatrix} \]
we have of course $(ad,\ bc) = -(bc,\ ad)$, and there are thus the three distinct determinants $(ad,\ bc)$, $(bd,\ ac)$ and $(cd,\ ab)$.

We have then for each of the functions
\[ \sqrt{\frac{a-z}{d-z}},\quad \sqrt{\frac{b-z}{d-z}},\quad \sqrt{\frac{c-z}{d-z}} \]
a set of four equivalent expressions, the whole system being
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{a-z}{d-z}} = \frac{\sqrt{a-b\cdot a-c}\,\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}} + \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}}\}}{(bc,\ ad)} = \frac{\sqrt{a-b\cdot a-c}\,(x-y)}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}}}$} \]
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle = \frac{\sqrt{a-b\cdot a-c}\,\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} + \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\}}{(a-c)\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - (b-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}}} = \frac{\sqrt{a-b\cdot a-c}\,\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} + \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}}\}}{(a-b)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - (c-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}}}$} \]

\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{b-z}{d-z}} = \sqrt{-\frac{a-b}{a-d}}\,\frac{\{(a-c)\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} + (b-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\}}{(bc,\ ad)} = \sqrt{-\frac{a-b}{a-d}}\,\frac{\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\}}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}}}$} \]
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle = \sqrt{-\frac{a-b}{a-d}}\,\frac{(cd,\ ab)}{(a-c)\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - (b-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}}} = -\sqrt{-\frac{a-b}{a-d}}\,\frac{\{(a-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}} + (b-c)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}}\}}{(a-b)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - (c-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}}}$} \]

\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle \sqrt{\frac{c-z}{d-z}} = -\sqrt{-\frac{a-c}{a-d}}\,\frac{\{(a-b)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} + (c-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}}\}}{(bc,\ ad)} = -\sqrt{-\frac{a-c}{a-d}}\,\frac{\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}}\}}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}}}$} \]
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle = -\sqrt{-\frac{a-c}{a-d}}\,\frac{\{(a-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}} - (b-c)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}}\}}{(a-c)\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - (b-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}}} = \sqrt{-\frac{a-c}{a-d}}\,\frac{(bd,\ ac)}{(a-b)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - (c-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}}}$} \]

The expressions in the like fourfold form for the functions $\operatorname{sn}(u + v)$, $\operatorname{cn}(u + v)$, $\operatorname{dn}(u + v)$ are given p.~63 of my \textit{Treatise on Elliptic Functions.}

It is easy to verify first that the four expressions for the same function of $z$ are identical, and next that the expressions for the three several functions
\[ \sqrt{\frac{a-z}{d-z}},\quad \sqrt{\frac{b-z}{d-z}},\quad \sqrt{\frac{c-z}{d-z}} \]
are consistent with each other. For instance, comparing the first and second expressions of $\sqrt{\dfrac{a-z}{d-z}}$, the equation to be verified is
\[ = \mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} - \mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c} - (x-y)(bc,\ ad), \]
which is at once shown to be true. Again comparing the first and second expressions for $\sqrt{\dfrac{b-z}{d-z}}$, we ought to have
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle \{(a-c)\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} + (b-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}}\}\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}}\} = (bc,\ ad)\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\}.$} \]

Here the product on the left-hand side is
\[ = (a-c)\,\{\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - \mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\} + (b-d)\,\{-\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} + \mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\}, \]
viz. this is
\[ = \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}}\,\{(a-c)\,\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d} - (b-d)\,\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}\,\{(a-c)\,\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{1} - (b-d)\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\}, \]
and in this last expression the two terms in $\{\,\}$ are at once shown to be each $= (bc,\ ad)$; whence the identity in question.

\begin{align*}
&-(b-d)(bc,\ ad)^{2}\sqrt{-\tfrac{a-c}{a-d}}\,\dfrac{1}{z}\\

&\hspace{4em}= -(a-b)\,\{(a-c)^{2}\,\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1} + (b-d)^{2}\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} + 2(a-c)(b-d)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}}\},
\end{align*}
whence, adding, the radical on the right-hand side disappears; the whole equation divides by $-(a-b)$, and omitting this factor, the relation to be verified is
\[ -(bc,\ ad)^{2} = (a-c)^{2}\,\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1} + (b-d)^{2}\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} - (a-c)(b-d)\,(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} + \mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}) \]
the right-hand side is here
\[ = -\{(a-c)\,\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{d} - (b-d)\,\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{c}\}\,\{(a-c)\,\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{d}_{1} - (b-d)\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\}, \]
and each of the two factors being $= (bc,\ ad)$, the identity is verified. It thus appears that the twelve equations are in fact equivalent to a single equation in $x$, $y$, $z$.

\[ \begin{array}{cccc}

\end{array} \]

viz. for $x = a$, the relation is $z = y$, but in the other three cases respectively the relation is a linear one, $z = \dfrac{\alpha y + \beta}{\gamma y + \delta}$.

and thence
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle \{(bc,\ ad)^{2}(a-z) - (a-b)(a-c)(d-z)\,(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} + \mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c})\}^{2} = (a-b)^{2}(a-c)^{2}(d-z)^{2}\cdot 4\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}.$} \]

\end{align*}
and writing also $\alpha = 1$, $-\beta = (b + c + d)$, $\gamma = bc + bd + cd$, $-\delta = bcd$, (whence

\begin{align*}

&\quad - 2\beta\delta\,xyz(x + y + z)\\
&\quad - 4\beta\varepsilon\,xyz(yz + zx + xy)\\
&\quad + (\delta^{2} - 4\gamma\varepsilon)\,x^{2}y^{2}z^{2} = 0,
\end{align*}
given p.~348 of my \textit{Elliptic Functions} as a particular integral of the differential equation when the radical is $\sqrt{\beta x + \gamma x^{2} + \delta x^{3} + \varepsilon x^{4}}$.

Let the equation in $(x, y, z)$ be called $\mathfrak{u} = 0$; $\mathfrak{u}$ has been given in the form $\mathfrak{u} = \mathfrak{P} z^{2} - 2\mathfrak{Q} z + \mathfrak{R}$, and we thence have $\dfrac{d\mathfrak{u}}{dz} = 0 = -\mathfrak{Q} + \mathfrak{P} z$; which, in virtue of the equation $\mathfrak{u} = 0$ itself, becomes $\mathfrak{Q}^{2} - \mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{R} = 0$. We have easily
\[ \mathfrak{Q}^{2} - \mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{R} = (a-b)^{2}(a-c)^{2}(a-d)^{2}\,\{(\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} - \mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c})^{2} - (bc,\ ad)^{2}(x-y)^{2}\}, \]
or, attending to the relation
\[ (\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} - \mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c})^{2} = (\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1} + \mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c})^{2} - 4\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}, \]
this is
\[ = (bc,\ ad)^{2}(x-y)^{2} - 4\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}, \]
or we have
\[ \mathfrak{Q}^{2} - \mathfrak{P}\mathfrak{R} = -4(a-b)^{2}(a-c)^{2}(a-d)^{2}\,\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}. \]
Writing
\[ \tfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d\mathfrak{u}}{dx} = (a-b)(a-c)(a-d)\sqrt{abcd}\,\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}}, \]
\[ a-z,\ b-z,\ c-z,\ d-z = \mathfrak{a}_{2},\ \mathfrak{b}_{2},\ \mathfrak{c}_{2},\ \mathfrak{d}_{2}, \]
we have of course the like formulae
\[ \tfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d\mathfrak{u}}{dy} = (a-b)(a-c)(a-d)\sqrt{abcd}\,\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{2}\mathfrak{b}_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{2}\mathfrak{d}_{2}}, \]
\[ \tfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d\mathfrak{u}}{dz} = (a-b)(a-c)(a-d)\sqrt{abcd}\,\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}\mathfrak{a}_{2}\mathfrak{b}_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{2}\mathfrak{d}_{2}} \]

and the equation $d\mathfrak{u} = 0$ then gives
\[ \frac{dx}{\sqrt{abcd}\,\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{b}\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{d}}} + \frac{dy}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}}} + \frac{dz}{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{2}\mathfrak{b}_{2}\mathfrak{c}_{2}\mathfrak{d}_{2}}} = 0, \]
as it should do. The differential equation might also have been verified directly from any one of the expressions for
\[ \sqrt{\frac{a-z}{d-z}},\quad \sqrt{\frac{b-z}{d-z}},\quad \sqrt{\frac{c-z}{d-z}}. \]

Writing for shortness
\[ X = a-x \cdot b-x \cdot c-x \cdot d-x,\ \text{etc.,} \]
then the general integral of the differential equation
\[ \frac{dx}{\sqrt{X}} + \frac{dy}{\sqrt{Y}} + \frac{dz}{\sqrt{Z}} = 0 \]
by Abel's theorem is
\[ \begin{vmatrix} x^{2}, & x, & 1, & \sqrt{X}\\ y^{2}, & y, & 1, & \sqrt{Y}\\ z^{2}, & z, & 1, & \sqrt{Z}\\ w^{2}, & w, & 1, & \sqrt{W} \end{vmatrix} = 0, \]
where $w$ is the constant of integration: and it is to be shown that the value of $w$ which corresponds to the integral given in the present paper is $= a$. Observe that writing in the determinant $w = a$, the determinant on putting therein $x = a$, would vanish whether $z$ were or were not $= y$; but this is on account of an extraneous factor $a - w$, so that we do not thus prove the required theorem that ($w$ being $= a$) we have $y = z$ when $x = a$.

An equivalent form of Abel's integral is that there exist values $A$, $B$, $C$ such that
\begin{align*}
A x^{2} + B x + C &= \sqrt{X},\\
A y^{2} + B y + C &= \sqrt{Y},\\
A z^{2} + B z + C &= \sqrt{Z},\\
A w^{2} + B w + C &= \sqrt{W},
\end{align*}
or, what is the same thing, that we have identically
\[ -(A\theta^{2} + B\theta + C)^{2} + \Theta = (A^{2} - 1)\cdot\theta - x \cdot \theta - y \cdot \theta - z \cdot \theta - w. \]
We have therefore
\[ C^{2} - abcd = (A^{2} - 1)\,xyzw, \]
or say
\[ xyzw = \frac{C^{2} - abcd}{A^{2} - 1}. \]

which equation, regarding therein $A$, $B$, $C$ as determined by the three equations
\begin{align*}

A y^{2} + B y + C &= \sqrt{Y},\\

is a form of Abel's integral, giving $z$ rationally in terms of $x$, $y$, $w$.

\begin{align*}

\[ ay^{2}w = \frac{C^{2} - abcd}{A^{2} - 1} \]

\end{align*}

\[ a - y,\ b - y,\ c - y,\ d - y = \mathfrak{a}_{1},\ \mathfrak{b}_{1},\ \mathfrak{c}_{1},\ \mathfrak{d}_{1}, \]
and also
\[ a - w,\ b - w,\ c - w,\ d - w = \mathfrak{a}_{3},\ \mathfrak{b}_{3},\ \mathfrak{c}_{3},\ \mathfrak{d}_{3}, \]
then $Y = \mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}$, $W = \mathfrak{a}_{3}\mathfrak{b}_{3}\mathfrak{c}_{3}\mathfrak{d}_{3}$, and the formulae become
\begin{align*}
A &= \frac{1}{(w - y)\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{a}_{3}}}\,\{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{3}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}} - \sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{3}\mathfrak{c}_{3}\mathfrak{d}_{3}}\},\\

\end{align*}

If in these formulae $w$ is indefinitely nearly $= a$, then $\mathfrak{a}_{3}$ is indefinitely small, so that $\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{3}\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{c}_{1}\mathfrak{d}_{1}}$ may be neglected in comparison with $\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\mathfrak{b}_{3}\mathfrak{c}_{3}\mathfrak{d}_{3}}$: also $w - y$ may be put $= \mathfrak{a}_{1}$; the formulae thus become
\[ A = -\,\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}_{3}\mathfrak{c}_{3}\mathfrak{d}_{3}}}{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{3}}},\quad B = (a + y)\,\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}_{3}\mathfrak{c}_{3}\mathfrak{d}_{3}}}{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{3}}},\quad C = -ay\,\frac{\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}_{3}\mathfrak{c}_{3}\mathfrak{d}_{3}}}{\mathfrak{a}_{1}\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{3}}}, \]
where the values of $A$, $B$, $C$ are each of them indefinitely large on account of the factor $\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}_{3}}$ in the denominator; the value of $C$ is $C = ay A$, and substituting this value in the equation
\[ ay^{2}w = \frac{C^{2} - abcd}{A^{2} - 1}, \]
and then considering $A$ as indefinitely large, the equation becomes $ay^{2}w = a^{2}y^{2}$, that is, $w = a$; so that $w = a$ is a value of $w$ satisfying this equation.
