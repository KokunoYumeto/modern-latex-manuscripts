\noindent\textbf{704] \quad A MEMOIR ON THE SINGLE AND DOUBLE THETA-FUNCTIONS. \hfill 481}

\medskip
\begin{center}\textit{The square set, $u'=0$; and $x$-formul\ae.}\end{center}

\textbf{37.} We use the square-set, in the first instance by writing therein $u'=0$; the equations become
\[
\begin{aligned}
A^2 u &= \alpha X + \beta Y, &&= \omega''\Ag\,(a-x),\\
B^2 u &= \beta X + \alpha Y, &&= \omega''\Bg\,(b-x),\\
C^2 u &= \alpha X - \beta Y, &&= \omega''\Cg\,(c-x),\\
D^2 u &= \beta X - \alpha Y, &&= \omega''\Dg\,(d-x),
\end{aligned}
\]
viz.\ the equations without their last members show that there exist functions $\omega''$ and $x$, linear functions of $X$ and $Y$, such that $\Ag,\Bg,\Cg,\Dg,\ \Ag a,\Bg b,\Cg c,\Dg d$, being constants, the squared functions may be assumed equal to $\Ag\omega''\cdot\omega''x$, \&c., that is, $\omega''\Ag(a-x)$, \&c., respectively; the squared functions are then proportional to the values $\Ag(a-x)$, \&c.

To show the meaning of the factor $\omega''$, observe that, from any two of the equations, for instance from the first and second, we have an equation without $\omega$,
\[
A^2 u - B^2 u = \Ag(a-x) - \Bg(b-x);
\]
and using this to determine $x$, and then substituting in $\omega'' = A^2 u \div \Ag(a-x)$, we find
\[
\omega'' = \frac{\Ag A^2 u - \Bg B^2 u}{(a-b)\,\Ag\Bg},
\]
where the numerator is a function not in anywise more important than any other linear function of $A^2 u$ and $B^2 u$.

\medskip
\textbf{38.} The function $Du$ vanishes for $u=0$, and we may assume that the corresponding value of $x$ is $=d$. Writing in the other equations $u=0$, they become
\[
\begin{aligned}
A^2 0 &= \alpha_0^2 + \beta_0^2 = \omega_0''\Ag(a-d),\\
B^2 0 &= 2\alpha_0\beta_0 = \omega_0''\Bg(b-d),\\
C^2 0 &= \alpha_0^2 - \beta_0^2 = \omega_0''\Cg(c-d),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\omega_0''$ is what $\omega''$ becomes on writing therein $x=d$. It is convenient to omit altogether these factors $\omega''$ and $\omega_0''$; it being understood that, without them, the equations denote not absolute equalities but only equalities of ratios: thus, without the $\omega_0''$, the last-mentioned equations would denote
\[
A^2 0 : B^2 0 : C^2 0 = \alpha_0^2+\beta_0^2 : 2\alpha_0\beta_0 : \alpha_0^2-\beta_0^2 = \Ag(a-d) : \Bg(b-d) : \Cg(c-d).
\]
The quantities $\Ag,\Bg,\Cg,\Dg$ only present themselves in the products $\Ag\omega''$, \&c., and their absolute magnitudes are therefore essentially indeterminate; but regarding $\omega''$ as

\newpage
\noindent containing a constant factor of properly determined value, the absolute values of $\Ag,\Bg,\Cg,\Dg$ may be regarded as determinate, and this is accordingly done in the formul\ae{} $\Ag^2 = -agh$, \&c., which follow.

\begin{center}\textit{Relations between the constants.}\end{center}

\textbf{39.} The formul\ae{} contain the differences of the quantities $a,b,c,d$; denoting these differences
\[
b-c,\quad c-a,\quad a-b,\quad a-d,\quad b-d,\quad c-d,
\]
in the usual manner by
\[
\mathrm{a},\ \mathrm{b},\ \mathrm{c},\ \mathrm{f},\ \mathrm{g},\ \mathrm{h},
\]
so that
\[
\begin{aligned}
{}-\mathrm{h}+\mathrm{g}-\mathrm{a} &= 0,\\
\mathrm{h}-\mathrm{f}-\mathrm{b} &= 0,\\
-\mathrm{g}+\mathrm{f}-\mathrm{c} &= 0,\\
\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{b}+\mathrm{c} &= 0,
\end{aligned}
\]
and also
\[
\mathrm{a}\mathrm{f} + \mathrm{b}\mathrm{g} + \mathrm{c}\mathrm{h} = 0,
\]
and then assuming the absolute value of one of the quantities $\Ag,\Bg,\Cg,\Dg$, we have the system of relations
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\begin{aligned}
\Ag^2 &= -\mathrm{a}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{h}, & \Bg\Cg\Dg &= \Ag^2\Dg\mathrm{f}, & \Ag\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{f} &= -\Bg\Cg\Dg, & \Ag\Bg\Cg\Dg &= \mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{h},\\
\Bg^2 &= \mathrm{b}\mathrm{h}\mathrm{f}, & \Cg\Dg\Ag &= -\Bg^2\Dg\mathrm{g}, & \Bg\mathrm{c}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{g} &= \Cg\Ag\Dg,\\
\Cg^2 &= \mathrm{c}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{g}, & \Ag\Bg\Cg &= -\Cg^2\Dg\mathrm{h}, & \Cg\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{h} &= \Ag\Bg\Dg,\\
\Dg^2 &= -\mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}, & & & \Dg\mathrm{f}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{h} &= -\Ag\Bg\Cg,
\end{aligned}$}
\]
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\begin{aligned}
0 &= \Bg^2 + \mathrm{b}^2\Cg^2 - \mathrm{f}^2\Dg^2 = \mathrm{b}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{f}\,(\mathrm{a}\mathrm{f}+\mathrm{b}\mathrm{g}+\mathrm{c}\mathrm{h}) = 0,\\
0 &= -\mathrm{c}^2\Ag^2 + \mathrm{a}^2\Cg^2 - \mathrm{g}^2\Dg^2 = \mathrm{c}\mathrm{a}\mathrm{g}\,(\ \ \mathrm{"}\ \ ) = 0,\\
0 &= -\mathrm{b}^2\Ag^2 + \mathrm{a}^2\Bg^2 - \mathrm{h}^2\Dg^2 = \mathrm{a}\mathrm{b}\mathrm{h}\,(\ \ \mathrm{"}\ \ ) = 0,\\
0 &= -\mathrm{f}^2\Ag^2 + \mathrm{g}^2\Bg^2 + \mathrm{h}^2\Cg^2 = \mathrm{f}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{h}\,(\ \ \mathrm{"}\ \ ) = 0.
\end{aligned}$}
\]

It is to be remarked that, taking $c,a,b,d$ in the order of decreasing magnitude, we have $-\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b},\mathrm{c},\mathrm{f},\mathrm{g},\mathrm{h}$ all positive; hence $\Ag^2,\Bg^2,\Cg^2,\Dg^2$ all real; and taking as we may do, $\Dg$ negative, then $\Ag,\Bg,\Cg$ may be taken positive; that is, we have $-\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b},\mathrm{c},\mathrm{f},\mathrm{g},\mathrm{h},\Ag,\Bg,\Cg,-\Dg$ all of them positive.

where in the first and second columns the terms of the same column correspond to each other with a reversal of sign, whereas in the third and fourth columns the lower term of either column corresponds to the upper term of the other column, but without a reversal of sign.

\textbf{43.} We can from each set form two fractions (each of them a function of $u+u'$ and $u-u'$), which are equal to one and the same function of $u'$ only: for instance, from the first set we have two fractions, each $=\dfrac{Y'}{X'}$; putting in such equation $u=0$, we obtain a new expression for the function of $u'$ involving only the theta-functions $Au'$, \&c., which new expression we may then substitute in the equations first obtained; we thus arrive at the six equations

\textbf{44.} Taking herein $u'$ indefinitely small, we obtain
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Au\,.\,C'u-Cu\,.\,A'u}{Bu\,.\,Du} &= \frac{Bu\,.\,D'u-Du\,.\,B'u}{Cu\,.\,Au} = \frac{D'0\,.\,B0}{C0\,.\,A0} = -K\frac{D^2 0}{A^2 0},\\

\end{aligned}$}

where the last column is added in order to introduce $K$ in place of $D'0$.

\medskip
\textbf{45.} These formulae in effect give the derivatives of the quotient-functions in terms of quotient-functions: for instance, one of the equations is
\[
\frac{d}{du}\frac{Du}{Au} = -K\frac{Bu\,\,Cu}{Au\,\,Au},
\]
substituting herein for the quotient-fractions their values in terms of $x$, this becomes
\[
\frac{d}{du}\frac{1}{\Ag}\sqrt{\frac{a-x}{d-x}} = -\frac{K}{\Ag^2}\sqrt{\Bg\Cg}\sqrt{\frac{a-x}{d-x}}\cdot\frac{1}{\Ag}\sqrt{\frac{a-x}{d-x}},

K\,du = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-\mathrm{a}\mathrm{f}}\,dx}{\sqrt{a-x\,.\,b-x\,.\,c-x\,.\,d-x}},
\]
where on the right-hand side it would be better to write $\sqrt{-\mathrm{a}\mathrm{f}}$ in the numerator and $x-d$ in place of $d-x$ in the denominator.

\begin{center}\textit{Comparison with Jacobi's formul\ae.}\end{center}

\textbf{46.} The comparison of the formul\ae{} with Jacobi's formul\ae{} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{sn}\,Ku &= -\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\,Du \div Cu, &&= \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{g}}}\sqrt{\frac{d-x}{c-x}}\ \ (\text{or better}\ \sqrt{\frac{-\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{g}}}\sqrt{\frac{x-d}{c-x}}),\\
\mathrm{cn}\,Ku &= \sqrt{\frac{k'}{k}}\,Bu \div Cu, &&= \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{g}}}\sqrt{\frac{b-x}{c-x}},\\
\mathrm{dn}\,Ku &= \sqrt{k'}\,Au \div Cu, &&= \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{h}}{\mathrm{f}}}\sqrt{\frac{a-x}{c-x}},
\end{aligned}
\]
where it will be recollected that
\[
k^2 = \frac{\mathrm{b}\mathrm{g}}{-\mathrm{a}\mathrm{f}},\qquad k'^2 = \frac{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{h}}{-\mathrm{a}\mathrm{f}}.
\]
It may be remarked that we seek to determine everything in terms of $a,b,c,d$. The formula just written down, $\mathrm{f}^2 = \mathrm{b}\mathrm{g} + \mathrm{h} - \mathrm{a}\mathrm{f}$, gives $k$ in terms of these quantities; and $k$, $K$ being each given in terms of $q$, we have virtually $K$ as a function of $k$, that is, of $a,b,c,d$: but it would not be easy from the expressions of $k$, $K$, each in terms of $q$, to deduce the actual expression
\[
K = \int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\phi}}

of $K$ as a function of $k$.

\end{aligned}

\end{aligned}
\]

\textbf{61.} The sines and cosines are converted into infinite products by the ordinary formul\ae, which neglecting constant factors may conveniently be written

it is assumed that the limiting values $\mu,-\mu$ of $m$ are indefinitely large in regard to $u+n\alpha$; and therefore, since $n$ may approach to its limiting value $+\nu$, it is necessary that $\mu$ should be indefinitely large in comparison with $\nu$, or that $\dfrac{\nu}{\mu}=0$.

\textbf{62.} It is on account of this unsymmetry as to the limits $\dfrac{\nu}{\mu}=0$, $\dfrac{\mu}{\nu}=\infty$, that we have $1$ as a quarter-period, $\dfrac{\omega}{\pi i}$ only as a quarter-quasi-period of the single theta-functions.

The algebraical relations between the double theta-functions are such that, introducing six constant quantities $a,b,c,d,e,f$ and two variable quantities $x,y$, it is allowable to express the 16 functions as proportional to given functions of these quantities $(a,b,c,d,e,f; x,y)$; viz.\ there are six functions the notations of which depend on the single letters $a,b,c,d,e,f$ respectively, and 10 functions the notations of which depend on the pairs $ab, ac, ad, ae, bc, bd, be, cd, ce, de$ respectively: each of the 16 functions is, in fact, proportional to the product of two factors, viz.\ a constant factor depending only on $(a,b,c,d,e,f)$, and a variable factor containing also $x$ and $y$.
