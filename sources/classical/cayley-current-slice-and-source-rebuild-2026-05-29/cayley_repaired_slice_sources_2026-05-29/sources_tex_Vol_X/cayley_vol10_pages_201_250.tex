\section{[665] A Memoir on the Double \texorpdfstring{$\te$}{theta}-Functions (conclusion)}
\label{sec:665}

\noindent\textit{[From the \emph{Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London}, vol.~169 (1878), pp.~663--730; continued from preceding pages.]}

\medskip

\noindent (Page 201.) We have, as the equation of a quadric surface enveloped by
\[
\alpha\,(x - x') + \beta\,(y - y') + \gamma\,(z - z') + \delta\,(w - w') = 0,
\]
which is the same form as before, the relations
\[
ab' + ba' = 0, \quad cd' + dc' = 0, \quad \text{etc.},
\]
or, more precisely,
\[
\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial x} \cdot X' + \frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial y} \cdot Y' + \frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial z} \cdot Z' + \frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial w} \cdot W' = 0.
\]

It is to be remarked that there are 15 expressions such as $A1B + B1A$, and 45 expressions such as $AC1BD + AD1BC$; and that each of these $(15 + 45 = ) 60$ expressions is a quadric function of $\partial_x$, $\partial_y$, $\partial_z$, $\partial_w$ alone.

In like manner $\Omega$, $\Omega_1$, $\Omega_2$, $\Omega_3$ are quadric functions of $(x, y, z, w)$, and similarly $\Omega'$, $\Omega'_1$, $\Omega'_2$, $\Omega'_3$ of $(x', y', z', w')$; and hence $\Omega$, $\Omega_1$, $\Omega_2$, $\Omega_3$ satisfy the conditions
\[
\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial x} - 2x + \frac{\partial\Omega_1}{\partial y} \cdot 2 + \frac{\partial\Omega_2}{\partial z} \cdot 2 + \frac{\partial\Omega_3}{\partial w} \cdot 2 = 0,
\]
and similarly for the conjugate functions.

\medskip

\noindent \textit{Expression of $\Th\,\Omega - \Omega\Th'$: second subheading.}

We assume $\Th\,\Omega - \Omega\Th' = \tfrac{1}{2}M\,\Omega''$, where $M$ is a quadric function of $\partial_x$, $\partial_y$; suppose
\[
M \equiv \Th\,(x x' + y y') + \Th'\,(x x' + y y').
\]
It is to be remarked that there are 15 expressions which can be presented in the form
\[
\Big(\Th x' \frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \Th y' \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Big) \sqrt{X} = \Big( \Th x' + \Th y' \cdot \frac{x y'}{X} \Big) \sqrt{X} + \Big(\Th y' \cdot \frac{x x'}{Y}\Big) \sqrt{X};
\]
it is clear that the term in question is at once expressible in the form indicated by means of these.

It is to be remarked that there are 15 expressions which form a quadric system of $(x, y)$ alone; viz., $X$, $Y$, $\frac{x y'}{X}$ etc.\ are linear functions of $(x, y)$ together with the symbols
\[
\Big(\frac{\partial\Th}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial\Th}{\partial y}\Big) \sqrt{X} + \Big(\frac{\partial\Th}{\partial x'} + \frac{\partial\Th}{\partial y'}\Big) \sqrt{Y},
\]
where the bracketed symbols satisfy the conditions
\[
\xi \alpha + \eta \beta = 0, \qquad \nu \alpha + \mu \beta = 0,
\]
and the like for $\xi'$, $\eta'$, $\nu'$, $\mu'$.

Putting for the moment
\[
\lambda \alpha + \mu \beta = \nu \alpha, \qquad \mu \beta + \nu \gamma = \mu \beta, \qquad \nu \gamma + \lambda \delta = \nu \gamma, \qquad p \alpha + q \beta = r \alpha + s \beta,
\]
$\mu = ab$, $\nu = ac$, ...; the values of $\xi$, $\eta$, $\nu$, $\mu$, $\xi'$, $\eta'$, $\nu'$, $\mu'$ are
\[
\xi = 0, \qquad \eta = 0, \qquad \nu = 0, \qquad \mu = 0,
\]
respectively.

I found it convenient to assume
\[
\Th = -2 \big(x'^2 + y'^2 \big)E = b\,xx' - ay\,y' + p\,(yy' + xx') + q\,(yy' - xx'),
\]
and that the system of seven is
\[
\Th = -2\,(x'^2 + y'^2)E - \alpha\,xy' + \beta\,yx' + (\gamma + \delta)\,xx' + (\gamma - \delta)\,yy'.
\]

and the value of $\Pi$ thus, suppose

or so they may be written,

\[

\selectlanguage{french}

\noindent Je reprends l'investigation de M.~Hermite sur lui rapport aux int\'egrales r\'eductibles

\]

Nous avons les constantes $a$, $b$ et les variables $x$, $y$, $u$, $v$; et on posant
\[
X = a\cdot 1 - x\cdot 1 + a x\cdot 1 + b x\cdot 1 - a b x,
\]
\[

\[
\frac{du}{\sqrt{X}} + \frac{dv}{\sqrt{Y}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{a}} (du + dv),
\]
\[
\frac{x\,dx}{\sqrt{X}} + \frac{y\,dy}{\sqrt{Y}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}} (du - dv).
\]
