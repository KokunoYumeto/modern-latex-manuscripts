\begin{equation*}
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}

\end{equation*}
where letters of the same alphabet denote lines in the same plane, if only the letters are the same or the suffixes the same; thus $A_1, A_2, A_3$ lie in a plane $A_1 A_2 A_3$; $A_1, B_1, C_1$ lie in a plane $A_1 B_1 C_1$. \subsection*{Equation of the Cubic Surface.}

We have $X = 0$, $Y = 0$, $Z = 0$, $W = 0$ for the equations of the planes $(12.34.56 = x)$, $(42' = y)$, $(14' = z)$, $(12' = w)$; and representing by $\xi = lX + \frac{Y}{m} + \frac{Z}{n} + W = 0$ the equation of any other plane $(41' = \xi)$, the equation of the cubic surface may be presented in the several forms:
\begin{align*}
&= Fgg_1 + k\eta\zeta ZX, \\
&= Fhh_1 + k\zeta\theta XY, \\
&= W\eta\eta_1 + k\theta\iota YZ, \\
&= W\zeta\zeta_1 + k\iota\kappa ZX, \\
&= W\theta\theta_1 + k\kappa\lambda XY, \\
&= W\iota\iota_1 + k\lambda\mu YZ, \\
&= W\kappa\kappa_1 + k\mu\nu ZX, \\
&= W\lambda\lambda_1 + k\nu\xi XY, \\
&= W\mu\mu_1 + k\xi\eta YZ, \\
&= W\nu\nu_1 + k\xi\eta\zeta, \\
&= W\xi\xi_1 + k\eta\zeta\iota, \\
&= W\pi\pi_1 + k\xi\eta z, \\
&= W\rho\rho_1 + k\zeta\theta z, \\
&= W\sigma\sigma_1 + k\xi\iota x.
\end{align*}

\subsection*{The Hessian Surface.}

Resuming the equation $W(a,b,c,f,g,h \,\backslash\, X,Y,Z)^2 + 2kXYZ = 0$, the equation of the Hessian surface is found to be:
\begin{multline*}
kW^2(a,b,c,f,g,h \,\backslash\, X,Y,Z)^2 \\
+ 2kW\{(a,b,c,f,g,h \,\backslash\, X,Y,Z)^2(FX + GY + HZ) - k^2 XYZ\} \\
- k\{a^2 X^4 + b^2 Y^4 + c^2 Z^4 - 2bc Y^2 Z^2 - 2ca Z^2 X^2 - 2ab X^2 Y^2 \\
- 4XYZ[(af+gh)X + (bg+hf)Y + (ch+fg)Z]\} = 0,
\end{multline*}
where
\begin{equation*}
(A,B,C,F,G,H) = (bc-f^2,\, ca-g^2,\, ab-h^2,\, gh-af,\, hf-bg,\, fg-ch),
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
K = abc - af^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 + 2fgh.
\end{equation*}

\subsection*{Invariants and Reciprocal Surface.}

Write as before $(A,B,C,F,G,H)$ for the inverse coefficients ($A = bc - f^2$, \&c.), and $K = abc - af^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 + 2fgh$; and moreover:
\begin{align*}
\Phi &= (A,B,C,F,G,H \,\backslash\, x,y,z)^2, \\
P &= Ax + Hy + Gz, \\
Q &= Hx + By + Fz, \\
R &= Gx + Fy + Cz, \\
t &= fx + gy + hz, \\
U &= afyz + bgzx + chxy, \\
V &= 2Kxyz - aPyz - bQzx - cRxy \\
  &= -aHy^2z - bFz^2x - cGx^2y - aGyz^2 - bEzx^2 - cFxy^2 \\
  &\quad + (-abc - ap^2 - bg^2 - ch^2 + 4fgh)xyz, \\
W &= (A,B,C,F,G,H \,\backslash\, ayz, bzx, cxy)^2, \\
L &= kW - 2ktw - \Phi, \\
M &= kwU + V, \\
N &= 2kabcxyzw + W.
\end{align*}

Then the invariants of the ternary cubic are:
\begin{align*}
S &= L^2 - 12kwM, \\
T &= L^3 - 18kwLM - 54k^2w^2N,
\end{align*}
and the required equation of the reciprocal surface is:
\begin{equation*}
\frac{1}{k^2w^2}\left[(L^3 - 18kwLM - 54k^2w^2N)^2 - (L^2 - 12kwM)^3\right] = 0,
\end{equation*}
viz. this is:
\begin{equation*}
0 = DN = (kW - 2ktw - \Phi)^2(2kabcxyzw + W) + L^2M^2 + \cdots
\end{equation*}

\subsection*{Equation $Y^2 + Y(X+Z+W) + 4\phi XZW = 0$.}

Each of the lines $(X - Y = 0, Y + Z = 0)$ and $(X + W = 0, Y - Z = 0)$ is a double tangent of the spinode octic; in fact for the first of these lines we have:
\begin{equation*}
16\theta^4 + 8\theta^2 + 16\phi\theta^2 + 5 = 0, \quad 16\theta^4 + 16\theta^2 + 4\phi = 0,
\end{equation*}
that is:
\begin{equation*}
(2\theta^2 + 1)(8\theta^2 - 4\theta + 5) = 0, \quad 4\theta(2\theta^2 + 1) = 0,
\end{equation*}
so that the line touches at the two points given by $2\theta^2 + 1 = 0$; and similarly the other line touches at the two points given by $2\theta^2 - 1 = 0$.

The edge $X = 0$, $Z = 0$ has apparently a higher contact with the spinode octic, viz. the equations $X = 0$, $Z = 0$ are satisfied by $\theta = \infty$ twice, $\theta = 0$ five times; but it must be reckoned only as a double tangent. Hence $\beta' = 2 \cdot 2 + 2 = 6$.
