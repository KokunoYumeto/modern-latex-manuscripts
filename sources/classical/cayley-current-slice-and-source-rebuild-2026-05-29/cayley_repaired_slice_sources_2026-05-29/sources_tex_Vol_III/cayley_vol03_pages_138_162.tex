\bigskip
\begin{center}\small\textsc{118}\hfill[185\end{center}

\begin{center}
\textbf{185.}\\[0.5em]
\textsc{Note on the `Circular Relation' of Prof. M\"obius.}\\[0.4em]
\small [\textit{From the Quarterly Mathematical Journal}, vol.\ \textsc{ii}.\ (1858), p.\ 162.]
\end{center}

\medskip
\noindent \textsc{Theorem.}

Given the points
\[
A,\ B,\ C;\ P,
\]
and the points
\[
A',\ B',\ C';
\]
describe the circles $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$, $\omega$ as follows: viz.
\begin{align*}
\alpha &\text{ through } (B,C,P),\\
\beta &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (C,A,P),\\
\gamma &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (A,B,P),\\
\omega &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (A,B,C),
\end{align*}
and the circles $\alpha'$, $\beta'$, $\gamma'$, $\omega'$ as follows: viz.,
\begin{align*}
\alpha' &\text{ through } (B',C')\ \text{cutting } \omega' \text{ at the angle at which } \alpha \text{ cuts } \omega,\\
\beta' &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (C',A')\ \text{,, } \omega' \quad\text{,, } \quad\text{,, } \beta \text{,, } \omega,\\
\gamma' &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (A',B')\ \text{,, } \omega' \quad\text{,, } \quad\text{,, } \gamma \text{,, } \omega,\ \text{and}\\
\omega' &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (A',B',C')
\end{align*}
then will $\alpha'$, $\beta'$, $\gamma'$ meet in a point $P'$, i.e.\ we shall have the points $A'$, $B'$, $C'$;

%---------------------------------------
% PDF p.141 = book p.119
%---------------------------------------
\begin{center}\small[185\hfill\textsc{note on the `circular relation' of prof.\ m\"obius.}\hfill\textsc{119}\end{center}

\noindent such that the circles $\alpha'$, $\beta'$, $\gamma'$, $\omega'$ pass
\begin{align*}
\alpha' &\text{ through } (B',C',P'),\\
\beta' &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (C',A',P'),\\
\gamma' &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (A',B',P'),\\
\omega' &\quad\;\; \text{,, } (A',B',C').
\end{align*}
We may construct in this manner two figures, such that to three points of the first
figure there correspond in the second figure three points which may be taken at
pleasure, but these once selected to every other point of the first figure there will
correspond in the second figure a perfectly determinate point. And the two figures
will be such that whenever in the first figure four or more points lie in a circle,
then in the second figure the corresponding points will also lie in a circle. The
relation in question is due to Prof.\ M\"obius, who has termed it the \textit{Kreis-verwandschaft}
(circular relation) of two plane figures. See his paper \textit{Crelle}, t.\ \textsc{lii}.\ [1856], pp.\ 218--228,
extracted from the \textit{Berichte} of the Royal Saxon Society of Sciences of the 5th Feb.\
1853, [and \textit{Werke}, t.\ \textsc{ii}.\ pp.\ 207--217].

\begin{center}
\textbf{186.}\\[0.5em]
\textsc{On the Determination of the Value of a Certain Determinant.}\\[0.4em]
\small [\textit{From the Quarterly Mathematical Journal}, vol.\ \textsc{ii}.\ (1858), pp.\ 163--166.]
\end{center}

\medskip
\noindent \textsc{Considering} the determinant
\[
\left|\,\begin{array}{cccc}
\theta, & 1, & \cdot & \cdots\\
n, & \theta, & 2 & \\
\cdot & n-1, & \theta, & 3\\
\cdot & \cdot & n-2, & \theta, \quad 4\\
\cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \cdots
\end{array}\,\right|
\]
let the successive diagonal minors be $U_{0}, U_{1}, U_{2},\ldots U_{x},\ldots$, it is easy to find
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{0} &= 1,\\
U_{1} &= \theta,\\
U_{2} &= \theta^{2} - n,\\
U_{3} &= \theta(\theta^{2}-4) - 3(n-2)\theta,\\
U_{4} &= (\theta^{2}-1)(\theta^{2}-9) - 6(n-3)(\theta^{2}-1) + 3(n-3)(n-1),
\end{aligned}
\]
which in fact suggests the law, viz.
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{x} = {}&(\theta+x-1)(\theta+x-3)(\theta+x-5)\ldots(\theta-x+5)(\theta-x+3)(\theta-x+1)\\
&-\frac{x(x-1)}{2}(n-x+1)(\theta+x-3)(\theta+x-5)\ldots(\theta-x+3)\\
&+\frac{x(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}{2\cdot 4}(n-x+1)(n-x+3)(\theta+x-5)\ldots(\theta-x+5)\\
&-\,\&\text{c.}\\
&\;\vdots\\
&+(-)^{s}\frac{x(x-1)\ldots(x-2s+1)}{2\cdot 4\ldots 2s}(n-x+1)(n-x+3)\ldots(n-x+2s-1)\\
&\quad\times(\theta+x-2s-1)(\theta+x-2s-3)\ldots(\theta-x+2s+1)
\end{aligned}
\]
to $s = \tfrac{1}{2}x$ or $\tfrac{1}{2}(x-1)$, as $x$ is even or odd.

\noindent And of course if $x$ denote the number of lines or columns of the determinant, then
$U_{x}$ is the value of the determinant. This theorem, or a particular case of it, is due
to Prof.\ Sylvester: I have not been able to find an easier demonstration than the
following one, which, it must be admitted, is somewhat complicated. I observe that
$U_{x}$ satisfies the equation
\[
U_{x} - \theta U_{x-1} + (x-1)(n-x+2)\, U_{x-2} = 0.
\]
Hence writing $x-1$ and $x-2$ for $x$, we have the system
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{x} - \theta U_{x-1} + (x-1)(n-x+2)\, U_{x-2} &= 0,\\
U_{x-1} - \theta U_{x-2} + (x-2)(n-x+3)\, U_{x-3} &= 0,\\
U_{x-2} - \theta U_{x-3} + (x-3)(n-x+4)\, U_{x-4} &= 0,
\end{aligned}
\]
or, eliminating $U_{x-1}$ and $U_{x-3}$,
\[
U_{x} + \{(x-1)(n-x+2) + (x-2)(n-x+3) - \theta^{2}\}\, U_{x-2} + (x-2)(x-3)(n-x+3)(n-x+4)\, U_{x-4} = 0.
\]
Suppose, for shortness,
\[
(\theta+x-1)(\theta+x-3)(\theta+x-5)\ldots(\theta-x+5)(\theta-x+3)(\theta-x+1) = H_{x},
\]
and assume
\[
U_{x} = A_{x,1} H_{x} - A_{x,1} H_{x-2}\ldots + (-)^{s} A_{x,s} H_{x-2s}\ldots,
\]
where $A_{x,s}$ is independent of $\theta$, then
\[
U_{x} \text{ contains the term } (-)^{s} A_{x,s} H_{x-2s},
\]
\[
U_{x-2} \text{ contains the term } (-)^{s} A_{x-2,s} H_{x-2s-2},
\]
which is to be multiplied by
\[
(x-1)(n-x+2) + (x-2)(n-x+3) - \theta^{2}.
\]
This multiplier may be written under the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(x-1)(n-x+2) + (x-2)(n-x+3) - (x-2s-1)^{2} - \{\theta^{2} - (x-2s-1)^{2}\}\\
&= M_{x,s} - \{\theta^{2} - (x-2s-1)^{2}\},
\end{aligned}
\]
if, for shortness,
\[
M_{x,s} = (x-1)(n-x+2) + (x-2)(n-x+3) - (x-2s-1)^{2}.
\]
Now
\[
M_{x,s} - \{\theta^{2} - (x-2s-1)^{2}\}
\]
multiplied into
\[
(-)^{s} A_{x-2,s} H_{x-2s-2}
\]

the value of the determinant.

\bigskip
\begin{center}\small\textsc{124}\hfill[187\end{center}

\begin{center}
\textbf{187.}\\[0.5em]
\textsc{On the Sums of Certain Series arising from the Equation $x = u + tf x$.}\\[0.4em]
\small [\textit{From the Quarterly Mathematical Journal}, vol.\ \textsc{ii}.\ (1858), pp.\ 167--171.]
\end{center}

\medskip
\noindent \textsc{Lagrange} has given the following formula for the sum of the inverse $n$th powers
of the roots of the equation $x = u + t f x$,
\[
\Sigma(x^{-n}) = u^{-n} + (-nu^{-n-1}f u)\frac{t}{1} + (-nu^{-n-1}f^{2} u)\frac{t^{2}}{1\cdot 2} + \&\text{c.} \qquad (1)
\]
where $n$ is a positive integer and the series on the second side of the equation is
to be continued as long as the exponent of $u$ remains negative (\textit{Th\'eorie des \'Equations
Num\'eriques}, p.\ 225). Applying this to the equation $x = 1 + tx^{s}$, we have
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\Sigma(x^{-n}) = 1 - \frac{n}{1}\,t\cdot 1^{-n+s-1} + \frac{n(n-2s+1)}{1\cdot 2}\,t^{2}\cdot 1^{-n+2s-1}\ldots + (-)^{q}\frac{n(n-qs+q-1)\ldots(n-qs+q+1)}{1\cdot 2\ldots q}\,t^{q}\cdot 1^{-n+qs-q} - \&\text{c.} \qquad (2)
$} \]
to be continued while the exponent of $1$ remains negative.

\medskip
Let $n = \mu + p$, $\mu$ being not greater than $s-1$, the series may always be continued
up to $q = \mu$, and no further. In fact writing the above value for $n$ and putting
$q = p + \theta$, the general term is
\[
(-)^{p+\theta}\frac{t^{p+\theta}}{1\cdot 2\ldots(\mu+\theta)}\,(\mu+p)(\mu+p-\theta s + \theta - 1)\ldots(\mu+p-\theta s + \theta + 1)\cdot 1^{-\mu+p-\theta s+\theta}.
\]
Now if $p + \mu - \theta(s-1)$ is negative or zero, the term is to be rejected on account
of the index of $1$ not being negative, and if this quantity be positive, then since

%---------------------------------------
% PDF p.147 = book p.125
%---------------------------------------
\begin{center}\small[187\hfill\textsc{on the sums of certain series \&c.}\hfill\textsc{125}\end{center}

\noindent $p - \theta s + 1$ is necessarily negative for any value of $\theta$ greater than zero, the factorial
$(p - \theta s + \mu + \theta - 1)\ldots(p - \theta s + 1)$ begins with a positive and ends with a negative factor,
and since the successive factors diminish by unity, one of them is necessarily equal to
zero, or the term vanishes; hence the series is always to be continued up to $q = \mu$.

Hence
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\Sigma(x^{-\mu-p}) = 1 - \frac{\mu+p}{1}\,t + \frac{(\mu+p)(\mu-2s+\rho+1)}{1\cdot 2}\,t^{2}\ldots + (-)^{\mu}\frac{(\mu+p)(\mu-qs+p+q+1)\ldots(\mu-qs+p+1)}{1\cdot 2\ldots q}\,t^{\mu} - \&\text{c.} \qquad (3)
$} \]
continued to $q = \mu$.

By taking the terms in a reverse order, it is easy to derive
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
(-)^{\mu} t^{-\mu}\Sigma(x^{-\mu-p}) = (\mu+p)\Bigg\{\frac{(\mu+p-1)\ldots(\mu+1)}{2\cdot 3\ldots p} + (-)^{q}\frac{(\mu+p)(\mu+p-1)\ldots(\mu+1-q)}{2\cdot 3\ldots(p+q)\cdot s}\,t^{-q} - \&\text{c.}\Bigg\} \qquad (4)
$} \]
continued to $q = \mu$.

\medskip
Suppose in particular $s = 2$, and $t = -\dfrac{a+1}{a^{2}}$, so that the equation in $x$ becomes
$\dfrac{1}{x^{2}} = \dfrac{a+1}{x} - \dfrac{a}{x^{2}}$, whence $x = a$ or substituting in (2), we find
\[
\Sigma\!\left(\frac{1}{x^{n}}\right) - \frac{a}{a^{n}} = 1 + \frac{n}{1}\frac{a+1}{a^{2}} + \frac{n(n-3)}{2}\frac{(a+1)^{2}}{a^{4}} + \&\text{c.} \qquad (5)
\]
continued to the term involving $\left(\dfrac{a+1}{a^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}n}$ or $\left(\dfrac{a+1}{a^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}(n-1)}$.

Put $a = -\dfrac{b}{a}$ and therefore
\[

\noindent Comparing these with the relations between $(x,y,z)$ and $(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})$, we see that

form by means of the linear substitutions, and any invariant of the system will remain
unaltered to a factor \textit{pr\`es}, such factor being a power of the determinant of substitution. The invariants are, $1^{\circ}$ the discriminant of the quadric; $2^{\circ}$ the reciprocant,
considered not as a contravariant of the quadric, but as an invariant of the system.

\noindent \textsc{In} Jacobi's Memoir ``De usu legitimo formulae summatoriae Maclaurinianae,'' \textit{Crelle},
t.\ \textsc{xii}.\ [1834], pp.\ 263--273 (1834), expressions are given for the sums of the odd
powers of the natural numbers $1, 2, 3\ldots x$ in terms of the quantity
\[
u = x(x+1),
\]
viz.\ putting for shortness
\[
S x^{r} = 1^{r} + 2^{r} + \ldots + x^{r},
\]
the expressions in question are
\[
\begin{aligned}
S x^{3} &= \tfrac{1}{2} u^{2},\\
S x^{5} &= \tfrac{1}{3} u^{2}(u - \tfrac{1}{2}),\\
S x^{7} &= \tfrac{1}{6} u^{2}(u^{2} - \tfrac{4}{3}u + \tfrac{2}{3}),\\
S x^{9} &= \tfrac{1}{5} u^{2}(u^{3} - 3u^{2} + 3u - \tfrac{3}{5}),\\
S x^{11} &= \tfrac{1}{2} u^{2}(u^{4} - 4u^{3} + \tfrac{27}{2}u^{2} - 10u + 5),\\
S x^{13} &= \tfrac{1}{4} u^{2}(u^{5} - \tfrac{5}{3}u^{4} + \tfrac{25}{3}u^{3} - 11u^{2} + \tfrac{691}{60}u - \tfrac{691}{105}),
\end{aligned}
\]
\&c.,
which, especially as regards the lower powers, are more simple than the ordinary
expressions in terms of $x$.

The expressions are continued by means of a recurring formula, viz.\ if
\[
S x^{2p-1} = \frac{1}{2p-2}\bigl[a_{1}u^{p} - a_{2}u^{p-1} + \ldots + (-)^{p-1} a_{p-1}u^{2}\bigr],
\]
\[
S x^{2p+1} = \frac{1}{2p}\bigl[u^{p} - b_{1}u^{p-1} + \ldots + (-)^{p} b_{p}u^{2}\bigr],
\]

%---------------------------------------
% PDF p.155 = book p.133
%---------------------------------------
\begin{center}\small[189\hfill\textsc{note on a formula in finite differences.}\hfill\textsc{133}\end{center}

\noindent then
\[
\begin{aligned}
2p(2p-1)\,a_{1} &= (2p-2)(2p-3)\,b_{1} - p(p-1),\\
2p(2p-1)\,a_{2} &= (2p-2)(2p-3)\,b_{2} - 4(p-1)(p-2)\,b_{1},\\
2p(2p-1)\,a_{3} &= (2p-6)(2p-7)\,b_{3} - (p-2)(p-3)\,b_{2},\\
&\;\vdots\\
2p(2p-1)\,a_{p-2} &= 5\cdot 6\, b_{p-2} - 3\cdot 4\, b_{p-3},\\
0 &= 3\cdot 4\, b_{p-2} - 2\cdot 3\, b_{p-3},
\end{aligned}
\]
by means of which the coefficients $b$ can be determined when the coefficients $a$ are
known.

Jacobi remarks also that the expressions for the sums of the even powers may
be obtained from those for the odd powers by means of the formula
\[
S x^{2p} = \frac{1}{2p+1}\,\partial_{u} S x^{2p+1},
\]
which shows that any such sum will be of the form $(2x+1)u$ into a rational and
integral function of $u$: thus in particular
\[
S x^{2} = \tfrac{1}{6}(2x+1)u.
\]

To show \textit{a priori} that $S x^{2p+1}$ can be expressed as a rational and integral function
of $u$, it may be remarked that $S x^{2p+1} = \phi x$, where $\phi x$ denotes the summatory integral
$\Sigma(x+1)^{2p+1}$, taken so as to vanish for $x=0$: $\phi x$ is a rational and integral function
of $x$ of the degree $2p+2$, and which, as is well known, contains $x^{2}$ as a factor.
Suppose that $y$ is any positive or negative integer less than $x$, we have
\[
\phi x - \phi y = (y+1)^{2p+1} + (y+2)^{2p+1} + \ldots + x^{2p+1},
\]
and in particular putting $y = -1 - x$,
\[
\phi x - \phi(-1-x) = (-x)^{2p+1} + (1-x)^{2p+1} + \ldots + x^{2p+1} = 0,
\]
since the terms destroy each other in pairs; we have therefore $\phi x = \phi(-1-x)$.
Now $u = x^{2} + x$, or writing this equation under the form $x^{2} = -x + u$, we see that any
rational and integral function of $x$ may be reduced to the form $Px + Q$, where
$P$ and $Q$ are rational and integral functions of $u$. Write therefore $\phi x = Px + Q$: the
substitution of $-1-x$ in the place of $x$ leaves $u$ unaltered, and the equation
$\phi(-1-x) = \phi x$ thus shows that $P = 0$; we have therefore $\phi x = Q$, a rational and
integral function of $u$. Moreover $\phi x$ as containing the factor $x^{2}$, must clearly contain
the factor $u^{2}$, and the expressions for $S x^{2p+1}$ are thus shown to be of the form given
by Jacobi.

We may obtain a finite expression for $S x^{n}$ in terms of the differences of $0^{n}$ as
follows: we have
\[

\noindent and putting $(1+\Delta)^{x} = e^{x\log(1+\Delta)}$ and observing that the term independent of $x$ vanishes,

term in the ratio $2:1$, or we have
\[
S_{k}\left\{\frac{1+\Delta}{\Delta}\log^{k}(1+\Delta)\right\}\,0^{2p}\cdot\frac{(-)^{k}(M_{k-1} - 2u M_{k})}{\Pi k} = 0,\quad (k=1 \text{ to } k = 2p+1),
\]
and the formula becomes
\[
S x^{2p} = (2x+1)\, S_{k}\left\{\frac{1+\Delta}{\Delta}\log^{k}(1+\Delta)\right\}\,0^{2p}\cdot\frac{(-)^{k} u M_{k}}{\Pi k},\quad (k=1 \text{ to } k = 2p+1).
\]

% PDF p.157 = book p.135
%---------------------------------------
\begin{center}\small[189\hfill\textsc{note on a formula in finite differences.}\hfill\textsc{135}\end{center}

\noindent The values of the functions $M$ are as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{1} &= 0,\\
M_{2} &= 1,\\
M_{3} &= 1,\\
M_{4} &= 1 + u,\\
M_{5} &= 1 + 2u,\\
M_{6} &= 1 + 3u + u^{2},\\
M_{7} &= 1 + 4u + 3u^{2},\\
&\&\text{c.}
\end{aligned}
\]
As a simple example of the formulae, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
S x^{3} &= \left\{\frac{1+\Delta}{\Delta}\log^{2}(1+\Delta)\right\}\,0^{3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}u\\
&\quad - \left\{\frac{1+\Delta}{\Delta}\log^{3}(1+\Delta)\right\}\,0^{3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{6}u\\
&\quad + \left\{\frac{1+\Delta}{\Delta}\log^{4}(1+\Delta)\right\}\,0^{3}\cdot\tfrac{1}{24}(u + u^{2}),
\end{aligned}
\]
and the coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\Delta - \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{2})\, 0^{3} &= 1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 6 = 1,\\
(\Delta^{2} - \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{3})\, 0^{3} &= 6 - 4\cdot 6 = -3,\\
\Delta^{4}\, 0^{3} &= 3\cdot 3 = 6,
\end{aligned}
\]
and therefore
\[
S x^{3} = \tfrac{1}{2}u - \tfrac{1}{2}u + \tfrac{1}{4}(u + u^{2}) = \tfrac{1}{2}u^{2},
\]
which is right; the example shows however that the calculation for the higher powers
would be effected more readily by means of Jacobi's recurring formula.

\medskip
\noindent I \textsc{consider} the system of conics passing through the same four points; these
points may be real or imaginary, but it is assumed that there is a real system of
conics, this will in fact be the case if two conics of the system are real. The four
points are therefore given as the points of intersection of two real conics, and it will
be proper to assume in the first instance that the conics intersect in four separate
and distinct points, none of them at infinity. The four points may be all real, or
two real and two imaginary, or all imaginary.

First, if the points are all real, we have here two cases, viz.\ each of the points
may lie outside of the triangle formed by the other three, or as this may be expressed,
the points may form a convex quadrangle; or else one of the points may be inside
the triangle formed by the other three, or as this may be expressed, the points may
form a triangle and interior point. In each case the pairs of lines joining the points,
two and two together, will be conics (degenerate hyperbolas) forming part of the system
of conics. Consider the two cases separately.

\bigskip
\begin{center}\small\textsc{191}]\hfill\textsc{139}\end{center}

\begin{center}
\textbf{191.}\\[0.5em]
\textsc{Note on the Expansion of the True Anomaly.}\\[0.4em]
\small [\textit{From the Quarterly Mathematical Journal}, vol.\ \textsc{ii}.\ (1858), pp.\ 229--232.]
\end{center}

\medskip
\noindent \textsc{If} the true anomaly and the mean anomaly are respectively denoted by $u$, $m$,
and if $e$ be the eccentricity, then as usual $u - e \sin u = m$; and if we write
\[
\lambda = \frac{1 - \sqrt{1 - e^{2}}}{e},
\]
and take $c$ to denote the base of the hyperbolic system of logarithms, we have
\[
u = m + 2\Sigma_{1}^{\infty} A_{r}\frac{\sin r m}{r},
\]
and
\[
A_{r} = \lambda^{r} c^{\frac{1}{2}r e(\lambda - \lambda^{-1})} + \lambda^{-r} c^{-\frac{1}{2}r e(\lambda - \lambda^{-1})},
\]
where, after expanding the exponentials, the negative powers of $\lambda$ are to be rejected
and the term independent of $\lambda$ is to be multiplied by $\tfrac{1}{2}$ (see \textit{Camb.\ Math.\ Journal},
t.\ \textsc{i}.\ [1839] p.\ 228 and t.\ \textsc{iii}.\ [1843] p.\ 165, [4]).

It is easily seen that $e^{r}$ is the lowest power of $e$ which enters into the value
of $A_{r}$, and the question arises to find the numerical coefficient of the term in
question; this is readily obtained from the formula; in fact considering first a term
of the form
\[
\lambda^{-r} c^{\frac{1}{2}r e \cdot s}(\lambda - \lambda^{-1})^{s},
\]
since $\lambda$ is itself of the order $e$, when the negative powers of $\lambda$ are rejected this is at
least of the order $e^{s}$ and it is consequently to be neglected if $s > r$. But if $s < r$ all
the powers of $\lambda$ are negative and the term is to be rejected. The only case to be

%---------------------------------------
% PDF p.162 = book p.140
%---------------------------------------
\begin{center}\small\textsc{140}\hfill\textsc{note on the expansion of the true anomaly.}\hfill[191\end{center}

\noindent considered is therefore that of $s = r$, in which case there is a term containing $e^{r}$. We
thus obtain from $\lambda^{-r} c^{\frac{1}{2}r e \cdot r}(\lambda - \lambda^{-1})^{r}$ the term
\[
\frac{1}{2^{r}}\cdot\frac{r^{r} e^{r}}{1\cdot 2\cdot 3\ldots r}.
\]
In the next place a term of the form $\lambda^{r} c^{\frac{1}{2}r e \cdot s}(\lambda - \lambda^{-1})^{s}$ is at least of the order $e^{s}$
if $s > r$, or the terms to be considered are those for which $s = r$ or $s < r$. But in such
term the only part of the order $e^{r}$ is
\[
(-)^{s}\lambda^{-s+r}\, c^{\frac{1}{2}r e \cdot s},
\]
or, since neglecting higher powers of $e$ we have $\lambda = \tfrac{1}{2}e$, this is
\[
(-)^{s}\, 2^{-r+s}\, r^{s} e^{r},
\]
and the set of terms arising from
\[
\lambda^{r} c^{\frac{1}{2}r e \cdot s}(\lambda - \lambda^{-1})^{s}
\]
is
\[
\frac{e^{r}}{2^{r}}\left\{\frac{1}{1} + \frac{r}{1} + \frac{r^{2}}{1\cdot 2} + \ldots + \frac{r^{r-1}}{1\cdot 2\ldots(r-1)} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{r^{r}}{1\cdot 2\ldots r}\right\},
\]
the last term being divided by $2$ because arising from a term independent of $\lambda$.
Hence the first term of $A_{r}$ is
\[
\frac{e^{r}}{2^{r}}\left\{\frac{1}{1} + \frac{r}{1} + \frac{r^{2}}{1\cdot 2} + \ldots + \frac{r^{r}}{1\cdot 2\ldots r}\right\},
\]
a result which it may be remarked is contained in the general formula given in
Hansen's Memoir ``Entwickelung des Products u.\ s.\ w.,'' \textit{Leipzig Trans.}, t.\ \textsc{ii}.\ p.\ 277 (1853).

The preceding expression is
\[
\frac{e^{r} c^{r}}{2^{r}}\cdot\frac{1}{\Gamma(r+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty} x^{r} c^{-x}\, dx,
\]
and to find its value when $r$ is large, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{0}^{\infty} x^{r} c^{-x}\, dx &= \int_{0}^{\infty}(y + r)^{r} c^{-y-r}\, dy = r^{r} c^{-r}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1 + \frac{y}{r}\right)^{r} c^{-y}\, dy\\
&= r^{r} c^{-r}\int_{0}^{\infty} c^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2r} + \frac{y^{3}}{3r^{2}} - \&\text{c.}}\, dy\\
&= r^{r} c^{-r}\int_{0}^{\infty} c^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2r}}\left(1 + \frac{y^{3}}{3r^{2}} + \ldots\right) dy\\
&= r^{r} c^{-r}\sqrt{2r}\left(1 + \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3\sqrt{r}} + \ldots\right) c^{-\frac{1}{2}}\, dz.
\end{aligned}
\]
