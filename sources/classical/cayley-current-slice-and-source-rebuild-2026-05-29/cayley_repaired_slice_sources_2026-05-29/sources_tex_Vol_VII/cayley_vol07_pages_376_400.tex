\section*{463. \textsc{Note on a Differential Equation.}}

\begin{center}
\textit{[From the Memoirs of the Literary and Philosophical Society of
Manchester, vol.\ \textsc{ii}.\ ($1865$), pp.\ $111$--$114$. Read
February $18$, $1862$.]}
\end{center}

\medskip

The following investigation was suggested to me by Mr Harley's
``Remarks on the Theory of the Transcendental Solution of Algebraic
Equations,'' communicated to the Society at the Meeting of the $4$th
of February.

Mr Harley's equation
\[
y^{n} - ny + (n-1)\, x = 0
\]
may be written
\[
y = \frac{n-1}{n}\, x + \frac{1}{n}\, y^{n};
\]
or putting
\[
\frac{n-1}{n}\, x = u, \qquad \frac{1}{n} = a,
\]
it becomes
\[
y = u + a y^{n},
\]
which equation may be considered instead of the original equation; and
it is to be shown that $y$, regarded as a function of $u$, satisfies a
certain linear differential equation of the order $n-1$. In fact,
expanding $y$ by Lagrange's theorem, we have
\begin{align*}
y &= u + a u^{n} + \frac{a^{2}}{1\cdot 2}\, (u^{2n})'
   + \frac{a^{3}}{1\cdot 2\cdot 3}\, (u^{3n})'' + \&\mathrm{c}.\\[2pt]
  &= u + a u^{n} + \frac{a^{2}}{1\cdot 2}\, 2n\, u^{2n-1}
   + \frac{a^{3}}{1\cdot 2\cdot 3}\, 3n\,(3n-1)\, u^{3n-2} + \&\mathrm{c}.,
\end{align*}
the law whereof is obvious, and using the ordinary notation of
factorials, viz.
\[
[u]^{r} = u\,(u-1)\,(u-2)\,\ldots\,(u-r+1),
\]
we may write
\[
y = S_{\theta}\, \frac{[n\theta]^{\theta-1}}{[\theta]^{\theta}}\, a^{\theta}\, u^{n\theta-\theta+1},
\]
where $\theta$ extends from $0$ to $\infty$.

It is now very easy to show that $y$ satisfies the differential
equation
\[
\left[u\,\frac{d}{du}\right]^{n-1} y
\;=\; n a\, \frac{d}{du}\cdot
\left[\frac{n}{n-1}\, u\,\frac{d}{du} - \frac{2n-1}{n-1}\right]^{n-1}
u^{n-1}\, y.
\]
In fact, using on the left-hand side the foregoing value of $y$, and
on the right-hand side the following value of $u^{n-1}\,y$, obtained
from that of $y$ by writing $\theta-1$ in the place of $\theta$, viz.
\[
u^{n-1}\, y \;=\; S_{\theta}\,
\frac{[n\theta - n]^{\theta-2}}{[\theta-1]^{\theta-1}}\,
a^{\theta-1}\, u^{n\theta-\theta+n},
\]
and observing that in general the symbol $u\,\dfrac{d}{du}$, as
regards $u^{m}$, is $=m$, the equation in question will be satisfied,
if only
\[
\frac{[n\theta]^{\theta-1}}{[\theta]^{\theta}}\,\{(n-1)\theta + 1\}^{n-1}
\;=\;
\frac{n\,[n\theta - n]^{\theta-2}}{[\theta-1]^{\theta-1}}
\left[\frac{n}{n-1}\,\{(n-1)\theta+1\} - \frac{2n-1}{n-1}\right]^{n-1},
\]
where the right-hand side is
\[
=\; \frac{n\,[n\theta - n]^{\theta-2}}{[\theta-1]^{\theta-1}}\,\{n\theta - 1\}^{n-1};
\]
and the equation may be written
\[
\frac{n\theta\,[n\theta - 1]^{\theta-1}}{\theta\,[\theta - 1]^{\theta-1}}\,
\{(n-1)\theta + 1\}^{n-1}
\;=\;
\frac{n\,[n\theta - n]^{\theta-2}}{[\theta - 1]^{\theta-1}}\,
\{n\theta - 1\}^{n-1};
\]
that is,
\[
\{n\theta - 1\}^{\theta - 1}\,\{(n-1)\theta + 1\}^{n-1}
\;=\;
[n\theta - 1]^{n-1}\,\{n\theta - n\}^{\theta-2},
\]
which, since each side of the equation is $[n\theta-1]^{\theta+n-2}$,
is obviously true.

The foregoing differential equation is developable in the form
\[
\left[a_{0} + a_{1}\, u\,\frac{d}{du}
        + a_{2}\, u^{2}\!\left(\frac{d}{du}\right)^{\!2}
        + \ldots + a_{n-1}\, u^{n-1}\!\left(\frac{d}{du}\right)^{\!n-1}\right] y
\;=\; \frac{1}{n a}\left(\frac{d}{du}\right)^{\!n-1} y;
\]
but to find the coefficients $a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{n-1}$, I start
from this form, and proceed to substitute in the equation the value of
$y$, which on the left-hand side I use in the original form, and on
the right-hand side in the form obtained by writing $\theta + 1$ in
the place of $\theta$, viz.
\[
y \;=\; S_{\theta}\, \frac{[n(\theta+1)]^{\theta}}{[\theta+1]^{\theta+1}}\,
a^{\theta+1}\, u^{n\theta + n - \theta}.
\]

The equation to be satisfied is
\[
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\frac{[n\theta]^{\theta-1}}{[\theta]^{\theta}}
\Big( a_{0} + a_{1}[(n-1)\theta + 1]^{1} + a_{2}[(n-1)\theta+1]^{2}
+ \ldots + a_{n-1}[(n-1)\theta+1]^{n-1} \Big)
\;=\; \frac{1}{n}\,\frac{[n(\theta+1)]^{\theta}}{[\theta+1]^{\theta+1}}\,
\{(n-1)\theta + n\}^{n-1},
$}\]
or, what is the same thing,
\[
\frac{1}{[\theta]^{\theta}}\Big( a_{0}[n\theta]^{\theta-1}
+ a_{1}[n\theta]^{\theta} + a_{2}[n\theta]^{\theta+1}
+ \ldots + a_{n-1}[n\theta]^{\theta+n-2} \Big)
\;=\; \frac{1}{n}\,\frac{[n(\theta+1)]^{\theta+n-1}}{[\theta+1]^{\theta+1}}.
\]
Observing that the right-hand side may be written
\[
\frac{1}{n}\,\frac{n(\theta+1)\,[n\theta + n - 1]^{\theta+n-2}}{(\theta+1)\,[\theta]^{\theta}},
\]
the equation becomes
\[
a_{0}[n\theta]^{\theta-1} + a_{1}[n\theta]^{\theta}
+ a_{2}[n\theta]^{\theta+1} + \ldots
+ a_{n-1}[n\theta]^{\theta+n-2}
\;=\; [n\theta + n - 1]^{\theta+n-2},
\]
or, what is the same thing,
\[
a_{0} + a_{1}[(n-1)\theta + 1]^{1}
+ a_{2}[(n-1)\theta + 1]^{2} + \ldots
+ a_{n-1}[(n-1)\theta + 1]^{n-1}
\;=\; [n\theta + n - 1]^{n-1};
\]
so that $a_{0}, a_{1}, \ldots, a_{n-1}$ are the coefficients of the
expansion of $[n\theta + n - 1]^{n-1}$ (which is a rational and
integral function of $\theta$, of the degree $n-1$) in a factorial
series, as shown by the left-hand side of the equation.

To determine the actual values, write
\[
(n-1)\theta + 1 = \phi;
\]
this gives
\[
n\theta + n - 1 \;=\; \frac{n\phi + n^{2} - 3n + 1}{n - 1},
\]
and we have therefore
\[
\left[\frac{n\phi + n^{2} - 3n + 1}{n - 1}\right]^{n-1}
\;=\; a_{0} + a_{1}[\phi]^{1} + a_{2}[\phi]^{2}
+ \ldots + a_{n-1}[\phi]^{n-1};
\]
and thus the general expression is
\[
a_{r} \;=\; \frac{1}{[r]^{r}}\,
\Delta^{r}\!\left(\frac{n\phi + n^{2} - 3n + 1}{n - 1}\right)^{\!n-1},
\]
where $\Delta$ denotes the difference in regard to $\phi$
($\Delta U_{\phi} = U_{\phi+1} - U_{\phi}$), and, after the operation
$\Delta^{r}$ is performed, $\phi$ is to be put equal to zero.

\section*{464. \textsc{Note on Plana's Lunar Theory.}}

\begin{center}
\textit{[From the Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society,
vol.\ \textsc{xxiii}.\ ($1862$:$1863$), pp.\ $211$--$215$.]}
\end{center}

\medskip

I have been much surprised to find that there is an error of the
order $m^{2}\gamma^{2}$, arising from the omission of a factor
$(1+\gamma^{2})^{-1}$, in the expression for $\dfrac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} + \delta u$,
as given by the equation (II.)' (\textit{Th\'eorie de la Lune},
t.\ \textsc{i}., p.\ $267$), being the equation made use of in the
theory for the determination of $\delta u$, the perturbation of the
reciprocal of the radius vector. This error may probably be the cause
of some of the discrepancies in the terms of the fourth and higher
orders, between Plana's results and those of Pont\'ecoulant and
Delaunay.

Plana's equation $(6)$, t.\ \textsc{i}., p.\ $260$, is
\begin{align*}
\frac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} + \delta u
&= a R'' \\
&\quad + f(e,\gamma)\, Q'e\cos\!\left(cv - \int\!\varpi\,dv\right) \\
&\quad - \left\{ f(e,\gamma)(1+\gamma^{2})(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}
   - \frac{a(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{a_{1}\,\psi(e,\gamma)} \right\} \\
&\quad + f(e,\gamma)\, P\gamma^{2}\,(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,\Theta,
\end{align*}
if for shortness
\[
\Theta \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}
- \left(1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\right)\cos\!\left(2gv - 2\!\int\!\theta\,dv\right)
+ \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\cos\!\left(4gv - 4\!\int\!\theta\,dv\right).
\]
$R''$ (p.\ $256$) should be
\[
R'' \;=\; \frac{1}{a a_{1}\,\psi(e,\gamma)}\cdot
\frac{\Omega_{1}\,\dfrac{du}{dv}\,\dfrac{d\Omega}{u\,dv}
- \pi\,(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv}{1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv},
\]
but, by an error which is implicitly corrected, the $\sigma$ which
multiplies $(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv$ is omitted.
Hence the equation $(6)$ becomes
\begin{align*}
\left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right)\!\left(\frac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} + \delta u\right)
&= \frac{a}{a a_{1}\,\psi(e,\gamma)}
\left\{ \Omega_{1} - \frac{du}{dv}\,\frac{d\Omega}{u\,dv}
- \sigma\,(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv \right\}\\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, Q'e\cos\!\left(cv - \int\!\varpi\,dv\right)\\
&\quad - \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right)\!\left\{
   f(e,\gamma)(1+\gamma^{2})(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}
   - \frac{a(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}}{a_{1}\,\psi(e,\gamma)} \right\}\\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, P\gamma^{2}(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,\Theta,
\end{align*}
in which equation
\[
\Omega_{1} = \frac{d\Omega}{du} + \frac{s}{u}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dv},
\quad \text{pp.\ $26$, $245$,} \qquad
\frac{d\Omega}{u^{t}\,dv} = \frac{\sigma\sigma_{t}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\, U,
\quad \text{p.\ $265$,}
\]
\[
\psi(e,\gamma) = \lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}, \qquad
f(e,\gamma) = \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}, \quad \text{p.\ $261$.}
\]
But retaining for greater convenience the function $f(e,\gamma)$ in
two of the terms, we have
\begin{align*}
\left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right)\!\left(\frac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} + \delta u\right)
&= \frac{a}{a a_{1}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}
\Big[\frac{d\Omega}{du} + \frac{s}{u}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dv} \\
&\hphantom{={}}\qquad
   - \frac{\sigma\sigma_{t}}{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\, U\,\frac{du}{dv}
   - \pi(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv \Big]\\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, Q'e\cos\!\left(cv - \int\!\varpi\,dv\right)\\
&\quad - \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}
\left\{(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}} - \frac{a}{a_{1}}\,(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right\}\\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, P\gamma^{2}(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,\Theta,
\end{align*}
which is
\begin{align*}
&\;= \frac{a}{a a_{1}}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
\left(\frac{d\Omega}{du} + \frac{s}{u}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dv}\right) \\
&\quad - a\, U\,\frac{du}{dv} \\
&\quad - \frac{a}{a_{1}}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv
   \quad \text{(destroyed by term \textit{infra})}\\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, Q'e\cos\!\left(cv - \int\!\varpi\,dv\right) \\
&\quad - \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}
\left\{(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}} - \frac{a}{a_{1}}(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right\} \\
&\quad - \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\,(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv \\
&\quad + \frac{a}{a_{1}}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv
   \quad \text{(destroyed by term \textit{supra})}\\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, P\gamma^{2}(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,\Theta;
\end{align*}
or, putting $u = \dfrac{1}{a}\,(\nu + \delta u)$, this becomes
\begin{align*}
\left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right)\!\left(\frac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} + \delta u\right)
&= \frac{a}{a a_{1}}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
\left(\frac{d\Omega}{du} + \frac{s}{u}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dv}\right) \\
&\quad - U\!\left(\frac{du}{dv} + \frac{d\delta u}{dv}\right) \\
&\quad - \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\,(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\, 2\!\int\!U\,dv \\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, Q'e\cos\!\left(cv - \int\!\varpi\,dv\right) \\
&\quad - \lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}
\left\{(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}} - \frac{a}{a_{1}}(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right\} \\
&\quad + \left(1 + 2\!\int\!U\,dv\right) f(e,\gamma)\, P\gamma^{2}(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,\Theta,
\end{align*}
agreeing with the Formula II.\ p.\ $265$, except that in Plana's last
term, instead of the factor $f(e,\gamma)\;(=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}})$,
we have the factor $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}$. That is, the last
term, as given by Plana, should be divided by $1 + \gamma^{2}$. And this
error is introduced from the formula (II.) into the formula (II.)',
p.\ $267$, viz.\ the incorrect factor $\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}$
is there replaced by its value $q$; whereas, the true value being
$\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}$, the factor in (II.)' should be
$\dfrac{q}{1+\gamma^{2}}$.

The corrected formula (II.)' is
\begin{align*}
- \frac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} - \delta u
&= - Q'\,\frac{qe}{1+\gamma^{2}}\,\cos\!\left(cv - \int\!\varpi\,dv\right) \\
&\quad + q\left\{(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}} - \frac{a}{a_{1}}(1+s^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\right\} \\
&\quad + \mu^{2}(R_{2} + R_{4}) - \mu^{2}\!\left(\frac{du}{dv} + \frac{d\delta u}{dv}\right) R_{1} \\
&\quad - 2\mu^{2}\!\left[\frac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} + \delta u
   + q\,(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}} - \frac{Q'qe}{1+\gamma^{2}}\cos\!\left(cv - \int\!\varpi\,dv\right)\right]\!\int R_{1}\,dv \\
&\quad - P q \gamma^{2}\,(1+\gamma^{2})^{-1}\,(1+s_{1}^{2})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,(1 - 2\mu^{2}\!\int\!R_{1}\,dv)\times \\
&\qquad \left\{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2} - (1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2})\cos(2gv - 2\!\int\!\theta dv)
   + \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\cos(4gv - 4\!\int\!\theta dv)\right\}.
\end{align*}

Observing that $P$ is of the order $m^{2}$, and that $q$ is
approximately equal to unity, the error in
$\dfrac{d^{2}\delta u}{dv^{2}} + \delta u$ is of the order
$m^{2}\gamma^{2}$, as noticed above. It may be right to mention that I
obtained the correction in the first instance by starting from the
fundamental equations, and not as here from the intermediate equation
$(6)$, so that there is not in that equation any error afterwards
implicitly corrected in the transformation to (II.)'.

\section*{465. \textsc{Note on the Lunar Theory.}}

\begin{center}
\textit{[From the Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society,
vol.\ \textsc{xxv}.\ ($1864$--$1865$), pp.\ $182$--$189$.]}
\end{center}

\medskip

I attend, in the expressions for the lunar coordinates, only to the
coefficients independent of $m$. Plana's values, taken to the fourth
order only, are as follows; for greater simplicity I write $a = 1$;
and, instead of $nt + \text{constant}$, $cnt + \text{constant}$,
$gnt + \text{constant}$, I write $g$, $l$, $c$, respectively; viz.,
$l$ is the mean longitude, $c$ the mean anomaly, $g$ the mean distance
from node: this being so, then $r$, $v$, $y$, denoting the radius
vector, longitude and latitude respectively, we have
\[
\tfrac{1}{r}\,(\text{Plana}) =
\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}
 & & \cos\ \ c \\
 + e - \tfrac{1}{8}e^{3} - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}e & \text{,,} & c\\
 + e^{2} - \tfrac{1}{3}e^{4} - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c\\
 + \tfrac{3}{8}e^{3} & \text{,,} & 3c\\
 + \tfrac{1}{3}e^{4} & \text{,,} & 4c\\
 - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2} & \text{,,} & 2g\\
 - \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{2}e & \text{,,} & c - 2g
\end{array}
\]
(but I omit Plana's term $+ \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{2}e\,\cos(2c+2g)$ which
should be $=0$).

\[
v\,(\text{Plana}) = l +
\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}
 & & \sin\ \ c \\
 + 2e - \tfrac{1}{4}e^{3} - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}e & \text{,,} & c\\
 + \tfrac{5}{4}e^{2} - \tfrac{11}{24}e^{4} - \tfrac{3}{4}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c\\
 + \tfrac{13}{12}e^{3} & \text{,,} & 3c\\
 + \tfrac{103}{96}e^{4} & \text{,,} & 4c\\
 - \tfrac{1}{4}\gamma^{2} - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2} + \tfrac{1}{4}\gamma^{4} & \text{,,} & 2g\\
 + \tfrac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}e & \text{,,} & c - 2g\\
 - \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{2}e & \text{,,} & c + 2g\\
 - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c - 2g\\
 + \tfrac{11}{48}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c + 2g\\
 + \tfrac{1}{32}\gamma^{4} & \text{,,} & 4g
\end{array}
\]

\[
y\,(\text{Plana}) =
\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}
 & & \sin\ \ g \\
\gamma - \gamma e^{2} - \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{4} & \text{,,} & g\\
+ \gamma e - \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma e^{3} & \text{,,} & c - g\\
+ \gamma e - \tfrac{7}{8}\gamma e^{3} - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{3}e & \text{,,} & c + g\\
+ \tfrac{17}{8}\gamma e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c - g\\
+ \tfrac{5}{8}\gamma e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c + g\\
+ \tfrac{17}{12}\gamma e^{3} & \text{,,} & 3c - g\\
+ \tfrac{5}{12}\gamma e^{3} & \text{,,} & 3c + g\\
- \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{3} & \text{,,} & 3g\\
+ \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{3}e & \text{,,} & c - 3g\\
- \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{3}e & \text{,,} & c + 3g.
\end{array}
\]

To compare these with the elliptic values, it is necessary to write
$e\,(1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2})$ in place of $e$. Making this change, or
say reducing Plana's $(e,\gamma)$ to the elliptic $(e,\gamma)$, I write
down in a first column the transformed coefficients, and in a second
column the elliptic coefficients, as follows:
\[
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{array}{l|l|l}
\multicolumn{1}{c|}{\text{Plana, with Elliptic } e, \gamma}
& \multicolumn{1}{c|}{\text{Elliptic}}
& \\ \hline
\dfrac{1}{r} = & \dfrac{1}{r} = & \\
\hline
1 & 1 & \cos\ \ c \\
+ e - \tfrac{1}{8}e^{3} & + e - \tfrac{3}{8}e^{3} & \text{,,}\ \ \ c \\
+ e^{2} - \tfrac{1}{3}e^{4} & + e^{2} - \tfrac{1}{3}e^{4} & \text{,,}\ \ 2c \\
+ \tfrac{3}{8}e^{3} & + \tfrac{3}{8}e^{3} & \text{,,}\ \ 3c \\
+ \tfrac{1}{3}e^{4} & + \tfrac{1}{3}e^{4} & \text{,,}\ \ 4c \\
- \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2} & 0 & \text{,,}\ \ 2g \\
- \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{2}e & 0 & \text{,,}\ \ c - 2g.
\end{array}
\]
\[
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{array}{l|l|l}
\multicolumn{1}{c|}{\text{Plana, with Elliptic } e, \gamma}
& \multicolumn{1}{c|}{\text{Elliptic}}
& \\ \hline
v = & v = & \\
\hline
l & l & \sin\ \ c \\
+ 2e - \tfrac{1}{4}e^{3} & + 2e - \tfrac{1}{4}e^{3} & \text{,,}\ \ \ c \\
+ \tfrac{5}{4}e^{2} - \tfrac{11}{24}e^{4} - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2}
   & + \tfrac{5}{4}e^{2} - \tfrac{11}{24}e^{4} & \text{,,}\ \ 2c \\
+ \tfrac{13}{12}e^{3} & + \tfrac{13}{12}e^{3} & \text{,,}\ \ 3c \\

\]

where, for greater clearness, I remark that the values called
``elliptic'' of $e$, $\gamma$, $c$, $g$, refer to an ellipse, such
that the longitude of the node, and the longitude (in orbit) of the
pericentre, vary uniformly with the time, viz., we have mean
$\text{distance} = 1$, $\text{excentricity} = e$, $\text{tangent of inclination} = \gamma$,
$\text{mean longitude} = l$, $\text{mean anomaly} = c$, $\text{distance from node} = g$.

We have therefore
\[
\delta\frac{1}{r} =
\begin{array}{r@{\quad}l@{\quad}l}
 - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \cos & 2g \\
 - \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{2}e & \text{,,} & c - 2g, \\
\delta v = - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \sin & 2c \\
 - \tfrac{11}{48}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2g \\
 + \tfrac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}e & \text{,,} & c - 2g \\
 - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c - 2g, \\
\delta y = - \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{3} + \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma e^{2} & \sin & c - g \\
 + \tfrac{2}{3}\gamma e^{2} & \text{,,} & 2c - g \\
 + \tfrac{2}{3}\gamma e^{3} & \text{,,} & 3c - g \\
 + \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{3}e & \text{,,} & c - 3g,
\end{array}
\]
viz., these are the increments to be added to the elliptic values of
$\dfrac{1}{r}$, $v$, $y$, respectively, in order to obtain the
disturbed values of $\dfrac{1}{r}$, $v$, $y$, attending only to the
coefficients independent of $m$; \textit{they represent, in fact, the
Lunar inequalities which rise two orders by integration.}

The elliptic values of $\dfrac{1}{r}$ and $y$ are functions, and that
of $v$, is equal to $l +$ a function, of $e$, $\gamma$, $c$, $g$, and
the foregoing disturbed values may be obtained by affecting each of
the quantities $e$, $\gamma$, $c$, $g$, and $l$, with an inequality
depending on the argument $2c - 2g$, viz., these inequalities are
\begin{align*}
\delta e &= - \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{2}e \cos\,2c - 2g \\
\delta c &= \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{2} \sin\,2c - 2g \\
\delta\gamma &= - \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma e^{2} \cos\,2c - 2g \\
\delta g &= \tfrac{3}{8}e^{2} \sin\,2c - 2g \\
\delta l &= - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2} \sin\,2c - 2g.
\end{align*}
The verification may be effected without difficulty; thus, for
instance, starting from the elliptic value of $\dfrac{1}{r}$, we have
to the fourth order
\begin{align*}
\delta\frac{1}{r} &= \delta\!\binom{e\cos\, c}{+\, e^{2}\cos\, 2c}
   \binom{-\,e\sin\, c}{-\,2e^{2}\sin\, 2c}\delta c'
   + \binom{\cos\, c}{+\,2e\cos\, 2c}\delta e \\
&= - \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma e\,(-\sin\, c\,\sin\, 2c - 2g\,\cos\, c\,\cos\, 2c - 2g) \\
&\quad + \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{2}\,(-\sin\, 2c\,\sin\, 2c - 2g\,-\,\cos\, 2c\,\cos\, 2c - 2g) \\
&= - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}e\,\cos\, c - g \\
&\quad - \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{2}\,\cos\, 2g,
\end{align*}
which is right; and the verification of the values of $\delta v$,
$\delta y$, may be effected in a similar manner.

I have, in order to fix the ideas, preferred to give in the first
instance the foregoing \textit{\`a posteriori} proof; but I now
inquire generally as to the form of the values of $\dfrac{1}{r}$,
$v$, $y$, or say of $r$, $v$, $y$, taking account only of coefficients
independent of $m$; and I proceed to show that these may be obtained
from the elliptic values expressed as above in terms of $l$, $e$,
$\gamma$, $c$, $g$, by affecting $l$, $e$, $\gamma$, $c$, $g$, each
with an inequality depending on the multiple sines or cosines of
$c - g$.

Writing for greater simplicity $n = 1$, we have
$l = t + L,\ c = ct + C,\ g = gt + G$, where
$c = 1 - \tfrac{3}{4}m^{2} + \&\mathrm{c}.,\ g = 1 + \tfrac{3}{4}m^{2} + \&\mathrm{c}.$;
viz., $c$, $g$, are constants which differ from unity by terms
involving $m^{2}$.

The required values of $r$, $v$, $y$, satisfy the undisturbed
equations of motion, if after the differentiations we write in the
coefficients (which coefficients are functions of $m$ through $c$, $g$)
$m = 0$; that is, if we write in the coefficients $c = 1$, $g = 1$. In
fact, the required values of $r$, $v$, $y$, are what the complete
values become, upon writing in the coefficients of the complete values
$m = 0$; that is, the required values of $r$, $v$, $y$, differ from the
complete values by terms the coefficients whereof contain $m$ as a
factor; and the disturbed equations differ from the undisturbed
equations in that they contain the differential coefficients of the
disturbing function; that is, terms the coefficients whereof have the
factor $m^{2}$. Imagine the complete values of $r$, $v$, $y$,
substituted in the disturbed equations of motion; the resulting
equations are satisfied identically; and, therefore, whatever be the
value of $m$; that is, they are satisfied if in these equations
respectively we write $m = 0$: it requires a little consideration to
see that this is so, if in \textit{the coefficients only} we write
$m = 0$; but recollecting that $c$, $g$, stand for functions $ct + C$,
$gt + G$, so that, for example, $c - g, = (c - g)t + C - G$, upon
writing therein $m = 0$, becomes equal, not to zero, but to the
constant value $C - G$, the identity subsists in regard to the
coefficient of the sine or cosine of each separate argument
$ac \pm \beta g$, and, consequently, it subsists notwithstanding that
in the arguments $c$ and $g$, instead of being each put $= 1$, are
left indeterminate. And granting this (viz.\ that the equations are
satisfied if in the coefficients only we write $m = 0$), then it is
clear that, as above stated, the required values of $r$, $v$, $y$,
satisfy the undisturbed equations of motion, if after the
differentiations we write in the coefficients $c = 1$, $g = 1$.

The required values of $r$, $v$, $y$, are of the form
$r = \phi(c,g),\ y = \psi(c,g),\ v = l + \chi(c,g)$, but writing
$w = v + c - l, = c + \chi(c,g)$, the last mentioned property will
equally subsist in regard to the functions $r$, $w$, $y$: in fact, $v$
enters into the differential equations only through its differential
coefficient $\dfrac{dv}{dt}$, and the differential coefficients of $v$
and $w$, that is, of $l + \chi(c,g)$ and $c + \chi(c,g)$, differ only
by the quantity $c - 1$, which becomes $=0$, in virtue of the assumed
relations $c = 1$, $g = 1$.

Hence the undisturbed equations are satisfied by the values
$r = \phi(c,g),\ y = \psi(c,g),\ w = c + \chi(c,g)$, when after the
differentiations we write in the coefficients $c = 1$, $g = 1$; the
foregoing values contain $t$ through the quantities $c$, $g$, only;
and we have, therefore,
\[
\frac{d}{dt} \;=\; \frac{d}{dc} + \frac{d}{dg}.
\]
Hence, writing in the coefficients $c = 1$, $g = 1$, we have
$\dfrac{d}{dt} = \dfrac{d}{dc} + \dfrac{d}{dg}$; that is, the values
$r = \phi(c,g),\ y = \psi(c,g),\ w = \chi(c,g)$, regarding $r$, $v$,
$y$, as functions of $c$, $g$, satisfy the partial differential
equations obtained from the undisturbed equations of motion by
writing therein $\dfrac{d}{dc} + \dfrac{d}{dg}$ in place of
$\dfrac{d}{dt}$. Hence also, considering $r$, $w$, $y$, as functions
of $c$ and $c - g$, then observing that $\left(\dfrac{d}{dc} + \dfrac{d}{dg}\right)(c-g) = 0$,
the values of $r$, $v$, $y$, satisfy the partial differential
equations obtained by writing $\dfrac{d}{dc}$ in place of
$\dfrac{d}{dt}$; and inasmuch as these partial differential equations
do not contain $\dfrac{d}{dg}$, they are to be integrated as ordinary
differential equations in regard to $c$ as the independent variable,
the constants of integration being replaced by arbitrary functions of
$c - g$.

Consider the pure elliptic values of $r$, $v$, $y$, in an elliptic
orbit with the following elements: $A$, the mean distance; $N$, the
mean motion ($N^{2}A^{3} = 1$ and therefore $A = N^{-\frac{2}{3}}$);
$E$, the excentricity; $Nt + D$, the mean anomaly; $Nt + H$, the mean
distance from node; $Nt + K$, the mean longitude; then writing $c$ in
place of $t$, we have
\begin{align*}
r &= N^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,\mathrm{elqr}\,(E,\, Nc + D), \\
v\,(= l - c + w) &= l - c + Nc + K + P(E,\, \Gamma,\, Nc + D,\, Nc + H), \\
y &= Q(E,\, \Gamma,\, Nc + D,\, Nc + H),
\end{align*}
where $N$, $E$, $\Gamma$, $D$, $H$, $K$, are arbitrary functions of
$c - g$: $P$ and $Q$ denote given functional expressions. But, in
order that $r$, $v$, $y$, considered as functions of $c$ and $g$, may
be of the proper form, it is necessary as regards $N$ to write simply
$N = 1$; we have then
\begin{align*}
r &= \mathrm{elqr}\,(E,\, c + D), \\
v &= l + K + P(E,\, \Gamma,\, c + D,\, c + H), \\
y &= Q(E,\, \Gamma,\, c + D,\, c + H),
\end{align*}
where $E$, $\Gamma$, $D$, $H$, $K$, are arbitrary functions of $c - g$;
or, what is the same thing, writing for these quantities respectively
$e + \delta e,\ \gamma + \delta \gamma,\ c + \delta c,\ g + \delta g,\ l + \delta l$,
where $\delta e,\ \delta \gamma,\ \delta c,\ \delta g,\ \delta l$ are
arbitrary functions of $c - g$, we have
\begin{align*}
r &= \mathrm{elqr}\,(e + \delta e,\, c + \delta c), \\
v &= l + \delta l + P(e + \delta e,\, \gamma + \delta \gamma,\, c + \delta c,\, g + \delta g), \\
y &= Q(e + \delta e,\, \gamma + \delta \gamma,\, c + \delta c,\, g + \delta g),
\end{align*}
that is, the values of $r$, $v$, $y$, are obtained from the elliptic
values
\begin{align*}
r &= \mathrm{elqr}\,(e,\, c), \\
v &= l + P(e,\, \gamma,\, c,\, g), \\
y &= Q(e,\, \gamma,\, c,\, g),
\end{align*}
by affecting each of the quantities $e,\ \gamma,\ c,\ g,\ l$, with an
inequality which is a function of $c - g$.

\section*{466. \textsc{Second Note on the Lunar Theory.}}

\begin{center}
\textit{[From the Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society,
vol.\ \textsc{xxv}.\ ($1864$--$1865$), pp.\ $203$--$207$.]}
\end{center}

\medskip

The elliptic values of
\[
r,\ \text{the radius vector,} \qquad
v,\ \text{the longitude,} \qquad
y,\ \text{the latitude,}
\]
are functions of
\begin{align*}
&a,\ \text{the mean distance,}\\
&e,\ \text{the excentricity,}\\
&\gamma,\ \text{the tangent of the inclination,}\\
&l,\ \text{the mean longitude,}\\
&c,\ \text{the mean anomaly,}\\
&g,\ \text{the mean distance from node;}
\end{align*}
see my Note in the last Monthly Notice, p.\ $182$, [465], where, for the
present purpose, $\dfrac{a}{r}$ should be written instead of
$\dfrac{1}{r}$; and it is there shown that the disturbed values,
attending only to the coefficients independent of $m$, are obtained by
affecting $a,\ e,\ \gamma,\ c,\ g,\ l$, with the inequalities
\begin{align*}
\delta a &= 0 \\
\delta e &= - \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{2}e \cos\,2c - 2g \\
\delta \gamma &= + \tfrac{3}{8}\gamma e^{2}\ \ \,,, \ \ 2c - 2g \\
\delta c &= + \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{2}\,\sin\,2c - 2g \\
\delta g &= + \tfrac{3}{8}e^{2}\ \ \,,, \ \ 2c - 2g \\
\delta l &= - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2}\,,, \ \ 2c - 2g,
\end{align*}
or, what is the same thing, adding to the elliptic values the
inequalities
\begin{align*}
\delta\frac{a}{r} &= - \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma^{2}\cos\,2g \\
&\quad - \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{2}e \quad ,, \quad c - 2g, \\
\delta v &= - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2}\sin\,2c \\
&\quad - \tfrac{11}{48}\gamma^{2}e^{2}\ \ ,, \ \ 2g \\
&\quad + \tfrac{1}{4}\gamma^{2}e \quad ,, \quad c - 2g \\
&\quad - \tfrac{1}{16}\gamma^{2}e^{2}\ \ ,, \ \ 2c - 2g, \\
\delta y &= -\tfrac{3}{8}\gamma^{3} + \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma e^{2}\,\sin\, c - g \\
&\quad + \tfrac{2}{3}\gamma e^{2}\quad ,, \quad 2c - g \\
&\quad + \tfrac{2}{3}\gamma e^{3}\quad ,, \quad 3c - g \\
&\quad + \tfrac{1}{8}\gamma^{3}e\quad ,, \quad c - 3g.
\end{align*}

I propose to show how these results may be obtained by the method of
the variation of the elements. For this purpose, treating $a,\ e,\ \gamma,\ c,\ g,\ l$,
as elements, the proper formulae are obtained very readily from those
given in my ``Memoir on the Problem of Disturbed Elliptic Motion,''
Mem.\ R.\ Ast.\ Soc., vol.\ \textsc{xxvii}.\ ($1859$), pp.\ $1$--$29$,
[212]; viz., writing $c$ in place of $g$, the formulae, p.\ $25$,
give the variations of $a$, $e$, $c$, $\eta$, $\theta$, $\phi$; we
have then
\[
g = c + \mathfrak{C}, \qquad
l = c + \mathfrak{C} + \theta,
\]
and therefore
\[
\gamma = \tan \phi,
\]
\begin{align*}
dg &= dc + d\mathfrak{C}, \\
dl &= dc + d\mathfrak{C} + d\theta, \\
d\gamma &= (1 + \gamma^{2})\,d\phi,
\end{align*}
which give for the transformation of the differential coefficients of
$\Omega$,
\begin{align*}
\frac{d\Omega}{dc} &= \frac{d\Omega}{dc} + \frac{d\Omega}{dg} + \frac{d\Omega}{dl}, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{d\mathfrak{C}} &= \frac{d\Omega}{dg} + \frac{d\Omega}{dl}, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{d\theta} &= \frac{d\Omega}{dl}, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{d\phi} &= (1+\gamma^{2})\,\frac{d\Omega}{d\gamma},
\end{align*}
and the formulae finally become
\begin{align*}
\frac{da}{dt} &= \frac{2}{na}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dc}
   + \frac{2}{na}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dg}
   + \frac{2}{na}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dl}, \\[2pt]
\frac{de}{dt} &= \frac{1 - e^{2}}{na^{2}e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dc}
   + \frac{1 - e^{2} - \sqrt{1 - e^{2}}}{na^{2}e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dg}
   + \frac{1 - e^{2} - \sqrt{1 - e^{2}}}{na^{2}e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dl}, \\[2pt]
\frac{d\gamma}{dt} &= \frac{1 + \gamma^{2}}{na^{2}\sqrt{1 - e^{2}}\,\gamma}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dg}
   + \frac{(1+\gamma^{2})(1 - \sqrt{1 + \gamma^{2}})}{na^{2}\sqrt{1 - e^{2}}\,\gamma}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dl}, \\[2pt]
\frac{dc}{dt} &= -\,\frac{2}{na}\,\frac{d\Omega}{da}
   - \frac{1 - e^{2}}{na^{2}e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{de}, \\[2pt]
\frac{dg}{dt} &= -\,\frac{2}{na}\,\frac{d\Omega}{da}
   - \frac{1 - e^{2} - \sqrt{1 - e^{2}}}{na^{2}e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{de}
   - \frac{1 + \gamma^{2}}{na^{2}\sqrt{1 - e^{2}}\,\gamma}\,\frac{d\Omega}{d\gamma}, \\[2pt]
\frac{dl}{dt} &= -\,\frac{2}{na}\,\frac{d\Omega}{da}
   - \frac{1 - e^{2} - \sqrt{1 + e^{2}}}{na^{2}e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{de}
   - \frac{(1+\gamma^{2})(1 - \sqrt{1 + \gamma^{2}})}{na^{2}\sqrt{1 - e^{2}}\,\gamma}\,\frac{d\Omega}{d\gamma}.
\end{align*}
The disturbing function contains the term
\[
m^{2}a^{2}a''\left(+\,\tfrac{3}{8}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\right)\cos\,2c - 2g.
\]
If after the differentiations we write for greater simplicity $a = 1$,
$n = 1$, we have
\begin{align*}
\frac{d\Omega}{da} &= +\,\tfrac{15}{4}\,m^{2}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\cos\,2c - 2g, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{de} &= +\,\tfrac{3}{4}\,m^{2}e\gamma^{2}\ ,, \ 2c - 2g, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{d\gamma} &= +\,\tfrac{3}{4}\,m^{2}e^{2}\gamma\ ,, \ 2c - 2g, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{dc} &= -\,\tfrac{3}{4}\,m^{2}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\sin\,2c - 2g, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{dg} &= -\,\tfrac{3}{4}\,m^{2}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\ ,, \ 2c - 2g, \\
\frac{d\Omega}{dl} &= 0,
\end{align*}
and the formulae for the variations give
\begin{align*}
\frac{da}{dt} &= \frac{2}{na}\!\left(\frac{d\Omega}{dc} + \frac{d\Omega}{dg}\right)
   = 0, \\
\frac{de}{dt} &= -\,\frac{1}{e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dc}
   = -\,\tfrac{3}{8}\,m^{2}e\gamma^{2}\sin\,2c - 2g, \\
\frac{d\gamma}{dt} &= -\,\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\frac{d\Omega}{dg}
   = -\,\tfrac{3}{8}\,m^{2}\gamma e^{2}\ ,, \ 2c - 2g, \\
\frac{dc}{dt} &= -\,\frac{1}{e}\,\frac{d\Omega}{de}
   = -\,\tfrac{3}{8}\,m^{2}\gamma^{2}\cos\,2c - 2g, \\
\frac{dg}{dt} &= -\,\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\frac{d\Omega}{d\gamma}
   = -\,\tfrac{3}{8}\,m^{2}e^{2}\ ,, \ 2c - 2g, \\
\frac{dl}{dt} &= -\,2\,\frac{d\Omega}{da} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,e\,\frac{d\Omega}{de}
   + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\gamma\,\frac{d\Omega}{d\gamma}
   = \left(-\,\tfrac{15}{2} + \tfrac{3}{8} + \tfrac{3}{8}\right)m^{2}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\ ,, \ 2c - 2g,
\end{align*}
but this value of $\dfrac{dl}{dt}$ is, as will presently be seen,
incomplete.

Writing $a + \delta a,\ e + \delta e$, \&c., in place of $a$, $e$,
\&c., and observing that the divisor for the integration of the term
in $2c - 2g$ is $2(c - g),\ = -\,3m^{2}$, the first five equations give
respectively
\begin{align*}
\delta a &= 0, \\
\delta e &= -\,\tfrac{3}{8}\,\gamma^{2}e\,\cos\,2c - 2g, \\
\delta \gamma &= +\,\tfrac{3}{8}\,\gamma e^{2}\ \ ,, \ \ 2c - 2g, \\
\delta c &= +\,\tfrac{1}{8}\,\gamma^{2}\,\sin\,2c - 2g, \\
\delta g &= +\,\tfrac{3}{8}\,e^{2}\ \ ,, \ \ 2c - 2g.
\end{align*}

The constant term in $\Omega$ is
\[
=\;m^{2}a^{2}a''(\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{3}{4}e^{2} - \tfrac{3}{4}\gamma^{2}),
\]
and this gives in
\[
\frac{dl}{dt} \;=\; -2\,\frac{d\Omega}{da} + \tfrac{1}{2}e\,\frac{d\Omega}{de}
+ \tfrac{1}{2}\gamma\,\frac{d\Omega}{d\gamma},
\]
a term
\[
m^{2}\!\left(-1 - \tfrac{9}{4}e^{2} + \tfrac{9}{4}\gamma^{2}\right)
+ \tfrac{3}{4}e^{2} - \tfrac{3}{4}\gamma^{2},
\]
which is
\[
=\;m^{2}\!\left(-1 - \tfrac{3}{2}e^{2} + \tfrac{3}{2}\gamma^{2}\right).
\]
Substituting for $e$, $\gamma$, their correct values $e + \delta e$,
$\gamma + \delta \gamma$, it appears that $\dfrac{dl}{dt}$ contains
the term
\[
m^{2}\!\left(-\tfrac{3}{2}e\,\delta e + \tfrac{3}{2}\gamma\,\delta\gamma\right),
\]
which is
\[
= m^{2}\!\left(\tfrac{9}{16} + \tfrac{9}{16}\right)e^{2}\gamma^{2}\cos\,2c - 2g
= \tfrac{9}{8}\,m^{2}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\ ,,\ 2c - 2g,
\]
and joining to this the before-mentioned term
\[
=\;-\,\tfrac{27}{8}\,m^{2}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\ ,,\ 2c - 2g,
\]
we find
\[
\frac{dl}{dt} \;=\; \left(\tfrac{9}{8} - \tfrac{27}{8}\right)m^{2}e^{2}\gamma^{2}\ ,,\ 2c - 2g,
\]
whence, writing as above $l + \delta l$ for $l$, and integrating, we
have
\[
\delta l \;=\; -\,\tfrac{1}{16}\,e^{2}\gamma^{2}\,\sin\,2c - 2g,
\]
and it thus appears that the values of $\delta a,\ \delta e,\ \delta\gamma,\ \delta c,\ \delta g,\ \delta l$,
agree with those obtained in my former Note.

\section*{467. \textsc{Expressions for Plana's $e$, $\gamma$ in Terms of the Elliptic $e$, $\gamma$.}}

\begin{center}
\textit{[From the Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society,
vol.\ \textsc{xxv}.\ ($1864$--$1865$), pp.\ $265$--$271$.]}
\end{center}

\medskip

The coefficient of $\sin cnt$ in Plana's expression for the true
longitude $v$ (see Plana, t.\ \textsc{i}.\ p.\ $574$), putting therein
$E' = e' = e'$, that is, neglecting the terms which depend on the
variation of the solar excentricity, is

\medskip

If for the planet $m$, referred to any fixed plane and origin of
longitudes, we have
\begin{align*}
&v,\ \text{the longitude in orbit,}\\
&\theta,\ \text{the longitude of node,}\\
&\phi,\ \text{the inclination,}
\end{align*}
and similarly for the planet $m'$ referred to the same fixed plane and
origin of longitudes, if the corresponding quantities are $v'$,
$\theta'$, $\phi'$; then the angular distance of the two planets will
of course be expressible in terms of $v$, $\theta$, $\phi$, $v'$,
$\theta'$, $\phi'$, but I am not aware that the actual expression has
been given. To obtain it in the most simple manner, I write further
for the planet $m$:
\begin{align*}
&\theta + x,\ \text{the reduced longitude,}\\
&y,\ \text{the latitude,}\\
&z,\ \text{the distance from node,}
\end{align*}
so that $z\ (= v - \theta)$, $x$, $y$, are the hypothenuse, base, and
perpendicular of a right-angled spherical triangle, the base angle of
which is $=\phi$. And similarly $\theta' + x',\ y',\ z'$, have the like
significations for the planet $m'$. I write also $r$, $r'$, for the
distances of the two planets respectively.

This being so, the rectangular coordinates of the planet $m$ are
\begin{align*}
&r\cos y\,\cos(\theta + x), \\
&r\cos y\,\sin(\theta + x), \\
&r\sin y.
\end{align*}

But observing that from the right-angled triangle we have
\begin{align*}
\cos z &= \cos x\,\cos y, \\
\cos \phi &= \tan x\,\cot z, \\
\sin x &= \cot \phi\,\tan y, \\
\sin y &= \sin \phi\,\sin z,
\end{align*}
and therefore also
\[
\sin x \cos y \;=\; \cot \phi\,\sin y, \;=\; \cos \phi\,\sin z,
\]
the expressions for the coordinates become
\begin{align*}
&r\,(\cos z\,\cos \theta - \sin \phi\,\sin \theta\,\cos \phi), \\
&r\,(\cos z\,\sin \theta + \sin z\,\cos \theta\,\cos \phi), \\
&r\,(\sin z\,\sin \phi).
\end{align*}

Forming the analogous expressions for the coordinates of $m'$, then if
$H$ be the angular distance of the two planets, we deduce at once the
expression for $\cos H$, viz.\ this is
\begin{align*}
r r'\cos H &= (\cos z\,\cos \theta - \sin z\,\sin \theta\,\cos \phi)\,(\cos z'\,\cos \theta' - \sin z'\,\sin \theta'\,\cos \phi') \\
&\quad + (\cos z\,\sin \theta + \sin z\,\cos \theta\,\cos \phi)\,(\cos z'\,\sin \theta' + \sin z'\,\cos \theta'\,\cos \phi') \\
&\quad + (\sin z\,\sin \phi)\,(\sin z'\,\sin \phi'),
\end{align*}
or, multiplying out, this is
\begin{align*}
r r'\cos H &= \cos z\,\cos z'\,\cos(\theta - \theta') \\
&\quad + \cos z\,\sin z'\,\sin(\theta - \theta')\,\cos \phi' \\
&\quad - \sin z\,\cos z'\,\sin(\theta - \theta')\,\cos \phi \\
&\quad + \sin z\,\sin z'\,(\cos(\theta - \theta')\,\cos \phi\,\cos \phi' + \sin \phi\,\sin \phi'),
\end{align*}

\[
r r'\cos H \;=\; A\,\cos z\,\cos z' + B\,\cos z\,\sin z' + C\,\sin z\,\cos z' + D\,\sin z\,\sin z',
\]

&\quad + \sin(z + z')\cdot \tfrac{1}{2}B + \tfrac{1}{2}C.
\end{align*}
