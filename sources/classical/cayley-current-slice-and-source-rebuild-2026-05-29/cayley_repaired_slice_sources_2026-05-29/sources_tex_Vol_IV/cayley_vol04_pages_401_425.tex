It may be remarked that in the present case, assuming the invariance of the
function in $y$, we may obtain the transformed equation in a very simple manner by
writing in the first instance $C=0$; this gives
\[ (a,b,c,d \mid x,1)^{3}=0, \qquad y=(ax+b)B, \]
and thence
\[ \tfrac{1}{a}(a,b,c,d \mid y-bB,\; aB)^{3}=0, \]
or developing,
\[ y^{3}+3y(ac-b^{2})B^{2}+(a^{2}d-3abc+2b^{3})B^{3}=0, \]
where the expressions for the coefficients are to be completed by the consideration
that these coefficients are covariants of the form $(a,b,c,d \mid B,C)^{3}$. But it is only in
the case in hand of a cubic equation that the transformed equation can be obtained
in this manner.

In the case of a quartic equation, we have
\[ (a,b,c,d,e \mid x,1)^{4}=0, \]
\[ y=(ax+b)B+(ax^{2}+2bx+3c)C+(ax^{3}+3bx^{2}+6cx+4d)D, \]
and these give the system of equations
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\left(\,y-bB-3cC-4dD,\; -aB-4bC-6cD,\; -aC-4bD,\; -aD\;\middle|\; 1,x,x^{2},x^{3}\right)=0,
$} \]
\[ \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\begin{vmatrix}
y-bB-3cC-4dD & -aB-4bC-6cD & -aC-4bD & -aD \\
eD & y-bB-3cC+\phantom{0}dD & -aB-4bC & -aC \\
eC & 4dC+eD & y-bB+3cC+dD & -aB \\
eB & 4dB+eC & 6cB+4dC+eD & y+3bB+3cC+dD
\end{vmatrix}=0,
$} \]
and the transformed equation is therefore found by equating to zero the determinant
formed out of the matrix contained in this equation.

The developed result, which was obtained by a different process in the
``Deuxi\`eme Note'' above referred to, is
\[ (1,0,\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{E} \mid y,1)^{4}=0, \]
where the coefficients $\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{E}$ are quartic, sextic, and octic
functions of the form $(a,b,c,d,e\mid B,C,D)^{k}$ in the two sets of variables; the
detailed expressions, given in the original on this and the following pages as a
two--dimensional array of monomial coefficients in $(a,b,c,d,e)$ keyed against the
monomial products of $(B,C,D)$, are reproduced below:

I write
\[ U'=aB^{3}+4bB^{2}C+c(2BD+4C^{2})+4dCD+eD^{2}, \]
\[ H'=(ac-b^{2})B^{2}+2(ad-bc)BC+(ae-2bd+c^{2})BD
       +4(bd-c^{2})C^{2}+2(be-cd)CD+(ce-d^{2})D^{2}, \]
and I represent by $\Phi'$ the expression which has just been found for $\mathfrak{E}$. These
functions $U',H',\Phi'$ are invariants of the two forms
\[ (a,b,c,d,e \mid X,Y)^{4},\qquad (B,C,D \mid Y,-X)^{2}; \]
we have, moreover, the invariants
\[ I=ae-4bd+3c^{2},\qquad J=ace-ad^{2}-b^{2}e+2bcd-c^{3} \]

The expression for the invariant $I$ (quadrinvariant) of the function
$(1,0,\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{E} \mid y,1)^{4}$ is $\mathfrak{E}+3\mathfrak{C}^{2}$, or
$\Phi'+3(H'-\tfrac{1}{3}I\Theta')^{2}$, viz.\ it is
\[ IU'^{\,2}-3H'^{\,2}+I^{2}\Theta'^{\,2}+12J\Theta'U'+2I\Theta'H' \]
or, finally, it is
\[ IU'^{\,2}+9H'^{\,2}+\tfrac{1}{3}I^{2}\Theta'^{\,2}+12J\Theta'U'-2I\Theta'H', \]
which is equal to
\[ IU'^{\,2}+\tfrac{1}{3}I^{2}\Theta'^{\,2}+12J\Theta'U', \]
\[ =\tfrac{1}{I}\!\left[(IU'+6J\Theta')^{2}+\tfrac{1}{3}(I^{3}-27J^{2})\Theta'^{\,2}\right]; \]
so that the condition in order that this invariant may be equal to zero is
\[ IU'+\!\left[6J\pm 2\sqrt{-\tfrac{1}{3}(I^{3}-27J^{2})}\right]\Theta'=0, \]
which agrees with a result of M.~Hermite's.

There should, I think, be an identical equation of the form
\[ JU'^{\,3}-IU'^{\,2}H'+4H'^{\,3}+M\Theta'=-\Phi'^{\,2}, \]
which would serve to express the square of the invariant $\Phi'$ in terms of the other
invariants $U',H',\Theta',I,J$; but assuming that such an equation exists, the form of
the factor $M$ remains to be ascertained. The invariant $J$ (cubinvariant) of the form
$(1,0,\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{E} \mid y,1)^{4}$ contains $\Phi'^{\,2}$, and it would be necessary
to make use of the identical equation just referred to in order to reduce it to its
simplest form; and (this being so) I have not sought for the expression of the
cubinvariant of $(1,0,\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D},\mathfrak{E} \mid y,1)^{4}$.

For the quintic we have the equations
\[ (a,b,c,d,e,f \mid x,1)^{5}=0, \]
\begin{align*}

  &\phantom{=} +(ax^{2}+2bx+3c)\,C \\
  &\phantom{=} +(ax^{3}+3bx^{2}+6cx+4d)\,D \\
  &\phantom{=} +(ax^{4}+4bx^{3}+10cx^{2}+10dx+5e)\,E,

And since upon writing $a=b=c=d=e=f=1$ the transformed equation becomes
$y^{5}=0$, it is clear that in the coefficient of any monomial product of $B,C,D,E$, the
sum of the numerical coefficients of the several monomial products of $a,b,c,d,e,f$
must be $=0$, which is the property above referred to as affording a verification of the
calculated expression of the transformed equation.

surface, and then, by means of a cubic equation, a point of intersection of this line

\textsc{16.} The last equation shows that $(a,\dots \mid \eta\zeta'-\eta'\zeta,\,\zeta\xi'-\zeta'\xi,\,\xi\eta'-\xi'\eta)^{2}$, considered
as a function of $(\xi',\eta',\zeta')$, breaks up into factors. Or since the expression is not

whatever, $(*\mid \eta\zeta'-\eta'\zeta,\,\zeta\xi'-\zeta'\xi,\,\xi\eta'-\xi'\eta)^{2}$, considered as a function of $(\xi,\eta,\zeta)$, breaks up
into linear factors; for in virtue of the equation $\xi'(\eta\zeta'-\eta'\zeta)+\eta'(\zeta\xi'-\zeta'\xi)+\zeta'(\xi\eta'-\xi'\eta)=0$,
any one of the quantities $\eta\zeta'-\eta'\zeta,\,\zeta\xi'-\zeta'\xi,\,\xi\eta'-\xi'\eta$ can be expressed as a linear
function of the other two; so that the quantic can be expressed as a linear function of
any two of the three quantities; and \emph{quel} homogeneous function of two quantities, it
of course breaks up into factors, linear functions of these two quantities.
