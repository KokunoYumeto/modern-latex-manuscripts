\noindent\textbf{204}\hfill\textsc{on the double tangents of a plane curve.}\hfill\textbf{[260}

\medskip

\noindent 29. I write now
\[
\alpha + \beta = \sigma,\qquad r - 2\delta - \sigma = \beta,
\]
and I consider the expression
\[
D^r H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^r H_1 + \&\text{c.};
\]
the general term of which is
\[
R_r^\delta\{\delta+1,\ \delta+2+s,\ (r-2\delta+1-s)\}
\]
\[
\times \left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\phantom{-} \frac{[n-1]^\delta}{1}\,[n-2]^\sigma[n-2]^\beta\\[2pt]
&{} - \frac{n-1}{1}\,[n-2]^\sigma[n-2]^\beta\\[2pt]
&{} + \frac{n-1\cdot n-2}{1\cdot 2}\,[n-3]^\sigma[n-3]^\beta\\[2pt]
&\hphantom{xxx}{} + \&\text{c.}
\end{aligned}
\right\},
\]
or, as this may be written, putting $q = n - \delta - 1$,
\[
R_r^\delta\{\delta+1,\ \delta+2+s,\ (r-2\delta+1-s)\}
\]
\[
\times [n-1]^\delta\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\phantom{-} [n-2]^\sigma[n-2]^\beta\\[2pt]
&{} - \frac{q}{1}\,[n-3]^\sigma[n-3]^\beta\\[2pt]
&{} + \&\text{c.}
\end{aligned}
\right\}.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 30. I assume $r \not> n - 2$, we have then $\alpha + \beta = r - \delta \not> n - \delta - 2$, and therefore $\alpha + \beta < q$. The general term of the series in $\{\ \}$ is
\[
(-)^\xi\,\frac{[q]^\xi}{[\xi]^\xi}\,[n - 2 - \xi]^\sigma[n - 2 - \xi]^\beta,
\]
where the terms for which $n - 2 - \xi$ is negative are to be excluded, or what is the same thing, the series is not to be continued beyond $\xi = n - 2$. But observing that $[q]^\xi$ vanishes for $\xi > q$, that is, $\xi > n - \delta - 1$, it is in fact the same thing whether the series is continued indefinitely or only to the term for which $\xi = n - \delta - 1$, and we may consistently with the condition $\xi \not> n - 2$, continue the series as far as $\xi = n - \delta - 1$, except in the case $\delta = 0$, when by doing so we include the term corresponding to $\xi = n - 1$, which in virtue of the condition ought to be excluded. The expression for the term in question is $(-)^{n-1}[-1]^\sigma[-1]^\beta$; hence if the sum of the series continued to the proper point is $S$, the sum continued indefinitely (in the particular case $\delta = 0$) is $S + (-)^{n-1}[-1]^\sigma[-1]^\beta$, but in every other case the sum continued indefinitely is simply $S$. And by a well-known theorem in finite differences, the sum continued indefinitely is in fact zero. That is, except in the case $\delta = 0$, we have $S = 0$, but in the excepted case
\[
S + (-)^{n-1}[-1]^\sigma[-1]^\beta = 0;
\]
or observing that $\alpha + \beta\,(= r - \delta)$ is in this case $= r$, and transforming the factorials, we have
\[
S = (-)^{n-r}[\alpha]^\alpha[\beta]^\beta,
\]
or substituting for $\alpha$ and $\beta$ their values,
\[
S = (-)^{n-r}[s]^s[r-s]^{r-s}.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 31. Hence the general term of
\[
D^r H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^r H_1 + \&\text{c.}
\]
vanishes except for $\delta = 0$, but when $\delta = 0$, its value is
\[
R_r^s\{1,\ 2+s,\ (r+1-s)\} \times (-)^{n-r}[s]^s[r-s]^{r-s};
\]
or observing that $R_r^s$ is equal to $[r]^r \div [s]^s[r-s]^{r-s}$, the value is simply
\[
(-)^{n-r}[r]^r\{1,\ 2+s,\ (r+1-s)\},
\]
that is, we have
\[
\Delta^2\!\left(D^r H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^r H_1 + \&\text{c.}\ldots\right)
   = (-)^{n-r}[r]^r\,\Sigma\{1,\ 2+s,\ (r+1-s)\},
\]
the summation in respect to $s$ extending from $s = 0$ to $s = r$. In particular, giving to $r$ the values $n - 2$ and $n - 1$, and attending to the expressions for III and IV, we find
\[
\Delta^2\!\left(D^{n-2}H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^{n-2}H_1 + \&\text{c.}\ldots\right) = -[n-2]^{n-2}\,\mathrm{III},
\]
\[
\Delta^2\!\left(D^{n-1}H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^{n-1}H_1 + \&\text{c.}\ldots\right) = -[n-3]^{n-1}\,\mathrm{IV}.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 32. The equation $\mathrm{III} = 0$ belongs to the curve which by its intersections with the tangent, gives the tangentials of a point of the curve $U = 0$. Hence the equation of the curve in question is
\[
D^{n-2}H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^{n-2}H_1 + \&\text{c.} = 0,
\]
which is Mr Salmon's theorem, leading to the solution of the problem of double tangents.

\medskip

\noindent 33. The expressions for I and II are obtained from those of IV and III by interchanging $(X, Y, Z)$ and $(x, y, z)$, and reversing the sign. Hence if, as before, $\mathfrak{H}$, $\mathfrak{D}$, \&c.\ denote the values which $H$, $D$, \&c.\ assume by this interchange, we have
\[
\Delta^2\!\left(\mathfrak{D}^{n-2}\mathfrak{H} - \frac{n-1}{1}\,\mathfrak{D}^{n-2}\mathfrak{H}_1 + \&\text{c.}\ldots\right) = [n-2]^{n-2}\,\mathrm{II},
\]
\[
\Delta^2\!\left(\mathfrak{D}^{n-1}\mathfrak{H} - \frac{n-1}{1}\,\mathfrak{D}^{n-1}\mathfrak{H}_1 + \&\text{c.}\ldots\right) = [n-3]^{n-1}\,\mathrm{I},
\]
and the identical equation
\[
a_0\,\mathrm{I} + a_1\,\mathrm{II} + a_{n-1}\,\mathrm{III} + a_n\,\mathrm{IV} = 0
\]

\medskip

\noindent becomes therefore
\[
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&\phantom{+\,}\left(\mathfrak{D}^{n-1}\mathfrak{H} - \frac{n-1}{1}\,\mathfrak{D}^{n-1}\mathfrak{H}_1 + \&\text{c.}\right)U\\[3pt]
&{}+\frac{1}{n(n-2)}\!\left(\mathfrak{D}^{n-2}\mathfrak{H} - \frac{n-1}{1}\,\mathfrak{D}^{n-2}\mathfrak{H}_1 + \&\text{c.}\right)DU\\[3pt]
&{}-\frac{1}{n(n-2)}\!\left(D^{n-2}H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^{n-2}H_1 + \&\text{c.}\right)\mathfrak{D}\mathbf{T}\\[3pt]
&{}-\left(D^{n-1}H - \frac{n-1}{1}\,D^{n-1}H_1 + \&\text{c.}\right)\mathbf{T}
\end{aligned}
\right\} = 0,
\]
which is the general identical equation referred to in the introduction to the present memoir.

\medskip

\noindent 34. It is to be noticed that for $n = 3$, the equation is
\[
(\mathfrak{D} - 2\mathfrak{H}_1)U + \tfrac{1}{3}\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{H} \cdot DU - \tfrac{1}{3}DH \cdot \mathfrak{D}\mathbf{T} - (H - 2H_1)\mathbf{T} = 0.
\]
But we have $H_1 = H$, and in like manner $\mathfrak{H}_1 = \mathfrak{H}$, and the equation thus becomes
\[
-\mathfrak{H}\,U + \tfrac{1}{3}\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{H} \cdot DU - \tfrac{1}{3}DH \cdot \mathfrak{D}\mathbf{T} + H\mathbf{T} = 0.
\]
And so also for $n = 4$, the equation is
\[
(\mathfrak{D}^2\mathfrak{H} - 3\mathfrak{D}^2\mathfrak{H}_1)U + \tfrac{1}{8}(\mathfrak{D}^2\mathfrak{H} - 3\mathfrak{D}^2\mathfrak{H}_1)DU
   - \tfrac{1}{8}(D^2H - 3D^2H_1)\mathfrak{D}\mathbf{T} - (DH - 3DH_1)\mathbf{T} = 0.
\]
But we have in general $DH_1 = \dfrac{n-3}{n-2}\,DH$, and therefore in the present case $DH_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}DH$, and consequently $\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{H}_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{H}$, and the equation thus becomes
\[
-\mathfrak{D}\mathfrak{H} \cdot U + \tfrac{1}{8}(\mathfrak{D}^2\mathfrak{H} - 3\mathfrak{D}^2\mathfrak{H}_1)DU - \tfrac{1}{8}(D^2H - 3D^2H_1)\mathfrak{D}\mathbf{T} + DH \cdot \mathbf{T} = 0,
\]
which agree with the results previously obtained for the two particular cases.

\noindent\textbf{261]}\hfill\textbf{207}

\bigskip

\begin{center}
{\large\bfseries 261.}

\medskip

{\scshape On the Conic of Five-pointic Contact at any Point of a Plane Curve.}
\end{center}

\medskip

\noindent[\textit{From the Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society of London}, vol.~\textsc{cxlix}.\ (for the year 1859), pp.~371--400. Received March 1,---Read March 24, 1859.]

\bigskip

\noindent The tangent is a line passing through two consecutive points of a plane curve, and we may in like manner consider the conic which passes through five consecutive points of a plane curve; and as there are certain singular points, viz.\ the points of inflexion where three consecutive points of the curve lie in a line, so there are singular points where six consecutive points of the curve lie in a conic. In the particular case where the given curve is a cubic, the last-mentioned species of singular points have been considered by Pl\"ucker and Steiner, and in the same particular case, the theory of the conic of five-pointic contact has recently been established by Mr Salmon. But the general case, where the curve is of any order whatever, has not so far as I am aware been hitherto considered;---the establishment of this theory is the object of the present memoir.

\bigskip

\begin{center}
\textsc{I.\ Investigation of the Equation of the Conic of Five-pointic Contact.}
\end{center}

\medskip

\noindent 1. I take $(X, Y, Z)$ as current coordinates, and I represent the equation of the given curve by
\[
\mathbf{T} = (*\,\Cdel X, Y, Z)^m = 0.
\]
Let $(x, y, z)$ be the coordinates of a given point on the curve, and let $U = (*\,\Cdel x, y, z)^m$ be what $\mathbf{T}$ becomes when $(x, y, z)$ are written in the place of $(X, Y, Z)$; we have therefore $U = 0$ as a condition satisfied by the coordinates of the point in question.

\medskip

\noindent 2. Write for shortness
\[
DU = (X\partial_x + Y\partial_y + Z\partial_z)U,
\]
\[
D^2U = (X\partial_x + Y\partial_y + Z\partial_z)^2 U,
\]
and let $\Pi = aX + bY + cZ = 0$ be the equation of a line. It is easy to see that
\[
D^2 U - \Pi \cdot DU = 0
\]

\newpage

\noindent\textbf{208}\hfill\textsc{on the conic of five-pointic}\hfill\textbf{[261}

\medskip

\noindent will be the equation of a conic having an ordinary (two-pointic) contact with the curve at the point $(x, y, z)$. In fact the equation $DU = 0$ is that of the tangent at the point in question, and the equation $D^2 U = 0$ is that of the penultimate polar (or polar conic) of the point, which conic is touched by the tangent; the assumed equation represents therefore a conic having an ordinary (two-pointic) contact with the polar conic, and therefore with the curve. It may be added that the two conics intersect besides in a pair of points, and that the line joining these, or common chord of the two conics, is the line represented by the equation $\Pi = 0$; and this being so, the constants $(a, b, c)$ of the line $\Pi = 0$ can be so determined as to give rise to a five-pointic contact.

\medskip

\noindent 3. Consider the coordinates of a point of the curve as functions of a single variable parameter; then for the present purpose the coordinates of a point consecutive to $(x, y, z)$ may be taken to be
\[
\begin{aligned}
&x + dx + \tfrac{1}{2}d^2x + \tfrac{1}{6}d^3x + \tfrac{1}{24}d^4x,\\
&y + dy + \tfrac{1}{2}d^2y + \tfrac{1}{6}d^3y + \tfrac{1}{24}d^4y,\\
&z + dz + \tfrac{1}{2}d^2z + \tfrac{1}{6}d^3z + \tfrac{1}{24}d^4z,
\end{aligned}
\]
values which, substituted for $X$, $Y$, $Z$, must satisfy the equations
\[
\mathbf{T} = 0,\qquad D^2U - \Pi \cdot DU = 0.
\]

\medskip

\noindent 4. I write for shortness
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_1 &= dx\,\partial_x + dy\,\partial_y + dz\,\partial_z,\\
\partial_2 &= d^2x\,\partial_x + d^2y\,\partial_y + d^2z\,\partial_z,\\
\partial_3 &= d^3x\,\partial_x + d^3y\,\partial_y + d^3z\,\partial_z,\\
\partial_4 &= d^4x\,\partial_x + d^4y\,\partial_y + d^4z\,\partial_z,
\end{aligned}
\]
then the consecutive value of $\mathbf{T}$ is
\[
\exp\bigl(\partial_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_2 + \tfrac{1}{6}\partial_3 + \tfrac{1}{24}\partial_4\bigr)\,U
\]
(Read exp.\ $z$, exponential of $z$, $= e^z$), which is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\times (1 + \partial_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{6}\partial_1^3 + \tfrac{1}{24}\partial_1^4)\\
&\times (1\phantom{+ \partial_1} + \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_2 + \tfrac{1}{4}\partial_1\partial_2 + \tfrac{1}{8}\partial_2^2)\\
&\times (1\phantom{+ \partial_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_2} + \tfrac{1}{6}\partial_3 + \tfrac{1}{6}\partial_1\partial_3)\\
&\times (1\phantom{+ \partial_1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\partial_2 + \tfrac{1}{6}\partial_3} + \tfrac{1}{24}\partial_4),
\end{aligned}
\]

&{} + \tfrac{1}{6}(\partial_1^3 + 3\partial_1\partial_2 + \partial_3)U\\
&{} + \tfrac{1}{24}(\partial_1^4 + 6\partial_1^2\partial_2 + 4\partial_1\partial_3 + 3\partial_2^2 + \partial_4)U,
\end{aligned}
\]

\noindent\textit{Proof of the expressions for} $(\partial_1^3 + 3\partial_1\partial_2)\,U$ \textit{and} $(\partial_1^4 + 6\partial_1^2\partial_2)\,U$:

\medskip

\noindent 17. It will be remembered that $\partial_1$, $\partial_2$ stand originally for
\[
\begin{aligned}
&dx\,\partial_x + dy\,\partial_y + dz\,\partial_z,\\
&d^2x\,\partial_x + d^2y\,\partial_y + d^2z\,\partial_z,
\end{aligned}
\]
and that $A$, $B$, $C$ being the first derived functions of $U$, $dx$, $dy$, $dz$ are changed into
\[
B\nu - C\mu,\quad C\lambda - A\nu,\quad A\mu - B\lambda,
\]
and that the resulting value of $\partial_1$, viz.
\[
(B\nu - C\mu)\partial_x + (C\lambda - A\nu)\partial_y + (A\mu - B\lambda)\partial_z,
\]
is also represented by $\mathbf{D}$, so that $\partial_1 = \mathbf{D}$. The corresponding values of $d^2x$, $d^2y$, $d^2z$ are
\[
\begin{aligned}
d^2x &= \nu\,dB - \mu\,dC,\\
d^2y &= \lambda\,dC - \nu\,dA,\\
d^2z &= \mu\,dA - \lambda\,dB,
\end{aligned}
\]
where we have
\[
dA = a\,dx + h\,dy + g\,dz,\quad \&\text{c.},
\]
in which $dx$, $dy$, $dz$ are to be replaced by the values
\[
B\nu - C\mu,\quad C\lambda - A\nu,\quad A\mu - B\lambda;
\]
and $\partial_2$ really denotes what
\[
d^2x\,\partial_x + d^2y\,\partial_y + d^2z\,\partial_z
\]
becomes when the above values are substituted for $d^2x$, $d^2y$, $d^2z$. But in the expressions $\partial_1^2 U$, $\partial_1\partial_2 U$ \&c., the symbols $\partial_x$, $\partial_y$, $\partial_z$ contained in $\partial_1$ and $\partial_2$ operate only on $U$, and not on the variable quantities $A$, $B$, $C$, \&c.\ contained in $\partial_1$ and $\partial_2$.

\medskip

\noindent 18. If now we treat $\partial_1$ as an operand, that is, perform the differentiations on the variable quantities $A$, $B$, $C$ which enter into $\partial_1$, we obtain
\[
\partial_1 \cdot \partial_1 = \partial_1^2 + \partial_2,
\]
or, what is the same thing, operating on $\partial_1 U$ with $\partial_1$, the result is
\[
\partial_1^2 U + \partial_2 U,
\]
and in like manner
\[
\partial_1 \cdot \partial_1^2 U = \partial_1^3 U + 2\partial_1\partial_2 U.
\]
It is, in fact, upon these principles that Hesse's values of $\partial_1^2 U$, $\partial_1^3 U$, \&c.\ were obtained, and we may by means of them obtain the other expressions assumed in the preceding section.

\noindent 19. In fact, starting from Hesse's equation,

\[
(\partial_1^3 + 2\partial_1\partial_2)\,U = \frac{m}{m-1}\,U\mathbf{D}\Phi - \frac{1}{(m-1)^2}\,\mathbf{D}H \cdot \varpi^2;
\]
this is precisely Hesse's value of $\partial_1^3 U$, and thus we have $\partial_1\partial_2 U = 0$, and therefore
\[
\partial_1^3 U = \frac{m}{m-1}\,U\mathbf{D}\Phi - \frac{1}{(m-1)^2}\,\mathbf{D}H \cdot \varpi^3.

&{}- 2lyz(z^3 - x^3)(x^3 - y^3);
\end{aligned}
\]
and expanding and reducing, the result is
\[
x^2\bigl(-(y^3 + z^3)x^3 - x^3(y^3 + z^3)\bigr) + 2lyz\bigl(-2x^3(y^3 + z^3) + x^6\bigr) + 12l^2 x^2y^2z^2 \cdot x^2;
\]
whence, dividing by $x^2$, the equation becomes
\[
-(y^3 + z^3)(x^3 + y^3 + z^3) + 2lxyz\bigl(x^3 - 2(y^3 + z^3)\bigr) + 12l^2 x^2y^2z^2
   = (-y^3 - z^3 + 2lyz)(x^3 + y^3 + z^3 + 6lxyz),
\]
which is identically true.

\medskip

\noindent 36. Hence in the identical equation putting $U = 0$, we see that the equation of the five-pointic conic may be written
\[
\begin{aligned}
&9(1 + 8l^3)\,x^2y^2z^2\,(x, y, z, lx, ly, lz\,\Cdel X, Y, Z)^2\\
&{}+ \bigl[(x^2 + 2lyz)X + (y^2 + 2lzx)Y + (z^2 + 2lxy)Z\bigr]\\
&\quad\times \bigl[(\xi^2 + 2l\eta\zeta)X + (\eta^2 + 2l\zeta\xi)Y + (\zeta^2 + 2l\xi\eta)Z\bigr] = 0,
\end{aligned}
\]
where $\xi$, $\eta$, $\zeta$ stand for $x(y^3 - z^3)$, $y(z^3 - x^3)$, $z(x^3 - y^3)$, the coordinates of the tangential of the given point, and which puts in evidence the geometrical theorem above referred to, viz.

\medskip

\noindent\textsc{Theorem.}---\textit{The common chord of the five-pointic conic and the polar conic is the tangent to the cubic at the tangential of the given point.}

\medskip

\noindent 39. Hence putting for the moment
\[
M = (y^3 - z^3)(z^3 - x^3)(x^3 - y^3),
\]
it appears that the last of the three equations is $Mz = 0$; the first and second are of course $Mx = 0$ and $My = 0$, and the required condition is $M = 0$, that is,
\[
(y^3 - z^3)(z^3 - x^3)(x^3 - y^3) = 0,
\]
the equation which, combined with the equation of the curve
\[
x^3 + y^3 + z^3 + 6lxyz = 0,
\]
gives the sextactic points. There are consequently twenty-seven such points, and it is at once seen that these are the points of contact of the tangents to the cubic from the points of inflexion, or, what is the same thing, that the twenty-seven sextactic points form nine groups of three each, such that the three points of a group have for their common tangential one of the nine points of inflexion. In fact, let $\omega$ be a cube root (real or imaginary) of unity, the three sextactic points of one of the groups will be given by
\[
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&x - \omega y = 0,\\
&x^3 + y^3 + z^3 + 6lxyz = 0.
\end{aligned}
\right.
\]
Now consider the tangential of any one of these points, its coordinates are
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_1 &= x(y^3 - z^3),\\
y_1 &= y(z^3 - x^3),\\
z_1 &= z(x^3 - y^3);
\end{aligned}
\]
or, reducing by the equation $x - \omega y = 0$, $x_1 = \omega y(x^3 - z^3)$, $y_1 = -y(x^3 - z^3)$, $z_1 = 0$, or, what is the same thing, $x_1 + \omega y_1 = 0$, $z_1 = 0$; that is, the point $(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ is one of the points of inflexion. This is the construction of the sextactic points obtained by Pl\"ucker and Steiner.

\medskip

\noindent\textsc{Theorem.} \textit{The point of simple intersection of the cubic and the five-pointic conic is the third point of intersection with the cubic, of the line joining the point of contact with the second tangential of this point.}
