Now starting from the differential equation
\[
\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(a,\,b,\,c,\,d,\,e\,\check{a}\,x,\,1)^4}} \pm \frac{dy}{\sqrt{(a,\,b,\,c,\,d,\,e\,\check{a}\,y,\,1)^4}} = 0,
\]
the integral equation is known to be
\[
\frac{\sqrt{(a,\,b,\,c,\,d,\,e\,\check{a}\,x,\,1)^4} - \sqrt{(a,\,b,\,c,\,d,\,e\,\check{a}\,y,\,1)^4}}{x-y} = \tfrac{1}{2}(a,\,b,\,c,\,d,\,e\,\check{a}\,x,\,y,\,1)^2,
\]
where $\theta$ is the constant of integration. Writing, for shortness, $X=(a,\,b,\,c,\,d,\,e\,\check{a}\,x,\,1)^4$,
$Y=(a,\,b,\,c,\,d,\,e\,\check{a}\,y,\,1)^4$, this is
\[
X+Y-2\sqrt{(XY)}=a(x^2-y^2)^2+4b(x-y)(x^2-y^2)+6\theta(x-y)^2,
\]
or, what is the same thing,
\begin{align*}
a(x+y)^4 - 2\sqrt{(XY)} &= a(x^2-y^2)^2 - 4a(x-y)(x^3-y^3) + 6\theta(x-y)^2,\\
&\quad + 4b(x^3+y^3)\\
&\quad + 6c(x^2+y^2)\\
&\quad + 4d(x+y)\\
&\quad + 2e,
\end{align*}
viz.\ this gives
\begin{align*}
\sqrt{(XY)} &= ax^2y^2 \\
&\quad + 2b(x^2y+xy^2)\\
&\quad + 3c(x^2+y^2)\\
&\quad + 3d^2xy - 3\theta\\
&\quad + 2d(x+y)\\
&\quad + e,
\end{align*}
and, rationalising, the integral equation becomes
\begin{align*}
&= 6a\theta x^2y^2\\

&\quad - 12cd(x+y)\\
&\quad + 9d^2(x+y)^2 - 12d\theta(x+y) - 6ed + 4d^2 = 0.
\end{align*}
