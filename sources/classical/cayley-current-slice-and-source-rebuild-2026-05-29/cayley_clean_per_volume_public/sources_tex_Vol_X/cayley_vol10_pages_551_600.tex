\subsection*{The $xy$-expressions of the theta-functions.}

\noindent
125. The various quadric relations between the theta-functions, admitting that they constitute a 13-fold relation, show that the theta-functions may be expressed as proportional to functions of two arbitrary parameters $x$, $y$; and two of these functions being assumed at pleasure the others of them would be determinate; we have of course (though it would not be easy to arrive at it in this manner) such a system in the foregoing expressions of the 16 functions in terms of $x$, $y$; and conversely these expressions must satisfy identically the quadric relations between the theta-functions.

126. To show that this is so as to the general form of the equations, consider first the $xy$-factors $\surd k$, $\surd ab$, \&c. As regards the squared functions $\surd aby$, we have for instance
\[
(\surd ab)^2 = \tfrac12 \{ab_1 c_1 d_1 e_1 + a_1 b_1 cde + 2\surd\mathfrak{XY}\},
\]
\[
(\surd cdf)^2 = \tfrac12 \{cd_1 f_1 a_1 b_1 e_1 + c_1 d_1 f_1 abe + 2\surd\mathfrak{XY}\},
\]
each of these contains the same irrational part $\surd\mathfrak{XY}$, and the difference is therefore rational: and it is moreover integral, for we have
\[
\surd aby - \surdy cd = \tfrac12 (abc_1 d_1 - a_1 b_1 cd)(f_1 e - f e_1),
\]
where each factor divides by $\theta$, and consequently the product by $\theta^2$; the value is in fact
\[
= (e - f)
\begin{vmatrix}
1, & x + y, & xy \\
1, & a + b, & ab \\
1, & c + d, & cd
\end{vmatrix}.
\]

\noindent
a linear function of $1$, $x + y$, $xy$. This is the case as regards the difference of any two of the squares $(\surd ab)^2$, $(\surd ac)^2$, \&c.; hence selecting any one of these squares, for instance $(\surd de)^2$, any other of the squares is of the form $\lambda + \mu(x + y) + \nu xy + \rho(\surd de)^2$ ($\rho = 1$); and obviously, the other squares $(\surd a)^2$, \&c., are of the like form, the last coefficient $\rho$ being $=0$. We hence have the theorem that each square can be expressed as a linear function of any four (properly selected) squares.

127. But we have also the theorem of the 16 Kummer hexads. Obviously the six squares $(\surd a)^2$, $(\surd b)^2$, $(\surd c)^2$, $(\surd d)^2$, $(\surd e)^2$, $(\surd f)^2$ are a hexad, viz. each of these is a linear function of $1$, $x + y$, $xy$: and therefore selecting any three of them, each of the remaining three can be expressed as a linear function of these. But farther the squares $(\surd a)^2$, $(\surd bf)^2$, $(\surd abf)^2$, $(\surd cdf)^2$, $(\surd ce)^2$, $(\surd def)^2$ form a hexad. For reverting to the expression obtained for $\surd aby - \surdy cd$, the determinant contained therein is a linear function of $aa_1$ and $bb_1$, that is, of $(\surd a)^2$ and $(\surd b)^2$; we, in fact, have
\[
(a - b)
\begin{vmatrix}
1, & x + y, & xy \\
1, & a + b, & ab \\
1, & c + d, & cd
\end{vmatrix}
= (b - c)(b - d)(a - x)(a - y) - (a - c)(a - d)(b - x)(b - y).
\]
Hence $\surd aby - \surdy cd$ is a linear function of $(\surd a)^2$, $(\surd b)^2$; and by a mere interchange of letters $\surdy bc - \surdy ce$, $\surd aby - \surdy de$, are each of them also a linear function of $(\surd a)^2$ and $(\surd b)^2$; whence the theorem. And we have thus all the remaining 15 hexads.

128. We have a like theory as regards the products of pairs of functions. A tetrad of pairs is of one of the two forms $\surd a\surd b$, $\surd ac\surd bc$, $\surd ad\surd bd$, $\surd ae\surd be$, and $\surd f\surd ab$, $\surd c\surd de$, $\surd d\surd ce$, $\surd e\surd cd$; in the first case the terms are
\[
\surd aa_1\surd bb_1,\quad
\tfrac12 \{(ab_1 + a_1 b)\surd cdefc_1 d_1 e_1 f_1 + (cd_1 e_1 f_1 + c_1 d_1 ef)\surd\mathfrak{XY}\},
\]
\[
\tfrac12 \{(df_1 c_1 e_1 + d_1 f_1 ce)\surd\mathfrak{XY}\},\quad
\tfrac12 \{(ef_1 c_1 d_1 + e_1 f_1 cd)\surd\mathfrak{XY}\},
\]
and as regards the last three terms the difference of any two of them is a mere constant multiple of $\surd aa_1\surd bb_1$; for instance, the second term -- the third term is $\tfrac12 (cd_1 - c_1 d)(fe_1 - f_1 e) \surd aa_1\surd bb_1 = (c - d)(f - e) \surd aa_1\surd bb_1$.

we have thus a tetrad such that, selecting any two terms, each of the remaining terms is a linear function of these. In the second case, the terms are
\[
\tfrac12 \{f_1 \surd abc d_1 e_1 f_1 + f \surd a_1 bcde f\},

129. Considering next the actual equations between the squared theta-functions, take as a specimen that is,
\[
c_2 (\surd ab)^2 - c_2 (\surd cd)^2 + c_5 (\surd ce)^2 - c_6 (\surd de)^2 = 0,
\]
where $c_2$, $c_2$, $c_5$, $c_6 = \surd ab$, $\surd cd$, $\surd ce$, $\surd de$ respectively. Since the functions $\surd aby$, \&c., contain the same irrational term $\surd\mathfrak{XY}$, it is clear that the equation can only be true if $c_2 - c_2 + c_5 - c_6 = 0$ and, this being so, it will be true if
\[
c_2^2 \{(\surd ab)^2 - (\surd cd)^2\} - c_5^2 \{(\surd ab)^2 - (\surd ce)^2\} + c_6^2 \{(\surd ab)^2 - (\surd de)^2\} = 0,
\]
where, by what precedes, each of the terms in $\{\}$ is a linear function of $(\surd a)^2$ and $(\surd b)^2$. Attending first to the term in $(\surd ab)^2$, the coefficient hereof is
\[
ef \cdot bc \cdot bd \cdot c_2^2 - df \cdot bc \cdot be \cdot c_5^2 + cf \cdot bd \cdot be \cdot c_6^2,
\]
where for shortness $bc$, $bd$, \&c., are written to denote the differences $b - c$, $b - d$, \&c. substituting for $c_2^2$ its value $(\surd cd)^2 = cd \cdot cf \cdot df \cdot ab \cdot ae \cdot be$, and similarly for $c_5^2$ and $c_6^2$ their values, $= ce \cdot cf \cdot ef \cdot ab \cdot ad \cdot bd$, and $de \cdot df \cdot ef \cdot ab \cdot ac \cdot bc$ respectively, the whole expression contains the factor $ab \cdot bc \cdot bd \cdot be \cdot cf \cdot df \cdot ef$, and throwing this out, the equation to be verified becomes
\[
cd \cdot ae - ce \cdot ad + de \cdot ac = 0,
\]
\[
c_2^2 - c_5^2 + c_6^2 - c_2^2 = 0;
\]
which is true identically. The verification is thus made to depend upon that of $c_2^2 - c_5^2 + c_6^2 - c_2^2 = 0$; and similarly for the other relations between the squared functions, the verification depends upon relations containing the fourth powers, or the products of squares, of the constants $c$ and $k$.

130. Among these are included the before-mentioned system of equations involving the fourth powers or the products of squares of only the constants $c$; and it is interesting to show how these are satisfied identically by the values $c_0 = \surd bd$, \&c. Thus one of these equations is
\[
c_{14}^2 + c_{15}^2 + c_{12}^2 = c_{01}^2;
\]
substituting the values, there is a factor $ce$ which divides out, and the resulting equation is
\[
ad \cdot cf \cdot df \cdot bc \cdot be + cf \cdot ef \cdot ab \cdot ad \cdot bd + ab \cdot af \cdot bf \cdot cd \cdot de - ac \cdot ae \cdot bd \cdot bf \cdot df = 0.
\]
There are here terms in $a^2$, $a$, $a^0$ which should separately vanish; for the terms in $a^2$, the equation becomes
\[
df \cdot bc \cdot be + bd \cdot cf \cdot ef + bf \cdot cd \cdot de - bd \cdot bf \cdot df = 0,
\]
which is easily verified; and the equations in $a$ and $a^0$ may also be verified. An equation involving products of the squares is $c_{14}c_{15} - c_{12}c_{13} + c_{14}c_{15} = 0$. The term $c_{14}^2 c_{15}^2$ is here $\sudf \cdot bce \cdot \sdef \cdot abc$ which is $= \surd\{(bc)^2 (df)^2 \cdot ab \cdot ac \cdot ad \cdot af \cdot bc \cdot ce \cdot de \cdot ef\}$, which is taken $= bc \cdot df \cdot \surd(ab \cdot ac \cdot ad \cdot af \cdot bc \cdot ce \cdot de \cdot ef)$; similarly the values of $c_{12}^2 c_{13}^2$ and $c_{12}^2 c_{14}^2$ are $= bd \cdot cf$ and $bf \cdot cd$ each multiplied by the same radical, and the equation to be verified is
\[
bc \cdot df - bd \cdot cf + bf \cdot cd = 0,
\]
which is right: the other equations may be verified in a similar manner.

131. Coming next to the equations connecting the pairs of theta-functions, for instance this is
\[
c_5 c_6 c_9 c_{10} \surd bd \surd ad - \surd be \surd ae + c_4 c_8 c_{12} c_{16} \surd bf \surd af = 0,
\]
the products $\surd bd \surd ad$ and $\surd be \surd ae$ contain besides a common term the terms $\tfrac12 (df_1 e_1 + d_1 f_1 ce) \surd aa_1 \surd bb_1$, and $\tfrac12 (ef_1 c_1 d_1 + e_1 f_1 cd) \surd aa_1 \surd bb_1$; hence their difference contains $\tfrac12 (de_1 - d_1 e)(fc_1 - f_1 c) \surd aa_1 \surd bb_1$, which is $= de \cdot fc \surd aa_1 \surd bb_1$, that is, $de \cdot fc \surd a \surd b$: hence the equation to be verified is
\[
ae \cdot bc \cdot c_5 c_6 c_9 c_{10} + c_4 c_8 c_{12} c_{16} = 0.
\]
\[
c_5 c_6 c_9 c_{10} = \surd bef \cdot acd \surd arf \cdot bcd \surd bdf \cdot ace \surd adf \cdot bce,
\]
where under the fourth root we have 24 factors, which are, in fact, 12 factors twice repeated; and if we write $\Pi = ab \cdot ac \cdot ad \cdot ae \cdot af \cdot bc \cdot bd \cdot be \cdot bf \cdot cd \cdot ce \cdot cf \cdot de \cdot df \cdot ef$ for the product of all the 15 factors, then the 12 factors are in fact all those of $\Pi$, except $ab$, $cf$, $de$; viz. we have
\[
c_5 c_6 c_9 c_{10} = \surd\Pi^2 (ab)^2 (cf)^2 (de)^2.
\]

Again, $c_4 c_8 c_{12} c_{16} = \surd acf \cdot bde \surd bcf \cdot ade \surd acdef \surd bcdef$, is a fourth root of a product of 32 factors, which are in fact 16 factors twice repeated, and in the 16 factors, $ab$ does not occur, $cf$ and $de$ occur each twice, and the other 12 factors each once: we thus have
\[
c_4 c_8 c_{12} c_{16} = \surd\Pi^2 (cf)^2 (de)^2 (ab)^2,
\]
and the relation to be verified assumes the form
\[
fc \cdot de \surd\Pi^2 (cf)^2 (de)^2 + \surd\Pi^2 (cf)^2 (de)^2 = 0,
\]
which, taking $fc \cdot de = -\surd\{(cf)^2 (de)^2\}$, is right. And so for the other equations. It will be observed that, in the equation $de \cdot fc \cdot c_5 c_6 c_9 c_{10} + c_4 c_8 c_{12} c_{16} = 0$, and in the other equations upon which the verifications depend, there is no ambiguity of sign: the signs of the radicals have to be determined consistently with all the equations which connect the $c$'s and the $k$'s: that this is possible appears evident \textit{a priori}, but the actual verification presents some difficulty. I do not here enter further into the question.

\subsection*{The hyperelliptic case.}

\noindent
143. In the hyperelliptic case, the constants $c$ are connected by the relations $c_0^2 + c_4^2 + c_8^2 + c_{12}^2 = c_1^2 + c_5^2 + c_9^2 + c_{13}^2$, $c_2^2 + c_6^2 + c_{10}^2 + c_{14}^2 = c_3^2 + c_7^2 + c_{11}^2 + c_{15}^2$, and $c_0 c_2 + c_4 c_6 + c_8 c_{10} + c_{12} c_{14} = c_1 c_3 + c_5 c_7 + c_9 c_{11} + c_{13} c_{15}$.
