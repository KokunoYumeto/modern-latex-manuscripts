Adopting the language which I have frequently employed elsewhere, I call $\Syz_t$ a syzygetic function, or more briefly a conjunctive of $f$ and $\phi$, and $\Lambda_t$ and $L_t$ may be termed the syzygetic factors to $\Syz_t$ so considered. If we divide each term of the equation (15) by the allotrious factor $(M)$, we have
\[
\frac{\Lambda_t}{M}f-\frac{L_t}{M}\phi=R_t,
\]
where $R_t$ is the $t$th simplified residue to $(f,\phi)$; and if we call $\frac{\Lambda_t}{M}=\tau_t$, and $\frac{L_t}{M}=t_t$, so as to obtain the equation
\[
\tau_tf-t_t\phi=R_t,\tag{16}
\]
we see that $\frac{\tau_t}{t_t}$, the fraction formed by the component factors to any simplified residue of $(f,\phi)$, will be identical in value (although no longer in its separate terms) with one of the corresponding convergents to $\frac{\phi}{f}$, exhibited under the form of an improper continued fraction. I shall in the next section show how, not only the successive simplified residues, but also the component syzygetic factors of each of them, and consequently the successive convergents, may be expressed in terms of the roots of the two given functions.

Since the preceding section was composed the valuable memoir of the lamented Jacobi, entitled ``De Eliminatione Variabilis è duabus Equationibus Algebraicis,'' Crelle, Vol.~xvi., has fallen under my notice. That memoir is restricted to the consideration of two equations of the same degree, and the principal results in this section as regards the Bezoutic square and the allotrious factors applicable to that case will be found contained therein. The mode of treatment however is sufficiently dissimilar to justify this section being preserved unaltered under its original form.

\begin{center}
{\scshape Section II.}\par\smallskip
{\itshape On the general solution in terms of the roots, of any two given algebraical functions of $x$, of the syzygetic equation, which connects them with a third function, whose degree in $x$ is given, but whose form is to be determined.}
\end{center}

\art{14} Let $f$ and $\phi$ be two given functions in $x$ of the degrees $m$ and $n$ respectively in $x$, and for the sake of greater simplicity let the coefficients of the highest power of $x$ in $f$ and $\phi$ be each taken unity, and let it be proposed to solve the syzygetic equation
\[
\tau_t f-t_t\phi+\Syz_t=0,\tag{17}
\]

\sourcepage{459}
where $\Syz_t$ is given only in the number of its dimensions in $x$, which I suppose to be $t$; but the forms of $\tau_t,t_t,\Syz_t$ are all to be determined in terms of $h_1,h_2\ldots h_m$, the roots of $f$, and $\eta_1,\eta_2\ldots\eta_n$ the roots of $\phi$.

I shall begin with finding $\Syz_t$; and before giving a more general representation of $\Syz_t$, I propose now to demonstrate that we may make
\[
\Syz_t=\Sigma\{P_{q_1,q_2\ldots q_t}\times(x-h_{q_1})(x-h_{q_2})\ldots(x-h_{q_t})\},\tag{18}
\]
where $P_{q_1,q_2\ldots q_t}$ is used to denote
\[
\left\{\begin{array}{c}
(h_{q_{t+1}}-\eta_1)(h_{q_{t+1}}-\eta_2)\ldots(h_{q_{t+1}}-\eta_n)\\
\times(h_{q_{t+2}}-\eta_1)(h_{q_{t+2}}-\eta_2)\ldots(h_{q_{t+2}}-\eta_n)\\
\times(h_{q_{t+3}}-\eta_1)(h_{q_{t+3}}-\eta_2)\ldots(h_{q_{t+3}}-\eta_n)\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
\times(h_{q_m}-\eta_1)(h_{q_m}-\eta_2)\ldots(h_{q_m}-\eta_n)
\end{array}\right\}R(h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_t}),
\]
$R(h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_t})$ denoting any rational symmetrical function whatever of the quantities preceded by the symbol $R$, and $q_1,q_2\ldots q_t,q_{t+1}\ldots q_m$ being any permutation of the $m$ indices $1,2\ldots m$.

Suppose $f=0$ and $\phi=0$, then $x$ is equal to one of the series of roots
\[
h_1,h_2\ldots h_m,
\]
and also to one of the series of roots
\[
\eta_1,\eta_2\ldots\eta_n.
\]
Suppose then that
\[
x=h_a=\eta_\omega,
\]
and consider any term of $\Syz_t$.

If in any such term $a$ is found in the series $q_1,q_2\ldots q_t$, then
\[
(x-h_{q_1})(x-h_{q_2})\ldots(x-h_{q_t})=0.
\]
But if not, then $x$ must be found in the complementary series
\[
h_{q_{t+1}},h_{q_{t+2}}\ldots h_{q_m},
\]
and consequently $P_{q_1,q_2\ldots q_t}$ will contain a factor $h_a-\eta_\omega$ and $P_{q_1,q_2\ldots q_t}=0$; in every case therefore
\[
P_{q_1,q_2\ldots q_t}\times(x-h_{q_1})(x-h_{q_2})\ldots(x-h_{q_t})=0.
\]
Therefore $\Syz_t$ as expressed in equation (18) is a syzygetic function of $f$ and $\phi$; and we have found a function of the $t$th degree in $x$, and of course expressible by calculating the symmetric functions as a function only of $x$ and of the coefficients of $f$ and $\phi$, which will satisfy the equation
\[
\tau_t f-t_t\phi+\Syz_t=0.
\]

\sourcepage{460}
It will be remembered that by virtue of Art.~2 we know \emph{à priori} that all the values of $\Syz_t$ satisfying this equation are identical, save as to an allotrious factor, which is a function only of the coefficients in $f$ and $\phi$.

It is clear that we may interchange the $h$ and $\eta$, $m$ and $n$, and thus another representation of a value of $\Syz_t$ satisfying the equation (17) will be
\[
\Syz_t=\Sigma R(\eta_{q_1},\eta_{q_2}\ldots\eta_{q_t})
\left\{\begin{array}{c}
(\eta_{q_{t+1}}-h_1)(\eta_{q_{t+1}}-h_2)\ldots(\eta_{q_{t+1}}-h_m)\\
(\eta_{q_{t+2}}-h_1)(\eta_{q_{t+2}}-h_2)\ldots(\eta_{q_{t+2}}-h_m)\\
\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\\
(\eta_{q_n}-h_1)(\eta_{q_n}-h_2)\ldots(\eta_{q_n}-h_m)
\end{array}\right\}
(x-\eta_{q_1})(x-\eta_{q_2})\ldots(x-\eta_{q_t}).
\]

\art{15} If we employ in general the condensed notation
\[
\left[\begin{array}{cccc}l&m&n&\ldots p\\ \lambda&\mu&\nu&\ldots \rho\end{array}\right],
\]
to denote the product of the differences resulting from the subtraction of each of the quantities $\lambda,\mu\ldots\nu$ in the lower line from all of those in the upper line $l,m,n\ldots p$, the two values above given for $\Syz_t$ may be written under the respective forms
\[
\Sigma R(h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_t})
\sbrk{h_{q_{t+1}},h_{q_{t+2}}\ldots h_{q_m}}{\eta_1,\eta_2\ldots\eta_n}
(x-h_{q_1})(x-h_{q_2})\ldots(x-h_{q_t}),
\]
and
\[
\Sigma R(\eta_{\xi_1},\eta_{\xi_2}\ldots\eta_{\xi_t})
\sbrk{\eta_{\xi_{t+1}},\eta_{\xi_{t+2}}\ldots\eta_{\xi_n}}{h_1,h_2\ldots h_m}
(x-\eta_{\xi_1})(x-\eta_{\xi_2})\ldots(x-\eta_{\xi_t}),
\]
in each of which equations disjunctively and in some order of relation each with each
\[
q_1,q_2,q_3\ldots q_m=1,2,3\ldots m,
\]
and
\[
\xi_1,\xi_2,\xi_3\ldots\xi_n=1,2,3\ldots n.
\]
These two forms are only the two extremities of a scale of forms all equally well adapted to express $\Syz_t$; for let $v$ and $v'$ be any two integers so taken as to satisfy the equation
\[
v+v'=t,
\]
and let $R(\overline{\cdots};\overline{\cdots})$, where the dots denote any quantities whatever, be used to denote a rational function which remains unaltered in value when any two of the quantities under either of the two bars are mutually interchanged, then we may write
\begingroup\scriptsize
\[
\Syz_t=\Sigma\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
R(\overline{h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_v}};\overline{\eta_{\xi_1},\eta_{\xi_2}\ldots\eta_{\xi_{v'}}})\times
\sbrk{h_{q_{v+1}},h_{q_{v+2}}\ldots h_{q_m}}{\eta_{\xi_{v'+1}},\eta_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots\eta_{\xi_n}}\\[0.4em]
\times(x-h_{q_1})(x-h_{q_2})\ldots(x-h_{q_v})
(x-\eta_{\xi_1})(x-\eta_{\xi_2})\ldots(x-\eta_{\xi_{v'}})
\end{array}\right\}.\tag{19}
\]
\endgroup

\sourcepage{461}
For if, as above, we suppose $x=h_a=\eta_\omega$, any term of $\Syz_t$ in which $q_1,q_2\ldots q_v$ comprise among them $a$, or in which $\xi_1,\xi_2\ldots\xi_{v'}$ comprise among them $\omega$, will vanish by virtue of the factors
\[
(x-h_{q_1})(x-h_{q_2})\ldots(x-h_{q_v})\times
(x-\eta_{\xi_1})(x-\eta_{\xi_2})\ldots(x-\eta_{\xi_{v'}});
\]
but if neither $a$ nor $\omega$ is so comprised, then $a$ must be one of the terms in the complementary series $q_{v+1},q_{v+2}\ldots q_m$, and $\omega$ one of the terms in the complementary series $\xi_{v'+1},\xi_{v'+2}\ldots\xi_n$, and therefore one of the quantities $h_{q_{v+1}},h_{q_{v+2}}\ldots h_{q_m}$ will equal one of the quantities $\eta_{\xi_{v'+1}},\eta_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots\eta_{\xi_n}$, and consequently the term of $\Syz_t$ in question will vanish by virtue of the factor
\[
\sbrk{h_{q_{v+1}},h_{q_{v+2}}\ldots h_{q_m}}{\eta_{\xi_{v'+1}},\eta_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots\eta_{\xi_n}}
\]
vanishing. In either case therefore every term included within the sign of summation vanishes when $x=h_a=\eta_\omega$, that is, whenever $fx=0$ and $\phi x=0$. Hence $\Syz_t$, as given by equation (19), will satisfy the syzygetic equation $\tau_tf-t_t\phi+\Syz_t=0$ for all values of $v$ and $v'$ which make $v+v'=t$, and for all symmetrical forms of the function denoted by the symbol $R(\overline{\cdots};\overline{\cdots})$.

\art{16} I shall now proceed to show how to assign the arbitrary function whose form is denoted by this symbol in such a manner as to make $\Syz_t$ become identical with a simplified residue to $f$ and $\phi$. To this end I take for $R(h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_v};\eta_{\xi_1},\eta_{\xi_2}\ldots\eta_{\xi_{v'}})$ the value
\[
R=\frac{
\sbrk{h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_v}}{\eta_{\xi_1},\eta_{\xi_2}\ldots\eta_{\xi_{v'}}}
}{
\sbrk{h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_v}}{h_{q_{v+1}},h_{q_{v+2}}\ldots h_{q_m}}\times
\sbrk{\eta_{\xi_1},\eta_{\xi_2}\ldots\eta_{\xi_{v'}}}{\eta_{\xi_{v'+1}},\eta_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots\eta_{\xi_n}}
};\tag{20}
\]
we shall then have
\begingroup\scriptsize
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Syz_t=\Sigma&\frac{
\sbrk{h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_v}}{\eta_{\xi_1},\eta_{\xi_2}\ldots\eta_{\xi_{v'}}}\times
\sbrk{h_{q_{v+1}},h_{q_{v+2}}\ldots h_{q_m}}{\eta_{\xi_{v'+1}},\eta_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots\eta_{\xi_n}}
}{
\sbrk{h_{q_1},h_{q_2}\ldots h_{q_v}}{h_{q_{v+1}},h_{q_{v+2}}\ldots h_{q_m}}\times
\sbrk{\eta_{\xi_1},\eta_{\xi_2}\ldots\eta_{\xi_{v'}}}{\eta_{\xi_{v'+1}},\eta_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots\eta_{\xi_n}}
}\,\\[-0.1em]
&\times\{(x-h_{q_1})(x-h_{q_2})\ldots(x-h_{q_v})(x-\eta_{\xi_1})(x-\eta_{\xi_2})\ldots(x-\eta_{\xi_{v'}})\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{21}
\]
\endgroup
I shall first show this sum of fractions is in substance an integral function of the quantities $h_1,h_2\ldots h_m;\ \eta_1,\eta_2\ldots\eta_n$. For greater conciseness write in general $x-h=E$, $x-\eta=H$; we have then, since
\[
h-\eta=H-E,
\qquad h_{q_r}-h_{q_s}=E_{q_s}-E_{q_r},
\qquad \eta_{\xi_r}-\eta_{\xi_s}=H_{\xi_s}-H_{\xi_r},
\]
\begingroup\scriptsize
\[
\Syz_t=\Sigma\frac{
\sbrk{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}{E_{q_1},E_{q_2}\ldots E_{q_v}}\times
\sbrk{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}{E_{q_{v+1}},E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}
}{
\sbrk{E_{q_{v+1}},E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}{E_{q_1},E_{q_2}\ldots E_{q_v}}\times
\sbrk{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}
}
\,E_{q_1}\ldots E_{q_v}H_{\xi_1}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}.\tag{22}
\]
\endgroup

\sourcepage{462}
On reducing the fractions contained within the sign of summation to a common denominator, $\Syz_t$ will take the form $\frac{N}{D\cdot\Delta}$, where $D$ will be the product of the $\frac12m(m-1)$ differences of $E_1,E_2\ldots E_m$ subtracted each from each, and $\Delta$ the corresponding product of the differences \emph{inter se} of $H_1,H_2\ldots H_n$. Hence, unless the sum in question is an integral function of the $E$'s and $H$'s it will become infinite when any two of the $E$ series, or any two of the $H$ series of quantities are made equal. Suppose now $E_1=E_2$; the terms in (22) which contain $E_1-E_2$ in the denominator will evidently group themselves into pairs of the respective forms,
\begingroup\scriptsize
\[
\frac{(E_1E_{q_3}\ldots E_{q_v})\times(H_{\xi_1}H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}})\times
\sbrk{E_1,E_{q_3}\ldots E_{q_v}}{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}\times
\sbrk{E_2,E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}}{
\sbrk{E_1,E_{q_3}\ldots E_{q_v}}{E_2,E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}\times
\sbrk{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}}
\]
and
\[
\frac{(E_2E_{q_3}\ldots E_{q_v})\times(H_{\xi_1}H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}})\times
\sbrk{E_2,E_{q_3}\ldots E_{q_v}}{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}\times
\sbrk{E_1,E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}}{
\sbrk{E_2,E_{q_3}\ldots E_{q_v}}{E_1,E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}\times
\sbrk{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}};
\]
\endgroup
the sum of this pair of terms will be of the form
\[
\resizebox{0.95\textwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\frac{P}{Q}\left\{
\frac{E_1}{E_1-E_2}
\frac{\sbrk{E_1}{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}\times
\sbrk{E_2}{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}}{
\sbrk{E_1}{E_{q_{v+1}},E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}}
+
\frac{E_2}{E_2-E_1}
\frac{\sbrk{E_2}{H_{\xi_1},H_{\xi_2}\ldots H_{\xi_{v'}}}\times
\sbrk{E_1}{H_{\xi_{v'+1}},H_{\xi_{v'+2}}\ldots H_{\xi_n}}}{
\sbrk{E_2}{E_{q_{v+1}},E_{q_{v+2}}\ldots E_{q_m}}}
\right\},$}
\]
where $Q$, it may be observed, does not contain $H_1-H_2$, so that $\frac{P}{Q}$ remains finite when $H_1=H_2$.

The above pair of terms together make up a sum of the form
\[
\frac{P}{Q}\frac{1}{E_1-E_2}\frac{\phi(E_1,E_2)\psi E_2-\phi(E_2,E_1)\psi E_1}{\psi E_1\times\psi E_2},
\]
which, as the numerator of the third factor vanishes when $E_1=E_2$, remains finite on that supposition. Hence the whole sum of terms in (22) which is

\sourcepage{463}
made up of such pairs of terms, and of other terms in which $E_1-E_2$ does not enter, remains finite when $E_1-E_2=0$, and therefore generally when $D=0$, and similarly when $H_1-H_2=0$, and therefore also when $\Delta=0$; hence the expression for $\Syz_t$ in (22) is an integral function of the $E$ and $H$ series of quantities, as was to be proved.

\art{17} Let us now proceed to determine the dimensions of the coefficient of $x^t$, the highest power of $x$ in this value of $\Syz_t$, when supposed to be expressed under the form of an integral function (as it has been proved to be capable of being expressed) of $h_1,h_2\ldots h_m;\ \eta_1,\eta_2\ldots\eta_n;\ x$.

This coefficient is the sum of fractions the numerators of each of which consist of two factors, which are respectively of $v\times v'$ and of $(m-v)\times(n-v')$ dimensions in respect of the two sets of roots taken conjointly, and the denominators of two factors respectively of $v(m-v)$ and $v'(n-v')$ dimensions in respect of the same.

Consequently, the exponent of the total dimensions of the coefficient in question
\[
\begin{aligned}
&=vv'+(m-v)(n-v')-v(m-v)-v'(n-v')\
&=(m-v-v')\times(n-v-v')\
&=(m-t)(n-t),
\end{aligned}
\]
and thus is seen to depend only on the degree $t$ in $x$ of $\Syz_t$, and not upon the mode of partitioning $t$ into two parts $v$ and $v'$, for the purpose of representing $\Syz_t$ by means of formula (19).

\art{18} I shall now demonstrate that every form in this scale (to a numerical factor \emph{près}) is identical with a simplified residue to $f,\phi$, of the same degree $t$ in $x$. Any such simplified residue is, like $\Syz_t$, a syzygetic function, or to use a briefer form of speech a conjunctive of $f,\phi$; and if we agree to understand by the ``weight'' of any function of the coefficients of $f$ and $\phi$ its joint dimensions in respect of the roots of $f$ and $\phi$ combined, I shall prove,---first, that any simplified residue of $f$ and $\phi$ of a given degree in $x$ is that conjunctive, whose weight in respect of the roots of $f$ and $\phi$ is less than the weight of any other such conjunctive; and second, that $\Syz_t$, as determined above (in equation 22), is of the same weight as the simplified residue, and can therefore only differ from it by some numerical factor. For the purpose of comparison of weights, it will of course be sufficient to confine our attention to the coefficients of the highest power in $x$ (or any other, the same for each) of the forms whose weights are to be compared.

Suppose $f$ to be of $m$ dimensions, and $\phi$ to be of $n$ dimensions in $x$; and let $m=n+e$.

\sourcepage{464}
Suppose
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Lambda f+L\phi=Ax^t+Bx^{t-1}+\cdots+K,\tag{23}\\
\Lambda=\lambda_0x^q+\lambda_1x^{q-1}+\cdots+\lambda_q,\\
L=l_0x^{q+e}+l_1x^{q+e-1}+\cdots+l_{q+e},
\end{gathered}
\]
the number of homogeneous equations to be satisfied by the $q+1$ quantities $\lambda_0,\lambda_1\ldots\lambda_q$, and the $q+e+1$ quantities $l_0,l_1\ldots l_{q+e}$ will be $m+q-t$, and therefore $q+1$ and $q+e+1$ taken together must be not less than $m+q-t+1$, that is $2q+e+2$ must be not less than $q+m-t+1$, that is $q$ not less than $m-t-e-1$; and if this inequality be satisfied $2q+e+2-(q+m-t+1)+1$, that is $q+t+e-m+2$ will be the number of arbitrary constants entering into the solution of equation (23).

If $q$ be greater than $(n-1)$, let $q=(n-1)+t$; and let
\[
(\Lambda)=\lambda_0x^{n-1}+\lambda_1x^{n-2}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1},
\]
\[
(L)=l_0x^{n+e-1}+l_1x^{n+e-2}+\cdots+l_{e+n-1};
\]
and let $(\Lambda),(L)$ be so taken as to satisfy the equation
\[
(\Lambda)f+(L)\phi=Ax^t+Bx^{t-1}+\cdots+K;
\]
and make
\[
\Xi=(\Lambda)+(f+gx+\cdots+hx^{t-1})\phi,
\]
\[
X=(L)-(f+gx+\cdots+hx^{t-1})f,
\]
$f,g\ldots h$ being arbitrary constants; then
\[
\Xi f+X\phi=(\Lambda)f+(L)\phi=Ax^t+Bx^{t-1}+\cdots+K.
\]
Now the total number of arbitrary constants in the system $(\Lambda)$ and $(L)$ will be $n-1+t+e-m+2$, that is $t+1$; hence the total number of arbitrary constants in $\Xi$ and $X$ will be $t+1+t$, that is $q-n+t+2$, which is equal to $q+t+e-m+2$, the number of arbitrary constants in the most general values of $\Lambda$ and $L$. From this we infer that any conjunctive of $f$ and $\phi$ of the degree $t$, of which the leading coefficient is of the weight $(m-t)(n-t)$, all the coefficients being of course understood to be integral functions of the roots of $f$ and $\phi$, must, to a numerical factor \emph{près}, be equivalent to any other of the same weight; and furthermore, any supposed function of $x$ of the $t$th degree which possesses the property characteristic of a conjunctive of vanishing when $f$ and $\phi$ vanish simultaneously, but of which the weight of the leading coefficient would be \emph{less} than $(m-t)(n-t)$, must be a mere nugatory form and have all its terms identically zero\footnote{And more generally it admits of being demonstrated by precisely the same course of reasoning, that the number of arbitrary parameters in a conjunctive of the degree $t$, and of the weight $(m-t)(n-t)+e$ in the roots, cannot (abstraction being supposed to be made of an arbitrary numerical multiplier) exceed the number $e$.}.

\art{21} We have previously shown, Art.~16, that $\Syz$, as defined by equation (21), is an integral function of the roots $f$ and $\phi$, and vanishes when $f$ and $\phi$ vanish. Moreover, its weight in the roots has been proved to be $(m-t)(n-t)$, and consequently, if by way of distinguishing the several forms of $\Syz_t$ we name that one where $t$ in the equation above cited is supposed to be divided into two parts, $v$ and $v'$, $\Syz_{v,v'}$, we have for all values of $v$ and $v'$, such that $v+v'$ is not greater than $n$, $\Syz_{v,v'}$ to a constant numerical factor \emph{près} identical with the $(v+v')$th simplified residue to $(f,\phi)$, so that the form of $\Syz_{v,v'}$ depends only upon the value of $v+v'$.

\art{22} It must be well borne in mind that this permanency of the value of $\Syz_{v,v'}$ for different values of $v$ has only been established for the case where $t$ can be the degree of a residue to $f$ and $\phi$, that is to say, when $t$ is less than the lesser of the two indices $m$ and $n$. When $t$ does not satisfy this condition of inequality, the theorem ceases to be true. It is clear that when $m=n$ and $v+v'=m=n$, $\Syz_{v,v'}$, which always remains a conjunctive of $f$ and $\phi$, can only be a numerical linear function of $f$ and $\phi$; and I have ascertained when $m=n$ on giving to $v$ and $v'$ the respective values successively $(0,n),(1,n-1),(2,(n-2))\ldots(n,0)$ that
\[
\Syz_{0,n}=f,
\qquad
\Syz_{1,n-1}=(n-1)f+\phi,
\qquad
\Syz_{2,n-2}=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{1\cdot2}f+(n-1)\phi,\ldots
\]
\[
\Syz_{n-1,1}=f+(n-1)\phi,
\qquad
\Syz_{n,0}=\phi.
\]
Thus, by way of a simple example, let
\[
f=x^2+ax+b=(x-h_1)(x-h_2),
\]
\[
\phi=x^2+\alpha x+\beta=(x-k_1)(x-k_2),
\]

\sourcepage{469}
\[
\Syz_{0,2}=(x-h_1)(x-h_2)
\left(\frac{\sbrk{h_1h_2}{\cdots}}{\sbrk{\cdots}{h_1h_2}}\times
\frac{\sbrk{\cdots}{k_1k_2}}{\sbrk{\cdots}{k_1k_2}}\right)=(x-h_1)(x-h_2)=f,
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Syz_{1,1}&=\Sigma(x-h_1)(x-k_1)
\frac{\sbrk{h_1}{k_1}}{\sbrk{h_1}{h_2}\times\sbrk{k_1}{k_2}}\\
&=\Sigma\frac{x-h_1}{h_1-h_2}\,\frac{x-k_1}{k_1-k_2}\{(h_1-k_1)(h_2-k_2)\},
\end{aligned}
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}

&=\Sigma\frac{x-h_1}{h_1-h_2}\left[\frac1{k_1-k_2}\{(x-k_1)(h_1-k_1)(h_2-k_2)\right.\\

&=(x^2+ax+b)+(x^2+\alpha x+\beta)\\

\end{aligned}

so we find also $\Syz_{2,0}=\phi$.

\art{23} The expression $\Syz_{v,v'}$, which is universally a conjunctive of $f$ and $\phi$, continues algebraically interpretable so long as $v+v'$ has any value intermediate between 0 and $m+n$; when $v+v'=0$ we must of course have $v=0$ and $v'=0$, and $\Syz_{0,0}$ becomes the resultant of $f$ and $\phi$; when $v+v'=m+n$ we must also have the unique solution $v=m$ and $v'=n$, and $\Syz_{m,n}$ becomes necessarily $f\times\phi$, which we thus see stands in a sort of antithetical relation to the resultant of $f$ and $\phi$, say $(f,\phi)$. Nor is it without interest to remark that $f\times\phi=0$ implies that a factor of $f$ or else of $\phi$ is zero; and $(f,\phi)=0$ implies that if a factor of the one of the functions is zero, so also is a factor of the other, that is that a factor of each or of neither is zero. As $t$ increases from 0 to $n$ or decreases from $m+n$ to $m-1$, the number of solutions of the equation $v+v'=t$ in the one case, and the number of admissible solutions of the equation $v+v'=t$ in the other case, which is subject to the condition that $v$ must not exceed $n$, continues to increase by a unit at each step; there being thus $n+1$ different forms $\Syz_{v,v'}$ when $v+v'=n$, and the same number when $v+v'=m-1$. For all values of $t$ intermediate between $n$ and $(m-1)$ (both taken exclusively) it is very remarkable that $\Syz_{v,v'}$ will vanish, as I proceed to demonstrate.

\art{24} The weight of the coefficient of the highest power of $\Syz_{v,v'}$ ($v+v'$ being equal to $t$) is $(m-t)(n-t)$, and consequently, when $t$ is greater than $n$, and less than $m$, $\Syz_{v,v'}$ would contain fractional functions of the roots of $f$ and $\phi$, if there were in it a power $x^t$, but $\Syz_{v,v'}$ has been proved to be always an integer function of the roots. Hence the coefficient of $x^t$ will be zero, and so more generally the first power of $x$ in $\Syz_{v,v'}$ of which the coefficient is not zero, will be $x^{t-\omega}$, subject to the condition (since evidently the weight of the several coefficients goes on increasing by units as the degree of the terms in $x$ decreases by the same) that $\omega$ be not less than $(m-t)(t-n)$; let then $\omega=(m-t)(t-n)$, $\Syz_{v,v'}$ becomes of the form $Ax^{t-\omega}+Bx^{t-\omega-1}+\&c.$, where $A$ is of zero dimensions; but this is impossible if $t-\omega<n$, for then $Ax^{t-\omega}+\&c.$ is a conjunctive of weight lower than the lowest-weighted simplified residue of the degree $t-\omega$. Hence $\omega$ is not greater than $t-n$, that is $(m-t)(t-n)$ is not greater than $t-n$, that is $m-t$ cannot be greater than 1, that is $t$ when intermediate between $m$ and $n$ cannot be less than $m-1$, otherwise $\Syz_{v,v'}$ will vanish identically. Moreover, when $t=m-1$, $\omega=t-n$, and $t-\omega=n$, and accordingly $\Syz_{v,m-1-v}$ is not merely, as we might know, \emph{à priori} an algebraical, but more simply a numerical multiple of $\phi$ for all values of $v$. The same is of course true also, $m$ being greater than $n$, for every form $\Syz_{n,m-n}$, since this is always a conjunctive of $f$ and $\phi$ of which the former is of a degree higher than the $\Syz$ in question, so that the multiplier of $f$ in this conjunctive must be zero\footnote{It thus appears that if the indices $m$ and $n$ do not differ by at least 3 units, $\Syz$ will have an actual quantitative existence for all values of $t$ between 0 and $m+n$; or in other words, the failure in the quantitative existence of the forms $\Syz_t$ only begins to show itself when this difference is 3; thus if $m=n+3$, $\Syz_n$ exists, and $\Syz_{n+2}$ exists, but $\Syz_{n+1}=0$.}.

This latter has been already identified with the simplified residue obtained by expanding $\frac{\phi x}{fx}$ under the form of an improper continued fraction.
