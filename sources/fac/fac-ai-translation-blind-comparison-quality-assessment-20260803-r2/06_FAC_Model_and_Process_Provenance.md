# Model and process provenance

## Authority

Jean-Pierre Serre, *Faisceaux algébriques cohérents*, *Annals of Mathematics* (2) 61 (1955), 197--278, DOI `10.2307/1969915`.

Frozen project authority identity: 83 PDF pages, 6,305,726 bytes, SHA-256 `B8C6508249308F5D8BC886B9E6360F1EC4FDC8F0F18EFAA03EE0E36B591551CA`. The authority PDF is not redistributed in this deposit.

## Initial transcription lineage

The initial complete French working-transcription package was produced in a Claude Opus 5 project lane, according to the orchestrator's provenance record. Its public-control package is `serre-fac-complete-working-transcription-20260731`. The package identifies all 82 printed pages, records twelve manually recovered page units, and hash-binds the authority and source files.

This initial transcription was never treated as authority. Codex later checked it against the journal image, separated diplomatic and corrected French layers, recorded 42 transcription-repair groups and 38 disclosed printed-source corrections in the evolving project controls, and propagated source-established corrections to English where applicable.

The frozen transcription package itself records artifacts and hashes but does not cryptographically encode the hosted model name. "Claude Opus 5" is therefore an orchestrator/project-provenance assertion, not a claim derivable from the TeX bytes alone.

## English and comparison lineage

The source-aligned English translation, French correction layers, build checks, and blind-comparator adjudication were produced in an OpenAI Codex 5.6 session using Ultra reasoning mode, according to the active session and orchestrator record.

Codex translated nos. 1--79 before the comparator was found. Nos. 80--81 were completed afterward and are comparator-aware, though still source-adjudicated from French. The complete English reader is therefore not entirely blind; the separate no. 79 PDF is the blind artifact.

As with the Claude identification, "OpenAI Codex 5.6 / Ultra" is project runtime metadata. The artifact hashes prove the compared byte states and chronology boundary, not the identity of a remote hosted model. This distinction is retained to avoid overstating reproducibility.

## Human-orchestrator role

The project orchestrator:

- selected FAC for transcription and translation;
- was unaware of the Achinger--Krupa translation when the Codex work began;
- discovered the public comparator PDF only after the no. 79 freeze and later supplied its source archive;
- required source adjudication rather than English majority vote;
- required append-only logs, correction/reversal history, and privacy-clean publication evidence;
- did not supply a mathematician peer-review or certification layer.

## Tool and evidence boundary

Existing OCR or transcription was used only as a locator/drafting layer. Difficult readings were decided from the authority image at detail proportional to the ambiguity. Builds and rendered pages checked TeX integrity and presentation; they could not substitute for mathematical/source judgment. The finding and decision ledgers retain rejected alternatives and operational failures rather than presenting only successful final output.
