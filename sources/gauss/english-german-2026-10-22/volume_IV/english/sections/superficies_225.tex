III. The angle between two planes equals the spherical angle between the great circles that represent them. It is therefore also measured by the arc between the poles of these great circles. In the same way, the inclination of a straight line to a plane is measured by the arc drawn from the point corresponding to the direction of the line, perpendicular to the great circle that represents the orientation of the plane.

IV. If \(x,y,z;\ x',y',z'\) denote the coordinates of two points, \(r\) their distance apart, and \(L\) the point on the spherical surface that represents the direction of the line drawn from the first point to the second, then
\[
x'=x+r\cos(1)L,\qquad y'=y+r\cos(2)L,\qquad z'=z+r\cos(3)L
\]
\par\indent V. It follows readily that in general
\[
\cos(1)L^2+\cos(2)L^2+\cos(3)L^2=1
\]
\noindent and, if \(L'\) denotes any other point on the spherical surface, that
\[
\cos(1)L\mathord{.}\cos(1)L'+\cos(2)L\mathord{.}\cos(2)L'+\cos(3)L\mathord{.}\cos(3)L'=\cos LL'
\]
\par\indent VI. \textsc{Theorem.} \textit{If \(L,L',L'',L'''\) denote four points on the surface of the sphere and \(A\) the angle that the arcs \(LL',L''L'''\) form at their intersection, then}
\[
\cos LL''\mathord{.}\cos L'L'''-\cos LL'''\mathord{.}\cos L'L''=\sin LL'\mathord{.}\sin L''L'''\mathord{.}\cos A
\]
\par\indent\textit{Proof.} Let \(A\) also denote the point of intersection itself, and set
\[
AL=t,\quad AL'=t',\quad AL''=t'',\quad AL'''=t'''
\]
\noindent We therefore have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\cos LL''&=\cos t\cos t''+\sin t\sin t''\cos A\\
\cos L'L'''&=\cos t'\cos t'''+\sin t'\sin t'''\cos A\\
\cos LL'''&=\cos t\cos t'''+\sin t\sin t'''\cos A\\
\cos L'L''&=\cos t'\cos t''+\sin t'\sin t''\cos A
\end{aligned}
\]
\noindent and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\cos LL''\mathord{.}\cos L'L'''-\cos LL'''\mathord{.}\cos L'L''\\
&\quad=\cos A(\cos t\cos t''\sin t'\sin t'''+\cos t'\cos t'''\sin t\sin t''\\
&\qquad\qquad-\cos t\cos t'''\sin t'\sin t''-\cos t'\cos t''\sin t\sin t''')\\
&\quad=\cos A(\cos t\sin t'-\sin t\cos t')(\cos t''\sin t'''-\sin t''\cos t''')\\
&\quad=\cos A\mathord{.}\sin(t'-t)\mathord{.}\sin(t'''-t'')\\
&\quad=\cos A\mathord{.}\sin LL'\mathord{.}\sin L''L'''
\end{aligned}
\]
