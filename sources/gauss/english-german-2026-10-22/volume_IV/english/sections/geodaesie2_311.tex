\indent To expand equations I, II, and III fully, we make the further substitutions
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log m&=\tfrac1{12}rr+\tfrac7{1440}r^4+\tfrac{31}{90720}r^6+\tfrac{127}{4838400}r^8+\text{etc.}\\
mm&=1+\tfrac16rr+\tfrac{17}{720}r^4+\tfrac{31}{10080}r^6+\text{etc.}\\
m^4&=1+\tfrac13rr+\tfrac3{40}r^4+\text{etc.}\\
m^6&=1+\tfrac12rr+\text{etc.}\\
&\text{etc.}\\
\log n&=-\tfrac1{24}rr-\tfrac1{2880}r^4-\tfrac1{181440}r^6-\tfrac1{9676800}r^8-\text{etc.}\\
nn&=1-\tfrac1{12}rr+\tfrac1{360}r^4-\tfrac1{20160}r^6+\text{etc.}\\
n^4&=1-\tfrac16rr+\tfrac1{80}r^4-\text{etc.}\\
n^6&=1-\tfrac14rr+\text{etc.}\\
&\text{etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]

\indent We thus obtain compound series for the logarithms of \(t,l,b\), or more precisely for the differences between these logarithms and the approximate values \(\log\tau,\log\lambda,\log\beta\). These series proceed
\begin{list}{}{\setlength{\leftmargin}{1.4em}\setlength{\itemindent}{0pt}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}}
\item for \(\log t\), in even powers of \(\tau\) and \(r\), and their products,
\item for \(\log l\), similarly in powers of \(\lambda\) and \(r\),
\item for \(\log b\), in powers of \(\beta,\lambda\) and \(r\),
\end{list}
