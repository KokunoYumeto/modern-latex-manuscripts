As a fourth example, we will consider the representation of the surface of an ellipsoid of revolution in a plane. Let \(a\) and \(b\) be its two principal semiaxes, so that we can set
\[
\begin{aligned}
x&=a\cos t\sin u\\
y&=a\sin t\sin u\\
z&=b\cos u
\end{aligned}
\]
\SourceLeaf{211}%
\noindent This gives
\[
\omega=aa\sin u^2\,\mathrm{d}t^2+(aa\cos u^2+bb\sin u^2)\mathrm{d}u^2
\]
\noindent and the differential expression \(\omega=0\) gives the following if we put \(\BareRoot{(1-\frac{bb}{aa})}=\varepsilon\) for brevity, provided that the semiaxis along the axis of revolution satisfies \(b<a\)
\[
0=\mathrm{d}t\mp i\,\mathrm{d}u\mathord{.}\BareRoot{(\mathrm{cotang}\,u^2+1-\varepsilon\varepsilon)}
\]

If we set
\[
\BareRoot{(1-\varepsilon\varepsilon)}\mathord{.}\mathrm{tang}\,u=\mathrm{tang}\,w
\]
\noindent where, in the application to the Earth spheroid, \(90^0-w\) will denote geographic latitude and \(t\) longitude, the equation becomes
\[
0=\mathrm{d}t\mp i\,\mathrm{d}w\mathord{.}\frac{1-\varepsilon\varepsilon}{(1-\varepsilon\varepsilon\cos w^2)\sin w}
\]
\noindent whose integral is
\[
\text{Const.}=t\pm i\log\mathord{.}\Bigl\{\mathrm{cotang}\tfrac{1}{2}w\mathord{.}\Bigl(\frac{1-\varepsilon\cos w}{1+\varepsilon\cos w}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon}\Bigr\}
\]
\noindent Hence, with \(f\) denoting an arbitrary function, we take for \(X\) the real part and for \(iY\) the imaginary part of
\[
f\Bigl(t+i\log\Bigl\{\mathrm{cotang}\tfrac{1}{2}w\mathord{.}\Bigl(\frac{1-\varepsilon\cos w}{1+\varepsilon\cos w}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon}\Bigr\}\Bigr)
\]
\noindent --- If we choose a linear function for \(f\), that is, \(f\upsilon=k\upsilon\), we get
\[
X=kt,\quad Y=k\log\mathrm{cotang}\tfrac{1}{2}w-\tfrac{1}{2}k\varepsilon\log\frac{1+\varepsilon\cos w}{1-\varepsilon\cos w}
\]
\noindent which gives a projection analogous to \textsc{Mercator}'s.

If, on the other hand, we take for \(f\) an imaginary exponential function \(f\upsilon=ke^{i\lambda\upsilon}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=k\mathord{.}\mathrm{tang}\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\lambda}\mathord{.}\Bigl(\frac{1+\varepsilon\cos w}{1-\varepsilon\cos w}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\lambda}\mathord{.}\cos\lambda t,\\
Y&=k\,\mathrm{tang}\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\lambda}\mathord{.}\Bigl(\frac{1+\varepsilon\cos w}{1-\varepsilon\cos w}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\lambda}\mathord{.}\sin\lambda t
\end{aligned}
\]
\noindent This gives a projection analogous to the polar stereographic projection if we set \(\lambda=1\). In general it gives a very useful projection for representing part of the Earth’s surface when its flattening is to be taken into account.

What is to be said about the other case, where \(b>a\), can readily be deduced directly from the foregoing. If we keep the same
\SourceLeaf{212}
notation, \(\varepsilon\) becomes imaginary, but \((\frac{1+\varepsilon\cos w}{1-\varepsilon\cos w})^{\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon}\) is again real. For the sake of completeness, however, we will give the formulas for this case separately, setting \(\BareRoot{(\frac{bb}{aa}-1)}=\eta\) at the outset. We then determine \(w\) from the equation
\[
\BareRoot{(1+\eta\eta)}\mathord{.}\mathrm{tang}\,u=\mathrm{tang}\,w
\]
\noindent and the differential equation
\[
0=\mathrm{d}t\mp i\,\mathrm{d}w\mathord{.}\frac{1+\eta\eta}{(1+\eta\eta\cos w^2)\sin w}
\]
\noindent gives the integral
\[
\text{Const.}=t\pm i(\log\mathrm{cotang}\tfrac{1}{2}w+\eta\,\mathrm{Arc}\,\mathrm{tang}.\,\eta\cos w)
\]
\noindent so that \(X\) is to be taken for the real part and \(iY\) for the imaginary part of
\[
f(t+i(\log\mathrm{cotang}\tfrac{1}{2}w+\eta\,\mathrm{Arc}\,\mathrm{tang}.\,\eta\cos w))
\]
\noindent The counterparts of the two special applications above follow at once. In the first of these the values
\[
X=kt,\qquad Y=k\log\mathrm{cotang}\tfrac{1}{2}w+\eta k\,\mathrm{Arc}\,\mathrm{tang}.\,\eta\cos w
\]
\noindent and in the second the values
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=k\,\mathrm{tang}\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\lambda}\mathord{.}e^{-\eta\lambda\,\mathrm{Arc}\,\mathrm{tang}.\,\eta\cos w}\mathord{.}\cos\lambda t\\
Y&=k\,\mathrm{tang}\tfrac{1}{2}w^{\lambda}\mathord{.}e^{-\eta\lambda\,\mathrm{Arc}\,\mathrm{tang}.\,\eta\cos w}\mathord{.}\sin\lambda t
\end{aligned}
\]
\noindent must be used.
