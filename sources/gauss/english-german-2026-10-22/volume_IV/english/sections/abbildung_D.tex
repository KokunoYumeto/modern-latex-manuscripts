\Article{6}
If \(p'+iq'\) denotes any specified function of \(p+iq\) (where \(p',q'\) are real functions of \(p,q\)), it is easy to see that
\[
p'+iq'=\text{Const.}\quad\text{and}\quad p'-iq'=\text{Const.}
\]
\noindent also represent the integrals of the differential equation \(\omega=0\). Indeed, they will be entirely equivalent to the preceding equations
\[
p+iq=\text{Const.}\quad\text{and}\quad p-iq=\text{Const.}
\]
\noindent respectively. Likewise, the integrals of the differential equation \(\Omega=0\)
\[
P'+iQ'=\text{Const.}\quad\text{and}\quad P'-iQ'=\text{Const.}
\]
\noindent will be entirely equivalent to the preceding equations
\[
P+iQ=\text{Const.}\quad\text{and}\quad P-iQ=\text{Const.}
\]
\noindent if \(P'+iQ'\) denotes any specified function of \(P+iQ\) (where \(P',Q'\) are real functions of \(P,Q\)). It follows that in the general solution to our problem that we gave in the preceding article, \(p',q'\) may take the place of \(p,q\) and \(P',Q'\) the place of \(P,Q\), respectively. Although this change adds nothing to the generality of the solution, one form may sometimes be more convenient for one purpose and the other for another.

\Article{7}
If the functions obtained by differentiating the arbitrary functions \(f,f'\) are denoted by \(\varphi\) and \(\varphi'\), respectively, so that \(\mathrm{d}\mathord{.}f\upsilon=\varphi\upsilon\mathord{.}\mathrm{d}\upsilon\), \(\mathrm{d}\mathord{.}f'\upsilon=\varphi'\upsilon\mathord{.}\mathrm{d}\upsilon\), our general solution gives
\[
\frac{\mathrm{d}P+i\,\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}p+i\,\mathrm{d}q}=\varphi(p+iq),\qquad
\frac{\mathrm{d}P-i\,\mathrm{d}Q}{\mathrm{d}p-i\,\mathrm{d}q}=\varphi'(p-iq)
\]
\noindent hence
\[
\frac{mmn}{N}=\varphi(p+iq)\mathord{.}\varphi'(p-iq)
\]
\noindent The ratio of magnification is therefore determined by the formula
\[
m=\BareRoot{\Bigl\{\frac{\mathrm{d}p^2+\mathrm{d}q^2}{\omega}\mathord{.}\frac{\Omega}{\mathrm{d}P^2+\mathrm{d}Q^2}\mathord{.}\varphi(p+iq)\mathord{.}\varphi'(p-iq)\Bigr\}}
\]
