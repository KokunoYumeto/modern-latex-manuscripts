\textsc{Problem.} \textit{Let \(v,v',v''\) etc. denote the following linear functions of the indeterminates \(x,y,z\) etc.}
\[
\left.\begin{aligned}
v&=ax+by+cz+\text{etc.}+l\\
v'&=a'x+b'y+c'z+\text{etc.}+l'\\
v''&=a''x+b''y+c''z+\text{etc.}+l''\text{ etc.}
\end{aligned}\right\}\quad\text{(I)}
\]
\textit{From all systems of coefficients \(\kappa,\kappa',\kappa''\) etc. that give identically}
\[
\kappa v+\kappa'v'+\kappa''v''+\text{etc.}=x-k
\]
\textit{with \(k\) a determinate quantity, i.e. independent of \(x,y,z\) etc., find the one for which \(\kappa\kappa+\kappa'\kappa'+\kappa''\kappa''+\text{etc.}\) attains its smallest value.}

\textit{Solution.} Let us put
\[
\left.\begin{aligned}
av+a'v'+a''v''+\text{etc.}&=\xi\\
bv+b'v'+b''v''+\text{etc.}&=\eta\\
cv+c'v'+c''v''+\text{etc.}&=\zeta
\end{aligned}\right\}\quad\text{(II)}
\]
etc.: then \(\xi,\eta,\zeta\) etc. will also be linear functions of \(x,y,z\) etc., namely
\[
\left.\begin{aligned}
\xi&=x\Sigma aa+y\Sigma ab+z\Sigma ac+\text{etc.}+\Sigma al\\
\eta&=x\Sigma ab+y\Sigma bb+z\Sigma bc+\text{etc.}+\Sigma bl\\
\zeta&=x\Sigma ac+y\Sigma bc+z\Sigma cc+\text{etc.}+\Sigma cl\text{ etc.}
\end{aligned}\right\}\quad\text{(III)}
\]
(where \(\Sigma aa\) denotes the sum \(aa+a'a'+a''a''+\text{etc.}\), and similarly for the others), and the number of \(\xi,\eta,\zeta\) etc. is equal to the number of the indeterminates \(x,y,z\) etc., namely \(=\rho\). By elimination, therefore, an equation of the following kind can be obtained\footnote{The reason why we chose precisely these characters to denote the coefficients arising from such an elimination will become clear below.}
\SourceLeaf{028}
\[
x=A+[\alpha\alpha]\xi+[\alpha\beta]\eta+[\alpha\gamma]\zeta+\text{etc.}
\]
in which, on substituting for \(\xi,\eta,\zeta\) etc. their values from III, an identical equation must result. Thus, putting
\[
\left.\begin{aligned}
a[\alpha\alpha]+b[\alpha\beta]+c[\alpha\gamma]+\text{etc.}&=\alpha\\
a'[\alpha\alpha]+b'[\alpha\beta]+c'[\alpha\gamma]+\text{etc.}&=\alpha'\\
a''[\alpha\alpha]+b''[\alpha\beta]+c''[\alpha\gamma]+\text{etc.}&=\alpha''\text{ etc.}
\end{aligned}\right\}\quad\text{(IV)}
\]
we shall necessarily have, identically,
\[
\alpha v+\alpha'v'+\alpha''v''+\text{etc.}=x-A\quad\text{(V)}
\]
This equation shows that the systems of values of the coefficients \(\kappa,\kappa',\kappa''\) etc. must certainly also include \(\kappa=\alpha,\kappa'=\alpha',\kappa''=\alpha''\) etc., and that, for any system, we must have identically
\[
(\kappa-\alpha)v+(\kappa'-\alpha')v'+(\kappa''-\alpha'')v''+\text{etc.}=A-k
\]
which equation implies the following
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\kappa-\alpha)a+(\kappa'-\alpha')a'+(\kappa''-\alpha'')a''+\text{etc.}&=0\\
(\kappa-\alpha)b+(\kappa'-\alpha')b'+(\kappa''-\alpha'')b''+\text{etc.}&=0\\
(\kappa-\alpha)c+(\kappa'-\alpha')c'+(\kappa''-\alpha'')c''+\text{etc.}&=0\text{ etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying these equations respectively by \([\alpha\alpha],[\alpha\beta],[\alpha\gamma]\) etc., and adding, we obtain by (IV)
\[
(\kappa-\alpha)\alpha+(\kappa'-\alpha')\alpha'+(\kappa''-\alpha'')\alpha''+\text{etc.}=0
\]
or, what is the same,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\kappa\kappa+\kappa'\kappa'+\kappa''\kappa''+\text{etc.}\\
&=\alpha\alpha+\alpha'\alpha'+\alpha''\alpha''+\text{etc.}{}\\
&\quad+(\kappa-\alpha)^2+(\kappa'-\alpha')^2+(\kappa''-\alpha'')^2+\text{etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]
whence it is evident that the sum \(\kappa\kappa+\kappa'\kappa'+\kappa''\kappa''+\text{etc.}\) attains its smallest value if we put \(\kappa=\alpha,\kappa'=\alpha',\kappa''=\alpha''\) etc. Q.~E.~I.

Moreover, this smallest value itself is found in the following way. Equation (V) shows that
\SourceLeaf{029}
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha a+\alpha'a'+\alpha''a''+\text{etc.}&=1\\
\alpha b+\alpha'b'+\alpha''b''+\text{etc.}&=0\\
\alpha c+\alpha'c'+\alpha''c''+\text{etc.}&=0\text{ etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]
Multiplying these equations respectively by \([\alpha\alpha],[\alpha\beta],[\alpha\gamma]\) etc. and adding, we immediately obtain with the aid of equations (IV)
\[
\alpha\alpha+\alpha'\alpha'+\alpha''\alpha''+\text{etc.}=[\alpha\alpha]
\]
