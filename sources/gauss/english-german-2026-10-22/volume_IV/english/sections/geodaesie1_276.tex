\indent For the inverse problem, in which \(q\) is given and \(p\) is sought, expansion in a series is even more important, since in this case the finite formula 3 could lead to the result only indirectly. \textsc{Taylor}'s theorem gives
\[
p=\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}U}\mathord{.}q-\frac{\mathrm{dd}w}{2\mathrm{d}U^2}\mathord{.}qq+\frac{\mathrm{d}^3w}{6\mathrm{d}U^3}\mathord{.}q^3-\text{and so on}
\]
where the derivatives are to be replaced by the particular values corresponding to \(q=0\) or to \(U=90^0-Q\), \(w=90^0-P\). The general values of the first three derivatives are given by the following expressions.
\[
\frac{\mathrm{d}w}{\mathrm{d}U}=\frac{(1-ee\cos w^2)\sin w}{\alpha(1-ee)\sin U}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{dd}w}{\mathrm{d}U^2}={}&\frac{(1-ee\cos w^2)\sin w}{\alpha\alpha(1-ee)^2\sin U^2}\\
&\quad(\alpha(1-ee)\cos U-\cos w+ee\cos w(\cos w^2-2\sin w^2))
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}^3w}{\mathrm{d}U^3}={}&\frac{(1-ee\cos w^2)\sin w}{\alpha^3(1-ee)^3\sin U^3}\{\alpha\alpha(1-ee)^2(\cos U^2+2\sin U^2)\\
&-3\alpha(1-ee)\cos U\cos w(1-ee(\cos w^2-2\sin w^2))\\
&+\cos w^2-\sin w^2-ee(2\cos w^4-12\cos w^2\sin w^2+2\sin w^4)\\
&+e^4(\cos w^6-11\cos w^4\sin w^2+6\cos w^2\sin w^4)\}
\end{aligned}
\]
