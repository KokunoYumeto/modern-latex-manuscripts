\thispagestyle{empty}\begingroup\fontsize{9}{11}\selectfont\raggedright\noindent{\Large\bfseries Translation sources and terminology\par}\medskip\noindent The Latin text is from Carl Friedrich Gauss, \textit{Werke}, Band IV (Göttingen, 1873), issued by the Royal Society of Sciences at Göttingen. The original-language editable transcription and its historical portrait are available in the \href{https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/gauss-collected-works/releases/tag/gauss-original-language-2026-10-24}{original-language edition}. The modern source encoding and typesetting are credited to OpenAI GPT-6 Astra, Ultra; this English translation and its typesetting are AI-produced.\par\medskip\noindent Related English mathematical-argument usage was consulted in James Caddall Morehead and Adam Miller Hiltebeitel’s \textit{General Investigations of Curved Surfaces of 1827 and 1825} (1902), \href{https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36856}{Project Gutenberg 36856, TeX edition}. Related German quantity and mathematical-argument usage was consulted in H. Maser’s translation and edition, \textit{Carl Friedrich Gauss’ Untersuchungen über höhere Arithmetik} (1889), \href{https://archive.org/details/carlfriedrichga00gausgoog}{historical digitized edition}. These works are not parallel translations of the observation theory. The German notice of 26 February 1821 (pp. 100--105) is translated here in full; it also supplied related error and probability terminology for the First Part. No human translation prose or formulas are taken from Morehead--Hiltebeitel or Maser. Historical publication titles remain in the original. Exact versions and URLs are retained in \texttt{CITATIONS.bib}.\par\medskip\noindent For the Second Part, related English expression, substitution and mathematical-argument usage in Morehead and Hiltebeitel and related German quantity, substitution and equation usage in Maser were consulted. These are not parallel translations, attestations of the historical wording or authority for the formulas or proof. No human running prose or modern formulas are adopted. The German notice of 1821 was consulted for the First Part only. The Second Part was presented on 2 February 1823 and published in 1823.\par\medskip\noindent For the Supplement, related English derivative, expression, substitution and mathematical-argument usage in Morehead and Hiltebeitel and related German quantity, substitution and equation usage in Maser were consulted. These are not parallel translations or attestations of the historical wording, and are not authority for the formulas or proof. No human running prose or modern formulas are adopted. The German notice of 1821 was consulted for the First Part only. The Supplement was presented on 16 September 1826 and published in 1828.\par\medskip\noindent The complete German notices of 24 February 1823 and 25 September 1826, and the complete \textit{Bestimmung der Genauigkeit der Beobachtungen} from the March--April 1816 issue of \textit{Zeitschrift für Astronomie}, are translated directly from the historical German reprinted in \textit{Werke}, Band IV. The journal editors B. von Lindenau and J. G. F. Bohnenberger and the handwritten note retain their source credits. The reviewer’s voice in the notices remains distinct from Gauss’s voice in the accuracy paper. The original German texts are supplied separately.\par\medskip\noindent The posthumous \textit{General overview of the method} is translated directly from the German in \textit{Werke}, Band IV; the German original is supplied separately. Related actuarial terminology was consulted in the \href{https://afc.soa.org/}{Society of Actuaries\textquotesingle\ AFC glossary} and the \href{https://service.destatis.de/DE/leibrentenrechner/}{Statistisches Bundesamt\textquotesingle s Leibrentenrechner}; \textit{Versur} was consulted in \href{https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Versur}{Duden online}. These are terminology references, not parallel translations or sources for Gauss\textquotesingle s formulas.\par\medskip\noindent Section II is also translated directly from the German in \textit{Werke}, Band IV; the original is retained separately. Gauss\textquotesingle s quotations and notes retain their source attributions to Brandes, University Councillor O., Meiners, Quetelet, Price and J. A. Kritter, whose complete opinion is dated 19 July 1794. Gauss also attributes mortality tables to Brune and Deparcieux. The same modern actuarial references serve terminology only.\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\par\medskip\noindent The complete Section III, \textit{Commissionsbericht}, and its accompanying \textit{Note zum Commissionsberichte} are translated directly from the German in \textit{Werke}, Band IV. The commission's voice, its quotation from Minister Nebenius's \textit{Über die Herabsetzung der Zinsen der öffentlichen Schulden} (pp. 129 and 21), and its references to the three other widows' funds retain their historical attributions.\par\medskip\noindent Historical spouse-title usage was consulted in Johann Christoph Gottsched's \textit{Grundlegung der deutschen Sprachkunst}, Section 30, in \textit{Ausgewählte Werke}, edited by Joachim Birke and P. M. Mitchell, vol. 8 (Walter de Gruyter, Berlin and New York, 1968--1987), \href{https://www.textgridrep.org/browse/nkxb.0}{TextGrid Digitale Bibliothek, 2012}, \texttt{textgrid:nkxd.0}, revision 0; and in Sabine Krome's \href{https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/fragen/3050}{\textit{Frau Professor oder Frau Professorin? --- Kongruenz bei Anreden und Titeln}}, IDS grammis, last modified 2 May 2024. These are lexical references; widowhood is identified from the fund's pension context. TextGrid credits its annotations and metadata under CC BY 3.0 DE and identifies the underlying historical text as public domain.
\par\medskip\noindent The complete balance report signed 19 October 1851, with its balance table for 1 October 1851, is translated directly from the German in \textit{Werke}, Band IV. The entry \textit{Punktation} in \href{https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Punktation}{Duden online}, Cornelsen Verlag GmbH, was consulted for lexical usage; it is not a source for the fund's legal history or Gauss's calculations. The entry \textit{Kopialien} in \href{https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/Kopialien}{Duden online}, Cornelsen Verlag GmbH, was consulted for the historical term for copying fees; this is lexical usage only. Related English financial usage was consulted in the \href{https://de.pons.com/\%C3\%BCbersetzung/englisch-deutsch/redeemable}{PONS English--German entry \textit{redeemable / non-redeemable}}, PONS Langenscheidt GmbH, Stuttgart. This is a terminology reference, not a source for the fund's particular securities terms or calculations.
\par\medskip\noindent The accompanying historical editorial introduction and its table of 134 former members are translated from the German printed in \textit{Werke}, Band IV. The editor\textquotesingle s statements and attribution of mortality tables to Brune remain distinct from Gauss\textquotesingle s signed report. The German original is retained separately.
\par\medskip\noindent The complete annuity work, including its formal title, all ten tables, explanations, worked examples and Schering's closing remarks, is translated directly from the German in \textit{Werke}, Band IV. The historical editorial explanations remain marked by brackets, and Schering's closing remarks retain his first-person voice, distinct from Gauss's notes. Related English usage for single life and joint life annuities was consulted in the \href{https://afc.soa.org/}{Society of Actuaries\textquotesingle\ AFC Technical Terms}. These are terminology references; the historical text supplies the payment conditions. No modern formula or numerical value is adopted.
\par\medskip\noindent The complete mapping paper by Gauss, with both titles, its preface, Articles 1--14, the author footnote and the marginal remarks, is translated from the German in \textit{Werke}, Band IV. Its historical English translation in the \href{https://archive.org/details/philosophicalmax04lond}{\textit{Philosophical Magazine and Annals of Philosophy}}, New Series, vol. 4 (1828), was consulted in machine-readable OCR on pp. 104--113 and 206--215. Several short English formulations in the surface definition and Articles 4--6, 8--10 and 12--14 follow that translation. Its translator is not identified in the opening. The formulas retain the German source's notation.
\par\medskip\noindent German terminology for complex quantities was compared with Ulrich Görtz's \textit{Lineare Algebra I, WS 2020/21}, version of 21 February 2025, \href{https://math.ug/la1-ws2021/sect0017.html}{section 4.1, Die komplexen Zahlen}.
\par\medskip\noindent The corresponding English translation by James Caddall Morehead and Adam Miller Hiltebeitel was consulted for the complete 1827 paper on curved surfaces, Articles 1--29, in the \href{https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36856}{Project Gutenberg 36856 TeX edition}. The 1873 Latin text governs the formulas and the title matter. The \href{https://www.didaktik.mathematik.uni-wuerzburg.de/projekt/mathei/kugelgeo/einfuehr.html}{University of Würzburg introduction to spherical geometry} supplied related German terminology.
