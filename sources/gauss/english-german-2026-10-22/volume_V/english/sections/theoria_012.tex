Evidently, the case in which $M$ lies on the surface of the body itself is to be counted with the former or the latter case according as the radius $M\Pi$ initially goes away from the solid part of the body or enters it. Next, imagine straight lines drawn from $M$ to the boundary of the small patch $\mathrm{d}\Sigma$. They will form a conical surface (in the broader sense) and will define on the surface of our body at the points $P',P'',P'''$ etc., respectively, the small patches $\mathrm{d}s',\mathrm{d}s'',\mathrm{d}s'''$ etc. Finally, through the points $P',P'',P'''$ etc. draw small portions of spherical surfaces about the center $M$ with radii
\[
MP'=r',\quad MP''=r'',\quad MP'''=r'''\text{ etc.}
\]
and let the small patches that the cone cuts out from them be $\mathrm{d}\sigma',\mathrm{d}\sigma'',\mathrm{d}\sigma'''$ etc. We will regard all these small patches $\mathrm{d}\Sigma,\mathrm{d}s',\mathrm{d}\sigma'$ etc. as positive. With these preliminaries we have
\[
\mathrm{d}\Sigma=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma'}{r'r'}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma''}{r''r''}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma'''}{r'''r'''}\text{ etc.}
\]
The small patch $\mathrm{d}\sigma'$ can be regarded as the projection of the small patch $\mathrm{d}s'$ onto a plane normal to the straight line $P'M$. Hence $\mathrm{d}\sigma'=\pm\mathrm{d}s'.\cos MQ'$, taking the upper or lower sign according as $MQ'$ is acute or obtuse. The former case occurs whenever the straight line drawn from $P'$ to $M$ goes away from the body, that is, whenever $M$ lies outside the body. The latter occurs whenever the straight line $P'M$ enters the body at $P'$, that is, whenever $M$ lies inside the body. Likewise $\mathrm{d}\sigma''=\mp\mathrm{d}s''\cos MQ''$, $\mathrm{d}\sigma'''=\pm\mathrm{d}s'''.\cos MQ'''$ etc. It follows that
\par
I.\quad If $M$ lies outside the body, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{d}s'.\cos MQ'&=+r'r'\mathrm{d}\Sigma\\
\mathrm{d}s''.\cos MQ''&=-r''r''\mathrm{d}\Sigma\\
\mathrm{d}s'''.\cos MQ'''&=+r'''r'''\mathrm{d}\Sigma\\
&\text{etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]
\par
II.\quad If, however, $M$ lies inside the body, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{d}s'.\cos MQ'&=-r'r'\mathrm{d}\Sigma\\
\mathrm{d}s''.\cos MQ''&=+r''r''\mathrm{d}\Sigma\\
\mathrm{d}s'''.\cos MQ'''&=-r'''r'''\mathrm{d}\Sigma\\
&\text{etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]
