\[
\frac{\mathrm{d}s'.\cos MQ'}{r'r'}+\frac{\mathrm{d}s''.\cos MQ''}{r''r''}+\frac{\mathrm{d}s'''.\cos MQ'''}{r'''r'''}+\text{ etc.}=0

\[
\frac{\mathrm{d}s'.\cos MQ'}{r'r'}+\frac{\mathrm{d}s''.\cos MQ''}{r''r''}+\frac{\mathrm{d}s'''.\cos MQ'''}{r'''r'''}+\text{ etc.}=-\mathrm{d}\Sigma
\]
\par
Treating all elements $\mathrm{d}\Sigma$ in the same way and summing, we will evidently have on the left the integral $\int\frac{\mathrm{d}s.\cos MQ}{rr}$ taken over the whole surface of the body. On the right we will have $0$ in the former case and in the latter the entire area of the spherical surface of radius $=1$ with a minus sign, that is, $-4\pi$, where $\pi$ denotes half the circumference of a circle of radius $=1$.

The case in which $M$ lies on the surface of the body itself must be discussed separately. Imagine the plane tangent to the surface of the body at point $M$. It divides the spherical surface into two equal hemispheres, one on the same side of the plane as the solid part of the body at $M$, the other on the opposite side. For all the elements $\mathrm{d}\Sigma$ in the first hemisphere, point $M$ must be regarded as an interior point, and for the remaining elements as an exterior point. It is clear from this that summing all the expressions
\[
\frac{\mathrm{d}s'.\cos MQ'}{r'r'}+\frac{\mathrm{d}s''.\cos MQ''}{r''r''}+\frac{\mathrm{d}s'''.\cos MQ'''}{r'''r'''}+\text{ etc.}
\]
gives only half the area of the sphere with a minus sign. Thus we establish
\par\medskip\Needspace{4\baselineskip}
\begin{center}THEOREM FOUR.\end{center}
\par
\textit{The integral $\int\frac{\mathrm{d}s.\cos MQ}{rr}$ taken over the whole surface of the body is $=0$, $=-2\pi$, or $=-4\pi$, according as $M$ lies outside the body, on its surface, or inside it.}
\par
{\rightskip=0pt plus 1em\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}The same reasoning also shows that, in general, the integral $\int\frac{P\mathrm{d}s.\cos MQ}{rr}$ vanishes in the first case if $P$ denotes any rational function of the quantities $\cos MX$, $\cos MY$, $\cos MZ$.\par}
