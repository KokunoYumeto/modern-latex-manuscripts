\begin{center}GENERAL FORMULAS FOR THE EFFECT OF A LUMINOUS POINT $P$ ON A POINT $p$.\end{center}
I. Let $P$ be separated from $p$ by a surface that is either closed or infinite, and call its open part $s$. Let $\mathrm ds$ be an element of $s$ and $R,r$ its distances from $P,p$, respectively. Let $\rho$ be an unspecified normal to $\mathrm ds$ pointing toward the side on which $p$ lies, $\lambda$ a wavelength, $\dfrac{2\pi i}{\lambda}=\alpha$, and $w$ the angle between $R$ and $r$. The state of vibration at $p$ is then expressed by
\[
\int\frac{\mathrm ds}{Rr}\left(\frac{\partial R}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial r}{\partial\rho}\right)\frac{e^{\alpha(R+r)}}{\sin w}
\]
with the integral taken over all parts of $s$. Here $\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial\rho},-\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial\rho}$ are evidently the sines of the angles that $R$ and $r$ make with $\mathrm ds$.

II. Let the surface region $s$ be bounded by the line $u$. Let $\mathrm du$ be an. element of $u$ and $R,r$ its distances from $P$ and $p$. Let $w$ be the angle between $R$ and $r$, and $v$ the angle between $u$ and the plane through $R,r$.

Then the state of vibration at $p$ is
\[
=\int\frac{\mathrm du.\sin v}{Rr.\sin w}e^{\alpha(R+r)}
\]
More briefly

Let $x,y,z$ be the coordinates of each point in space, with
\[
\begin{array}{lccc}
P\, .\, .\, .&0,&0,&0\\
p\, .\, .\, .&0,&0,&h
\end{array}
\]
The state of vibration at $p$ is then
\[
=\int\frac{x\mathrm dy-y\mathrm dx}{xx+yy}.e^{\alpha(R+r)}:h
\]
with the integral taken along the boundary. Or, more briefly, if $\dfrac yx=\operatorname{tg}\theta$,
\[
=\int e^{\alpha(R+r)}\mathrm d\theta:h
\]
