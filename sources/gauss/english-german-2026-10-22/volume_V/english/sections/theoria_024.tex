\[
[6]\qquad X=\frac{4a\pi BC}{AA}\int\frac{tt\mathrm{d}t}{\BareRoot{((1-(1-\frac{BB}{AA})tt)(1-(1-\frac{CC}{AA})tt)}}
\]
with the integration taken from $t=0$ to $t=1$. The attractions parallel to the coordinate axes $y,z$ evidently follow directly by interchanging $a,A$ with $b,B$ or with $c,C$.

This formula therefore gives the attraction on all points inside the spheroid. Since it is rigorously true however close the attracted point is to the spheroid’s surface, it will also hold for points on the surface. The attraction on exterior points has already been reduced to the attraction on points on this surface, so the problem is now completely solved.

Equation $[6]$ further shows that, for an interior point, the attraction of all similar and similarly situated spheroids is exactly the same. Imagine such a spheroid divided into several layers whose inner and outer surfaces are similar to the surface of the spheroid and similarly situated. Clearly, the individual layers surrounding the attracted point will contribute nothing to the attraction on it, leaving only the attraction of the inner core. The surface of that core passes through the point itself.

There is no need to discuss the integration of formula $[6]$ at length. As is well known, it depends on transcendental functions higher than circular functions and logarithms if $A,B,C$ are all unequal. In that case, then, we turn to series, which converge the faster the less the spheroid differs from the sphere. But if two of the quantities $A,B,C$ are equal, for example $A=B$, in which case the spheroid is generated by revolution about the axis $=2C$, we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
X&=\frac{4\pi aC}{A}\int\frac{tt\mathrm{d}t}{\BareRoot{(1-(1-\frac{CC}{AA})tt)}}\\
&=\frac{2\pi a\cos\varphi}{\sin\varphi^3}(\varphi-\tfrac12\sin2\varphi)
\end{aligned}
\]
on setting $\frac{C}{A}=\cos\varphi$, or $\BareRoot{(1-\frac{CC}{AA})}=\sin\varphi$, if $C<A$, or
\[
X=\frac{2\pi aCC}{CC-AA}-\frac{2\pi aAAC}{(CC-AA)^{\frac32}}\log\frac{C+\BareRoot{(CC-AA)}}{A}
\]
if $C>A$.
