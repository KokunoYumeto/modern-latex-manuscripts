To illustrate the formula, let us apply it to the magnetic elements for
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l|l|l|l}
Göttingen&$\delta^0=18^0\ 38'$&$i^0=67^0\ 56'$&$\psi^0=1{,}357$\\
Milan&$\delta'=18\ 33$&$i'=63\ 49$&$\psi'=1{,}294$\\
Paris&$\delta''=22\ 4$&$i''=67\ 24$&$\psi''=1{,}348$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega^0&=0{,}50980\\
\omega'&=0{,}57094\\
\omega''&=0{,}51804
\end{aligned}
\]
If we take the following geographical positions as the basis,
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l|l|l}
Göttingen&$51^0\ 32'$ latitude&$9^0\ 58'$ longitude from Greenwich\\
Milan&$45\ 28$&$9\ 9$\\
Paris&$48\ 52$&$2\ 21$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
and carry out the calculation as for a spherical surface, we find
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left.\begin{aligned}(01)&=5^0\ 11'\ 31''\\(10)&=184\ 35\ 35\end{aligned}\right\}&\quad P^0P'=6^0\ 5'\ 20''\\[4pt]
\left.\begin{aligned}(12)&=128\ 47\ 31\\(21)&=303\ 48\ 1\end{aligned}\right\}&\quad P'P''=5\ 44\ 6\\[4pt]
\left.\begin{aligned}(20)&=238\ 20\ 20\\(02)&=64\ 10\ 12\end{aligned}\right\}&\quad P^0P''=5\ 32\ 4
\end{aligned}
\]
\par\SourceLeaf{137}
Substituting these values and the values above for $\delta^0,\delta',\delta''$ into our equation, with distances expressed in arcseconds, gives
\[
0=17556\omega^0+2774\omega'-20377\omega''
\]
or
\[
\omega''=0{,}86158\omega^0+0{,}13613\omega'
\]
The observed horizontal intensities in Göttingen and Milan thus give $\omega''=0{,}51696$ for Paris, in almost exact agreement with the observed value $0{,}51804$.

It is also easy to see that, if one is willing to replace the distances $P^0P'$ and so on by their sines, the formula above can be expressed directly in terms of the geographical longitudes and latitudes of the places.
