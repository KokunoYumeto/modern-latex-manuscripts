\Article{27}
To obtain the absolute intensity, the intensity $T$ of the horizontal terrestrial magnetic force must be multiplied by the secant of the inclination. The observations of the distinguished \textsc{Humboldt} showed that the inclination varies in Göttingen and is decreasing in our time. In December $1805$ he found $69^0\ 29'$, and in September $1826$ he found $68^0\ 29'\ 26''$. On June $23$, $1832$, I myself found $68^0\ 22'\ 52''$ with the same inclination instrument that the late \textsc{Mayer} had once used. This seems to indicate a slowing of the decrease. I would, however, put less trust in this observation, both because the instrument was less perfect and because the observation was made in the observatory, where it was inadequately protected from disturbance by masses of iron. This quantity, too, will receive closer attention in future.
