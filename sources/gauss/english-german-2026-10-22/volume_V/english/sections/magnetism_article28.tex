In this treatise we have followed the commonly accepted way of explaining magnetic phenomena, both because it fully accounts for them and because it uses much simpler calculations than the account that attributes magnetism to galvano-electric circuits around the particles of the magnetic body. Our intention was to leave judgment open on this other account, which certainly has several points in its favor. It would have been premature to take a position, since the law of mutual action between the elements of such circuits still seems insufficiently understood. Whatever account of purely magnetic and electromagnetic phenomena is adopted in future, it must in every case lead to the same result as the usual account for the purely magnetic phenomena. What we have developed in this treatise by following that account can change in form alone, while its substance remains the same.
