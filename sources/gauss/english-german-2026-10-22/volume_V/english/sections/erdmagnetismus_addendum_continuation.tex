The observations at Kodiack, Baker’s Bay, Fort Vancouver, Bodega, Monterey, Sta Barbara, San Pedro, San Diego, San Quentin, San Bartolomeo, Magdalena Bay, Mazatlan, San Lucas Bay, San Blas, Acapulco, Cocos Island and Puna Island were made by Captain \textsc{Belcher} in the years 1837–1840 and published by \textsc{Sabine} in a paper presented to the Royal Society in London, \textit{Contributions to terrestical Magnetism}. The declinations at Socorro, Clarion, Martins and Bow Island were also determined, but had yet to be reported in \textsc{Sabine}’s paper. To resolve the remaining uncertainty about the intensity at Otaheite, \textsc{Belcher} returned by way of Otaheite and determined the elements at Point Venus by means of numerous observations. Besides this location, Sitka, San Francisco and Oahu, where \textsc{Belcher} made new observations, were already included in the first comparison table on the basis of other observations.

The elements at Kremsmünster were determined by Professor \textsc{Koller}. The observations at Trevandrum, made by Mr. \textsc{Caldecott}, the director of the magnetic observatory there, are cited in a short pamphlet by \textsc{Sabine}, \textit{Observations made at the magnetic observatories of Toronto, Trevandrum and St. Helena during a remarkable magnetic disturbance on the 25th and 26th Sept.} 1841.
