The historical title matter and the complete 1799 dissertation, Articles 1–24, follow, with Gauss’s ten historical footnotes and all four figures. The complete second proof, Articles 1–20 with both historical footnotes, follows with its title matter. It was presented in 1815 and published in 1816. The complete third proof, presented on 30 January 1816 and published that year, follows with its title matter, introduction, Articles 1–4 and historical footnote. The complete 1828 paper Proof of an Algebraic Theorem follows, on positive and negative roots and changes and continuations of coefficient signs, including missing terms. The complete Contributions to the Theory of Algebraic Equations, read on 16 July 1849 and published in 1850, follow with their title, introduction, Articles 1–20, two historical footnotes and figure. The complete reviews of 23 December 1815, 2 March 1816 and 30 July 1849 follow under Reviews, including the handwritten remarks accompanying the review of 1816. The complete reviews of Coytier of 6 March 1813 and Fourier of 25 February 1833 follow. The complete First Part of the General Investigations of the Infinite Series (1812/1813) follows with its title, introduction, Articles 1–37, both authorial footnotes and the historical numerical table. The complete A New Method for Finding the Values of Integrals by Approximation (presented in 1814, published in 1816) follows with its title, Articles 1–23, both authorial footnotes, coefficient tables and seven numerical examples. The complete reviews of 10 February 1812 and 26 September 1814 follow under Reviews. The complete posthumous Determination of Our Series by a Differential Equation of the Second Order follows with its heading, Articles 38–57 and Schering’s historical editorial remarks. The complete review of 1 July 1815 of Pfaff’s Methodus generalis follows, including the reductions of differential expressions, the three integration problems and the concluding exceptional case. The complete available division Remarks on logarithm tables begins with the complete errata notice of November 1802 and the complete reviews of Prasse’s tables of 25 May 1811 and 19 December 1814. These are followed by the complete available explanatory text of 1812 on logarithms of sums and differences, with its three examples, and the complete review of Pasquich’s tables of 4 October 1817. The complete reviews of Matthiessen (30 January 1819), Babbage (19 January 1828) and Hassler (31 March 1831), and Gauss’s letter to Schumacher of 25 November 1822, follow in their historical order. The complete application to quadratic equations from Vega and Hülsse’s tables (1840) follows, with its rules and example. The division closes with the complete remarks of 1851 on Vega’s Thesaurus Logarithmorum, including Gauss’s footnote. The posthumous Theory of Interpolation Treated by a New Method follows with its title, Articles 1–41, all three historical footnotes and Schering’s subsequent editorial remarks. The work of 1818 on the attraction of a planetary orbit follows with its formal and repeated titles, Articles 1–19 and both historical footnotes.
