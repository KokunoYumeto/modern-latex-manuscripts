Secondly, if the function $X$ is of the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\beta'\sin x+\beta''\sin 2x+\beta'''\sin 3x+\mathrm{etc.}+\beta^n\sin nx
\end{aligned}
\]
and thus the number of unknown coefficients is $=n$, the number of given values,
\SourceLeaf{332}
after subtracting the superfluous ones, must likewise be $=n$, so that they suffice for a complete determination of the coefficients. Now, since in this case those values of the function $X$ that correspond to a value of $x$ greater than $180^0$, as well as to the values $0$ and $180^0$, must be rejected as superfluous, we shall have, for the four cases distinguished above:
\[
\begin{array}{rllcl}
1.&\text{when }\mu\text{ is even},&a=0,&\text{there will be}&n=\frac12\mu-1\\
2.&\text{when }\mu\text{ is even},&a=\frac{180^0}{\mu},&\text{there will be}&n=\frac12\mu\\
3.&\text{when }\mu\text{ is odd},&a=0.&\text{there will be}&n=\frac12\mu-\frac12\\
4.&\text{when }\mu\text{ is odd},&a=\frac{180^0}{\mu},&\text{there will be}&n=\frac12\mu-\frac12
\end{array}
\]
\Needspace{31\baselineskip}
Now, in precisely the same way as in Article 37, in the function
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\gamma+\gamma'\cos x+\gamma''\cos 2x+\text{etc.}+\gamma^m\cos mx\\[6pt]
&{}+\delta'\sin x+\delta''\sin 2x+\text{etc.}+\delta^m\sin mx
\end{aligned}
\]
obtained according to Articles 20, 22, which satisfies all the $\mu$ values and in which $m$ is either $=\frac12\mu-\frac12$ or $=\frac12\mu$, the coefficients $\gamma$, $\gamma'$, $\gamma''$ etc. will vanish of themselves. For in the series $A,B,C,D$ etc. the last terms, taken in reverse order, become in the present case either $=-B,-C,-D$ etc. or $=-A,-B,-C$ etc., according as $a=0$ or $=\frac{180^0}{\mu}$, and moreover in the former case $A=0$; hence evidently
\[
\begin{aligned}
&A\cos\lambda a+B\cos\lambda b+C\cos\lambda c+D\cos\lambda d+\text{etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]
for any value of $x$ will be $=0$. \Needspace{42\baselineskip}
Hence the function $X'=$
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\delta'\sin x+\delta''\sin 2x+\delta'''\sin 3x+\text{etc.}+\delta^m\sin mx
\end{aligned}
\]
in which the coefficients $\delta'$, $\delta''$, $\delta'''$ etc. are determined by the equations
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\delta'=\frac2\mu(A\sin a+B\sin b+C\sin c+D\sin d+\text{etc.})\\[6pt]
&\delta''=\frac2\mu(A\sin 2a+B\sin 2b+C\sin 2c+D\sin 2d+\text{etc.})\\[6pt]
&\delta'''=\frac2\mu(A\sin 3a+B\sin 3b+C\sin 3c+D\sin 3d+\text{etc.})
\end{aligned}
\]
etc., but the last, when $\mu$ is even, and thus $m=\frac12\mu$, by this one
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\delta^m=\frac1\mu(A\sin ma+B\sin mb+C\sin mc+D\sin md+\text{etc.})
\end{aligned}
\]
will necessarily be identical with $X$, which is easily proved in the same way as in Article 37. Moreover, the function $X'$ in the first case, where $a=0$ and $\mu$ is even, actually rises only to the or\SourceLeaf{333}%
der $\frac12\mu-1$, since
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\delta^m=\frac1\mu(A\sin 0+B\sin 180^0+C\sin 360^0+D\sin 540^0+\text{etc.})\\[6pt]
&=0
\end{aligned}
\]
\Needspace{43\baselineskip}
\InterpolationArticle{40}
If the function $X$ is not of the $n^{\mathrm{th}}$ order, as we assumed, but continues further, the following equations will show how the difference between $X'$ and $X$ depends on the following coefficients (see Articles 21, 23):
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\delta'=\beta'\mp\beta^{\mu-1}\pm\beta^{\mu+1}-\beta^{2\mu-1}+\beta^{2\mu+1}\mp\text{etc.}\\[6pt]
&\delta''=\beta''\mp\beta^{\mu-2}\pm\beta^{\mu+2}-\beta^{2\mu-2}+\beta^{2\mu+2}\mp\text{etc.}\\[6pt]
&\delta'''=\beta'''\mp\beta^{\mu-3}\pm\beta^{\mu+3}-\beta^{2\mu-3}+\beta^{2\mu+3}\mp\text{etc.}
\end{aligned}
\]
and so on as far as the last coefficient $\delta^m$ or the next to last $\delta^{m-1}$, according as $\mu$ is odd or even; the upper signs apply here when $a=0$, the lower when $a=\frac{180^0}{\mu}$: finally, for the last coefficient there is, in case (1), $\delta^m=0$, but in case (2)
$\delta^m=\beta^m-\beta^{3m}+\beta^{5m}-\beta^{7m}+\text{etc.}$
All periodic functions that satisfy the $\mu$ proposed values, and among which $X'$ is the simplest, will be contained in the form $X'+Y\sin(\frac12\mu x-\frac12\mu a)$, or more generally in the form $X''+Y\sin(\frac12\mu x-\frac12\mu a)$, where $X''$ denotes any such function; this formula reduces, for cases 1 and 3, to $X''+Y\sin\frac12\mu x$, but for cases 2 and 4 to $X''+Y\cos\frac12\mu x$. However, $Y$, since we consider no functions other than those similar to $X$, i.e. composed only of sines, must necessarily be:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{in case 1 of the form}&g+g'\cos x+g''\cos 2x+\text{etc.}\\
\text{in case 2 of the form}&g\sin x+g'\sin 2x+g''\sin 3x+\text{etc.}\\
\text{in case 3 of the form}&g\cos\frac12x+g'\cos\frac32x+g''\cos\frac52x+\text{etc.}\\
\text{in case 4 of the form}&g\sin\frac12x+g'\sin\frac32x+g''\sin\frac52x+\text{etc.}
\end{array}
\]
\Needspace{23\baselineskip}
Here it will be possible to determine the function $Y$ so that a function results reduced to the order $\frac12\mu-1$, $\frac12\mu$, $\frac12\mu-\frac12$, $\frac12\mu-\frac12$, which will necessarily be identical with $X'$. For reducing the function $X''$ to $X'$ the following general rule holds:

Let any term in $X''$ of the form $L\sin\lambda x=L\sin(k\mu\pm\lambda')x$ be transformed either into $\pm L\sin\lambda'x$ (whenever $a=0$ or $k$ is even), or into $\mp L\sin\lambda'x$ (whenever neither $a=0$ nor $k$ is even, i.e. whenever both $a=\frac{180^0}{\mu}$ and $k$ is odd): finally, whenever in the
\SourceLeaf{334}
first case, i.e. for \(a=0\) and an even value of \(\mu\), \(\lambda'=\frac12\mu\) results, let the term \(L\sin\lambda x\) be destroyed entirely.
