qq-QQ&=-A\,.\,\surd\frac{q}{Q}, &5QQ-qq&=B\surd\frac{Q}{q}\\[8pt]
\GaussCircleArrow\quad rr-RR&=A\,.\,\surd\frac{r}{R},
&5RR-rr&=-B\surd\frac{R}{r}
\end{aligned}
\]

For trisection, retaining the previous meanings of \(p,P\) etc.
and setting \(P(x^{\frac13},1)=P^0\), the following also holds
\[
\begin{gathered}
\left(\frac{3PP-P^0P^0}{2}\right)^2=p^4-4\left(\frac{pqr}{2}\right)^{\frac43}\\[8pt]
\left(\frac{3QQ-Q^0Q^0}{2}\right)^2=q^4+4\left(\frac{pqr}{2}\right)^{\frac43}
\end{gathered}
\]
In general, if \(P(x,1),Q(x,1)\) are given and \(P(x^n,1),Q(x^n,1)\) are sought,
where \(n\) is odd, the quantities associated with \(P(x^n,1)\) are
\(\pm\surd\frac1n\, .\,P(x^{\frac1n},1)\)
or \(\pm i\surd\frac1n\, .\,P(x^{\frac1n},1)\), according as \(n\) has the form
\(4k+1\) or \(4k-1\).
The equation is easily found by expanding the sum of the even powers
of the \(n+1\) roots.

\[
\begin{gathered}P(x^5,y^5)=\frac{R}{r^5}\, .\,P(x,y)^5
\\[8pt]\quad{}+A\, .\,P(x,y)^3Q(x,y)^2
+\frac{5PQR}{pqr^5}\, .\,P(x,y)Q(x,y)^4\end{gathered}
\]

\SourceLeaf{486}
is set, then also
\[
\begin{gathered}
Q(x^5,y^5)
=\frac{5PQR}{pqr^5}\, .\,P(x,y)^4\, .\,Q(x,y)\\[8pt]
\quad+A\, .\,P(x,y)^2\, .\,Q(x,y)^3
+\frac{R}{r^5}Q(x,y)^5
\end{gathered}
\]

\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
Setting \(y=1\) here gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
Ap^3q^3=Pq-\frac{R}{r^5}\, .\,p^5q
-\frac{5PQR}{r^5}\, .\,q^4\\[8pt]
=Qp-\frac{R}{r^5}\, .\,q^5p
-\frac{5PQR}{r^5}\, .\,p^4
\end{gathered}
\]
from which the earlier equation [Art.\ 9] follows
\[
0=pQr-Pqr+pqR-5PQR
\]
hence
\[
A=\frac{r(p^3P-q^3Q)-(p^4+q^4)R}{ppqqr^5}
\]
