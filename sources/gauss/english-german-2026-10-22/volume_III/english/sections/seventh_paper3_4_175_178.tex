\SeventhArticle{3}
Now our integral $\int y\,\mathrm{d}x$ becomes $\Delta\int y\,\mathrm{d}t$, to be taken from $t=0$ to $t=1$. In its place, according to what we have just remarked, we shall use $\Delta\int Y\,\mathrm{d}t$. Thus, if $T^{(\mu)}$ is expanded as
\[
\alpha t^n+\beta t^{n-1}+\gamma t^{n-2}+\delta t^{n-3}+\text{etc.}
\]
one obtains for $\int T^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}t$, from $t=0$ to $t=1$,
\SourceLeaf{176}
\[
=\frac{\alpha}{n+1}+\frac{\beta}{n}+\frac{\gamma}{n-1}+\frac{\delta}{n-2}+\text{etc.}
\]
Setting this quantity $=M^{(\mu)}R^{(\mu)}$ gives for the required integral
\[
=\Delta(AR+A'R'+A''R''+A'''R'''+\text{etc.}+A^{(n)}R^{(n)})
\]
As an example, we shall give the calculation of the coefficient $R''$ for $n=5$. Here one obtains
\[
\begin{aligned}
T''&=5^5t^5-13\, .\,5^4t^4+59\, .\,5^3t^3-107\, .\,5^2tt+60\, .\,5\, .\,t\\[5pt]
M''&=2\times1\times(-1)\times(-2)\times(-3)=-12
\end{aligned}
\]
Hence $-12R''=\allowbreak \frac{3125}{6}-1625+\allowbreak \frac{7375}{4}-\frac{2675}{3}+\allowbreak 150=\allowbreak -\frac{25}{12}$, and therefore $R''=\frac{25}{144}$.

The calculation becomes somewhat shorter on setting $2t-1=u$. Then one obtains
\[
T^{(\mu)}=\frac{\begin{gathered}(nu+n)(nu+n-2)(nu+n-4)\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp\\(nu-n+4)(nu-n+2)(nu-n)\end{gathered}}{2^n(nu+n-2\mu)}
\]
Let us set
\[
\frac{\begin{gathered}(nnuu-nn)\, .\,(nnuu-(n-2)^2)\, .\,(nnuu-(n-4)^2)\\\, .\,(nnuu-(n-6)^2)\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp\end{gathered}}{nnuu-(n-2\mu)^2}=U^{(\mu)}
\]
where the numerator must end in $\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp(nnuu-9)(nnuu-1)$ if $n$ is odd, or in $\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp(nnuu-4)nu$ if $n$ is even; then
\[
T^{(\mu)}=\frac{(nu-n+2\mu)U^{(\mu)}}{2^n}
\]
Now the integral $\int T^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}t$, taken from $t=0$ to $t=1$, is equal to the integral
\[
\int\tfrac12 T^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}u=\int\frac{nuU^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}u}{2^{n+1}}+\int\frac{(2\mu-n)U^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}u}{2^{n+1}}
\]
from $u=-1$ to $u=+1$.

Thus, if
\[
U^{(\mu)}=\alpha u^{n-1}+\beta u^{n-3}+\gamma u^{n-5}+\delta u^{n-7}+\text{etc.}
\]
\begingroup\rightskip=0pt plus1fil\relax\noindent (for it is immediately evident that the powers $u^{n-2},\allowbreak u^{n-4},\allowbreak u^{n-6}$, etc., are absent), the part $\int\dfrac{nuU^{(n)}\,\mathrm{d}u}{2^{n+1}}$ of the integral vanishes for an odd value of $n$, whereas the other part $\int\dfrac{(2\mu-n)U^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}u}{2^{n+1}}$ vanishes for an even value. Hence the integral $\int T^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}t$ becomes, for even $n$,\par\endgroup
\[
=\frac{n}{2^n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{n+1}+\frac{\beta}{n-1}+\frac{\gamma}{n-3}+\frac{\delta}{n-5}+\text{etc.}\right)
\]
\SourceLeaf{177}
but for odd $n$,
\[
=\frac{2\mu-n}{2^n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{n}+\frac{\beta}{n-2}+\frac{\gamma}{n-4}+\frac{\delta}{n-6}+\text{etc.}\right)
\]
\Needspace{8\baselineskip}In our example, the result is
\[
U''=(25uu-25)(25uu-9)=625u^4-850uu+225,\quad\text{therefore}
\]
\[
-12R''=-\tfrac1{32}\left(125-\tfrac{850}{3}+225\right)=-\tfrac{25}{12}\quad\text{as above.}
\]
It should be observed that $U^{(n-\mu)}=U^{(\mu)}$, and hence $\int T^{(n-\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}t=\pm\int T^{(\mu)}\,\mathrm{d}t$. The upper sign applies when $n$ is even, and the lower when it is odd. Thus, since it is easy to see that likewise $M^{(n-\mu)}=\pm M^{(\mu)}$, one always has $R^{(n-\mu)}=R^{(\mu)}$; that is, among the coefficients $R,R',R''\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp R^{(n)}$, the last equals the first, the next to last the second, and so on.
