\AttractionArticle{18}
By the method explained here, even \textit{indefinite} integrals (starting at the value of the variable $=0$) may be given with the greatest elegance. Namely, if $T''$ is assumed to be determined by $m',\ n',\ T'$ in the same way as $T'$ by $m.\ n,\ T$, and again $T'''$ by $m'',\ n'',\ T''$, and so on, then for any determinate value of $T$ too, the values of the terms of the sequence $T,\ T',\ T'',\ T'''$ etc. will converge very rapidly to a limit $\Theta$, and it will be the case that
\[
\int\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\surd(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)}=\frac{\Theta}{\mu}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}&\int\frac{(\cos T^2-\sin T^2)\mathrm{d}T}{\surd(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)}\\[7pt]&=-\frac{\nu\Theta}{\mu}\\[7pt]&\quad+\frac{\begin{gathered}\lambda'\cos T\sin T'+2\lambda''\cos T'\sin T''\\+4\lambda'''\cos T'\sin T'''+\mathrm{etc.}\end{gathered}}{\lambda\lambda}\end{aligned}
\]
\par But it may suffice to have indicated these matters here in passing, since they are not necessary for our purpose.

\Needspace{40\baselineskip}
\AttractionArticle{19}
If we now set $m=\surd(G+G')$, $n=\surd(G+G'')$, the values of the quantities $P,\ Q$ will readily be reduced to the transcendental quantities $\mu,\ \nu$. For since $P,\ Q$ are the values of the integrals
\SourceLeaf{364}
\[
\begin{gathered}\int\frac{\cos T^2\mathrm{d}T}{2\pi(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)^{\frac32}},\\[7pt]\int\frac{\sin T^2\mathrm{d}T}{2\pi(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)^{\frac32}}\end{gathered}
\]
taken from $T=0$ to $T=360^{0}$, it is immediately evident, first, that
\[
mmP+nnQ=\frac{1}{\mu}\quad .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\quad[24]
\]
\Needspace{36\baselineskip}
\par Furthermore,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac{(\cos T^2-\sin T^2)\mathrm{d}T}{2\pi\surd(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)}\\[7pt]&\quad+\frac{(mm\cos T^2-nn\sin T^2)\mathrm{d}T}{2\pi(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)^{\frac32}}\\[6pt]
&\qquad=\frac{(mm\cos T^4-nn\sin T^4)\mathrm{d}T}{\pi(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)^{\frac32}}\\[6pt]
&\qquad=\mathrm{d}.\frac{\cos T\sin T}{\pi\surd(mm\cos T^2+nn\sin T^2)}
\end{aligned}
\]
\par Integrating this equation from $T=0$ to $T=360^{0}$ gives
\[
-\frac{\nu}{\mu}+mmP-nnQ=0\quad .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\, .\quad[25]
\]
\par From the combination of equations 24, 25 we finally conclude that
\[
P=\frac{1+\nu}{2mm\mu},\qquad Q=\frac{1-\nu}{2nn\mu}
\]
