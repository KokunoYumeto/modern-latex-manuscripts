\noindent{\Large\bfseries Translation sources and terminology\par}\medskip
Historical text: Carl Friedrich Gauss, \emph{Werke}, Volume III, Göttingen, 1866. These English and German texts are AI-translated from the original-language edition. The following human translations and writings are consulted for the stated terminology and usage; their running text is not reproduced. Gauss’s original mathematical formulations remain controlling.
\par\medskip\textbf{Paul Taylor; Bernard Leak.} Taylor’s English translation of Gauss’s second proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra, with corrections by Leak, 1983. The corresponding passages of the 1815 second proof were consulted for English polynomial, divisor, degree, symmetric-function and proof terminology. Related terminology was also consulted for the 1799 dissertation, the 1828 sign-rule paper, the 1849 introduction and Articles 1 and 10, the reviews of 1815 and 1849, the reviews of Coytier and Fourier, Articles 7–17 of the series work of 1812/1813, and the English title and Articles 1–2, 11–12 and 19–21 of the work on approximate integral values of 1814/1816 and the reviews of 10 February 1812 and 26 September 1814; this is not a parallel translation of those works. No running text from this human translation is adopted. Its later use of complex quantities does not replace Gauss’s definition of imaginary quantities here. The consulted online edition identifies the translation as December 1983, its LaTeX version as August 2003, and the underlying source as last modified on 8 June 2007. Its acknowledgements credit Mark Wainwright for returning Taylor’s notes and Nikita Danilov for typo corrections.\par
\url{https://www.paultaylor.eu/misc/gauss.html}
\par\medskip\textbf{H. Maser.} German translator and editor, \emph{Carl Friedrich Gauss’ Untersuchungen über höhere Arithmetik}, Berlin: Julius Springer, 1889. Related German terminology for algebraic functions, roots, divisibility, quotients, degrees, powers, series, infinite products, continued fractions, logarithms, tables, limits, diminishing quantities, proof and existence usage; not a parallel translation of these works.\par
\url{https://archive.org/details/carlfriedrichga00gausgoog}
\par\medskip\textbf{James Caddall Morehead and Adam Miller Hiltebeitel.} Translators of Gauss’s \emph{General Investigations of Curved Surfaces of 1827 and 1825}, Princeton University Library, 1902; Project Gutenberg 36856, TeX edition. Related English geometrical, trigonometric, series, coordinate, continuity, integration, signed-area and infinitely small quantity terminology, and differential-equation and general-solution usage for Articles 38–57 of the posthumous Determination of Our Series; not a parallel translation of these works.\par
 Related English mathematical usage was also consulted for Schering’s historical editorial remarks. Related usage for partial and ordinary differential equations, general integration, arbitrary functions, differentiation and combining equations was also consulted for the review of Pfaff of 1 July 1815. Related English angle, calculation and decimal-place usage was consulted for the logarithm-table notices of 1802, 1811 and 1814.
\url{https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36856}

\noindent{\Large\bfseries Translation sources and terminology (continued)\par}\medskip

\par\medskip\textbf{Logarithm calculations.} Related English angle, calculation and decimal-place usage in Morehead and Hiltebeitel’s translation, and related German logarithm, table and calculation usage in Maser’s translation, were consulted for the explanatory text of 1812 and the review of Pasquich of 1817.

\par\medskip\textbf{Logarithm tables and correspondence.} Related English calculation and decimal-place usage in Morehead and Hiltebeitel’s translation, and related German logarithm, table and calculation usage in Maser’s translation, were consulted for the reviews of Matthiessen (1819), Babbage (1828) and Hassler (1831) and the letter to Schumacher (1822).

\par\medskip\textbf{Quadratic equations.} Related English calculation and decimal-place usage in Morehead and Hiltebeitel’s translation, and related German logarithm, table and calculation usage in Maser’s translation, were consulted for the application of 1840 and the remarks on Vega of 1851.
\par\medskip\textbf{Interpolation.} Related English substitution, algebraic-expression, mathematical argument, angle-calculation and decimal-place usage in Morehead and Hiltebeitel’s translation, and related German indeterminate, rational integral function, coefficient, root, product, logarithm, table and calculation usage in Maser’s translation, were consulted for Articles 1–41, all three historical footnotes and Schering’s subsequent editorial remarks in the posthumous Theory of Interpolation. These are not parallel translations of this paper.
\par\medskip\textbf{Attraction of a planetary orbit.} Related English mathematical formulation, differential-expression, algebraic-argument, expression and substitution usage in Morehead and Hiltebeitel’s translation, and related German usage for denoting mathematical quantities, indeterminates, coefficients, substitution and products in Maser’s translation, were consulted for the titles, Articles 1–19 and both historical footnotes of the work of 1818 and the review of 9 February 1818. These are not parallel translations of this work.
\par\medskip\textbf{Arithmetic–geometric mean.} Related English algebraic argument and designation in Morehead and Hiltebeitel’s translation, and related German usage for mathematical quantities, roots and algebraic argument in Maser’s translation, were consulted for Articles 1–10 and all four historical footnotes of the posthumous work, the surviving opening of Article 11, and the title, introduction and Articles 12–26 of the historical editorial continuation, and the heading of the following numerical table. These are not parallel translations of this work.
\par\medskip\textbf{Ulrich Görtz.} \emph{Algebra}, Wintersemester 2021/22, section 3.1.1, Examples 3.1–3.2. Related contemporary German usage for polynomials, indeterminates, sums, numerators and denominators, and mathematical argument. This is a terminology reference, not a parallel translation of these proofs. No running text or modern ring-theoretic argument is adopted.\par
\url{https://math.ug/a-ws2122/sect0006.html}

Roy.} Authors of Chapter 5 of the \emph{NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions}, section 5.2(iii), version 1.2.8, 15 September 2026; online text and TeX formula encodings. Related English factorial-function terminology for the 1812/1813 work and its review of 10 February 1812; not a parallel translation of either. No running prose, modern notation or formulas are adopted. The chapter acknowledges P. J. Davis’s Chapter 6 in Abramowitz and Stegun (1964) as part of its basis.\par
\url{https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.2.iii}
\par\medskip\textbf{Historical calculations.} In his note to Article 31, Gauss credits Fridericus Bernhardus Gothofredus Nicolai for repeating and extending the calculation reported there, and for the second part of his numerical table. Mascheroni\textquoteright s earlier calculation is cited in the same historical note. Gauss cites Bessel’s calculation of the integral in Article 23 of A New Method for Finding the Values of Integrals by Approximation.
