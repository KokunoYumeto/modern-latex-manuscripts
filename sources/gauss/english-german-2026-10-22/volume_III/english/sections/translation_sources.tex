\noindent{\Large\bfseries Translation sources and terminology\par}\medskip
Historical text: Carl Friedrich Gauss, \emph{Werke}, Volume III, Göttingen, 1866. These English and German texts are AI-translated from the original-language edition. The following human translations and writings are consulted for the stated terminology and usage; their running text is not reproduced. Gauss’s original mathematical formulations remain controlling.
\par\medskip\textbf{Paul Taylor; Bernard Leak.} Taylor’s English translation of Gauss’s second proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra, with corrections by Leak, 1983. The corresponding passages of the 1815 second proof were consulted for English polynomial, divisor, degree, symmetric-function and proof terminology. Related terminology was also consulted for the 1799 dissertation and the 1828 sign-rule paper; this is not a parallel translation of either work. No running text from this human translation is adopted. Its later use of complex quantities does not replace Gauss’s definition of imaginary quantities here. The consulted online edition identifies the translation as December 1983, its LaTeX version as August 2003, and the underlying source as last modified on 8 June 2007. Its acknowledgements credit Mark Wainwright for returning Taylor’s notes and Nikita Danilov for typo corrections.\par
\url{https://www.paultaylor.eu/misc/gauss.html}
\par\medskip\textbf{H. Maser.} German translator and editor, \emph{Carl Friedrich Gauss’ Untersuchungen über höhere Arithmetik}, Berlin: Julius Springer, 1889. Related German terminology for algebraic functions, roots, divisibility, quotients and degrees; not a parallel translation of these works.\par
\url{https://archive.org/details/carlfriedrichga00gausgoog}
\par\medskip\textbf{James Caddall Morehead and Adam Miller Hiltebeitel.} Translators of Gauss’s \emph{General Investigations of Curved Surfaces of 1827 and 1825}, Princeton University Library, 1902; Project Gutenberg 36856, TeX edition. Related English geometrical, coordinate, continuity, integration, signed-area and infinitely small quantity terminology; not a parallel translation of these algebraic proofs.\par
\url{https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36856}
\par\medskip\textbf{Ulrich Görtz.} \emph{Algebra}, Wintersemester 2021/22, section 3.1.1, Examples 3.1–3.2. Related contemporary German usage for polynomials, indeterminates, sums, numerators and denominators, and mathematical argument. This is a terminology reference, not a parallel translation of these proofs. No running text or modern ring-theoretic argument is adopted.\par
\url{https://math.ug/a-ws2122/sect0006.html}
