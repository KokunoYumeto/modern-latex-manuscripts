\Needspace{19\baselineskip}
Let
\[
\begin{gathered}
T={}1+\frac{a^n-1}{a-1}.t
+\frac{a^n-1.a^n-a}{a-1.aa-1}.tt
\\[8pt]\quad{}+\frac{a^n-1.a^n-a.a^n-aa}{a-1.aa-1.a^3-1}.t^3\\[8pt]
+\frac{a^n-1.a^n-a.a^n-aa.a^n-a^3}
{a-1.aa-1.a^3-1.a^4-1}.t^4+\text{etc.}
\end{gathered}
\]
where we leave $n,a,t$ entirely unspecified. Whenever $n$ is a nonnegative
integer, the series obviously terminates and consists of $n+1$ terms; moreover,
as we have shown in the memoir \textit{Summatio quarundam serierum singularium}, all the coefficients are polynomial functions of $a$. But if $n$ is fractional or negative, neither property holds.


\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
Multiplying $T$ by $1+a^n t$ gives
\[
\begin{gathered}T.(1+a^n t)=1+\frac{a^{n+1}-1}{a-1}.t
+\frac{a^{n+1}-1.a^{n+1}-a}{a-1.aa-1}.tt
\\[8pt]\quad{}+\frac{a^{n+1}-1.a^{n+1}-a.a^{n+1}-aa}{a-1.aa-1.a^3-1}.t^3
+\text{etc.}\end{gathered}
\]


\Needspace{20\baselineskip}
Thus, if in $T$ one regards the element $n$ as variable and uses
the function symbol $\Theta$ so that
\[
T=\Theta n
\]
one will have
\[
\Theta(n+1)=(1+a^n t)\Theta n
\]

\Needspace{26\baselineskip}
From this follows the 1st \textsc{Theorem.}

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{center}
\textit{If $n$ denotes a positive integer, then}
\end{center}
\[
\begin{gathered}
1+\frac{a^n-1}{a-1}\,t
 +\frac{a^n-1.a^n-a}{a-1.aa-1}\,tt
 \\[8pt]\quad{}+\frac{a^n-1.a^n-a.a^n-aa}{a-1.aa-1.a^3-1}\,t^3
 +\text{etc.}\ .\ .
\\[8pt]
\qquad=(1+t)(1+at)(1+aat)(1+a^3t)\mathinner{.\,.\,.}(1+a^{n-1}t)
\end{gathered}
\]
