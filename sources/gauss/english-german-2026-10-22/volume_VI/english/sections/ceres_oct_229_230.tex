CERES FERDINANDEA.\par
\medskip\hrule\medskip
Monatliche Correspondenz. Vol. VI. pp.\ 382—389. October 1802.\par
\medskip\hrule
\end{center}\medskip
—\quad —\quad —

Several astronomers have already calculated Jupiter’s perturbations of the longitude and distance of \textit{Ceres}, but such intricate and difficult calculations cannot be repeated often enough. Prof.\ \textsc{Wurm} wrote to us only recently on this subject, when he sent some corrections and explanatory additions to the formulas for the
\newcommand{\JupiterSymbol}{\text{\mdseries\AstroSymbols ♃}}
\SourceLeaf{230}%
perturbation by Mars, which we will publish in forthcoming issues: I generally believe that anyone who has never come across thorns along this path of perturbation calculations has surely never set foot on it. In work of this kind one can hardly avoid getting caught among thorns now and then, or even making a small \textit{quid pro quo} before one has found one’s bearings.

Dr.\ \textsc{Gauss} has therefore repeated these perturbation calculations using his elements VII of the orbit of Ceres and found small differences here and there. He first worked out all the analytical formulas he uses in this calculation himself. Some of them turned out to differ slightly in form from \textsc{La Place}’s.
