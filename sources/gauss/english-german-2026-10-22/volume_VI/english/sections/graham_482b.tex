\begin{flushright}Göttingen, 4 June 1848.\end{flushright}

I can add only one more observation of \textsc{Graham}’s planet to the two I sent you on 22 May. This one, too, was made with the meridian circle:

\begin{center}
1848 May 24.\quad $10^{\rm u}\ 18^{\rm m}\ 39^{\rm s}0$ mean time\\
R.A. $217^\circ\ 15'\ 58''8$\qquad Decl. $11^\circ\ 29'\ 29''65$ south.
\end{center}

If anything, the planet was even harder to observe than on the 11th. But the time interval had now nearly doubled, so immediately afterward I asked Professor \textsc{Goldschmidt} to calculate the orbit again. The following elements are the result:

\begin{center}
\textsc{Graham}’s planet.\\[3pt]

These elements were based on \textsc{Graham}’s observation of 26 April, the Berlin observation of 11 May and my own of 24 May. Professor \textsc{Goldschmidt} has compared these elements with all the meridian observations that have come to my attention so far. I enclose this comparison on a separate sheet. My observations with the meridian circle have come to a halt because the instrument has now been taken down so it can be altered.
