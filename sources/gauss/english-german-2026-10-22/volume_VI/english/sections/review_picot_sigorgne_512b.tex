\begin{center}\small Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen. No. 164. pp. 1639..1640. 13 October 1808.\\[3pt]\rule{.72\linewidth}{.3pt}\end{center}

Geneva. In the May 1808 issue of the Bibliothèque Britannique, under the heading \emph{Sur les vingt-une dernières Comètes et les nouvelles planètes; par le Prof.} \textsc{P. Picot}, we find an excerpt from a letter by \textsc{Olbers} giving some information (well enough known here) about the latest comet and the four new planets. The compilation of the parabolic elements for the 21 most recent comets to have appeared since 1790 is valuable. It uses the most reliable determinations, \emph{nine} of which are by \textsc{Olbers} himself. This excellent astronomer also gives us reason to hope for a paper of his own on his well-known hypothesis about the origin of the asteroids. --- Also, \emph{Demonstration de la création immédiate de la terre en état solide, et de l'impossibilité des causes physiques pour la formation de sa figure, par Mr. l'Abbé} \textsc{Sigorgne}. Sigorgne argues that the Earth could have been neither liquid nor soft when it acquired its rotation, since both the degree of latitude measured by \textsc{Lacaille} at the Cape of Good Hope and the various degrees of latitude measured in the Northern Hemisphere had proved that the Earth was irregular in shape. It would already have had to be a solid body at the time and hence would have had to be flattened at the poles beforehand. The reviewer considers the premises unsettled and the inference unsound. \textsc{Lacaille}’s degree of latitude may need a fresh correction just as much as the one in Lapland did. Even the one measured in Peru is still open to doubt. The Earth is undoubtedly irregular to some degree, but one can by no means conclude from this that it could not have formed through physical causes. The Earth could have become a perfectly regular body only if all parts of the mass from which it formed had been perfectly fluid and had remained so until they had arranged themselves according to the laws of hydrostatics.
