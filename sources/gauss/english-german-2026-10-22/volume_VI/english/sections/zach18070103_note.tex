\begin{center}\textbf{Sources for the 3 January 1807 Letter}\end{center}
\noindent This AI translation follows Carl Friedrich Gauss’s letter of 3 January 1807 to Franz Xaver von Zach in \href{https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/PPN236007467}{Gauss’s \textit{Werke}, vol.~VI} (Göttingen, 1874), pp.~280--281. It appeared as article VIII in the February 1807 \textit{Monatliche Correspondenz}, vol.~XV, \href{https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sKAAAAAAMAAJ/page/n157/mode/1up}{pp.~152}--\href{https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sKAAAAAAMAAJ/page/n162/mode/1up}{157}. Von Zach’s note on the delayed letter appears on \href{https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sKAAAAAAMAAJ/page/n157/mode/1up}{p.~152}. The journal ephemeris starts on \href{https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sKAAAAAAMAAJ/page/n159/mode/1up}{p.~154}, where a separate note by von Zach says Harding’s charts for Ceres, Pallas and Juno were already with the engraver.
\par\medskip
The two references to Pasquich’s observations lead to \href{https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10538605?page=200}{vol.~XIII, p.~192} and \href{https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/en/view/bsb10538606?page=102}{vol.~XIV, p.~92}. In the following article, \href{https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sKAAAAAAMAAJ/page/n163/mode/1up}{vol.~XV, pp.~158}--\href{https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sKAAAAAAMAAJ/page/n164/mode/1up}{159}, Oriani transmits the Milan observations and credits Carlini with making and calculating them. The collected edition prints \emph{Delimalen} in the bracketed description of the ephemeris. This refers to decimal places.
\par\medskip
The February issue is from the New York Public Library copy digitized by Google and available through the Internet Archive. The 1806 issues are from the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek. Translation and typesetting produced by AI.
