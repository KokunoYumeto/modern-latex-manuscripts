\begin{center}
{\large COMET.}
\end{center}

\begin{center}
\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}

Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen. No. 23. p. 217. 10 February 1821.

\noindent\rule{\linewidth}{0.4pt}
\end{center}

Mr. \textsc{Nicolet} discovered the comet in Paris on 21 January this year. Councillor \textsc{Gauss} found and observed it at our observatory on 30 January. At $7^{\mathrm{u}}\,34^{\mathrm{m}}\,32^{\mathrm{s}}$ mean time, its coordinates were as follows.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Right ascension . . . . . . . . & $359^\circ\;27'\;7''$ \\
Northern declination . . . . . . & $16\quad4\quad36$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This comet is invisible to the naked eye, but is readily visible even in a comet seeker, in which the tail also appears a few degrees long. There is no trace of a distinct nucleus.
