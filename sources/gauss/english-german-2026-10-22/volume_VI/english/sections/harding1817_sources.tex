\begin{center}\Large\bfseries Sources for the review of Harding’s celestial charts\end{center}

The translation follows Carl Friedrich Gauss’s review in \emph{Werke}, vol.~VI (Göttingen, 1874), pp.~593--595. It first appeared in \emph{Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen}, no.~5, 9 January 1817, pp.~41--45. The review refers back to the notice in no.~158 of the same journal for 1812.

The collected edition has \emph{Athas} where the first printing reads \emph{Atlas}. It has a comma after \emph{ist} where the first printing has a full stop. Both readings of the collected edition are retained in the translation. The \emph{3oth} degree reproduces the round, o-shaped glyph of the collected edition’s \emph{3osten}. The historical description of the projection follows the review.

Gauss’s review anticipates 28 sheets. The title page of Karl Ludwig Harding’s \emph{Atlas novus coelestis} (Göttingen, 1822) specifies 27 sheets. The review describes the maps in words.
