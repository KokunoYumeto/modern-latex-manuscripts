\begin{center}\Large\bfseries Sources for the review of the Philosophical Transactions\end{center}

The translation follows Carl Friedrich Gauss’s review in \emph{Werke}, vol.~VI (Göttingen, 1874), pp.~561--563. The collected review corresponds to the portion of \emph{Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen}, no.~27, 14 February 1814, pp.~269--272, beginning with Pond’s solstice observations. The title pages of the volume Gauss reviews give \emph{Year} where the collected review has \emph{Jear}.

John Pond’s first printed catalogue lists 44 stars (pp.~75--76). His later printed catalogue lists 84 (pp.~280--303). The first two solstice papers appear on pp.~27--30 and 123--125. The third, on p.~304, gives the 1813 summer result and records a correction to the summer 1812 result.

The denominator in the wire’s load is an indistinct character in the collected edition. The translation reproduces its visible shape. The Göttingen first printing (p.~271) and William Hyde Wollaston’s paper (p.~117) give $1\frac{1}{3}$ grains. Wollaston’s papers also give $\frac{1}{4000}$ and $\frac{1}{5000}$ of an inch for the wires suited to astronomical instruments (p.~115) and $\frac{1}{25}$ of an inch for the distance of the hole from the centre (p.~119).

Henry Kater’s comparison of Cassegrainian and Gregorian telescopes was communicated by Sir Joseph Banks (pp.~206--212). The University of Toronto copy contains both parts of the 1813 \emph{Philosophical Transactions}, printed in London by W. Bulmer and Co. and sold by G. and W. Nicol.
