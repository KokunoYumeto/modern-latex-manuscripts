\begin{center}\Large\bfseries Sources for the review of the Milan ephemerides for 1814\end{center}

The translation follows Carl Friedrich Gauss’s review in \emph{Werke}, vol.~VI (Göttingen, 1874), pp.~566--567. The first printing appeared in \emph{Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen}, no.~48, 24 March 1814, pp.~475--478. The collected review adds the bracketed page reference to the previous year’s review.

The title page of the copy held by the Austrian National Library names Francesco Carlini as the calculator of the 1814 ephemerides and gives Milan, Stamperia Reale, 1813. The contents credit Carlo Brioschi with the mean star positions reduced to the epoch of 1810. The copy was digitized by Google and is available through Mario Carpino’s digital bibliography at the INAF Brera Observatory.

Barnaba Oriani discusses the apparent nucleus in the appendix (p.~6), and Giuseppe Piazzi’s letter of 4 July 1812 is on p.~70. G.\,Angelo Cesaris’s meteorological tables for 1810--1812 begin on pp.~105, 117 and 129. The July 1811 table on p.~123 gives its highest thermometer reading as $+25{,}4$. The translation retains the review’s form $25^\circ\,4$.
