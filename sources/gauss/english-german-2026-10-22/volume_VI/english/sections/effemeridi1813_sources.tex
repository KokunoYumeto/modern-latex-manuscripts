\begin{center}\textbf{Sources for the review of the Milan ephemerides}\end{center}
\noindent AI-translated from Carl Friedrich Gauss's German review in \href{https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/PPN236007467}{\emph{Werke}, vol.\ VI}, Göttingen, 1874, pp.\ 556--558. The review first appeared under Milan in the \emph{Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen}, issue 95, 14 June 1813, pp.\ 945--952. In the collected edition the Piazzi epoch ends in a character that appears as a \emph{c}, and the 30-second comparison is for clear sunny days. The first printing gives 1810 and clear summer days. Both give 42 hours for the complement mentioned in the Pallas passage.

\par\smallskip\noindent The ephemerides for \href{https://books.google.it/books?id=GDlZAAAAcAAJ}{1812} and \href{https://books.google.it/books?id=4HCOwKAJ28kC}{1813} were calculated by Francesco Carlini and Carlo Brioschi. The scans linked here are from the Austrian National Library and the Biblioteca di Matematica G.~Castelnuovo of Sapienza Università di Roma, respectively. Brera's \href{https://www.beniculturali.inaf.it/opac/pst-iccd/circolo-moltiplicatore_13070}{catalogue of the Reichenbach repeating circle} records its supporting column and cites Barnaba Oriani's observations in the 1812 appendix.
