\begin{center}
Observations of the Comet of 1824 in Göttingen.
\end{center}

All these observations were indeed made with the meridian circle at the upper culminations. But the comet was so faint that it was impossible to see it with even the slightest illumination of the wires. The wires and the comet therefore had to be seen in alternation, the wires with the light admitted and the comet with the light shut off. This is why the observations cannot be nearly as precise as they would have been under more favourable conditions. The weather prevented observations of all the earlier culminations, although I had made myself ready each time. I also note that the crossings were not always observed or estimated at all 7 wires. Hence the assigned times do not always agree with the passages through the meridian itself. The assigned time always corresponds to the mean of the observed crossings. The declinations were reduced to that time if they had not been set then.
