The simplest way to combine the observations into a result for
the difference in latitude between the observing sites is to consider each star
separately. If, with the limb to the east and to the west respectively,
the observed zenith distances in Göttingen are $a$ and $a'$, and in Altona $b$ and $b'$, then
the difference in latitude is $=\frac12(a+a')-\frac12(b+b')$. We therefore obtain as
many results as there are stars with complete observations. Our observations yield
42 results, since only No.\ 5 is excluded, having been observed at Altona in just one limb position.

Were the observations on which the determinations $a$, $a'$, $b$, $b'$ are based
equally numerous for all stars, all the individual results
for the difference in latitude would be regarded as equally reliable, and hence the
simple arithmetic mean would be the most probable final result. In our
observations the counts vary. The results must therefore be assigned
unequal weights according to the number of
observations.

independent of one another, take the weight of a single observation
as the unit, and denote the numbers of observations contributing to

we can express the weight of the result $\frac12a+\frac12a'-\frac12b-\frac12b'$
