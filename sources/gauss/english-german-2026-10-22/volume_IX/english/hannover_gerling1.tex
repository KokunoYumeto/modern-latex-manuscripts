\textsc{Gauss} to \textsc{Gerling}.\quad Göttingen, 5~October 1821.
\end{center}

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}My hearty congratulations on the task you have undertaken.
I am glad it has come into your hands, since you will make it your duty to carry out the entire triangulation network with all possible care.
I consider this most desirable, and I always regret it when people, even at the stage of first-order triangles, are so sparing with their efforts that they resolve to use only as much precision as is absolutely necessary for the ultimate purpose.
The \textls[120]{most precise} knowledge of the relative positions of the most interesting points in a country can be useful in many respects, quite apart from the fact that it provides the best foundation for a detailed survey.
It would surely be of the utmost importance for the greater part of Europe to be covered entirely by a single network, and we shall get there by degrees. Each state should make it a point of honour to do its share so well that it is worthy of a place beside the best.

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}I should think you would do well to choose the Meisner as a triangulation point too. You will be able to connect it \textls[120]{directly} to at least 5 of my triangulation points and probably to as many of \textsc{Müffling}'s besides.
I shall gladly lend a hand and, if it can be arranged at all, make the observations towards it from my side.
