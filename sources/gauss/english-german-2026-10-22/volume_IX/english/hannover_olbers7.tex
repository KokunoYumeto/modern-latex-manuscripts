\begin{center}
\textsc{Gauss} to \textsc{Olbers}.\quad Göttingen, 9~October 1825.
\end{center}

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}\mbox{.\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .}\quad In recent days, with a view to my future work on Higher Geodesy, I have begun to put my thoughts in some order on a (very) small part of the subject of curved surfaces.
I am coming to see, however, that the distinctive character of my entire treatment obliges me, for the sake of coherence, to \textls[120]{start a long way back}, so that I must even begin by setting out my view of the radii of curvature of plane curves.
This has left me almost wondering whether it would be more advisable first to work out separately a part of this theory that is purely geometrical (in analytical form) and combines new and familiar material in a new form, perhaps detaching it from the work and putting it into our Commentationen as one or two papers.
For the present, however, I can leave the form of publication undecided, and in the meantime I shall continue putting it on paper.
