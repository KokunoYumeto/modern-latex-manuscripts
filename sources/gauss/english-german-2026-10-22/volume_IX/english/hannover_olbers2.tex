\textsc{Gauss} to \textsc{Olbers}.\quad Göttingen, 18~April 1822.
\end{center}

\par\noindent\hspace*{\parindent}.\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .\quad All my computations are based on the following dimensions of the Earth
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\;}c@{}}
a & = & 3\,271\,821 & \text{[toises]} \\
b & = & 3\,261\,011 & \text{»} \\
\text{Flatt.} & = & \tfrac{1}{302{,}68}.\;\text{[\textsuperscript{*})]} &
\end{array}
\]
I had actually intended to adopt \textsc{Walbeck}'s result exactly, \(\frac{1}{302{,}78}\).
Through a slip of the pen, however, I had long ago used the former value as the basis for calculating various auxiliary tables. I considered it all the less worthwhile to redo them on that account, since the difference is far below the accuracy attainable from all the arc measurements.
