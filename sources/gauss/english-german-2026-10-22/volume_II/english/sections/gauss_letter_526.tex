I offer you, my highly esteemed friend, my most sincere thanks for your kind letter and for kindly sending your two memoirs. I am pleased to see the growing interest now beginning to be taken in investigations of higher arithmetic. I very much liked the successful way in which you derive the second, concerning the biquadratic residuacity of the number 2, from the first.
\par Presumably the 6th volume of our Commentationes has now reached Breslau, so you will probably be familiar by now with my Commentatio prima on biquadratic residues: if an opportunity arises, I would also gladly send you a separate copy. I could have chosen among several methods of proving the theorem in it; but you will see why I preferred the method used there, chiefly because the classification of 2 for those moduli for which it is a quadratic nonresidue (under $B$ or $D$) must be regarded as an essential part of the theorem, to which most other methods of proof seem inapplicable.
\par I have had all the material for the entire investigation for 23 years, and the proofs of its main theorems (among which the theorem in the first memoir is \textit{not} yet to be counted) for about 14 years \textemdash{} (although I wish and hope to be able to simplify something further in the latter, the proofs) \textemdash{}. I have planned the investigation for about 3 memoirs. I have already begun writing the second, and hope to finish it before long, unless the surveying duties recently assigned to me again cause some delay.

Anzeigen three years ago and drawn attention to the remarkable knot still to be untied in connection with it; but I have not yet heard of anyone attempting it.
