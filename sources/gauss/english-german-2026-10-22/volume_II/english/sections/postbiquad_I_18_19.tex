\begin{center}\emph{Theory of the biquadratic residue $-1-2i$.}\end{center}
Let the modulus $=m=a+bi$ be such that $a$ is odd and $b$ even; we also assume that it is a prime number.

The following diagrams represent the decident; their identity is easily seen:
\begin{center}\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{diagrams/native332_schemata.tex}}\end{center}

For brevity, we denote $S(x,x+\alpha,x+\alpha+\beta,x+\beta)$ by $[x,\alpha,\beta]$, so that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\relax
[x,\alpha,\beta]&=-[x,\beta,\alpha]=[x+\alpha,\beta,-\alpha]\\
&=-[x+\alpha,-\alpha,\beta]\\
&=[x+\alpha+\beta,-\alpha,-\beta]\\
&=-[x+\alpha+\beta,-\beta,-\alpha]
\end{aligned}
\]

If we also put
\[
\frac{m}{-2-4i}=\frac{-a-2b}{10}+\frac{-b+2a}{10}i=Q
\]
\SourceLeaf{333}

then the decident consists of the following eight parts:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{I}&=[0,\tfrac12,-iQ]\\
-2\mathrm{II}&=-2[0,\tfrac12,Q]\\
\mathrm{III}&=+[Q,\tfrac12,-iQ]\\
\mathrm{IV}&=+[-iQ,\tfrac12,Q]\\
-3\mathrm{V}&=-3[Q,\tfrac12,Q]\\
-3\mathrm{VI}&=-3[2Q,\tfrac12,-iQ]\\
+2\mathrm{VII}&=+2[(1-i)Q,\tfrac12,Q]\\
+\mathrm{VIII}&=+[0,\tfrac12,\tfrac12im]
\end{aligned}
\]

If $F$ denotes an arbitrary elementary residue of the modulus $-1-2i$, then
\[
\Sigma[2FQ,\tfrac12,iQ]=[0,-\tfrac12-i,\tfrac12im]
\]

Thus, for $F$ substitute $0,1,i,-1,-i$; we have
\[
\begin{array}{rll}
0={}&[0,\tfrac12,iQ]&=\mathrm{IX}\\
&+[2Q,\tfrac12,iQ]&=\mathrm{X}\pdot\\
&+[2iQ,\tfrac12,iQ]&=\mathrm{XI}\\
&+[-2Q,\tfrac12,iQ]&=\mathrm{XII}\\
&+[-2iQ,\tfrac12,iQ]&=\mathrm{XIII}\\
&-[0,-\tfrac12-i,\tfrac12im]&=-\mathrm{XIV}
\end{array}
\]

Add this to the preceding value of the decident. Combining them gives the following results:

(1) Since $(1+2i)Q+\frac12$ is an integer, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{XI}&=[-Q-\tfrac12,\tfrac12,iQ]= -[-Q,-\tfrac12,iQ]\\
&=-[Q,\tfrac12,-iQ]
\end{aligned}
\]
and hence
\[
\mathrm{III}+\mathrm{XI}=0
\]

(2) We combine $\mathrm{IV},-3\mathrm{V},\mathrm{X},\mathrm{XIII}$ as follows:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{IV}&=+[-2Q+\tfrac12i,\tfrac12,Q]=[-2iQ-\tfrac12,\tfrac12i,iQ]\\
\mathrm{V}'&=-[2Q,+\tfrac12,-Q]=-[-2iQ,-\tfrac12i,iQ]\\
\mathrm{X}&=[+iQ-\tfrac12i,\tfrac12,iQ]\\
&=[-iQ+\tfrac12i,-\tfrac12,-iQ]=[-2iQ-\tfrac12+\tfrac12i,\tfrac12,iQ]\\
\mathrm{XIII}&=[-2iQ,\tfrac12,iQ]
\end{aligned}
\]

\SourceLeaf{334}

Thus the entire result from these parts is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&-4\mathrm{V}\\
&+R(-2iQ)-R(-iQ)-I(-2iQ)+I(-iQ)\\
&-\text{Sq. }[-2iQ-\tfrac14+\tfrac14i]\\
&+\text{Sq. }[-2iQ+\tfrac14-\tfrac14i]
\end{aligned}
\]

(3) Combining $\mathrm{I},-2\mathrm{II},-3\mathrm{VI},+2\mathrm{VII},+\mathrm{IX},+\mathrm{XII}$ gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{I}&=[0,\tfrac12,-iQ]\\
\mathrm{II}&=[0,-\tfrac12i,-iQ]\\
\mathrm{VI}&=[iQ-\tfrac12i,\tfrac12,-iQ]=[\tfrac12-\tfrac12i,-\tfrac12,iQ]\\
&=[-\tfrac12+\tfrac12i,\tfrac12,-iQ]\\
\mathrm{VII}&=[-Q-\tfrac12i,\tfrac12,Q]=[-\tfrac12+\tfrac12i,-\tfrac12,-Q]\\
&=[+\tfrac12+\tfrac12i,-\tfrac12i,-iQ]\\
\mathrm{IX}&=[0,-\tfrac12,-iQ]\\
\mathrm{XII}&=[-iQ+\tfrac12i,\tfrac12,iQ]=[\tfrac12+\tfrac12i,-\tfrac12,-iQ]
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
={}&+4\mathrm{I}-4\mathrm{II}-4\mathrm{VI}+4\mathrm{XII}\\
&-I(\tfrac12i)+I(-iQ+\tfrac12i)+I0-I(-iQ)-2R0+2R(-iQ)\\
&+2\text{Sq. }(-iQ+\tfrac14+\tfrac14i)\\
={}&4\mathrm{I}-4\mathrm{II}-4\mathrm{VI}+4\mathrm{XII}\\
&+R(-1-2i)Q-R(-2iQ)-I(-iQ)+2R(-iQ)\\
&+2\text{Sq. }(-2iQ+\tfrac14+\tfrac14i)
\end{aligned}
\]

All this together therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&+4\mathrm{I}-4\mathrm{II}-4\mathrm{V}-4\mathrm{VI}+4\mathrm{XII}+\tfrac12(a-1)-\tfrac12b\\
&+\text{Sq. }(-2iQ+\tfrac14-\tfrac14i)\\
&+2\text{Sq. }(-2iQ+\tfrac14+\tfrac14i)\\
&-\text{Sq. }(-2iQ-\tfrac14+\tfrac14i)
\end{aligned}
\]

Finally, $\mathrm{VIII}-\mathrm{XIV}=\frac12(a-1)+\frac12b$.
Thus, since the three square parts equal the decident of $\frac{m}{-1-2i}$, we obtain
\[
\text{Dec. }\frac{-1-2i}{m}\equiv a-1+\text{Dec. }\frac{m}{-1-2i}\quad\text{Q. E. D.}
\]

The proof can probably be simplified considerably further by the fact that
\[
\text{Dec. }\frac{1+2i}{m}=\vcenter{\hbox{\resizebox{.35\textwidth}{!}{\input{diagrams/native334_triangle.tex}}}}
\]

\Article{19}
The following has been found by induction:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Dec. }\frac{a-bi}{a+bi}&\equiv\frac{aa+2ab-1}{4},& a&\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod.}\ 4)\\
&\equiv\frac{aa+2ab+2bb-1}{4},&a+bi&\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod.}\ 2+2i)
\end{aligned}
\]

The following is connected with this:
Let $aa+bb=p$ (a prime number), $a\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod.}\ 4)$
\[
\begin{aligned}
1\pdot2\pdot3\mathinner{.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.}\tfrac14(p-1)&\equiv\alpha\ \mathrm{mod.}p\\
\tfrac14(p+3)\pdot\tfrac14(p+7)\ldots\tfrac12(p-1)&\equiv\beta\\
\tfrac12(p+1)\pdot\tfrac12(p+3)\ldots\tfrac34(p-1)&\equiv\gamma\\
\tfrac14(3p+1)\mathinner{.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.}\ p-1&\equiv\delta
\end{aligned}
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\alpha&\equiv\delta,&\beta&\equiv\gamma&\text{if }\tfrac12b\text{ is even}\\
\alpha&\equiv-\delta,&\beta&\equiv-\gamma&\text{if }\tfrac12b\text{ is odd}
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{gathered}
\pm\alpha\beta\equiv i,\quad\pm\beta\beta\equiv2b,\quad\frac\beta\alpha\equiv2a,\\
\frac\beta{1+\alpha\beta}\equiv\surd a,\quad\tfrac12\beta(1-\alpha\beta)\equiv\surd a
\end{gathered}
\]

It therefore remains only to determine the decidents for real residues.
\[
\begin{gathered}
a^{\frac14bb}\pdot b^{\frac14(aa-1)}\equiv1\ (\mathrm{mod.}\ (aa+bb))\\
\text{if }a\equiv1)(\mathrm{mod.}\ 4)\quad b\text{ even}\quad aa+bb\text{ prime}
\end{gathered}
\]

If only real numbers are to be used, the matter comes down to the following main theorem. Let $a-1$ be divisible by $4$, and $b$ by $2$; let $a$ and $b$ have no common divisor, and let $k$ denote the numbers $1,2,3\ldots aa+bb-1$.
Let
\begin{list}{}{\setlength{\labelwidth}{1.5em}\setlength{\leftmargin}{2em}}
\item[$\alpha$] the number of all values of $k$ for which the least residues of $ak,\allowbreak bk,\quad\allowbreak aak,\quad\allowbreak abk$ all lie between $0$ and $\frac12(aa+bb)$
\item[$\beta$] $ak,bk,\quad aak,-abk$
\item[$\gamma$] $ak,bk,-aak,-abk$
\item[$\delta$] $ak,bk,-aak,\quad abk$
\end{list}
Then $\beta+2\gamma+3\delta-\frac14(aa-1)$ is divisible by $4$.
