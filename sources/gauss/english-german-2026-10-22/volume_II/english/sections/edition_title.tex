{\large Volume II: arithmetical papers, reviews and manuscript studies\par}\bigskip\vfill
\begin{minipage}{0.90\linewidth}\small
This selection contains the historical title matter and first five complete papers of Volume II: A New Proof of an Arithmetical Theorem (Articles 1–7); Summation of Certain Special Series (Articles 1–36); New Proofs and Extensions of the Fundamental Theorem in the Theory of Quadratic Residues, with its introduction, fifth proof (Articles 1–3), sixth proof (Articles 1–6) and new algorithm (Articles 1–6); and both papers on the Theory of Biquadratic Residues (Articles 1–23 and 24–76). Five self-reviews from 1808–1831 and seven reviews of other writings from 1809–1831 follow. The latter concern irrational radicals, the factor tables of Chernac and Burckhardt, Erchinger’s construction of the regular seventeen-sided polygon, and Seeber’s positive ternary quadratic forms.

The manuscript studies include Solution of a Pure Congruence, Analysis of Residues, Chapter VI, first part (Articles 237–252); General Investigations of Congruences, Chapter VIII (Articles 330–375 as printed, including the unfinished ending of Article 375), with R. Dedekind’s historical remarks; and Further Development of the Investigations of Pure Equations, complete as printed (Articles 1–19), with Dedekind’s remarks. The fragment Proof of Some Theorems Concerning the Periods of Classes of Binary Quadratic Forms follows, with both theorems, the unfinished ending of the second proof, and Stern’s historical remark.

The texts marked [I.] and [II.] of On the Connection between the Number of Classes into Which Binary Quadratic Forms Are Distributed and Their Determinant, First Paper, follow in full as printed, each with its title and Articles 1–8. The text marked [II.] was presented in 1837. Fragments III–X follow, with their historical editorial notes. Historical remarks on [I.]–[VII.] follow.

Originally German title matter, reviews, the manuscript division title, Fragments III–IV and VIII–X and the historical remarks are rendered in modern German in the German reader. Their historical originals are supplied separately. Later texts of Volume II lie beyond this selection. Complete Volume I accompanies it in the collected reader.

Historical source: Carl Friedrich Gauss, Werke, Volume II, second printing, Göttingen, 1876.
