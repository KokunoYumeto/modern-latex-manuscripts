Carl Friedrich Gauss: Collected Works
Modern English and German translations

The collected English reader has 459 pages; the German reader has 482 pages.
Each contains complete Volume I, followed by historical title matter from
Volume II and its first two complete papers: A New Proof of an Arithmetical
Theorem (Theorematis arithmetici demonstratio nova), Articles 1–7, presented
on January 15, 1808, and Summation of Certain Special Series (Summatio
quarumdam serierum singularium), Articles 1–36, presented on August 24, 1808.

The readers also contain the title and complete introduction of New Proofs
and Extensions of the Fundamental Theorem in the Theory of Quadratic Residues
(Theorematis fundamentalis in doctrina de residuis quadraticis demonstrationes
et ampliationes novae), presented on February 10, 1817. The proofs and algorithm
of this third paper, the remaining works in Volume II and the other volumes
are outside this selection.

Volume I contains the historical title matter, dedication, preface, all seven
sections (Articles 1–366), the five historical additions, Tables I–III,
the historical contents, both imprints, the manuscript appendix and the
historical editor's closing note. Page references in the historical contents
are those of the source rather than the page numbers of these readers.

Historical sources: Carl Friedrich Gauss, Werke, Volume I, Göttingen, 1863;
Volume II, second printing, Göttingen, 1876. Disquisitiones Arithmeticae
was first published in Leipzig in 1801.

The originally German passages of the Volume-I appendix and historical
editor's closing note, and the German Volume-II title matter, are identified
as modern German renderings in the German readers. Historical German and
Latin TeX bodies are preserved separately in the originals directories.

The source archive contains editable LaTeX text, mathematics and tables,
and the genuine portraits used in the readers. The volume_I and volume_II
directories each contain english and german source directories. Build
main.tex in each source directory with XeLaTeX twice, using Latin Modern,
Babel, pdflscape and longtable. The collected readers join the volume readers
in numerical order; the individual volume readers are also supplied.
