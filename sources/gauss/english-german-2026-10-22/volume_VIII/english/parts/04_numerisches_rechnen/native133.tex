More importantly, one must by no means regard the third differences as vanishing everywhere, as \textsc{Leonelli} imagines, considering it sufficient to take account only of the second differences. He seems to have been led into this error by the sample he calculated for the beginning of the table, where the third differences do indeed vanish. A simple calculation shows, however, that these third differences \emph{increase} farther on and can become appreciable if one wants to give 14 decimal places and use no increments in $P$ smaller than 0,0005. For $x = 2+\BareRoot{3}$, or for $P = 0,5719$, where the third differences reach their maximum, we find under the above assumptions that their value is $= 6377$ units in the fourteenth decimal place. Thus \textsc{Leonelli} is far from being able to make do with fewer than 5000 entries. If the second differences are to suffice everywhere, the table would have to be so extensive that calculating it would not repay the effort. But why use fourteen decimal places at all? Calculations that need such precision are exceedingly rare. There is little point in securing a fairly small saving of work for so rare a case. For everyday calculations, on the other hand, where seven decimal places are quite enough, even a modest saving of work would be valuable if it recurred often. To offer the necessary convenience, however, the table would then have to be arranged so that the second differences also vanish everywhere. This could readily be achieved with a little more than 15000 entries, which would make only a modest little volume. This is not the place to go into further detail.

A further example of how superficially \textsc{Leonelli} treated his subject is the formula given on p.\,60. For a given value of $R$ falling between two tabulated values, it is meant to find the corresponding value of $Q$ with allowance for the second differences. The formula, whose derivation cost the translator so much fruitless effort by his own admission, is quite wrong. In place of
\[
2\delta(r'n - n) \quad \text{should stand} \quad \frac{(D+d)\delta rn}{d}.
\]
