\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}The main steps in the proof of the preceding theorems are as follows.

Let $r$ be the length of a shortest line starting from the point $A$ on the curved surface, $\theta$ the angle it makes with a similar fixed line, $\BareRoot{\frac1f}$ the radius of the sphere with the same curvature as the curved surface at the endpoint of $r$, and $\rho d\theta$ the line element between two points corresponding to $r,\theta$ and $r,\theta+d\theta$. Thus both $f$ and $\rho$ are functions of $r$ and $\theta$, and one has
\[
\frac{\partial\partial\rho}{\partial r^2}=-f\rho.
\]
\indent Let $dt$ be an element of a second shortest line and $\zeta$ its angle with the corresponding $r$. Then
\[
dr=dt.\cos\zeta,\quad\rho d\theta=dt.\sin\zeta,\quad d\zeta=-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial r}.d\theta.
\]
\noindent If we set
\[
f=F+F'r+F''rr+.\, .\, .,
\]
\noindent then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\rho={}&r+*-\tfrac16Fr^3-\tfrac1{12}F'r^4-(\tfrac1{20}F''-\tfrac1{120}FF)r^5\\
&-(\tfrac1{30}F'''-\tfrac1{120}FF')r^6+.\, .\, .\\
\rho={}&r+*-\tfrac1{12}(F+f)r^3+*+(\tfrac1{30}F''+\tfrac1{120}FF)r^5\\
&+(\tfrac1{20}F'''+\tfrac1{120}FF')r^6+.\, .\, .
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\log\sin\zeta&=-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial r}\frac1\rho dr\\
&=-\frac{dr}r+rdr\Bigl\{\tfrac13F+\tfrac14F'r+(\tfrac15F''+\tfrac1{45}FF)rr\\
&\qquad+(\tfrac16F'''+\tfrac1{36}FF')r^3+.\, .\, .\Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\log(r\sin\zeta)={}&\tfrac1{12}(F+3f)rdr-(\tfrac1{20}F''-\tfrac1{45}FF)r^3dr\\
&-(\tfrac1{12}F'''-\tfrac1{36}FF')r^4dr+.\, .\, .
\end{aligned}
\]
\indent We now have [in the geodesic triangle $ABC$] for $\theta=0$
\[
\zeta=180^0-B,\quad r=c,\quad f=\beta,\quad F=\alpha,\quad t=0
\]
\noindent [and for] $\theta=A$
\[
\zeta=C,\quad r=b,\quad f=\gamma,\quad t=a.
\]
