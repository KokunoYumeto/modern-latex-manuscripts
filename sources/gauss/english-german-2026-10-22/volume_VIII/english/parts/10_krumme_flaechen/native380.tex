\begin{center}\large\bfseries [AREA-PRESERVING MAP FROM ONE PLANE\\ TO ANOTHER.]\end{center}

Problem. Map the points in a plane to a second plane so that each infinitesimal figure in the first plane corresponds to one of equal area in the other.

Let the coordinates in one plane be $x$ and $y$,\\
\hspace*{1.6em}and the corresponding coordinates in the other be $\xi$ and $\eta$.

\noindent Then the \textls[120]{general} solution is as follows.

Let $u$ be an arbitrary function of $x$ and $\xi$. Set
\[
y=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},\qquad \eta=-\frac{\partial u}{\partial\xi}.
\]

\indent This solution is entirely correct and general and can easily be visualized geometrically.

For a spherical surface, one need only use polar coordinates $(\theta,r)$ and substitute $\theta,\ \cos r$ for $x,y$. This makes it easy to map one spherical surface onto another or onto the plane, etc.
