\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}Now consider a finite portion of the line from $L$ to $L'$ and call $\varphi$, $\varphi'$ the values of the angle at the two endpoints. Then
\[
\varphi'=\varphi+\operatorname{Area}LL'P'P,
\]
where the sign of the area is understood in the same way.

Assume further that countless other shortest lines issue from the starting point on the curved surface. Call $A$ the angle that the first element of an arbitrary one of these lines makes with the first element of the original line, measured leftward. Draw a curved line through the second endpoints of these various curved lines, leaving it undecided for now whether it is a shortest line or not. Suppose now that, for an arbitrary one of these lines, what was $\varphi$, $\varphi'$ for the first line is denoted by $\psi$, $\psi'$. Then $\psi'-\psi$ is similarly represented on the auxiliary sphere by the region $LL'P'P$, and since evidently $\psi=\varphi-A$, the region is
\[
\begin{aligned}
LL'_1P'_1P'L'L&=\psi'-\psi-\varphi'+\varphi\\
&=\psi'-\varphi'+A\\
&=LL'_1L'L+L'L'_1P'_1P'.
\end{aligned}
\]
