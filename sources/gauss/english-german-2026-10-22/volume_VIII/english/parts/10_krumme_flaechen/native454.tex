\begin{center}\large [MOST GENERAL SOLUTION\\OF THE PROBLEM OF DEVELOPING SURFACES.]\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}The fully general solution of the problem of developing surfaces is as follows:

1) The nature of a curved surface is specified by representing $z$ as a finite function of $x,y$. Let differentiation give
\[
dz=pdx+qdy.
\]

2) Set
\[
t=\frac{1}{\BareRoot{(pp+qq+1)}}
\]
\noindent and assume that differentiation of an arbitrary function of $t,T$ gives
\[
udt+UdT,
\]
\noindent where $u,U$ will be given functions of $t$ and $T$.

Thus by elimination $T,U$ become functions of $t,u$ and, if
\[
\operatorname{tang}u=\frac{q}{p}
\]
\noindent is set, functions of $x$ and $y$.

3) Now set
\[
\frac{Q}{P}=\operatorname{tang}U,\qquad
T\BareRoot{(1+PP+QQ)}=1,
\]
\noindent and $P,Q$ will become functions of $x$ and $y$.
