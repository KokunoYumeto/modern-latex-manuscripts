\begin{center}\large NEW GENERAL INVESTIGATIONS\\[4pt] ON\\[4pt] CURVED SURFACES.\\[7pt] [1825.]\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}Although the purpose of this work properly consists in developing new propositions concerning its subject, we shall at first also develop what is already known, partly for continuity and completeness and partly because our manner of treating it differs from that used hitherto. For the same reasons, we shall even begin with a few matters concerning curves in the plane.

\begin{center}1.\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}To compare conveniently the different directions of straight lines in one plane, imagine a circle of radius $1$ drawn in that plane about an arbitrary centre. The position of the radius of this circle drawn parallel to a given line then represents the position of that line, and the inclination of two straight lines to one another is measured by the angle of the two representative radii or by the arc between their endpoints.
