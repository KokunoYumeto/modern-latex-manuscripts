\begin{center}\textbf{REMARKS.}\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}If we regard the coordinates of two corresponding points $P$ and $P'$ on surfaces $S$ and $S'$ that can be developed onto one another as functions of two independently varying variables $p,q$, then the quadratic differential expression
\[
dx^2+dy^2+dz^2=dx'^2+dy'^2+dz'^2=Edp^2+2Fdpdq+Gdq^2
\]
\noindent represents the square of the line element of these surfaces. If we consider at the same time the two spherical images of these surfaces on the unit sphere, denote by $\xi,\eta,\zeta$ the coordinates of the image of $P$ and by $\xi',\eta',\zeta'$ the corresponding coordinates of the image of $P'$, and determine the squares of the line elements of these images from the equations
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\xi=\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\cos u,&\eta=\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\sin u,\\[4pt]
\xi'=\sqrt{1-\zeta'^2}\cos u',&\eta'=\sqrt{1-\zeta'^2}\sin u',
\end{array}
\]
\noindent then we obtain:
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\xi^2+d\eta^2+d\zeta^2&=\frac{d\zeta^2}{1-\zeta^2}+(1-\zeta^2)du^2\\[5pt]
d\xi'^2+d\eta'^2+d\zeta'^2&=\frac{d\zeta'^2}{1-\zeta'^2}+(1-\zeta'^2)du'^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

\noindent If we introduce the quantities $\zeta,\zeta'$ as the variables that determine the position of $P$ and $P'$ on $S$ and $S'$, the following three equations result:
\[
dx^2+dy^2+dz^2=dx'^2+dy'^2+dz'^2=Ed\zeta^2+2Fd\zeta d\zeta'+Gd\zeta'^2
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\xi^2+d\eta^2+d\zeta^2
&=\frac{d\zeta^2}{1-\zeta^2}+(1-\zeta^2)\left[\frac{\partial u}{\partial\zeta}d\zeta+\frac{\partial u}{\partial\zeta'}d\zeta'\right]^2\\
&=\mathfrak{E}d\zeta^2+2\mathfrak{F}d\zeta d\zeta'+\mathfrak{G}d\zeta'^2
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
d\xi'^2+d\eta'^2+d\zeta'^2
&=\frac{d\zeta'^2}{1-\zeta'^2}+(1-\zeta'^2)\left[\frac{\partial u'}{\partial\zeta}d\zeta+\frac{\partial u'}{\partial\zeta'}d\zeta'\right]^2\\
&=\mathfrak{E}'d\zeta^2+2\mathfrak{F}'d\zeta d\zeta'+\mathfrak{G}'d\zeta'^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
