\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}From a given point on a curved surface, let countless shortest lines go out on that surface. They are to be distinguished by the angle their first element makes with the first element of one \textls[100]{specified} shortest line. This angle is to be called $\theta$. Further, let $s$ be the length [measured from the given point] of a portion of such a shortest line, and let the endpoints have coordinates $x,y,.z$. Since $\theta$ and $s$ thus belong to a definite point on the curved surface, one may regard $x,y,z$ as functions of $\theta$ and $s$. Let the direction of the element of $s$ correspond on the sphere to the point $\lambda$, whose coordinates are $\xi,\eta,\zeta$, so that the following equations hold
\[
\xi=\frac{\partial x}{\partial s},\qquad\eta=\frac{\partial y}{\partial s},\qquad\zeta=\frac{\partial z}{\partial s}
\]

The endpoints of all shortest lines of the same length $s$ correspond to a curved line whose length may be called $t$.
