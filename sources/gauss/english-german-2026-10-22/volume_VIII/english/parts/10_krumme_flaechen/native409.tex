\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}The general investigation of triangles on curved surfaces has given us the following important result.

Let $\BareRoot{\frac1\alpha}$, $\BareRoot{\frac1\beta}$, $\BareRoot{\frac1\gamma}$ be the radii of the spheres having the same curvature as the curved surface at the points $A,B,C$, respectively. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log\frac{b\sin C}{c\sin B}&=\tfrac1{24}(bb-cc)\alpha+\tfrac1{48}(-aa+3bb-3cc)\beta\\
&\quad+\tfrac1{48}(aa+3bb-3cc)\gamma\\
A+B+C&=180^0+\tfrac16(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)bc\sin A,
\end{aligned}
\]
\noindent and if we put
\[
\begin{aligned}
dA&=\frac{bc\sin A}{24}(2\alpha+\beta+\gamma)\\
dB&=\frac{bc\sin A}{24}(\alpha+2\beta+\gamma)\\
dC&=\frac{bc\sin A}{24}(\alpha+\beta+2\gamma)
\end{aligned}
\]
\noindent then
\[
\frac{\sin(A-dA)}a=\frac{\sin(B-dB)}b=\frac{\sin(C-dC)}c.
\]
\indent It is unnecessary to note that here $A,B,C$ denote the angles of the spheroidal triangle itself.
