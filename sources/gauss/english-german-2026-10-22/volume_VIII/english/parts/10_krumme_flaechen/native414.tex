\noindent [we have:]
\begin{equation}\tag{4}
\xi.\frac{\partial X}{\partial\theta}+\eta.\frac{\partial Y}{\partial\theta}+\zeta.\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\theta}=-mq
\end{equation}
\noindent [and]
\begin{equation}\tag{5}
\begin{aligned}
(YZ'-ZY')\frac{\partial X}{\partial\theta}+(ZX'-XZ')\frac{\partial Y}{\partial\theta}&\\
+(XY'-YX')\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\theta}&=\frac{\partial\partial m}{\partial r^2}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\indent The meaning of equation (4) can be illustrated as follows. The quantities $dr$ and $md\theta$ are two mutually perpendicular line elements on the surface. On the celestial sphere, $Q,Q',Q''$ represent the normals at $P,P',P''$, respectively. $QR'$ represents the vertical plane through $PP'$, and $QR''$ the vertical plane through $PP''$. Then:
\begin{center}\input{figure414a.tex}\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}Equation (4) means that
\[
\frac{Q''R''}{PP''}=\frac{Q'R'}{PP'},
\]
\begin{center}\input{figure414b.tex}\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}Equation (5) means that the area is given by
\[
2QQ'Q''=\frac{\partial\partial m}{m\partial r^2}PP'.PP''.
\]
