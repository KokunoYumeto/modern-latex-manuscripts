\begin{center}\large [DEVELOPABLE SURFACES.]\end{center}
\begin{center}[1.]\end{center}
\noindent\hspace*{1.2em}1825 Dec. 4.\quad Reflection on the theory of developable surfaces has furnished us with the following elegant analytical theorem.

If $z$ is such a function of $x,y$ that
\[
\frac{\partial\partial z}{\partial x^2}.\frac{\partial\partial z}{\partial y^2}
=\left(\frac{\partial\partial z}{\partial x\partial y}\right)^2,
\]
\noindent then in place of $x,y$ two other variable quantities $t,u$ can be introduced, so that $x,y,z$, expressed as functions of $t,u$, are linear functions with respect to $u$. Indeed, for $u$ any linear function of $x,y,z$ may be chosen.
