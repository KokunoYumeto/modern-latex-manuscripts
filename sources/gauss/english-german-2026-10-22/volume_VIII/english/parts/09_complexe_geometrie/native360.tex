\bfseries Central projection.

If $a+bi$, $a'+b'i$ are the complex numbers for the projections of two points, then for their arc distance we have

\[
\text{cosine} = \frac{rr+aa'+bb'}{\Delta}
\]
\[
\text{sine} = \BareRoot{\left\{[(a'-a)^2+(b'-b)^2]rr+(ab'-ba')^2\right\}}:\Delta,
\]
\[
\Delta = \BareRoot{\left\{(rr+aa+bb)(rr+a'a'+b'b')\right\}}.
\]

\bfseries THEOREM.

\begin{center}
\bfseries [ON STEREOGRAPHIC PROJECTION.]
\end{center}

The actual (straight-line) distance between two points on the spherical surface is obtained from the distance between their stereographic projections. Multiply that distance by the product of the cosines of the two angles that the lines drawn from the projections to the endpoint of a normal at the center make with the normal. The length of the normal is the diameter of the sphere (the radius of the equator in the projection).

\[
\text{Actual distance} = PP'\cdot\frac{CN}{PN}\cdot\frac{CN}{P'N}.
\]

\begin{center}\input{figure360a.tex}\end{center}
\indent One thus obtains the chord, or a quantity proportional to the sine of half the arc. It is better either to express the projections by bicomplex quantities $t$, $t'$, in which case the distance is represented by [the] tangent of half the arc, as measured by

\[
\frac{t'-t}{1+tt'},
\]

\noindent or to make use of tricomplex quantities for the same purpose.
