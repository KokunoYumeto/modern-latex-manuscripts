\textbf{\Large POSTHUMOUS PAPERS AND CORRESPONDENCE.}\par\bigskip
\textbf{[THE TRIANGLE.]}
\end{center}
\bigskip

The problem of finding the position of a point from the known ratio of its distances from three points whose positions are known.

Coordinates of the given points $\left\{\begin{array}{c}a,a',a''\\b,b',b''\end{array}\right\}$; of the unknown point $\left\{\begin{array}{c}x\\y\end{array}\right\}$; the three distances are in the ratio $m$, $m'$, $m''$.

\textls{First method of solution.}

Set
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
a''-a'=h\cos A,&a-a''=h'\cos A',&a'-a=h''\cos A''\\
b''-b'=h\sin A,&b-b''=h'\sin A',&b'-b=h''\sin A''
\end{array}
\]
\noindent and assume
\[
\begin{aligned}
x&=a+r\cos\varphi\\
y&=b+r\sin\varphi.
\end{aligned}
\]

This gives the equations:
\[
\begin{aligned}
rr-2rh''\cos(\varphi-A'')+h''h''&=\frac{rrm'm'}{mm}\\
rr+2rh'\cos(\varphi-A')+h'h'&=\frac{rrm''m''}{mm}.
\end{aligned}
\]
