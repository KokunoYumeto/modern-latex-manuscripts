\noindent Thus $(a,b,c,d)(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)$ must be distinguished from $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)(a,b,c,d)$.

We then have
\[
(a,b,c,d)(a,-b,-c,-d)=(aa+bb+cc+dd,0,0,0).
\]
Further, denote the combination $(a,b,c,d)$ by a letter, e.~g. $g$, and then the combination $(a,-b,-c,-d)$ by $g'$.

Thus
\[
gg'=g'g=(aa+bb+cc+dd,0,0,0)=(m,0,0,0).
\]
Further, on putting $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=h$, $\alpha\alpha+\beta\beta+\gamma\gamma+\delta\delta=\mu$, we have
\[
ghg'=(\alpha m,-\beta m,-\gamma m,-\delta m)=h'gg'=gg'h'.
\]

One can also regard the scale $a,b,c,d$ as referring to the displacement of the coordinate axes that corresponds to the substitution
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
+a+\dfrac{bb}{n+a},&+d+\dfrac{bc}{n+a},&-c+\dfrac{bd}{n+a},\\[6pt]
-d+\dfrac{bc}{n+a},&+a+\dfrac{cc}{n+a},&+b+\dfrac{cd}{n+a},\\[6pt]
+c+\dfrac{bd}{n+a},&-b+\dfrac{cd}{n+a},&+a+\dfrac{dd}{n+a}
\end{array}
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
n&=\BareRoot{(aa+bb+cc+dd)}&&\text{enlargement,}\\
a&=n\cos\lambda,&\BareRoot{(bb+cc+dd)}&=n\sin\lambda,\\
\lambda&\text{ total rotation.}
\end{aligned}
\]

\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\begin{center}[8.]\end{center}

The transformation scale $a,b,c,d$ signifies\\
an enlargement $=(aa+bb+cc+dd)$\\
together with rotation $=\lambda$ about the point whose coordinates are
\[
\frac ba\operatorname{cotg}\tfrac12\lambda,\qquad
\frac ca\cot\tfrac12\lambda,\qquad
\frac da\operatorname{cotg}\tfrac12\lambda.
\]
