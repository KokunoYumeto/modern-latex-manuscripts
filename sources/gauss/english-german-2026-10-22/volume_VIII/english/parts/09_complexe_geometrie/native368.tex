\begin{center}\bfseries MUTATIONS OF THREE-DIMENSIONAL SPACE.\end{center}
\begin{center}1.\end{center}

For a long time I have used the procedure of expressing every mutation of a space (by which I mean its displacement in another space, together with a general enlargement or reduction of the former while preserving similarity) by four quantities
\[
a,b,c,d
\]
whose aggregate I call the mutation scale. It is also assumed that one point of the moving space always remains motionless in the other space, which is regarded as fixed or absolute. This point may be designated the zero point.
