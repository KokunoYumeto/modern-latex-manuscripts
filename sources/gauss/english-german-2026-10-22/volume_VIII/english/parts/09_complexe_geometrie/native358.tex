\begin{center}
\bfseries ORTHOGRAPHIC PROJECTION OF THE SPHERE.
\end{center}

\begin{center}
How the appearance of an infinitesimal part of the spherical surface changes\\
when the projection plane changes.
\end{center}

Choose as the $x$-axis the intersection of the two projection planes, or rather, as the $y$-axis in the projection plane that remains, a line parallel to the straight line joining the two points of projection.

The question is how the part of the spherical surface that appeared as a circle in the first projection will appear in the second.

Coordinates of the center of this part of the surface (radius $= 1$):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
in & the & first & projection & $x,y,z$\\
,, & ,, & second & ,, & $x',y',z'$.
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The angle of rotation is $= \lambda$.

We have
\[
x' = x
\]
\[
\BareRoot{(1-xx)} = \rho
\]
\[
\BareRoot{(1-x'x')} = \rho
\]
\[
\frac{y}{\rho} = \sin u \qquad \frac{y'}{\rho} = \sin(u+\lambda)
\]
\[
dy' = \frac{z'}{z}\cdot dy - \frac{x\sin\lambda}{z}\cdot dx.
\]
