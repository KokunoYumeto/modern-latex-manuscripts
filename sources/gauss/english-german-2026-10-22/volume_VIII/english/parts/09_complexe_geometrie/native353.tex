\begin{center}[\emph{Lecture on the Theory of Imaginary Quantities.}]\end{center}

\begin{center}\bfseries Projection of the Cube.\end{center}

If a cube is projected onto a plane by dropping perpendiculars from the vertices to the plane, the projection must have the property that the sum of the squares of the numbers representing the projections of any three edges meeting at a vertex is $=0$.

To show this, denote the vertices of one face by $0,1,7,3$ and those of the parallel face by $2,4,5,6$. If $(0,1)$, an edge of the cube, is $=r$, then the three edges that meet at vertex $0$ are each $=r$:

\begin{center}\input{figure353a.tex}\end{center}

\[
(0,1),\qquad (0,2),\qquad (0,3).
\]

Draw two perpendicular axes $AB$ and $AC$, the $x$- and $y$-axes, through the point $0$ or $A$ in the plane of projection. If the complex numbers representing the projections of $1,2,3$ are $A,B,C$, we have

\begin{center}\begin{minipage}{5.2cm}\input{figure353b.tex}\end{minipage}\hspace{.3cm}\begin{minipage}{7.2cm}

\[
r(\cos(1,0,x)+i\cos(1,0,y)) = A
\]
\[
r(\cos(2,0,x)+i\cos(2,0,y)) = B
\]
\[
r(\cos(3,0,x)+i\cos(3,0,y)) = C.
\]
\end{minipage}\end{center}
We also have the equations expressing the conditions
\[
\cos(1,0,x)^2+\cos(2,0,x)^2+\cos(3,0,x)^2 = 1
\]
\[
\cos(1,0,y)^2+\cos(2,0,y)^2+\cos(3,0,y)^2 = 1
\]
and
\[
\begin{gathered}\cos(1,0,x)\cos(1,0,y)+\cos(2,0,x)\cos(2,0,y)\\{}+\cos(3,0,x)\cos(3,0,y)\\{}=\cos(x,0,y)=\cos 90^0=0.\end{gathered}
\]
It follows that we do indeed have
\[
A^2+B^2+C^2=0.
\]
The same follows for the other vertices.

We may wish to choose $A$, $B$, $C$ so that their real parts are integers. To do so, return to the real numbers and recall that if $a^2+b^2=c^2$ is to hold, with $a,b,c$ integers and $p$ and $q$ also integers, we must have
\[
a^2=(p^2-q^2)^2,\qquad b^2=4p^2q^2,\qquad c^2=(p^2+q^2)^2,
\]
hence
\[
a=p^2-q^2,\qquad b=2pq,\qquad c=p^2+q^2.
\]
E.\,g., if $p=2$, $q=1$, then $a=3$, $b=4$, $c=5$ and
\[
5^2=4^2+3^2.
\]
