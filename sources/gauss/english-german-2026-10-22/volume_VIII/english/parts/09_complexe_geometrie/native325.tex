that the above criterion falls short of being completely general in that it tacitly assumes that none of the sines in question is $=0$. To complete it one would have to add the following.

1) If \textls[120]{one} of the three sines is $=0$, e.\,g. the first, while neither of the other two is, then 0 coincides with 1. In that case any value may of course be taken for the direction 01.

2) If \textls[120]{two} are $=0$, the third is of course also $=0$ (since the sum of the three angles is identically $=0$). In this case \textsc{Pothenot}'s problem is either indeterminate or physically impossible. The latter occurs if the cosines of all three angles are $=+1$, the former if two cosines are each $=-1$ and the third is $=+1$ (the combinations $+1,+1,-1$ and $-1,-1,-1$ are evidently impossible because the sum of all three angles is identically $=0$).
