Recording is greatly simplified by numbering the positions 0, 1, 2,
3 and\,so\,on\ and placing the pairs of positions that correspond to a single knot
side by side. In a possible closed track
each pair must consist of an even and an odd number. For\,example,

\begin{center}
No.\ 91 $\left|\begin{array}{c}0.7\\2.5\\4.1\\6.3\end{array}\right\|$
\quad No.\ 48 $\left|\begin{array}{c}0.3\\2.7\\4.1\\6.5\end{array}\right|$
and\,so\,on.
\end{center}

For periods of more than 4 knots, however, this criterion ceases to be
sufficient for possibility. For\,example, \emph{abcadcedbe} or
$\left(\begin{array}{ccccc}0. & 2. & 4. & 6. & 8 \\ 3. & 5. & 7. & 9. & 1\end{array}\right)$
is impossible even though it satisfies the criterion.

Likewise impossible is \emph{abcabdecde} [or]
$\left(\begin{array}{ccccc}0. & 2. & 4. & 6. & 8 \\ 3. & 7. & 1. & 9. & 5\end{array}\right)$.
