The preceding is correct but can be greatly simplified. By
dividing the line, through the above-mentioned cutting of the knots, into a number of
lines returning to themselves that intersect neither themselves nor each other,
one need only state for how many ($\mu$) lines the
infinite region is on the $-$ side and for how many ($\nu$) it is on the
$+$ side. One then has the amplitude

\[
= (\mu-\nu)\,360^0.
\]

This is easy to specify, since one can even give the relation of every point.
Let the lines be $a, b, c, d$
etc.\ und

\[
\frac{\partial(a)}{\partial a} = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial(a)}{\partial b} = \pm 1,
\]

according as $a$ lies on the $+$ or $-$ side of $b$, and likewise for the
others. We then have the following, if the original figure contains the
knot:

\[

&\varepsilon,&

\]

\[
\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} = \varepsilon,
