The proof is, after all, very easy. Let $n$ be the number of knots
and label them, in the order in which they are encountered as one
traverses the curve in a chosen direction, by
$1, 2, 3,\,.\,.\,.\,.\,n$. Since each knot is encountered twice during this traversal,
let $\Omega$ be the sequence of these numbers consisting of $2n$ terms,
writing the sign $+$ whenever one comes to the inner (right)
side of the arm being crossed, and $-$ otherwise. Count the $+$ and
$-$ signs only where the numbers occur for the first time,
thus obtaining $+\alpha$ and $-\beta$ occurrences, respectively. Now express the characters of the part
of the curve immediately before the first knot by $\gamma,
\gamma'$. Then the amplitude of the entire curve is
\[
= (\gamma+\gamma'+\alpha-\beta)\,360^0.
\]

These arrangements are, however, subject to certain conditions,
so that an arrangement picked out of thin air need not be possible. Each
knot must occur once in an even and once in an odd
position. Between the two positions the sum of all the
$+\alpha, -\beta$ must be zero. This, however, does not suffice to
show that the scheme

\[
\begin{array}{@{}*{16}{c}@{}}
a&&b&&c&&b&&a&.&b&&c&&b&\\
&1&&2&&3&&1&&4&&3&&2&&4\\
&+&&-&&+&&-&&+&&-&&+&&-
\end{array}
\]

is impossible. Here the signs of 2 and 3 must necessarily be
