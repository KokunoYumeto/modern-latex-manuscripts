[ON THE THEORY OF PARALLELS.]

[\textsc{Schumacher} \emph{to} \textsc{Gauss}.\quad \emph{Copenhagen, 3 May 1831.}]
\end{center}

\{\mbox{.\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .} I take the liberty of sending you an enclosed attempt to prove, without using parallel lines or their theory, that the sum of all three angles of a rectilinear triangle is $= 180^{0}$. The proof of Euclid's axiom would then follow from this proposition. I assume only that the sum of all the angles about a point is $= 360^{0} = 4R$ and that alternate angles are equal.

I know from experience how strangely blind one can sometimes be (I at least) about one's own work, so I very much fear that it rests on a petitio principii. I cannot find it at present, however, and look to you to enlighten me.
