\begingroup\small
The paper in Russian mentioned in the letter to \textsc{Encke} is
very probably the one published in the first issue for 1836 of the Learned Writings of Kazan,
\gausscyr{Примѣненіе воображаемой геометріи къ нѣкоторымъ
интеграламъ} (Application of imaginary geometry to some integrals). An offprint of it
with its own title page is preserved among \textsc{Gauss}'s papers.
Several marginal remarks and a slip enclosed in it containing note
[2] show that \textsc{Gauss} did in fact read some of this work.


The short work in German is the \emph{Geometrischen Untersuchungen
zur Theorie der Parallellinien}, Berlin 1840, reviewed in \textsc{Gersdorf}'s Repertorium
der gesammten deutschen Literatur, 1840, vol. 3, p. 147.
This review seems to have drawn \textsc{Gauss}'s attention to it.


Most likely, \textsc{Knorre} should read \textsc{Knorr}. The physicist \textsc{Ernst Knorr},
a close friend of \textsc{Lobachevsky}, was a professor in Kazan from 1832
to 1846 and made an extended journey to Western Europe in 1840,
while the astronomer \textsc{K. F. Knorre} in Nikolayev had no connection with
\textsc{Lobachevsky} and had been in Germany only once before 1841, without meeting \textsc{Gauss} in
Göttingen.


A simple derivation of the formula for the element $dP$ of a pyramid had been given by
\textsc{Lobachevsky} in 1830 in the paper \gausscyr{О началахъ геометріи} (On
the principles of geometry) (Eq. 62). He writes

\[
dP = \tfrac{1}{2}\, d\psi\,(r\cos\varphi - h).
\]


Indeed, in non-Euclidean geometry $r\cos\varphi$ is greater than $h$
or $x$. He does not explicitly single out the formula for the volume $ABCabc$,
which \textsc{Gauss} appears to have had as early as 1832
(see p. 229 of this volume). It can also be expressed by saying
that the volume $ABCabc$ equals half the product of the baseline
$BC$ and the area of the infinitely small quadrilateral $ADda$. \begin{flushright}\textsc{Stäckel.}\end{flushright}
