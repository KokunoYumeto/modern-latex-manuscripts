7. \emph{Theorem.} Two parallel lines, extended backwards, cannot intersect on that side.

\begin{center}

\end{center}

\textls{Proof.} Suppose $AM\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$, $BN\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ intersect on their backward extensions at $P$. Let $Q$ be any point beyond $P$ on the backward extension of $BN\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$. Join $QA$. Extended further, this straight line will intersect $PN$ at a point $R$. We then have two different straight lines through the points $Q$, $R$, which is absurd.
