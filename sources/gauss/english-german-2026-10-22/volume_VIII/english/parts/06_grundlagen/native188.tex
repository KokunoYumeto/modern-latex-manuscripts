If this constant for \textls{us} is half the Earth's axis (so that every line drawn in space from one fixed star to another, the two being $90^{0}$ apart, would be a tangent to the \textls{terrestrial globe}), then it is infinitely large in relation to the spaces encountered in daily life.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
Euclidean geometry holds only on the assumption that the constant is infinitely large. Only then is it true that the three angles of every triangle equal two right angles. This can readily be proved once one grants the proposition that the constant is infinitely large.

.\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .\ Professor \textsc{Schweikart}'s note gave me immense pleasure, and I ask you to give him my warmest compliments on it. Almost all of it expresses my own thoughts. There is just one point, beginning as follows, on which I have comments.

If this constant for \textls{us} is half the Earth's axis, and\,so\,on, I must make three remarks.

1) I do not see how a constant could hold only for us and another for other beings. I do not know whether Mr \textsc{Schw.} meant it this way either, though he himself underlined \textls{for us}.
