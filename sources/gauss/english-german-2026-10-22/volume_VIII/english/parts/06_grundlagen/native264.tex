\frac{d\,dy}{dx^{2}} = -\alpha\alpha\,\frac{1-\dfrac{dy^{2}}{\alpha\alpha\,dx^{2}}}{y},

\frac{y\,ddy}{dx^{2}} - \frac{dy^{2}}{dx^{2}} + \alpha\alpha = 0.

\noindent Multiplying by $\dfrac{2\,dy}{y^{3}}$:

\[
\frac{2\dfrac{dy}{dx}\cdot d\dfrac{dy}{dx}}{yy} - \frac{2\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2}dy}{y^{3}} + \frac{2\alpha\alpha\,dy}{y^{3}} = 0.
\]

\noindent Integrating

\[
\frac{1}{yy}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2} - \frac{\alpha\alpha}{yy} = \text{Const.} = -kk,
\]
\[
-kkyy + \alpha\alpha - \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^{2} = 0,
\]
\[
dx = \frac{dy}{\BareRoot{(\alpha\alpha-kkyy)}} = \frac{\dfrac{k}{\alpha}dy}{k\BareRoot{\left(1-\dfrac{kk}{\alpha\alpha}yy\right)}},
\]
\[
kx = \text{Arc.}\sin\frac{k}{\alpha}y + \text{Const.},
\]
\[
y = \frac{\alpha}{k}\sin(kx+\lambda),
\]
\[
fx = \frac{\alpha}{k}\sin kx,
\]
\[
gx = \cos kx.

\noindent \textsc{Gauss} wrote the preceding note on a slip of paper (4 pages, 8\textsuperscript{0}) found as an insert in his copy of \emph{»Geometrischen Untersuchungen zur Theorie der Parallellinien} by Nicolaus \textsc{Lobatschewsky}, Berlin 1840«. It may therefore be assumed to have been written between 1840 and 1846.

The purpose of the note is to derive the equations relating the sides and angles of a finite triangle, assuming that Euclidean geometry holds for triangles whose three sides are infinitesimal. It suffices to derive these relations for a right triangle. On the basis of the preceding text, the argument proceeds as follows.
