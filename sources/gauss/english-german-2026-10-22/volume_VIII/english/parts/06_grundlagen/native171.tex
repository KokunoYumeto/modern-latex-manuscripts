\smallskip

The preceding note is found on the last page of a notebook entitled “Mathematische Brouillons. October 1805.” Although its date of composition cannot be determined exactly, its contents place it at the beginning of \textsc{Gauss}'s investigations into the first principles of geometry.

The proof sketched in 3) rests on the following lemma. On one arm of an angle of magnitude
\[
\frac{90^{0}}{2^n}
\]
lay off the segment $2^n.l$ from the vertex. The perpendicular dropped from the endpoint of this segment to the other arm is longer than $l$.

\begin{center}\input{figure171.tex}\end{center}

Let $BAC$ be a right angle and let $AB = AC = l$. Then $BC > l$. If we bisect the angle $BAC$ by $AF$ and make $AD = AE = 2l$, then $DE > 2l$. Consequently, for the perpendicular dropped from $D$ to $AF$ we have $DF > l$.

If we now lay off on the bisector $AF$ of the right angle $BAC$ the segment $AG = 2l$, then it follows all the more that $DG > l$. If we bisect the angle $DAG$ by $AK$ and make $AH = AJ = 4l$, then $HJ > 2l$. Consequently, for the perpendicular dropped from $H$ to $AK$ we have $HK > l$.

Carrying out this construction $n$ times in succession establishes the assertion above.
