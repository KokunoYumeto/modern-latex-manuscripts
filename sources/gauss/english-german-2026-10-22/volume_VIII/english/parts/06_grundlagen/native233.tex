\begin{center}\Large\textsc{On Astral Geometry.}\end{center}
\bigskip
\begin{center}[I.]\end{center}
The main steps in proving that the sum of the angles of a triangle differs from $180^{0}$ by an amount proportional to its area are as follows.
\begin{center}\input{figure233a.tex}\hspace{1em}\input{figure233b.tex}\end{center}
Area of the infinite triangle $1=T$.

Area of the infinite triangle $2=\varphi A$.

Then we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
1.\quad &\varphi A+\varphi(180^{0}-A)=T,\\
2.\quad &\varphi B+\varphi C+\varphi(180^{0}-B-C)=T,
\end{aligned}
\]
both by construction.

Thus, on setting $A=B+C$, we obtain
\[
3.\quad\varphi B+\varphi C=\varphi(B+C),
\]
hence $\varphi t=at$, where $a$ is a constant factor and
\[
a.180^{0}=T.
\]
\Needspace{25\baselineskip}
\begin{center}\input{figure233c.tex}\end{center}
Further, if $A$, $B$, $C$ are the three angles of a triangle with area $=Q$, then by construction we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
T&=Q+\varphi A+\varphi B+\varphi C\\
&=Q+a(A+B+C),
\end{aligned}
\]
from which the desired result follows at once.
