\noindent 1. If $d\omega$ is an infinitesimal central angle $ACB$ of a circle of radius $AC=BC=r$, the corresponding infinitesimal arc $AB=ds$ is proportional to $d\omega$, and the factor of proportionality depends only on $r$. Hence:

\[
\tag*{(A)} ds = f(r)\,d\omega.
\]

\noindent The chord $AB$ can be replaced by the arc $AB$ up to infinitesimals of the second order.

\noindent In the triangle $PQR$, if the angle at $Q$ is right and the angle at $P$ is infinitesimal, we have

\[
\tag*{(A')} QR = f(PR)\,.\,QPR,
\]

\noindent for if $RQ$ is extended by $QR' = RQ$, then by (A)

\[
RR' = f(PR)\,.\,RPR'.
\]

\Needspace{30\baselineskip}\noindent 2. Let $ABCD$ be a quadrilateral with infinitesimal base $AB=du$. Let the angles at $A$ and $B$ be right angles and let $AD=BC=y$. Then the length of the fourth side $CD=du'$ is proportional to $du$, and the factor of proportionality depends only on $y$. Hence:

\[
\tag*{(B)} du' = g(y)\,du.
\]

\Needspace{37\baselineskip}\noindent 3. In the right triangle $ABC$ with legs $a,b$ and hypotenuse $c$, let $b$ increase by an infinitesimal segment $CC'=db$ while the angle $A$ remains unchanged. Let the perpendicular $C'B'$, drawn at $C'$ to $AC'$, meet $AB$ at $B'$. This gives a right triangle $AB'C'$ with legs $a+da, b+db$ and hypotenuse $c+dc$. If on $C'B'$ we mark off \ $C'D=a$, then by (B):

\[
BD = g(a)\,.\,db.
\]

\noindent On the other hand, Euclidean geometry holds in the infinitesimal triangle $BB'D$ and the angles at $D$ and $B'$ differ only infinitesimally from a right angle and $B$ respectively. Therefore:

\[
BD = \sin B\,.\,dc,
\]

\noindent hence we have:

\[
\tag*{1)} g(a)\,.\,db = \sin B\,.\,dc.
\]
