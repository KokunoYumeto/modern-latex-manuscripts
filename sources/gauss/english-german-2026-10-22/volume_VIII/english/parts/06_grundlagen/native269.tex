$PS$ at $S'$, and on $R'S'$ lay off the segment $R'T = RS$.

Since the angles of the infinitesimal quadrilateral $RR'TS$ may be regarded as
right angles, (A') gives
\[
\begin{aligned}TS' &= f(SS') \,.\, S'ST\\ &= f(SS') \,.\, (90^{0} - PSR)\end{aligned}
\]
or
\[
R'S' - RS = f(SS') \,.\, (90^{0} - PSR)
\]
or, by (D),
\[
\tag*{8)} R'S' - RS = -f(RR') \,.\, \frac{1 - g(r)^2}{f(r)} \,.\, PQ.
\]
Now denote $QR$ by $x$ and $RR'$ by $dx$. Then by (B) we have
\[
RS = g(x) \,.\, PQ,
\]
hence
\[
\tag*{9)} R'S' - RS = dg(x) \,.\, PQ,
\]
and comparison of the two values of $R'S' - RS$ gives the relation
\[
\tag*{(E)} dg(x) = -f(dx) \,.\, \frac{1 - g(x)^2}{f(x)}.
\]
