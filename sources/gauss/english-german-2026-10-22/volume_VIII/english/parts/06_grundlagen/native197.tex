[ON THE ANGLES OF THE TRIANGLE.]
\end{center}

\begin{center}
[1.]
\end{center}

The proof that the sum of the three angles of a triangle cannot be greater
than $180^{0}$ can be given independently of the 11th axiom as follows.



\begin{center}
\input{figure197.tex}
\end{center}

Let $A+B+C>180^{0}$. Extend $AB$ indefinitely and repeat the
preceding triangle. Then, by hypothesis,
\[
CBE < ACB \quad \text{thus (Elements I. 24)} \quad CE < AB.
\]
Likewise $EG=CE$ and\,so\,on. It is readily deduced that if the
triangle is repeated sufficiently often, the straight [line] $AM$ is greater
than the broken line $ACEG\mathinner{.\,.\,.\,.}NM$, and the contradiction is easily
shown. Repeating it $n$ times suffices if
\[AC+CB-AB < n(AB-CE).\]
