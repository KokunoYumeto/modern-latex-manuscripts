\begin{center}[CORRESPONDING POINTS ON PARALLEL LINES.]\end{center}

1. \emph{\textls{Definition.}} \textls{Corresponding points on parallel lines}, based on the equal angles at the joining line.

2. If $A$, $B$ are corresponding points and $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$,

4. \emph{Theorem.} If $A$, $B$ are corresponding points on the parallels $1$, $2$, and $A'$, $B'$ likewise, then $AA' = BB'$, and conversely.

\Needspace{16\baselineskip}
5. \emph{Theorem.} If $A$, $B$, $C$ are points on the parallels $1$, $2$, $3$, with $A$ corresponding to $B$ and $B$ to $C$, then $A$ also corresponds to $C$.

\textls{Proof.} In the contrary case, suppose the angle $C > A$. Take

\begin{center}
\input{figure214b.tex}
\end{center}
\noindent $ACM = A$. Then $CM$ will intersect $AN$ at $N$. Thus $AN = CN$. But by \emph{Th.Th.}, $AN < BN$ and $BN < CN$, which is a contradiction.

If one were to assume that $A = B$ and $A = C$, and if $B$ were not $= C$, let $B = C'$, from which $A = C'$ would follow.
