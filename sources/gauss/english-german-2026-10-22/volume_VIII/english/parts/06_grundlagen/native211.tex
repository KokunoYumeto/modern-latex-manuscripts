We now have to distinguish two cases.

I. If $AC\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ intersects the line $1$ at $D$, make $BE = BD$ by taking

\noindent $E$ on $1$ on the opposite side from $D$. Through $D$ draw between $1$ and $DA$ the straight line $DF\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ such that $ADF = AED$. This line will therefore intersect $2$ at $G$. Make [on $1$] $EH = DG$ and join $AH$. The triangles $ABD$, $ABE$ will be congruent, so $AE = AD$. Consequently the triangles $ADG$, $AEH$ are also congruent, so $EAH = DAG$. [Hence] $GAH = DAE = (2,3)$, [i.\,e.] $AH$ is identical to $3$ or $3$ intersects $1$ at $H$. Thus, since $3$ can be any straight line lying between $2$ and $AB$, $2$ is parallel to $1$.
