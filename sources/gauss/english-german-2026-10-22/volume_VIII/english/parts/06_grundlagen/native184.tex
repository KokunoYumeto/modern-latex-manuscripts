\begin{center}\textsc{Gauss} to \textsc{Olbers}. Göttingen, 28 April 1817.\end{center}

.\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .\ \textsc{Wachter} has had a small pamphlet printed on the
first principles of geometry. You will probably receive a copy through \textsc{Lindenau}.
Although W. has penetrated more deeply into the nature of the matter
than his predecessors, his proof is no more conclusive
than any of the others. I am increasingly convinced that the
necessity of our geometry cannot be proved, at least
not \textls{by} the \textbf{\textls{human}} understanding or \textls{for} the human understanding.
Perhaps in another life we shall reach other insights into the
nature of space that are inaccessible to us now. Until then, geometry ought
to be placed on the same footing as something like mechanics instead of arithmetic, which
stands on purely a priori grounds. .\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .
