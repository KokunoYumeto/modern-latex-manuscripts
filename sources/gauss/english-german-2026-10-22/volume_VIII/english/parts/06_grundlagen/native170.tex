1) A line parallel to a straight line is one whose
perpendiculars to the latter are everywhere of equal length.

2) It remains undecided whether the parallel line itself is straight.

3) $AB$ straight, [$AC$ perpendicular to $AB$,] $CD$ parallel to it.
The straight line $AE$, at the given angle $BAE$, will, if extended far enough,
certainly intersect $CD$. The proof is to proceed as follows.

\begin{center}\input{figure170a.tex}\end{center}

Let
\[
2^n > \frac{90^{0}}{BAE}, \qquad BAF = \frac{90^{0}}{2^n}, \qquad
AF \EqualsOverGreater{} 2^n AC, 
\]
then $F$ certainly lies beyond $CD$, so $AF$ intersects $CD$ and
$AE$ must all the more certainly intersect $CD$.

4) $BE$ and $AC$ perpendicular to $AB$, $CD$ parallel, $CE$ straight.
[$ACE$ a right angle.] If $BEC$ is \textls{obtuse}, then $BE < AC$. In that case
$CE$ lies entirely below $CD$.
