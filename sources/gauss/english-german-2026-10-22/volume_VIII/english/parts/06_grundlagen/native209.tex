PARALLEL LINES.
\end{center}

1. If the straight lines $AM\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$, $BN\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ do not intersect, while every straight line drawn through $A$ between $AM\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ and $AB\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ intersects $BN\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$, then $AM\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ is called parallel to $BN\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$.

2. Suppose a straight line always passes through the point $A$ and rotates from the position $AB\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ on the side on which $BN$ lies, until it reaches the position $AC\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$ opposite the first. At first it intersects and at the end it does not. Surely there must therefore be one and only one position separating the intersecting and nonintersecting [straight lines]. It will be the first nonintersecting line, hence by our definition the parallel $AM\mathinner{.\,.\,.}$, since there can evidently be no last intersecting line.
