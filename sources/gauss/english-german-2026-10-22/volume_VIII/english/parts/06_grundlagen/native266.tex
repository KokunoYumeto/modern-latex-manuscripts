4. In the right triangle $ABC$, let the angle $A$ increase by $BAB' = dA$
while $AB' = c$ remains fixed. Dropping a perpendicular from $B'$ onto $AB$
gives $B'C'$ and forms the right triangle $AB'C'$ with legs $a+da$, $b+db$ and
hypotenuse $c$. Here $db$ is negative. If on $B'C'$ we mark off $C'D = a$,
the length of $BD$ is:
\[
BD = -g(a) \,.\, db.
\]
On the other hand, in the infinitesimal triangle $BB'D$ we have:
\[
B'D = da
\]
and by (A):
\[
BB' = f(c) \,.\, dA,
\]
while the angles at $D$ and $B'$ differ only infinitesimally from a right
angle and $90^{0} - B$ respectively. Hence
\[
BD = \cos B\, f(c) \,.\, dA,
\]
and we have
\[
\tag*{2)} g(a) \,.\, db = -\cos B\, f(c) \,.\, dA.
\]

5. Combining 1) and 2) shows that in passing from triangle $a, b, c; A, B$ to triangle
$a+da, b+db, c+dc; A+dA, B+dB$ the quantities $db, dc$ and $dA$ satisfy the relation
\[
\tag*{I.} g(a) \,.\, db - \sin B \,.\, dc + f(c) \cos B \,.\, dA = 0
\]
Since we may simultaneously interchange $a$ with $b$ and $A$ with
$B$, the following equation also holds:
\[
\tag*{II.} g(b) \,.\, da - \sin A \,.\, dc + f(c) \cos A \,.\, dB = 0.
\]

6. In the right triangle $ABC$, let $A$ increase by $BAB' = dA$. Extending
$BC$ until it meets $AB'$ gives the right triangle $ACB'$ with legs $a+da$
and $b$. If on $AB'$ we mark off $AD = c$, then in the infinitesimal triangle
$BB'D$ we have:
\[
BB' = da, \qquad BD = f(c) \,.\, dA,
\]
hence
\[
\tag*{3)} f(c) \,.\, dA = \sin B \,.\, da.
\]

7. Again let $A$ increase by $BAB' = dA$, keeping the leg $B'C' = a$ fixed.
Extending $AB'$ until $AD = c$ gives the infinitesimal triangle $BB'D$ with
\[
BD = f(c) \,.\, dA
\]
and
\[
BB' = -g(a) \,.\, db,
\]
hence:
\[
\tag*{4)} f(c) \,.\, dA = -\cos B\, g(a) \,.\, db.
\]
