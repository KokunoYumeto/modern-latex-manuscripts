We want to make a particular line 1\,2 horizontal and finally prove that the operations we carry out achieve this. Call the direction of 1\,2 relative to $H$[orizon] $v$.

We must therefore prove that $v=0$ in the final position. If we consider the source of certainty, we find that it rests on the two preceding values being opposite.

Why were they opposite? Because another line, which had the same inclination to $H$ on both occasions, coincided with it in opposite positions. This other line is 3\,6. Call its inclination to $H$[orizon] $u$. We must therefore prove that $u$ was the same in the experiments. How was this recognized? By the identical position of the spirit level.

The position of the spirit level must therefore determine $u$. How is this proved? First ask whether it is true. It is clear that it is strictly true only for an adjusted spirit level.

We therefore seek an intermediate quantity that must be 0 when the spirit level really is adjusted. This is the inclination of $G$ to $P$, called $i$. If we call the inclination of $G$ to $H$[orizon] $g$, it is clear that the position of the spirit level depends on $g$, and that the equation
\[
g = i + u
\]
