\textbf{[ON THE SUM OF THE EXTERIOR ANGLES OF A POLYGON.]}

\medskip
\textsc{Gauss} to \textsc{Gerling}. \quad Göttingen, 2 October 1846.
\end{center}

\mbox{.\,.\,.\,.\,.\,.} The theorem Mr \textsc{Schweikart} mentioned to you, that in every geometry the sum of all the exterior angles of a polygon differs from $360^0$ by an amount (it is smaller than $360^0$ in astral geometry as \textsc{Schw.} understood it) proportional to the area, is the first theorem that stands, as it were, at the threshold of the theory. I recognized its necessity as early as 1794. I have no time for further remarks on this occasion.

\begin{center}\textsc{Remark.}\end{center}
