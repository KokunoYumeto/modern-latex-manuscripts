To make Euclid's proof of I. 7 rigorous with the plane defined only as
»the surface which, from the rotation of a straight line about an axis
with which it makes right angles, [is produced]«, one must bisect $CD$ in figures 2 and 3
at $M$ and pass through $M$ a plane to which $CD$ is normal.
\emph{In} this plane lie $A$ and $B$, while $C$ and $D$ lie outside it. Thus
one need only prove that no point of $AD$ or $BC$ ($AD$ in
figure 2 being extended indefinitely) lies in the plane. This presents no difficulty.
It then follows immediately that the assumption is absurd as soon as $AD$ and
$BC$ intersect.
