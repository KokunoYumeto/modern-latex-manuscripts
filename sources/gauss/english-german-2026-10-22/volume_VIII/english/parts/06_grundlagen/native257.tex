\begin{center}
{\Large\textsc{Theorem from Spherology.}}
\end{center}

\begin{center}\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}\centering\input{figure257.tex}\end{minipage}\end{center}

[1.]

If $ABCD$ is a spherical quadrilateral with right angles at $A$, $B$, $C$, and if one extends $DA$, $CB$ until $DE$ and $CF$ become quadrants, then $E$ is a right angle and [, with the arcs reckoned as angles, so that a quadrant comes out $= 90^{0}$,] $F = 90^{0} + CD$, $EF = D - 90^{0}$.

\textls{Proof}. The arc $DF$ is a quadrant, from which everything follows at once.

[2.]

We denote by

\begin{quote}
$R$ the right angle, by\\
$\rho$ the quadrant of a great circle [, so that the arc $x$ is to be reckoned as $\dfrac{R}{\rho}x$ degrees], by\\
$\lambda$ one eighth of the surface of the sphere. Further, by\\
$F(a,b)$ the area of a spherical quadrilateral $[ACBD]$ with three right angles $[A, B, C]$, with the middle one $[C]$ adjacent to the sides $a, b$. Let the sides opposite these be $\alpha, \beta$ and the fourth angle $= R + \omega$.
\end{quote}
