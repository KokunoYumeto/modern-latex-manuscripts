\mbox{.\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .\ .} As for parallel lines, I would very gladly have given you my judgment in reply to your very first letter, had I not had to assume that it would be of little use to you without a full exposition. To be truly convincing, however, such a full exposition might require an explanation running to several sheets
in response to what you have in effect only indicated in a few lines. At present I lack the peace of mind needed for such explanations. To show you my good will, however, I will set down the following.

You apply the main point at once to every triangle. But you would essentially use the same reasoning if you first took up the simplest case and stated the proposition:

1) In every triangle in which one side is finite and the second and hence also the third are infinite, the sum of the two angles at the finite side is $=180^0$.

Proof in your manner:

The circular arc $CD$ measures the angle $CAD$ just as well as $CBD$, since in a circle of infinite radius a finite displacement of the center is to be counted as $0$. Hence

\[ CAD = CBD, \qquad CAD + CBA = CBD + CBA = 180^0. \]
