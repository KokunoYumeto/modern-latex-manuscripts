8. Combining 3) and 4) gives the relation:
\[
\tag*{III.} \sin B \,.\, da - \cos B\, g(a) \,.\, db - f(c) \,.\, dA = 0,
\]
Simultaneously interchanging $a$ with $b$ and $A$ with $B$ yields:
\[
\tag*{IV.} \cos A \,.\, db - \cos A\, g(b) \,.\, da - f(c) \,.\, dB = 0
\]

9. Eliminating $dA$ from I. and III. gives the equation:
\[
\cos B \,.\, da + g(a) \sin B \,.\, db - dc = 0.
\]
Similarly,
\[
\cos A \,.\, db + g(b) \sin A \,.\, da - dc = 0,
\]
so we must have
\[
(\cos B - g(b) \sin A)\, da + (\cos A - g(a) \sin B)\, db = 0
\]
Since $da$ and $db$ are independent of one another, this is possible only
if both
\[
\tag*{V. 1)} \cos A = g(a) \sin B,
\]
\[
\tag*{V. 2)} \cos B = g(b) \sin A
\]
hold. These equations give the relation:
\[
\tag*{V.} \cos B \,.\, da + \cos A \,.\, db - dc = 0.
\]

10. If we proceed as in No. 3, then in the infinitesimal triangle $BB'D$
(Fig. 4) we have:
\[
\tag*{5)} da = \cos B \,.\, dc,
\]
while $dA = 0$. If we proceed as in No. 4, then in the infinitesimal triangle
$BB'D$ (Fig. 5) we have:
\[
\tag*{6)} da = \sin B\, f(c) \,.\, dA,
\]
while $dc = 0$. Thus combining 5) and 6) gives the general formula:
\[
\tag*{VI.} da - \cos B \,.\, dc - \sin B\, f(c) \,.\, dA = 0,
\]
and correspondingly:
\[
\tag*{VII.} db - \cos A \,.\, dc - \sin A\, f(c) \,.\, dB = 0.
\]

11. Extend $CA$ by $AA' = db$ and at $A'$ construct on $CA'$ the angle
$A$, whose other ray meets the extension of the leg $CB$ at $B'$,
so that $BB' = da$. From $A$ drop onto $A'B'$ the perpendicular $AD$
and on $A'B'$ in the direction of $B'$ mark off $DE = c$. Then $A'D = \cos A \,.\, db$.
By Fig. 8 and equation V. we have
\[
\begin{aligned}dc &= A'D + EB'\\ &= \cos B \,.\, da + \cos A \,.\, db\end{aligned}
\]
so $EB' = \cos B \,.\, da$ and hence $BE = \sin B \,.\, da$. The quadrilateral $ADEB$ has right angles at
$A$ and $D$, while $AB = DE = c$. Hence by (A):
\[
BE = g(c) \,.\, AD
\]
or
\[
\sin B \,.\, da = g(c) \sin A \,.\, db.
\]
