V. Theorem. In general we have $f\varphi+f\psi+f(180^{0}-\varphi-\psi)=t$.

The proof is readily apparent from the figure, in which the three parts of the region $\underline{1,\,2,\,3}$ have the values
\[
\begin{aligned}
\underline{1}&=f(180^{0}-\varphi-\psi),\\
\underline{2}&=f\varphi,\\
\underline{3}&=f\psi
\end{aligned}
\]
and their sum is $=t$.

VI. Corollary. Thus we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
f\varphi+f\psi&=t-f(180^{0}-\varphi-\psi)\\
&=f(\varphi+\psi),
\end{aligned}
\]
from which we readily obtain
\[
\frac{f\varphi}{\varphi}=\mathit{Constans},
\]
where the further equality
\[
=\frac{t}{180^{0}}
\]
gives the value of the constant.

VII. Theorem. The area of a triangle whose angles are $A$, $B$, $C$ is
\[
=\frac{180^{0}-(A+B+C)}{180^{0}}\times t.
\]
The figure gives the proof. For we have
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{the area}\quad\alpha&=fA=\frac{A}{180^{0}}\cdot t,\\
\beta&=fB=\frac{B}{180^{0}}\cdot t,\\
\gamma&=fC=\frac{C}{180^{0}}\cdot t,
\end{aligned}
\]
\[
\begin{aligned}
t&=\alpha+\beta+\gamma+Z\\
&=\frac{A+B+C}{180^{0}}\cdot t+Z.
\end{aligned}
\]
