\begin{center}

The passage you mention in \textsc{Zach}'s A.\,G.\,E. is well known to me. The application of the method of least squares mentioned there concerns an excerpt printed earlier in the same journal from \textsc{Ulugh Beigh}'s table of the equation of time, which had led to some quite curious results. I had communicated these results to \textsc{Zach}, remarking that in doing so I had used a method of my own, which I had employed for years, to combine quantities affected by accidental errors in a consistent manner free of arbitrariness, without, however, telling him the nature of the method itself. I believe I have already written to you that under no circumstances shall I call attention to this passage, in which the method was first publicly hinted at, nor do I wish any of my friends to do so with my consent. That would be \textls{tantamount to admitting that} my statement (Theoria Motus Corporum Coelestium) that I had often used this method since 1794 stood in need of justification, and I will never consent to that. When \textsc{Olbers} attested\HistoricalNote{[*)]}{[*)}{In his paper Über den veränderlichen Stern im Halse des Schwans; v.\,\textsc{Lindenau} and \textsc{Bohnenberger}, Zeitschrift für Astronomie. Volume II. Page 192. September-October 1816.]} that I had communicated the entire method to him in 1802\HistoricalNote{[**)]}{[**)}{Should read »1803«.]}, he meant well, but had he asked me beforehand, I would have strongly disapproved.
