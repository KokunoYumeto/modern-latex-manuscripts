[V.]

{\large [MINOR CONTRIBUTIONS\\
TO THE METHOD OF LEAST SQUARES.]}
\end{center}



Let $A,\ B,\ B',\ \ldots\ B^{(n-1)}$ be linear functions of $x$ and other unknown quantities.
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
A&=\alpha x+\cdots\\
B&=\beta x+\cdots\\
B'&=\beta' x+\cdots\\
.&\hphantom{=} .\\[-.6em]
.&\hphantom{=} .\\
B^{(n-1)}&=\beta^{(n-1)}x+\cdots.
\end{array}
\]
The quantity
\[
\Omega = (\lambda A)^2+(\mu B)^2+(\mu' B')^2+(\mu'' B'')^2+\cdots
\]
is to be made a minimum.

Set
\[
\frac{\alpha}{\beta}=l,\qquad \frac{\mu}{\lambda l}=\mathrm{tang}\,\varphi,\qquad
\frac{\mu\,\mathrm{tang}\,\varphi}{\lambda}=k,
\]
\[
A+kB=A',\qquad A-lB=C,
\]
\[
\lambda\cos\varphi=\lambda',\qquad \lambda\sin\varphi=\nu,
\]
Then
\[
(\lambda' A')^2+(\nu C)^2=(\lambda A)^2+(\mu B)^2,
\]
and $C$ is independent of $x$.

The coefficient of $x$ in $A'$ becomes $=\dfrac{\alpha}{\cos\varphi^2}$.
