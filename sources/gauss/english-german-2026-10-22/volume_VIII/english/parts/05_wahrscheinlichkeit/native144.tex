\{I believe I have already told you that \textsc{Zach} had a letter of yours printed in the Geographische Ephemeriden (1799, October.\ p.\,378) in which you plainly mention the method of least squares, which you had therefore already communicated to \textsc{Zach} at that time. You refer to the French meridian-arc measurement:

\begin{quote}
»I discovered this error by applying my method, of which I \textls{have given you a sample}« and\,so\,on.
\end{quote}

\textsc{Zach} remarks there »More on this another time«, but that other occasion never came. Since you give the results of your calculation, it seems to me easy to show that they were obtained by the method of least squares. Moreover, \textsc{Zach} is still alive and has surely kept your letter. Would you not find it worth the trouble to settle the matter once and for all beyond dispute, even in the face of the Frenchmen's \textls{polite} doubts, which I find more repugnant than any other?\}
