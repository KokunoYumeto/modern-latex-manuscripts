\bigskip

\bigskip

There are $p$ positions among which $m$ objects are distributed
entirely at random. For each object, each position is assumed to have
the same probability.

We denote the probability that the number of positions thus occupied is $= m-n$
by $(m,n)$ and set $\dfrac{1}{p}=x$.


Then we have

\[
\begin{array}{l}
(2,0) = 1-x \\
(2,1) = x \\
(3,0) = (1-x)(1-2x) \\
(3,1) = 3x(1-x) \\
(3,2) = xx \\
(4,0) = (1-x)(1-2x)(1-3x) \\
(4,1) = 6x(1-x)(1-2x) \\
(4,2) = 7xx(1-x) \\
(4,3) = x^3 \\
(5,0) = (1-x)(1-2x)(1-3x)(1-4x) \\
(5,1) = 10x(1-x)(1-2x)(1-3x) \\
(5,2) = 25xx(1-x)(1-2x) \\
(5,3) = 15x^3(1-x) \\
(5,4) = x^4.
\end{array}
\]
