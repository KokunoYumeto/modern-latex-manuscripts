\section*{Preliminary note}

Let the positive ternary quadratic form
\[
  axx+byy+czz+2dyz+2bzx+2cxy
\]
(whose determinant is $=-D$) be reduced in Seeber's sense; by $a',b',c'$ are
denoted those coefficients which occur in the preliminary equations ($\dots$) as
$2a', 2b', 2c'$.

\section*{I. Case: none of the numbers $a',b',c'$ is negative}

Put
\[
  b-2a'=d,\quad c-2b'=e,\quad a-2c'=f,
\]
\[
  c+2a'=g,\quad a+2b'=h,\quad b+2c'=i,
\]
\emph{where, from the definition of reduced positive forms, it follows at once}
that if $axx+byy+czz+2dyz+2bzx+2cxy$ is such a form, none of those six
numbers is negative. Now if the (negative) determinant of the form is denoted by
$-D$, then, as is easily verified by expansion, one has the identity
\[
\boxed{\;2D-abc \;=\; a\,a'\,d+b\,b'\,e+c\,c'\,f+a'\,h\,i+b'\,g\,i+c'\,g\,h+g\,h\,i\;}
\]
in which none of the seven terms on the right can be negative, and consequently
$abc$ is not greater than $2D$. The same follows in the same way from the
identity
\[
\boxed{\;2D-abc \;=\; a\,a'\,g+b\,b'\,h+c\,c'\,i+a'\,e\,f+b'\,d\,f+c'\,d\,e+d\,e\,f\;}
\]

\section*{II. Case: none of the numbers $a',b',c'$ is positive}

Put
\[
  b+2a'=d,\quad c+2b'=e,\quad a+2c'=f,
\]
\[
  c+2a'=g,\quad a+2b'=h,\quad b+2c'=i,
\]
\[
  b+c+2a'+2b'+2c'=k,
\]
\[
  a+c+2a'+2b'+2c'=l,
\]
\[
  a+b+2a'+2b'+2c'=m,
\]
and let the determinant of the form be, as before, $=-D$. By virtue of the
definition of reduced positive forms, none of the nine numbers
$d,e,f,g,h,i,k,l,m$ can be negative; and so from the identity
\begin{multline*}
6D-3abc \;=\; -a\,a'(d+2k)-b\,b'(e+2l)-c\,c'(f+2m)\\
{}-a'\,h\,i-b'\,g\,i-c'\,g\,h+d\,e\,f+2\,g\,h\,i
\end{multline*}
all terms on the right become positive or zero, since $a',b',c'$ are not
positive but negative or zero. Thus $3abc$ cannot be greater than $6D$, or
$abc$ cannot be greater than $2D$. The same likewise follows from the identity
\begin{multline*}
6D-3abc \;=\; -a\,a'(g+2k)-b\,b'(h+2l)-c\,c'(i+2m)\\
{}-a'\,e\,f-b'\,d\,f-c'\,d\,e+2\,d\,e\,f+g\,h\,i.
\end{multline*}

\section*{Simplified unsymmetric identity}

\emph{Both identities are symmetric. If one gives up complete symmetry, the proof
can be carried out with an even smaller number of terms, for example by the
identity}
\[
\boxed{\;8D-4abc \;=\; -2\,a\,a'(g+k)-2\,b\,b'(e+l)-4\,c\,c'\,m+(c+e)\,d\,f+(c+g)\,h\,i\;}
\]

\noindent\footnotesize Translation note: the algebraic notation is intentionally kept
parallel to the German source; the determinant sign convention $=-D$ and the primed
coefficients $a',b',c'$ are preserved without normalization.
