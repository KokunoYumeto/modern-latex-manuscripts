\section{Supplement on \'etale morphisms. Henselian local rings and strictly local rings}
\label{section:IV.18}

\oldpage[IV]{109}

In this paragraph, we study various special properties of \'etale morphisms.
Moreover, the notion of \'etale morphism allows us to develop in a natural way the theory of Nagata of Henselian rings, as well as that of strictly local rings.
These rings play an important r\^ole in numerous recent developments, appearing each time one needs a procedure of ``localisation'' finer than that furnished by the Zariski topology (cf.\ for example~\cite{IV-43}, while awaiting the publication of the chapter of our Treatise devoted to the study of the ``\'etale topology'').


\subsection{A remarkable equivalence of categories.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.1}

\begin{proposition}[18.1.1]
\label{IV.18.1.1}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $S_0$ a closed sub-prescheme of $S$, lisse (resp.\ \'etale) on $S_0$, $x_0$ a point of $X_0$.
There exists then an open neighbourhood $U_0$ of $x_0$ in $X_0$, an S-prescheme $U$ lisse (resp.\ \'etale) on $S$ and an $S_0$-isomorphism $U\times_S S_0\xrightarrow{\sim} U_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Note that if $X_0$ is \'etale on $S_0$ at the point $x_0$, it is \emph{a fortiori} non ramified on $S_0$ at this point; if one constructs an S-prescheme $U$ lisse on $S$ such that $U\times_S S_0$ is isomorphic to $X_0$ in a neighbourhood of $x_0$ \sref{IV.17.4.1},~$d$)), it will then follow that $U$ is non ramified on $S$ at the point $x_0$ \sref{IV.17.4.1},~$d$)), hence also \'etale in a neighbourhood of $x_0$.
It suffices therefore to prove the proposition when one only supposes $X_0$ lisse on $S_0$.

The question being local on $S$ and $X_0$, one can suppose that $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ and $X_0=\operatorname{Spec}(C_0)$, where $A_0$ is a quotient ring of $A$, $C_0=B_0/\mathfrak{J}_0$, where $B_0=A_0[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_n]$ and $\mathfrak{J}_0$ is an ideal of finite type of $B_0$; finally, $C_0$ is an $A_0$-algebra formally lisse for the discrete topologies.
Let $\mathfrak{p}_0$ be the ideal corresponding to $\mathfrak{q}_0$ in $B_0$.
The Jacobian criterion ($\mathbf{0}$, \sref{IV.22.6.4}) applied to ($\mathbf{0}$, \sref{IV.19.1.12}) shows that there exist in $\mathfrak{J}_0$ a family of $r$ polynomials $u_i$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$ and $r$ indices $j_h$ $(1\leq h\leq r)$ such that the images of the $u_i$ in $(\mathfrak{J}_0)_{\mathfrak{q}_0}/(\mathfrak{J}_0^2)_{\mathfrak{q}_0}$ engender this $(\mathrm{B}_0)_{\mathfrak{q}_0}$-module, and that one has
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.1.1.1}
  \det(\partial u_i/\partial\mathrm{T}_{j_h})\notin\mathfrak{q}_0.
\end{equation}
As $(\mathrm{B}_0)_{\mathfrak{q}_0}$ is a local ring, it results from the Nakayama lemma that one can suppose that the images of the $u_i$ in $(\mathfrak{J}_0)_{\mathfrak{q}_0}$ engender this $(\mathrm{B}_0)_{\mathfrak{q}_0}$-module, then, replacing $X_0$ by an affine open neighbourhood of $x_0$ ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.5.2.2}), that the $u_i$ engender $\mathfrak{J}_0$.
Set then $B=A[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_n]$; $B_0$ is thus a quotient ring of $B$.
For every $i$, let $v_i\in B$ be an element whose $u_i$ is the image in $B_0$, and let $\mathfrak{J}$ be the ideal of $B$ engendered by the $v_i$.
The proposition will be established by taking $U$ to be an open affine neighbourhood of the point of $\operatorname{Spec}(B/\mathfrak{J})$ corresponding to the ideal prime $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{J}$, provided that one proves that $\mathrm{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{J}_{\mathfrak{q}}$ is an A-algebra formally lisse for the discrete topologies.
Or, by virtue of the Jacobian criterion ($\mathbf{0}$, \sref{IV.22.6.1} and \sref{IV.20.5.12.1}), it suffices to take $\det(\partial v_i/\partial\mathrm{T}_{j_h})\notin\mathfrak{q}$, which is possible since $\mathfrak{J}_0$ is the image of $\mathfrak{J}$ in $B_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[18.1.2]
\label{IV.18.1.2}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $S_0$ a closed sub-prescheme of $S$ whose underlying space is identical to that of $S$.
Then the functor
\[
X \mapsto X\times_S S_0
\]
from the category of S-preschemes \'etale and finite on $S$, to the category of $S_0$-preschemes \'etale and finite on $S_0$, is an equivalence of categories.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let us show first that this functor is \emph{pleinement fid\`ele}.
Let $X$, $Y$ be two S-preschemes \'etale on $S$, and set $X_0=X\times_S S_0$, $Y_0=Y\times_S S_0$.
If $Z=X\times_S Y$, the set $\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,Y)$ is in canonical bijection with the set of X-sections $\Gamma(Z/X)$, and likewise $\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(X_0,Y_0)$ is in canonical bijection with $\Gamma(Z_0/X_0)$, where $Z_0=Z\times_S S_0=X_0\times_{S_0} Y_0$.
Or $Z$ is \'etale on $X$, $Z_0$ \'etale on $X_0$ (resp.\ $Z_0$) is a closed sub-prescheme of $X$ (resp.\ $Z$) with the same underlying space.
The open parts of $Z$ on which the restriction of the morphism $Z\to X$ is surjective and radiciel are thus the \emph{same} as the parts of $Z_0$ having the corresponding properties and our assertion follows from \sref{IV.17.9.3}.

To finish the proof of \sref{IV.18.1.2}, it suffices to see that for every $S_0$-prescheme $X_0$ \'etale and finite on $S$, there exists an $\sh{O}_X$-Algebra $\sh{B}$ finite and \'etale and an $S_0$-isomorphism $\sh{B}_0\xrightarrow{\sim}\sh{B}\otimes_{\sh{O}_x}\sh{O}_{X_0}$.
Or, it results from prop.\ \sref{IV.18.1.1} that there is a projective system $(\sh{B}_n)$ such that $\mathrm{B}_n$ is an $\sh{O}_{X_n}$-Algebra finite and \'etale and the homomorphisms $\mathrm{B}_{n+1}\otimes_{A_{n+1}}A_n\to\mathrm{B}_n$ are bijectifs.
It results from \sref{IV.18.3.2.1} and \sref{IV.18.3.1} that the A-algebra $B=\varprojlim B_n$ is a projective A-module of finite type and that $B_0$ is isomorphic to $B\otimes_A A_0$.
To prove that $B$ is an A-algebra \'etale, it suffices, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.2.5}, to show that for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $A$, $\mathrm{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}/\mathfrak{m}\mathrm{B}_{\mathfrak{m}}$ is a $k(y)$-algebra separable.
But since the morphism structural $f:X\to S$ is \emph{proper}, $f(x)$ is a point belonging to $S_0$, hence $\mathfrak{J}$ is contained in the radical of $A$ ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.7.1.10}); the conclusion results from the fact that $X_0=f^{-1}(S_0)$ and that $\sh{B}_0$ is an $\sh{O}_{X_0}$-algebra finite and \'etale.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[18.1.3]
\label{IV.18.1.3}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $X$ an S-prescheme \'etale on $S$, $S'$ a sub-prescheme of $S'$ having the same underlying space.
Then the canonical application $X(S')_S\to X(S'_0)_S$ ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.3.4.3}) is bijective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
One sets indeed $X'=X\times_S S'$, $X'_0=X\times_S S'_0$, so that $X(S')_S=\Gamma(X'/S')$ and $X(S'_0)_S=\Gamma(X'_0/S'_0)$; the corollary results from the fact that the functor defined in \sref{IV.18.1.2} (with $S$ and $S_0$ replaced by $S'$ and $S'_0$ respectively) is pleinement fid\`ele (or directly from \sref{IV.17.9.3}).
\end{proof}


\subsection{\'Etale coverings.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.2}

\begin{env}[18.2.1]
\label{IV.18.2.1}
Given a ring $A$ and a commutative A-algebra $B$ which is \emph{finite} and is an A-module \emph{libre}, we recall (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.}, chap.~VIII, \textsection~12, n\textsuperscript{o}~2) that one defines on $B$ an A-\emph{linear form} $\operatorname{Tr}_{B/A}$, the ``trace form''; one deduces from this the definition of a \emph{symmetric A-bilinear form} (also called ``trace form'')
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.1.1}
  (x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{B/A}(xy)
\end{equation}
whose data is equivalent to that of the A-linear application associated $\operatorname{astr}_{B/A}:B\to\check{B}$, where $\check{B}$ is the dual of the A-module $B$, equal to its transposed.
When $A$ is a \emph{field}, it is equivalent to say that this bilinear form is \emph{non degenerate} or that $B$ is a \emph{separable} algebra (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.}, chap.~IX, \textsection~2, prop.~5).

Let $f:A\to A'$ be a homomorphism of rings; set $B'=B\otimes_A A'$, and let $g:B\to B'$ be the canonical homomorphism; the image by $g$ of a basis of the A-module $B$ is then a basis of the A'-module $B'$, and it follows from the definitions that for every $x\in B$,
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.1.2}
\label{IV.18.2.1.2}
  \operatorname{Tr}_{B'/A'}(g(x))=f(\operatorname{Tr}_{B/A}(x)).
\end{equation}
\end{env}

\oldpage[IV]{111}
\begin{env}[18.2.2]
\label{IV.18.2.2}
Consider now a \emph{ringed space} $(X,\sh{O}_X)$ and let $\sh{B}$ be an $\sh{O}_X$-\emph{Algebra} which, as an $\sh{O}_X$-Module, is \emph{locally free of finite rank}; then, for every open subset $U\subset X$ such that $\sh{B}|U$ is (as an $\sh{O}_U$-Module) isomorphic to $\sh{O}_U^n$ (for some $n$ depending on $U$), $\Gamma(U,\sh{B})$ is a $\Gamma(U,\sh{O}_X)$-algebra which, as a $\Gamma(U,\sh{O}_X)$-module, is free of finite rank and thus defines a $\Gamma(U,\sh{O}_X)$-linear form $\operatorname{Tr}_{\Gamma(U,\sh{B})/\Gamma(U,\sh{O}_X)}$, which we also denote $\operatorname{Tr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X,U}$; one deduces from this an associated linear application
\[
  \operatorname{astr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X,U}:\Gamma(U,\sh{B})\to\Gamma(U,\sh{B})^\vee=\Gamma(U,\check{\sh{B}}).
\]

Furthermore, it follows from \sref{IV.18.2.1.2} that these linear applications are compatible with the restriction operations from $U$ to a smaller open subset, and thus define, on the one hand, a \emph{homomorphism of $\sh{O}_X$-Modules}, also called \emph{trace homomorphism}:
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.2.1}
\label{IV.18.2.2.1}
  \operatorname{Tr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X}:\sh{B}\to\sh{O}_X
\end{equation}
and on the other hand a \emph{homomorphism of $\sh{O}_X$-Modules}
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.2.2}
\label{IV.18.2.2.2}
  \operatorname{astr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X}:\sh{B}\to\check{\sh{B}}
\end{equation}
called \emph{associated to the trace}, and equal to its transpose.
It also follows from \sref{IV.18.2.1.2} that for every $x\in X$, we have
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.2.3}
\label{IV.18.2.2.3}
  (\operatorname{Tr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X})_x=\operatorname{Tr}_{\sh{B}_x/\sh{O}_{X,x}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.2.4}
\label{IV.18.2.2.4}
  (\operatorname{astr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X})_x=\operatorname{astr}_{\sh{B}_x/\sh{O}_{X,x}}.
\end{equation}

Finally, under the conditions of \sref{IV.18.2.1}, if we set $X=\Spec(A)$ and if $\sh{B}=\widetilde{B}$ is the $\sh{O}_X$-Algebra corresponding to $B$, the form $\operatorname{Tr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X}$ (resp.\ the homomorphism $\operatorname{astr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_X}$) corresponds to the form $\operatorname{Tr}_{B/A}$ (resp.\ to the homomorphism of A-modules $\operatorname{astr}_{B/A}$), as follows again from \sref{IV.18.2.1.2}.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[18.2.3]
\label{IV.18.2.3}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a finite morphism of preschemes and set $\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $f$ is \'etale.
  \item[a')] $f$ is a morphism flat of finite presentation, and for every $x\in X$, if one sets $y=f(x)$, $\sh{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_y\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a field, finite and separable extension of $k(y)$.
  \item[b)] $\sh{B}$ is an $\sh{O}_Y$-Module locally free and for every $y\in Y$, $\sh{B}_y\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y,y}} k(y)$ is a $k(y)$-algebra finite separable (hence a direct composition of a finite number of fields, separable finite extensions of $k(y)$).
  \item[c)] $\sh{B}$ is an $\sh{O}_Y$-Module locally free, and the homomorphism $\operatorname{astr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_Y}:\sh{B}\to\check{\sh{B}}$ \sref{IV.18.2.2} is bijective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[18.2.4]
\label{IV.18.2.4}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism finite and of finite presentation, and set $\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$.
Let $y$ be a point of $Y$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] There exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ in $Y$ such that the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is an \'etale morphism.
  \item[b)] $\sh{B}_y$ is an $\sh{O}_{X,y}$-module libre of finite type and $\sh{B}_y\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y,y}} k(y)$ is a $k(y)$-algebra separable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.2.5]
\label{IV.18.2.5}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme quasi-compact or locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism finite and of finite presentation; set $\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$.
Suppose that for every closed point $y$ of $Y$, $\sh{B}_y$ is an $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$-module libre and $\sh{B}_y\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y,y}} k(y)$ a $k(y)$-algebra separable.
Then $f$ is \'etale.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.2.6]
\label{IV.18.2.6}
If $f:X\to Y$ is a morphism finite and \'etale, and if one sets $\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$, then the $\sh{O}_Y$-homomorphism $\operatorname{Tr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_Y}:\sh{B}\to\sh{O}_Y$ \sref{IV.18.2.2} (also denoted $\operatorname{Tr}_f$) is surjective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remarks}[18.2.7]
\label{IV.18.2.7}
(i)
When $f:X\to Y$ is a finite morphism such that $f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ is an $\sh{O}_Y$-Module locally libre (resp.\ locally libre of rank $n$), one says also that $f$ is a morphism \emph{finite locally libre} (resp.\ \emph{locally libre of rank $n$}).
This condition, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.2.3}, is satisfied if $f$ is a finite \'etale morphism, but does not imply by itself that $f$ be \'etale, as is shown by the example where $X=\operatorname{Spec}(K)$ and $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(k)$ are spectra of fields, $K$ being a finite non separable extension of $k$.
When $f:X\to Y$ is a finite \'etale morphism, one says also that $X$ is an \emph{\'etale covering} of $Y$.
In particular $f(X)$ is an open and closed part of $Y$.

One says that a covering is \emph{trivial} if $X$ is the sum of a finite number of preschemes isomorphic to $Y$.
One says that a covering $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{locally trivial} if the morphism $f:X\to Y$ is such that for every point $y\in Y$ there is an open neighbourhood $U$ such that $f^{-1}(U)$ is a trivial covering of $U$.

(ii)
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a finite morphism, locally libre of rank $n$; set $\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ and let $u=\operatorname{astr}_{\sh{B}/\sh{O}_Y}:\sh{B}\to\check{\sh{B}}$; one deduces from this an exterior $n$-th power homomorphism $\bigwedge^n u:\bigwedge^n\sh{B}\to(\bigwedge^n\sh{B})^\vee$ of $\sh{O}_Y$-Modules \emph{inversibles}, and hence ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.5.4.2}) an element
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.7.1}
  d_{X/Y}\in\Gamma(Y,(\bigwedge^n\check{\sh{B}})\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}(\bigwedge^n\check{\sh{B}}))
\end{equation}
called the \emph{discriminant} of $X$ over $Y$.
Since $(\bigwedge^n\sh{B})\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}(\bigwedge^n\sh{B})$ is the dual of $(\bigwedge^n\check{\sh{B}})\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}(\bigwedge^n\check{\sh{B}})$, $d_{X/Y}$ can also be identified with a homomorphism
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.2.7.2}
\label{IV.18.2.7.2}
  (\bigwedge^n\sh{B})\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}(\bigwedge^n\sh{B})\to\sh{O}_Y
\end{equation}
and one denotes $\sh{D}_{X/Y}$ the quasi-coherent Ideal of finite type of $\sh{O}_Y$, image of the homomorphism \sref{IV.18.2.7.2}, also called the \emph{Ideal discriminant} of $X$ over $Y$.
\end{remarks}

\begin{proposition}[18.2.8]
\label{IV.18.2.8}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism \'etale, separated and of finite type, and for every $y\in Y$, let $n(y)$ be the geometric number of points of $f^{-1}(y)$.
Then the function $y\mapsto n(y)$ is semi-continuous inferiorly in $Y$.
For it to be continuous in a neighbourhood of $y$ (and hence constant in a neighbourhood of $y$), it is necessary and sufficient that there exist an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ such that the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is a finite (\'etale) morphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As $f$ is quasi-finite \sref{IV.17.6.1} and locally of finite presentation, it amounts to the same to say that $f$ is finite or that $f$ is proper \sref{IV.8.11.1}: moreover each fibre $f^{-1}(y)$ is geometrically reduced on $k(y)$.
The conclusions then result from \sref{IV.15.5.9},~(i) and~(ii)) and from the fact that $f$ is flat.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[18.2.9]
\label{IV.18.2.9}
Let $Y$ be a connected prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism \'etale separated and of finite type.
For $f$ to be finite (in other words, for $X$ to be an \'etale covering of $Y$), it is necessary and sufficient that all the fibres of $f$ have the same geometric number of points.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remarks}[18.2.10]
\label{IV.18.2.10}
\oldpage[IV]{114}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The example of the ``affine line with a doubled point'' ($\mathbf{I}$, 5.5.11) shows that a morphism \'etale of finite type of noetherian preschemes can be \emph{non separated}; for this example, the first assertion of \sref{IV.18.2.8} is no longer exact.
  \item For a morphism separated, of finite type and \'etale $f:X\to Y$ to make $X$ a \emph{locally trivial} covering, it is necessary and sufficient that for every $x\in X$ there exist an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y=f(x)$ and a U-section $g$ of $f^{-1}(U)$ such that $g(y)=x$.
  Indeed, the condition is evidently necessary; the fact that it is sufficient results from the fact that every fibre $f^{-1}(y)$ is finite (\sref{IV.17.6.1} and ($\mathbf{I}$, 6.2.2)), from the characterisation of sections of a Y-schema \'etale \sref{IV.17.9.3} and from the proposition \sref{IV.18.2.8}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}


\subsection{Finite and \'etale algebras.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.3}

\begin{proposition}[18.3.1]
\label{IV.18.3.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an A-algebra of finite presentation.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item For $B$ to be a non ramified A-algebra, it is necessary and sufficient that $B$ be an A-module of finite presentation and that $B$ is a $(\mathrm{B}\otimes_A\mathrm{B})$-module projective.
  \item Suppose moreover that $B$ is a finite A-algebra.
  For $B$ to be an \'etale A-algebra, it is necessary and sufficient that $B$ is an A-module projective and a $(\mathrm{B}\otimes_A\mathrm{B})$-module projective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[18.3.2]
\label{IV.18.3.2}
Let $A$ be a ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$ such that, for the $\mathfrak{J}$-pre-adic topology, $A$ is separated and complete; set $A_0=A/\mathfrak{J}$.
Then the functor
\[
B\mapsto B\otimes_A A_0
\]
is an equivalence of the category of A-algebras finite and \'etale, to the category of $A_0$-algebras finite and \'etale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We shall first prove the following lemma:

\medskip
\label{IV.18.3.2.1}\emph{Lemma} \sref{IV.18.3.2.1}. ---
\emph{Let $A$ be a ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$ such that, for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology, $A$ is separated and complete.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item \emph{Every projective A-module of finite type $M$ is separated and complete for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology, hence a projective limit of the projective $(A/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1})$-modules $M/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}M$.}
  \item \emph{Conversely, set $A_n=A/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}$, and let $(M_n)$ be a projective system of $A_n$-modules, such that, for every $n$, the homomorphism $M_{n+1}\otimes_{A_{n+1}}A_n\to M_n$ deduced from the transition homomorphism $M_{n+1}\to M_n$ is bijective.
  Suppose moreover the $M_n$ projective and of finite type.
  Then $M=\varprojlim M_n$ is a projective A-module of finite type such that $M\otimes_A A_0\to M_0$ is bijective.}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
(i) There exists a free A-module of finite type $L$ such that $M$ is isomorphic to a direct factor of $L$; since $L$ is separated for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology, the same is true of every submodule $N$ of $L$ since $\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}N\subset\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}L$; in particular $M$ is separated for this topology, and since the surjective homomorphism $f:L\to M$ is continuous for the topology induced by that of $L$; since $L$ is complete and $f$ a strict morphism, one concludes that $M$ is complete (Bourbaki, \emph{Top.\ g\'en.}, chap.~IX, 2\textsuperscript{e}~\'ed., \textsection~3, n\textsuperscript{o}~1, prop.~4).

(ii) It follows from Nakayama's lemma that if $M_0$ is generated by a finite family $(x_{i0})$ of $r$ elements and if for every $n$, $x_{in}$ is an element of $M_n$ whose image in $M_{n-1}$ is $x_{i,n-1}$, then $(x_{in})$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$ is a system of generators of $M_n$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~II, \textsection~3, cor.~2 de la prop.~4).
This being so, for every $n$, set $L_n=A_n^r$; if $(\varepsilon_{in})_{1\leq i\leq r}$ is the canonical basis of $L_n$, let $u_n:L_n\to M_n$ be the A-linear application such that $u_n(\varepsilon_{in})=x_{in}$ for every $i$.
By hypothesis one has a split exact sequence
\[
  0\to N_n\xrightarrow{v_n} L_n\xrightarrow{u_n} M_n\to 0
\]
\oldpage[IV]{116}
and since $L_n=L_{n+1}/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}L_{n+1}$ and $M_n=M_{n+1}/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}M_{n+1}$, the vertical arrows in the commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  0 \ar[r] & N_{n+1} \ar[r]^-{v_{n+1}} \ar[d] & L_{n+1} \ar[r]^-{u_{n+1}} \ar[d] & M_{n+1} \ar[r] \ar[d] & 0 \\
  0 \ar[r] & N_n \ar[r]^-{v_n} & L_n \ar[r]^-{u_n} & M_n \ar[r] & 0
}
\]
are all three surjective.
Now we have $M=\varprojlim M_n$ and $L=A^r=\varprojlim L_n$; if one sets $N=\varprojlim N_n$, $u=\varprojlim u_n$, $v=\varprojlim v_n$, one has, by virtue of ($\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 13.2.2), the exact sequence
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.3.2.2}
\label{IV.18.3.2.2}
  0\to N\xrightarrow{v} L\xrightarrow{u} M\to 0.
\end{equation}
Furthermore, since for every $n$, $v_n$ is left invertible and $M_n$ is a projective $A_n$-module, it follows from ($\mathbf{0}$, 19.1.8) that the exact sequence \sref{IV.18.3.2.2} is split, which proves the lemma.

\medskip
This being so, let us first show that the functor of the statement of \sref{IV.18.3.2} is \emph{fully faithful}.
Set, as in the lemma, $A_n=A/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}$; let $B$, $C$ be two A-algebras finite and \'etale, and set, for every $n$, $B_n=B\otimes_A A_n$, $C_n=C\otimes_A A_n$; by virtue of \sref{IV.18.3.1} and \sref{IV.18.3.2.1}, $B$ and $C$ are separated and complete for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology, and one has $B=\varprojlim B_n$, $C=\varprojlim C_n$; moreover every homomorphism of A-algebras $u:B\to C$ is continuous for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topologies, and gives therefore a projective system of homomorphisms of $A_n$-algebras $u_n=u\otimes 1:B_n\to C_n$, of which it is the projective limit; the reciprocal being evident, one has therefore a canonical bijection
\[
  \Hom_{\text{A-alg.}}(B,C)\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim\Hom_{A_n\text{-alg.}}(B_n,C_n).
\]
But since $B$ and $C$ are A-algebras \emph{\'etale}, it also follows from \sref{IV.18.2.1.2} that the canonical application
\[
  \Hom_{A_{n+1}\text{-alg.}}(B_{n+1},C_{n+1})\to\Hom_{A_n\text{-alg.}}(B_n,C_n)
\]
is \emph{bijective} for $n\geq 0$, which completes the proof that the canonical application $\Hom_{\text{A-alg.}}(B,C)\to\Hom_{A_0\text{-alg.}}(B_0,C_0)$ is bijective.

To complete the proof of \sref{IV.18.3.2}, it suffices to show that for every $A_0$-algebra $B_0$ finite and \'etale there exists a finite A-algebra $B$ and an $A_0$-isomorphism $B_0\xrightarrow{\sim} B\otimes_A A_0$.
Now, it follows from \sref{IV.18.2.1.2} that there is a projective system $(B_n)$ such that $B_n$ is an $A_n$-algebra finite and \'etale and the homomorphisms $B_{n+1}\otimes_{A_{n+1}}A_n\to B_n$ are bijective.
It follows from \sref{IV.18.3.1} and \sref{IV.18.3.2.1} that the A-algebra $B=\varprojlim B_n$ is a projective A-module of finite type and that $B_0$ is isomorphic to $B\otimes_A A_0$.
To prove that $B$ is an A-algebra \'etale, it suffices, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.2.5}, to show that for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $A$, $B_\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m} B_\mathfrak{m}$ is a $k(\mathfrak{m})$-algebra separable.
Now, since $\mathfrak{J}$ is contained in the radical of $A$ ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.1.10), the conclusion follows from the fact that $B_0$ is an $A_0$-algebra finite and \'etale \sref{IV.18.2.5}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[18.3.3]
\label{IV.18.3.3}
Prop.\ \sref{IV.18.3.2} applies in particular when $A$ is a \emph{local ring, separated and complete}, $\mathfrak{J}$ being the maximal ideal of $A$, so that $A_0$ is a field, and the category of $A_0$-algebras finite and \'etale is identical to that of the $A_0$-algebras finite and separable, hence isomorphic to direct compositions of fields, separable finite extensions of $A_0$.
In particular, if the field $A_0$ is \emph{separably closed}, these \'etale coverings are all identical to $A_0$, and hence every \'etale covering of $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is \emph{trivial} \sref{IV.18.2.7} by virtue of \sref{IV.18.3.2}.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[18.3.4]
\label{IV.18.3.4}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$ such that $A$ is separated and complete for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology, $A_0=A/\mathfrak{J}$.
Set $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(A_0)$.
Let $X$ be an S-scheme proper on $S$, and set $X_0=X\times_X X_0$.
Then the functor
\[
Z\mapsto Z\times_X X_0
\]
from the category of X-schemes finite and \'etale on $X$, to the category of $X_0$-schemes \'etale and finite on $X_0$, is an equivalence of categories.
\end{theorem}


\subsection{Local structure of non ramified and \'etale morphisms.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.4}

\begin{lemma}[18.4.1]
\label{IV.18.4.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an A-algebra finite monogene, $u$ a generator of the A-algebra $B$, $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$ a polynomial such that $F(u)=0$, $F'$ the derived polynomial; set $u'=F'(u)$.
Then the Ideal of $B$, annihilator of $\Omega^1_{B/A}$, contains $u'B$; it is equal to $u'B$ if the ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ of $A[\mathrm{T}]$ consisting of the polynomials $G$ such that $G(u)=0$, is engendered by $F$, in other words if the surjective canonical homomorphism $\varphi:A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]\to B$ sending the image of $\mathrm{T}$ to $u$, is bijective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[18.4.2]
\label{IV.18.4.2}
With the notations of \sref{IV.18.4.1}, let $\mathfrak{q}$ be a prime ideal of $B$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $\mathfrak{q}$ does not contain $u'$, $B_{\mathfrak{q}}$ is an $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-algebra formally non ramified ($\mathfrak{p}$ being the inverse image of $\mathfrak{q}$ in $A$); in other words, $\operatorname{Spec}(B_u)$ is formally non ramified on $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$.
  \item Suppose moreover that $F$ is unitary and engenders $\mathfrak{J}$.
  Then, for $B$ to be an A-algebra \'etale on $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ at the point $\mathfrak{q}$, it is necessary and sufficient that $u'\notin\mathfrak{q}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[18.4.3]
\label{IV.18.4.3}
With the notations of \sref{IV.18.4.2}, suppose that $F$ is unitary and engenders $\mathfrak{J}$.
Then, for $B$ to be an A-algebra \'etale, it is necessary and sufficient that $u'$ be inversible in $B$ (or, what amounts to the same, that the ideal of $A[\mathrm{T}]$ engendered by $F$ and $F'$ be equal to $A[\mathrm{T}]$).

One says that a unitary polynomial $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$ such that the ideal of $A[\mathrm{T}]$ engendered by $F$ and $F'$ is equal to $A[\mathrm{T}]$ is \emph{separable}; it is immediate that this definition coincides with the usual definition (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.}, chap.~V, \textsection~7, n\textsuperscript{o}~6) when $A$ is a field.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[18.4.4]
\label{IV.18.4.4}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $B$ an A-algebra finite monogene, $u$ a generator of the A-algebra $B$.
Let $\mathfrak{n}_i$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$ be the maximal ideals of $B$ such that $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ is formally non ramified on $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ at the points $\mathfrak{n}_i$.
Then there exists a unitary polynomial $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$ such that $F(u)=0$ and $F'(u)\notin\mathfrak{n}_i$ for every index $i$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$.
Moreover, if $k$ is the residue field of $A$ and $f\in k[\mathrm{T}]$ the minimal polynomial of the image of $u$ in $B\otimes_A k$ over $k$, there exists $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$ satisfying all the conditions, whose image canonical is $f$; and $F$ is necessarily of degree $n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[18.4.5]
\label{IV.18.4.5}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $k$ its residue field, $B$ an A-algebra finite (resp.\ of finite presentation).
Suppose moreover, either that the field $k$ is infinite, or that $B$ is a local ring.
Let $n$ be the rank of $L=B\otimes_A k$ over $k$.
For $B$ to be an A-algebra formally non ramified (resp.\ \'etale), it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a unitary separable polynomial $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$ \sref{IV.18.4.3} such that $B$ is isomorphic to a quotient of $A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$ (resp.\ isomorphic to $A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$).
Moreover, one can suppose that $F$ is of degree $n$ (resp.\ $F$ is necessarily of degree $n$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[18.4.6]
\label{IV.18.4.6}
(Chevalley).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$, and set $A=\sh{O}_{Y,y}$.
  For $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ to be an A-algebra formally smooth (resp.\ formally non ramified), it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a unitary polynomial $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$ and a maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n}$ of $B=A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$ (resp.\ an algebra quotient $B$ of $A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$) such that $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is A-isomorphic to $B_{\mathfrak{n}}$ and that, if $u$ is the image of $\mathrm{T}$ in $B$, one has $F'(u)\notin\mathfrak{n}$.
  \item Suppose moreover that $f$ is locally of finite presentation.
  Then, for $f$ to be \'etale at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that one can take $B=A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[18.4.7]
\label{IV.18.4.7}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$.
For $f$ to be formally non ramified at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $f|U$ factors as $U\xrightarrow{j}X'\xrightarrow{h}Y$, where $h$ is \'etale and $j$ is a closed immersion.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.4.8]
\label{IV.18.4.8}
\oldpage[IV]{122}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation.
For $f$ to be non ramified (resp.\ \'etale), it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a family of flat morphisms $g_\alpha:\mathrm{Y}'_\alpha\to\mathrm{Y}$ and, for each $\alpha$, an open set $\mathrm{U}_\alpha$ in $\mathrm{X}'_\alpha=\mathrm{X}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{Y}'_\alpha$, such that, if $g'_\alpha:\mathrm{X}'_\alpha\to\mathrm{X}$ and $f'_\alpha:\mathrm{X}'_\alpha\to\mathrm{Y}'_\alpha$ are the canonical projections, the $g'_\alpha(\mathrm{U}_\alpha)$ form a covering of $\mathrm{X}$, and each of the composed morphisms $\mathrm{U}_\alpha\to\mathrm{X}'_\alpha\xrightarrow{f'_\alpha}\mathrm{Y}'_\alpha$ is a closed immersion (resp.\ open immersion).
One can moreover then take the $g_\alpha$ \'etale.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The necessity of the condition for \'etale morphisms is trivial, taking for each $\alpha$ a single $\mathrm{Y}'_\alpha$ equal to $\mathrm{X}$, the morphism $g_\alpha$ corresponding being equal to $f$, and the open set $\mathrm{U}\subset\mathrm{X}\times_{\mathrm{Y}}\mathrm{X}$ being the diagonal.
When $f$ is non ramified, the necessity of the condition results from \sref{IV.18.4.7}; one takes a covering open $(\mathrm{V}_\alpha)$ of $\mathrm{X}$ such that for each $\alpha$, $f|\mathrm{V}_\alpha$ factors as $\mathrm{V}_\alpha\xrightarrow{j_\alpha}\mathrm{Y}'_\alpha\xrightarrow{g_\alpha}\mathrm{Y}$, where $j_\alpha$ is a closed immersion and $g_\alpha$ an \'etale morphism.
Then $j_\alpha:\mathrm{V}_\alpha\to\mathrm{Y}'_\alpha$, where $s_\alpha$ is a $\mathrm{V}_\alpha$-section of $\mathrm{X}'_\alpha$, and as the morphism $g'_\alpha:\mathrm{X}'_\alpha\to\mathrm{X}$ is \'etale, $s_\alpha$ is an open immersion \sref{IV.17.4.1}, and it suffices to take $\mathrm{U}_\alpha=s_\alpha(\mathrm{V}_\alpha)$ to answer the question.

The suffisance of the conditions results from \sref{IV.17.7.1}; one concludes that $f$ is non ramified (resp.\ \'etale) at each point of $g'(\mathrm{U}_\alpha)$, hence in all of $\mathrm{X}$ since the $g'(\mathrm{U}_\alpha)$ cover $\mathrm{X}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[18.4.9]
\label{IV.18.4.9}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $f:X\to S$ a morphism, $h:Y\to S$ a morphism locally of finite presentation, $g:X\to Y$ an S-morphism, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=g(x)$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $h$ is \'etale at the point $y$ and $g$ is flat at the point $x$.
  \item[b)] $h$ is non ramified at the point $y$ and $f$ is flat at the point $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $f=h\circ g$, $a)$ implies that $f$ is flat at the point $x$ \sref[I]{I.2.1.6}, and it is evident that $h$ is non ramified at the point $y$, hence $a)$ implies $b)$.

To prove that $b)$ implies $a)$, one can first suppose that $h$ is non ramified (by replacing $Y$ by an open neighbourhood of $y$); then, by replacing $S$ by an open neighbourhood of $s=h(y)=f(x)$, one can suppose that there exists an \'etale morphism $u:S'\to S$, a point $y'$ of $Y'=Y_{(S')}$ above $y$ and an open neighbourhood $V$ of $y'$ in $Y'$ such that, if $h'=h_{(S')}:Y'\to S'$, the restriction of $h'$ to $V$ is a closed immersion \sref{IV.18.4.8}.
If one proves that $h'$ is \'etale at the point $y$, it will result that $h$ is \'etale at the point $y$ \sref{IV.17.7.1}, (ii)); moreover, $f'=f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to S'$ is flat at every point $x'$ above $x$.
As the projections $v:Y'\to Y$, $w:X'\to X$ are \'etale morphisms (hence flat), if one proves that $g'=g_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is flat at the point $x'$, it will result from \sref[I]{I.2.2.11}, (iv))
that $g$ will be flat at the point $x$.
One can hence restrict to the case where $h$ is a closed immersion, $f$ being supposed flat at the point $x$.
Let $\mathscr{J}$ be the quasi-coherent Ideal of finite type of $\sh{O}_S$ which defines the closed sub-prescheme $Y$ of $S$.
The hypothesis that $f$ is flat at the point $x$ implies the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{S,s}\to\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is injective ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.6.5.1}); \emph{a fortiori} the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{S,s}\to\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is injective, which means that $\mathscr{J}_s=0$, since this is the kernel of the preceding homomorphism.
Since $\mathscr{J}$ is of finite type, there is an open neighbourhood $U$ of $s$ in $S$ such that $\mathscr{J}|U=0$ ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.5.2.2}).
One can hence suppose that $h$ is an open immersion, and then it is clear that $g$ is flat at the point $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[18.4.10]
\label{IV.18.4.10}
\oldpage[IV]{123}
Let us denote by $\mathbf{P}(f,x)$ a property verifying the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[$1\textsuperscript{o}$] For every morphism $f:X\to Y$ and every local isomorphism $h:Y\to Z$, $\mathbf{P}(f,x)$ is equivalent to $\mathbf{P}(h\circ f,x)$ for $x\in X$.
  \item[$2\textsuperscript{o}$] For every morphism $f:X\to Y$, every \'etale morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, every point $x\in X$, if one sets $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$ and if $x'\in X'$ is above $x$, the properties $\mathbf{P}(f,x)$ and $\mathbf{P}(f',x')$ are equivalent (``invariance by \'etale base change'').
\end{enumerate}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $f:X\to S$ and $h:Y\to S$ two S-morphisms, $g:X\to Y$ an S-morphism, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=g(x)$, and suppose $h$ \'etale at the point $y$.
Then the properties $\mathbf{P}(f,x)$ and $\mathbf{P}(g,x)$ are equivalent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With the same notations as in the proof of \sref{IV.18.4.9} and by replacing $S$ (resp.\ $Y$) by an open neighbourhood of $s=h(y)$ (resp.\ of $y$), one can here, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.4.8}, find an \'etale morphism $u:S'\to S$ such that $h'$ is an open immersion; if $x'\in X'$ is above $x$, $\mathbf{P}(f',x')$ (resp.\ $\mathbf{P}(g',x')$) is then by hypothesis equivalent to $\mathbf{P}(f,x)$ (resp.\ $\mathbf{P}(g,x)$).
As the projections $v:Y'\to Y$, $w:X'\to X$ are \'etale morphisms (hence flat), if one proves that $g'=g_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is flat at the point $x'$, one deduces the conclusion.
The hypothesis implies that $\mathbf{P}(g,x)$ is then equivalent to $\mathbf{P}(h\circ g,x)$, from which one deduces the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{examples}[18.4.11]
\label{IV.18.4.11}
One can take for property $\mathbf{P}(f,x)$ any one of the following, taking into account \sref{IV.17.7.4}, (ii)):
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $f$ is flat at the point $x$ \sref[I]{I.2.2.11}, (iv));
  \item $f$ is locally of finite presentation and of coprofondeur $\leq n$ at the point $x$ (\sref{IV.6.8.1} and \sref{IV.6.7.8});
  \item $f$ is locally of finite presentation and Cohen--Macaulay at the point $x$ (\sref{IV.6.8.1} and \sref{IV.6.7.8});
  \item $f$ is locally of finite presentation and possesses the property $(\mathrm{S}_n)$ at the point $x$ (\sref{IV.6.8.1} and \sref{IV.6.7.8});
  \item $f$ is locally of finite presentation and possesses the property $(\mathrm{R}_n)$ at the point $x$ (\sref{IV.6.8.1} and \sref{IV.6.7.8});
  \item $f$ is locally of finite presentation and normal at the point $x$ (\sref{IV.6.8.1} and \sref{IV.6.7.8});
  \item $f$ is locally of finite presentation and reduced at the point $x$ (\sref{IV.6.8.1} and \sref{IV.6.7.8});
  \item $f$ is non ramified at the point $x$ \sref{IV.17.7.4};
  \item $f$ is smooth at the point $x$ \sref{IV.17.7.4};
  \item $f$ is \'etale at the point $x$ \sref{IV.17.7.4}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{examples}

\begin{corollary}[18.4.12]
\label{IV.18.4.12}
\oldpage[IV]{124}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$.
  If $f$ is flat and formally non ramified at the point $x$, then, in every factorisation $f|U:U\xrightarrow{j}X'\xrightarrow{h}Y$, where $U$ is an open neighbourhood of $x$, $j$ a closed immersion and $h$ an \'etale morphism \sref{IV.18.4.7}, the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{X',j(x)}\to\sh{O}_{X,x}$ corresponding to $j$ is bijective (in particular $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is an $\sh{O}_{Y,j(x)}$-algebra essentially \'etale \sref{IV.18.6.1}).
  \item For a morphism $f:X\to Y$ to be \'etale, it is necessary and sufficient that it be locally of finite type, formally non ramified and flat.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(i) As the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{X',j(x)}\to\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is surjective, it suffices to prove that it makes $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ an $\sh{O}_{X',j(x)}$-module \emph{faithfully flat}, or only \emph{flat} ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.6.5.1} and \sref{IV.6.6.2}); in other words, it suffices to show that $j$ is a morphism \emph{flat at the point $x$}; but since $h\circ j=f$ is by hypothesis flat at the point $x$ and $h$ is \'etale, this results from \sref{IV.18.4.10} and \sref{IV.18.4.11}, (i)).

(ii) There is only to prove the suffisance of the conditions stated.
For every $x\in X$, one has therefore in an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ a factorisation of $f|U$ having the properties considered in (i).
Since by hypothesis $f$ is flat and formally non ramified at \emph{all} the points of $U$, the result of (i) applies not only at $x$ but at all the points of $U$; this means that if $\mathscr{J}$ is the quasi-coherent Ideal of $\sh{O}_{X'}$ corresponding to the closed sub-prescheme of $X'$ associated to $j$, one has $\mathscr{J}_{j(z)}=0$ for every $z\in U$, hence $j$ is an open immersion, and $f$ is therefore \'etale at every point of $U$, hence at every point of $X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[18.4.13]
\label{IV.18.4.13}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$.
Suppose that $y$ admits an open neighbourhood which is a reduced prescheme with only a finite number of irreducible components.
Then, for $f$ to be \'etale at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be flat and formally non ramified at the point $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
There is only to show the suffisance of the condition.
The question being local on $X$ and $Y$, one can suppose \sref{IV.18.4.7} that $f$ factors as $X\xrightarrow{j}X'\xrightarrow{h}Y$ where $h$ is \'etale and $j$ is a closed immersion.
Then $X'$ is reduced \sref{IV.17.5.7} and, by replacing $X'$ by an open neighbourhood of $j(x)$, one can suppose that $X'$ has only a finite number of irreducible components.
Everything comes down to showing, with the notations of the proof of \sref{IV.18.4.12}, that $\mathscr{J}_{x'}=0$ for all the points $x'$ of a neighbourhood of $j(x)$ in $X'$, knowing that $\mathscr{J}_{j(z)}=0$ for every $z\in U$, one can replace $X'$ by an affine neighbourhood of $j(x)$, one can suppose that all the irreducible components of $X'$ contain $j(x)$; if $X'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')$, and if $\mathfrak{p}'$ is the prime ideal of $A'$ corresponding to the point $j(x)$, the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(A'_{\mathfrak{p}'})\to\operatorname{Spec}(A')$ is dominant, hence $A'\to A'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ is \emph{injective} since $A'$ is reduced ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.1.2.7}).
If $\mathscr{J}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}$, where $\mathfrak{J}$ is an ideal of $A'$, $\mathfrak{J}$ identifies with a part of $\mathfrak{J}_{\mathfrak{p}'}$, and the hypothesis $\mathfrak{J}_{\mathfrak{p}'}=0$ implies $\mathfrak{J}=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[18.4.14]
\label{IV.18.4.14}
Let $A$ be a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, residue field $k$, $B$ a finite A-algebra.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item For $B$ to be a formally non ramified A-algebra, it is necessary and sufficient that $B\otimes_A k$ be an \'etale $k$-algebra.
  \item For $B$ to be an \'etale A-algebra and that $B$ be a flat A-module (which is equivalent to saying ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.6.3.3}) that for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n}$ of $B$ (necessarily above $\mathfrak{m}$), $B_{\mathfrak{n}}$ is a flat A-module).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\oldpage[IV]{125}
\begin{proof}
One has evidently only to prove the suffisance of the stated conditions.
It is clear that (ii) results from (i) and from \sref{IV.18.4.12}, (ii)), taking into account \sref{IV.17.1.2}, (i)).
To prove (i) we remark that if $B\otimes_A k$ is an \'etale $k$-algebra, one has $\Omega^1_{(B\otimes_A k)/k}=0$ \sref{IV.17.2.1}.
But $\Omega^1_{(B\otimes_A k)/k}=\Omega^1_{B/A}\otimes_B k$ ($\mathbf{0}$, \sref{IV.20.5.5}) and since $B$ is an A-algebra of finite type, $\Omega^1_{B/A}$ is a $B$-module of finite type ($\mathbf{0}$, \sref{IV.20.4.7}).
But since $B$ is a finite A-algebra, $\mathfrak{m}B$ is contained in the radical of $B$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~V, \textsection~2, n\textsuperscript{o}~1, prop.~1), hence the Nakayama lemma proves that $\Omega^1_{B/A}=0$, and hence $B$ is an A-algebra formally non ramified \sref{IV.17.2.1}.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Henselian local rings.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.5}

\begin{env}[18.5.1]
\label{IV.18.5.1}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $\sh{E}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-Module locally free of finite rank; the dual $\check{\sh{E}}=\sh{H}\!om_{\sh{O}_X}(\sh{E},\sh{O}_X)$ is therefore an $\sh{O}_X$-Module locally free whose rank at every point of $X$ is equal to that of $\sh{E}$ at that point, and the canonical homomorphism $\sh{E}\to\sh{H}\!om_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}},\sh{O}_X)=(\check{\sh{E}})^\vee$ is \emph{bijective}.
For every morphism $X'\to X$, set $\sh{E}_{(X')}=\sh{E}\otimes_{\sh{O}_X}\sh{O}_{X'}$, which is an $\sh{O}_{X'}$-Module locally free, and consider the \emph{set} $\Gamma(X',\sh{E}_{(X')})$ of sections of this $\sh{O}_{X'}$-Module above $X'$.
We will see that one thus defines a \emph{contravariant representable functor}
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.5.1.1}
  {}^t\!\mathbf{V}_{\sh{E}}:X'\mapsto\Gamma(X',\sh{E}_{(X')})
\end{equation}
from the category of X-preschemes to that of \emph{sets} ($\mathbf{0}_{III}$, \sref{IV.8.1.8}).

Indeed, we first see that the X-prescheme $\mathbf{V}(\check{\sh{E}})$ ($\mathbf{II}$, \sref{IV.1.7.8}) \emph{represents} the functor ${}^t\!\mathbf{V}$.
It is indeed immediate that $(\sh{E}_{(X')})^\vee=(\check{\sh{E}})_{(X')}$, hence $\mathbf{V}(\check{\sh{E}})\times_X X'=\mathbf{V}((\sh{E}_{(X')})^\vee)$; taking into account ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.3.3.14}), one is led to define a bijection $\Gamma(\mathbf{V}(\check{\sh{E}}_{(X')})/X')\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(X',\sh{E})$, and to verify that the bijection canonical of $\Gamma(\mathbf{V}(\check{\sh{E}})/X)$ to $\Hom_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}},\sh{O}_X)$ ($\mathbf{II}$, \sref{IV.1.7.8}), and the transposition $u\mapsto{}^t\!u$ is a bijection of $\Hom_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}},\sh{O}_X)$ to $\Hom_{\sh{O}_X}(\sh{O}_X,\sh{E})=\Gamma(X,\sh{E})$, by reason of the identification of $(\check{\sh{E}})^\vee$ and $\sh{E}$.
The verification of the functorial property is immediate.

Recall ($\mathbf{II}$, \sref{IV.1.7.8}) that one has by definition $\mathbf{V}(\check{\sh{E}})=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{S}_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}}))$.
Let $\mathscr{J}$ be a quasi-coherent Ideal of $\mathbf{S}_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}})$, so that $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{S}_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}})/\mathscr{J})$ is a closed sub-prescheme of $\mathbf{V}(\check{\sh{E}})$; we will interpret it as a \emph{representing functor} from the category of X-preschemes to that of sets.

Let $\operatorname{Al}(X,\sh{E},\mathscr{J})$ denote the set of $u\in\Gamma(X,\sh{E})$ such that $\mathscr{J}$ is \emph{contained in the kernel of $v$}; it results immediately from these definitions that
\[
X'\mapsto\operatorname{Al}(X',\sh{E}_{(X')},\mathscr{J}\otimes_{\sh{O}_X}\sh{O}_{X'})
\]
is a functor represented by $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf{S}_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}})/\mathscr{J})$.

We note this X-prescheme also $\mathbf{Al}(\sh{E},\mathscr{J})$.
One will note that if $\mathscr{J}$ is an Ideal of finite type of $\mathbf{S}_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}})$, $\mathbf{Al}(\sh{E},\mathscr{J})$ is an X-prescheme of \emph{finite presentation}, since $\mathbf{S}_{\sh{O}_X}(\check{\sh{E}})$ is an $\sh{O}_X$-Algebra of finite presentation.

\begin{lemma}[18.5.3]
\label{IV.18.5.3}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $f:X\to S$ a morphism finite and locally free \sref{IV.18.2.7}.
Consider the contravariant functor from the category of S-preschemes to the category of sets
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.5.3.1}
  S'\mapsto\operatorname{Of}(X\times_S S')
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Of}(X\times_S S')$ is the set of parts at once open and closed of the underlying space of $X\times_S S'$.
Then this functor is representable by an S-prescheme $\mathbf{Of}(X)$, which is affine, \'etale and of finite presentation over $S$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[18.5.4]
\label{IV.18.5.4}
\oldpage[IV]{127}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $S_0$ a closed sub-prescheme of $S$; consider the following properties:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] For every finite morphism $g:S'\to S$, the canonical application
  \begin{equation}
  \tag{18.5.4.1}
  \label{IV.18.5.4.1}
    \operatorname{Of}(S')\to\operatorname{Of}(S'\times_S S_0) \qquad (\text{cf.\ \sref{IV.18.5.3}})
  \end{equation}
  is bijective.
  \item[a')] For every morphism finite and locally free $g:S'\to S$, the application \sref{IV.18.5.4.1} is bijective.
  \item[b)] For every \'etale and separated morphism $g:S'\to S$, the canonical application
  \begin{equation}
  \tag{18.5.4.2}
  \label{IV.18.5.4.2}
    \Gamma(S'/S)\to\Gamma(S'\times_S S_0/S_0)
  \end{equation}
  is bijective.
\end{enumerate}
The condition $b)$ implies $a')$; if moreover $S$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, the condition $a)$ implies $b)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV]{128}
Let us show first that $b)$ implies $a')$.
Suppose $b)$ verified, and let $g:S'\to S$ be a morphism finite and locally free; set $S'_0=S'\times_S S_0$, so that $g_0=g_{(S_0)}:S'_0\to S_0$ is finite and locally free.
Then it results from \sref{IV.18.5.3} that $P=\mathbf{Of}(S')$ is an S-prescheme \'etale and separated over $S$; moreover, the definition of the functor $\operatorname{Of}$ shows that if one sets $P_0=\mathbf{Of}(S'_0)$ (for the category of $S_0$-preschemes), one has $P_0=P\times_S S_0$.
We have therefore by definition the commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  \Gamma(P/S) \ar[r] \ar[d]_-{\ell} & \Gamma(P_0/S_0) \ar[d]^-{\ell} \\
  \operatorname{Of}(S') \ar[r] & \operatorname{Of}(S'_0)
}
\]
where the vertical arrows are the canonical bijections.
As the hypothesis $b)$, applied to the morphism $P\to S$, implies that the first line is a bijection, the same is true of the second, which establishes our assertion.

\label{IV.18.5.4.3}\emph{Lemma} \sref{IV.18.5.4.3}. ---
If $S$ and $S_0$ verify condition $a)$ of \sref{IV.18.5.4}, then, for every finite morphism $g:S'\to S$, $S'$ is the unique neighbourhood of $S'_0=g^{-1}(S_0)=S'\times_S S_0$ in $S'$.

Indeed, it amounts to the same to say that if $T'$ is a closed part of $S'$ such that $T'\cap S'_0=\varnothing$, then $T'=\varnothing$.
If one also designates by $T'$ a sub-prescheme of $S'$ with underlying space $T'$, the composed morphism $h:T'\to S'\xrightarrow{g}S$ is finite and $h^{-1}(S_0)$ is empty; the condition $a)$ applied to the morphism $h$ implies that $T'$ is necessarily empty.

This lemma established, let us prove first that, under the hypothesis $a)$, the application \sref{IV.18.5.4.2} is \emph{injective}.
Indeed, if $u'$, $u''$ are two S-sections of $S'$, the fact that the morphism $S'\to S$ is non ramified implies the prescheme of coincidences of $u'$ and $u''$ is induced on an open set $U$ of $S$ \sref{IV.17.4.6}.
If the restrictions of $u'$ and $u''$ to $S_0$ are the same, the fact that $u'$ and $u''$ are open immersions \sref{IV.17.4.1} implies that $U$ contains $S_0$, hence is equal to $S$ by virtue of the lemma \sref{IV.18.5.4.3} applied to the case where $S'=S$.

It remains to show that under hypothesis $a)$, the application \sref{IV.18.5.4.2} is \emph{surjective} ($S$ being quasi-compact and quasi-separated).
Let $u_0:S_0\to S'_0$ be an $S_0$-section of $S'_0$; $u_0(S_0)$ being quasi-compact in $S'$, can be covered by a finite number of open affines $V_i$ such that the restriction $g|V_i$ is a morphism of finite type; if $V$ is the reunion of the $V_i$, one concludes that $g|V:V\to S$ is a morphism of \emph{finite presentation}, being separated and locally of finite presentation by hypothesis (\sref{IV.1.6.1}).
Replacing $S'$ by $V$, one can hence suppose that $g$ is of finite presentation.
As $S$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated and $g$ quasi-finite and separated \sref{IV.17.6.1}, it results from the ``Main theorem'' \sref{IV.8.12.6} that $g$ factors as $S'\xrightarrow{j}S''\xrightarrow{f}S$, where $j$ is an open immersion and $f$ a finite morphism.
Set $S''_0=S''\times_S S_0$, $j_0=j_{(S_0)}:S'_0\to S''_0$, which is an open immersion and $f_0=f_{(S_0)}:S''_0\to S_0$, which is a finite morphism.
Then $u_0$ is also an $S_0$-section of $S''_0$.
As $g_0:S'_0\to S_0$ is \'etale, $u_0$ is an open immersion of $S_0$ in $S'_0$ \sref{IV.17.4.1}, hence \emph{a fortiori} in $S''_0$; but on the other hand, as $f_0$ is a finite morphism, hence separated, $u_0$ is a closed immersion of $S_0$ in $S''_0$ ($\mathbf{I}$, 5.4.6), hence $X_0=u_0(S_0)$ is \emph{at once open and closed} in $S''_0$.
By virtue of hypothesis $a)$, there exists an open and closed set $X$ of $S''$ such that $X\cap S''_0=X_0$.
\oldpage[IV]{129}
Let us show first that the morphism $f:S''\to S$ is \'etale at the points of $X$; indeed, the set $U$ of points of $X$ where $f|X$ is \'etale is open and contains by hypothesis $X_0\subset S'_0$.
But the lemma \sref{IV.18.5.4.3} applied to the finite morphism $f|X$ proves that $U=X$.
On the other hand, $S'\cap X$ is open in $X$ and contains $X_0$ by hypothesis, hence the same argument proves that $S'\cap X=X$, that is to say $X\subset S'$.
It remains to show that, for every $s\in S$, the geometric number $n(s)$ of points of $X\cap f^{-1}(s)$ is equal to $1$, for it will result that $f|X$ is radiciel and surjective, and as $f|X$ is \'etale, one will have shown \sref{IV.17.9.1} that $f|X$ is an open isomorphism of $X\subset S'$ over $S$, whose inverse will be the S-section sought.
But as $f|X$ is \'etale and \emph{finite}, $s\mapsto n(s)$ is continuous in $S$ \sref{IV.18.2.8}, and as $X\cap S'_0=X_0$, one has $n(s)=1$ in $S_0$; the set of points $s\in S$ such that $n(s)=1$ being open in $S$ and containing $S_0$, it is equal to $S$ by \sref{IV.18.5.4.3}. Q.E.D.
\end{proof}

One can show that the statement \sref{IV.18.5.4} remains valid when, in condition $b)$, one supposes only the morphism $g$ \'etale (but not necessarily separated)~\cite{IV-43}.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[18.5.5]
\label{IV.18.5.5}
One says that a prescheme $S$ and a closed sub-prescheme $S_0$ of $S$ form a \emph{Henselian couple} if they verify condition $a)$ of \sref{IV.18.5.4}.
\end{definition}

Taking into account ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.5.1.8}), it amounts to the same to say that $(S,S_0)$ is a Henselian couple or that $(S_{\mathrm{red}},(S_0)_{\mathrm{red}})$ is one.

\begin{proposition}[18.5.6]
\label{IV.18.5.6}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $(S,S_0)$ is a Henselian couple, then, for every finite morphism $f:S'\to S$, if $S'_0$ is the sub-prescheme $f^{-1}(S_0)$ of $S'$, the couple $(S',S'_0)$ is Henselian.
  \item Let $S=\coprod S^{(\alpha)}$ be a sum of preschemes, $S_0$ a closed sub-prescheme of $S$, sum of the closed sub-preschemes $S_0^{(\alpha)}$ of the $S^{(\alpha)}$.
  For the couple $(S,S_0)$ to be Henselian, it is necessary and sufficient that each of the couples $(S^{(\alpha)},S_0^{(\alpha)})$ be Henselian.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[18.5.7]
\label{IV.18.5.7}
\oldpage[IV]{130}
Let $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ be an affine scheme, $S_0$ a closed sub-prescheme of $S$ defined by an ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ of $A$.
Then, if the couple $(S,S_0)$ is Henselian, the ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ is necessarily contained in the \emph{radical} of $A$.
Indeed, if $\mathfrak{m}$ is a maximal ideal of $A$, it must belong to $V(\mathfrak{J})=S_0$, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.5.4.3}, otherwise $\mathfrak{m}$ would not be a maximal ideal, which gives the conclusion.
In particular, suppose that $S_0$ is \emph{reduced to a point}, that is to say that the ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ is \emph{maximal}; then $\mathfrak{J}$ must be the radical of $A$, in other words $A$ must be a \emph{local} ring and $S_0$ the unique closed point of $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[18.5.8]
\label{IV.18.5.8}
One says that a ring $A$ is \emph{Henselian} if it is semi-local and if, denoting by $\mathfrak{r}$ the radical of $A$, the couple $(\operatorname{Spec}(A),\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{r}))$ is Henselian.
One calls a \emph{Henselian scheme} a scheme isomorphic to the spectrum of a Henselian local ring.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[18.5.9]
\label{IV.18.5.9}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item For a semi-local ring $A$ to be Henselian, it is necessary and sufficient that it be a direct composition of Henselian local rings.
  \item For a local ring $A$ to be Henselian, it is necessary and sufficient that every finite A-algebra $B$ be isomorphic to a product of local rings.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Indeed, the definition, applied to $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ and $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{r})$, shows that, since $S_0$ is a finite discrete set of points and closed, $S$ is the reunion of a finite number of open and closed parts $S_i$ $(1\leq i\leq n)$, pairwise disjoint, each of which contains exactly one of the maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}_i$ of $A$; the conclusion results from \sref{IV.18.5.6}, (ii)) and from the remark \sref{IV.18.5.7}.

(ii) Every finite morphism $S'\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is of the form $\operatorname{Spec}(B)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, where $B$ is a finite A-algebra.
If $k$ is the residue field of $A$, $\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_A k)$ is a spectrum of an artinian ring, hence finite and discrete.
To say that the couple $(\operatorname{Spec}(A),\operatorname{Spec}(k))$ is Henselian means therefore that $B$ is a direct composition of local rings $A_i$ such that $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i\otimes_A k)$ is reduced to a point, that is to say that $A_i$ (which is a finite A-algebra) must have only one maximal ideal (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~V, \textsection~2, n\textsuperscript{o}~1, prop.~1).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[18.5.10]
\label{IV.18.5.10}
If $A$ is a Henselian ring, every finite A-algebra $B$ is a Henselian ring (hence a direct composition of Henselian local rings \sref{IV.18.5.9}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, if $\mathfrak{r}'$ is the radical of $B$, the inverse image of $\mathfrak{r}'$ in $A$ is the radical $\mathfrak{r}$, and every ideal of $B$ above a maximal ideal of $A$ is a maximal ideal of $B$, hence $V(\mathfrak{r}')$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ is the inverse image of $V(\mathfrak{r})$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$.
The proposition is then a consequence of \sref{IV.18.5.6}, (i)).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[18.5.11]
\label{IV.18.5.11}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $\mathfrak{m}$ its maximal ideal.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $A$ is Henselian, in other words, every finite A-algebra $B$ is isomorphic to a product of local rings.
  \item[a')] The condition $a)$ is satisfied for all A-algebras $B$ of the form $A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$, where $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$ is a unitary polynomial.
  \item[b)] Let $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(k)$.
  For every \'etale morphism $g:S'\to S$, if one sets $S'_0=S'\otimes_S S_0$, every $S_0$-section $u_0$ of $S'_0$ is the restriction of an S-section $u$ of $S'$.
\oldpage[IV]{131}
  \item[c)] For every morphism $f:X\to S$, separated and locally of finite type, and every point $x\in X$ such that $f(x)$ is equal to the closed point $s$ of $S$ and that $f$ is quasi-finite at the point $x$ ($\mathbf{Err}_{\mathbf{III}}$, 20), $X$ is the sum of two preschemes $X'$, $X''$ such that $X'=\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{X,x})$ and that $f|X':X'\to S$ is a finite morphism.
  \item[c')] For every morphism locally of finite type $f:X\to S$, and every point $x\in X$ such that $f(x)$ is quasi-finite at the closed point $s$ of $S$, $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a finite algebra over $\sh{O}_{S,s}=A$.
  \item[c'')] For every morphism locally of finite presentation $f:X\to S$, and every point $x\in X$ such that $f$ is quasi-finite at the point $x$, and that $f(x)$ is equal to the closed point $s$ of $S$, $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a finite algebra and of finite presentation over $\sh{O}_{S,s}=A$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Note first that the condition $c')$ (resp.\ $c'')$) is equivalent to the same condition where one supposes moreover $f$ separated, the question being local on $X$.
In the same way, the condition $b)$ is equivalent to the same condition where one supposes moreover $g$ \emph{separated}: indeed, it suffices to apply the latter to the restriction of $g$ to an open affine neighbourhood of the point $u_0(S_0)$ in $S'$.
We restrict henceforth to the case where, in $b)$, $c')$ and $c'')$, the given morphisms are \emph{separated}.

The fact that $a)$ implies $b)$ and that $b)$ implies $a')$ is a particular case of \sref{IV.18.5.4}.
Let us show moreover that $a')$ implies $a)$.
It suffices to prove that if $e_0$ is an idempotent of $C=B\otimes_A k$, there exists an idempotent $e\in B$ whose image canonical is $e_0$.
If $b$ is an element of $B$ whose image in $C$ is $e_0$, the sub-A-algebra $A[b]=B'$ of $B$ is finite, $B'\otimes_A k$ is a $k$-algebra finite, hence a direct composition of $k$-algebras local finites, and hence $e_0$ is the image in $C'$ of an idempotent $e'_0$ of $B'\otimes_A k$.
Or, $B'\otimes_A k$ is a $k$-algebra finite, hence a direct composition of fields, separable finite extensions of $k$; and hence $B$ is thus led to the case where $B$ is monogene, and hence isomorphic to a quotient algebra of the form $A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$, where $F$ is a unitary polynomial.
Or, by virtue of $a')$, $A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$ is a direct composition of local rings, hence it is the same for all its quotient algebras, which achieves the proof of the existence of the idempotent $e$.

It is immediate that $c)$ implies $a)$, as one sees by reasoning by recurrence on the number of maximal ideals of the semi-local ring $B$.
To show that $a)$ implies $c)$, one can, by virtue of \sref{IV.13.1.4}, restrict to the case where $f$ is an affine and quasi-finite morphism, se borner au cas o\`u $f$ est un morphisme affine et quasi-fini.
Then, by application of the ``Main theorem'' \sref{IV.8.12.8}, $f$ can be written as a composed morphism $X\xrightarrow{j}Y\xrightarrow{g}S$, where $g$ is a finite morphism and $j$ an open immersion.
As $B$ is a finite A-algebra, it results from $a)$ that $B$ is a direct composition of local rings, and $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ identifies with one of these local rings; moreover, every open set of $Y$ containing $x$ contains necessarily $\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{X,x})$.
\oldpage[IV]{132}

It is trivial that $c)$ implies $c')$, by virtue of the initial remark.
Let us prove that $c')$ implies $c'')$.
Suppose $c')$ verified, and let us prove that under the conditions of $c'')$, the set $Z=\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{X,x})$ identifies with an open and closed part of $X$, which will establish $c'')$ ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.2.4.2}).
First, the composed morphism $Z\xrightarrow{j}X\xrightarrow{f}S$, where $j$ is the canonical morphism ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.2.4.1}), is finite and of finite presentation by hypothesis, and since $f$ is separated and locally of finite presentation, $j$ is also a morphism finite and of finite presentation (($\mathbf{II}$, \sref{IV.6.1.5}) and \sref{IV.1.4.3})); $j$ is therefore a closed morphism (($\mathbf{II}$, \sref{IV.6.1.10}), which proves that $Z$ is closed in $X$.
But if $\mathscr{J}$ is the Ideal of $\sh{O}_X$ defining $Z$, on has by hypothesis also $\mathscr{J}_z=0$ in every point $z$ of an open neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ in $X$, since by hypothesis $\mathscr{J}$ is an $\sh{O}_X$-Module quasi-coherent of finite type ((\sref{IV.1.4.7}) and ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.5.2.2})).
Now, such a neighbourhood contains $Z$, hence $Z$ is open in $X$.

Finally, $c'')$ implies $a')$: indeed, if $B=A[\mathrm{T}]/F.A[\mathrm{T}]$, the morphism $X=\operatorname{Spec}(B)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)=Y$ is finite and of finite presentation, and the preceding demonstration shows that $B$ is a direct composition of finite A-algebras $\sh{O}_{X,x_i}$, where the $x_i$ are the points of the fibre of the closed point of $Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[18.5.12]
\label{IV.18.5.12}
Let $A$ be a semi-local ring, $\mathfrak{r}$ its radical; set $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{r})$.
For $A$ to be Henselian, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every finite morphism $f:X\to S$ and every \'etale and separated morphism $g:Y\to S$, if one sets $X_0=X\times_S S_0$ and $Y_0=Y\times_S S_0$, the canonical application
\[
\Hom_S(X,Y)\to\Hom_{S_0}(X_0,Y_0)
\]
is bijective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The suffisance of the condition results from the equivalence of $a)$ and $b)$ in \sref{IV.18.5.11}, by applying this condition to the case $f=1_S$.
To see that the condition is necessary, note that if $A$ is Henselian, the couple $(X,X_0)$ is Henselian by \sref{IV.18.5.6}, (i)); moreover $\Hom_S(X,Y)=\Gamma(X\times_S Y/X)$ and $\Hom_{S_0}(X_0,Y_0)=\Gamma(X_0\times_{S_0}Y_0/X_0)$; as $X\times_S Y$ is \'etale and separated over $X$, the conclusion results from the fact that $a)$ implies $b)$ in \sref{IV.18.5.4}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[18.5.13]
\label{IV.18.5.13}
The equivalent conditions $a)$, $a')$, $b)$ of the theorem \sref{IV.18.5.11} are moreover equivalent to the following (``Hensel's lemma''):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a'')] For every unitary polynomial $F\in A[\mathrm{T}]$, with canonical image $F_0\in k[\mathrm{T}]$, and every decomposition $F_0=G_0 H_0$ of $F_0$ into a product of two \emph{coprime} unitary polynomials $G_0$, $H_0$ of $k[\mathrm{T}]$, there exists a unique couple $(G,H)$ of unitary polynomials of $A[\mathrm{T}]$ possessing the following properties: $G_0$ and $H_0$ are the respective canonical images of $G$ and $H$, one has $F=GH$, and the ideal of $A[\mathrm{T}]$ generated by $G$ and $H$ is equal to $A[\mathrm{T}]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\begin{proposition}[18.5.14]
\label{IV.18.5.14}
\oldpage[IV]{133}
Every semi-local ring $A$, separated and complete for the $\mathfrak{r}$-preadic topology (where $\mathfrak{r}$ is the radical of $A$) is Henselian.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, $A$ is a direct composition of local rings separated and complete (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~III, \textsection~2, n\textsuperscript{o}~13, cor.\ of the prop.~19), hence one is reduced to the case where $A$ is a local ring.
Let us verify the criterion $a')$ of \sref{IV.18.5.11}.
As $B$ is a free A-module of finite type, $B$ is evidently separated and complete for the $\mathfrak{r}$-preadic topology ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.7.3.6}).
Replacing $B$ by $A$ and $\mathfrak{J}B$ by $\mathfrak{J}$, everything comes down to seeing that the application which, to every idempotent of $A$, associates its class modulo $\mathfrak{J}$, is \emph{bijective}.
But one has $A=\varprojlim(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$.
Let us denote $\operatorname{Idem}(A)$ the set of idempotents of $A$, and for every ring homomorphism $\varphi:A\to B$, let $\operatorname{Idem}(\varphi)$ be the application of $\operatorname{Idem}(A)$ into $\operatorname{Idem}(B)$, the restriction of $\varphi$; it results from the definition of the projective limit that $\operatorname{Idem}(A)=\varprojlim\operatorname{Idem}(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$ for the applications $\psi_{nm}:\operatorname{Idem}(A/\mathfrak{J}^m)\to\operatorname{Idem}(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$ restrictions of the canonical applications $A/\mathfrak{J}^m\to A/\mathfrak{J}^n$.
But since $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{J}^m)$ is a homeomorphism, the $\psi_{nm}$ are bijections (as one saw in the proof of \sref{IV.18.5.3}); this proves our assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[18.5.15]
\label{IV.18.5.15}
\oldpage[IV]{134}
Let $A$ be a Henselian ring, $\mathfrak{r}$ its radical.
Then the functor $B\mapsto B/\mathfrak{r}B$ is an equivalence of the category of finite and \'etale A-algebras with the category of $(A/\mathfrak{r})$-algebras finite and \'etale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The fact that the stated functor is fully faithful is a particular case of \sref{IV.18.5.12}.
To show that this functor is an equivalence of categories, one can restrict to the case where $A$ is local; it suffices then to apply \sref{IV.18.1.1} (for \'etale morphisms), to $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ and $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{r})$, reduced to a single point.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[18.5.16]
\label{IV.18.5.16}
(i) We note that if the proposition \sref{IV.18.5.15} generalises to a Henselian couple $(S,S_0)$, even when $S$ is affine and Noetherian.

(ii) Let $A$ be a \emph{Noetherian} ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$ such that $A$ is \emph{separated and complete} for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology.
Then the couple $(\operatorname{Spec}(A),\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{J}))$ is Henselian: indeed, for every finite A-algebra $B$, $B$ is separated and complete for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.7.3.6}).
Replacing $B$ par $A$ and $\mathfrak{J}B$ by $\mathfrak{J}$, everything comes down to seeing that the application which, to every idempotent of $A$, associates its class modulo $\mathfrak{J}$, is bijective.
But one has $A=\varprojlim(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$, and since $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak{J}^m)$ is a homeomorphism, the $\psi_{nm}$ are bijections.
\end{remarks}

\begin{theorem}[18.5.17]
\label{IV.18.5.17}
Let $A$ be a Henselian local ring, $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $s$ the closed point of $S$.
For every smooth morphism $f:X\to S$, the canonical application
\[
\Gamma(X/S)\to\Gamma(X_s/k(s))
\]
(where $X_s=f^{-1}(s)$) is surjective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The data of a $k(s)$-section of $X_s$ is equivalent to that of a rational point $x\in X$ above $s$ over $k(s)$, and it suffices to prove that there exists an S-section $u:S\to X$ such that $u(s)=x$.
Taking into account \sref{IV.17.16.3}, (i)), one can suppose that $f$ is \emph{\'etale}.
Then the conclusion results from the criterion \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $b)$).
(The reader will note that by virtue of this criterion, the validity of \sref{IV.18.5.17} for a given local ring $A$ is a necessary and sufficient condition for $A$ to be Henselian).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[18.5.18]
\label{IV.18.5.18}
The conditions of \sref{IV.18.5.11} are moreover equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[d)] For every morphism locally of finite type $f:X\to S$ and every point $x\in X$ such that $f(x)$ is the closed point $s$ of $S$ and that $\sh{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_s\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a field canonically isomorphic to $k(s)=k$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$ such that $f|U$ is a closed immersion.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[18.5.19]
\label{IV.18.5.19}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian and Henselian local ring, $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $s$ the closed point of $S$, $f:X\to S$ a proper morphism; set $X_s=f^{-1}(s)$.
Then the application $Y\mapsto Y\cap X_s$ is a bijection of the set of connected components of $X$ to the set of connected components of $X_s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By considering a closed sub-prescheme of $X$ having for underlying space a connected component of $X$, one is led to prove that if $X$ is \emph{connected and non empty}, then $X_s$ is \emph{connected and non empty}; the fact that $X_s$ is non empty results from ($\mathbf{II}$, \sref{IV.7.2.1}); to prove that $X_s$ is connected, reason by the absurd, by considering the Stein factorisation of $f$ ($\mathbf{III}$, \sref{IV.4.3.3}); by hypothesis, the finite discrete set $g^{-1}(s)$ would contain at least two points.
Since $A$ is Henselian and $g$ separated and of finite type, it would result from \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $c)$) that $S'$ would be a sum of two non empty preschemes $S'_1$, $S'_2$ (since the intersection of one of them with $g^{-1}(s)$ is reduced to a single point).
Since $f'$ is surjective ($\mathbf{III}$, \sref{IV.4.3.1}), one would conclude that $X$ is a sum of two non empty preschemes, contradicting the hypothesis.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Henselisation.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.6}

\begin{env}[18.6.1]
\label{IV.18.6.1}
Given a local ring $A$, we will say that a local A-algebra $B$ is \emph{essentially \'etale} if there exists an \'etale A-algebra $C$ and a prime ideal $\mathfrak{n}$ of $C$ such that $B$ is A-isomorphic to $C_{\mathfrak{n}}$ and that the composed homomorphism $A\to C\to C_{\mathfrak{n}}$ is \emph{local} (in other words, that $\mathfrak{n}$ is above the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $A$); this implies \sref{IV.17.6.1} that $\mathfrak{n}$ is the \emph{only} prime ideal of $B$ above $\mathfrak{m}$ since $B$ is a local ring; if $B$, $B'$ are two local A-algebras essentially \'etale, every A-homomorphism $B\to B'$ is hence \emph{local}.

If $A'$ is a local A-algebra essentially \'etale and $A''$ an $A'$-algebra locally essentially \'etale, then $A''$ is a local A-algebra essentially \'etale.
Indeed, one has by hypothesis $A'=B_{\mathfrak{n}}$ where $B$ is an \'etale A-algebra and $\mathfrak{n}$ a prime ideal of $B$ above the maximal ideal of $A$, and $A''=B'_{\mathfrak{n}'}$, where $B'$ is an $A'$-algebra \'etale and $\mathfrak{n}'$ a prime ideal of $B'$ above the ideal $\mathfrak{n}B_{\mathfrak{n}}$.
If one sets $S=B-\mathfrak{n}$, so that $A'=S^{-1}B$, $B'$ is of the form $S^{-1}C$, where $C$ is a B-algebra of finite presentation.
Hence $C$ is an A-algebra of finite presentation, and $A''$ is of the form $C_{\mathfrak{r}}$, where $\mathfrak{r}$ is a prime ideal of $C$ above the maximal ideal of $A$.
As $A'$ is an A-algebra formally \'etale and $A''$ an $A'$-algebra formally \'etale (for the discrete topologies \sref{IV.17.1.3}); the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(C)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is hence \'etale at the point $\mathfrak{r}$ \sref{IV.17.6.1}, and hence there is an element $g\in C$ such that $C_g$ is an A-algebra \'etale, which proves that $A''$ is a local A-algebra essentially \'etale.
\end{env}

\oldpage[IV]{136}
\begin{env}[18.6.1bis]
\label{IV.18.6.1bis}
Given a local ring $A$, there exists an \emph{ensemble} $\mathfrak{E}$ of local A-algebras essentially \'etale such that every local A-algebra essentially \'etale is A-isomorphic to an algebra belonging to $\mathfrak{E}$.
It suffices evidently for this to observe that there exists an ensemble $\mathfrak{F}$ of A-algebras of finite type such that every A-algebra of finite type is isomorphic to an algebra belonging to $\mathfrak{F}$; one can take $\mathfrak{F}$ equal to the set of quotients of polynomial algebras $A[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_n]$ ($n\in\bb{N}$).
\end{env}

In what follows, if $A$ and $B$ are two local rings, we will denote $\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A,B)$ the set of \emph{local} homomorphisms of $A$ into $B$.

\begin{lemma}[18.6.2]
\label{IV.18.6.2}
Let $A$, $A'$ be two local rings, $k$, $k'$ their respective residue fields, $\varphi:A\to A'$ a local homomorphism making $A'$ a local A-algebra essentially \'etale \sref{IV.18.6.1} and such that the corresponding homomorphism $k\to k'$ is bijective.
Then, for every Henselian local ring $B$, the canonical application
\[
\Hom(\varphi,1_B):\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A',B)\to\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A,B)
\]
is bijective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(B)$, and let $s$ and $y$ be the closed points of $S$ and $Y$ respectively.
By hypothesis, $A'$ is isomorphic to a local ring $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ of an S-scheme $X$ \'etale on $S$, at a point $x\in X$ above $s$, and note that since $k(x)=k(s)$, it exists a unique point $x'\in X$ above $x$ and $y$, and that $k(x')=k(y)$.
If one sets $Y_0=\operatorname{Spec}(k(y))$, there exists hence a unique $Y_0$-section $f'_0$ of $X'_0$ such that $f'_0(y)=x'$, and the conclusion results from this that $B$ is supposed Henselian and from \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $b)$).
\end{proof}

We will say that a local A-algebra $A'$ verifying the conditions of \sref{IV.18.6.2} is \emph{strictly essentially \'etale}.
Note that the criterion \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $b)$) signifies that, \emph{for $A$ to be Henselian, it is necessary and sufficient that every A-algebra strictly essentially \'etale be A-isomorphic to $A$}.

\begin{lemma}[18.6.3]
\label{IV.18.6.3}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $A_1$, $A_2$ two local A-algebras strictly essentially \'etale.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item There exists at most one A-homomorphism (necessarily local) of $A_1$ in $A_2$.
  \item There exists a local A-algebra strictly essentially \'etale $A_3$ and two A-homomorphisms $A_1\to A_3$, $A_2\to A_3$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{env}[18.6.4]
\label{IV.18.6.4}
\oldpage[IV]{137}
Let us designate by $\mathfrak{G}$ the sub-ensemble of the ensemble $\mathfrak{E}$ defined in \sref{IV.18.6.1} formed of A-algebras strictly essentially \'etale belonging to $\mathfrak{E}$.
It results from \sref{IV.18.6.3} that the relation ``there exists an A-homomorphism of $A_1$ in $A_2$'' is a relation of \emph{preorder} in $\mathfrak{G}$ and makes $\mathfrak{G}$ a \emph{filtering directed} set.
Indexing $\mathfrak{G}$ by itself via the identity application, it results from \sref{IV.18.6.3}, (i)) that if $\lambda\leq\mu$ in $\mathfrak{G}$, there is a unique A-homomorphism $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}:A_\lambda\to A_\mu$ and $(A_\lambda,\varphi_{\mu\lambda})$ is evidently an \emph{inductive system} of local A-algebras, the $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}$ being local homomorphisms.
Moreover, it results from \sref{IV.17.3.5} that for $\lambda\leq\mu$, $A_\mu$ is an $A_\lambda$-algebra strictly essentially \'etale.
\end{env}

\begin{definition}[18.6.5]
\label{IV.18.6.5}
One calls the \emph{henselisation} of a local ring $A$ and one denotes ${}^hA$ the A-algebra inductive limit of the inductive system $(A_\lambda,\varphi_{\mu\lambda})$ defined in \sref{IV.18.6.4}.
\end{definition}

This definition does not depend on the apparent choice of $\mathfrak{E}$; if $\mathfrak{E}'$ is another ensemble of local A-algebras essentially \'etale having the same property as $\mathfrak{E}$, $\mathfrak{G}'$ the sub-ensemble of $\mathfrak{E}'$ formed of strictly essentially \'etale A-algebras, it results from \sref{IV.18.6.3}, (ii)) that, for the preorder relations considered, $\mathfrak{G}$ and $\mathfrak{G}'$ are \emph{cofinal} to $\mathfrak{G}''=\mathfrak{G}\cup\mathfrak{G}'$, hence they give the same inductive limit up to a unique isomorphism.

One will remark that if $A$ is a field, one has evidently ${}^hA=A$.
In the general case, ${}^hA$ is also the henselisation of all the A-algebras strictly essentially \'etale $A_\lambda$ ($\lambda\in\mathfrak{G}$), for if $B$ is an $A_\lambda$-algebra strictly essentially \'etale, $B$ is also an A-algebra strictly essentially \'etale \sref{IV.18.6.1}, hence (up to A-isomorphism) one of the $A_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ and such that $A_\mu$ is an $A_\lambda$-algebra strictly essentially \'etale, which forms a cofinal set in the preordered set of $A_\lambda$, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.6.1} and \sref{IV.18.6.3}.

\begin{theorem}[18.6.6]
\label{IV.18.6.6}
Let $A$ be a local ring, ${}^hA$ its henselisation and the structural homomorphism $A\to{}^hA$ is local.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item ${}^hA$ is a Henselian local ring and the structural homomorphism $A\to{}^hA$ is local.
  \item For every Henselian local ring $B$, the canonical application
  \[
  \Hom.\mathrm{loc}({}^hA,B)\to\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A,B)
  \]
  is bijective.
  \item ${}^hA$ is an A-module faithfully flat, and if $\mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{m}.{}^hA$ is the maximal ideal of ${}^hA$, and the homomorphism $A/\mathfrak{m}A\to{}^hA/\mathfrak{m}.{}^hA$ of the residue fields is bijective.
  \item If $\hat{A}$ and $({}^hA)^\wedge$ are the separated completions of the local rings $A$ and ${}^hA$, the homomorphism $\hat{A}\to({}^hA)^\wedge$ deduced from the structural homomorphism $A\to{}^hA$ by completion is bijective.
  \item For ${}^hA$ to be Noetherian, it is necessary and sufficient that $A$ be Noetherian.
  \item If $A$ is Henselian, the canonical homomorphism $A\to{}^hA$ is bijective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\oldpage[IV]{138}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathfrak{m}_\lambda$ be the maximal ideal of $A_\lambda$; it results from the fact that, in \sref{IV.17.6.1}, $a)$), $\mathfrak{m}_\lambda=\mathfrak{m}_\lambda A_\mu$, the homomorphism $A_\lambda/\mathfrak{m}_\lambda\to A_\mu/\mathfrak{m}_\mu$ is bijective and $A_\mu$ is a flat $A_\lambda$-module.
The fact that ${}^hA$ is local, that the homomorphism $A\to{}^hA$ is local, the assertion (iii) and the suffisance of (v) result therefore from ($\mathbf{0}_{III}$, \sref{IV.10.3.1.3}); the necessity of (v) results from the fact that ${}^hA$ is an A-module faithfully flat ($\mathbf{0}_I$, \sref{IV.6.5.2}).

To prove that ${}^hA$ is Henselian, apply the criterion \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $b)$).
Set $S=\operatorname{Spec}({}^hA)$, $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}({}^hA/\mathfrak{m}.{}^hA)$, and let $g:S'\to S$ be a morphism \'etale; set $S'_0=g^{-1}(S_0)$ and let $f_0:S_0\to S'_0$ be an $S_0$-section of $S'_0$.
Reasoning as usual in \sref{IV.18.5.4}, one can suppose that $g$ is of finite presentation.
It results from \sref{IV.8.8.2} and \sref{IV.17.7.5} that there exists an index $\lambda\in S$, a morphism \'etale $g_\lambda:S'^{(\lambda)}\to S^{(\lambda)}=\operatorname{Spec}(A_\lambda)$ and, if one sets $S_0^{(\lambda)}=\operatorname{Spec}(A_\lambda/\mathfrak{m}A_\lambda)$, an $S_0^{(\lambda)}$-section $f_0^{(\lambda)}:S_0^{(\lambda)}\to S_0'^{(\lambda)}$ such that $S'=S'^{(\lambda)}\times_{g^{(\lambda)}}S$, $g=g^{(\lambda)}\times 1$ and $f_0=f_0^{(\lambda)}\times 1$.
Let $s$ be the closed point of $S$, $x=f_0(s)$; as $x_\lambda$ is above the closed point $s_\lambda$ of $S^{(\lambda)}$, the local ring $C_\lambda$ of $S'^{(\lambda)}$ at the point $x_\lambda$ is an $A_\lambda$-algebra essentially \'etale; moreover, as $f_0^{(\lambda)}(s_\lambda)=x_\lambda$, one has $k(x_\lambda)=k(s_\lambda)$, in other words, $C_\lambda$ is an $A_\lambda$-algebra strictly essentially \'etale, and hence by \sref{IV.18.6.1} an $A_\lambda$-isomorphism $C_\lambda\to{}^hA$, that is to say there is an $A_\lambda$-homomorphism $C_\lambda\to{}^hA$, and hence an $S^{(\lambda)}$-morphism $h:S\to S'^{(\lambda)}$ such that $h(s)=x_\lambda$, and hence there is indeed an S-section $f$ of $S'$ such that $f(s)=x$, which achieves the proof of (i).

To prove (ii), it suffices to note that $\Hom.\mathrm{loc}({}^hA,B)\cong\varprojlim\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A_\lambda,B)$ and that by \sref{IV.18.6.2} the canonical homomorphisms $\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A_\lambda,B)\leftarrow\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A,B)$ are bijective.

To demonstrate (iv), note that for every $\lambda$ and every integer $n>0$, $\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^n=\mathfrak{m}^n A_\lambda$, and $(\mathfrak{m}.{}^hA)^n=\mathfrak{m}^n.{}^hA=\varinjlim\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^n$.
One concludes, by exactness of the functor $\varinjlim$ in the category of $A$-modules, that ${}^hA/(\mathfrak{m}.{}^hA)^n=\varinjlim(A_\lambda/\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^n)$, and hence it suffices to show that for every $n$, and every index $\lambda$, the homomorphism $A/\mathfrak{m}^n\to A_\lambda/\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^n$ is bijective.
This is true by hypothesis for $n=1$; on the other hand, since $A_\lambda$ is a flat A-module, one has
\[
\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^n/\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^{n+1}=(\mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1})\otimes_A A_\lambda=(\mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1})\otimes_{A/\mathfrak{m}}(A_\lambda/\mathfrak{m}A_\lambda)
\]
and as $A/\mathfrak{m}\to A_\lambda/\mathfrak{m}_\lambda=A_\lambda/\mathfrak{m}A_\lambda$ is bijective, the homomorphism $\mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}\to\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^n/\mathfrak{m}_\lambda^{n+1}$ is also bijective; the conclusion results from Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~III, \textsection~2, n\textsuperscript{o}~8, cor.~3 du th.~1.

Finally, if $A$ is Henselian, it results from the remark following \sref{IV.18.6.3} that the homomorphisms $A\to A_\lambda$ are bijective, which proves (vi) by definition of ${}^hA$.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[18.6.7]

Let $A$ be a \emph{semi-local} ring, $\mathfrak{m}_i$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$ its maximal ideals.
One calls the \emph{henselisation} of $A$ and denotes again ${}^hA$ the ring \emph{product} $\prod_i{}^h(A_{\mathfrak{m}_i})$ of the henselisations of the local rings $A_{\mathfrak{m}_i}$.

\begin{proposition}[18.6.14]
\label{IV.18.6.14}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Every filtering inductive limit of Henselian local rings (where the transition homomorphisms are local) is a Henselian local ring.
  \item The functor $A\mapsto{}^hA$ in the category of local rings (where the morphisms are local homomorphisms) commutes with filtering inductive limits.
  \item Every Henselian local ring $A$ is the inductive limit of an inductive system filtering of Henselian and Noetherian local rings $A_\lambda$ (the transition homomorphisms $A_\lambda\to A_\mu$ being local).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) Let $(A_\lambda)$ be a filtering inductive system of Henselian local rings, the transition homomorphisms being local.
Let us show that $A'=\varinjlim A_\lambda$, which is local ($\mathbf{0}_{III}$, \sref{IV.10.3.1.3}).
Let $C$ be an $A'$-algebra \'etale, $\mathfrak{n}$ a prime ideal of $C$ above the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}'$ of $A'$, such that $C_{\mathfrak{n}}$ is strictly essentially \'etale over $A'$ \sref{IV.18.6.1}, in other words that $k(\mathfrak{m}')\to k(\mathfrak{n})$ is an isomorphism.
By virtue of \sref{IV.8.8.2}, (ii)) and of \sref{IV.17.7.8}, there exists an index $\lambda$ and an $A_\lambda$-algebra \'etale $C_\lambda$ such that $C=C_\lambda\otimes_{A_\lambda}A'$ up to isomorphism; moreover, if $\mathfrak{n}_\lambda$ is the inverse image of $\mathfrak{n}$ in $C_\lambda$, and $\mathfrak{m}_\lambda$ the maximal ideal of $A_\lambda$, one can, by virtue of \sref{IV.8.8.2.4} and of the transitivity of the fibres ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.3.6.4}), suppose that $(C_\lambda)_{\mathfrak{n}_\lambda}$ is strictly essentially \'etale over $A_\lambda$; since $A_\lambda$ is Henselian, $(C_\lambda)_{\mathfrak{n}_\lambda}$ is necessarily isomorphic to $A_\lambda$ \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $b)$)); there is hence an open neighbourhood of $\mathfrak{n}_\lambda$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(C_\lambda)$ isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}(A_\lambda)$ ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.6.5.4}); one concludes that there is an open neighbourhood of $\mathfrak{n}$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(C)$ isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}(A')$, which proves that $A'$ is Henselian \sref{IV.18.6.2} and achieves the proof.

(ii) Suppose that $B=\varinjlim B_\lambda$, where the $B_\lambda$ are local rings, the transition homomorphisms $B_\lambda\to B_\mu$ ($\lambda\leq\mu$) being local.
Set $A_\lambda={}^hB_\lambda$; the $A_\lambda$ are Henselian local rings \sref{IV.18.6.6}, (vi)), and by virtue of \sref{IV.18.6.6}, (ii)) the transition homomorphisms $B_\lambda\to B_\mu$ determine in a unique way local homomorphisms $A_\lambda\to A_\mu$, so that $(A_\lambda)$ is an inductive system.
Everything amounts to seeing that $A'=\varinjlim A_\lambda$ is canonically isomorphic to $A={}^hB$.
By virtue of \sref{IV.18.6.6}, (ii)) and of the definition of inductive limits, one has, for every Henselian local ring $E$,
\[
\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A',E)=\varprojlim\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(A_\lambda,E)=\varprojlim\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(B_\lambda,E)=\Hom.\mathrm{loc}(B,E).
\]
But as $A'$ is Henselian by (i), this proves our assertion.

(iii) The ring $A$ is the inductive limit of its sub-rings local Noetherian $B_\lambda$, the transition homomorphisms being local (\sref{IV.5.13.3}, (iii)).
As the ${}^hB_\lambda$ are Henselian local and Noetherian \sref{IV.18.6.6}, (v)) and that $A={}^hA$ \sref{IV.18.6.6}, (vi)), it suffices to apply (ii) to the inductive system $(B_\lambda)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[18.6.15]
\label{IV.18.6.15}
\oldpage[IV]{142}
Let $A$ be a Henselian local ring, $X$ an A-prescheme of finite presentation over $A$.
There exists then a Noetherian local Henselian ring $A_0$, a local homomorphism $A_0\to A$, a prescheme $X_0$ of finite type over $A_0$ and an A-isomorphism $X_0\otimes_{A_0}A\xrightarrow{\sim}X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This results from \sref{IV.18.6.14} and from \sref{IV.8.8.2}, (ii)).
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[18.7.1]

For every separable algebraic extension $k'$ of $k$, and every $k'$-algebra local Noetherian $A$, the property $\mathbf{Q}(A,k)$ is \emph{equivalent} to $\mathbf{Q}(A,k')$.

It is immediate that all the properties $\mathbf{P}$ considered in \sref{IV.7.3.8} verify the preceding condition, taking into account the fact that if $K$ is a finite extension of $k$, $k'\otimes_k K$ is a direct composition of separable algebraic extensions of $K$.

\begin{proposition}[18.7.2]
\label{IV.18.7.2}
\oldpage[IV]{143}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian local ring.
For ${}^hA$ to be a $\mathbf{P}$-ring \sref{IV.7.3.13}, it is necessary and sufficient that $A$ be one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.6.6}, (iv)), the completion $\hat{A}$ of $A$ is also the completion of ${}^hA$ and one has $A\subset{}^hA\subset\hat{A}$.
By \sref{IV.18.6.9}, for every $x\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, the fibre at $x$ of the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}({}^hA)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is discrete and finite, and at each of the points $y_i$ of this fibre, $k(y_i)$ is a separable algebraic extension of $k(x)$.
The formal fibre of $A$ at the point $x$ is hence a prescheme \emph{sum} of the formal fibres of ${}^hA$ at the points $y_i$.
The conclusion results then from the hypothesis made on $\mathbf{P}$ in \sref{IV.18.7.1}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Strictly local rings and strict henselisation.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.8}

\begin{proposition}[18.8.1]
\label{IV.18.8.1}
Let $A$ be a local ring.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $A$ is a Henselian ring and its residue field $k$ is separably closed.
  \item[b)] $A$ is a Henselian ring and every \'etale covering of $S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is trivial.
  \item[c)] For every \'etale morphism $f:X\to S$ and every point $x\in X$ such that $f(x)$ is the closed point $s$ of $S$, there exists an S-section $u:S\to X$ of $X$ such that $u(s)=x$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The hypothesis $c)$ implies that for every \'etale morphism $f:X\to S$, the residue fields at the points of $f^{-1}(s)$ are all isomorphic to $k$, and moreover that the condition $b)$ of \sref{IV.18.5.11} is verified.
Hence $A$ is Henselian, and the fact that every \'etale covering of $S$ is trivial results from \sref{IV.18.2.10}, (ii)); hence $c)$ implies $b)$.
As the \'etale coverings of $\operatorname{Spec}(k)$ are trivially only if $k$ is separably closed, $b)$ implies $a)$ by virtue of \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $b)$).
Finally, it results also from \sref{IV.18.5.11}, $b)$) that $a)$ implies $c)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[18.8.2]
\label{IV.18.8.2}
One says that a local ring is a \emph{strictly local} ring if it verifies the equivalent conditions of \sref{IV.18.8.1}.
One calls a \emph{strictly local scheme} a scheme isomorphic to the spectrum of a strictly local ring.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[18.8.3]
\label{IV.18.8.3}
The conditions of \sref{IV.18.8.1} are moreover equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[d)] For every morphism locally of finite type $f:X\to S$ and every point $x\in X$ such that $f(x)$ is the closed point $s$ of $S$ and that $\sh{O}_{X,x}/\mathfrak{m}_s\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is a field, a finite separable extension of $k(s)=k$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$ such that $f|U$ is a closed immersion.
\end{enumerate}
\oldpage[IV]{145}
(One will note that the hypothesis of $d)$ is verified if $f$ is formally non ramified at the point $x$ \sref{IV.17.4.1.2}.)
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[18.8.4]
\label{IV.18.8.4}
Let $A$, $A'$ be two local rings, $\varphi:A\to A'$ a local homomorphism making $A'$ a local A-algebra essentially \'etale \sref{IV.18.6.1}.
Then, for every strictly local ring $B$, every local homomorphism $\psi:A\to B$ and every $k(A)$-algebra homomorphism $\alpha:k(A')\to k(B)$, there exists a unique local homomorphism $\psi':A'\to B$ such that $\psi=\psi'\circ\varphi$ and that $\alpha$ is equal to the homomorphism $\bar{\psi}'$ deduced from $\psi'$ by passage to the quotients.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[18.8.5]
\label{IV.18.8.5}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $A_1$, $A_2$ two local A-algebras essentially \'etale, $K$ a field, extension of $k(A)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item For every $k(A)$-homomorphism $\gamma:k(A_1)\to k(A_2)$, there exists at most one A-homomorphism (necessarily local) $\psi:A_1\to A_2$ such that $\gamma$ is the homomorphism $\bar{\psi}$ deduced from $\psi$ by passage to the quotients.
  \item For every couple of local A-homomorphisms $\beta_1:A_1\to K$, $\beta_2:A_2\to K$ there exists a local A-algebra essentially \'etale $A_3$, two A-homomorphisms $\varphi_1:A_1\to A_3$, $\varphi_2:A_2\to A_3$ and an A-homomorphism $\beta:A_3\to K$ such that $\beta_1=\beta\circ\varphi_1$ and $\beta_2=\beta\circ\varphi_2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{env}[18.8.6]
\label{IV.18.8.6}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $\mathfrak{m}$ its maximal ideal, $k$ its residue field, $\Omega$ a \emph{separable closure} of $k$.
Consider the ensemble $\mathfrak{E}$ of A-algebras essentially \'etale defined in \sref{IV.18.6.1}, and designate by $L$ the set of A-homomorphisms local $A'\to\Omega$, where $A'$ ranges over $\mathfrak{E}$; one will note that for each such homomorphism, it factors as $\lambda:A'\to k(A')\xrightarrow{\omega_\lambda}\Omega$, where $\omega_\lambda:k(A')\to\Omega$ is a $k$-homomorphism (since $\mathfrak{m}A'$ is the maximal ideal of $A'$); inversely the data of such a homomorphism $\omega_\lambda$ determines uniquely a homomorphism $\lambda\in L$.
For every $\lambda\in L$, we designate by $A_\lambda$ the local A-algebra essentially \'etale on which $\lambda$ is defined.
The set $L$ is \emph{preordered} by the relation ``there exists an A-homomorphism $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}:A_\lambda\to A_\mu$ such that $\lambda=\mu\circ\varphi_{\mu\lambda}$'', and it results from \sref{IV.18.8.5}, (i)) that this homomorphism $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}$ is \emph{unique}.
Moreover, for the preceding preorder relation (which one denotes again $\lambda\leq\mu$), $L$ is \emph{filtering directed}, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.8.5}, (ii)).
If $\bar{\varphi}_{\mu\lambda}:k(A_\lambda)\to k(A_\mu)$ is the $k$-homomorphism deduced from $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}$ by passage to quotients, $(k(A_\lambda),\bar{\varphi}_{\mu\lambda})$ is an \emph{inductive system} of separable algebraic extensions of $k$, and the $\omega_\lambda:k(A_\lambda)\to\Omega$ form an inductive system of $k$-homomorphisms.
Moreover, the inductive limit
\oldpage[IV]{146}
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.8.6.1}
  \omega:\varinjlim k(A_\lambda)\to\Omega
\end{equation}
is a $k$-\emph{isomorphism}.
It suffices indeed to prove that for every finite separable extension $k'$ of $k$, there exists a local A-algebra essentially \'etale $A'$ such that $k'$ is the residue field of $A'$ and that the homomorphism $k\to k'$ is deduced from $A\to A'$ by passage to quotients.
But this results from \sref{IV.18.1.1} applied to \'etale morphisms, taking into account that $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is the only open neighbourhood of its closed point.

Note now that if $\mathfrak{m}_\lambda$ is the maximal ideal of $A_\lambda$, it results from \sref{IV.17.6.1} that for $\lambda\leq\mu$, one has $\mathfrak{m}_\mu=\mathfrak{m}_\lambda A_\mu$ and that $A_\mu$ is a flat $A_\lambda$-module.
Hence it results from ($\mathbf{0}_{III}$, \sref{IV.10.3.1.3}) that the ring $\varinjlim A_\lambda$ is local, that the canonical homomorphism $w:A\to\varinjlim A_\lambda$ is \emph{local}, and that one has a $k$-isomorphism canonical
\[
\varinjlim k(A_\lambda)\xrightarrow{\sim}k(\varinjlim A_\lambda);
\]
identifying these two fields, one deduces a $k$-isomorphism canonical
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.8.6.2}
  \omega^{-1}:\Omega\xrightarrow{\sim}k(\varinjlim A_\lambda).
\end{equation}
\end{env}

\begin{definition}[18.8.7]
\label{IV.18.8.7}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $k=k(A)$ its residue field, $i:k\to\Omega$ a homomorphism of $k$ into a separable closure $\Omega$ of $k$; one calls the \emph{strict henselisation} of $A$ relative to $i$, and one denotes ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ (or ${}^{hs}A$ when this does not lead to confusion) the inductive limit of the inductive system $(A_\lambda,\varphi_{\mu\lambda})$ defined in \sref{IV.18.8.6}.
\end{definition}

As in \sref{IV.18.6.5} one sees that the definition given in \sref{IV.18.8.7} does not depend on the apparent choice of $\mathfrak{E}$; we will see below \sref{IV.18.8.8}, (i) and (ii)) that ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ is an object representing a functor entirely determined by the data of $A$ and of $i$ ($\mathbf{0}_{III}$, \sref{IV.8.1.8}), and is hence determined up to a unique isomorphism.

\begin{proposition}[18.8.8]
\label{IV.18.8.8}
Let $A$ be a local ring, $k$ its residue field, $i:k\to\Omega$ a homomorphism into a separable closure of $k$, ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ the strict henselisation of $A$ relative to $i$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ is a strictly local ring \sref{IV.18.8.2} and the structural homomorphism $w:A\to{}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ is local.
  \item For every local homomorphism $u:A\to B$, where $B$ is a strictly local ring, and every $k$-homomorphism $\psi:\Omega\to k(B)$, there exists a unique A-homomorphism $v:{}^{hs}A_{(i)}\to B$ such that $\psi$ factors as $\Omega\xrightarrow{\omega^{-1}}k({}^{hs}A_{(i)})\xrightarrow{\bar{v}}k(B)$, where $\bar{v}$ is deduced from $v$ by passage to quotients.
  \item ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ is an A-module faithfully flat, and if $\mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{m}.{}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ is the maximal ideal of ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$, and $k({}^{hs}A_{(i)})$ is a separable closure of $k$.
  \item For ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ to be Noetherian, it is necessary and sufficient that $A$ be so.
  \item If $A$ is strictly local (hence $\Omega=k$), one has ${}^{hs}A\cong A$.
  \item If $i':k\to\Omega'$ is a second homomorphism of $k$ into a separable closure of $k$, for every $k$-isomorphism $\sigma:\Omega\xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega'$, the A-homomorphism corresponding (by (ii)) $v_\sigma:{}^{hs}A_{(i)}\to{}^{hs}A_{(i')}$ is an isomorphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{env}[18.8.8.1]
\label{IV.18.8.8.1}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the category of strictly local rings, with local homomorphisms as morphisms; for every $B\in\mathcal{C}$, designate by $F(B)$ the set of couples $(u,\psi)$ formed of a local homomorphism $u:A\to B$ and a $k$-isomorphism $\psi:\Omega\to k(B)$ such that the composed $k(A)=k\xrightarrow{i}\Omega\xrightarrow{\psi}k(B)$ is deduced from $u$ by passage to quotients.
One can say that the object ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ \emph{represents} the covariant functor $F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathbf{Ens}$ ($\mathbf{0}_{III}$, \sref{IV.8.1.11}).

One will remark that by virtue of \sref{IV.18.8.8}, (vi)) the strict henselisations ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ are all A-isomorphic (for the diverse $k$-homomorphisms $i$ of $k$ into separable closures of $k$), but for given $i$ and $i'$, there is in general an infinity of A-isomorphisms ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}\xrightarrow{\sim}{}^{hs}A_{(i')}$.
To be precise, the group of A-automorphisms of ${}^{hs}A_{(i)}$ is isomorphic to the Galois group of $\Omega$ over $k$.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[18.8.9]
\label{IV.18.8.9}
\oldpage[IV]{147}
Let $A$ now be a \emph{semi-local} ring, $\mathfrak{m}_j$ $(1\leq j\leq r)$ its maximal ideals, and for every index $j$, consider a homomorphism $i_j:k(A_{\mathfrak{m}_j})\to\Omega_j$ of $k(A_{\mathfrak{m}_j})$ into a separable closure of this field.
One calls the \emph{strict henselisation} of $A$ relative to the $i_j$ and one denotes again ${}^{hs}A$ (when this does not lead to confusion) the ring product $\prod_{j=1}^r({}^{hs}(A_{\mathfrak{m}_j}))$ of the strict henselisations of the local rings $A_{\mathfrak{m}_j}$ relative to the homomorphisms $i_j$.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[18.8.10]
\label{IV.18.8.10}
\oldpage[IV]{148}
Let $A$ be a semi-local ring, $B$ a finite A-algebra.
Let $\mathfrak{n}_j$ $(1\leq j\leq r)$ be the maximal ideals of $B$.

\begin{remark}[18.8.11]

\subsection{Formal fibres of Noetherian Henselian rings.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.9}

\begin{theorem}[18.9.1]
\label{IV.18.9.1}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian local Henselian ring, whose formal fibres are geometrically normal.
Then the formal fibres of $A$ are geometrically integre (hence geometrically connected).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two assertions of the statement are in fact equivalent, a Noetherian connected and normal prescheme locally being integre.
As every finite A-algebra integre is a local ring, hence the henselisation is ${}^hB$ \sref{IV.18.6.8}, it results from \sref{IV.7.3.16.2} that one is reduced to proving that, if one supposes moreover $A$ integre, then the fibre of the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(\hat{A})\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ at the generic point is integre, which results from the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[18.9.2]
\label{IV.18.9.2}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian local Henselian and integre ring, whose formal fibres are geometrically normal.
Then $\hat{A}$ is integre.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.9.3]
\label{IV.18.9.3}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.18.9.2}, the field of fractions $L$ of $\hat{A}$ is a separable extension of the field of fractions $K$ of $A$, and $K$ is algebraically closed in $L$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.9.4]
\label{IV.18.9.4}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian local ring, Henselian and whose formal fibres are geometrically normal (for example a Noetherian local Henselian and excellent ring), and set $A'=\hat{A}$.
Let $X$ be an A-prescheme, $X'=X\otimes_A A'$, $g:X'\to X$ the canonical projection.
Then the application $U\mapsto g^{-1}(U)$ is a bijection of the set of open and closed parts of $X$ to the set of open and closed parts of $X'$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.9.5]
\label{IV.18.9.5}
\oldpage[IV]{151}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.18.9.4}, the functor
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.9.5.1}
\label{IV.18.9.5.1}
  Z\mapsto Z\otimes_A A'
\end{equation}
from the category of \'etale preschemes over $X$, to that of \'etale preschemes over $X'$, is fully faithful.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remarks}[18.9.6]
\label{IV.18.9.6}
(i) It is possible that, if the residue field of $A$ is of characteristic $0$, the functor \sref{IV.18.9.5.1} induces even an \emph{equivalence} of the category of \'etale coverings of $X$ and of the category of \'etale coverings of $X'$.
One can in any case demonstrate this when $A$ is \emph{excellent}, by using the resolution of singularities of Hironaka (M.~Artin).

(ii) One can also ask whether the formal fibres of $A$ are geometrically connected (or even geometrically irreducible) when $A$ is any Henselian local ring.
It would suffice for this that, for every Noetherian local ring, Henselian and \emph{integre} $A$, $\operatorname{Spec}(\hat{A})$ be irreducible.
This is not known in general.

(iii) One can also ask whether, when $A$ is an excellent local ring, its strict henselisation ${}^{hs}A$ is excellent.
\end{remarks}

\begin{theorem}[18.9.7]
\label{IV.18.9.7}
\oldpage[IV]{152}
Let $A$, $B$ be two Noetherian local rings, $\rho:A\to B$ a local homomorphism, $f:\operatorname{Spec}(B)=X\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)=Y$ the corresponding morphism.
Suppose the following conditions verified:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $f$ is flat and all the fibres $X_y=f^{-1}(y)$ ($y\in Y$) are geometrically reduced \sref{IV.4.6.2}.
  \item Either the ring $A$ is geometrically unibranch ($\mathbf{0}$, \sref{IV.23.2.1}), or else the ring $A$ is unibranch and $k(B)$ is a primary extension (\sref{IV.4.3.1}) of $k(A)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, if $\eta$ is the generic point of $Y$, $X_\eta$ is connected, and for every closed part $T$ of $X$ such that $f(T)$ is rare in $Y$, $X-T$ is connected.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[18.9.8]
\label{IV.18.9.8}
\oldpage[IV]{156}
With the notations of \sref{IV.18.9.7}, suppose verified the condition (i) of \sref{IV.18.9.7} and one of the two following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(ii')] $A$ is a strictly local ring \sref{IV.18.8.2}.
  \item[(ii'')] $A$ is a Henselian local ring and $k(B)$ is a primary extension of $k(A)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then all the fibres $X_y$ ($y\in Y$) are geometrically connected.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.9.9]
\label{IV.18.9.9}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian local Henselian ring, universally Japanese (i.e.\ \sref{IV.7.6.4} and \sref{IV.7.7.2}), whose formal fibres \sref{IV.7.3.13} are geometrically reduced; then the formal fibres are also geometrically connected.
\end{corollary}

The conclusions of these two propositions are hence still valid when one supposes $A$ a Noetherian local Henselian and universally Japanese ring.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[18.9.11]
\label{IV.18.9.11}
\oldpage[IV]{157}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two locally Noetherian preschemes, $Y$ being supposed geometrically unibranch and $X$ connected.
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a reduced morphism (i.e.\ \sref{IV.6.8.1}) flat and with geometrically reduced fibres.
Then, for every closed part $T$ of $X$ such that $f(T)$ is rare in $Y$, $X-T$ is connected.
\end{corollary}


\subsection{\'Etale preschemes over a geometrically unibranch or normal prescheme.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.10}

\begin{theorem}[18.10.1]
\label{IV.18.10.1}
\oldpage[IV]{157}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$.
Suppose that $Y$ is integre and geometrically unibranch at the point $y$.
Then, for $f$ to be \'etale at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that the two following conditions be verified:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[$1\textsuperscript{o}$] $f$ is formally non ramified at the point $x$;
  \item[$2\textsuperscript{o}$] the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{Y,y}\to\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is injective.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, if this is so, $X$ is integre and geometrically unibranch at the point $x$.

Finally, if $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is Noetherian, one can replace condition (ii) by the condition
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(ii \emph{bis})] $\dim\sh{O}_{Y,y}\leq\dim\sh{O}_{X,x}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remarks}[18.10.2]
\label{IV.18.10.2}
\oldpage[IV]{158}
(i) When $A=\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is Noetherian, and one knows that its completion $\hat{A}$ is integre (for example if $A$ is regular), one can, in the preceding proof, replace $A'$ by $\hat{A}$.

(ii) When $Y$ is locally Noetherian, one can give of \sref{IV.18.10.1} a proof more rapid, not using the strict henselisation, but making intervene the rather delicate results of \textsection\textsection~14 and~15.

(iii) With the notations and hypotheses on $Y$ being those of \sref{IV.18.10.1}, suppose only that $f$ is \emph{locally of finite type} (and not necessarily locally of finite presentation).
Then, for $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ to be an $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$-algebra \emph{essentially \'etale} \sref{IV.18.6.1}, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ verify the two following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[$1\textsuperscript{o}$] $f$ is formally non ramified at the point $x$;
  \item[$2\textsuperscript{o}$] the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{Y,y}\to\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is injective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\begin{corollary}[18.10.3]
\label{IV.18.10.3}
\oldpage[IV]{159}
Let $Y$ be an integre prescheme and geometrically unibranch, $\eta$ its generic point, $X$ a connected prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type and such that $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is non empty.
Then, if $f$ is formally non ramified, $f$ is \'etale, and $X$ is integre and geometrically unibranch.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.10.4]
\label{IV.18.10.4}
\oldpage[IV]{160}
Let $g:Y\to S$, $h:X\to S$ be two morphisms locally of finite presentation, $f:X\to Y$ an S-morphism, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$, $s=g(y)=h(x)$.
Suppose that $h$ is flat at the point $x$ and $g^{-1}(s)$ normal at the point $y$.
Then, for $f$ to be \'etale at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be non ramified at the point $x$ and that $\dim_x h^{-1}(s)=\dim_y g^{-1}(s)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.10.5]
\label{IV.18.10.5}
With the notations of \sref{IV.18.10.4}, suppose that $h$ is flat and that $g^{-1}(s)$ is normal for every $s\in S$ (which will be the case for example if $g$ is smooth).
For $f$ to be a monomorphism and an open immersion, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be non ramified and that, for every $x\in X$, $\dim_x h^{-1}(s)=\dim_y g^{-1}(s)$, where $y=f(x)$ and $s=g(y)=h(x)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[18.10.6]
\label{IV.18.10.6}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism flat and locally quasi-finite ($\mathbf{Err}_{\mathbf{III}}$, 20).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The set $\operatorname{Max}(X)$ of maximal points of $X$ is in canonical bijection with the set $\coprod_{y\in\operatorname{Max}(Y)}f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item If $Y$ is irreducible with generic point $\eta$, the set of irreducible components of $X$ is in canonical bijection with $f^{-1}(\eta)$, and in particular is finite if and only if $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is finite.
  \item If the set of irreducible components of $Y$ is locally finite, the same is true of that of $X$, and in particular $X$ is locally connected.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[18.10.7]
\label{IV.18.10.7}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme geometrically unibranch and irreducible (resp.\ integre), $f:X\to Y$ an \'etale morphism.
Then $X$ is isomorphic to a sum of preschemes (resp.\ integre) $X_\lambda$, where $\lambda$ ranges over the fibre $f^{-1}(\eta)$ of the generic point $\eta$ of $Y$.
The $X_\lambda$ are geometrically unibranch; if $Y$ is normal, the $X_\lambda$ are normal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[18.10.8]
\label{IV.18.10.8}
\oldpage[IV]{161}
Let $Y$ be a normal and integre prescheme, with generic point $\eta$, $K=k(\eta)$ its field of rational functions, $f:X\to Y$ an \'etale morphism.
Suppose that $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is finite and non empty, so that the K-scheme $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is the spectrum of a finite separable K-algebra $L$, direct composition of a finite number of fields $K_i$ ($1\leq i\leq n$), finite separable extensions of $K$ \sref{IV.17.6.2}.
Let $Y'$ be the integral closure of $Y$ in $L$, isomorphic to the sum of the integral closures $Y_i$ of $Y$ in the $K_i$ ($\mathbf{II}$, \sref{IV.6.3.6}).
Then the morphism $f$ factors in a unique way as $f:X\xrightarrow{j}Y'\xrightarrow{g}Y$, such that the restriction $f'_\eta:f^{-1}(\eta)\to g^{-1}(\eta)$ is the canonical isomorphism.
Moreover $f'$ is an isomorphism local; for $f'$ to be an open immersion, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be separated.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[18.10.9]
\label{IV.18.10.9}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.18.10.8}, for $f$ to be a finite morphism (i.e.\ for $X$ to be an \'etale covering of $Y$), it is necessary and sufficient that $X$ be isomorphic to the integral closure $Y'$ of $Y$ in $L$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{env}[18.10.10]
\label{IV.18.10.10}
Let $Y$ be a normal and integre prescheme, $K=R(Y)$ its field of rational functions.
We say that a K-algebra of finite rank $L$ is \emph{non ramified over $Y$} if: $1\textsuperscript{o}$ $L$ is a finite separable K-algebra, hence a direct composition of finite separable extensions $K_i$ of $K$ ($1\leq i\leq n$); $2\textsuperscript{o}$ the integral closure $Y'$ of $Y$ in $L$ (sum of the preschemes of integral closures of $Y$ in the $K_i$) is \emph{non ramified over $Y$} (which, by \sref{IV.18.10.3}, is equivalent to saying that $Y'$ is \'etale over $Y$).
\end{env}

\begin{remark}[18.10.11]
\label{IV.18.10.11}
Instead of saying that $L$ is non ramified over $Y$, some authors also say that $L$ is ``non ramified over $K$''; we will refrain from following this usage which lends itself to confusions.
\end{remark}

One can then express \sref{IV.18.10.9} in the following form:

\begin{corollary}[18.10.12]
\label{IV.18.10.12}
Let $Y$ be a normal and integre prescheme, $K=R(Y)$ its field of rational functions.
Then the functor $X\mapsto R(X)$ is an equivalence of the category of \'etale coverings of $Y$, and of the category of K-algebras finite \'etale which are non ramified over $Y$.
One obtains a quasi-inverse functor by associating to every finite \'etale K-algebra $L$, non ramified over $Y$, the integral closure $Y'$ of $Y$ in $L$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[18.10.13]
\label{IV.18.10.13}
\oldpage[IV]{162}
Let $Y$ be a normal and integre prescheme, $K=R(Y)$ its field of rational functions.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The field $K$ is a K-algebra non ramified over $Y$.
  \item Let $L$ be a finite extension of $K$, non ramified over $Y$, and let $Z$ be the integral closure of $Y$ in $L$.
  Then, if $M$ is an L-algebra non ramified over $Z$, $M$ is also a K-algebra non ramified over $Y$ (``transitivity'' of non-ramification).
  \item Let $Y'$ be a normal and integre prescheme, $K'$ its field of rational functions, $g:Y'\to Y$ a dominant morphism.
  If $L$ is a K-algebra non ramified over $Y$, then $L\otimes_K K'$ is a $K'$-algebra non ramified over $Y'$ (property of ``translation'').
  If moreover $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ and $Y'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')$ are affine, and if $C$ is the integral closure of $A$ in $L$, then $C'=C\otimes_A A'$ is the integral closure of $A'$ in $L\otimes_K K'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[18.10.14]
\label{IV.18.10.14}
\oldpage[IV]{162}
Let $Y$ and $Y'$ be two normal and integre preschemes, $K$, $K'$ their respective fields of rational functions, $g:Y'\to Y$ a dominant morphism, so that $K'$ is an extension of $K$.
Let $L$ be a finite extension of $K$ non ramified over $Y$, $L_1$ a composed extension (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.}, chap.~VIII, \S\,8, def.~1) of $L$ and $K'$.
Then $L_1$ is an extension of $K'$ non ramified over $Y'$.
If moreover $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $Y'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')$ are affine, and if $C$ is the integral closure of $A$ in $L$, then the sub-ring $C_1=A[C,A']$ of $L_1$ is the integral closure of $A'$ in $L_1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, $L\otimes_K K'$ is a composed direct of finite separable extensions of $K'$, and $L_1$ is one of these extensions; hence the integral closure of $Y$ in $L$ is a sum of preschemes of which one is the integral closure of $Y$ in $L_1$.
The corollary therefore results immediately from \sref{IV.18.10.13}.
\end{proof}

When for example $A$ is the ring of integers of an algebraic number field $K$, $K'$ and $L$ algebraic extensions of $K$, there are classical examples showing that the relation $C_1=A[C,A']$ is in default when $L$ is not non ramified over $A$.

\begin{env}[18.10.15]
\label{IV.18.10.15}
Suppose that $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is affine, $A$ being integre and integrally closed; let $K$ be its field of fractions, $L$ a finite separable extension of $K$, and suppose that the integral closure $C$ of $A$ in $L$ is an $A$-module projective of finite type (which will be the case for example if $A$ is a Dedekind ring (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~VII, \S\,4, n\textsuperscript{o}\,10, prop.~22)).
To say that $L$ is non ramified over $A$ means that $C$ is \'etale (\sref{IV.18.10.10}) and by virtue of (\sref{IV.18.2.7},~(ii)), this is equivalent to saying that the \emph{discriminant} $d_{C/A}$ of $\operatorname{Spec}(C)$ over $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is \emph{inversible} in $A$.
In the particular case where $C$ is a free $A$-module and $(x_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ a base of $C$ over $A$, this means that $\det(\operatorname{Tr}_{C/A}(x_i x_j))$ is inversible in $A$.
\end{env}

\begin{theorem}[18.10.16]
\label{IV.18.10.16}
\oldpage[IV]{163}
Let $Y$ be an integre prescheme, $\eta$ its generic point, $f:X\to Y$ a separated morphism and locally of finite type.
Suppose that every irreducible component of $X$ dominates $Y$ and that the generic fibre $X_\eta=f^{-1}(\eta)$ is a finite prescheme over $k(\eta)$, hence $(X_\eta)_{\mathrm{red}}$ is equal to $\operatorname{Spec}(L)$, where $L=\prod L_i$ is a product of finite extensions $L_i$ of $K$.
Set
\[
n=[\mathrm{L}:\mathrm{K}]=\sum[\mathrm{L}_i:\mathrm{K}],\quad n_s=\sum[\mathrm{L}_i:\mathrm{K}]_s \quad\text{(sum of separable degrees of the $L_i$).}
\]
Let $y$ be a geometrically unibranch point of $Y$, and designate by $n(y)$ the sum of separable degrees over $k(y)$ of the residue fields of the isolated points of $f^{-1}(y)$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item One has $n(y)\leq n_s\leq n$.
  \item Suppose $X$ reduced and the point $y$ normal in $Y$.
  For $n(y)=n$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ in $Y$ such that the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is an \'etale and finite morphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{A) \emph{Reduction to the case where $f$ is quasi-finite.}}
One knows (\sref{IV.13.1.4}) that the set $Z$ of isolated points $x\in X$ in $f^{-1}(f(x))$ is open in $X$, and by hypothesis $Z$ contains $f^{-1}(\eta)$.
Since $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is finite, $Z$ is a filtered increasing union of quasi-compact opens $V_\lambda$ containing $f^{-1}(\eta)$ (hence dense in $X$ by hypothesis).
If $n_\lambda(y)$ is the sum of separable degrees over $k(y)$ of the residue fields of the points of $V_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(y)$, one has $n(y)=\sup n_\lambda(y)$; to prove~(i), it will thus suffice to show that $n_\lambda(y)\leq n_s$.
On the other hand, if $n(y)=n$, there is an index $\lambda$ such that $n_\lambda(y)=n$, and one shows that there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ in $Y$ such that the restriction $V_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is an \'etale and finite morphism.
Since $f$ is separated, the canonical injection $V_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(U)\to f^{-1}(U)$ is then also a finite morphism (\textbf{II},~6.1.5,~(v)), hence $V_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(U)$ is closed in $f^{-1}(U)$ (\textbf{II},~6.1.10); but being everywhere dense in $f^{-1}(U)$, it is necessarily equal to it.

\textbf{B) \emph{Reduction to the case where $X$ is reduced, $f$ finite and $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$.}}
\oldpage[IV]{164}
On the other hand, by virtue of (\sref{IV.18.12.13})\footnote{The reader will verify that \sref{IV.18.10.16} is not used in the proof of \sref{IV.18.12.13}. If $f$ is supposed locally of finite presentation (resp.\ quasi-projective), one can replace \sref{IV.18.12.13} by (8.12.8) (resp.\ (8.12.6)).}, one can (after having replaced $Y$ by an affine open neighbourhood $V$ of $y$ and $f$ by its restriction $f^{-1}(V)\to V$) factorise $f$ as $X\xrightarrow{j}X'\xrightarrow{g}Y$, where $g$ is \emph{finite} and $j$ an open immersion.
Since $X$ is reduced, one can replace $X'$ by the reduced closed sub-prescheme of underlying space $\overline{j(X)}$ (\textbf{I},~5.2.2), hence suppose $X'$ reduced and such that every irreducible component of $X'$ dominates $Y$.
Moreover, $j(X)$ is a dense open in $X'$ and has only a finite number of irreducible components by hypothesis; hence ($\mathbf{0_I}$,~1.2.7) the irreducible components of $X'$ are the adherences of those of $j(X)$, and therefore the fibre $g^{-1}(\eta)$ identifies with $f^{-1}(\eta)$.
If one supposes the theorem proved when in addition $f$ is a \emph{finite} morphism, one can apply it to $g$, and as $f^{-1}(y)$ identifies with a sub-prescheme of $g^{-1}(y)$, the relation~(i) for $g$ implies the same relation for $f$.
Suppose on the other hand that $n(y)=n$; then, by virtue of what precedes, one can apply the conclusion of~(ii) to the morphism $g$, and replacing $Y$ by an open neighbourhood of $y$ if needed, one can suppose $g$ \'etale, and hence the same holds for $f$; moreover, one then has $f^{-1}(y)=g^{-1}(y)$.
As the morphism $g$ is closed and $j(X)$ is open in $X'$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ in $Y$ such that $j(X)\cap g^{-1}(U)=j(f^{-1}(U))$, which proves that~(ii) is also true for $f$.

One is thus reduced to the case where $f$ is moreover a \emph{finite} morphism.
It is immediate that, to prove~(i), one can restrict to the case where $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$.
Let us show that the same holds for proving~(ii).
Indeed, suppose proved that the condition $n(y)=n$ implies that $X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$ is \'etale and finite over $\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$; as this implies that $f$ is flat and formally non ramified at the points of $f^{-1}(y)$, and that $Y$ is integre, one concludes from (\sref{IV.18.4.13}) that $f$ is \'etale at the points of $f^{-1}(y)$, hence also in an open neighbourhood $V$ of $f^{-1}(y)$ in $X$; but since $f$ is a finite morphism, hence closed, $V$ contains an open of the form $f^{-1}(U)$, where $U$ is an open neighbourhood of $y$ in $Y$.

\textbf{C) \emph{Reduction to the case where $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is strictly local.}}
Set $A=\sh{O}_{Y,y}$, and let $A'={}^{\mathrm{hs}}A$ be the strict henselisation of $A$ (\sref{IV.18.8.7}), which is a local integre and geometrically unibranch ring (\sref{IV.18.8.16}).
Let $Y'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')$, and let $y'$ be the closed point and $\eta'$ the generic point of $Y'$; $y'$ is above $y$ and since the morphism $g:Y'\to Y$ is flat (\sref{IV.18.8.8}), $\eta'$ is above $\eta$ (\sref{IV.2.3.4}).
Set $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$; $f'$ is finite and since $g$ is flat, every irreducible component of $X'$ dominates $Y'$ (\sref{IV.2.3.7}).
If $n'(y')$, $n'$ and $n'_s$ are the numbers defined for $f'$ in the same way as $n(y)$, $n$ and $n_s$ for $f$, one has $n'_s=n_s$ and $n'(y')=n(y)$ (\textbf{I},~6.4.8), and it therefore amounts to the same to prove the assertion~(i) of the statement for $f$ or for $f'$.
Moreover, if $X$ is reduced and $y$ a normal point of $Y$ (hence $A$ integrally closed), then $A'$ is integrally closed (\sref{IV.18.8.12}).
On the other hand it results from the definition (\sref{IV.18.8.7}), from the remark (8.1.2,~$a$)) and from the theorem of the double inductive limit, that $A'$ is an inductive limit of rings $A_\alpha$ such that the morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}(A_\alpha)\to\operatorname{Spec}(A)=Y$ are \'etale; hence $X'$ is a projective limit of the $X_\alpha=X\otimes_A A_\alpha$, which are \'etale over $X$, hence reduced since $X$ is (\sref{IV.17.5.7}); by passage to the limit,
\oldpage[IV]{165}
one deduces that $X'$ is reduced (8.7.1).
This being so, if $n(y)=n$, one has $n_s=n$ and hence all the residue fields of the points of $X_s$ are necessarily separable over $k(\eta)$; the residue fields at the points of $X'_{\eta'}$ are therefore algebraic and separable over $k(\eta')$ (4.6.1), in other words $n'_s=n'=n$.
The morphism $f'$ has the same properties as $f$, and if one proves that $f'$ is \'etale, it will follow that $f$ is \'etale by (\sref{IV.17.7.1}).

\textbf{D) \emph{End of the proof.}}
Suppose therefore $A$ strictly local. Since $A$ is henselian and the morphism $f$ finite, it follows from (\sref{IV.18.5.11}) that if $x_j$ ($1\leq j\leq m$) are the distinct points of $f^{-1}(y)$, $X$ is the \emph{sum} of the open sub-preschemes $\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{X,x_j})=X_j$, which are thus the connected components of $X$. By hypothesis, each of them dominates $Y$, hence meets $f^{-1}(\eta)$, and consequently the number of points of $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is $\geq m$; \emph{a fortiori} we have $n_s\geq m$. But since $k(y)$ is separably closed and the $k(x_j)$ are algebraic over $k(y)$, they are necessarily \emph{radiciel} extensions of $k(y)$, hence $m=n(y)$, and this proves~(i).
Suppose now that the hypotheses of~(ii) are satisfied; the relations $n(y)=n_s=n$ entail first, since every irreducible component of $X$ dominates $Y$, that each $X_j$ is \emph{irreducible}; moreover, since the $X_j$ are \emph{reduced}, they are \emph{integral}; finally the relations $m=n_s=n$ entail that for the generic point $\xi_j$ of $X_j$, $k(\xi_j)$ is a separable extension of $k(\eta)$ of degree~$1$, hence isomorphic to $k(\eta)$; in other words, the restriction $f|X_j:X_j\to Y$ of $f$ is a finite and \emph{birational} morphism; moreover, $X_j$ is integral and by hypothesis $Y$ is \emph{normal}; hence (8.12.10.1) $f|X_j$ is an \emph{isomorphism}, which proves that $f$ is \'etale, and completes the proof of (\sref{IV.18.10.16}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[18.10.17]
\label{IV.18.10.17}
The method of ``\'etale localisation'' used in the proof of \sref{IV.18.10.16} allows also to improve the results of \sref{IV.15.5.1} by eliminating the Noetherian hypothesis.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[18.10.18]
\label{IV.18.10.18}
\oldpage[IV]{166}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two preschemes, $f:X\to Y$ a birational morphism\footnote{We mean by this the notion defined in (\sref{IV.6.15.4}), but where we do not assume $X$ and $Y$ reduced.}.
For $f$ to be an isomorphism local at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be \'etale at the point $x$.
For $f$ to be an open immersion, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be \'etale and separated.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The stated conditions being trivially necessary, it suffices to see that they are sufficient. For the first assertion, the question is local on $X$ and $Y$, so we can suppose $f$ \'etale, $X$ and $Y$ affine, hence $f$ separated, and we are reduced to proving the second assertion. By virtue of (\sref{IV.17.9.1}), it suffices to prove that $f$ is radiciel. Let then $y\in Y$ and let us prove that $f^{-1}(y)$ is radiciel over $k(y)$. The base change $\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})\to Y$ not changing the fact that $f$ is \'etale, separated and birational, we can restrict to the case where $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, with $A=\sh{O}_{Y,y}$. Let then $A'={}^{\mathrm{hs}}A$, which is a local ring and a faithfully flat $A$-module (\sref{IV.18.8.8}), the homomorphism $A\to A'$ being local. Set $Y'=\operatorname{Spec}(A')$, $X'=X_{(Y')}$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$, so that $f'$ is \'etale and separated; moreover, since the morphism $Y'\to Y$ is flat, the reasoning of (\sref{IV.6.15.4.1}) shows that $f'$ is also birational. If $y'$ is the closed point of $Y'$, it will suffice, by virtue of (\sref{IV.2.6.1},~(v)), to prove that $f'^{-1}(y')$ is radiciel over $k(y')$; since $f'$ is \'etale, it will suffice to prove that $f'^{-1}(y')$ contains at most one point (\sref{IV.17.6.1},~$c'$)).

Suppose therefore $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, $A$ being strictly local, $f$ separated, \'etale and birational, and let us show that $f^{-1}(y)$ cannot contain more than one point. Indeed, if there existed two distinct points $x_1$, $x_2$ in $f^{-1}(y)$, there would exist two $Y$-sections $u_1$, $u_2$ of $f$ such that $u_1(y)=x_1$ and $u_2(y)=x_2$ (\sref{IV.18.8.1}); but since $f$ is \'etale and separated, $u_1$ and $u_2$ would be isomorphisms of $Y$ onto two connected (open) components of $X$ (\sref{IV.17.9.4}) and there would thus be at least two maximal points of $X$ above every maximal point of $Y$, which contradicts the hypothesis that $f$ is \emph{birational}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[18.10.19]
\label{IV.18.10.19}
\oldpage[IV]{167}
Let $Y$ be a reduced prescheme whose set of irreducible components is locally finite, $f:X\to Y$ a separated morphism, locally of finite type and formally non ramified, $g$ a Y-rational section of $f$ (i.e.\ a Y-rational application of $Y$ into $X$), $U$ the domain of definition of $g$ ($\mathbf{I}$, \sref{IV.7.2.1}), and let $Z$ be the adherence of $g(U)$ in $X$.
Then, for every point $y\in Y-U$ such that $Y$ is geometrically unibranch at the point $y$, one has $Z_y=Z\cap f^{-1}(y)=\varnothing$.
In particular, if $Y$ is geometrically unibranch, $g(U)$ is an open and closed part of $X$.
If, for every closed irreducible part $T$ of $X$, $f(T)$ is closed in $Y$, $g$ is defined at every geometrically unibranch point of $Y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $Y$ is reduced and $f$ separated, it follows from ($\mathbf{I}$,~7.2.2) that there exists a $U$-section $u$ of $f^{-1}(U)$ belonging to the class $g$; moreover (\sref{IV.17.4.1.2}), since $f$ is formally non ramified, $u$ is an isomorphism of $U$ onto the prescheme induced on the open set $u(U)$ of $X$. If we also denote by $Z$ the reduced sub-prescheme of $X$ having $Z$ for underlying space, $g(U)=u(U)$, being reduced, is also induced by $Z$, so the restriction $f'=f|Z$ of $f$ is a birational morphism of $Z$ into $Y$, which is moreover formally non ramified, since $f$ is (\sref{IV.17.1.3}). The question being local on $Y$, since $Y$ is reduced and geometrically unibranch at the point $y$, we can restrict ourselves (by replacing $Y$ by an open neighbourhood of $y$) to the case where $Y$ is integre; then $u(U)$ is irreducible, hence also $Z$, and consequently $Z$ is also integre. Then ($\mathbf{I}$,~8.2.7) since $f'$ is dominant, the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{Y,y}\to\sh{O}_{Z,x}$ is injective for all $x\in Z_y$, and we conclude from (\sref{IV.18.10.2},~(iii)) that $f'$ is an \'etale morphism; being separated and birational, it is an isomorphism local at the point $x$ by virtue of (\sref{IV.18.10.18}); but this would entail that the $U$-section $u$ would be extendable to an open set distinct from $U$, contrary to the definition of the latter. We therefore necessarily have $Z_y=\varnothing$, which establishes the first assertion; the second is an obvious consequence. Moreover, under the hypotheses of the last assertion of the statement, since $Z$ is closed and irreducible, $f(Z)$ is closed in $Y$, hence equal to $Y$, i.e.\ we have $Z_y\neq\varnothing$ for all $y\in Y$, which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[18.10.20]
\label{IV.18.10.20}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme.
We say that a morphism $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{essentially proper} if it is \emph{locally of finite type} and if, for every Y-scheme of the form $Y'=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ where $A$ is a discrete valuation ring, the canonical map (\textbf{II},~7.3.2.2) relative to the $Y'$-prescheme $X\times_Y Y'$, is bijective.
The reasoning of (\textbf{II},~7.3.8) proves that it amounts to the same to say that $f$ (supposed locally of finite type) is \emph{separated} and that for every base change $Y''\to Y$, where $Y''$ is locally Noetherian, the image by $f_{(Y'')}:X_{(Y'')}\to Y''$ of every closed irreducible part of $X''$ is \emph{closed}.
To say that $f$ is \emph{proper} (for $Y$ locally Noetherian) thus means that $f$ is essentially proper and of finite type (\textbf{II},~7.3.8); but one encounters important examples of essentially proper morphisms that are not of finite type (for example certain ``Picard preschemes'' or certain ``N\'eron-Severi preschemes'' (chap.~VI)).
It follows clearly from \sref{IV.18.10.19} that if $Y$ is locally Noetherian, reduced and geometrically unibranch, and if the morphism $f:X\to Y$ is essentially proper and non ramified, then every $Y$-rational section of $f$ is defined everywhere.
\end{remark}

The following proposition generalises the criterion \sref{IV.17.15.5} of smoothness of a morphism $X\to Y$ when $Y$ is not the spectrum of a field:

\begin{proposition}[18.10.21]
\label{IV.18.10.21}
\oldpage[IV]{168}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$ and suppose the ring $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ integre and geometrically unibranch.
Let $r$ be the minimum number of generators of the $\sh{O}_{X,x}$-module $(\Omega^1_{X/Y})_x$ (equal to the rank of the $k(x)$-vector space $(\Omega^1_{X/Y})_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_{X,x}}k(x)$).
Then, for $f$ to be smooth at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that, if $y'$ is the generic point of the unique irreducible component of $Y$ containing $y$, there exists a generalisation $x'$ of $x$ such that $f(x')=y'$ and $\dim_{x'}f^{-1}(y')\geq r$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $f$ is smooth at the point $x$, it is also smooth in an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, hence at every generalisation $x'$ of $x$, and $\dim_{x'}(f^{-1}(f(x')))=r$ at each of these points, by virtue of (\sref{IV.17.10.2}). Since $f$ is moreover flat in $U$, there exists a generalisation of $x$ above every generalisation of $y$ (\sref{IV.2.3.4}), which proves that the condition is necessary. To show that it is sufficient, note first that by (\sref{IV.17.5.1}), it suffices to prove that the morphism deduced from $f$ by the base change $\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})\to Y$ is smooth at the point $x$; we can therefore suppose $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$.

The question is local on $X$, so ((\sref{IV.16.4.22}) and ($\mathbf{0_I}$,~5.2.2)) we can suppose that $X$ is affine, and that there exist $r$ sections $s_i$ ($1\leq i\leq r$) of $\sh{O}_X$ above $X$ such that the sections $d_{X/Y}(s_i)$ generate $\Omega^1_{X/Y}$. Let
\[
g:X\to Z=Y[T_1,\ldots,T_r]=\bb{V}^r_Y
\]
be the $Y$-morphism corresponding to the homomorphism $\sh{O}_Z\to f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ of $\sh{O}_Y$-Modules defined by the $s_i$ (considered as sections of $f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ above $Y$) (\textbf{II},~1.2.7). We have the exact sequence (\sref{IV.16.4.19})
\[
g^*(\Omega^1_{Z/Y})\to\Omega^1_{X/Y}\to\Omega^1_{X/Z}\to 0.
\]
Since the $dT_i$ ($1\leq i\leq r$) generate $\Omega^1_{Z/Y}$, it follows from the definition of $g$ that the homomorphism $g^*(\Omega^1_{Z/Y})\to\Omega^1_{X/Y}$ is surjective, hence $\Omega^1_{X/Z}=0$, and $g$ is consequently \emph{non ramified} (\sref{IV.17.4.1}). We shall see that $g$ is \'etale at the point $x$, and taking into account (\sref{IV.17.3.8}), this will prove that $f$ is smooth at the point $x$. Since $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is assumed integre and geometrically unibranch, the same holds for $\sh{O}_{Z,z}$ (\sref{IV.17.5.7}) where $z=g(x)$, since the structural morphism $Z\to Y$ is smooth (\sref{IV.17.3.8}). By virtue of (\sref{IV.18.10.1}), it suffices to show that the homomorphism $\sh{O}_{Z,z}\to\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is injective; this amounts to seeing that the corresponding morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{X,x})\to\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Z,z})$ is dominant.

Let $y'$ be the generic point of $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$ and let $z'$ be the generic point of $Z=\operatorname{Spec}(\sh{O}_{Y,y}[T_1,\ldots,T_r])$, which is integre; if $h:Z\to Y$ is the structural morphism, we have $h(z')=y'$. It suffices to prove that there exists in $f^{-1}(y')$ a generalisation $x'$ of $x$ such that the image of $x'$ by the morphism $g_{y'}:f^{-1}(y')\to h^{-1}(y')$ is equal to $z'$. Moreover, $z'$ is also the generic point of $h^{-1}(y')=\operatorname{Spec}(k(y')[T_1,\ldots,T_r])$; since the morphism $g_{y'}$ is non ramified, hence quasi-finite (\sref{IV.17.4.2}), the same holds for its restriction to every irreducible component of $f^{-1}(y')$; since $f^{-1}(y')$ and $h^{-1}(y')$ are locally Noetherian and $\dim(h^{-1}(y'))=r$, it follows from (\sref{IV.5.4.1}) that the restriction of $g_{y'}$ to every irreducible component of \emph{dimension} $\geq r$ of $f^{-1}(y')$ is a dominant morphism. Now, there exists by hypothesis such a component containing a generalisation of $x$; \emph{a fortiori} its generic point is a generalisation of $x$, whence the conclusion.
\end{proof}


\subsection{Application to complete local Noetherian algebras over a field.}
\label{subsection:IV.18.11}

\begin{lemma}[18.11.1]
\label{IV.18.11.1}
\oldpage[IV]{169}
Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a local Noetherian complete ring which is a $k$-algebra, $K$ the residue field of $A$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If the K-vector space $\Omega^1_{K/k}$ is of finite rank, the A-module $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}$ is of finite type.
  \item If moreover $A$ is a $k$-algebra formally smooth (for the adic topology), $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}$ is a free A-module of rank equal to
  \[
  \dim(A)+\operatorname{rg}_K(\Omega^1_{K/k})-\operatorname{rg}_K(\Upsilon_{K/k}).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, for every sub-field $k_0$ of $k$ such that $\Omega^1_{K/k_0}$ is a K-vector space of finite rank, $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k_0}$ is a free A-module of rank equal to
\[
\dim(A)+\operatorname{rg}_K(\Omega^1_{K/k})-\operatorname{rg}_K(\Upsilon_{K/k})+\operatorname{rg}_k(\Omega^1_{k/k_0}).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Assertion~(i) is only recalled for convenience, having been proved in ($\mathbf{0}$,~20.7.15). To prove~(ii), note that this assertion was in fact established by the proof of ($\mathbf{0}$,~21.9.2), the statement of ($\mathbf{0}$,~21.9.2) alone requiring the finiteness of the degree of transcendence of $K$ over $k$ (via Cartier's equality ($\mathbf{0}$,~21.7.1)).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[18.11.2]
\label{IV.18.11.2}
Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$, $C$ a local $k$-algebra, Noetherian complete, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$, and which is integre; let $n$ be its dimension, $L$ its field of fractions.
Then $\Omega^1_{L/k}$ and $\Upsilon_{L/k}$ are L-vector spaces of finite rank, and one has
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.11.2.1}
  \operatorname{rg}_L(\Omega^1_{L/k})-\operatorname{rg}_L(\Upsilon_{L/k})\geq n.
\end{equation}
Suppose moreover that $[k:k^p]<+\infty$.
Then one has the equality
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.11.2.2}
  \operatorname{rg}_L(\Omega^1_{L/k})-\operatorname{rg}_L(\Upsilon_{L/k})=n.
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\Omega^1_{C/k}$ is then isomorphic to $\hat{\Omega}^1_{C/k}$, hence \sref{IV.18.11.1} a C-module of finite type.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, one knows ($\mathbf{0}$, 19.8.9) that there exists a sub-$k$-algebra $C_0$ of $C$ isomorphic to a formal power series algebra $k[[T_1,\ldots,T_n]]$ and such that $C$ is a finite $C_0$-algebra.
Hence $L$ is a finite extension of the field of fractions $L_0=k((T_1,\ldots,T_n))$ of $C_0$.

Now, one has the exact sequence of $L$-vector spaces obtained by applying ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.6.17.1) to the prime subfield of $k$ and to the three fields $k\subset L_0\subset L$ (and taking into account ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.6.21, (i)))
\[
  0\to\Upsilon_{L_0/k}\otimes_{L_0}L\to\Upsilon_{L/k}\to\Upsilon_{L/L_0}\to\Omega^1_{L_0/k}\otimes_{L_0}L\to\Omega^1_{L/k}\to\Omega^1_{L/L_0}\to 0.
\]
Since $L$ is a finite extension of $L_0$, $\Omega^1_{L/L_0}$ and $\Upsilon_{L/L_0}$ are $L$-vector spaces of finite rank having the same rank, by virtue of Cartier's equality ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.7.1).
As $L_0$ is separable over $k$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.9.6.4), one has $\Upsilon_{L_0/k}=0$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.6.3); one therefore deduces from the preceding exact sequence that $\Upsilon_{L/k}$ is of finite rank and that one has in all cases ($\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 11.10.2)
\[
  \operatorname{rg}_L(\Omega^1_{L/k})-\operatorname{rg}_L(\Upsilon_{L/k})=\operatorname{rg}_{L_0}(\Omega^1_{L_0/k})-\operatorname{rg}_{L_0}(\Upsilon_{L_0/k}).
\]

To prove \hyperref[IV.18.11.2.1]{(18.11.2.1)} or \hyperref[IV.18.11.2.2]{(18.11.2.2)}, one can therefore reduce to proving these relations by replacing $C$ and $L$ by $C_0$ and $L_0$.
As $L_0$ is separable over $k$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.9.6.4), one has $\Upsilon_{L_0/k}=0$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.6.3); on the other hand $\Omega^1_{L_0/k}=\Omega^1_{C_0/k}\otimes_{C_0}L_0$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.5.9).
One knows ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.9.4) that if $C_1=k[[T_1^p,\ldots,T_n^p]]$, $\hat{\Omega}^1_{C_0/k}$ identifies with $\Omega^1_{C_0/C_1}$; on the other hand, one has $\Omega^1_{C_0/k}=\Omega^1_{C_0/k[C_1^p]}$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.1.5), and as $C_1^p=k^p[[T_1^p,\ldots,T_n^p]]$, one has $k[C_1^p]\subset C_1$, with equality when $[k:k^p]<+\infty$.
Now, one has an exact sequence $\Omega^1_{C_0/k[C_1^p]}\to\hat{\Omega}^1_{C_0/k}\otimes_{C_0}L_0\to\Omega^1_{C_0/C_1}\to 0$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.5.7), whence a surjection $\Omega^1_{L_0/k}\to\hat{\Omega}^1_{C_0/k}\otimes_{C_0}L_0\to 0$.
As $\hat{\Omega}^1_{C_0/k}$ is a free $C_0$-module of rank $n$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.9.3), one sees that one has in all cases $\operatorname{rg}_{L_0}(\Omega^1_{L_0/k})\geq n$, with equality when $[k:k^p]<+\infty$; this already proves \hyperref[IV.18.11.2.1]{(18.11.2.1)} and \hyperref[IV.18.11.2.2]{(18.11.2.2)}.

Finally, to see that $\Omega^1_{C/k}$ is isomorphic to $\hat{\Omega}^1_{C/k}$ when $[k:k^p]<+\infty$, it suffices to prove that $\Omega^1_{C/k}$ is a $C$-module of finite type, since $C$ is a complete local Noetherian ring ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.3.3).
Since $\Omega^1_{C/k}$ is isomorphic to $\Omega^1_{C/k[C_1^p]}$ and $k[C_1^p]\subset C_1$, it all reduces to proving that $C$ is a finite $C_0$-module and $C_0$ a $k[C_1^p]$-module of finite type ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.4.7); but this results from the fact that $C$ is by hypothesis a finite $C_0$-algebra and $C_0$ is a $k[C_1^p]$-module of finite type by virtue of the hypothesis $[k:k^p]<+\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[18.11.3]
\label{IV.18.11.3}
\oldpage[IV]{170}
Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a local Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{p}$ a prime ideal of $A$ such that $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a Cohen--Macaulay ring.
For every regular system of parameters $(t_i)_{1\leq i\leq s}$ of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$, there exists a suite $(x_i)_{1\leq i\leq m}$ of elements of $\mathfrak{p}$ such that the $x_i$ form part of a system of parameters of $A$ and that, for every $i$, $x_i/1$ is congruent to $t_i$ modulo the ideal $t_1A_{\mathfrak{p}}+\ldots+t_{i-1}A_{\mathfrak{p}}$.
In particular, if $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is regular and the $t_i$ form a regular system of parameters of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$, the same is true of the $x_i/1$.
\end{lemma}

\oldpage[IV]{173}
\begin{lemma}[18.11.3.5]
\label{IV.18.11.3.5}
Let $k$ be a field, $R$, $S$ two local Noetherian complete $k$-algebras, such that, if $K$ is the residue field of $S$, $\Omega^1_{K/k}$ is of finite rank over $K$; let $u:R\to S$ be a $k$-homomorphism making $S$ a finite R-algebra; then one has $\hat{\Omega}^1_{S/R}=\Omega^1_{S/R}$, and the sequence
\begin{equation}
\tag{18.11.3.6}
\label{IV.18.11.3.6}
  \hat{\Omega}^1_{R/k}\otimes_R S\xrightarrow{v}\hat{\Omega}^1_{S/k}\xrightarrow{w}\Omega^1_{S/R}\to 0
\end{equation}
(cf.\ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.7.17.3)) is exact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion results from the fact that $\Omega^1_{S/R}$ is an S-module of finite type ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.4.7) and from ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.3.6).
On the other hand, one knows ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.7.17.3) that the image of $v$ is dense in $\operatorname{Ker}(w)$ and that $w$ is surjective; but it follows from \sref{IV.18.11.1} that $\hat{\Omega}^1_{S/k}$ is an $S$-module of finite type; one concludes that every sub-$S$-module of $\hat{\Omega}^1_{S/k}$ is closed ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.3.5), whence the lemma.

Applying this lemma to the case where $R=A_0$, $S=A$, and noting that $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A_0/k}$ is a free $A_0$-module of rank $n$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.9.3); since $\operatorname{Spec}(B)\to\operatorname{Spec}(B_0)$ is non ramified at the point $\mathfrak{p}'$, one has $(\Omega^1_{A/A_0})_\mathfrak{p}=\Omega^1_{A_\mathfrak{p}/B_{\mathfrak{r}}}=0$ (16.4.15 and 17.4.1).
If one localises the exact sequence \sref{IV.18.11.3.6} at $\mathfrak{p}$, one obtains hence a surjective homomorphism
\[
  v_\mathfrak{p}:(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A_0/k}\otimes_{A_0}A)_\mathfrak{p}\to(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}
\]
whence one concludes that the $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$ admits a system of $n$ generators.
But the $A$-module $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}$ is of rank $n$, by virtue of ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.9.5), which is applicable to the complete integral $A$ since the field of fractions of $A$ is of characteristic $0$.
The $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$ is hence also of rank $n$, and as its quotient by its torsion submodule admits a system of $n$ generators, one deduces immediately that these $n$ generators form a free system, whence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[18.11.4]
\label{IV.18.11.4}
Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a $k$-algebra local Noetherian complete, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$.
Let $k'$ be any extension of $k$, and set $A'=A\hat{\otimes}_k k'$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $A'$ is a semi-local Noetherian complete ring, direct composition of complete local rings $A'_i$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$ which are faithfully flat A-modules, and whose residue fields are finite extensions of $k'$; if $\mathfrak{m}$ is the maximal ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{m} A'$ is an ideal of definition of $A'$; one has $\dim(A'_i)=\dim(A)$ for every $i$.
  \item For every $i$, $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A'/k'}$ is canonically isomorphic to $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\otimes_A A'_i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV]{174}
(i) The first assertions result immediately from \sref{IV.7.5.5} and from ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.6.2); the fact that $\mathfrak{m} A'$ is an ideal of definition of $A'$ also results from \sref{IV.7.5.5}, for since $K$ is the residue field of $A$, $K\otimes_k k'$ is a $k'$-algebra finite.
Finally $A'_i/\mathfrak{m} A'_i$ is one of the artinian local rings of which $K\otimes_k k'$ is composed direct (\sref{IV.7.5.5}), hence is of dimension $0$; since $A'_i$ is a flat A-module, the equality of dimensions results from \sref{IV.6.1.2}.

(ii) As $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}$ is, by virtue of the hypothesis on $K$, an A-module of finite type \sref{IV.18.11.1}, $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\otimes_A A'$ is complete and identifies with the completed tensor product ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.7.4), $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\otimes_A A'=\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\hat{\otimes}_A(A\hat{\otimes}_k k')$ identifies with $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\hat{\otimes}_k k'$.
But $\Omega^1_{A/k}\otimes_k k'$ identifies with $\Omega^1_{A''/k'}$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.5.5), where $A''=A\otimes_k k'$, and since $A'$ is by definition the separated completion of $\Omega^1_{A''/k'}$, one has a canonical isomorphism $\hat{\Omega}^1_{A'/k'}\xrightarrow{\sim}\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\otimes_A A'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[18.11.5]
\label{IV.18.11.5}
\oldpage[IV]{174}
Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a local $k$-algebra Noetherian and complete, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$, $\mathfrak{p}$ a prime ideal of $A$ distinct from the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, such that there exists a unique minimal prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $A$ contained in $\mathfrak{p}$ for which $\dim(A/\mathfrak{q})=\dim(A)$ (which will take place in particular when $A$ is equidimensional), $m$ an integer $\geq 0$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] The $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_{\mathfrak{p}}$ admits a system of $m$ generators.
  \item[b)] There exists a $k$-homomorphism local $u:B\to A$, where $B=k[[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_m]]$, making $A$ a B-algebra finite, and such that the corresponding morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(A)\to\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ is non ramified at the point $\mathfrak{p}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
To prove that $b)$ implies $a)$, note that by virtue of the lemma \sref{IV.18.11.3.5}, one has then the exact sequence
\[
  \hat{\Omega}^1_{B/k}\otimes_B A\to\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\to\Omega^1_{A/B}\to 0
\]
since $A$ is a finite B-algebra; localising at $\mathfrak{p}$ and noting that by hypothesis one has then $(\Omega^1_{A/B})_\mathfrak{p}=0$ \sref{IV.17.4.1}, one obtains a surjective homomorphism $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{B/k}\otimes_B A)_\mathfrak{p}\to(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$, and the conclusion follows from the fact that $\hat{\Omega}^1_{B/k}$ is a free B-module of rank $m$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 21.9.3).

To prove that $a)$ implies $b)$, let us first prove the following lemmas.

\medskip
\label{IV.18.11.5.1}\emph{Lemma} \sref{IV.18.11.5.1}. ---
\emph{Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a $k$-algebra local Noetherian and complete, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$, $\mathfrak{p}$ a prime ideal of $A$, $\mathfrak{q}$ a minimal ideal of $A$ contained in $\mathfrak{p}$.
Then the minimum number of generators of the $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$ is at least equal to $\dim(A/\mathfrak{q})$.}

\medskip
\oldpage[IV]{175}
Set $n=\dim(A/\mathfrak{q})$, and let $m$ be the minimum number of generators of the $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$, which is equal to $\operatorname{rg}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}((\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{A_\mathfrak{p}}k(\mathfrak{p}))$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~II, \textsection~3, n\textsuperscript{o}~2, cor.~2 de la prop.~4).
Note that $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{q}=(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{A_\mathfrak{p}}A_\mathfrak{q}$, hence the minimum number of generators of the $A_\mathfrak{q}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{q}$ is \emph{at most} equal to $m$.
It suffices hence to consider the case where $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{q}$ is minimal.
In second place, let us show that one can restrict to the case where $k$ is algebraically closed.
Indeed, let $k'$ be an algebraic closure of $k$, and set $A'=A\otimes_k k'$, which is a direct composition of local $k'$-algebras $A'_i$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$, whose residue fields are isomorphic to $k'$, and which are faithfully flat $A$-modules \sref{IV.18.11.4}.
Applying \sref{IV.2.3.4} and \sref{IV.6.1.1}, one sees that in any given $A'_i$, there exists a minimal ideal $\mathfrak{q}'_i$ above $\mathfrak{q}$ such that $n'=\dim(A'/\mathfrak{q}'_i)\geq\dim(A/\mathfrak{q})=n$.
On the other hand \sref{IV.18.11.4}, the minimum number $m'$ of generators of the $(A'_i)_{\mathfrak{q}'_i}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A'/k'})_{\mathfrak{q}'_i}$ is at most equal to $m$; whence our assertion.

Suppose therefore $k$ algebraically closed, and let us show that one can moreover restrict to the case where $\mathfrak{q}=0$.
Indeed, one has $\Omega^1_{(A/\mathfrak{q})/A}=0$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.4.12), hence the exact sequence \sref{IV.18.11.3.6} furnishes a surjective homomorphism $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{q})\to\hat{\Omega}^1_{(A/\mathfrak{q})/k}$, whence one deduces immediately that the minimum number of generators of the K-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{(A/\mathfrak{q})/k})\otimes_{A/\mathfrak{q}}K$, where $K$ is the field of fractions of $A/\mathfrak{q}$, is at most equal to $m$.

But if $A$ is integral and $K$ is its field of fractions, $K$ is separable over the algebraically closed field $k$, hence a geometrically regular $k$-algebra \sref{IV.6.7.6}; one can apply \sref{IV.18.11.3} for $\mathfrak{p}=0$ observing that $k=k^p$ since $k$ is algebraically closed, and in this case one has $m=n$.

\medskip
\label{IV.18.11.5.2}\emph{Lemma} \sref{IV.18.11.5.2}. ---
\emph{Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a $k$-algebra local Noetherian complete, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$, $\mathfrak{m}$ its maximal ideal, $\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{m}$ a prime ideal of $A$.
Let $m$ be the minimum number of generators of the $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$, and let $(x_i)_{1\leq i\leq m}$ be a family of elements of $\mathfrak{m}$ not belonging to $\mathfrak{p}$.
Then there exist $m$ invertible elements $u_i$ $(1\leq i\leq m)$ of $A$ such that if one sets $y_i=u_ix_i$, the canonical images of the $dy_i$ in $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$ generate this $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module.}

\medskip
For every $x\in A$, denote by $\delta(x)$ the canonical image of $dx$ $(=d_{A/k}x)$ in $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{A_\mathfrak{p}}k(\mathfrak{p})$; since this $k(\mathfrak{p})$-vector space is of rank $m$, it suffices (by virtue of Nakayama's lemma) to prove that one can determine the $u_i$ such that the $\delta(u_ix_i)$ form a free system.
We argue by induction and suppose that, for some integer $r<m$, we have determined the $u_i$ $(1\leq i\leq r)$ such that the $\delta(u_ix_i)$ for $1\leq i\leq r$ are linearly independent over $k(\mathfrak{p})$; if $\delta(x_{r+1})$ is not a linear combination of the $\delta(u_ix_i)$ for $1\leq i\leq r$, it suffices to take $u_{r+1}=1$ to continue the induction.
In the contrary case, note that for $u\in A$, $\delta(ux_{r+1})$ is the canonical image of $d(u)x_{r+1}+u(dx_{r+1})$; since $u(dx_{r+1})$ has a canonical image that is a linear combination of $\delta(u_ix_i)$ for $1\leq i\leq r$, and since on the other hand the canonical image of $x_{r+1}$ in $k(\mathfrak{p})$ is $\neq 0$, one sees that it suffices to prove that there exists an invertible element $u\in A$ such that $\delta(u)$ is not a linear combination of the $\delta(u_ix_i)$ for $1\leq i\leq r$.
Now, it follows from ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.7.15) and ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.2.9) that the $\delta(x)$ generate the $k(\mathfrak{p})$-vector space $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{A_\mathfrak{p}}k(\mathfrak{p})$;
\oldpage[IV]{176}
since by hypothesis $r<m$, there exists therefore $z\in A$ such that $\delta(z)$ is not a linear combination of the $\delta(u_ix_i)$ for $1\leq i\leq r$; if $z\notin\mathfrak{m}$, one takes $u=z$; otherwise, $1+z$ is invertible and one has $\delta(1+z)=\delta(z)$ since $d(1+z)=d(z)$; one takes then $u=1+z$, which completes the proof of \sref{IV.18.11.5.2}.

\medskip
Let us now return to the proof of the implication $a)\Rightarrow b)$ in \sref{IV.18.11.5}.
Note first that since $\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}$, there exists a system of parameters $(x_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ of $A$ (with $n=\dim(A)$) such that $x_i\notin\mathfrak{p}$ for $1\leq i\leq n$: indeed, one cannot have $x_i\in\mathfrak{p}$ for every $i$, otherwise $A/\mathfrak{p}$ would be of finite length, and $\mathfrak{p}$ would be maximal, contrary to the hypothesis.
But if for instance $x_1\notin\mathfrak{p}$, it suffices, for each index $i$ such that $x_i\in\mathfrak{p}$, to replace $x_i$ by $x_1+x_i$, to have a system of parameters none of whose elements belong to $\mathfrak{p}$.
The hypothesis $a)$ and the relation $\dim(A/\mathfrak{q})=n$ imply, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.11.5.1}, that $m\geq n$; one can therefore consider a family $(x_i)_{1\leq i\leq m}$ of elements $x_i\notin\mathfrak{p}$, whose $n$ first form a system of parameters of $A$.
Multiplying moreover the $x_i$ by invertible elements $u_i$ of $A$, one can, thanks to \sref{IV.18.11.5.2}, suppose that the images of $dx_i$ $(1\leq i\leq m)$ in $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$ generate this $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module, and the multiplication by the $u_i$ has not altered the fact that the $x_i$ for $1\leq i\leq n$ form a system of parameters.
Consider then the $k$-homomorphism local $u:B\to A$ such that $u(\mathrm{T}_i)=x_i$ for $1\leq i\leq m$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~III, \textsection~4, n\textsuperscript{o}~5, prop.~6); since the $x_i$ generate an ideal of definition of $A$, it follows from ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.4.4 and 7.4.3) that $u$ makes $A$ a finite B-algebra.
One has therefore \sref{IV.18.11.3.5} the exact sequence
\[
  (\hat{\Omega}^1_{B/k})\otimes_B A\xrightarrow{v}\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k}\xrightarrow{w}\Omega^1_{A/B}\to 0
\]
But the $d_Ax_i$ are the canonical images by $v$ of the elements $d_B\mathrm{T}_i\otimes 1$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 20.5.2.6).
If one localises the preceding exact sequence at $\mathfrak{p}$, one sees hence that $v_\mathfrak{p}:(\hat{\Omega}^1_{B/k})\otimes_B A_\mathfrak{p}\to(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_\mathfrak{p}$ is surjective, and hence one has $0=(\Omega^1_{A/B})_\mathfrak{p}=\Omega^1_{A_\mathfrak{p}/B_\mathfrak{r}}$ (where $\mathfrak{r}$ is the inverse image of $\mathfrak{p}$ in $A$, cf.\ \sref{IV.16.4.15}).
By virtue of \sref{IV.17.4.1}, this implies property $b)$ of \sref{IV.18.11.5}. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[18.11.6]
\label{IV.18.11.6}
In the statement of \sref{IV.18.11.5}, one cannot suppress the hypothesis $\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}$.
Indeed, for every $k$-homomorphism local $u:B\to A$, where $B=k[[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_r]]$ ($r$ any integer) making $A$ a finite B-algebra, $\mathfrak{m}$ is the only point of $\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ above the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n}$ of $B$; the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(A)\to\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ can be non ramified at $\mathfrak{m}$ only if $A/\mathfrak{n} A$ is a field, separable extension of $k$ \sref{IV.17.4.1}, which implies that the residue field $K$ of $A$ is a separable extension of $k$.
If this condition is not satisfied, the conclusion of \sref{IV.18.11.5} can never be verified for $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}$, whatever the integer $m$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[18.11.7]
\label{IV.18.11.7}
\oldpage[IV]{176}
Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a $k$-algebra local Noetherian complete equidimensional, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$, $\mathfrak{p}$ a prime ideal of $A$.
Then the minimum number of generators of the $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is at least equal to $\dim(A)$.

This is a particular case of \sref{IV.18.11.5.1}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[18.11.8]
\label{IV.18.11.8}
Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a $k$-algebra local Noetherian complete integre, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$.
If $K$ is the field of fractions of $A$, one has
\[
\operatorname{rg}_K((\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})\otimes_k K)\geq\dim(A).
\]
\end{corollary}

It suffices to take $\mathfrak{p}=0$ in (\sref{IV.18.11.7}).

\begin{corollary}[18.11.9]
\label{IV.18.11.9}
\oldpage[IV]{177}
Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a $k$-algebra local Noetherian complete of dimension $n$, whose residue field is a finite extension of $k$.
Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal of $A$ distinct from the maximal ideal, and containing a unique minimal prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ such that $\dim(A/\mathfrak{q})=n$.
Suppose that the $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_{\mathfrak{p}}$ admits $n$ generators.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is free of rank $n$.
  \item There exists a $k$-homomorphism local $u:B\to A$, where $B=k[[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_n]]$, making $A$ a B-algebra finite, and such that the corresponding morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(A)\to\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ is \'etale at the point $\mathfrak{p}$.
  \item The $k$-algebra $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is geometrically regular.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let us first prove~(ii); by virtue of (\sref{IV.18.11.5}), there exists a local homomorphism $u:B=k[[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_n]]\to A$ making $A$ a finite $B$-algebra, and such that the corresponding morphism $\Spec(A)\to\Spec(B)$ is non ramified at the point $\mathfrak{p}$; set $\mathfrak{r}=u^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})$.
The hypothesis on $\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{p}$, and the fact that $A$ is a quotient of a regular ring ($\mathbf{0}$, 19.8.8) imply that $\dim(A_{\mathfrak{p}})=n-\dim(A/\mathfrak{p})$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 16.5.12).
Similarly $\dim(B_{\mathfrak{r}})=n-\dim(B/\mathfrak{r})$.
Finally, since the morphism $\Spec(A)\to\Spec(B)$ is non ramified at the point $\mathfrak{p}$, the fibre of this morphism at the point $\mathfrak{r}$ is of dimension $0$, hence ($\mathbf{0}$, 16.3.9) $\dim(A/\mathfrak{p})\leq\dim(B/\mathfrak{r})$ and consequently $\dim(B_{\mathfrak{r}})\leq\dim(A_{\mathfrak{p}})$.
One therefore concludes from (\sref{IV.18.10.1}) that the morphism $\Spec(A)\to\Spec(B)$ is \'etale at the point $\mathfrak{p}$.

Assertion~(iii) results from the fact that $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is formally smooth over $B_{\mathfrak{r}}$ and that $B_{\mathfrak{r}}$ is formally smooth over $k$ (for the preadic topologies) ($\mathbf{0}$, 19.3.4 and 19.3.5), hence $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is formally smooth over $k$ for its preadic topology, and consequently is geometrically regular over $k$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 22.5.8).

Let us finally prove~(i).
Let $k'$ be an algebraically closed extension of $k$, and consider the $k'$-algebra semi-local $A'=A\otimes_k k'$.
Viewing $A$ as a $B$-module of finite type via the homomorphism $u$, one can write, up to canonical isomorphism, $A'=A\otimes_B(B\widehat{\otimes}_k k')$ ((7.5.7.1), where we recall that it is not necessary to suppose the residue field of $B$ is a finite extension of $k$).
Setting $B'=B\widehat{\otimes}_k k'$, which identifies canonically with the formal power series algebra $k'[[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_n]]$.
Since the morphism $\Spec(A)\to\Spec(B)$ is finite and \'etale at the point $\mathfrak{p}$, the morphism $\Spec(A')\to\Spec(B')$ is finite, and \'etale at every point $\mathfrak{p}'$ above $\mathfrak{p}$ (\sref{IV.17.3.3}); moreover, $A'$ is a direct sum of local rings $A'_i$ of dimension $n$ (\sref{IV.18.11.4}) and $\mathfrak{p}'$ identifies with a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}'_i$ of one of the $A'_i$.
The same reasoning as above then proves that $(A'_i)_{\mathfrak{p}'_i}$ is regular.
The same reasoning that proves~(iii) shows that $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A'_i/k'})_{\mathfrak{p}'_i}$ is a free $(A'_i)_{\mathfrak{p}'_i}$-module of finite type.
But since $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A'/k'})_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ is isomorphic to $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(A'_i)_{\mathfrak{p}'_i}$ (\sref{IV.18.11.4}) and $(A'_i)_{\mathfrak{p}'_i}$ is a faithfully flat $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module, one sees that $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a free $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module of finite type (\sref{IV.2.5.2}); moreover, by virtue of (\sref{IV.18.11.5.1}) and the hypothesis $\dim(A/\mathfrak{q})=n$, one concludes that $(\hat{\Omega}^1_{A/k})_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is a free $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module of rank $n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Applications of \'etale localisation to quasi-finite morphisms (generalisations of earlier results).}
\label{subsection:IV.18.12}

The results of this section were communicated to us by P.~Deligne.

\begin{theorem}[18.12.1]
\label{IV.18.12.1}
\oldpage[IV]{181}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$.
Suppose that $x$ is an isolated point of the space $f^{-1}(y)$.
Then there exists an \'etale morphism $Y'\to Y$, a point $x'$ of $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ above $x$, and an open neighbourhood $V'$ of $x'$ in $X'$ such that, setting $f'=f_{(V')}:X'\to Y'$, $f'|V'$ is a finite morphism.
If moreover $f$ is separated, $V'$ is both open and closed, and $X'$ is thus the sum of two sub-preschemes induced on opens of $X'$, one of which is finite over $Y$ and contains $x'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The last assertion follows from the fact that, if $f$ is separated, it suffices to note that $f'$ is also separated; hence, if $j':V'\to X'$ is the injection, the morphism $f'\circ j'$ is finite and therefore $j'$ is also finite (\sref[II]{II.6.1.5}), and consequently $V'=j'(V')$ is closed in $X'$ (\sref[II]{II.6.1.1}).

The question being local on $X$, we may suppose $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ and $X=\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ affine, $B$ being an A-algebra of finite type, and $C=A[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_r]$ and $\mathfrak{J}$ is an ideal of type fini of $C$ contained in $\mathfrak{J}$, so that $\mathfrak{J}$ is the union of the filtering family of the $\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$.
If $X$ and $X_\lambda=\operatorname{Spec}(C/\mathfrak{J}_\lambda)$ are considered as closed sub-preschemes of $Z=\operatorname{Spec}(C)$, one has, for the underlying spaces, $X=\bigcap X_\lambda$; if $f_\lambda:X_\lambda\to Y$ is the structural morphism,
one deduces that $f^{-1}(y)=\bigcap_\lambda f_\lambda^{-1}(y)$, and the sets $f_\lambda^{-1}(y)$ are closed in the Noetherian space $\operatorname{Spec}(k(y)[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_r])$.
One can therefore suppose that $f^{-1}(y)=\{x\}$.
One can moreover suppose that the set of points of $X$ isolated in their fibre is open and that we can restrict to the case where $f$ is \emph{quasi-finite}.
Let $A''$ be the henselisation of $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$, and set $Y''=\operatorname{Spec}(A'')$.
If $X''=X\times_Y Y''$, $f''=f_{(Y'')}:X''\to Y''$ is separated, quasi-finite, and of finite presentation; as $A''$ is henselian, there exists for every point $x''\in X''$ above $x$ an open neighbourhood, open and closed $V''$ of $x''$ in $X''$ which is finite over $Y''$ (\sref{IV.18.5.11}[c)]); as the immersion $V''\to X''$ is open and closed, hence quasi-compact, of finite presentation (\sref{IV.1.6.2}), hence $f''|V''$ is of finite presentation.

$A''$ being, by definition, the inductive limit of a filtering family of essentially \'etale strictly local $(A_\lambda)$ of $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$-algebras; each $A_\lambda$ can be supposed to be an $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$-algebra of finite presentation (\sref[I]{I.8.1.2}[a]) and (\sref[I]{I.8.1.2}[a])).
Finally, using the theorem of inductive limits (\sref[IV]{IV.8.10.5}), the fact that $Y$ is the projective limit of a filtering family $(Y'_\alpha)$ of affine schemes over $Y$.
If $x'_\alpha$ is the projection of $x'$ onto $X'_\alpha=X\times_Y Y'_\alpha$, one can suppose the neighbourhood $V'_\alpha$ is of the form $V'_\alpha\times_{Y'_\alpha} Y''$ where $V'_\alpha$ is an open neighbourhood of $x'_\alpha$ in $X'_\alpha$ (\sref{IV.8.2.11}).
Finally, $V''$ being of finite presentation over $Y''$, we deduce that for $\alpha$ sufficiently large, $V'_\alpha$ is finite over $Y'_\alpha$ (\sref[IV]{IV.8.10.5}[(x)]) and that for any such $\alpha$ it is convenient.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[18.12.2]
\label{IV.18.12.2}
In the preceding demonstration, we see (taking into account \sref{IV.18.6.2}) that one has constructed a $Y'$ answering the question, and such in addition that if $y'=f'(X')$, the homomorphism $k(y)\to k(y')$ is bijective.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[18.12.3]
\label{IV.18.12.3}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type and separated, $y$ a point of $Y$ such that the subspace $f^{-1}(y)$ is finite and discrete.
Then there exists an \'etale morphism $Y'\to Y$, a point $y'\in Y'$ above $y$, such that $k(y')=k(y)$, and a decomposition of $X'$ into a sum of two sub-preschemes $X'_1$, $X'_2$ induced on opens of $X'$, such that the restriction $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$ to $X'_1$ is a finite morphism and that $X'_2\cap f'^{-1}(y')=\emp$.
\end{corollary}

If $n$ is the number of points of $f^{-1}(y)$, one argues by induction on $n$, the corollary being trivial for $n=0$.
Let $x$ be a point of $f^{-1}(y)$; by virtue of \sref{IV.18.12.1} and \sref{IV.18.12.2} there is an \'etale morphism $Y_1\to Y$, a point $y_1$ of $Y_1$ above $y$ such that $k(y_1)=k(y)$, and if one sets $S_1=X\times_Y Y_1$, $f_1=f_{(S_1)}:S_1\to Y_1$, there exists a point $x_1$ of $f_1^{-1}(y_1)$
such that $S_1$ is the sum of two opens $V_1$, $X_1$, $V_1$ being finite over $Y_1$ and a neighbourhood of $x_1$.
By virtue of the relation $k(y_1)=k(y)$, the fibre $f_1^{-1}(y_1)$ in $S_1$ is isomorphic to $f^{-1}(y)$, hence $X_1\cap f_1^{-1}(y_1)$ is finite, discrete and has $n-1$ points.
As $f_1|X_1$ is locally of finite type and separated, one applies the induction hypothesis to this morphism: there is an \'etale morphism $Y'\to Y_1$, a point $y'\in Y'$ above $y_1$ such that $k(y')=k(y_1)$, and, if $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ and if $p:X'\to S_1$ is the canonical projection, one has a decomposition of $p^{-1}(X_1)$ into a sum of two sub-preschemes $U'$ and $X'_2$ induced on opens of $X'$ such that $U'$ is finite over $Y'$.
Setting $V'=p^{-1}(V_1)$ and $X'_1$ the sum of $U'$ and $V'$, the answer to the question is obtained by taking $X'_1$ the sum of $U'$ and $V'$.
The following corollary improves (\sref[IV]{IV.8.11.1}):

\begin{corollary}[18.12.4]
\label{IV.18.12.4}
\oldpage[IV]{182}
For a morphism $f:X\to Y$ to be finite, it suffices that it be separated, universally closed, locally of finite type, and that, for every $y\in Y$, $f^{-1}(y)$ be a finite discrete space; in particular, a morphism that is proper and quasi-finite is finite.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, one can apply the corollary \sref{IV.18.12.3} to any point $y$ of $Y$.
With the notations of this corollary, $f'$ is a closed morphism, hence, since $X'_2$ is closed in $X'$, $f'(X'_2)$ is closed in $Y'$ and does not contain $y'$;
it follows that there exists an open neighbourhood $U'$ of $y'$ in $Y'$ such that $U'\to Y$ is of finite type and that $f^{-1}(U')=X\times_Y U'$ is finite on $U'$.
Let $U$ be the image of $U'$ in $Y$, which is an open of $Y$ by virtue of \sref{IV.11.3.1} since $Y'\to Y$ is \'etale, hence of finite presentation;
it is clear that $X\times_Y U'=f^{-1}(U)\times_U U'$.
Considering now the morphism $U'\to U$ which is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, one deduces from (\sref[IV]{IV.2.7.1}[(xv)]) that the morphism $f$, restriction of $f$, is finite, which proves the corollary.

\begin{remark}[18.12.5]
\label{IV.18.12.5}
One notes that in the proof of \sref{IV.18.12.4} one has not used the hypothesis that $f$ is universally closed in its general form, but only that $f_{(Y')}$ is a closed morphism for every \'etale morphism $Y'\to Y$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[18.12.6]
\label{IV.18.12.6}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{\alph*)}]
  \item $f$ is a closed immersion.
  \item $f$ is a proper monomorphism.
  \item $f$ is proper and, for every $y\in Y$, the $k(y)$-prescheme $X_y=f^{-1}(y)$ is radiciel and geometrically reduced over $\operatorname{Spec}(k(y))$ (that is to say, empty or $k(y)$-isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}(k(y))$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

This follows from \sref{IV.18.12.4} together with (\sref[IV]{IV.8.11.5}) and (\sref[IV]{IV.8.11.1}).

\begin{proposition}[18.12.7]
\label{IV.18.12.7}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$.
For there to exist an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ such that the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is a closed immersion, it is necessary and sufficient that $X_y$ be radiciel and geometrically reduced over $k(y)$ (that is to say, empty or $k(y)$-isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec}(k(y))$) and that there exist an open neighbourhood $V$ of $y$ such that the restriction $f^{-1}(V)\to V$ of $f$ is a universally closed morphism.
\end{proposition}

The conditions being evidently necessary, let us show they are sufficient.
One can restrict to the case where $X_y\neq\emp$.
By virtue of the second condition, one can already suppose $f$ universally closed.
We show furthermore that we can suppose $f$ affine; this follows from the following lemma:

\begin{lemma}[18.12.7.1]
\label{IV.18.12.7.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a closed morphism, $y\in Y$ a point such that every affine open neighbourhood of $X_y$ contains a single affine open neighbourhood of $X_y$ (a condition that is always satisfied when $X_y$ is empty or reduced to a single point).
Then there exists an affine open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ such that $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is an affine morphism.
\end{lemma}

Indeed, let $U_0$ be an open affine neighbourhood of $y$ in $Y$ and $V$ an affine open neighbourhood of $X_y$ contained in $f^{-1}(U_0)$.
Since $f$ is closed, there exists an open affine neighbourhood $U\subset U_0$ of $y$ such that $f^{-1}(U)\subset V$.
As we can suppose $Y$ and $X$ affine, we can suppose $f$ proper, and it suffices to show that there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ such that $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ is a quasi-finite morphism (\sref{IV.18.12.4}).
But there exists a neighbourhood $V$ of the set $X_y$ (reduced to a single point) such that $f|V$ is quasi-finite (\sref{IV.13.1.4}), and since $f$ is closed there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $y$ such that $f^{-1}(U)\subset V$, which finishes the proof of \sref{IV.18.12.6}.

\begin{proposition}[18.12.8]
\label{IV.18.12.8}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes.
For $f$ to be entire, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be affine and universally closed.
\end{proposition}

The conditions are necessary (\sref[II]{II.6.1.10}); let us show they are sufficient.
We may suppose $Y=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ affine, hence also $X=\operatorname{Spec}(B)$, and it suffices to show that every element $b\in B$ is integral over $A$ (\sref[II]{II.6.1.1}).
Let $B'$ be the sub-A-algebra of $B$ generated by $b$, which is an A-algebra of finite type; setting $X'=\operatorname{Spec}(B')$, so that $f:X\to Y$ factorises as $X\xrightarrow{g}X'\xrightarrow{h}Y$, where $h$ is of finite type, and $g$ is dominant since the homomorphism $B'\to B$ is injective (\sref[I]{I.1.2.7}).
As $h$ is separated and $h\circ g$ universally closed, $g$ is universally closed (\sref[II]{II.5.4.3} and \sref[II]{II.5.4.9}); one concludes (\sref[II]{II.5.4.3} and \sref[II]{II.5.4.9}) that $h$ is also universally closed, hence \emph{proper} since it is of finite type and separated.
But then, for every $y\in Y$, the morphism $h^{-1}(y)\to\operatorname{Spec}(k(y))$ deduced of $h$ is \emph{quasi-finite}; it follows from \sref{IV.18.12.4} that $h$ is \emph{quasi-finite}; and therefore from \sref{IV.18.12.4} that $h$ is finite, which proves that $B'$ is an A-algebra finite, and hence that $b$ is integral over $A$. \quad Q.E.D.

\begin{remark}[18.12.9]
\label{IV.18.12.9}
\oldpage[IV]{183}
One could ask whether a morphism $f:X\to Y$ be entire when one supposes only that it is separated, universally closed, and such that for every $y\in Y$, the morphism $f^{-1}(y)\to\operatorname{Spec}(k(y))$ deduced of $f$ is entire.
It would suffice for this to show that under these conditions $f$ is affine, or else that every fibre $X_y$ is contained in an affine open neighbourhood.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[18.12.10]
\label{IV.18.12.10}
A morphism $f:X\to Y$ that is injective and universally closed is entire.
\end{corollary}

It suffices, by \sref{IV.18.12.8}, to show that $f$ is affine, which follows from the lemma \sref{IV.18.12.7.1} and the hypothesis.

\begin{corollary}[18.12.11]
\label{IV.18.12.11}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes (resp.\ a morphism locally of finite type).
For $f$ to be a universal homeomorphism \sref[I]{I.2.4.2}, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be entire (resp.\ finite), radiciel, and surjective.
\end{corollary}

We know that the sufficient conditions are sufficient; they are also necessary by virtue of \sref{IV.18.12.10} and \sref{IV.18.12.4}.
The following proposition improves (\sref[IV]{IV.8.11.2}):

\begin{proposition}[18.12.12]
\label{IV.18.12.12}
Every morphism $f:X\to Y$ that is quasi-finite and separated is quasi-affine.
\end{proposition}

Set $\mathscr{A}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$, which is a $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra quasi-coherent since $f$ is quasi-compact and separated \sref[I]{I.1.7.4};
let $Z=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathscr{A})$ (\sref[II]{II.1.3.1}) so that $f$ factorises canonically as $X\xrightarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{h}Y$, $h$ being affine and $g$ an \emph{open immersion},
or, what amounts to the same (\sref[IV]{IV.17.9.1}), that $g$ is \'etale and radiciel.
It suffices evidently to show that, for every $y\in Y$, $g$ is \'etale and radiciel at every point of $f^{-1}(y)$.
For every $y\in Y$, one can apply $f$ the result of \sref{IV.18.12.3}, whose notation we retain; since the morphism $Y'\to Y$ is flat, and $f$ is quasi-compact and separated, one has, up to canonical isomorphism, $f'_*(\sh{O}_{X'})=\mathscr{A}'\simeq\mathscr{A}'$ (\sref[II]{II.2.3.1}), hence $Z'=Z\times_Y Y'$ is identified with $\operatorname{Spec}(f'_*(\sh{O}_{X'}))$, and in the canonical factorisation $X'\xrightarrow{g'}Z'\xrightarrow{h'}Y'$ of $f'$ (\sref[II]{II.1.2.7}), one has $h'=h_{(Y')}$ and $g'=g_{(Y')}$.
This being so, the decomposition of $X'$ into two sub-preschemes $X'_1$, $X'_2$ induces the decomposition of $\mathscr{A}'$ into a direct product of two $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebras quasi-coherent $\mathscr{A}'_1$, $\mathscr{A}'_2$, direct images of $\sh{O}_{X'_1}$ and $\sh{O}_{X'_2}$, so that $Z'$ is identified with the sum $Z'_1\amalg Z'_2$, where $Z'_i=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathscr{A}'_i)$ and $g'(X'_i)\subset Z'_i$ for $i=1,2$.
As $X'_1$ is finite over $Y'$, $g'_1=g'|X'_1$ is affine; as $X'_2\cap f'^{-1}(y')=\emp$, one sees that $g'_1=g'|X'_1:X'_1\to Z'_1$ is an isomorphism.
The morphism $Y'\to Y$ being \'etale, flat, and locally of finite presentation, one deduces first from (\sref[IV]{IV.17.7.4}) that $g$ is \'etale at all points of $X$ which are projections of points of $f'^{-1}(y')$,
that is to say at all points of $f^{-1}(y)$ (\sref[II]{II.1.2.5}).
On the other hand, the morphism $g'_y:f'^{-1}(y')\to h^{-1}(y')$ deduced from $g'$ is also radiciel at every point of $f^{-1}(y)$, which proves the proposition.
The following result improves \sref{IV.8.12.6}:

\begin{corollary}[18.12.13]
\label{IV.18.12.13}
(\emph{``Main Theorem'' of Zariski.})
Let $Y$ be a prescheme quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $f:X\to Y$ a quasi-finite and separated morphism.
Then there exists a factorisation of $f$
\[
  X\xrightarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{u}Y
\]
where $g$ is an open immersion (not necessarily quasi-compact) and $u$ a finite morphism.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, it follows from \sref{IV.18.12.12} that $f$ is quasi-affine, hence quasi-projective.
One can then apply \sref{IV.18.12.8}, where the hypothesis on the existence of an ample $\sh{O}_Y$-Module can in fact be replaced by the assumption that $Y$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated:
indeed, it follows from (\sref{IV.8.12.3}) that the existence of the announced factorisation in \sref{IV.18.12.8} is a local property on $Y$, and it suffices therefore to prove it when $Y$ is affine.

\begin{proposition}[18.12.16]
\label{IV.18.12.16}
Let $g:Y\to S$ be a morphism quasi-compact, $f:X\to Y$ a separated and quasi-finite morphism.
For every $\sh{O}_Y$-Module invertible $\mathscr{L}$, ample relatively to $S$ (\sref[II]{II.4.6.1}), the $\sh{O}_X$-Module $f^*(\mathscr{L})$ is ample relatively to $S$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, by virtue of \sref{IV.18.12.12}, the morphism $f$ is quasi-affine, hence (\sref[II]{II.5.1.6}) the $\sh{O}_X$-Module $\sh{O}_X$ is ample for $f$.
One deduces (\sref[II]{II.4.6.13}[(iii)]) that $\sh{O}_X\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}f^*(\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n})=f^*(\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n})=f^*(\mathscr{L})^{\otimes n}$ is ample relatively to $S$ for $n$ large enough.
Since $f^*(\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n})=(f^*(\mathscr{L}))^{\otimes n}$, this implies that $f^*(\mathscr{L})$ is ample relatively to $S$ (\sref[II]{II.4.6.9}[(i)]).

\begin{corollary}[18.12.17]
\label{IV.18.12.17}
\oldpage[IV]{185}
Let $Z$ be an affine scheme, $h:X\to Z$ a morphism of finite type, $\mathscr{L}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-Module invertible.
With the notations of (\sref[II]{II.4.5.2}), the conditions of (\sref[II]{II.4.5.2}) (which is equivalent to saying that $\mathscr{L}$ is ample) are also equivalent to each of the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{\alph*'')}]
  \item $h$ is separated, one has $G(\varepsilon)=X$ and the canonical morphism $u:G(\varepsilon)\to\operatorname{Proj}(S)$ has finite and discrete fibres.
  \item One has $G(\varepsilon)=X$ and the morphism canonical $u$ is radiciel.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

If $Z=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$, the ring $S$ is canonically equipped with a structure of a graded A-algebra, and one has $h=g\circ u$ where $g:\operatorname{Proj}(S)\to Z$ is a separated morphism (\sref[II]{II.2.4.2}), and one has $h=g\circ u$.
As every radiciel morphism is separated \sref[I]{I.1.8.7.1}[b'']) implies that $h$ is separated, hence $b''$).
As the condition $b)$ of (\sref[II]{II.4.5.2}) obviously implies $b''$), it remains to show that $b'')$ implies that $\mathscr{L}$ is ample.
Since $h=g\circ u$ is of finite type by hypothesis and $g$ is separated, $u$ is also of finite type (\sref[I]{I.6.3.4}), hence the hypothesis $b'')$ implies that $u$ is quasi-finite and separated (\sref[I]{I.5.5.1}).
To show that $\mathscr{L}$ is ample, we will apply \sref{IV.18.12.16}.
Setting $Y=\operatorname{Proj}(S)$, since $X$ is quasi-compact, there exists an integer $n>0$ and a finite number of elements $f_j\in S_n$ such that the inverse images $u^{-1}(D_+(f_j))$ cover $X$; one can therefore consider $u$ as a morphism quasi-finite and separated from $X$ to the open $Y'=\bigcup D_+(f_j)$ of $Y$.
Consider then the $\sh{O}_X$-Module invertible $\mathscr{L}'=\sh{O}_Y(n)|Y'$ (\sref[II]{II.2.5.8}); it is very ample relatively to $Z$ (\sref[II]{II.4.4.3}), and $u^*(\mathscr{L}')$ is none other by definition than $\mathscr{L}^{\otimes n}$.
We leave to the reader the task of stating conditions equivalent to those of (\sref[II]{II.4.6.3}) for $\sh{O}_X$-Modules relatively ample.
