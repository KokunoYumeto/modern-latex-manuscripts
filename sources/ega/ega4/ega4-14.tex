\section{Universally open morphisms}
\label{section:IV.14}

\oldpage[IV-3]{199}

Sections~14 and~15 are devoted to the study of the notion of \emph{universally open} morphism (2.4.2).
We have already seen (2.4.6) that a \emph{flat} morphism locally of finite presentation is universally open, the converse being inexact.
In \textsection~14, we examine first the properties of the fibres of a universally open morphism $f:X\to Y$; when $X$ and $Y$ are locally Noetherian, $f$ behaves in this respect (14.2.1) like a flat morphism (cf.~6.1.2), and is in particular \emph{equidimensional} when it is locally of finite type and dominant and $X$ is irreducible (14.2.2).
Conversely, an equidimensional morphism locally of finite type $f:X\to Y$ is universally open when one supposes that $Y$ is \emph{geometrically unibranch}, and in particular when $Y$ is \emph{normal} (Chevalley criterion, 14.4.4).
We also show that universally open morphisms locally of finite type $f:X\to Y$ (when $X$ and $Y$ are locally Noetherian and $Y$ irreducible) admit ``quasi-sections'', i.e.\ in a neighbourhood of a closed point $x$ of a fibre $f^{-1}(y)$, there is a closed sub-prescheme $X'$ of $X$ containing $x$ such that the restriction $X'\to Y$ of $f$ is a morphism \emph{quasi-finite} (hence with discrete fibres) and dominant (14.5.3).

In \textsection~15 we study various properties of the fibres of universally open morphisms $f:X\to Y$, locally of finite type, notably when $X$ and $Y$ are locally Noetherian.
One thus obtains in particular a criterion for a point $x\in X$ to belong to a \emph{single} irreducible component of the \emph{fibre} $f^{-1}(f(x))$ containing $x$: it suffices that $Y$ be geometrically unibranch (for example normal) at $f(x)$ and that $f^{-1}(f(x))$ be geometrically punctually integral at the point $x$ (15.5.3); if moreover $Y$ is locally integral at $f(x)$, $f$ flat at $x$ and $X$ locally integral at $x$, we also show (15.5.7) that the number of \emph{geometric connected components} of a fibre $f^{-1}(y)$ is \emph{locally constant} (15.5.7).
Finally, when $f$ admits a \emph{section} $g$ (which will be the case in the context of \textsection~15.7), and the fibres of $f$ are geometrically reduced, then $X^0$ (the union of the $X^0_y$ for $y\in Y$, where $X^0_y$ designates the connected component of $X_y=f^{-1}(y)$ at the point $g(y)$ (``neutral component'' in the group case)) is studied, and one shows (15.6.4) that if $f$ is universally open and the fibres $X_y$ geometrically reduced, then $X^0$ is open in $X$.

\subsection{Open morphisms}
\label{subsection:IV.14.1}

\begin{env}[14.1.1]
\label{IV.14.1.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{200}
Recall (1.10.2) that a continuous map $\psi:X\to Y$ is said to be \emph{open at a point} $x\in X$ if the image by $\psi$ of \emph{every} neighbourhood of $x$ in $X$ is a neighbourhood of $\psi(x)$ in $Y$.
One notes that this does \emph{not imply} that there exists a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $x$ whose images are \emph{open} in $Y$.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[14.1.2]
\label{IV.14.1.2}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two topological spaces, $\psi:X\to Y$ a continuous map, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=\psi(x)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $\psi$ is open at $x$, then for every part $Y'$ of $Y$ containing $\psi(x)$ the restriction $\psi^{-1}(Y')\to Y'$ of $\psi$ is open at the point $x$.
  \item Suppose that $Y$ is a union of a locally finite family of closed parts $(Y_i)$ and that for every $i$ such that $\psi(x)\in Y_i$, the restriction $\psi^{-1}(Y_i)\to Y_i$ of $\psi$ is open at the point $x$; then $\psi$ is open at the point $x$.
  \item Let $\gamma:X'\to X$ be a continuous map, $x'$ a point of $X'$; if the composed map $\psi\circ\gamma:X'\to Y$ is open at the point $x'$, $\psi$ is open at the point $\gamma(x')$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remarks}[14.1.3]
\label{IV.14.1.3}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The set $Z$ of points $x\in X$ at which a morphism is open is not necessarily \emph{open}.
  \item Let $X$, $Y$ be two Noetherian irreducible preschemes of points generics $\xi$, $\eta$ respectively, $f:X\to Y$ a dominant morphism locally of finite type at the point $\xi$; then $f$ is \emph{open at the point} $\xi$.
  Indeed, one can evidently restrict to the case where $X$ and $Y$ are reduced (hence integral) and affine; by the generic flatness theorem (6.9.1), there exists a non-empty open $V$ of $Y$ such that the restriction $f^{-1}(V)\to V$ of $f$ is flat; it follows from (2.4.6) that this restriction is an open morphism.
  However, $f$ is not necessarily universally open (not even \emph{a fortiori} flat), as is shown by (11.7.5).
  \item We do not know if, when $X$ and $Y$ are locally Noetherian and $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, the set of points of $X$ where $f$ is open or not is \emph{locally constructible}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\begin{proposition}[14.1.4]
\label{IV.14.1.4}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two topological spaces, $\psi:X\to Y$ a continuous map.
For every $y\in Y$, the set of $x\in\psi^{-1}(y)$ where $\psi$ is open is a \emph{closed} part of $\psi^{-1}(y)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, suppose that $\psi$ is not open at a point $x\in\psi^{-1}(y)$; there exists then a neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ in $X$ such that $\psi(V)$ does not contain a neighbourhood of $y$; it follows that for every point $x'\in V\cap\psi^{-1}(y)$, $\psi$ is not open at the point $x'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[14.1.5]
\label{IV.14.1.5}
Even if $X$ and $Y$ are locally Noetherian and $f$ a finite morphism, it may happen that $f$ is open at all points of a fibre $f^{-1}(y)$, without there existing a \emph{neighbourhood} of $f^{-1}(y)$ in which $f$ is open.
\end{remarks}

\begin{env}[14.1.6]
\label{IV.14.1.6}
\oldpage[IV-3]{201}
In what follows, the essential role will be played by the criterion (1.10.3) characterising the morphisms locally of finite presentation $f:X\to Y$ that are open at a point $x$ by the ``lifting of generisations $y'$ of $y=f(x)$'': for every generisation $y'$ of $y=f(x)$, there exists $x'\in X$, generisation of $x$, such that $y'=f(x')$.
\end{env}

\subsection{Open morphisms and dimension of fibres}
\label{subsection:IV.14.2}

\begin{proposition}[14.2.1]
\label{IV.14.2.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{201}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type.
Let $y$ be a point of $Y$ and $x$ a point of $f^{-1}(y)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Suppose that $f$ is open at $x$.
  Then, for every irreducible component $Y_0$ of $Y$ containing $y$ with generic point $\eta$, there exists an irreducible component $Z$ of $X$ containing $x$, dominating $Y_0$ and such that $\dim_x(Z\cap X_y)=\dim(Z\cap X_\eta)$.
  \item Suppose $f$ universally open at $x$; then for every irreducible component $Y_0$ of $Y$ containing $y$, for every irreducible component $Z$ of $X$ containing $x$ and dominating $Y_0$, one has $\dim_x(Z\cap X_y)=\dim(Z\cap X_\eta)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[14.2.2]
\label{IV.14.2.2}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ an open morphism of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$, $x$ a maximal point of the fibre $X_y$.
Then there exists an irreducible component $Z$ of $X$ containing $x$ and equidimensional over $Y$ (13.2.2) at the point $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{env}[14.2.4]
\label{IV.14.2.4}
With the notations of \sref{IV.14.2.1}, define $E(y)=\{\dim(Z_i)~:~\text{$Z_i$ irreducible component of $f^{-1}(y)$}\}$ and $d(y)=\dim(f^{-1}(y))=\sup(E(y))$.
\end{env}

\begin{corollary}[14.2.5]
\label{IV.14.2.5}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.14.2.4}, suppose $f$ is a proper morphism and $Y$ is irreducible with generic point $\eta$.
Then $d(y)=d(\eta)$ for every $y\in Y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[14.2.6]
\label{IV.14.2.6}
\oldpage[IV-3]{203}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ an open morphism of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$.
Let $y_i$ ($1\leq i\leq n$) be the generic points of the irreducible components of $Y$ containing $y$.
Then, with the notations of \sref{IV.14.2.4}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If for $1\leq i\leq n$, one has $E(y)=E(y_i)$ (resp.\ $d(y)=d(y_i)$), the function $E$ (resp.\ $d$) is constant in a neighbourhood of $y$.
  \item There exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $f^{-1}(y)$ such that the function $z\mapsto\dim(U\cap f^{-1}(z))$ is constant in a neighbourhood of $y$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{remarks}[14.2.7]
\label{IV.14.2.7}
The example (13.2.12, (iii)) shows that under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.14.2.5} or \sref{IV.14.2.6}, (ii), one cannot, in the conclusion, replace the function $z\mapsto\dim(f^{-1}(z))$ by the function $z\mapsto E(z)$; indeed, in this example, $f$ is proper and flat (hence universally open (2.4.6)) and every neighbourhood of the unique point of $f^{-1}(y_0)$ contains the generic points of the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(\eta)$.
\end{remarks}

\subsection{Universally open morphisms}
\label{subsection:IV.14.3}

\begin{env}[14.3.1]
\label{IV.14.3.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{204}
Recall (2.4.2) that to say a morphism $f:X\to Y$ is universally open means that for every morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, $f_{(Y')}:X_{(Y')}\to Y'$ is open; it suffices moreover that this be so when $Y'=Y[T_1,\ldots,T_e]$, for every $e$ (8.10.2) (and if $Y$ is locally Noetherian, it suffices that it be so for every $Y'$ locally Noetherian).
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[14.3.2]
\label{IV.14.3.2}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes.
If $f$ is universally open, then, for every morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, where $Y'$ is irreducible, the image by $f'=f_{(Y')}:X_{(Y')}\to Y'$ of every irreducible component of $X_{(Y')}$ is dense in $Y'$.
Conversely, if this condition is verified for every irreducible $Y'$ and every morphism of finite type $g:Y'\to Y$, and if moreover $f$ is locally of finite presentation, then $f$ is universally open.
\end{proposition}

\begin{definition}[14.3.3]
\label{IV.14.3.3}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes, $x$ a point of $X$.
We say that $f$ is \emph{universally open at the point $x$} if, for every morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, setting $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, the morphism $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$ is open at every point $x'$ of $X'$ whose projection in $X$ is $x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remarks}[14.3.3.1]
\label{IV.14.3.3.1}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The reasoning of (8.10.1) shows (with the same notations) that if $x$ is a point of $X$, $x_\lambda$ its projection in $X_\lambda$, then, if $f_\mu$ is open at $x_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $f$ is open at $x$; it suffices thus to restrict to open sets $U$ of $X$ containing $x$ and to remark that the hypothesis implies that $f_\mu(U_\mu)$ is a neighbourhood of $f(x)$.
  One deduces that the statement (8.10.2) is still exact when one replaces ``universally open'' by ``universally open at the point $x$'', and ``open morphism'' by ``morphism open at every point $x_n$ of $X_n$ whose projection in $X$ is $x$'': it suffices in the proof to restrict to opens $V$ containing $x_n$.
  \item The result of \sref{IV.14.1.4} is still true for a morphism $f:X\to Y$, replacing ``open'' by ``universally open''.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\begin{proposition}[14.3.4]
\label{IV.14.3.4}
\oldpage[IV-3]{205}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Let $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ two morphisms, $x$ a point of $X$.
  If $f$ is universally open at $x$ and $g$ universally open at $f(x)$, then $g\circ f$ is universally open at $x$.
  Conversely, if $g\circ f$ is universally open at $x$, $g$ is universally open at $f(x)$.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$ is an $S$-morphism universally open at $x\in X$, then, for every base change $S'\to S$, $f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is universally open at every point of $X_{(S')}$ above $x$.
  \item For $f:X\to Y$ to be universally open at $x\in X$, it suffices that $f_\mathrm{red}$ be.
  \item Let $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite presentation, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$; set $Y_1=\Spec(\sh{O}_y)$, $X_1=X\times_Y Y_1$, $f_1=f_{(Y_1)}$.
  For $f$ to be universally open at $x$, it suffices that $f_1$ be (recall (\textbf{I}, 3.6.5) that $X_1$ is canonically identified with a sub-space of $X$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[14.3.5]
\label{IV.14.3.5}
Let $X$, $Y$ two preschemes, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism, $x$ a point of $X$.
Let $(Y_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ be a finite family of closed sub-preschemes of $Y$ such that $Y$ is a union of the $Y_i$, and suppose that for every $i$ such that $f(x)\in Y_i$, the restriction $f^{-1}(Y_i)\to Y_i$ of $f$ is universally open at $x$; then $f$ is universally open at $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[14.3.6]
\label{IV.14.3.6}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type.
For $f$ to be universally open at a point $x\in X$, it is necessary and sufficient that the following condition be verified: for every morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, where $Y'=\Spec(A)$ is the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring, such that the image $g(y')$ of the closed point $y'$ of $Y'$ is equal to $y=f(x)$, and for every point $x'\in X'=X\times_Y Y'$ whose projections on $X$ and $Y'$ are $x$ and $y'$ respectively, there exists a generisation $z'$ of $x'$ in $X'$ whose projection in $Y'$ is the generic point of $Y'$ (in other words, there exists an irreducible component of $X'$ containing $x'$ and dominating $Y'$).

Moreover, one can, in the preceding condition, restrict to the case where $A$ is \emph{complete}, with \emph{algebraically closed} residue field, and where $x'$ is \emph{rational} over $\kappa(y')$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[14.3.7]
\label{IV.14.3.7}
\oldpage[IV-3]{206}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.14.3.6}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Given a point $y\in Y$, for $f$ to be universally open at all points of $f^{-1}(y)$, it is necessary and sufficient that for every morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, where $Y'$ is the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring, such that the image $g(y')$ of the closed point $y'$ of $Y'$ is $y$, every irreducible component of $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ dominate $Y'$.
  \item For $f$ to be universally open, it is necessary and sufficient that for every morphism $g:Y'\to Y$, where $Y'$ is the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring, every irreducible component of $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ dominate $Y'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[14.3.8]
\label{IV.14.3.8}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, irreducible, regular and of dimension $1$ (for example the spectrum of a Dedekind ring), $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $f_\mathrm{red}$ is flat at every point of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item $f$ is universally open in a neighbourhood of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item $f$ is open in a neighbourhood of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item Every irreducible component of $X$ meeting $f^{-1}(y)$ dominates $Y$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remarks}[14.3.9]
\label{IV.14.3.9}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item In \sref{IV.14.3.8}, one cannot dispense from the hypothesis that $Y$ is \emph{regular}.
  \item The example of \sref{IV.14.1.3}, (i) shows that the set of points of $X$ where a morphism is universally open is not necessarily open.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\begin{lemma}[14.3.10]
\label{IV.14.3.10}
Let $A$ be a valuation ring (not necessarily discrete), $k$ its residue field, $K$ its field of fractions, and set $S=\Spec(A)$.
Let $X$ be a prescheme irreducible, $f:X\to S$ a dominant morphism of finite type; let $X_0=X\otimes_A k$ (resp.\ $X_1=X\otimes_A K$) be the fibre of $f$ at the closed point (resp.\ the generic point) of $S$.
Then, if $X_0\neq\varnothing$, one has $\dim(X_0)=\dim(X_1)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[14.3.11]
\label{IV.14.3.11}
\oldpage[IV-3]{208}
Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism of finite type, $(g_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ a family of universally open morphisms $g_\lambda:Y_\lambda\to S$, and for every $\lambda\in L$, let $u_\lambda:Y_\lambda\to X$ be an $S$-morphism.
For every $s\in S$, set $X_s=X\times_S\Spec(\kappa(s))$, $(Y_\lambda)_s=Y_\lambda\times_S\Spec(\kappa(s))$, and let $(u_\lambda)_s:(Y_\lambda)_s\to X_s$ be the morphism $u_\lambda\times 1$ deduced from $u_\lambda$ by base change.
Let $Z(s)$ be the adherence in $X_s$ of the union of the sets $(u_\lambda)_s((Y_\lambda)_s)$, and set $d(s)=\dim(Z(s))$.
Then, for every generisation $s'$ of a point $s\in S$, one has $d(s)\leq d(s')$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remarks}[14.3.12]
\label{IV.14.3.12}
The case considered by M.~Artin was that where $Y_\lambda=S$ for every $\lambda$, i.e.\ where $(u_\lambda)$ is a family of $S$-\emph{sections} of $X$.
Another useful case is that where the family $(u_\lambda)$ is reduced to a single element; one can always reduce to this case by considering the prescheme $Y$ which is the coproduct of the $Y_\lambda$ and the morphisms $g:Y\to S$ and $u:Y\to X$ whose restrictions to each $Y_\lambda$ are respectively $g_\lambda$ and $u_\lambda$.
\end{remarks}

\begin{proposition}[14.3.13]
\label{IV.14.3.13}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$, $x$ a maximal point of the fibre $X_y=X\times_Y\Spec(\kappa(y))$.
Consider the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $f$ is universally open at the point $x$ (or also at every point of the irreducible component of $X_y$ of generic point $x$ (14.3.3.1, (ii))).
  \item For every irreducible component $Y_0$ of $Y$ containing $y$, there exists an irreducible component $Z$ of $X$ containing $x$, dominant $Y_0$ and such that $\dim_x(Z\cap X_y)=\dim_x(Z\cap X_\eta)=\dim(Z\cap X_\eta)$, where $\eta$ is the generic point of $Y_0$ (which means that $Z$ is \emph{equidimensional} over $Y_0$ at the point $x$ (13.2.2)).
  \item[\emph{b')}] For every open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$ and every generisation $y'$ of $y$, one has $\dim(U\cap X_{y'})\geq\dim_x(U\cap X_y)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then one has the implications $a)\Rightarrow b)\Leftrightarrow b')$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remarks}[14.3.14]
\label{IV.14.3.14}
We do not know if in \sref{IV.14.3.13}, the conclusion subsists when one replaces hypothesis $a)$ by the weaker hypothesis that $f$ be \emph{open} at the point $x$.
\end{remarks}

\subsection{The Chevalley criterion for universally open morphisms}
\label{subsection:IV.14.4}

\begin{theorem}[14.4.1]
\label{IV.14.4.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{209}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$, $x$ a maximal point of the fibre $X_y=X\times_Y\Spec(\kappa(y))$.
Suppose $y$ \emph{geometrically unibranch}.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $f$ is universally open at the point $x$ (or also at every point of the irreducible component of $X_y$ of generic point $x$ (14.3.3.1, (ii))).
  \item If $Y_0$ is the unique irreducible component of $Y$ containing $y$, $\eta$ its generic point, there exists an irreducible component $Z$ of $X$ containing $x$, dominating $Y_0$ and such that $\dim_x(Z\cap X_y)=\dim(Z\cap X_\eta)$ (i.e.\ $Z$ is equidimensional over $Y_0$ at the point $x$ (13.2.2)).
  \item[\emph{b')}] For every open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$ and every generisation $y'$ of $y$, one has $\dim(U\cap X_{y'})\geq\dim_x(U\cap X_y)$.
\end{enumerate}
If moreover $Y$ is locally Noetherian, these conditions are also equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
  \item $f$ is open at the point $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[14.4.2]
\label{IV.14.4.2}
\oldpage[IV-3]{211}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point geometrically unibranch of $Y$, $\eta$ the generic point of the unique irreducible component $Y_0$ of $Y$ containing $y$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $f$ is universally open at all points of $X_y$ (or, which amounts to the same (14.3.3.1, (ii)), at the maximal points of $X_y$).
  \item For every $x\in X_y$, there exists an irreducible component $Z$ of $X$ containing $x$ and equidimensional over $Y$ at the point $x$ (13.2.2).
  \item[\emph{b')}] For every $x\in X_y$ and every open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$, one has $\dim(U\cap X_\eta)\geq\dim_x(U\cap X_y)$.
  \item[\emph{b'')}] For every open $U$ of $X$, one has $\dim(U\cap X_\eta)\geq\dim(U\cap X_y)$.
\end{enumerate}
When moreover $Y$ is locally Noetherian, these conditions are also equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
  \item $f$ is open at all points of $X_y$ (or, which amounts to the same (14.3.3.1, (ii)), at the maximal points of $X_y$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[14.4.3]
\label{IV.14.4.3}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme geometrically unibranch, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $f$ is universally open.
  \item For every open $U$ of $X$, every $y\in Y$ and every generisation $y'$ of $y$ one has $\dim(U\cap X_{y'})\geq\dim(U\cap X_y)$.
\end{enumerate}
If moreover $Y$ is locally Noetherian, these conditions are also equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
  \item $f$ is open.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[14.4.4]
\label{IV.14.4.4}
\textnormal{(Chevalley criterion).}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $f$ is equidimensional at a point $x\in X$ (13.3.2) and if $y=f(x)$ is a point geometrically unibranch of $Y$, $f$ is universally open at the point $x$.
  \item If $Y$ is geometrically unibranch, $f$ is universally open at all points of $X$ where $f$ is equidimensional, and the set of these points is open in $X$.
  In particular, if $f$ is equidimensional, it is universally open.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Assertion (ii) is a trivial consequence of (i), since the set of points where $f$ is equidimensional is already open (13.3.2).
As to assertion (i), it follows from the fact that condition $b)$ of \sref{IV.14.4.1} is verified at the generic point of an irreducible component of $X_y$ containing $x$, taking into account \sref{IV.13.3.1}; it thus suffices to apply \sref{IV.14.4.1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[14.4.5]
\label{IV.14.4.5}
One can prove that if $Y$ is locally Noetherian, and if all the generisations of $y=f(x)$ are geometrically unibranch points of $Y$ (cf.\ (6.15.2)), then, if $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$, it is universally open \emph{in a neighbourhood} of $x$.
\end{remarks}

\begin{corollary}[14.4.6]
\label{IV.14.4.6}
\oldpage[IV-3]{213}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type.
Let $y$ be a point geometrically unibranch of $Y$, and suppose that for every $x\in f^{-1}(y)$, the ring $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is equidimensional.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $f$ is equidimensional (13.3.2) at all points of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item $f$ is open at all points of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item $f$ is universally open at all points of $f^{-1}(y)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[14.4.7]
\label{IV.14.4.7}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, such that $y=f(x)$ is geometrically unibranch.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $f$ is equidimensional (13.3.2) at the point $x$.
  \item The ring $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is equidimensional and $f$ is open at the generic points of the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$ containing $x$ (hence also at every point of such a component).
  \item The ring $\sh{O}_{X,x}$ is equidimensional, and $f$ is universally open at the generic points of the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$ containing $x$ (hence also at every point of such a component).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[14.4.8]
\label{IV.14.4.8}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$, $x$ a point maximal of $X_y=f^{-1}(y)$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item The morphism $f$ is universally open at the point $x$, i.e.\ for every base change $g:Y'\to Y$, the property $\mathbf{P}(Y')$ holds: for every point $x'$ of $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ above $x$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$ is open at $x'$.
  \item[\emph{a')}] The property $\mathbf{P}(Y')$ is true for every finite morphism $g:Y'\to Y$.
  \item[\emph{a'')}] The property $\mathbf{P}(Y'')$ is true for the normalised $Y''$ of $Y_\mathrm{red}$ (\textbf{II}, 6.3.8).
  \item For every point $x''$ of $X''=X\times_Y Y''$ above $x$, there exists an irreducible component $Z''$ of $X''$ containing $x''$ and equidimensional over $Y''$ at the point $x''$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[14.4.8.1]
\label{IV.14.4.8.1}
Let $v:Y_1\to Y$ be a morphism closed (resp.\ universally closed) and surjective.
For a morphism $f:X\to Y$ to be open (resp.\ universally open) at a point $x\in X$, it suffices that $f_1=f_{(Y_1)}:X_1=X\times_Y Y_1\to Y_1$ be open (resp.\ universally open) at all points of $X_1$ above $x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[14.4.9]
\label{IV.14.4.9}
\oldpage[IV-3]{215}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.14.4.8}, suppose moreover that $Y$ is \emph{excellent}.
Then the equivalent conditions of \sref{IV.14.4.8} are also equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*')]
  \item The property $\mathbf{P}(Y')$ is true for any faithfully flat morphism $g:Y'\to Y$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, $f$ is universally open at $x$ if and only if $f$ is open at the maximal points of $X_y$ above $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remarks}[14.4.10]
\label{IV.14.4.10}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item In \sref{IV.12.1.8}, (ii), we claimed that for $\sh{F}=\sh{O}_X$ (so $f$ flat), one cannot weaken the condition ``$f$ is flat'' to ``$f$ is universally open'' in (12.1.1.5).
  Here is the example: take $A_0=k[S,T]$, $\mathfrak{a}=(T_1)+(T_2-T_3)$, $X=\Spec(A/\mathfrak{a})$ with two irreducible components, $Y=X_1=\Spec(A/\mathfrak{b})$ for suitable $\mathfrak{b}$.
  \item With the same notations, this is also an example where $f$ is proper, equidimensional and flat, but $E(y)$ has two elements for $y\neq y_0$, and the fibre $X_{y'}$ at the generic point has two irreducible components of dimensions $0$ and $1$, whereas $X_{y_0}$ has a single irreducible component of dimension $1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\subsection{Quasi-sections of flat morphisms of finite type}
\label{subsection:IV.14.5}

\begin{env}[14.5.1]
\label{IV.14.5.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{216}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$, $x$ a \emph{closed} point of $X_y=f^{-1}(y)$.
By a \emph{quasi-section} of $f$ at the point $x$ (relative to $Y$, or to $y$), we shall mean a closed sub-prescheme $X'$ of an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$, such that $x\in X'$ and that the restriction $f|X':X'\to Y$ is a morphism \emph{quasi-finite} and \emph{dominant}.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[14.5.2]
\label{IV.14.5.2}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, irreducible of generic point $\eta$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$, $x$ a closed point of $X_y$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item For there to exist a quasi-section of $f$ at $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist an irreducible component $Z$ of $X$ containing $x$ and dominating $Y$.
  \item When $f$ is universally open at $x$ (in particular when $f$ is flat at $x$), such a quasi-section always exists.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{217}
We can restrict to the case where $X=U$ is an affine open neighbourhood of $x$ such that all the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$ contain $x$.
These components correspond to the minimal prime ideals of $\sh{O}_x/\mathfrak{m}_y\sh{O}_x$, and by hypothesis these ideals are distinct from $\mathfrak{m}_x/\mathfrak{m}_y\sh{O}_x$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.~comm.}, chap.~II, \textsection1, n\textsuperscript{o}~1, prop.~2); to obtain a $g\in\Gamma(U,\sh{O}_X)$ satisfying the conditions of the statement, it suffices to take $g\in\mathfrak{I}_x$ such that the image of $g$ in $\mathfrak{m}_x$ does not belong to any of the preceding prime ideals.
Moreover, we have $\codim(X',X)\leq 1$ \sref{IV.5.1.8}, and since $X'$ contains none of the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$ and these are of dimension $e$, we have ($\mathbf{0}$, 14.2.2.2) $\dim_x(X'\cap f^{-1}(y))\leq e-1$.
It then suffices to apply \sref{IV.14.5.1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[14.5.3]
\label{IV.14.5.3}
Let $Y$ be an irreducible prescheme, locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$.
Suppose that $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$ and that $x$ is closed in $f^{-1}(f(x))$.
Then there exists an irreducible closed part $X'$ of $X$, locally closed in $X$, containing $x$, and such that the restriction $X'\to Y$ of $f$ \emph{(}where $X'$ denotes the reduced subprescheme of $X$ having $X'$ as underlying space\emph{)} is a quasi-finite and dominant morphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, with the notations of \sref{IV.14.5.2}, the hypothesis that $x$ is closed in $f^{-1}(y)$ implies that $x$ is not a maximal point of $X'\cap f^{-1}(y)$ as long as $e-1\geq 1$.
It suffices to apply \sref{IV.14.5.2} by descending induction on $e=\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))$ until one arrives at $e=1$; applying \sref{IV.14.5.2} in this last case gives an $X'$ such that $X'\cap f^{-1}(f(x))$ is of dimension $0$, hence finite and discrete; since $X'$ is equidimensional over $Y$, $X'\to Y$ de $f$ is the reduced subprescheme of $X$ having $X'$ as underlying space, which is quasi-finite and dominant.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[14.5.4]
\label{IV.14.5.4}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.14.5.3}, suppose further that $Y=\Spec(A)$, where $A$ is a Noetherian local integral ring, complete, and that $y=f(x)$ is the unique closed point of $Y$.
Then there exists an integral local ring $A'$, containing $A$, which is a finite $A$-algebra and has the following property: if one sets $Y'=\Spec(A')$ and $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, there exists a $Y'$-section $h:Y'\to X'$ such that the composite morphism $Y'\xrightarrow{h}X'\to X$ is an immersion whose image contains $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Replacing if necessary $X$ by an irreducible reduced subprescheme of $X$, we can, by virtue of \sref{IV.14.5.3}, restrict to the case where the morphism $f$ is already quasi-finite and dominant.
Using (\textbf{II}, 6.2.5), we deduce that $\sh{O}_x=A'$ is an integral ring which is a finite $A$-algebra, and that $X$ is the disjoint sum of the closed subprescheme $Y'=\Spec(A')$ and of a subprescheme $Y''$; the prescheme $Y'$ answers the question, the composite morphism $Y'\to X'\to X$ being none other than the canonical morphism $\Spec(\sh{O}_x)\to X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[14.5.5]
\label{IV.14.5.5}
\textbf{Remark.}
If, in the statement of \sref{IV.14.5.4}, one does not require that the morphism $Y'\to X$ be an immersion, one can suppose that $A'$ is moreover \emph{integrally closed}: it suffices to replace $A'$ by its \emph{integral closure} $A_1$, since one knows ($\mathbf{0}$, 23.1.5) that $A_1$ is an $A$-module of finite type.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[14.5.6]
\label{IV.14.5.6}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, irreducible, regular, and of dimension $1$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $f_{\mathrm{red}}$ is flat at every point of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item[b)] $f$ is universally open in a neighbourhood of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item[c)] $f$ is open in a neighbourhood of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item[d)] Every irreducible component of $X$ meeting $f^{-1}(y)$ dominates $Y$.
  \item[e)] For every point $x\in f^{-1}(y)$, closed in $f^{-1}(y)$, and every irreducible component $X_i$ of $X$ containing $x$, there exists an irreducible closed part $X'$ of $X$, locally closed in $X$, containing $x$, contained in $X_i$, and such that the restriction $X'\to Y$ of $f$ is a quasi-finite and dominant morphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\oldpage[IV-3]{218}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence of \emph{a)}, \emph{b)}, \emph{c)}, and \emph{d)} has already been proved \sref{IV.14.3.8}.
It is clear that \emph{e)} implies \emph{d)}.
Finally, to prove that \emph{c)} implies \emph{e)}, we can restrict to a morphism of finite type; consider a closed point $x$ of $f^{-1}(y)$ and let $X_i$ be an irreducible component of $X$ containing $x$.
Since the restriction $f_i:X_i\to Y$ of $f$ to $X_i$ is a dominant morphism, it follows from \sref{IV.14.2.2} that $f_i$ is open at the generic points of $X_i\cap f^{-1}(y)$, hence $f_i$ is equidimensional at the point $x$, and one concludes with the aid of \sref{IV.14.5.3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[14.5.7]
\label{IV.14.5.7}
\textbf{Remark.}
If, in the statement of \sref{IV.14.5.5}, one supposes that $Y=\Spec(A)$, where $A$ is a discrete valuation ring, \emph{complete}, and that $y$ is the closed point of $Y$, one can moreover suppose that $X'=\Spec(A')$, where $A'$ is a discrete valuation ring which is a finite $A$-algebra, as the proof shows from \sref{IV.14.5.4} and the fact that a local integral ring, regular and of dimension $1$, is a discrete valuation ring (\textbf{II}, 7.1.6).
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[14.5.8]
\label{IV.14.5.8}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$.
For every $Y$-prescheme $Y'$, set $X(Y')=\Hom_Y(Y',X)$; let $x$ be a point of $f^{-1}(y)$; in order that $f$ be universally open at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that the following condition be satisfied:

For every discrete valuation ring $A$, complete, of algebraically closed residue field, every morphism $g:Y'=\Spec(A)\to Y$ such that the image by $g$ of the closed point $y'$ of $Y'$ is equal to $y$, and every element $u_0\in X(\Spec(k(y')))$ such that $u_0(y')=x$, there exists a discrete valuation ring $B$, a local $A$-homomorphism $A\to B$ making $B$ a finite $A$-algebra, and, if one sets $Z'=\Spec(B)$, an element $u\in X(Z')$ such that, if $z'$ is the closed point of $Z'$, the diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  \Spec(k(z')) \ar[r] \ar[d] & Z' \ar[d]^{u} \\
  \Spec(k(y')) \ar[r]^-{u_0} & X
}
\]
is commutative.
\end{proposition}

\oldpage[IV-3]{219}

\begin{proof}
Note that if $A$ and $B$ satisfy the conditions of the statement, $B$ is a complete discrete valuation ring (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.~comm.}, chap.~III, \textsection3, n\textsuperscript{o}~3, prop.~7, and chap.~IV, \textsection2, n\textsuperscript{o}~2, cor.~3 of prop.~9) of residue field isomorphic to that of $A$, hence algebraically closed; the condition of the statement is therefore \emph{necessary}.
Let us now prove that the condition is \emph{sufficient}.
Set $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$.
By hypothesis, there exists a point $x'\in X'$ above $x$ and $y'$ that is rational over $k(y')$; moreover, since $u(z')=x''$, there is in $X''=X\times_Y Z'$ a point $x''$ above $x'$ and the closed point $z'$ of $Z'$; the projections in $X$ and $Z'$ are $x$ and $z'$ respectively, and if $T'$ is the underlying space of $Z'$, the morphism composed $Z'\xrightarrow{v}X''\to X$ shows that $x'$ lies in the closure of the image.
By virtue of \sref{IV.14.3.6}, the condition of the statement implies that $\overline{T}=g^{-1}(T')$ for all closed parts $T'$ of $X_{(K')}$; applying \sref{IV.14.4.1} to the quasi-finite and dominant morphism $f_i$, one sees that $f_i$ is universally open; since $f$ is open at all the points of $X_i\cap f^{-1}(y)$ and since the closed points of $X_y$ are dense in $X_y$ \sref{IV.10.3.1}, it follows from \sref{IV.14.1.4} that $f$ is universally open at the point $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[14.5.9]
\label{IV.14.5.9}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian irreducible prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $y$ a point of $Y$ that is geometrically unibranch.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $f$ is universally open at every point of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item[b)] $f$ is open at every point of $f^{-1}(y)$.
  \item[c)] For every irreducible component $Z$ of $f^{-1}(y)$, with generic point $z$, there exists an irreducible component $X_i$ of $X$ containing $z$ and equidimensional over $Y$ at the point $z$.
  \item[d)] For every closed point $x$ of $f^{-1}(y)$, there exists an irreducible closed part $X'$ of $X$, locally closed in $X$, containing $x$, and such that the restriction $X'\to Y$ of $f$ is a quasi-finite and dominant morphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence of \emph{a)}, \emph{b)}, and \emph{c)} has already been proved \sref{IV.14.4.2}.
To prove that \emph{a)} implies \emph{d)}, note that by virtue of \sref{IV.14.4.2}, \emph{a)} implies that there exists an irreducible component containing $x$ and equidimensional on $Y$ at the point $x$; the existence of $X'$ then follows from \sref{IV.14.5.3}.
Inversely, suppose \emph{d)} satisfied; by virtue of the Chevalley criterion \sref{IV.14.4.4}, the restriction $X'\to Y$ of $f$ is universally open at the point $x$, and \emph{a fortiori} $f$ is open at the point $x$.
Hence $f$ is open at all the closed points of $X_y=f^{-1}(y)$.
But $X_y$ is a $k(y)$-prescheme locally of finite type, hence a Jacobson prescheme; the set of closed points of $X_y$ is therefore dense in $X_y$ \sref{IV.10.3.1}, and it follows from \sref{IV.14.1.4} that $f$ is universally open at every point of $f^{-1}(y)$, which proves \emph{d)} implies \emph{b)}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[14.5.10]
\label{IV.14.5.10}
Let $Y$ be a Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism universally open, surjective, and locally of finite type.
Then there exists a finite surjective morphism $g:Y'\to Y$ such that, if one sets $X'=X\times_Y Y'$ and $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$, every point $y'\in Y'$ admits a neighbourhood $U'$ such that there exists a $U'$-section of $f'^{-1}(U')$.
\end{proposition}

\oldpage[IV-3]{220}

\begin{proof}
We will prove the proposition in several steps.

\emph{I) Reduction to the case where $Y$ is integral.}
If one has proved the proposition for each of the reduced subpreschemes $Y_i$ having for underlying space an irreducible component of $Y$, and for the inverse images $f^{-1}(Y_i)$, it is clear that the prescheme $Y'$ which is the \emph{sum} of the corresponding $Y'_i$ answers the question.
We can therefore suppose $Y$ \emph{integral}.
Then, in the conclusion, we can also take $Y'$ integral (it suffices to replace it by an irreducible component suitable).

\emph{II) Local character on $Y$.}
We will now show that if one can cover $Y$ by finitely many open sets $U_j$ such that, for every $j$, the conclusion of the proposition is true for the morphism $f^{-1}(U_j)\to U_j$, the conclusion is also true for $f$.
Indeed, one can evidently suppose $U_j$ affine, so that $U_j=\Spec(A_j)$, where $A_j$ is an integral Noetherian ring whose field of fractions $K=\mathrm{R}(Y)$ is the field of rational functions on $Y$.
For every $j$, by hypothesis there is a finite integral $A_j$-algebra $A'_j$ such that the morphism $g_j:U'_j=\Spec(A'_j)\to\Spec(A_j)=U_j$ is injective and that the morphism corresponding $g_j:U'_j=\Spec(A'_j)\to\Spec(A_j)=U_j$ satisfies the conditions of the proposition for $U_j$ and $f^{-1}(U_j)$.
Let then $K'$ be a finite extension of $K$ containing the field of fractions of all the $A'_j$ (which are finite extensions of $K$).
Consider the \emph{normalisé} $Y''$ of $Y$ in $K'$ (\textbf{II}, 6.3.8), which is of the form $\Spec(\sh{B})$, where $\sh{B}$ is an $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra coherent of $\sh{B}|U_j$.
These definitions show that for every $j$, $A'_j=\Gamma(U_j,\sh{O}_{U_j})=\Gamma(U'_j,\sh{O}_{U'_j})$ identifies with a sub-$A_j$-algebra finite of $\Gamma(U_j,\sh{B})$; in other words, $(g_j)_*(\sh{O}_{U'_j})$ is a \emph{sub}-$\sh{O}_{U_j}$-Algebra coherent of $\sh{B}|U_j$.
There exists therefore a sub-$\sh{O}_Y$-Module coherent $\sh{C}'_j$ of $\sh{B}$ such that $\sh{C}'_j|U_j=\sh{A}'_j$ \sref[I]{I.9.4.7}.
If one sets $\sh{C}'=\sum_j\sh{C}'_j$, the sub-$\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra $\sh{B}'$ of $\sh{B}$ generated by $\sh{C}'$ is a \emph{coherent} $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra.
Let us then show that $Y'=\Spec(\sh{B}')$ answers the question.
Indeed, it is clear that the morphism $g:Y'\to Y$ is finite surjective and that $Y'$ is integral; moreover, for every $j$, $\Gamma(U_j,\sh{B}')$ is a sub-$A_j$-algebra finite, and since the restriction of $g$ to $g^{-1}(U_j)\to U_j$ factors as $g^{-1}(U_j)\to U'_j\to U_j$, and since the existence of local sections is stable under base change, this establishes our assertion for every $y\in Y$ belonging to some $U_j$.

\emph{III) Reduction to the case where $Y$ is integral, local and geometrically unibranch.}
Suppose first that the proposition has been proved when $Y$ is integral and \emph{local} (with $Y'$ integral), and let us show that it is valid when $Y$ is integral (noetherian) affine of arbitrary dimension.
Indeed, by virtue of reduction II), it suffices to prove for every point $y\in Y$ that there exists an affine open $V$ of $y$ in $Y$ for which the proposition holds.
Let $Y_1=\Spec(A_\mathfrak{p})$, where $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{i}_y$; by hypothesis, there exists a finite morphism $g_1:Y'_1\to Y_1$, surjective, with $Y'_1=\Spec(B_1)$, $B_1$ being a finite $A_\mathfrak{p}$-algebra integral.
The $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module $B_1$ admits a finite system of generators of the form $z_i/s$ (with $s\in A-\mathfrak{p}$, $z_i$ integer over $A_s$), which one can, by multiplying by a suitable element of $A$, take to be elements of the field of fractions of $B_1$ integer over $A_s$; it follows that if $V$ is the affine open $\mathrm{D}(s)\subset Y$, $Y'_1$ identifies with $Y_1\times_Y V'$, where $V'$ is the affine open $V$ satisfies the conditions of the proposition.
Reasoning as at the start of reduction III), we can therefore suppose $A$ local, integral and geometrically unibranch.

\emph{IV) Reduction to the case where $X$ is integral, affine, $f$ quasi-finite, surjective, birational, and universally open.}
Suppose therefore $Y=\Spec(A)$ integral, local and geometrically unibranch.
There exists then an irreducible subprescheme $X_0$ of $X$ such that the restriction $f_0:X_0\to Y$ of $f$ is a quasi-finite and dominant morphism and that $f_0(X_0)$ contains the closed point $y$ of $Y$ \sref{IV.14.5.9}; since $Y$ is geometrically unibranch, it follows from \sref{IV.14.4.1} that $f_0$ is still \emph{universally open}.
Since moreover one can suppose $X_0$ reduced, hence integral, and on the other hand, we can, by replacing $X$ by $X_0$, suppose that $X$ is \emph{integral} and $f$ \emph{quasi-finite and dominant}; since $f$ is open and surjective, every open of $Y$ containing $y$ is equal to $Y$, $f$ is \emph{surjective}.

\oldpage[IV-3]{221}

Let $\xi$, $\eta$ be the generic points of $X$ and $Y$ respectively; $k(\xi)$ is therefore a finite extension of $K=k(\eta)$.
By \sref{IV.4.6.8}, there is a finite extension $K'$ of $K$ such that $(\Spec(k(\xi)\otimes_K K'))_{\mathrm{red}}$ is geometrically reduced over $K$ and that its irreducible components are geometrically irreducible; it follows ((\sref{IV.4.5.9}) and \sref{IV.4.6.1})) that the residue fields of $\Spec(k(\xi)\otimes_K K')$ are finite extensions, primary and separable, of $K'$, hence are \emph{equal} to $K'$.
Applying again ($\mathbf{0}$, 23.2.5) and \sref{IV.6.15.5}), there exists a finite $A$-algebra integral $B$, having $K'$ as field of fractions, containing $A$ and such that $\Spec(B)$ is geometrically unibranch; since the morphism $\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)$ is surjective, one can evidently replace $Y$ by $\Spec(B)$ and $X$ by $X\otimes_A B$ to prove the proposition.
Reasoning as at the beginning of the reduction III), one can therefore suppose $A$ local, integral and $Y=\Spec(A)$ geometrically unibranch, closed point $y$.
Moreover, $f$ is then a quasi-finite morphism, \emph{surjective} and \emph{universally open}; each irreducible component $X_i$ of $X$ ($1\leq i\leq m$) dominates $Y$ \sref{IV.1.10.4} and is \emph{birational} over $Y$.
Let then $x$ be a point of $f^{-1}(y)$ and let $X_i$ be an irreducible component of $X$ containing $x$, and let $X_i$ be the reduced subprescheme (hence integral) of $X$ having $X_i$ as underlying space, and let $f_i:X_i\to Y$ be the restriction of $f$.
Applying again \sref{IV.14.4.1} to the quasi-finite and dominant morphism $f_i$, one sees that $f_i$ is universally open; if $U$ is an affine open of $X_i$ containing $x$, $f_i(U)$ is therefore an open of $Y$ containing $x$.

\oldpage[IV-3]{222}

\emph{V) End of the proof.}
Suppose therefore $Y$ local, integral and geometrically unibranch, $X$ integral and affine, $f$ quasi-finite, surjective, birational and universally open; it follows that $f$ is automatically \emph{separated}.
By virtue of the Main Theorem \sref{IV.8.12.6}, there exists a factorisation $X\xrightarrow{j}Z\xrightarrow{u}Y$, where $j$ is an open immersion and $u$ a finite morphism.
Replacing moreover $Z$ by the closed image of $j$ \sref[I]{I.9.5.10}, one can suppose that $Z$ is \emph{integral}; since $u$ is finite and birational and $Y$ geometrically unibranch, it follows from (\textbf{III}, 4.3.5 and 4.3.4) that $u$ (and hence $f$) is a \emph{universally radiciel homeomorphism}.
By \sref{IV.2.4.5}[, (ii)] $f$ is a finite morphism.
But then one responds to the conditions of the statement of $Y'$ \emph{integral} by simply taking $Y'=X$ and $g=f$, the $Y'$-section being the diagonal morphism $\Delta_f$.
Q.E.D.
\end{proof}

This result can be used to develop criteria of ``descent'' for the properties of universally open and surjective morphisms.
We mention in particular the following criterion, due to D.~Mumford:

\begin{corollary}[14.5.11]
\label{IV.14.5.11}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $g:Y'\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, universally open and surjective; set $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$.
Then, for $f$ to be affine, it is necessary and sufficient that $f'$ be so.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
One need only prove that the condition is sufficient.
The question being local on $Y$, we can suppose $Y$ affine, hence Noetherian.
By virtue of \sref{IV.14.5.10}, there exists a finite surjective morphism $h:Y_1\to Y$ such that, if one sets $Y'_1=Y'\times_Y Y_1$ and $g'=g_{(Y_1)}:Y'_1\to Y_1$, every point of $Y_1$ admits a neighbourhood $U_1$ such that there exists a $U_1$-section of $g'^{-1}(U_1)$.
If one sets $X_1=X\times_Y Y_1$, the projection $p:X_1\to X$ is a finite surjective morphism; hence, if one proves that $X_1$ is a scheme, then $X_1$ is affine if and only if $X$ is affine, by the theorem of Chevalley (\textbf{II}, 6.7.1).
It then suffices to prove that the morphism $f_1=f_{(Y_1)}:X_1\to Y_1$ is affine.
Now, if one sets $f'_1=(f_1)_{(Y'_1)}:X'_1\to Y'_1$, $f'_1$ is affine by virtue of the hypothesis.
The question being local on $Y_1$, it suffices to prove that $f$ is affine when $Y$ admits a $Y$-section $s$ of $Y'$.
Now, by the lemma:
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[14.5.11.1]
\label{IV.14.5.11.1}
Let $f:X\to S$, $g:Y\to S$ be two morphisms, $p_1:X\times_S Y\to X$, $p_2:X\times_S Y\to Y$ the canonical projections.
If $s:S\to Y$ is a section of $g$, then $s'=(1,s\circ f)_S$ is a section of the morphisms $f:X\to S$ and $X$, equipped with the morphisms $f$ and $s':X\to X\times_S Y$, identifies with the product of the $Y$-preschemes $S$ and $X\times_S Y$ for the morphisms $s:S\to Y$ and $p_2:X\times_S Y\to Y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is a particular case of \sref[I]{I.3.3.11}, where one replaces the diagram by
\[
\xymatrix{
  X \ar[r]^-{p_1} \ar[d]_f & X\times_S Y \ar[r]^-{s'} \ar[d]^{p_2} & X \ar[d]^f \\
  S \ar[r]_-{s} & Y \ar[r]_-{g} & S
}
\]
\end{proof}

Applying this lemma with $S$, $Y$ replaced by $Y$, $Y'$, one sees that one can write $f=(f')_{(Y)}$ for the base change $s:Y\to Y'$, hence $f$ is affine since $f'$ is.
Q.E.D.

A variant of this criterion is the following:

\begin{corollary}[14.5.12]
\label{IV.14.5.12}
\oldpage[IV-3]{223}
With the notations and general hypotheses of \sref{IV.14.5.11}, let $\sh{L}$ be an $\sh{O}_X$-Module inversible.
Suppose that there exists a closed part $Z$ of $X$, proper over $Y$ (\textbf{II}, 5.4.10) and such that the prescheme induced by $X$ on $X-Z$ is affine; it then suffices that $\sh{L}'=\sh{L}\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}\sh{O}_{Y'}$ be ample relative to $f'$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Keeping the notations of the proof of \sref{IV.14.5.11}, set $\sh{L}_1=\sh{L}\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}\sh{O}_{Y_1}$; it suffices to prove that $\sh{L}_1$ is ample relative to $f_1$, and one can again suppose that $g'$ admits a section $s$.
If $\sh{L}'_1=\sh{L}'\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y'}}\sh{O}_{Y'_1}$, one can then write, by virtue of the lemma \sref{IV.14.5.11.1}, $\sh{L}_1=\sh{L}'_1\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y'}}\sh{O}_{Y_1}$ for the base change $s:Y_1\to Y'_1$; the conclusion follows therefore from two applications of (\textbf{II}, 4.6.13, (iii)).
\end{proof}
