\section{Topological properties of finitely presented flat morphisms. Flatness criteria}
\label{section:IV.11}

\oldpage[IV-3]{116}

Recall that in \textsection\,2 we considered results concerning flatness that do not depend on any finiteness hypothesis, and that \textsection\,6 studies the notion of flatness in the context of locally Noetherian preschemes (but without finiteness hypothesis on morphisms); the present paragraph is devoted to the notion of $f$-flatness in the case where the morphism $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{locally of finite presentation}.
The interest of the notion of flat morphism of finite presentation lies in the fact that it is this notion which seems to express in the most technically adequate way the intuitive notion of a ``family of algebraic preschemes parametrized by a scheme $Y$'', the study of which is one of the principal objects of Algebraic Geometry.
Moreover, even if one were only interested at the outset in the case of a Noetherian base scheme, it is indispensable, for certain technical reasons (for example for certain applications of the theory of ``descent'', which leads to introducing non-Noetherian schemes), not to limit oneself to this case, as soon as it is a question of problems essentially related to morphisms locally of finite presentation.
We follow this principle systematically, already supported by the results of \textsection\textsection\,8 and~9, throughout the rest of this Chapter, and even in the rest of our Treatise, at the cost of occasionally sacrificing the simplicity of certain proofs, that Noetherian hypotheses sometimes allow to simplify.
In the present paragraph, this principle leads us to take up again, in the context of ``finite presentation'' (notably in n\textsuperscript{o}~3), certain results of flatness, already obtained in the Noetherian context.
The essential technical tool for the reduction to the Noetherian case is the theorem of compatibility of flatness with projective limits of preschemes \sref{IV.11.2.6}, complementing the general results of \textsection\,8.
We also prove in passing \sref{IV.11.3.1} a result often used
\oldpage[IV-3]{117}
in the sequel, implying that the set of points of flatness of a morphism locally of finite presentation is open.

In n\textsuperscript{os}~4 to~8, we study the question of the ``descent'' of flatness, consisting of finding useful conditions on a base change morphism $Y'\to Y$ (not flat in general) to be able to conclude that if $X\times_Y Y'$ is flat over $Y'$, then $X$ is flat over $Y$.
These results, more technical than those of n\textsuperscript{os}~1 to~3, are of less frequent use in the sequel; they will however play an important role in certain non-projective construction techniques, in the following chapter.
The only result from n\textsuperscript{os}~4 to~8 used in the rest of chap.~IV is the valuative criterion of flatness \sref{IV.11.8}, which will be applied in \sref{IV.15.2}.

Finally, n\textsuperscript{os}~9 and~10 are devoted to the study of the notion that refines, in scheme theory, the notion of density in the topological sense, namely the notion of a family of sub-preschemes \emph{schematically dense} in a given prescheme, and in particular the study of the behaviour of this notion under (flat or arbitrary) base change.
This notion is mainly used, for the moment, in the study of group schemes.

\subsection{Flatness loci (Noetherian case)}
\label{subsection:IV.11.1}

\begin{theorem}[11.1.1]
\label{IV.11.1.1}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$ is open in $X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The question being evidently local on $X$ and $Y$, we can suppose $Y=\Spec(A)$, $X=\Spec(B)$, $A$ being Noetherian and $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite type.
One has then $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$, where $M$ is a $B$-module of finite type.
Let us apply the criterion $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.2.6): it suffices therefore to prove the following assertion:
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[11.1.1.1]
\label{IV.11.1.1.1}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite type, $M$ a $B$-module of finite type, $\mathfrak{q}$ a prime ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{p}$ its inverse image in $A$.
Suppose that $M_\mathfrak{q}$ is an $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module flat.
Then there exists $g\in B-\mathfrak{q}$ such that, for every prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}'\supset\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$ such that $g\notin\mathfrak{q}'$, $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ is an $A_{\mathfrak{p}'}$-module flat, denoting by $\mathfrak{p}'$ the inverse image of $\mathfrak{q}'$ in $A$ (which amounts to saying $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.1) that $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ is an $A$-module flat).
\end{env}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \sref{IV.11.1.1.1}]
\oldpage[IV-3]{118}
Consider $B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ as an $A$-algebra; evidently $\mathfrak{p}B_{\mathfrak{q}'}\subset\mathfrak{q}'B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$.
We know then $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.2) that for $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ to be an $A$-module flat, it is necessary and sufficient that $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}/\mathfrak{p}M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ be an $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-module flat and that $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_{\mathfrak{q}'},A/\mathfrak{p})=0$.
Now, we have $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}=M\otimes_B B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$; $B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ being flat over $B$, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_{\mathfrak{q}'},A/\mathfrak{p})=(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_{\mathfrak{q}'}=(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ ($M/\mathfrak{p}M$ being considered in the second member as an $A$-module); moreover $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h$ being an $A$-module of finite type for a suitable $h$, $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h=0$ if and only if there is a suitable element $g$ and $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h=0$ is equivalent to $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{\mathfrak{q}'}=0$.
Since $A$ and $B$ are Noetherian, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p})$ is a $B$-module of finite type, hence $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 5.2.2) there exists $g\in B-\mathfrak{q}$ such that $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_g=0$.
Moreover, $M/\mathfrak{p}M$ is a $B/\mathfrak{p}B$-module of finite type, and it suffices to apply $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.2).
On the other hand, using the theorem of generic flatness \sref{IV.6.9.1} applied to the integral ring $A/\mathfrak{p}$, the $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-algebra of finite type $B/\mathfrak{p}B$, and the $(B/\mathfrak{p}B)$-module of finite type $M/\mathfrak{p}M$, there exists $h\in A-\mathfrak{p}$ such that, if $\overline{h}$ is its canonical image in $A/\mathfrak{p}$, $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{\overline{h}}$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-module flat.
On the other hand, as $M_\mathfrak{q}$ is an $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module flat, and hence an $A$-module flat $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.1), we also have $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_\mathfrak{q},A/\mathfrak{p})=0$, which writes $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_\mathfrak{q}=0$.
Moreover, on has $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}/\mathfrak{p}M_{\mathfrak{q}'}=(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h$ being considered as $B$-modules;
finally, on has $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_{hg}=0$ and $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{hg}$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-module flat; hence $M_{hg}$ is an $A$-module flat by $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.2), and the conclusion follows since $hg\notin\mathfrak{q}$.
C.Q.F.D.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.2]
\label{IV.11.1.2}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}'$, $\sh{F}$ two coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Modules, $u:\sh{F}'\to\sh{F}$ a homomorphism of $\sh{O}_X$-Modules.
Suppose that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that, setting $y=f(x)$, the homomorphism $u_x\otimes 1:\sh{F}'_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}k(y)\to\sh{F}_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}k(y)$ is injective, is open in $X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, let $\sh{N}$ (resp. $\sh{P}$) be the kernel (resp. the cokernel) of $u$; applying $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.4) ($^1$) to the local rings $\sh{O}_y$ and the $\sh{O}_x$-modules $\sh{F}'_x$ and $\sh{F}_x$: to say that $u_x\otimes 1$ is injective is equivalent to saying that $\sh{N}_x=0$ and that $\sh{P}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
Now $\sh{F}$ and $\sh{G}$ are coherent, hence the corollary follows from \sref{IV.11.1.1} and $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 5.2.2).
\footnote{Here is a proof of $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.4), which has not been published in Bourbaki's \emph{Commutative Algebra}.
Taking into account $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.1.2), it suffices to see that \emph{b)} implies \emph{a)}.
Let $P=\Im(u)$, $Q=\operatorname{Coker}(u)$, $R=\Ker(u)$.
The composed map $M\otimes k\to P\otimes k\to N\otimes k$ is injective, and $M\otimes k\to P\otimes k$ is surjective, hence $P\otimes k\to N\otimes k$ is injective and $M\otimes k\to P\otimes k$ is bijective.
The exact sequence $0\to P\to N\to Q\to 0$ gives the exact sequence
$0=\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N,k)\to\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(Q,k)\to P\otimes k\to N\otimes k\to Q\otimes k\to 0$
and since $P\otimes k\to N\otimes k$ is injective, on has $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(Q,k)=0$; as $Q$ is a $B$-module of finite type, $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.2) shows that $Q$ is also an $A$-module flat; also $R\otimes k=0$ by exact sequence, hence $R=0$ by Nakayama.}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.3]
\label{IV.11.1.3}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $g$ a section of $\sh{O}_X$ over $X$.
The set of $x\in X$ such that $g_x\otimes 1$ is not a zero divisor in $\sh{O}_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_{f(x)}}k(f(x))$ is open in $X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to apply \sref{IV.11.1.2} to the endomorphism of $\sh{O}_X$ defined by $g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.4]
\label{IV.11.1.4}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module, and $f$-flat.
Let $(g_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ be a suite of sections of $\sh{O}_X$ over $X$.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that the suite $((g_i)_x\otimes 1)$ is $\sh{F}_x$-regular and the $\sh{O}_x$-module $\sh{G}_x$ (where $\sh{G}=\sh{F}/(\sum_{i=1}^n g_i\sh{F})$) is $f$-flat, is open in $X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{119}
As $\sh{F}$ and $\sh{G}$ are coherent, the corollary follows from \sref{IV.11.1.1} and $(\mathbf{0}$, 15.2.4).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.5]
\label{IV.11.1.5}
Let $X$, $Y$, $Z$ be three locally Noetherian preschemes, $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ two morphisms of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module.
Then the set $U$ of $y\in Y$ such that, for every generisation $y'$ of $y$, $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat at all the points of $f^{-1}(y')$ (i.e.\ such that $\sh{F}\otimes_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$ is flat relative to the morphism $X\times_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})\to Z$) is open in $Y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $V$ is the set of $x\in X$ where $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat, $U$ is the set of $y\in Y$ such that for every generisation $y'$ of $y$, one has $f^{-1}(y')\subset V$.
Now $V$ is locally open \sref{IV.11.1.1}, hence locally constructible in $X$, and the set $W$ of $y\in Y$ such that $f^{-1}(y)\subset V$ is also locally constructible in $Y$ by virtue of Chevalley's theorem \sref{IV.1.8.4}.
But it follows then from $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.2.5) that the points of $U$ are the interior points of $W$, whence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.6]
\label{IV.11.1.6}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite type, $C$ a $B$-algebra of finite type, $M$ a $C$-module of finite type.
Then the set of $\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(B)$ such that $M_\mathfrak{q}$ is an $A$-module flat is open in $\Spec(B)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Taking into account \sref{IV.2.1.2}, this is a consequence of \sref{IV.11.1.5} applied to $Z=\Spec(A)$, $Y=\Spec(B)$, $X=\Spec(C)$, $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$.

The results of this subsection will be freed of the Noetherian hypotheses in \sref{IV.11.3}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Flatness of a projective limit of preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.11.2}

\begin{env}[11.2.1]
\label{IV.11.2.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $M$, $N$ two $A$-modules, $A'$ an $A$-algebra; set $M'=M\otimes_A A'$, $N'=N\otimes_A A'$.
Recall (\textbf{III}, 6.3.8) that one defines, for every $i$, a canonical homomorphism
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.1}\tag{11.2.1.1}
  \psi_i:\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M,N)\otimes_A A'\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A'}(M',N')
\]
in the following way: one considers a left resolution of $M$ by \emph{free} $A$-modules
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.2}\tag{11.2.1.2}
  \cdots\to L_{i+1}\xrightarrow{f_i}L_i\to\cdots\to L_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}M\to 0
\]
from which one deduces by tensorisation with $A'$ a complex of $A'$-modules
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.3}\tag{11.2.1.3}
  \cdots\to L'_{i+1}\xrightarrow{f'_i}L'_i\to\cdots\to L'_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon'}M'\to 0
\]
where $L'_i=L_i\otimes_A A'$, $f'_i=f_i\otimes 1_{A'}$, $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon\otimes 1_{A'}$.
\oldpage[IV-3]{120}
Considering on the other hand a left resolution of $M'$ by free $A'$-modules
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.4}\tag{11.2.1.4}
  \cdots\to L''_{i+1}\xrightarrow{f''_i}L''_i\to\cdots\to L''_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon''}M'\to 0
\]
as the $L'_i$ are free $A'$-modules, one knows (M, V, 1.1) that there are $A'$-homomorphisms $u_i$ forming a commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  \cdots\ar[r] & L'_{i+1}\ar[r]^{f'_i}\ar[d]^{u_{i+1}} & L'_i\ar[r]\ar[d]^{u_i} & \cdots\ar[r] & L'_0\ar[r]^{\varepsilon'}\ar[d]^{u_0} & M'\ar[r]\ar@{=}[d] & 0\\
  \cdots\ar[r] & L''_{i+1}\ar[r]^{f''_i} & L''_i\ar[r] & \cdots\ar[r] & L''_0\ar[r]^{\varepsilon''} & M'\ar[r] & 0
}
\]
Composing the homomorphism $u_\bullet:L'_\bullet\to L''_\bullet$ of complexes thus defined with the canonical homomorphism of $A$-module complexes $L_\bullet\to L'_\bullet$; noting that $(L_i\otimes_A N)\otimes_A A'=L'_i\otimes_{A'} N'$, one deduces a homomorphism of $A$-module complexes $L_\bullet\otimes_A N\to L''_\bullet\otimes_{A'} N'$, whence, passing to homology, the canonical homomorphisms \sref{IV.11.2.1.1}.
As the $u_i$ are well-determined up to homotopy (M, V, 1.1), the homomorphisms \sref{IV.11.2.1.1} do not depend on the choice of the $u_i$ nor on the choice of the free resolutions $L_\bullet$ and $L''_\bullet$.

As the $\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A'}(M',N')$ are $A'$-modules, one canonically deduces from \sref{IV.11.2.1.1} $A'$-homomorphisms
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.5}\tag{11.2.1.5}
  \varphi_i:\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M,N)\otimes_A A'\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A'}(M',N').
\]

Consider now two homomorphisms of rings
\[
  \rho:A\to A^{(1)},\quad\sigma:A^{(1)}\to A^{(2)},
\]
and their composite $\sigma\circ\rho:A\to A^{(2)}$; setting $M^{(j)}=M\otimes_A A^{(j)}$, $N^{(j)}=N\otimes_A A^{(j)}$ for $j=1,2$.
Then the canonical composite homomorphism
\[
  \operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M,N)\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A^{(1)}}(M^{(1)},N^{(1)})\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A^{(2)}}(M^{(2)},N^{(2)})
\]
is the same as the canonical homomorphism deduced from $\sigma\circ\rho$; this follows from the fact that, if $L^{(j)}_\bullet$ is a free resolution of $M^{(j)}$, the diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  L_\bullet\ar[r]\ar[d] & L_\bullet\otimes_A A^{(1)}\ar[r]\ar[d] & L^{(1)}_\bullet\\
  L_\bullet\otimes_A A^{(2)}\ar[r] & L^{(1)}_\bullet\otimes_{A^{(1)}}A^{(2)}\ar[r] & L^{(2)}_\bullet
}
\]
is commutative.
\end{env}

\begin{lemma}[11.2.2.2]
\label{IV.11.2.2.2}
Let $(A_\alpha,g_{\beta\alpha})$ be a filtered inductive system of rings, $(M_\alpha,h_{\beta\alpha})$ a filtered inductive system of sets, $A'=\varinjlim A_\alpha$, $M'=\varinjlim M_\alpha$, $g_\alpha:A_\alpha\to A'$, $h_\alpha:M_\alpha\to M'$ the canonical maps.
For every $\alpha$, let $F(M_\alpha)$ be the $A_\alpha$-module of formal linear combinations of elements of $M_\alpha$; let similarly $F(M')$ be the $A'$-module of formal linear combinations of elements of $M'$.
If $h'_{\beta\alpha}:F(M_\alpha)\to F(M_\beta)$ (for $\alpha\leq\beta$) and $h'_\alpha:F(M_\alpha)\to F(M')$ are the $A_\alpha$-homomorphisms deduced from $h_{\beta\alpha}$ and $h_\alpha$ respectively, $(F(M_\alpha),h'_{\beta\alpha})$ is an inductive system of $A_\alpha$-modules and $(h'_\alpha)$ an inductive system of homomorphisms.
Then
\[
  h''=\varinjlim_\alpha h'_\alpha:\varinjlim_\alpha F(M_\alpha)\to F(\varinjlim_\alpha M_\alpha)
\]
is an isomorphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the proof, see Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.}, chap.~II, 3\textsuperscript{e}~\'{e}d., \textsection\,6, n\textsuperscript{o}\,6, cor.~of prop.~10.
\end{proof}

\end{align}

\begin{lemma}[11.2.4]
\label{IV.11.2.4}
Let $A$ be a ring, $C$ an $A$-algebra, $M$ an $A$-module, $N$ a $C$-module.
Set $C'=C\otimes_A A'$, $M'=M\otimes_A A'$, $N'=N\otimes_A A'=N\otimes_C C'$.
Suppose that $M\otimes_A N$ is a $C$-module flat.
Then the canonical homomorphism
\[
  \varphi_1:\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,N)\otimes_A A'\to\operatorname{Tor}_1^{A'}(M',N')
\]
(cf.\ \sref{IV.11.2.1.5}) \emph{is surjective}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let us keep the notations of \sref{IV.11.2.3}; the right-exactness of the tensor product shows that the sequence $L'_1\xrightarrow{f'_1}L'_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon'}M'\to 0$ is exact; as $L'_0$ and $L'_1$ are $A'$-modules free, \emph{one can take} $L''_0=L'_0$, $L''_1=L'_1$, $u_0$ and $u_1$ being the identity maps and $f''_0=f'_0$.
As $R=\operatorname{Im}(f_0)$ and $R''=\operatorname{Im}(f'_0)$, the homomorphism $v$ is surjective, and it is also the case for $v\otimes 1$.
The proof will be finished if one proves that the first row of $(11.2.3.3)$ is \emph{exact}, $v\otimes 1$ being surjective and $u_0\otimes 1$ injective (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~I\textsuperscript{er}, \textsection~1, n\textsuperscript{o}~4, cor.~2 of prop.~2).
Let us use for this the hypothesis that $M\otimes_A N$ is a $C$-module flat.
Setting $Q=\Ker(\varepsilon\otimes 1)=\operatorname{Im}(j\otimes 1)$ in the exact sequence \sref{IV.11.2.3.1}, one has the two exact sequences $0\to Q\to L_0\otimes_A N\to M\otimes_A N\to 0$ and $T\to R\otimes_A N\to Q\to 0$, where the homomorphisms are homomorphisms of $C$-\emph{modules}; using the hypothesis of flatness, one deduces from $(\mathbf{0}_I, 6.1.2)$ the exact sequence
\[
  0\to Q\otimes_C C'\to(L_0\otimes_A N)\otimes_C C'\to(M\otimes_A N)\otimes_C C'\to 0
\]
and on the other hand, the tensor product being right-exact, one has the exact sequence
\[
  T\otimes_C C'\to(R\otimes_A N)\otimes_C C'\to Q\otimes_C C'\to 0
\]
whence finally the exact sequence
\[
  T\otimes_C C'\to(R\otimes_A N)\otimes_C C'\to(L_0\otimes_A N)\otimes_C C'\to(M\otimes_A N)\otimes_C C'\to 0.
\]
But by definition, for every $C$-module $P$, $P\otimes_C C'=P\otimes_A A'$, hence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[11.2.5]
\label{IV.11.2.5}
Let $A$ be a ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$, $B$ an $A$-algebra, $M$ a $B$-module.
Set $\mathfrak{J}'=\mathfrak{J}A'$, $B'=B\otimes_A A'$, $M'=M\otimes_A A'=M\otimes_B B'$.
Let $\mathfrak{p}'$ be a prime ideal of $B'$ containing $\mathfrak{J}B'$.
Suppose verified one of the hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $\mathfrak{J}$ is nilpotent.
  \item[b)] $A'$ is Noetherian, $B'$ is an $A'$-algebra of finite type, $M'$ a $B'$-module of finite type.
\end{enumerate}
Under these conditions, suppose the two following properties verified:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module flat.
  \item[(ii)] The composed canonical homomorphism
\[
  \operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{J})\xrightarrow{\psi_1}\operatorname{Tor}_1^{A'}(M',A'/\mathfrak{J}')\xrightarrow{\theta}(\operatorname{Tor}_1^{A'}(M',A'/\mathfrak{J}'))_{\mathfrak{p}'}
\]
(where $\psi_1$ is the canonical homomorphism \sref{IV.11.2.1.1} and $\theta$ the localisation of a $B'$-module at $\mathfrak{p}'$) is zero.
\end{enumerate}
Then $M'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ is an $A'$-module flat.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let us note that under hypothesis $b)$, $M'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ is a $B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$-module of finite type, $B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ an $A'$-algebra noethérienne, and $\mathfrak{J}'B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ is contained in the radical $\mathfrak{p}'B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ of $B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$; under hypothesis $a)$, $\mathfrak{J}'$ is nilpotent; one can therefore apply the flatness criterion $(\mathbf{0}_{III}, 10.2.1)$ or $(\mathbf{0}_{III}, 10.2.2)$ as appropriate.
First, $M'_{\mathfrak{p}'}\otimes_{A'}(A'/\mathfrak{J}')=(M'\otimes_{A'}(A'/\mathfrak{J}'))_{\mathfrak{p}'}=((M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J}))\otimes_{A/\mathfrak{J}}(A'/\mathfrak{J}'))_{\mathfrak{p}'}$; hypothesis~(i) then implies that $M'_{\mathfrak{p}'}\otimes_{A'}(A'/\mathfrak{J}')$ is an $(A'/\mathfrak{J}')$-module flat, taking into account $(\mathbf{0}_I, 6.2.1$ and $6.3.2)$.
It remains to see that $\operatorname{Tor}_1^{A'}(M'_{\mathfrak{p}'},A'/\mathfrak{J}')=0$; but this $B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$-module is equal to $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^{A'}(M',A'/\mathfrak{J}'))_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ by flatness of $B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$ over $B'$.
But by virtue of hypothesis~(ii), \sref{IV.11.2.4} applied to $C=A/\mathfrak{J}$ and $N=C$ shows that $\varphi_1$ is \emph{surjective} (since $A'/\mathfrak{J}'=C\otimes_A A'$); hence the homomorphism $\theta$ of $\operatorname{Tor}_1^{A'}(M',A'/\mathfrak{J}')$ engenders the $B'_{\mathfrak{p}'}$-module $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^{A'}(M',A'/\mathfrak{J}'))_{\mathfrak{p}'}$, and the latter is zero.
Q.E.D.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[11.2.6]
\label{IV.11.2.6}
The notations being those of $(8.5.1)$ and $(8.8.1)$, suppose $S_\alpha$ quasi-compact, $X_\alpha$ and $Y_\alpha$ of finite presentation over $S_\alpha$; let $f_\alpha:X_\alpha\to Y_\alpha$ be an $S_\alpha$-morphism, $\sh{F}_\alpha$ an $\sh{O}_{X_\alpha}$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] Let $x$ be a point of $X$, $x_\alpha$ its canonical projection in $X_\alpha$.
  For $\sh{F}$ to be $f$-flat at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $\sh{F}_\lambda$ be $f_\lambda$-flat at the point $x_\lambda$.
  \item[(ii)] For $\sh{F}$ to be $f$-flat, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $\sh{F}_\lambda$ be $f_\lambda$-flat.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\oldpage[IV-3]{124}

One need only prove the sufficiency of the conditions, the necessity being \sref{IV.2.1.4}.
We proceed in several steps.

\begin{corollary}[11.2.7]

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $f$ is a morphism of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ is an $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation and $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$ \emph{(resp.\ $f$-flat)}.

\begin{proposition}[11.2.8]
\label{IV.11.2.8}
Let $g:X\to S$, $h:Y\to S$ be two morphisms of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation; for every $s\in S$, let $X_s=g^{-1}(s)=X\times_S\Spec(k(s))$, $Y_s=h^{-1}(s)=Y\times_S\Spec(k(s))$, $f_s$ the morphism $f\times 1:X_s\to Y_s$, $\sh{F}_s=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_S}k(s)$.
Then the set $E$ of $s\in S$ such that $\sh{F}_s$ is $f_s$-flat is locally constructible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The property in question verifies the constructibility condition $(9.2.1,\text{ (i)})$, by virtue of $(2.2.13)$ and $(2.5.1)$.
Taking into account $(9.2.3)$, one can reduce to the case where $S$ is affine, Noetherian and integral of generic point $\eta$ and to prove that $E$ is an open neighbourhood of $\eta$ in $S$.
If $\eta\in S-E$, it results first from \sref{IV.11.2.6}, applying the method of $(8.1.2, a)$, that there is a neighbourhood open $U$ of $\eta$ in $S$ such that $\sh{F}|g^{-1}(U)$ is $h^{-1}(U)$-flat; \emph{a fortiori} $(2.1.4)$ $\sh{F}_s$ is $f_s$-flat for every $s\in U$, which finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

The following theorem generalises \sref{IV.11.2.6.1},~(ii)):

\begin{theorem}[11.2.9]
\label{IV.11.2.9}
\textnormal{(Raynaud)}
Let $A_0$ be a ring, $(A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ a filtered inductive system of $A_0$-algebras, $B_0$ an $A_0$-algebra of finite presentation, $\mathfrak{J}_0$ an ideal of finite type of $B_0$, $M_0$ a $B_0$-module of finite presentation.
Set $B_\lambda=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda$, $M_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{B_0}B_\lambda$, $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$, $B=\varinjlim B_\lambda=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A$, $\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}_0B$, $M=\varinjlim M_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A=M_0\otimes_{B_0}B$.
For $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{J}^*(M)$ to be an $A$-module flat, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist $\lambda$ such that $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{J}_\lambda}^*(M_\lambda)$ be an $A_\lambda$-module flat; the canonical homomorphisms
\begin{equation*}
\label{IV.11.2.9.1}\tag{11.2.9.1}
  \begin{cases}
    \operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{J}_\lambda}^*(M_\lambda)\otimes_{A_\lambda}A\to\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{J}^*(M)\\
    \operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{J}_\lambda}^*(M_\lambda)\otimes_{A_\lambda}A_\mu\to\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{J}_\mu}^*(M_\mu)
  \end{cases}
  \qquad\text{for }\lambda\leq\mu
\end{equation*}
are then bijective and $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{J}_\mu}^*(M_\mu)$ is an $A_\mu$-module flat for $\lambda\leq\mu$.
\end{theorem}

The proof of this theorem proceeds in several steps (I--VI) and occupies the following pages.
We only state the key intermediate results:

\begin{lemma}[11.2.9.2]
\label{IV.11.2.9.2}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra, $M$ a $B$-module, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $B$.
Let $A'$ be an $A$-algebra; set $B'=B\otimes_A A'$, $M'=M\otimes_A A'=M\otimes_B B'$, $\mathfrak{J}'=\mathfrak{J}B'$.
If $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{J}^*(M)$ is an $A$-module flat, the canonical homomorphism
\[
  (\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{J}^*(M))\otimes_A A'\to\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{J}'}^*(M')
\]
is bijective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[11.2.9.3]
\label{IV.11.2.9.3}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}B_i$ a graded $A$-algebra with positive degrees, $M=\bigoplus_{i\in\bb{Z}}M_i$ a graded $B$-module.
Suppose that $B_0$ is a local ring, and that each of the $M_i$ is a $B_0$-module of finite type.
For $M$ to be a $B$-module of finite type, it is necessary and sufficient that, if $\mathfrak{q}$ is the inverse image in $A$ of the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of $B_0$, $M\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q})$ be a $(B\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q}))$-module of finite type.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The condition being evidently necessary, let us prove that it is sufficient.
As $B$ is an $A_0$-algebra, one can replace $A$ by $A_\mathfrak{q}$, in other words suppose that $A$ is also a local ring with $\mathfrak{q}$ as maximal ideal.
By hypothesis, there exists an integer $N$ such that the canonical homomorphism
\[
  \bigoplus_{-N\leq i\leq N}(M_i\otimes_{B_0}B)\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q})\to M\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q})
\]
is surjective; this also means that, for every integer $n$, the canonical homomorphism of $k(\mathfrak{q})$-modules
\[
  \bigoplus_{-N\leq i\leq N}(M_i\otimes_{B_0}B_{n-i})\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q})\to M_n\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q})
\]
is surjective.
Now, $M_n$ is a $B_0$-module of finite type, $B_0$ a local ring and $\mathfrak{q}B_0\subset\mathfrak{m}$; hence Nakayama's lemma shows that each of the canonical homomorphisms
\[
  \bigoplus_{-N\leq i\leq N}(M_i\otimes_{B_0}B_{n-i})\to M_n
\]
is surjective, whence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.2.9.4]
\label{IV.11.2.9.4}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.11.2.9.3}, suppose moreover that each of the $B_i$ and each of the $M_i$ is a $B_0$-module of finite presentation, and that $M$ is an $A$-module flat.
For $M$ to be a $B$-module of finite presentation, it is necessary and sufficient that $M\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q})$ be a $(B\otimes_A k(\mathfrak{q}))$-module of finite type.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[11.3.1]
\label{IV.11.3.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$ is open in $X$.
If moreover $\Supp(\sh{F})=X$, $f|U$ is an open morphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The second assertion has already been proved \sref{IV.2.4.6} and has been included here only for reference.

The question being local on $X$ and $Y$, we can suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are affine, and that $f$ is a morphism of finite presentation.
Let $x\in X$ be a point such that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
Applying \sref{IV.11.2.7}, one can suppose that $X=X_0\times_{Y_0}Y$, $f=f_0\times 1$, where $Y_0$ is Noetherian, $f_0:X_0\to Y_0$ a morphism of finite type, $\sh{F}_0$ a coherent $\sh{O}_{X_0}$-Module; moreover, if $x_0$ is the projection of $x$ in $X_0$, one can suppose that $\sh{F}_0$ is $f_0$-flat at the point $x_0$.
Then, by virtue of \sref{IV.11.1.1}, the set $U_0$ of points of $X_0$ where $\sh{F}_0$ is $f_0$-flat is an open neighbourhood of $x_0$; hence $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the points of the inverse image of $U_0$ in $X$ \sref{IV.2.1.4}, and this proves that $U$ is a neighbourhood of $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.3.2]
\label{IV.11.3.2}
\oldpage[IV-3]{133}
Let $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ be two morphisms of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Then the set $U$ of $y\in Y$ such that, for every generisation $y'$ of $y$, $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat at all the points of $f^{-1}(y')$ (i.e.\ such that $\sh{F}\otimes_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$ is flat relative to the morphism $X\times_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})\to Z$) is open in $Y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The same reasoning as in \sref{IV.11.1.5} shows that if $V$ is the set of $x\in X$ where $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat, $U$ is the set of $y\in Y$ whose every generisation belongs to $E=Y-f(X-V)$.
As $g\circ f$ is of finite presentation, $V$ is open in $X$ by virtue of \sref{IV.11.3.1}, hence ind-constructible; and $f(X-V)$ is pro-constructible in $Y$ \sref{IV.1.9.5}[vii)], hence $E$ is ind-constructible in $Y$.
But it follows then from $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 10.1) that $U$ is the interior of $E$, hence open in $Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.3.4]
\label{IV.11.3.4}
Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{J}$ a quasi-coherent ideal of finite type of $\sh{O}_X$, $Y$ the closed sub-prescheme of $X$ defined by $\sh{J}$, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Suppose $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is $f$-flat.
Under these conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the points of $Y$.
  \item If $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is $f$-flat, $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is an $(\sh{O}_X/\sh{J})$-Algebra of finite presentation and $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{F})$ is an $(\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X))$-Module of finite presentation.
  \item Suppose $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is $f$-flat (which implies that $\sh{O}_X/\sh{J}$ is $f$-flat).
    Then the set $W$ of $y\in Y$ such that $(\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{F}))_y$ is an $(\sh{O}_X/\sh{J})_y$-module flat is open in $Y$.
  \item Suppose $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is $f$-flat; let $S'\to S$ be any morphism, let $X'=X\times_S S'$, $p:X'\to X$ the canonical projection, $Y'=p^{-1}(Y)$ the closed sub-prescheme of $X'$, defined by $\sh{J}'=p^*(\sh{J})\sh{O}_{X'}$, $\sh{F}'=p^*(\sh{F})=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_S}\sh{O}_{S'}$; then, if $W'=p^{-1}(W)|W'$, one has $\operatorname{gr}_{\sh{J}'}(\sh{F}')|W'\cong p^*(\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{F}))|W$, and $\operatorname{gr}_{\sh{J}'}(\sh{F}')$ is an $(\sh{O}_{X'}/\sh{J}')$-Module flat at the points of $W'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The questions being local on $X$ and $S$, one can suppose that $S=\Spec(A)$ and $X=\Spec(B)$ are affine, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, and $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$, where $M$ is a $B$-module of finite presentation, $\sh{J}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}$, where $\mathfrak{J}$ is an ideal of finite type of $B$; by virtue of \sref{IV.8.9.1} and \sref{IV.8.5.11}, there exists a Noetherian sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$, an $A_0$-algebra of finite type $B_0$, an ideal $\mathfrak{J}_0$ of $B_0$ of finite type, a $B_0$-module $M_0$ such that $B=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A$, $\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}_0 B$, $M=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A=M_0\otimes_{B_0}B$.
Moreover, $A$ is the inductive limit of its sub-$A_0$-algebras of finite type $A_\lambda$; setting $B_\lambda=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda$, $M_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{B_0}B_\lambda$, $\mathfrak{J}_\lambda=\mathfrak{J}_0 B_\lambda$, $M=\varinjlim M_\lambda$.
Since, by hypothesis, $\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(M)$ is an $A$-module flat, it follows from \sref{IV.11.2.9} that there exists $\lambda$ such that $\operatorname{gr}^*_{\mathfrak{J}_\lambda}(M_\lambda)$ is an $A_\lambda$-module flat.
To prove that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the points of $Y$, one can therefore restrict to the case where $S$ is \emph{Noetherian}; but then $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.1.2), applied by induction on $n$, proves that the $\sh{F}/\sh{J}^{n+1}\sh{F}$ are $f$-flat, and one concludes by $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.6).

The proof of (ii) reduces in the same way to the case where $S$ is Noetherian, as does (iii) and (iv).
\end{proof}

Generalising the definition of \sref{IV.6.10.1}, we say that for a prescheme $X$, a closed sub-prescheme $Y$ of $X$ defined by a quasi-coherent Ideal $\sh{J}$ of $\sh{O}_X$, and a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module $\sh{F}$, $\sh{F}$ is \emph{normally flat along $Y$ at a point $y$} if $(\operatorname{gr}^*_\sh{J}(\sh{F}))_y$ is a flat $\sh{O}_{X,y}$-module.

\begin{proposition}[11.3.7]
\label{IV.11.3.7}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$, $\sh{F}'$ two quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Modules of finite presentation, $u:\sh{F}'\to\sh{F}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-homomorphism, $x$ a point of $X$ and $y=f(x)$; suppose that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{\alph*)}]
  \item $(\Ker u)_x=0$ and $\operatorname{Coker} u$ is an $\sh{O}_X$-Module $f$-flat at the point $x$.
  \item The homomorphism $(u\otimes 1)_x:(\sh{F}'\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}k(y))_x\to(\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}k(y))_x$ is injective.
\end{enumerate}
If moreover $\sh{F}'$ is $f$-flat, the set of points $x$ satisfying the equivalent conditions above is open in $X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition \emph{a)} implies \emph{b)} by virtue of $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.1.2), without hypothesis on $\sh{F}$.
To prove the converse, one can restrict to the case where $X$ and $Y$ are affine, then, by virtue of \sref{IV.8.9.1} and \sref{IV.11.2.7}, suppose that $X=X_0\times_{Y_0}Y$, $f=f_0\times 1$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}'_0\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y_0}}\sh{O}_Y$, $\sh{F}=\sh{F}_0\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y_0}}\sh{O}_Y$, where $Y_0$ is Noetherian, $f_0:X_0\to Y_0$ a morphism of finite type, $\sh{F}_0$, $\sh{F}'_0$ two coherent $\sh{O}_{X_0}$-Modules, $u_0:\sh{F}'_0\to\sh{F}_0$ a homomorphism; moreover, if $x_0$ is the projection of $x$ in $X_0$, one can suppose that $\sh{F}_0$ is $f_0$-flat at the point $x_0$.
Setting $y_0=f_0(x_0)$; by the transitivity of fibres (\textbf{I}, 3.6.4), the fibre morphism $f^{-1}(y)\to f_0^{-1}(y_0)$ is faithfully flat \sref{IV.2.2.13}, and as $(u\otimes 1)_x$ is injective, this implies that $(u_0\otimes 1)_{x_0}$ is also injective \sref{IV.2.2.7}.
This reduces, by $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.4) applied to the \emph{Noetherian} local rings $\sh{O}_{y_0}$ and $\sh{O}_{x_0}$ and to the $\sh{O}_{x_0}$-modules $(\sh{F}'_0)_{x_0}$ and $(\sh{F}_0)_{x_0}$, to concluding that $(\Ker u_0)_{x_0}=0$ and that $\operatorname{Coker} u_0$ is $f_0$-flat at the point $x_0$.
One deduces from this that $\operatorname{Coker} u$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$ \sref{IV.2.1.4}; applying $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.1.2) to the exact sequence
\[
  0\to(\sh{F}'_0)_{x_0}\to(\sh{F}_0)_{x_0}\to(\operatorname{Coker} u_0)_{x_0}\to 0
\]
one deduces that $u_x$ is an injective homomorphism.
Finally, it follows from \sref{IV.11.1.2} that the set $U_0$ of $z_0\in X_0$ such that $(u_0\otimes 1)_{z_0}$ is injective is open; by flatness, for every $z\in X$ lying over $z_0\in U_0$, the morphism $(u\otimes 1)_z$ is injective, hence the inverse image $U$ of $U_0$ in $X$ is an open neighbourhood of $x$, which concludes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[11.3.8]

  \item The suite $((g_i)_x)$ is $\sh{F}_x$-regular and $\sh{F}_n$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
\end{enumerate}

  \item There exists a neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that the suite $(g_i|U)$ is $(\sh{F}|U)$-quasi-regular, and $\sh{F}_n$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{\alph*)}]
  \setcounter{enumi}{2}
  \item $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$, and the suite $((g_i\otimes 1)_x)$ of elements of $\sh{O}_{X_y,x}$, images of the $(g_i)_x$, is $(\sh{F}_y)_x$-regular.

\begin{theorem}[11.3.10]
\label{IV.11.3.10}
\emph{(Flatness criterion by fibres).}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item[$1\textsuperscript{o}$] $S$, $X$ and $Y$ are locally Noetherian and $\sh{F}$ is coherent.
  \item[$2\textsuperscript{o}$] $g$ and $h$ are locally of finite presentation and $\sh{F}$ is of finite presentation.

  \item $\sh{F}$ is $g$-flat at the point $x$ and $\sh{F}_s$ is $f_s$-flat at the point $x$.
  \item $h$ is flat at the point $y$ and $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
\end{enumerate}
\oldpage[IV-3]{138}
Moreover, under hypothesis $2\textsuperscript{o}$, the set of $x\in X$ satisfying condition \emph{b)} is open in $X$.

\begin{lemma}[11.3.10.2]
\label{IV.11.3.10.2}
Let $A$ be a commutative ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$, $M$ a $B$-module.
Consider the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\mathfrak{J}$ is nilpotent.
  \item $B$ is Noetherian and $M$ is ideally separated for the $\mathfrak{J}B$-preadic topology $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1).
  \item $\mathfrak{J}B$ is contained in the radical of $B$.
\end{enumerate}
Consider on the other hand the four properties:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\emph{\alph*)}]
  \item $M$ is a $B$-module flat.
  \item $B$ is an $A$-module flat.
  \item $M$ is an $A$-module flat and $M/\mathfrak{J}M$ is a $(B/\mathfrak{J}B)$-module flat.
  \item $B/\mathfrak{J}B$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module flat and for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}\supset\mathfrak{J}B$ of $B$ one has $\mathfrak{m}M\neq M$.
\end{enumerate}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[$1\textsuperscript{o}$] If (i) or (ii) is satisfied, the conjunction of \emph{a)} and \emph{b)} implies \emph{c)}, and \emph{c)} implies \emph{a)}.
  \item[$2\textsuperscript{o}$] If (i), or the conjunction of (ii) and (iii), is satisfied, the conjunction of \emph{c)} and \emph{d)} implies the conjunction of \emph{a)} and \emph{b)}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{139}
$1\textsuperscript{o}$~The first assertion is immediate $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.2.1).
Suppose \emph{c)} satisfied.
Consider the graded rings $\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(A)$, $\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(B)$ and the graded module $\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(M)$ relative to the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic filtrations, along with the surjective canonical maps $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.1.2)
\begin{align*}
  u&:\operatorname{gr}^0(B)\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}^0(A)}\operatorname{gr}^*(A)\to\operatorname{gr}^*(B)\\
  v&:\operatorname{gr}^0(M)\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}^0(A)}\operatorname{gr}^*(A)\to\operatorname{gr}^*(M)\\
  w&:\operatorname{gr}^0(M)\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}^0(B)}\operatorname{gr}^*(B)\to\operatorname{gr}^*(M).
\end{align*}
Hypothesis \emph{c)} implies that $v$ is bijective $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1); as the two other maps are surjective, they are also bijective.
But as $M/\mathfrak{J}M$ is a $(B/\mathfrak{J}B)$-module flat by \emph{c)}, it follows from $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1) that $M$ is a $B$-module flat.

$2\textsuperscript{o}$~Either of (i) or (iii) implies that every maximal ideal of $B$ contains $\mathfrak{J}B$.
It follows from $1\textsuperscript{o}$ and the conjunction of \emph{c)} and \emph{d)} that $M$ is a $B$-module faithfully flat, and hence $\operatorname{gr}^0(M)$ is a $\operatorname{gr}^0(B)$-module faithfully flat $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.2.1).
Since \emph{c)} implies that $v$ is bijective, $u$ is also bijective $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.4.1).
Conditions (ii) and (iii) imply that $B$ is ideally separated for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic filtration (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.~comm.}, chap.~III, \textsection\,5, n\textsuperscript{o}\,4, prop.~2); one deduces from $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1) that $B$ is an $A$-module flat.
This also finishes the proof of \sref{IV.11.3.10}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.3.15]
\label{IV.11.3.15}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, $M$ a $B$-module of finite presentation, which is flat as an $A$-module.
Then there exists a finite sequence $(f_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ of elements of $A$, such that the ideal generated by the $f_i$ is equal to $A$, and that, for $0\leq i<n$, $M_{f_{i+1}}/(\sum_{j=1}^i f_j M_{f_{i+1}})$ is an $(A_{f_{i+1}}/(\sum_{j=1}^i f_j A_{f_{i+1}}))$-module free.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One can also say that the $\mathrm{D}(f_i)$ form a covering of $X=\Spec(A)$, and that, setting $Z_1=\mathrm{D}(f_1)$ and by induction $Z_{i+1}=\mathrm{D}(f_{i+1})\cap\mathrm{V}(f_1 A+\cdots+f_i A)$, the $Z_i$ form a \emph{partition} of $X$ into locally closed sets, $A_{f_{i+1}}/(\sum_{j=1}^i f_j A_{f_{i+1}})$ being the ring of an affine sub-scheme of $X$ having $Z_{i+1}$ as underlying space.

To prove the proposition, one can first, by virtue of \sref{IV.11.2.7}, suppose that there exist a Noetherian ring $A_0$, an $A_0$-algebra of finite type $B_0$, a $B_0$-module of finite type $M_0$, flat over $A_0$, such that $B=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A$, $M=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A$; it is clear that it suffices to prove the proposition for $A_0$, $B_0$, $M_0$, for if the $f_i\in A_0$ satisfy the conditions of the statement, they will satisfy them also for $A$, $B$, $M$.
One can therefore restrict to the case where $A$ is Noetherian.

Note now that if $C$ is a Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{R}$ its nilradical and $P$ a flat $C$-module, then for $P$ to be a $C$-module free, it suffices that $P\otimes_C(C/\mathfrak{R})$ be a $(C/\mathfrak{R})$-module free.
Note also that if $C$ is a Noetherian \emph{reduced} ring, there exists $g\in C$ such that $C_g$ is integral.
Using now \sref{IV.6.9.2}: one can define by induction a sequence $(f_i)_{i\geq 1}$ of elements of $A$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[$1\textsuperscript{o}$] $f_1$ is such that $A_1=(A_{\mathrm{red}})_{f_1}$ is integral and $M\otimes_A A_1$ is an $A_1$-module free;
  \item[$2\textsuperscript{o}$] if the ideal $\mathfrak{J}_i$ generated by $f_1,\ldots,f_i$ is $A$, $f_{i+1}=f_i$;
  \item[$3\textsuperscript{o}$] otherwise $\mathfrak{J}_i\neq A$, $f_{i+1}$ is an element not belonging to $\mathfrak{J}_i$ such that $A_{i+1}=((A/\mathfrak{J}_i)_{\mathrm{red}})_{f_{i+1}}$ is integral and $M\otimes_A A_{i+1}$ is an $A_{i+1}$-module free.
\end{enumerate}
As $A$ is Noetherian, $(\mathfrak{J}_i)$ is stationary, hence there exists $n$ such that $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ generate the ideal $A$, and the $f_i$ answer the question since $((A/\mathfrak{J}_i)_{\mathrm{red}})_{f_{i+1}}=((A/\mathfrak{J}_i)_{f_{i+1}})_{\mathrm{red}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.3.16]
\label{IV.11.3.16}
\oldpage[IV-3]{142}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a faithfully flat morphism of finite presentation, $g:Y\to Z$ a morphism of finite type such that $g\circ f:X\to Z$ is a morphism of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation).
Then $g$ is a morphism of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As $f$ is surjective and $g\circ f$ quasi-compact, $g$ is quasi-compact \sref{IV.1.1.3}.
Let us show that $g$ is quasi-separated: for an affine open $W\subset Z$, and a finite affine covering $(V_i)$ of $g^{-1}(W)$, the $V_i\cap V_j$ are quasi-compact since $f$ is of finite presentation and surjective \sref{IV.1.2.6} and \sref{IV.1.2.7}.
The question being local on $Y$ and $Z$, one can suppose $Y=\Spec(B)$, $Z=\Spec(A)$ affine.
If $X'$ is the prescheme sum of the affine pieces of $X$, $f\circ p$ is faithfully flat of finite presentation and $g\circ f\circ p$ is of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation), so one can suppose $X=\Spec(C)$ affine.
The first assertion ($g$ of finite type) then follows by faithful flatness and the fact that the application $B_\lambda\to B$ is injective.
For the second (finite presentation), suppose $g\circ f$ is of finite presentation; then $C$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
Let $(B_\lambda)$ be the filtered family of sub-$A$-algebras of finite type of $B$; by \sref{IV.8.8.2}, there exists $\alpha$ such that $C=C_\alpha\otimes_{B_\alpha}B$ with $C_\alpha$ a $B_\alpha$-algebra of finite presentation faithfully flat \sref{IV.8.10.5} and \sref{IV.11.2.6}.
By faithful flatness $B=B_\lambda$ for some $\lambda$, hence $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.3.17]
\label{IV.11.3.17}
\oldpage[IV-3]{143}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra, $C$ a $B$-algebra of finite presentation which is a faithfully flat $B$-module.
Then, if $C$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation), $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the affine translation of \sref{IV.11.3.16}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Descent of flatness by arbitrary morphisms: case of an Artinian base prescheme}
\label{subsection:IV.11.4}

\begin{theorem}[11.4.1]
\label{IV.11.4.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ a nilpotent ideal of $A$, $u_\lambda:A\to B_\lambda$ $(\lambda\in L)$ a family of ring homomorphisms such that the intersection of the kernels of the $u_\lambda$ is reduced to $0$.
Let $M$ be an $A$-module such that for every $\lambda\in L$, $M\otimes_A B_\lambda$ is a $B_\lambda$-module free and that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module free.
Then $M$ is an $A$-module free.
If moreover the index set $L$ is finite, one can replace ``free'' by ``flat'' in the preceding statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In both cases it suffices to prove that $M$ is an $A$-module \emph{flat}: when $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module free, it follows that $M$ is an $A$-module free by $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.1.2).
We use the following lemma, which generalises $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1):
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[11.4.1.1]
\label{IV.11.4.1.1}
Let $A$ be a ring equipped with a finite filtration $(\mathfrak{J}_n)_{0\leq n\leq N+1}$ with $\mathfrak{J}_0=A$, $\mathfrak{J}_{N+1}=0$.
Let $M$ be an $A$-module equipped with the filtration $(\mathfrak{J}_n M)_{0\leq n\leq N+1}$, and denote by $\operatorname{gr}(A)$ and $\operatorname{gr}(M)$ the corresponding graded ring and graded module.
Suppose that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J}_1)$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J}_1)$-module flat and that the canonical homomorphism
\[
\label{IV.11.4.1.2}\tag{11.4.1.2}
  \operatorname{gr}^0(M)\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}^0(A)}\operatorname{gr}(A)\to\operatorname{gr}(M)
\]
is injective.
Then $M$ is an $A$-module flat.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{144}
The conditions imply, by recurrence, that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J}_N)$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J}_N)$-module flat.
The canonical homomorphism $(M/\mathfrak{J}_N M)\otimes\mathfrak{J}_N\to\mathfrak{J}_N M$ is injective.
Applying $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1) to the $\mathfrak{J}_N$-preadic filtration, $M$ is an $A$-module flat.

To apply the lemma to \sref{IV.11.4.1}, we denote by $\mathfrak{J}_n$ the intersection of the inverse images $u_\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak{J}^n B_\lambda)$: one has $\mathfrak{J}_0=A$, $\mathfrak{J}_m\mathfrak{J}_n\subset\mathfrak{J}_{m+n}$ for $m\geq 0$, $n\geq 0$; moreover, since $\mathfrak{J}^{N+1}=0$, one also has $\mathfrak{J}_{N+1}=0$ since the intersection of the kernels of the $u_\lambda$ is reduced to $0$.
Equipping $A$ with the filtration $(\mathfrak{J}_n)$, $M$ with the filtration $(\mathfrak{J}_n M)$, and $B_\lambda$ with the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic filtration; for each $\lambda$, one considers the commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  \operatorname{gr}^0(M)\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}^0(A)}\operatorname{gr}(A)\ar[r]^-{\varphi}\ar[d]^{f_\lambda} & \operatorname{gr}(M)\ar[d]^{\theta_\lambda}\\
  \operatorname{gr}^0_\mathfrak{J}(N_\lambda)\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}^0_\mathfrak{J}(B_\lambda)}\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(B_\lambda)\ar[r]^-{\varphi_\lambda} & \operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(N_\lambda)
}
\]
where $N_\lambda=M\otimes_A B_\lambda$.
The hypothesis that $N_\lambda$ is a $B_\lambda$-module flat implies that $\varphi_\lambda$ is injective $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1), hence $\Ker(\varphi)\subset\Ker(f_\lambda)$.
Setting $A_0=A/\mathfrak{J}_1$, $M_0=M\otimes_A A_0$, one notes that
\[
  \operatorname{gr}^0_\mathfrak{J}(N_\lambda)=N_\lambda/\mathfrak{J}N_\lambda=(M/\mathfrak{J}M)\otimes_A B_\lambda=(M/\mathfrak{J}_1 M)\otimes_A(B_\lambda/\mathfrak{J}B_\lambda)
\]
and hence $\operatorname{gr}^0_\mathfrak{J}(N_\lambda)\otimes_{\operatorname{gr}^0_\mathfrak{J}(B_\lambda)}\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(B_\lambda)=M_0\otimes_{A_0}\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(B_\lambda)$.
The homomorphism $f_\lambda$ can thus be written $1\otimes\operatorname{gr}(u_\lambda):M_0\otimes_{A_0}\operatorname{gr}(A)\to M_0\otimes_{A_0}\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(B_\lambda)$, where $R_\lambda$ is the kernel of $\operatorname{gr}(u_\lambda):\operatorname{gr}(A)\to\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(B_\lambda)$.
As $M_0$ is by hypothesis a flat $A_0$-module, the kernel of $f_\lambda$ equals $M_0\otimes_{A_0}R_\lambda$, whence the proposition in this case.
When $L$ is finite, the formula remains true under the sole hypothesis that $M_0$ is a flat $A_0$-module $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.1.3), which terminates the proof.
\end{proof}

\oldpage[IV-3]{145}

\begin{remarks}[11.4.2]
\label{IV.11.4.2}
The conclusion of \sref{IV.11.4.1} can be inexact if $L$ is infinite and one only supposes $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module \emph{flat}.
For example, let $V$ be a discrete valuation ring, $K$ its field of fractions, $A=V[T]/(T^2)$; take $\mathfrak{J}$ the image of $(T)$ in $A$, so $A/\mathfrak{J}=V$, and $M=K$; then $M$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module flat but not an $A$-module flat, since $\mathfrak{J}$ is nilpotent and the intersection of the maximal ideals of $A$ is the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ of the local Noetherian ring $A$; one has $\mathfrak{m}K=K$, hence $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{m}^n)=0$ for all $n$, and yet $M$ is not $A$-flat.
\end{remarks}

\begin{corollary}[11.4.3]
\label{IV.11.4.3}
Let $A$ be a semi-local ring whose radical $\mathfrak{J}$ is nilpotent (for example an Artinian ring), $u_\lambda:A\to B_\lambda$ $(\lambda\in L)$ a family of ring homomorphisms such that the intersection of the kernels of the $u_\lambda$ is reduced to $0$.
For an $A$-module $M$ to be flat, it is necessary and sufficient that for every $\lambda\in L$, $M\otimes_A B_\lambda$ be a $B_\lambda$-module flat.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
As $A/\mathfrak{J}$ is a direct product of a finite number of fields (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.~comm.}, chap.~II, \textsection\,3, n\textsuperscript{o}\,5, prop.~16), $A$ is a direct product of local rings $A_i$ with nilpotent radical.
One reduces to the case where $A$ is \emph{local}; then $A/\mathfrak{J}$ is a field, so $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ is free, and the result follows from \sref{IV.11.4.1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.4.4]
\label{IV.11.4.4}
Let $A$ be a ring, $M$ an $A$-module; suppose that there exists a nilpotent ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ of $A$ such that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module free.
Then the set of ideals $\mathfrak{R}$ of $A$ such that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{R})$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{R})$-module free admits a smallest element $\mathfrak{R}_0$ (which is also the smallest of the ideals $\mathfrak{R}$ such that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{R})$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{R})$-module \emph{flat}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{146}
The intersection $\mathfrak{R}_0$ of the ideals $\mathfrak{R}$ for which $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{R})$ is $(A/\mathfrak{R})$-free is the smallest such ideal, by \sref{IV.11.4.1} applied to $A/\mathfrak{R}_0$, its nilpotent ideal $\mathfrak{J}/\mathfrak{R}_0$, and the homomorphisms $A/\mathfrak{R}_0\to A/\mathfrak{R}$.
The assertion about ``flat'' follows from $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.1.2).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.4.5]
\label{IV.11.4.5}
Let $Y$ be an irreducible prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
There exist then a non-empty open $U$ in $Y$ and a closed sub-scheme $Z$ of $U$, finite over $U$, having the following property:
for every base change $U'\to U$, if one sets $X'=X\times_Y U'$, $f'=f_{(U')}$ and $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}\sh{O}_{U'}$, then, for $\sh{F}'$ to be $f'$-flat, it is necessary and sufficient that the morphism $U'\to U$ factor through $U'\to Z\to U$.
Such a $Z$ has the same underlying space as $U$.
Moreover, if $Y$ is affine and $(W_i)$ a finite affine covering of $X$, for $U'=\Spec(A')$ affine with $U'\to U$ factoring through $Z$, each $\Gamma(W_i\times_Y U',\sh{F}')$ is an $A'$-module free.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One reduces via \sref{IV.8.9.1} to the case where $Y$ is Noetherian.
Let $\mathfrak{R}$ be its nilradical; by \sref{IV.6.9.2}, replacing $Y$ by a non-empty open, one can suppose the $M_{i0}$ are $A_0$-modules free.
By \sref{IV.11.4.4}, there is an ideal $\mathfrak{J}_i\subset\mathfrak{R}$ such that $M_i\otimes_A A'$ is $A'$-free if and only if $\mathfrak{J}_i A'=0$.
Taking $\mathfrak{J}=\sum_i\mathfrak{J}_i$ and $Z=\mathrm{V}(\mathfrak{J})$ gives the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.4.6]
\label{IV.11.4.6}
\oldpage[IV-3]{147}
Let $A$ be a ring such that $\Spec(A)$ is irreducible, $\mathfrak{p}$ its unique minimal prime, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, $M$ a $B$-module of finite presentation.
Suppose $M_\mathfrak{p}$ is an $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module flat; then there exists $t\in A-\mathfrak{p}$ such that $M_t$ is an $A_t$-module free.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \sref{IV.11.4.5}, there is an ideal of finite type $\mathfrak{J}$ in $A$ such that $M\otimes_A A'$ is $A'$-free iff $\mathfrak{J}A'=0$.
The hypothesis $M_\mathfrak{p}$ flat gives $\mathfrak{J}_\mathfrak{p}=0$; as $\mathfrak{J}$ is of finite type, $\mathfrak{J}_t=0$ for some $t\in A-\mathfrak{p}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.4.7]
\label{IV.11.4.7}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$, $M$ an $A$-module ideally separated $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1) for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology.
Let $(\mathfrak{p}_i)_{1\leq i\leq r}$ be a finite family of prime ideals of $A$ containing $\mathfrak{J}$; for every $n\geq 0$, let $\mathfrak{p}_i^{(n)}$ be the $n$-th symbolic power; set $\mathfrak{J}_n=\bigcap_{i=1}^r\mathfrak{p}_i^{(n)}$, so $\mathfrak{J}_n\supset\mathfrak{J}^n$.
Suppose the topology defined by $(\mathfrak{J}_n)$ is the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology.
For $M$ to be $A$-flat, it is necessary and sufficient that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ be $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-flat and that $M_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$ be $A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$-flat for every $i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As $M$ is ideally separated, by $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.1) it suffices to show that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$ is $(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$-flat for every $n$.
In $A/\mathfrak{J}_n$, $\mathfrak{J}_1/\mathfrak{J}_n$ is nilpotent and the intersection of the kernels of $A/\mathfrak{J}_n\to A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}/\mathfrak{p}_i^n A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$ is $0$.
By \sref{IV.11.4.1}, it suffices that $M\otimes_A(A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}/\mathfrak{p}_i^n A_{\mathfrak{p}_i})$ be flat, which follows from $M_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$ being $A_{\mathfrak{p}_i}$-flat.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[11.4.8]
\label{IV.11.4.8}
The hypothesis in \sref{IV.11.4.7} on the topology defined by $(\mathfrak{J}_n)$ is satisfied if, for $n$ sufficiently large, $\operatorname{Ass}(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$ is contained in $\{\mathfrak{p}_1,\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_r\}$.
\end{remarks}

\begin{corollary}[11.4.9]
\label{IV.11.4.9}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ a nilpotent ideal of $A$, $M$ an $A$-module.
For $M$ to be $A$-flat (resp.\ free), it is necessary and sufficient that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ be $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-flat (resp.\ free) and that $M_\mathfrak{p}$ be $A_\mathfrak{p}$-flat for every $\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(A/\mathfrak{J})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The assertion relative to the case where $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ is free reduces to the assertion relative to the case where it is flat by ($\mathbf{0_{III}}$,~10.1.2).
For the flat case, since $\mathfrak{J}$ is nilpotent, $\mathfrak{J}^n=0$ for $n$ sufficiently large; the result then follows from \sref{IV.11.4.7} applied to the decreasing sequence $\mathfrak{J}_n=\mathfrak{J}^n$.
\end{proof}

\oldpage[IV-3]{148}

\begin{corollary}[11.4.10]
\label{IV.11.4.10}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $A$, $M$ an $A$-module.
Suppose $M$ is ideally separated for the $\mathfrak{J}$-preadic topology and $\operatorname{gr}_\mathfrak{J}(A)$ is $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-flat.
For $M$ to be $A$-flat, it is necessary and sufficient that $M\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{J})$ be $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-flat and that $M_\mathfrak{p}$ be $A_\mathfrak{p}$-flat for every $\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(A/\mathfrak{J})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \sref{IV.11.4.8}, it reduces to showing that $\operatorname{Ass}(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$ is contained in $\operatorname{Ass}(A/\mathfrak{J})$ for every $n$.
Now, if $a\in A$ does not belong to any $\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(A/\mathfrak{J})$, the multiplication by $a$ is injective in $A/\mathfrak{J}$; since each $\mathfrak{J}^k/\mathfrak{J}^{k+1}$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module, the element $(\mathfrak{J}^k/\mathfrak{J}^{k+1})$-regular, hence $a$ is $(A/\mathfrak{J}^n)$-regular for every $n$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~III, \S~2, no.~8, cor.~1 of th.~1), and therefore does not belong to any associated prime of $A/\mathfrak{J}^n$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~II, \S~1, no.~1, prop.~2).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.4.11]
\label{IV.11.4.11}
Let $A$ be an Artinian local ring, $\mathfrak{m}$ its maximal ideal, $Y=\Spec(A)$, $y$ its unique point, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module.
Let $(A'_\alpha)$ be a family of local rings, $u_\alpha:A\to A'_\alpha$ local homomorphisms.
Set $Y'_\alpha=\Spec(A'_\alpha)$, $X'_\alpha=X\times_Y Y'_\alpha$, $\sh{F}'_\alpha=\sh{F}\otimes_A A'_\alpha$.
Let $x$ be a point of $X$, and suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The intersection of the kernels of the $u_\alpha$ is reduced to $0$.
  \item The residue field extension $k(x)/k$ is primary \sref{IV.4.3.1} (automatically satisfied if $k$ is separably closed).
  \item For every $\alpha$, there exists $x'_\alpha\in X'_\alpha$ whose projections in $X$ and $Y'_\alpha$ are $x$ and $y'_\alpha$ respectively, such that $\sh{F}'_\alpha$ is $Y'_\alpha$-flat at $x'_\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{149}
One reduces in three steps.
\emph{I)} Reduction to $A'_\alpha$ a local ring of a $Y$-prescheme of finite type with residue field a finite extension of $k$: using \sref{IV.11.2.6} and the fact that $A$ is Artinian/Jacobson \sref{IV.10.4.7}.
\emph{II)} Reduction to finitely many finite $A$-algebras: as $A$ is Artinian, a finite number of the $u_\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak{m}'^{n_i}_\alpha)$ have intersection $0$; and $A'_\alpha/\mathfrak{m}'^{n_\alpha}_\alpha$ is finite over $A$.
\emph{III)} With the $A'_i$ finite, take the prescheme sum $Y'=\coprod Y'_i$, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$; $\sh{F}'$ is $Y'$-flat near the inverse image of $x$ \sref{IV.11.1.1}; as $Y'\to Y$ is finite hence closed, one finds an open $U$ of $x$ in $X$ where $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat by \sref{IV.11.4.3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.4.12]
\label{IV.11.4.12}
Let $A$ be an Artinian local ring of residue field $k$, $Y=\Spec(A)$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module.
Let $(A'_\alpha)$ be a family of local rings, $u_\alpha:A\to A'_\alpha$ homomorphisms.
Set $Y'_\alpha=\Spec(A'_\alpha)$, $X'_\alpha=X\times_Y Y'_\alpha$, $\sh{F}'_\alpha=\sh{F}\otimes_A A'_\alpha$.
Let $x\in X$, and suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The intersection of the kernels of the $u_\alpha$ is reduced to $0$.
  \item For every $\alpha$, $\sh{F}'_\alpha$ is $f'_\alpha$-flat at all $x'_\alpha\in X'_\alpha$ whose projection in $X$ is $x$ and in $Y'_\alpha$ is $y'_\alpha$, and $k(x)$ is a primary extension of $k(y)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{150}
One reduces to the case of finitely many $A'_\alpha$ (as $A$ is Artinian).
Passing to the algebraic closure $k'$ of $k$ via a flat local homomorphism $A\to B$ with $B\otimes_A k\cong k'$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.3.1), the hypotheses of \sref{IV.11.4.11} are satisfied for $B$, hence the result by \sref{IV.11.4.3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.4.13]
\label{IV.11.4.13}
Let $A$ be an Artinian local ring, $Y=\Spec(A)$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module.
Let $(Y'_\alpha)$ be a family of $A$-preschemes, $X'_\alpha=X\times_Y Y'_\alpha$, $\sh{F}'_\alpha=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'_\alpha}$.
Let $x\in X$ and suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The intersection of the kernels of the structural homomorphisms $A\to\Gamma(Y'_\alpha,\sh{O}_{Y'_\alpha})$ is reduced to $0$.
  \item For every $\alpha$, $\sh{F}'_\alpha$ is $f'_\alpha$-flat at all $x'_\alpha\in X'_\alpha$ whose projection in $X$ is $x$ and in $Y'_\alpha$ is a closed point.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Replacing the family $(Y'_\alpha)$ by the local schemes at the closed points reduces to \sref{IV.11.4.12}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Descent of flatness by arbitrary morphisms: general case}
\label{subsection:IV.11.5}

\begin{theorem}[11.5.1]
\label{IV.11.5.1}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module, $x\in X$, $y=f(x)$.
Let $(Y'_\alpha)$ be a family of $Y$-preschemes, $Y'_\alpha=\Spec(A'_\alpha)$ local, with closed points $y'_\alpha$ mapping to $y$; let $\mathfrak{m}'_\alpha$ be the maximal ideal of $A'_\alpha$ and $u_\alpha:\sh{O}_y\to A'_\alpha$ the canonical homomorphism.
Suppose the finite intersections of ideals $u_\alpha^{-1}(\mathfrak{m}'^{n_\alpha}_\alpha)$ form a fundamental system of neighbourhoods of $0$ in $\sh{O}_y$.
Set $X'_\alpha=X\times_Y Y'_\alpha$, $\sh{F}'_\alpha=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'_\alpha}$.
Suppose one of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item For every $\alpha$, $\sh{F}'_\alpha$ is $f'_\alpha$-flat at all points with projection $x$ in $X$ and $y'_\alpha$ in $Y'_\alpha$.
  \item For every $\alpha$, there exists $x'_\alpha\in X'_\alpha$ projecting to $x$ and $y'_\alpha$, with $\sh{F}'_\alpha$ flat at $x'_\alpha$, and $k(x)$ is a primary extension of $k(y)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{151}
By $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.2), it suffices to show $\sh{F}_x/\mathfrak{m}_y^n\sh{F}_x$ is $(\sh{O}_y/\mathfrak{m}_y^n)$-flat for every $n>0$.
By hypothesis, there exists $\lambda$ with $\mathfrak{J}_\lambda\subset\mathfrak{m}_y^n$; passing to quotients gives homomorphisms $u'_\alpha:\sh{O}_y/\mathfrak{J}_\lambda\to A'_\alpha/\mathfrak{m}'^{n_\alpha}_\alpha$ with intersection of kernels $0$.
One applies \sref{IV.11.4.11} in case (i) and \sref{IV.11.4.12} in case (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.5.2]
\label{IV.11.5.2}
Let $Y$ be locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ coherent, $x\in X$, $y=f(x)$; set $A=\sh{O}_y$.
Let $u:A\to A'$ be a homomorphism into a Zariski $A$-ring $A'$ such that $u^{-1}(\operatorname{rad}(A'))=\mathfrak{m}$ and $\hat{u}:\hat{A}\to\hat{A}'$ is injective.
Set $Y'=\Spec(A')$, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'}$.
For $\sh{F}$ to be $f$-flat at $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that $\sh{F}'$ be $f'$-flat at every point projecting to $x$ in $X$ and to a closed point of $Y'$.

If $A'$ is a finite $A$-algebra, one can replace the injectivity of $\hat{u}$ by the injectivity of $u$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
As $A$ (resp.\ $A'$) embeds into $\hat{A}$ (resp.\ $\hat{A}'$), $u$ is injective.
The result follows from \sref{IV.11.5.1}[(i)] applied to the family of localisations of $A'$ at its maximal ideals.
\end{proof}

\oldpage[IV-3]{152}

\begin{proposition}[11.5.3]
\label{IV.11.5.3}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation, $x\in X$, $y=f(x)$, $g=(\psi,\theta):Y'\to Y$ a proper morphism of finite presentation.
Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\theta_y:\sh{O}_y\to(g_*(\sh{O}_{Y'}))_y$ is injective.
  \item For every $x'\in X'=X\times_Y Y'$ projecting to $x$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}\sh{O}_{Y'}$ is $Y'$-flat at $x'$.
\end{enumerate}
Then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \sref{IV.11.3.1}, the $f'$-flatness locus $U'$ is open and contains $p^{-1}(x)$; as $p$ is proper hence closed, $U'$ contains a neighbourhood.
One reduces to $Y$ local Noetherian, then applies \sref{IV.11.5.1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.5.4]
\label{IV.11.5.4}
Let $f=(\psi,\theta):X\to Y$ be a proper morphism of finite presentation, $p:X\times_Y X\to X$ the first projection.
Suppose $\theta:\sh{O}_Y\to f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ is injective.
Then, for $f$ to be flat, it is necessary and sufficient that $p$ be flat.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \sref{IV.11.5.3} with $\sh{F}=\sh{O}_X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.5.5]
\label{IV.11.5.5}
\oldpage[IV-3]{153}
Let $A$ be a ring, $Y=\Spec(A)$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation, $x\in X$.
Let $A\to A'$ be an injective homomorphism making $A'$ an integral $A$-algebra.
Set $Y'=\Spec(A')$, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y}\sh{O}_{Y'}$.
If $\sh{F}'$ is $f'$-flat at every point of $X'$ projecting to $x$ in $X$, then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One reduces to $f$ of finite presentation, then to $Y$ local; by \sref{IV.5.13.3} and \sref{IV.8.9.1}, one can suppose $A$ Noetherian.
Setting $B=\hat{A}$, $Z=\Spec(B)$; $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat iff $\sh{F}\otimes_Y Z$ is $Z$-flat \sref{IV.2.1.4}.
As $A'$ is integral over $A$, $Y'\to Y$ is proper (\textbf{II}, 6.1.10).
One applies \sref{IV.11.5.3}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Descent of flatness by arbitrary morphisms: case of a unibranch base}
\label{subsection:IV.11.6}

\oldpage[IV-3]{154}

\begin{lemma}[11.5.5.2]
\label{IV.11.5.5.2}
Let $A$ be a ring, $A'$ an $A$-algebra, $(A'_\lambda)$ an inductive system of $A$-algebras such that $A'=\varinjlim A'_\lambda$; set $Y=\Spec(A)$, $Y'=\Spec(A')$, $Y'_\lambda=\Spec(A'_\lambda)$.
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation; set $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'}$, $X'_\lambda=X\times_Y Y'_\lambda$, $f'_\lambda=f_{(Y'_\lambda)}$, $\sh{F}'_\lambda=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'_\lambda}$.
Let $x$ be a point of $X$; if $\sh{F}'$ is $f'$-flat at all points $x'\in X'$ lying over $x$, then there exists $\lambda$ such that $\sh{F}'_\lambda$ is $f'_\lambda$-flat at all points of $X'_\lambda$ lying over $x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $U'$ be the set of $x'\in X'$ such that $\sh{F}'$ is $f'$-flat at $x'$; by \sref{IV.11.3.1}, $U'$ is open in $X'$ since $f'$ is of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.6.2}; likewise $U'_\lambda$ the flatness locus of $\sh{F}'_\lambda$ is open in $X'_\lambda$, and $U'=\bigcup v_\lambda^{-1}(U'_\lambda)$ where $v_\lambda:X'\to X'_\lambda$ is the canonical projection.
Consider $T=\Spec(k(x))$, $T'_\lambda=T\times_Y Y'_\lambda$, and $V'=g'^{-1}(U')$, $V'_\lambda=g_\lambda'^{-1}(U'_\lambda)$.
By hypothesis (\textbf{I}, 6.1.1)) $V'=T'$; as $T'$ is quasi-compact, there exists $\lambda$ such that $w_\lambda^{-1}(V'_\lambda)=T'$, hence $T'_\mu=V'_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$.
By \sref{IV.8.3.3}, applied to the closed complement $T'_\lambda-V'_\lambda$ of $T'_\lambda$, there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $T'_\mu=V'_\mu$, i.e.\ $\sh{F}'_\mu$ is $f'_\mu$-flat at all points of $X'_\mu$ whose projection in $X$ is $x$.
C.Q.F.D.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[11.6.1]
\label{IV.11.6.1}
Let $A$ be a local integral ring geometrically unibranch $(\mathbf{0}$, 23.2.1), $Y=\Spec(A)$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Let $A'$ be a local ring, $u:A\to A'$ a local injective homomorphism; set $Y'=\Spec(A')$, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'}$.
Let $x$ be a point of $X$ with $f(x)=y$ the closed point of $Y$, $x'$ a point of $X'$ whose projections in $X$ and $Y'$ are $x$ and the closed point $y'$ of $Y'$.
If $\sh{F}'$ is $f'$-flat at the point $x'$, then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{155}
One reduces to $f$ of finite presentation.
We proceed in several steps.

\emph{I) Reduction to $A$ and $A'$ integrally closed.}
As $u$ is injective and $A$ integral, one can suppose $A'$ integrally closed.
Let $A_1$ be the integral closure of $A$; since $A$ is geometrically unibranch, the morphism $\Spec(k(y_1))\to\Spec(k(y))$ is radiciel, and hence $x_1$ is the \emph{sole} point of $X_1$ projecting to $x$ in $X$ and $y_1$ in $Y_1$.
One can apply \sref{IV.11.5.5}, and this reduces to the case where $A$ is also integrally closed.

\emph{II) Reduction to $A$ and $A'$ local $\bb{Z}$-algebras of finite type integrally closed.}
One considers $A'$ as an inductive limit of its sub-$\bb{Z}$-algebras of finite type $B'_\lambda$ \emph{integrally closed}; since $A'$ is integrally closed and the integral closure of a $\bb{Z}$-algebra of finite type is also a finite $\bb{Z}$-algebra \sref{IV.7.8.3}, $A'=\varinjlim(B'_\lambda)_{\mathfrak{m}'_\lambda}=A'_\lambda$.
By \sref{IV.11.5.5.2} and \sref{IV.8.9.1}, there exists a pair $(\alpha,\lambda)$ sufficiently large and a coherent $\sh{O}_{X_{\alpha\lambda}}$-Module $\sh{F}_{\alpha\lambda}$ such that it suffices to show $\sh{F}_{\alpha\lambda}$ is $f_{\alpha\lambda}$-flat at $x_{\alpha\lambda}$.
By \sref{IV.11.2.6}, one can take $\mu\geq\lambda$ large enough so that $\sh{F}'_\mu$ is $Y'_\mu$-flat at the appropriate point.

\emph{III) Reduction to $k'$ a finite and radiciel extension of $k$.}
Using the part I) argument applied to \sref{IV.11.4.11}, and the fact that $\bb{Z}$ is a Jacobson ring, one reduces to the case where $k'$ is a \emph{finite} extension of $k$.
Let $k''$ be the largest separable extension of $k$ in $k'$, $k_1$ a finite Galois extension containing $k''$.
Since $k'$ is a radiciel extension of $k''$, $k'\otimes_k k_1$ is a direct product of extensions radiciel of $k_1$; hence the canonical homomorphism $A_1'\to\prod(A'_1)_{\mathfrak{n}_i}$ is injective.
Since $A_1$ is integrally closed, $A_1'$ is an $A'$-module free of finite type; hence $A_1$ is an $A$-module flat, and the homomorphism is injective.
Setting $Y_1'=\Spec(B_1)$, $X_1'=X'\times_{Y'}Y_1'$, $\sh{F}_1'=\sh{F}'\otimes_{Y'}\sh{O}_{Y_1'}$: $\sh{F}_1'$ is $Y_1'$-flat at the points over $x'$; and as $A_1\to B_1$ is injective and local, if $(\sh{F}_1)_{x_1}$ is an $\sh{O}_{y_1}$-module flat, $\sh{F}_x$ is an $\sh{O}_y$-module flat $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.2.1).

\emph{IV) End of proof.}
One is finally reduced to proving \sref{IV.11.6.1} under the additional hypotheses:
(i) $A$ and $A'$ are local $\bb{Z}$-algebras of finite type (hence excellent rings \sref{IV.7.8.3});
(ii) $A$ is integrally closed;
(iii) $k'$ is a finite and radiciel extension of $k$.
Under (i) and (ii), the $\mathfrak{m}$-adic and $\mathfrak{m}'$-adic topologies on $A$ and $A'$ are induced by the completions.
The completed $\hat{u}:\hat{A}\to\hat{A}'$ is an injective homomorphism.
Since $\Spec(k')\to\Spec(k)$ is radiciel, there is only \emph{one} point $x'$ of $X'$ projecting to $x$ and $y'$.
One can therefore apply \sref{IV.11.5.2}.
C.Q.F.D.
\end{proof}

\oldpage[IV-3]{157}

\begin{corollary}[11.6.2]
\label{IV.11.6.2}
Let $A$ be a local integral ring unibranch, $Y=\Spec(A)$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Let $A'$ be a local ring, $A\to A'$ a local injective homomorphism; set $Y'=\Spec(A')$, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'}$.
Let $x$ be a point of $X$ with $f(x)=y$ the closed point of $Y$; suppose $\sh{F}'$ is $f'$-flat at all points $x'$ of $X'$ whose projections in $X$ and $Y'$ are $x$ and $y'$ respectively.
Then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
One reprises part I) of the proof of \sref{IV.11.6.1}, which shows (with the same notations) that if $\sh{F}_1$ is $f_1$-flat at \emph{all} points $x_1$ of $X_1$ whose projections in $X$ and $Y_1$ are $x$ and $y_1$, then $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at $x$; one is thus reduced to the case where $A$ is integrally closed, hence geometrically unibranch, and the conclusion follows from \sref{IV.11.6.1}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Counter-examples}
\label{subsection:IV.11.7}

\begin{env}[11.7.1]
\label{IV.11.7.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{157}
Consider first the case where $A$ is an \emph{Artinian} local ring and where the hypotheses of \sref{IV.11.4.11} are satisfied \emph{except} the condition (ii) on the residue field.
We shall see that the conclusion can then fail.
Let $k$ be a field admitting a Galois extension $k'$ of degree $[k':k]>1$, and denote by $\Gamma$ the Galois group.
Let $A$ be a $k$-algebra having a base of $3$ elements $1$, $a$, $b$ with the multiplication $a^2=ab=ba=b^2=0$, so that $A$ is an Artinian local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}=ka+kb$ of square zero.
Set $A'=A\otimes_k k'$, which is a local Artinian ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}A'$ of square zero; $A$ identifies canonically with a sub-ring of $A'$.
Let $\mathfrak{J}$ be the sub-$k'$-space of $\mathfrak{m}'$ generated by $a+\gamma b$, where $\gamma\in k'$ does not belong to $k$; it is clear that $\mathfrak{J}$ is an ideal of $A'$.
Set $B=A'/\mathfrak{J}$; this is an Artinian ring which is an $A$-module \emph{not flat}; for otherwise (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.~comm.}, chap.~II, \textsection\,3, n\textsuperscript{o}\,2, cor.~2 of prop.~5), $B$ would be an $A$-module free, and since the canonical homomorphism $A'/\mathfrak{m}A'\to B/\mathfrak{m}B$ is bijective, the canonical homomorphism $A'\to B=A'/\mathfrak{J}$ would also be bijective (\emph{loc.\ cit.}, prop.~6), which is absurd.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.7.2]
\label{IV.11.7.2}
We now show that the result of \sref{IV.11.5.5} loses its validity when one no longer supposes $f$ a morphism \emph{proper} (and it suffices not to suppose $g$ of finite type).
Let $X=\Spec(B)$, $e_x$ the generic point, $Y=\Spec(A)$, and take the curve $Y$ reducible formed by two ``coordinate axes'' in the affine plane $V_y^2=\Spec(A_0)$.
The ring $A$ admits two minimal primes $\mathfrak{p}_1$, $\mathfrak{p}_2$; setting $B=B_1\oplus B_2$ where $B_i=A/\mathfrak{p}_i$, then $B_1$ identifies with $k[T]$ and $B_2$ with $k[S]$, and the sum $Z=\Spec(B)$ is the \emph{normalised} of $Y$ with respect to $Y$ (\textbf{II}, 6.3.8).
The corresponding morphism $f:X\to Y$ is not flat, even though for each branch one can show flatness after base change to a valuation ring dominating one of the branches; moreover the first projection $p:X\times_Y X\to X$ is an \emph{isomorphism}, hence $p$ is flat, yet $f$ is not flat at $z_1$.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.7.3]
\label{IV.11.7.3}
The examples in \sref{IV.11.7.2} and \sref{IV.11.7.3} explain the restriction to \emph{unibranch} local rings in \sref{IV.11.6.1} and \sref{IV.11.6.2}, and one cannot in general replace the hypothesis that $A$ is unibranch but not geometrically unibranch.
Consider the complete local integral ring $A=\bb{R}[[U,V]]/(U^2+V^2)$ which is unibranch but not geometrically unibranch \sref{IV.6.15.11}.
The integral closure $\overline{A}=A[t]$ with $t=n/u$, $t^2=-1$, is isomorphic to $\bb{C}[[U]]$.
The two ideals $\mathfrak{p}'$, $\mathfrak{p}''$ generated by $u+iv$ and $u-iv$ are the minimal primes of the local complete ring $B$.
$B$ has two minimal primes $\mathfrak{p}'$, $\mathfrak{p}''$ and the ideal $\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{p}'+\mathfrak{p}''$ is the maximal ideal of $B$.
The morphism $f$ is not flat, although after base change to $A'=\overline{A}$, which is an integrally closed local ring with $A\to A'$ injective and local, the base-changed module is flat.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.7.4]
\label{IV.11.7.4}
One notes that in the preceding example, the homomorphism $u$ is \emph{injective} when $Y$ is \emph{irreducible} (one can take $Y=\Spec(k[S,T]/(S(S^2+T^2)-(S^2-T^2)))$), a ``cubic with a double point''); one can then replace $Y$ by an affine neighbourhood of $y$) so that $Y=\Spec(A)$, where $A$ is integral, whence $Z=\Spec(B)$, where $B$ is the integral closure of $A$; as $B$, $\hat{\sh{O}}_y$ and $\hat{\sh{O}}_{z_1}$ identify with sub-rings of the field of fractions.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.7.5]
\label{IV.11.7.5}
The examples in \sref{IV.11.7.2} and \sref{IV.11.7.3} explain the restriction to \emph{unibranch} local rings in \sref{IV.11.6.1} and \sref{IV.11.6.2}.
We now show that in \sref{IV.11.6.1}, one cannot weaken the hypothesis on $A$ to merely \emph{unibranche} (rather than geometrically unibranch).
Consider the complete local integral ring $A=\bb{R}[[U,V]]/(U^2+V^2)$, which is unibranche \sref{IV.6.15.11}.
The integral closure is $\overline{A}\cong\bb{C}[[U]]$; setting $X=\Spec(\overline{A})$, $Y=\Spec(A)$, $A'=\bb{C}[[U]]$: the base change $Y'=\Spec(A')\to Y$ satisfies all hypotheses of \sref{IV.11.6.1}, yet one can verify that $B=\overline{A}\otimes_{\bb{R}}\bb{C}$ is not $A$-flat.
\end{env}

\subsection{A valuative criterion of flatness}
\label{subsection:IV.11.8}

\oldpage[IV-3]{159}

\begin{theorem}[11.8.1]
\label{IV.11.8.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$.
Suppose $\sh{O}_y$ integral (resp.\ reduced and Noetherian).
For $\sh{F}$ to be $f$-flat at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every valuation ring (resp.\ every discrete valuation ring) $A'$ and every local homomorphism $\sh{O}_y\to A'$, the following condition be satisfied: setting $Y'=\Spec(A')$, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}$, $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y'}$, the $\sh{O}_{X'}$-Module $\sh{F}'$ is $f'$-flat at all points $x'$ of $X'$ whose projections in $X$ and $Y'$ are $x$ and the closed point $y'$ of $Y'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The condition is evidently necessary \sref{IV.2.1.4}, it remains to prove it is sufficient.
One can evidently ((\textbf{I}, 3.6.5) and (\textbf{I}, 2.4.4)) restrict to the case where $Y=\Spec(A)$ and $y$ the closed point of $Y$.

\emph{(i) Case where $A$ is integral.}
Let $K$ be the field of fractions of $A$, $A_1$ the integral closure of $A$; setting $Y_1=\Spec(A_1)$, $X_1=X\times_Y Y_1$, $f_1=f_{(Y_1)}$, by virtue of \sref{IV.11.5.5}, it suffices to show that $\sh{F}_1=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y_1}$ is $f_1$-flat at \emph{all} points $x_1$ of $X_1$ projecting to $x$ in $X$ and to $y_1$ in $Y_1$.
Now, if $f_1(x_1)=y_1$, where $\mathfrak{m}_1$ is an ideal prime of $A_1$ above $\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{i}_y$ (which is necessarily a maximal ideal), $\sh{O}_{y_1}=(A_1)_{\mathfrak{m}_1}$ is integrally closed.
Let $A'$ be a valuation ring for $K$ dominating $\sh{O}_{y_1}$; the homomorphism $A\to\sh{O}_{y_1}$ being local, $A\to A'$ is also local.
There exists then at least one point $x'$ of $X'$ whose projections in $X_1$ and $Y'$ are $x_1$ and $y'$ respectively (\textbf{I}, 3.4.7); as by hypothesis $\sh{F}'$ is $f'$-flat at $x'$, and as $\sh{O}_{y'}$ is integrally closed, one can apply \sref{IV.11.6.1}, and one deduces that $\sh{F}_1$ is $f_1$-flat at the point $x_1$, whence the theorem in this case.

\emph{(ii) Case where $A$ is reduced and Noetherian.}
\oldpage[IV-3]{160}
Let $\mathfrak{p}_i$ $(1\leq i\leq m)$ be the minimal primes of $A$; as $A$ is reduced, it identifies canonically with a sub-ring of the product $\prod A_i=\prod A/\mathfrak{p}_i$, $A_i$ being local Noetherian integral.
Setting $Y_i=\Spec(A_i)$, $X_i=X\times_Y Y_i$, $\sh{F}_i=\sh{F}\otimes_Y\sh{O}_{Y_i}$, $f_i=f_{(Y_i)}$: by \sref{IV.11.5.2}, it suffices to show that for each $i$, $\sh{F}_i$ is $f_i$-flat at every point $x_i$ whose projections in $X$ and $Y_i$ are $x$ and the closed point $y_i$.
Now as $A_i$ is Noetherian integral, there exists in its field of fractions $K_i$ a sub-ring $A'_i$ which is a semi-local Noetherian ring (hence a $A_i$-algebra \emph{finite}) and whose local rings are geometrically unibranch \sref{IV.6.15.5} and $(\mathbf{0}$, 23.2.5).
As the maximal ideals of $A'_i$ are necessarily above the maximal ideal of $A_i$, by \sref{IV.11.5.2} one deduces that for each $i$, one can apply \sref{IV.11.6.1} (using (\textbf{I}, 3.4.7) and \sref{IV.11.5.2}) to deduce $\sh{F}_i$ is $f_i$-flat at $x_i$, then the result by \sref{IV.11.5.2}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[11.8.2]
\label{IV.11.8.2}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item In the statement of \sref{IV.11.8.1}, one can restrict to valuation rings $A'$ that are \emph{complete} and whose residue field is \emph{algebraically closed}.
    Indeed, one knows that every such valuation ring $A'$ is dominated by a valuation ring $A''$ (\textbf{II}, 7.1.2), and that if $A'$ is a discrete valuation ring, one can suppose $A''$ is also a discrete valuation ring $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.3.1).
  \item The proof of \sref{IV.11.8.1} simplifies when one supposes not only that $A$ is integral and Noetherian, but that its completion $\hat{A}$ is also \emph{integral}.
    Replacing $X$ by $X\otimes_A\hat{A}$ and reasoning as in the proof of \sref{IV.11.5.3}, one can indeed suppose $A=\sh{O}_y$ integral, Noetherian and \emph{complete}.
    Now, one knows (\textbf{II}, 7.1.7) that every such ring $A$ is dominated by a discrete valuation ring; the conclusion then follows directly from \sref{IV.11.5.2}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\subsection{Separating and universally separating families of homomorphisms of sheaves of modules}
\label{subsection:IV.11.9}

\oldpage[IV-3]{160}

\begin{env}[11.9.1]
\label{IV.11.9.1}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $(f_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ a family of morphisms $f_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to X$, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module; for every $\lambda\in L$, let $\sh{G}_\lambda$ be a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{Z_\lambda}$-Module and
\[
\label{IV.11.9.1.1}\tag{11.9.1.1}
  u_\lambda:\sh{F}\to(f_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}_\lambda)
\]
a homomorphism.
We say that the family $(u_\lambda)$ (or the corresponding family of $u^*_\lambda:(f_\lambda)^*(\sh{F})\to\sh{G}_\lambda$) is \emph{separating} if the intersection of the kernels of the $u_\lambda$ is \emph{zero}.
In other words, for every open $U$ of $X$, and every section $t$ of $\sh{F}$ over $U$, such that for \emph{every} $\lambda$, the section $u_\lambda(t)$ (which, by definition, is a section of $\sh{G}_\lambda$ over $f_\lambda^{-1}(U)$) is zero, then $t$ is itself zero.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.9.2]
\label{IV.11.9.2}
With the notations of \sref{IV.11.9.1}, let $M$ be a second index set; for every $\lambda\in L$, let $(g_{\lambda\mu})_{\mu\in M}$ be a family of morphisms $g_{\lambda\mu}:Z'_{\lambda\mu}\to Z_\lambda$; for every pair $(\lambda,\mu)$, let $\sh{H}_{\lambda\mu}$ be a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{Z'_{\lambda\mu}}$-Module and $v_{\lambda\mu}:\sh{G}_\lambda\to(g_{\lambda\mu})_*(\sh{H}_{\lambda\mu})$ a homomorphism; set $h_{\lambda\mu}=f_\lambda\circ g_{\lambda\mu}:Z'_{\lambda\mu}\to X$ and consider the composed homomorphism
\[
  w_{\lambda\mu}:\sh{F}\xrightarrow{u_\lambda}(f_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}_\lambda)\xrightarrow{(f_\lambda)_*(v_{\lambda\mu})}(h_{\lambda\mu})_*(\sh{H}_{\lambda\mu}).
\]
Suppose that, for every $\lambda\in L$, the family $(v_{\lambda\mu})_{\mu\in M}$ is separating: then, as is immediately seen, the family $(w_{\lambda\mu})_{(\lambda,\mu)\in L\times M}$ is also separating, and conversely if $(u_\lambda)$ is separating then it suffices for the family of the $(f_\lambda)_*(v_{\lambda\mu})$ to be separating.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.9.3]
\label{IV.11.9.3}
To verify that the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating (with the notations of \sref{IV.11.9.1}), one can evidently first reduce to the case where $X$ is \emph{affine}, and then assume $Z_\lambda=X$ for every $\lambda\in L$.
Moreover, by \sref{IV.11.9.2} and \sref{IV.11.9.5}, if $L$ is \emph{finite} and the $f_\lambda$ \emph{quasi-compact}, one can, in the preceding reduction, also take the $M_\lambda$ to be \emph{finite}.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.9.4]
\label{IV.11.9.4}
Consider the case where $Z_\lambda=X$ for every $\lambda$, and $X=\Spec(A)$ is affine; then $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$ and $\sh{G}_\lambda=\widetilde{N}_\lambda$, where $M$ and $N_\lambda$ are $A$-modules, and $u_\lambda=\widetilde{\varphi_\lambda}$, where the $\varphi_\lambda:M\to N_\lambda$ are $A$-homomorphisms.
To say that the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating then means that, for every $s\in A$, the intersection of the kernels of the $(\varphi_\lambda)_s:M_s\to(N_\lambda)_s$ is reduced to $0$.
We say that the family $(\varphi_\lambda)$ is also \emph{separating}.
Note that if $L$ is \emph{finite}, this is the same as saying that $\bigcap_{\lambda\in L}\Ker(\varphi_\lambda)=0$, since $(\bigcap\Ker(\varphi_\lambda))_s=\bigcap\Ker((\varphi_\lambda)_s)$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 1.3.2).
However, this relation is no longer exact in general when $L$ is \emph{infinite}.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.9.5]
\label{IV.11.9.5}
\oldpage[IV-3]{162}
We now principally consider the problem of \emph{base change} for separating families.
With the notations of \sref{IV.11.9.1}, consider a morphism $g:X'\to X$ and set $\sh{F}'=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_X}\sh{O}_{X'}=g^*(\sh{F})$, and, for every $\lambda$, $Z'_\lambda=Z_\lambda\times_X X'$, $f'_\lambda=f_\lambda\times 1$, $\sh{G}'_\lambda=\sh{G}_\lambda\otimes_{\sh{O}_X}\sh{O}_{X'}=g^*_\lambda(\sh{G}_\lambda)$, where $g'_\lambda:Z'_\lambda\to Z_\lambda$ is the projection canonique.
For every $\lambda$, denote by $u'_\lambda:\sh{F}'\to(f'_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}'_\lambda)$ the homomorphism obtained from $u_\lambda$ by base change $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 4.4.3.2) corresponding to $j_\lambda$.
Then $u'_\lambda$ is defined as the composite
\[
  g^*(\sh{F})\xrightarrow{g^*(u_\lambda)}g^*((f_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}_\lambda))\xrightarrow{g^*(\psi)}g_*(((f'_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}'_\lambda)))=(f'_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}'_\lambda)
\]
where $\rho_{g'_\lambda}:\sh{G}'_\lambda\to(j_{\lambda\mu})_*(j^*_{\lambda\mu}(\sh{G}'_\lambda))$ is the canonical homomorphism $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 4.4.3.2) corresponding to $j_\lambda$.
When $f_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to X$ is an affine morphism, in the case where $\sh{G}_\lambda=\sh{O}_{Z_\lambda}$ and $u_\lambda$ is the homomorphism $\rho_{\sh{F}}:\sh{F}\to(j_\lambda)_*((j_\lambda)^*(\sh{F}))=(j_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}_\lambda)$ corresponding to the injection canonical $j_\lambda$.
When $Z_\lambda$ is a sub-prescheme of $X$, $\sh{G}_\lambda=j^*_\lambda(\sh{F})$, and $u_\lambda$ is the homomorphism $\rho_{\sh{F}}:\sh{F}\to(j_\lambda)_*((j_\lambda)^*(\sh{F}))=(j_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}_\lambda)$ corresponding to $j_\lambda$.
\end{env}

\begin{env}[11.9.6]
\label{IV.11.9.6}
Under the conditions of \sref{IV.11.9.5}, suppose $X$ and $X'$ affine, and that one wants to prove that for every section $t'$ of $\sh{F}'$ over $X'$ whose images by all the $u'_\lambda$ are zero, $t'$ is itself zero.
Then, one can restrict to the case where $Z_\lambda=X$ for every $\lambda\in L$.
Indeed, with the notations of \sref{IV.11.9.3} and \sref{IV.11.9.5}, if one sets $Y'_{\lambda\mu}=g^{-1}_\lambda(Y_{\lambda\mu})$, $j'_{\lambda\mu}:Y'_{\lambda\mu}\to Z'_\lambda$ the injection canonical and $v'_{\lambda\mu}:\sh{G}'_\lambda\to(j'_{\lambda\mu})_*((j'_{\lambda\mu})^*(\sh{G}'_\lambda))$ the homomorphism deduced from $v_{\lambda\mu}$ by base change; by \sref{IV.11.9.2}, it is immediate that for each $\lambda\in L$, the family $(v_{\lambda\mu})_{\mu\in M_\lambda}$ is separating.
One is thus reduced to proving that the family of the composed homomorphisms $((f_\lambda)_*(v_{\lambda\mu}))\circ u_\lambda$ is separating, which, since the $(f_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}_\lambda)$ are quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Modules (\textbf{I}, 1.6.2) and by the definition, one can replace $Z_\lambda$ by $X$ and $\sh{G}_\lambda$ by $(f_\lambda)_*(\sh{G}_\lambda)$, whence our assertion.

Note that if $L$ is \emph{finite} and the $f_\lambda$ \emph{quasi-compact}, one can also take the $M_\lambda$ finite, hence one reduces to the case of verifying that a \emph{finite} family of homomorphisms of $\sh{F}$ into quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Modules is separating.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[11.9.7]
\label{IV.11.9.7}
With the notations of \sref{IV.11.9.1} and \sref{IV.11.9.5}, suppose $X=\Spec(A)$ and $X'=\Spec(A')$ affine, and further that $A'$ is an $A$-module \emph{projective}.
Then, if the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating, every section $t'$ of $\sh{F}'$ over $X'$ whose images by all the $u'_\lambda$ are zero is itself zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \sref{IV.11.9.6} one can restrict to $Z_\lambda=X$ for every $\lambda$.
The proposition is then a consequence of the following:
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[11.9.7.1]
\label{IV.11.9.7.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $(M_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ a family of $A$-modules, $M$ an $A$-module and for each $\lambda$, $u_\lambda:M\to M_\lambda$ an $A$-homomorphism.
Suppose the intersection of the kernels of the $u_\lambda$ is reduced to $0$.
Then, for every projective $A$-module $N$, the intersection of the kernels of the homomorphisms $u_\lambda\otimes 1:M\otimes_A N\to M_\lambda\otimes_A N$ is reduced to $0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$N$ being a direct factor of a free $A$-module $L$, it suffices to prove the result for $N=L$, which is trivial since the restriction of $v_\lambda$ is trivially the kernel of $u_\lambda\otimes 1_L$.
The assertion then follows trivially from the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[11.9.8]
\label{IV.11.9.8}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.11.9.7}, we do not know if the family $(u'_\lambda)$ is \emph{separating}: one would need to prove that a section $t'$ of $\sh{F}'$ over an open $\mathrm{D}(h')\subset X'$ (where $h'\in A'$) whose images by all $u'_\lambda$ are zero, is zero.
However, one cannot apply \sref{IV.11.9.4} to $\mathrm{D}(h')=\Spec(A'_{h'})$, since $A'$ being projective does not imply $A'_{h'}$ is projective (or even free) as an $A$-module.
\end{remarks}

\begin{corollary}[11.9.9]
\label{IV.11.9.9}
Let $X$ be an Artinian prescheme, $g:X'\to X$ a flat morphism (one notes that these two conditions are satisfied when $X$ is the spectrum of a field and $g$ an arbitrary morphism).
Then, with the notations of \sref{IV.11.9.1} and \sref{IV.11.9.5}, if the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating, so is the family $(u'_\lambda)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
One can restrict to $X=\Spec(A)$, the spectrum of an Artinian local ring $A$ (\textbf{I}, 6.2.2); one notes then that for every affine open $U'=\Spec(A')$ of $X'$, $A'$ is an $A$-module flat, hence \emph{projective} $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.1.3).
It suffices then to apply \sref{IV.11.9.7}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[11.9.10]
\label{IV.11.9.10}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $(u_\lambda)$ a family of homomorphisms \sref{IV.11.9.1.1}, $(u'_\lambda)$ the family of homomorphisms deduced from $(u_\lambda)$ by the base change $g$ \sref{IV.11.9.5}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $g$ is a faithfully flat morphism and if the family $(u'_\lambda)$ is separating, then the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating.
  \item Conversely, suppose that $g:X'\to X$ is a flat morphism, that $\sh{F}$ is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite type, and that the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating.
    Then the family $(u'_\lambda)$ is separating.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assertion (i) follows immediately from the faithful flatness of $g$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.2).
For (ii), one can restrict to $X$ and $X'$ affine, and then to the case $Z_\lambda=X$ for every $\lambda$ \sref{IV.11.9.6}.
Then $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$, $\sh{G}_\lambda=\widetilde{N}_\lambda$, $u_\lambda=\widetilde{\varphi_\lambda}$ with $M$ of finite type.
As $g$ is flat, $M'=M\otimes_A A'$ is a flat $A$-module, and $(\varphi_\lambda\otimes 1)$ have intersection of kernels equal to $(\bigcap\Ker\varphi_\lambda)\otimes_A A'=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[11.9.11]
\label{IV.11.9.11}
\oldpage[IV-3]{165}
It should be noted that if the family of homomorphisms $u_\lambda:\sh{F}\to\sh{G}_\lambda$ is separating, \emph{it does not necessarily follow} that, for every $x\in X$, the intersection of the kernels of the homomorphisms $(u_\lambda)_x:\sh{F}_x\to(\sh{G}_\lambda)_x$ is reduced to $0$.
For example, let $X$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, of Jacobson dimension $\geq 1$, and $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module; for every closed point $x$ of $X$, and every integer $n\geq 0$, $\mathfrak{m}_x^n\sh{F}$ is a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of support contained in $\{x\}$.
The family of canonical homomorphisms $\sh{F}\to\sh{F}/\mathfrak{m}_x^n\sh{F}$ (where $x$ ranges over the set $X_0$ of closed points of $X$ and $n$ the set of integers $\geq 0$) is \emph{separating}: indeed, if $t$ is a section of $\sh{F}$ over an open $U$ of $X$ whose images in all the $\Gamma(U,\sh{F}/\mathfrak{m}_x^n\sh{F})$ are zero, it follows immediately that for every closed point $x\in U$, we have $t_x=0$; as the set of closed points contained in $U$ is very dense in $U$, this implies $t=0$ (10.2.1).
However, if $\sh{F}=\sh{O}_X$, and if $y\in X$ is a non-closed point of $X$, we take $\sh{O}_{X,y}\neq 0$, but $(\sh{O}_X/\mathfrak{m}_x^n\sh{O}_X)_y=0$ for every closed point $x$ of $X$, and the intersection of the kernels of the homomorphisms $\sh{O}_{X,y}\to(\sh{O}_X/\mathfrak{m}_x^n\sh{O}_X)_y$ is equal to $\sh{O}_{X,y}$.
\end{remarks}

\begin{lemma}[11.9.12]
\label{IV.11.9.12}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.11.9.1} and \sref{IV.11.9.5}, suppose the family $(u_\lambda)$ separating; suppose in addition that $X$ is an $S$-prescheme, where $S=\Spec(A)$, and that $X'=X\times_S S'$, where $S'=\Spec(A')$, $A'$ being an $A$-algebra; suppose finally that the $\sh{G}_\lambda$ are $S$-flat.
Let $(A'_\alpha)_{\alpha\in I}$ be the filtering family of sub-$A$-algebras of $A'$ of finite type; for every $\alpha\in I$, set $S''_\alpha=\Spec(A'_\alpha)$, $X''_\alpha=X\times_S S''_\alpha$, $Z''_{\alpha\lambda}=Z_\lambda\times_X X''_\alpha$, and let $f''_{\alpha\lambda}$, $\sh{F}''_\alpha$, $\sh{G}''_{\alpha\lambda}$ and $u''_{\alpha\lambda}$ be the morphisms, Modules and homomorphisms of Modules deduced from $f_\lambda$, $\sh{F}$, $\sh{G}_\lambda$ and $u_\lambda$ by the base change $X''_\alpha\to X$.
Then, if, for every $\alpha\in I$, the family $(u''_{\alpha\lambda})_{\lambda\in L}$ is separating, so is the family $(u'_\lambda)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{166}
It suffices to prove that if a section $t'$ of $\sh{F}'$ over an affine open $U'$ of $X'$ is such that $u'_\lambda(t')=0$ for every $\lambda\in L$, then $t'=0$.
If $h_\alpha:X'\to X''_\alpha$ is the projection, by (8.2.11) there exist an index $\alpha$ and an open quasi-compact $U''_\alpha\subset X''_\alpha$ such that $U'=h_\alpha^{-1}(U''_\alpha)$; in addition, by (8.5.2, (i)), one can suppose that there is a section $t''_\alpha$ of $\sh{F}''_\alpha$ over $U''_\alpha$ such that $t'$ is the canonical image of $t''_\alpha$.
Replacing $S$ by $S''_\alpha$, $Z_\lambda$, $\sh{F}$, $\sh{G}_\lambda$ and $u_\lambda$ by $S''_\alpha$, $U''_\alpha$, $f''^{-1}_{\alpha\lambda}(U''_\alpha)$ and the restrictions of the corresponding modules and homomorphisms, one can thus suppose that $U'=X'$, that $t'$ is the canonical image of a section $t$ of $\sh{F}$ over $X$ and that the homomorphism $A\to A'$ is \emph{injective}.
But the composed homomorphism
\[
  \Gamma(X,\sh{F})\xrightarrow{\Gamma(u_\lambda)}\Gamma(Z_\lambda,\sh{G}_\lambda)\xrightarrow{\Gamma(\rho)}\Gamma(Z'_\lambda,\sh{G}'_\lambda)
\]
is equal by definition to the composed homomorphism
\[
  \Gamma(X,\sh{F})\xrightarrow{\Gamma(\rho)}\Gamma(X',\sh{F}')\xrightarrow{\Gamma(u'_\lambda)}\Gamma(Z'_\lambda,\sh{G}'_\lambda)
\]
hence the image of $t$ by these composed homomorphisms is $u'_\lambda(t')=0$; by the injectivity of the homomorphism $\Gamma(Z_\lambda,\sh{G}_\lambda)\xrightarrow{\Gamma(\rho)}\Gamma(Z'_\lambda,\sh{G}'_\lambda)$ one concludes that $u_\lambda(t)=0$ for every $\lambda\in L$, hence $t=0$, and finally $t'=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.9.13]
\label{IV.11.9.13}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.11.9.1} and \sref{IV.11.9.5}, suppose that $X$ is a prescheme over a field $k$, and that, setting $S=\Spec(k)$, one has $X'=X\times_S S'$, where $S'$ is an arbitrary $k$-prescheme.
Then, if the family $(u_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ is separating, so is the family $(u'_\lambda)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One can restrict to the case where $S'=\Spec(A')$ is affine.
If $A'$ is a $k$-algebra of finite type, the morphism $g:X'\to X$ is flat and of finite presentation, so one is in the conditions of application of \sref{IV.11.9.10}, (ii), $b)$).
In the general case, one considers $A'$ as the inductive limit of its sub-$k$-algebras $A'_\alpha$ of finite type, and one applies to each $A'_\alpha$ the result of \sref{IV.11.9.10}, (ii), $b)$); one then concludes with the aid of the lemma \sref{IV.11.9.12}, since the $\sh{G}_\lambda$ are $S$-flat.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[11.9.14]
\label{IV.11.9.14}
Let us keep the notations of \sref{IV.11.9.1} and \sref{IV.11.9.5} and suppose that $X$ is an $S$-prescheme.
If for every base change $g:X\times_S S'\to X$, where $S'\to S$ is an \emph{arbitrary} morphism, the corresponding family $(u'_\lambda)$ is separating, we say that the family $(u_\lambda)$ is \emph{universally separating relative to $S$}.
When the family $(u_\lambda)$ is reduced to a single element $u$, we also say that $u$ is \emph{universally injective}, relative to $S$.
It is clear that for every morphism $h:S'\to S$, the corresponding family $(u'_\lambda)$ is universally separating relative to $S'$; conversely, if $h$ is \emph{faithfully flat} and if $(u'_\lambda)$ is universally separating relative to $S'$, then $(u_\lambda)$ is universally separating relative to $S$, as follows immediately from \sref{IV.11.9.10}, (i)) and the fact that for every morphism $S''\to S$, the corresponding morphism $S''\times_S S'\to S''$ is faithfully flat.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[11.9.15]
\label{IV.11.9.15}
\oldpage[IV-3]{167}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.11.9.1}, suppose that $X$ is an $S$-prescheme, the $\sh{G}_\lambda$ being $S$-flat.
Let $S_0$ be a closed sub-prescheme of $S$ defined by a quasi-coherent nilpotent Ideal $\sh{J}$ of $\sh{O}_S$, such that the $(\sh{O}_S/\sh{J})$-Modules $\sh{J}^k/\sh{J}^{k+1}$ are all locally free.
Let $(u_{\lambda 0})$ be the family of homomorphisms obtained from the base change $X\leftarrow X_0=X\times_S S_0$.
Then, if the family $(u_{\lambda 0})$ is separating (resp.\ universally separating relative to $S_0$), the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating (resp.\ universally separating relative to $S$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The assertion relative to universally separating families is a consequence of the assertion relative to separating families.
To prove the latter, one can \sref{IV.11.9.3} reduce to the case where $S=\Spec(A)$, $X=\Spec(B)$ are affine, $Z_\lambda=X$ for every $\lambda$, $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$, $\sh{G}_\lambda=\widetilde{N}_\lambda$, where $M$ and the $N_\lambda$ are $B$-modules, the $N_\lambda$ being $A$-modules flat.
The question being local on $X$ and $S$, it suffices to verify that if $t\in M$ is such that $u_\lambda(t)=0$ for every $\lambda$, then $t=0$.
One has $\sh{J}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}$, where $\mathfrak{J}$ is a nilpotent ideal of $A$.
Suppose $\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}=0$ and argue by induction on $n$, the assertion being trivially true for $n=0$.
If $\bar{t}$ is the class of $t$ in $M/\mathfrak{J}^n M$, then, by the induction hypothesis, the class of $u_\lambda(t)$ in $N_\lambda/\mathfrak{J}^n N_\lambda$ is zero for every $\lambda$, hence $\bar{t}=0$, that is $t\in\mathfrak{J}^n M$.
But $\mathfrak{J}^n/\mathfrak{J}^{n+1}$ is a free $(A/\mathfrak{J})$-module; if $(e_\alpha)$ is a base of this module, one can write $t=\sum_\alpha e_\alpha t^0_\alpha$, with $t^0_\alpha\in A/\mathfrak{J}$.
Since $N_\lambda$ is an $A$-module flat, $\mathfrak{J}^n N_\lambda$ identifies with $\mathfrak{J}^n\otimes_{A/\mathfrak{J}}(N_\lambda/\mathfrak{J} N_\lambda)$ and one can write $u_\lambda(t)=\sum_\alpha e_\alpha\otimes u_{\lambda 0}(t^0_\alpha)$.
Since by hypothesis $u_\lambda(t)=0$, one deduces that $u_{\lambda 0}(t^0_\alpha)=0$ for every $\alpha$ and every $\lambda$; hence $t^0_\alpha=0$ since the family $(u_{\lambda 0})$ is separating, and consequently $t=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[11.9.16]
\label{IV.11.9.16}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.11.9.1}, suppose that $X$ is an $S$-prescheme locally Noetherian and that the $\sh{G}_\lambda$ are $S$-flat.
For every $s\in S$, let $((u_\lambda)_s)_{\lambda\in L}$ be the family obtained from $(u_\lambda)$ by the base change $X_s=X\times_S\Spec(\kappa(s))\to X$.
Then, for the family $(u_\lambda)$ to be universally separating relative to $S$, it is necessary and sufficient that for every $s\in S$, the family $((u_\lambda)_s)$ is separating.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The necessity of the condition follows trivially from the definitions.
Conversely, suppose the condition of the statement verified, and let us prove first that the family $(u_\lambda)$ is separating.
One can \sref{IV.11.9.3} reduce to the case where $S=\Spec(A)$, $X=\Spec(B)$ are affine, $Z_\lambda=X$ for every $\lambda$, $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$, $\sh{G}_\lambda=\widetilde{N}_\lambda$, where $B$ is Noetherian, $M$ is a $B$-module of finite type and the $N_\lambda$ are $A$-modules flat, and to show that, if $t\in M$ is such that $u_\lambda(t)=0$ for every $\lambda$, then $t=0$.
To show that $t=0$, it suffices to prove that for every maximal ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $B$, the image $t_\mathfrak{p}$ of $t$ in $M_\mathfrak{p}$ is zero (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.~comm.}, chap.~II, \textsection~3, n\textsuperscript{o}~3, cor.~1 du th.~1).
One can thus also suppose that $A$ is a local ring, and that $M$ is a $B$-module of finite type, the intersection of $\mathfrak{m}^n M$ being reduced to $0$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 7.3.5), hence it suffices to prove that the family deduced from $(u_\lambda)$ by the base change $\Spec(B/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}B)\to\Spec(B)$ is separating, which since the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$ is nilpotent, is precisely the conditions of application of \sref{IV.11.9.15}, hence the conclusion.

\oldpage[IV-3]{168}
Now let $h:S'\to S$ be a morphism base change, and $(u'_\lambda)$ the family obtained from $(u_\lambda)$ by the base change $h':X\times_S S'\to X$; let us prove that $(u'_\lambda)$ is also separating.
Suppose first that $h$ is \emph{locally of finite type}; then $X\times_S S'$ is locally Noetherian; moreover, if $s'\in S'$ is above $s\in S$, then $X'_{s'}=X_s\otimes_{\kappa(s)}\kappa(s')$, and by \sref{IV.11.9.13} applied to $X_s$ and to $X'_{s'}=X_s\times_{S_s} S'_{s'}$, the family $(u'_\lambda)_{s'}$ is separating; one can thus conclude by the first part of the proof that $(u'_\lambda)$ is separating.

Finally, if $h$ is arbitrary, one can evidently restrict to the case where $S=\Spec(A)$ and $S'=\Spec(A')$ are affine, and consider $A'$ as the inductive limit of its sub-$A$-algebras of finite type.
As the $\sh{G}_\lambda$ are $S$-flat, it suffices to apply the preceding argument and the lemma \sref{IV.11.9.12} to conclude.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.9.17]
\label{IV.11.9.17}
Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation and $f$-flat, $U$ an open of $X$, $j:U\to X$ the canonical injection, $u:\sh{F}\to j_*(j^*(\sh{F}))$ the canonical homomorphism $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 4.4.3.2).
For every $s\in S$, let $X_s$ be the fibre $X\times_S\Spec(\kappa(s))$, $U_s$ the open $U\cap X_s$ of $X_s$, $\sh{F}_s=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_S}\kappa(s)$, $j_s:U_s\to X_s$ the canonical injection, $u_s:\sh{F}_s\to(j_s)_*((j_s)^*(\sh{F}_s))$ the canonical homomorphism.
For $u$ to be universally injective relative to $S$, it is necessary and sufficient that $u_s$ be injective for every $s\in S$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to prove the sufficiency of the condition.
When $X$ is locally Noetherian, the proposition is an immediate corollary of \sref{IV.11.9.16}.
We shall reduce to this case in two steps, restricting ourselves, as one evidently can, to the case where $S=\Spec(A)$ and $X$ are affine.

\medskip\noindent\emph{A) Case where $U$ is quasi-compact.} ---
We shall use the following lemma:
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[11.9.17.1]
\label{IV.11.9.17.1}
Under the general hypotheses of \sref{IV.11.9.17}, and in addition assuming $S$ and $X$ affine and $U$ quasi-compact, the set $E$ of $s\in S$ such that $u_s$ is injective is constructible.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, the fibres $X_s$ are locally Noetherian preschemes, hence $E$ can also, by (5.10.2), be defined as the set of $s\in S$ such that $\operatorname{Ass}(\sh{F}_s)\subset U_s$.
Note in addition that $U$, being quasi-compact in an affine scheme, the verification of condition (9.2.1, (i)) follows also from (4.2.7); on the other hand, the verification of condition (9.2.1, (ii)) is done easily using the study of prime cycles associated to the neighbourhood of the generic fibre (9.8.3), as well as (9.5.2) and (9.5.3).

This lemma established, one can, by (8.9.1) and (8.2.11), suppose that there exists a Noetherian sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$, a prescheme of finite type $X_0$ over $S_0=\Spec(A_0)$, an open $U_0$ of $X_0$ and an $\sh{O}_{X_0}$-Module coherent $\sh{F}_0$ such that $X=X_0\times_{S_0} S$ and $\sh{F}=p_0^*(\sh{F}_0)$, where $p_0:X\to X_0$ is the projection.
Let $(A_\alpha)$ be the filtering family of sub-$A$-algebras of $A$ which are $A_0$-algebras of finite type, and set $X_\alpha=X_0\times_{S_0} S_\alpha$, $U_\alpha=U_0\times_{S_0} S_\alpha$, $\sh{F}_\alpha=\sh{F}_0\otimes_{S_0} S_\alpha$; let $u_\alpha$ the canonical homomorphism relative to $\sh{F}_\alpha$ and $U_\alpha$, defined as in \sref{IV.11.9.17}.
For every $s\in S$, the hypothesis that $u_s$ is injective implies also \sref{IV.11.9.10}, (i)), where $s_\alpha=q_\alpha(s)$ is the image of $s$ by the morphism $q_\alpha:S\to S_\alpha$.
If $E_\alpha$ is the set of $s_\alpha\in S_\alpha$ such that $(u_\lambda)_{s_\alpha}$ is injective, one has $S=q_\alpha^{-1}(E_\alpha)$, and the $E_\alpha$ form a projective system of sets.
But the lemma \sref{IV.11.9.17.1} applied to $u_\alpha$, shows that $E_\alpha$ is constructible in $S_\alpha$; one deduces hence (8.3.4) that there exists an index $\alpha$ such that $E_\alpha=S_\alpha$.
But then, since $X_\alpha$ is Noetherian, $u_\alpha$ is universally injective by \sref{IV.11.9.16}, hence so is $u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[11.9.17.2]
\label{IV.11.9.17.2}
\emph{B) General case.} ---
The open $U$ is the union of a filtering increasing family of quasi-compact opens $U_\lambda$; if $j_\lambda:U_\lambda\to X$ is the canonical injection and $u_\lambda:\sh{F}\to(j_\lambda)_*((j_\lambda)^*(\sh{F}))$ the corresponding homomorphism, the set $E_\lambda$ of $s\in S$ such that $(u_\lambda)_s$ is injective is constructible.
On the other hand, for a given $s\in S$, saying that $u_s$ (resp.\ $(u_\lambda)_s$) is injective means that $\operatorname{Ass}(\sh{F}_s)\subset U\cap X_s$ (resp.\ $\operatorname{Ass}(\sh{F}_s)\subset U_\lambda\cap X_s$) (5.10.2).
Since $\operatorname{Ass}(\sh{F}_s)$ is finite (3.1.6), saying that $u_s$ is injective thus means that there exists $\lambda$ such that $s\in E_\lambda$; the hypothesis thus implies $S=\bigcup_\lambda E_\lambda$.
By (1.9.9), there exists an index $\lambda$ such that $S=E_\lambda$, from which one concludes by the first part of the argument that $u_\lambda$ is also universally injective.
It follows then from the factorisation of $u_\lambda$:
\[
  \sh{F}\xrightarrow{u}j_*(\sh{F}|U)\to(j_\lambda)_*(\sh{F}|U_\lambda)
\]
that $u$ is also universally injective.
\end{env}

\begin{remarks}[11.9.18]
\label{IV.11.9.18}
\oldpage[IV-3]{169}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $\sh{F}$, $\sh{G}$ two quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Modules, $\sh{G}$ being in addition supposed \emph{locally free}, $u:\sh{F}\to\sh{G}$ a homomorphism.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item for every morphism $g:X'\to X$, the homomorphism $g^*(u):g^*(\sh{F})\to g^*(\sh{G})$ is injective;
  \item for every $x\in X$, the homomorphism $u\otimes 1_{\kappa(x)}:\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_x}\kappa(x)\to\sh{G}\otimes_{\sh{O}_x}\kappa(x)$ is injective;
  \item for every $x\in X$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $u|U:\sh{F}|U\to\sh{G}|U$ is an isomorphism of $\sh{F}|U$ onto a direct factor of $\sh{G}|U$.
\end{enumerate}
Indeed, it is clear that $c)$ implies $a)$ and that $a)$ implies $b)$.
The fact that $b)$ implies $c)$ follows from ($\mathbf{0}$, 19.1.12), $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 5.2.5) and $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 5.5.5).
When the preceding equivalent conditions are verified, we say that $u$ is \emph{universally injective}.
\end{remarks}

\subsection{Schematically dominant families of morphisms and schematically dense families of sub-preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.11.10}

\oldpage[IV-3]{170}

\begin{proposition}[11.10.1]
\label{IV.11.10.1}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $(Z_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ a family of preschemes, and for every $\lambda\in L$, let $f_\lambda=(\psi_\lambda,\theta_\lambda):Z_\lambda\to X$ be a morphism.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item The family of homomorphisms $\theta_\lambda:\sh{O}_X\to(f_\lambda)_*(\sh{O}_{Z_\lambda})$ is separating \sref{IV.11.9.1} (in other words the intersection of the kernels of the $\theta_\lambda$ is \emph{zero}).
  \item For every open $U$ of $X$, every section $t$ of $\sh{O}_X$ over $U$ whose images by all the canonical homomorphisms
  \[
  \label{IV.11.10.1.1}\tag{11.10.1.1}
    (\theta_\lambda)_U:\Gamma(U,\sh{O}_X)\to\Gamma(f_\lambda^{-1}(U),\sh{O}_{Z_\lambda})
  \]
  are zero, is itself zero.
  \item For every open $U$ of $X$, and every closed sub-prescheme $Y$ of $U$ such that for every $\lambda\in L$, there exists a factorisation
  \[
  \label{IV.11.10.1.2}\tag{11.10.1.2}
    f_\lambda^{-1}(U)\xrightarrow{g_\lambda}Y\xrightarrow{j}U
  \]
  of the restriction $f_\lambda^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f_\lambda$ (where $j$ is the canonical injection), one has $Y=U$.
\end{enumerate}
If moreover $X$ is an $S$-prescheme, these conditions are also equivalent to the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
  \item For every separated morphism $g:X'\to S$ and every couple of $S$-morphisms $u$, $v$ of an open $U$ of $X$ into $X'$, such that for every $\lambda$, the composites of $u$ and $v$ with the morphism $f_\lambda^{-1}(U)\to U$, restriction of $f_\lambda$, are equal, one has $u=v$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence of $a)$ and $b)$ follows from the definitions.
To see that $b)$ implies $c)$, it suffices to consider the quasi-coherent Ideal $\sh{J}$ of $\sh{O}_U$ defining $Y$, and to note that, for every open $V\subset U$, the hypothesis implies that the morphism $(\theta_\lambda)_V$ factorises through
\[
  \Gamma(V,\sh{O}_U)\to\Gamma(V,\sh{O}_U/\sh{J})\to\Gamma(f_\lambda^{-1}(V),\sh{O}_{Z_\lambda}).
\]
One concludes that every section $t$ of $\sh{J}$ over $V$ has image $0$ in all the $\Gamma(f_\lambda^{-1}(V),\sh{O}_{Z_\lambda})$, hence, by $b)$, $\sh{J}=0$ and $Y=U$.
Conversely, if $c)$ is verified, for proving $b)$, it suffices, for every section $t$ of $\sh{O}_U$ whose images by all the $(\theta_\lambda)_U$ are zero, to apply $c)$ to the closed sub-prescheme $Y$ of $U$ defined by the Ideal $t\sh{O}_U$: the hypothesis that the images of $t$ by the $(\theta_\lambda)_U$ are all zero implies that one has factorisations \sref{IV.11.10.1.2} for every $\lambda$ (\textbf{I}, 4.1.9).
To prove that $c)$ implies $d)$, it suffices to apply $c)$ to the closed sub-prescheme $Y$ of $U$, the image of the diagonal of $X'\times_S X'$ by $(u,v)_S$ and to use (\textbf{I}, 4.4.1).
Conversely, to deduce $b)$ from $d)$, one considers the $S$-scheme $X'=S\otimes_\mathbb{Z}\mathbb{Z}[T]$ ($T$ indeterminate) and one notes that the sections of $\sh{O}_U$ over $U$ correspond bijectively to $S$-morphisms $U\to S[T]$ (\textbf{I}, 3.3.15); to say that two sections of $\sh{O}_U$ have the same images by all the $(\theta_\lambda)_U$ is equivalent to saying that the composites of the two corresponding morphisms with all the morphisms $f_\lambda^{-1}(U)\to U$ are equal.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[11.10.2]
\label{IV.11.10.2}
\oldpage[IV-3]{171}
When the equivalent conditions of \sref{IV.11.10.1} are verified, we say that the family $(f_\lambda)$ is \emph{schematically dominant}.
When the $f_\lambda$ are the canonical immersions of a family $(Z_\lambda)$ of sub-preschemes of $X$, we also say that the family $(Z_\lambda)$ is \emph{schematically dense}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remarks}[11.10.3]
\label{IV.11.10.3}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The notion of schematically dominant family over $X$ is \emph{local} on $X$, as follows for example from condition $b)$ in \sref{IV.11.10.1}: if $(W_\alpha)$ is an open covering of $X$, the family $(f_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant if and only if each of the families formed by the morphisms $f_\lambda^{-1}(W_\alpha)\to W_\alpha$, restrictions of $f_\lambda$, is.
  \item If $Z$ is the prescheme coproduct of the $Z_\lambda$, $f:Z\to X$ the morphism coinciding with $f_\lambda$ on each $Z_\lambda$, saying that the family $(f_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant is equivalent to saying that the family reduced to the single element $f$ is schematically dominant.
  \item Let $M$ be a second index set and, for every $\lambda\in L$, let $(g_{\lambda\mu})_{\mu\in M}$ be a family of morphisms $g_{\lambda\mu}:Z'_{\lambda\mu}\to Z_\lambda$; if, for each $\lambda$, the family $(g_{\lambda\mu})_{\mu\in M}$ is schematically dominant, then it amounts to the same to say that the family $(f_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ is schematically dominant, or that the family $(f_\lambda\circ g_{\lambda\mu})_{(\lambda,\mu)\in L\times M}$ is \sref{IV.11.9.2}.
  \item Let $f:Z\to X$ be a morphism such that $f_*(\sh{O}_Z)$ is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module (for example a quasi-compact and quasi-separated morphism (1.7.4)).
  Then, saying that $f$ is schematically dominant means that the \emph{scheme-theoretic image} of $Z$ by $f$ (\textbf{I}, 9.5.3) is \emph{identical to $X$}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\begin{proposition}[11.10.4]
\label{IV.11.10.4}
If the family of morphisms $f_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to X$ is schematically dominant, the union of the $f_\lambda(Z_\lambda)$ is dense in $X$.
Conversely, if this union is dense in $X$ and if $X$ is \emph{reduced}, the family $(f_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows immediately from \sref{IV.11.10.1}, $b)$).
On the other hand, if $X$ is reduced, and if the union of the $f_\lambda(Z_\lambda)$ is dense in $X$, then, if one has for every $\lambda\in L$ factorisations \sref{IV.11.10.1.2} for every $\lambda\in L$, one also has factorisations
\[
  (f_\lambda^{-1}(U))_\mathrm{red}\xrightarrow{(g_\lambda)_\mathrm{red}} Y_\mathrm{red}\xrightarrow{j_\mathrm{red}} U,
\]
and the hypothesis implies that the underlying space of $Y$ is identical to $U$, hence $Y=Y_\mathrm{red}=U$ since $U$ is reduced.
\end{proof}

The results of n\textsuperscript{o}~11.9 on separating families translate into results on schematically dominant families:

\begin{theorem}[11.10.5]
\label{IV.11.10.5}
Let $(f_\lambda)$ be a family of morphisms $f_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to X$, $g:X'\to X$ a morphism, and set $Z'_\lambda=Z_\lambda\times_X X'$, $f'_\lambda=(f_\lambda)_{(X')}:Z'_\lambda\to X'$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $g$ is faithfully flat and if the family $(f'_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant, then the family $(f_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant.
  \item Suppose that $g$ is a flat morphism and in addition that one of the two following conditions is verified:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
    \item $L$ is finite and the $f_\lambda$ are quasi-compact.
    \item The morphism $g$ is locally of finite presentation.
  \end{enumerate}
  Then, if the family $(f_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant, so is the family $(f'_\lambda)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This follows from \sref{IV.11.9.1} applied to the corresponding separating families, via the equivalence established in \sref{IV.11.10.1} and \sref{IV.11.10.3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.10.6]
\label{IV.11.10.6}
\oldpage[IV-3]{172}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.11.10.5}, suppose that $X$ is a prescheme over a field $k$, and that, setting $S=\Spec(k)$, one has $X'=X\times_S S'$, where $S'$ is an arbitrary $k$-prescheme.
Then, if the family $(f_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant, so is the family $(f'_\lambda)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This follows from \sref{IV.11.9.2} by the translation established in \sref{IV.11.10.1} and \sref{IV.11.10.3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.10.7]
\label{IV.11.10.7}
Let $X$ be a prescheme over a field $k$, $(Z_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ a family of $k$-preschemes geometrically reduced over $k$, and for each $\lambda\in L$, let $f_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to X$ be a $k$-morphism.
Let $Y$ be the sub-prescheme reduced of $X$ having for underlying space the adherence of $\bigcup_{\lambda\in L}f_\lambda(Z_\lambda)$.
Let $k'$ be an extension of $k$, $X'$, $Z'_\lambda$, $f'_\lambda:Z'_\lambda\to X'$ the preschemes and morphisms deduced from $X$, $Z_\lambda$ and $f_\lambda$ by extension of the base to $k'$.
Then, if $Y'$ is the sub-prescheme reduced of $X'$ having for underlying space the adherence of $\bigcup_{\lambda\in L}f'_\lambda(Z'_\lambda)$, one has $Y'=Y\otimes_k k'$.
In particular, $Y$ is geometrically reduced over $k$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
As the $Z_\lambda$ are reduced, the morphisms $f_\lambda$ factorise as $Z_\lambda\xrightarrow{g_\lambda}Y\xrightarrow{j}X$, where $j$ is the canonical injection (\textbf{I}, 5.2.2).
It follows from \sref{IV.11.10.4} that $(g_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant.
Moreover, by hypothesis the $Z'_\lambda$ are \emph{reduced}; it follows from \sref{IV.11.10.6} that the family $(g'_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant.
Setting $Y'_1=Y\otimes_k k'$, the closed sub-prescheme of $X'$, and since $f'_\lambda$ factorises as $Z'_\lambda\to Y'_1\to X'$, and since the $g'_\lambda$ factorise through $Z'_\lambda\xrightarrow{g'_\lambda}Y'_1\xrightarrow{j'}X'$ by (\textbf{I}, 5.2.2), we have $Y'\subset Y'_1$.
Since the family $(g'_\lambda)$ is schematically dominant, $Y'=Y'_1$, establishing the corollary.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[11.10.8]
\label{IV.11.10.8}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.11.10.5}, suppose that $X$ is an $S$-prescheme.
We say that the family $(f_\lambda)$ is \emph{universally schematically dominant} (relative to $S$) if, for every base change $S'\to S$, the family $(f'_\lambda)$ corresponding to the base change $X'=X\times_S S'\to X$ is schematically dominant.
When the $f_\lambda$ are canonical immersions $Z_\lambda\hookrightarrow X$, we also say that the family of sub-preschemes $Z_\lambda$ is \emph{universally schematically dense in $X$} (relative to $S$).
\end{definition}

When $S$ is the spectrum of a field, \sref{IV.11.10.6} thus means that a schematically dominant family is universally schematically dominant (relative to $S$).

\begin{theorem}[11.10.9]
\label{IV.11.10.9}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.11.10.5}, suppose that $X$ is an $S$-prescheme locally Noetherian and that the $Z_\lambda$ are $S$-flat.
For every $s\in S$, let $X_s=X\times_S\Spec(\kappa(s))$, $(Z_\lambda)_s=Z_\lambda\times_S\Spec(\kappa(s))$, $(f_\lambda)_s=f_\lambda\times 1:(Z_\lambda)_s\to X_s$.
Then, for the family $(f_\lambda)$ to be universally schematically dominant relative to $S$, it is necessary and sufficient that for every $s\in S$, the family $((f_\lambda)_s)$ is schematically dominant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This follows by translation from \sref{IV.11.9.6}, via the equivalence of \sref{IV.11.10.1} and the characterisation of universally schematically dominant families given in \sref{IV.11.10.8}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.10.10]
\label{IV.11.10.10}
Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism flat locally of finite presentation, $U$ an open of $X$.
For $U$ to be universally schematically dense in $X$ relative to $S$, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every $s\in S$, $U_s=U\cap X_s$ is schematically dense in $X_s$ (or, what amounts to the same, that $\operatorname{Ass}(\sh{O}_{X_s})\subset U_s$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is a particular case of \sref{IV.11.10.9} applied to the family consisting of the single canonical immersion $U\hookrightarrow X$, noting that $U$ is $S$-flat since $f$ is flat and $U$ is open in $X$.
\end{proof}
