\section{Topological properties of finitely presented flat morphisms. Flatness criteria}
\label{section:IV.11}

\oldpage[IV-3]{116}

Recall that in \textsection\,2 we considered results concerning flatness that do not depend on any finiteness hypothesis, and that \textsection\,6 studies the notion of flatness in the context of locally Noetherian preschemes (but without finiteness hypothesis on morphisms); the present paragraph is devoted to the notion of $f$-flatness in the case where the morphism $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{locally of finite presentation}.
The interest of the notion of flat morphism of finite presentation lies in the fact that it is this notion which seems to express in the most technically adequate way the intuitive notion of a ``family of algebraic preschemes parametrized by a scheme $Y$'', the study of which is one of the principal objects of Algebraic Geometry.
Moreover, even if one were only interested at the outset in the case of a Noetherian base scheme, it is indispensable, for certain technical reasons (for example for certain applications of the theory of ``descent'', which leads to introducing non-Noetherian schemes), not to limit oneself to this case, as soon as it is a question of problems essentially related to morphisms locally of finite presentation.
We follow this principle systematically, already supported by the results of \textsection\textsection\,8 and~9, throughout the rest of this Chapter, and even in the rest of our Treatise, at the cost of occasionally sacrificing the simplicity of certain proofs, that Noetherian hypotheses sometimes allow to simplify.
In the present paragraph, this principle leads us to take up again, in the context of ``finite presentation'' (notably in n\textsuperscript{o}~3), certain results of flatness, already obtained in the Noetherian context.
The essential technical tool for the reduction to the Noetherian case is the theorem of compatibility of flatness with projective limits of preschemes \sref{IV.11.2.6}, complementing the general results of \textsection\,8.
We also prove in passing \sref{IV.11.3.1} a result often used
\oldpage[IV-3]{117}
in the sequel, implying that the set of points of flatness of a morphism locally of finite presentation is open.

In n\textsuperscript{os}~4 to~8, we study the question of the ``descent'' of flatness, consisting of finding useful conditions on a base change morphism $Y'\to Y$ (not flat in general) to be able to conclude that if $X\times_Y Y'$ is flat over $Y'$, then $X$ is flat over $Y$.
These results, more technical than those of n\textsuperscript{os}~1 to~3, are of less frequent use in the sequel; they will however play an important role in certain non-projective construction techniques, in the following chapter.
The only result from n\textsuperscript{os}~4 to~8 used in the rest of chap.~IV is the valuative criterion of flatness \sref{IV.11.8}, which will be applied in \sref{IV.15.2}.

Finally, n\textsuperscript{os}~9 and~10 are devoted to the study of the notion that refines, in scheme theory, the notion of density in the topological sense, namely the notion of a family of sub-preschemes \emph{schematically dense} in a given prescheme, and in particular the study of the behaviour of this notion under (flat or arbitrary) base change.
This notion is mainly used, for the moment, in the study of group schemes.

\subsection{Flatness loci (Noetherian case)}
\label{subsection:IV.11.1}

\begin{theorem}[11.1.1]
\label{IV.11.1.1}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$ is open in $X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The question being evidently local on $X$ and $Y$, we can suppose $Y=\Spec(A)$, $X=\Spec(B)$, $A$ being Noetherian and $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite type.
One has then $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$, where $M$ is a $B$-module of finite type.
Let us apply the criterion $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.2.6): it suffices therefore to prove the following assertion:
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[11.1.1.1]
\label{IV.11.1.1.1}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite type, $M$ a $B$-module of finite type, $\mathfrak{q}$ a prime ideal of $B$, $\mathfrak{p}$ its inverse image in $A$.
Suppose that $M_\mathfrak{q}$ is an $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module flat.
Then there exists $g\in B-\mathfrak{q}$ such that, for every prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}'\supset\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$ such that $g\notin\mathfrak{q}'$, $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ is an $A_{\mathfrak{p}'}$-module flat, denoting by $\mathfrak{p}'$ the inverse image of $\mathfrak{q}'$ in $A$ (which amounts to saying $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.1) that $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ is an $A$-module flat).
\end{env}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \sref{IV.11.1.1.1}]
\oldpage[IV-3]{118}
Consider $B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ as an $A$-algebra; evidently $\mathfrak{p}B_{\mathfrak{q}'}\subset\mathfrak{q}'B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$.
We know then $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.2) that for $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ to be an $A$-module flat, it is necessary and sufficient that $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}/\mathfrak{p}M_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ be an $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-module flat and that $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_{\mathfrak{q}'},A/\mathfrak{p})=0$.
Now, we have $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}=M\otimes_B B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$; $B_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ being flat over $B$, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_{\mathfrak{q}'},A/\mathfrak{p})=(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_{\mathfrak{q}'}=(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ ($M/\mathfrak{p}M$ being considered in the second member as an $A$-module); moreover $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h$ being an $A$-module of finite type for a suitable $h$, $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h=0$ if and only if there is a suitable element $g$ and $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h=0$ is equivalent to $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{\mathfrak{q}'}=0$.
Since $A$ and $B$ are Noetherian, $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p})$ is a $B$-module of finite type, hence $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 5.2.2) there exists $g\in B-\mathfrak{q}$ such that $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_g=0$.
Moreover, $M/\mathfrak{p}M$ is a $B/\mathfrak{p}B$-module of finite type, and it suffices to apply $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.2).
On the other hand, using the theorem of generic flatness \sref{IV.6.9.1} applied to the integral ring $A/\mathfrak{p}$, the $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-algebra of finite type $B/\mathfrak{p}B$, and the $(B/\mathfrak{p}B)$-module of finite type $M/\mathfrak{p}M$, there exists $h\in A-\mathfrak{p}$ such that, if $\overline{h}$ is its canonical image in $A/\mathfrak{p}$, $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{\overline{h}}$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-module flat.
On the other hand, as $M_\mathfrak{q}$ is an $A_\mathfrak{p}$-module flat, and hence an $A$-module flat $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.1), we also have $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M_\mathfrak{q},A/\mathfrak{p})=0$, which writes $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_\mathfrak{q}=0$.
Moreover, on has $M_{\mathfrak{q}'}/\mathfrak{p}M_{\mathfrak{q}'}=(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_h$ being considered as $B$-modules;
finally, on has $(\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(M,A/\mathfrak{p}))_{hg}=0$ and $(M/\mathfrak{p}M)_{hg}$ is an $(A/\mathfrak{p})$-module flat; hence $M_{hg}$ is an $A$-module flat by $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.3.2), and the conclusion follows since $hg\notin\mathfrak{q}$.
C.Q.F.D.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.2]
\label{IV.11.1.2}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}'$, $\sh{F}$ two coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Modules, $u:\sh{F}'\to\sh{F}$ a homomorphism of $\sh{O}_X$-Modules.
Suppose that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that, setting $y=f(x)$, the homomorphism $u_x\otimes 1:\sh{F}'_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}k(y)\to\sh{F}_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}k(y)$ is injective, is open in $X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, let $\sh{N}$ (resp. $\sh{P}$) be the kernel (resp. the cokernel) of $u$; applying $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.4) ($^1$) to the local rings $\sh{O}_y$ and the $\sh{O}_x$-modules $\sh{F}'_x$ and $\sh{F}_x$: to say that $u_x\otimes 1$ is injective is equivalent to saying that $\sh{N}_x=0$ and that $\sh{P}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
Now $\sh{F}$ and $\sh{G}$ are coherent, hence the corollary follows from \sref{IV.11.1.1} and $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 5.2.2).
\footnote{Here is a proof of $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.4), which has not been published in Bourbaki's \emph{Commutative Algebra}.
Taking into account $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.1.2), it suffices to see that \emph{b)} implies \emph{a)}.
Let $P=\Im(u)$, $Q=\operatorname{Coker}(u)$, $R=\Ker(u)$.
The composed map $M\otimes k\to P\otimes k\to N\otimes k$ is injective, and $M\otimes k\to P\otimes k$ is surjective, hence $P\otimes k\to N\otimes k$ is injective and $M\otimes k\to P\otimes k$ is bijective.
The exact sequence $0\to P\to N\to Q\to 0$ gives the exact sequence
$0=\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N,k)\to\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(Q,k)\to P\otimes k\to N\otimes k\to Q\otimes k\to 0$
and since $P\otimes k\to N\otimes k$ is injective, on has $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(Q,k)=0$; as $Q$ is a $B$-module of finite type, $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.2) shows that $Q$ is also an $A$-module flat; also $R\otimes k=0$ by exact sequence, hence $R=0$ by Nakayama.}
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.3]
\label{IV.11.1.3}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $g$ a section of $\sh{O}_X$ over $X$.
The set of $x\in X$ such that $g_x\otimes 1$ is not a zero divisor in $\sh{O}_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_{f(x)}}k(f(x))$ is open in $X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to apply \sref{IV.11.1.2} to the endomorphism of $\sh{O}_X$ defined by $g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.4]
\label{IV.11.1.4}
Let $Y$ be a locally Noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module, and $f$-flat.
Let $(g_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ be a suite of sections of $\sh{O}_X$ over $X$.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that the suite $((g_i)_x\otimes 1)$ is $\sh{F}_x$-regular and the $\sh{O}_x$-module $\sh{G}_x$ (where $\sh{G}=\sh{F}/(\sum_{i=1}^n g_i\sh{F})$) is $f$-flat, is open in $X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{119}
As $\sh{F}$ and $\sh{G}$ are coherent, the corollary follows from \sref{IV.11.1.1} and $(\mathbf{0}$, 15.2.4).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.5]
\label{IV.11.1.5}
Let $X$, $Y$, $Z$ be three locally Noetherian preschemes, $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ two morphisms of finite type, $\sh{F}$ a coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module.
Then the set $U$ of $y\in Y$ such that, for every generisation $y'$ of $y$, $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat at all the points of $f^{-1}(y')$ (i.e.\ such that $\sh{F}\otimes_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$ is flat relative to the morphism $X\times_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})\to Z$) is open in $Y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $V$ is the set of $x\in X$ where $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat, $U$ is the set of $y\in Y$ such that for every generisation $y'$ of $y$, one has $f^{-1}(y')\subset V$.
Now $V$ is locally open \sref{IV.11.1.1}, hence locally constructible in $X$, and the set $W$ of $y\in Y$ such that $f^{-1}(y)\subset V$ is also locally constructible in $Y$ by virtue of Chevalley's theorem \sref{IV.1.8.4}.
But it follows then from $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.2.5) that the points of $U$ are the interior points of $W$, whence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.1.6]
\label{IV.11.1.6}
Let $A$ be a Noetherian ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite type, $C$ a $B$-algebra of finite type, $M$ a $C$-module of finite type.
Then the set of $\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(B)$ such that $M_\mathfrak{q}$ is an $A$-module flat is open in $\Spec(B)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Taking into account \sref{IV.2.1.2}, this is a consequence of \sref{IV.11.1.5} applied to $Z=\Spec(A)$, $Y=\Spec(B)$, $X=\Spec(C)$, $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$.

The results of this subsection will be freed of the Noetherian hypotheses in \sref{IV.11.3}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Flatness of a projective limit of preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.11.2}

\begin{env}[11.2.1]
\label{IV.11.2.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $M$, $N$ two $A$-modules, $A'$ an $A$-algebra; set $M'=M\otimes_A A'$, $N'=N\otimes_A A'$.
Recall (\textbf{III}, 6.3.8) that one defines, for every $i$, a canonical homomorphism
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.1}\tag{11.2.1.1}
  \psi_i:\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M,N)\otimes_A A'\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A'}(M',N')
\]
in the following way: one considers a left resolution of $M$ by \emph{free} $A$-modules
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.2}\tag{11.2.1.2}
  \cdots\to L_{i+1}\xrightarrow{f_i}L_i\to\cdots\to L_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}M\to 0
\]
from which one deduces by tensorisation with $A'$ a complex of $A'$-modules
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.3}\tag{11.2.1.3}
  \cdots\to L'_{i+1}\xrightarrow{f'_i}L'_i\to\cdots\to L'_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon'}M'\to 0
\]
where $L'_i=L_i\otimes_A A'$, $f'_i=f_i\otimes 1_{A'}$, $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon\otimes 1_{A'}$.
\oldpage[IV-3]{120}
Considering on the other hand a left resolution of $M'$ by free $A'$-modules
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.4}\tag{11.2.1.4}
  \cdots\to L''_{i+1}\xrightarrow{f''_i}L''_i\to\cdots\to L''_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon''}M'\to 0
\]
as the $L'_i$ are free $A'$-modules, one knows (M, V, 1.1) that there are $A'$-homomorphisms $u_i$ forming a commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  \cdots\ar[r] & L'_{i+1}\ar[r]^{f'_i}\ar[d]^{u_{i+1}} & L'_i\ar[r]\ar[d]^{u_i} & \cdots\ar[r] & L'_0\ar[r]^{\varepsilon'}\ar[d]^{u_0} & M'\ar[r]\ar@{=}[d] & 0\\
  \cdots\ar[r] & L''_{i+1}\ar[r]^{f''_i} & L''_i\ar[r] & \cdots\ar[r] & L''_0\ar[r]^{\varepsilon''} & M'\ar[r] & 0
}
\]
Composing the homomorphism $u_\bullet:L'_\bullet\to L''_\bullet$ of complexes thus defined with the canonical homomorphism of $A$-module complexes $L_\bullet\to L'_\bullet$; noting that $(L_i\otimes_A N)\otimes_A A'=L'_i\otimes_{A'} N'$, one deduces a homomorphism of $A$-module complexes $L_\bullet\otimes_A N\to L''_\bullet\otimes_{A'} N'$, whence, passing to homology, the canonical homomorphisms \sref{IV.11.2.1.1}.
As the $u_i$ are well-determined up to homotopy (M, V, 1.1), the homomorphisms \sref{IV.11.2.1.1} do not depend on the choice of the $u_i$ nor on the choice of the free resolutions $L_\bullet$ and $L''_\bullet$.

As the $\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A'}(M',N')$ are $A'$-modules, one canonically deduces from \sref{IV.11.2.1.1} $A'$-homomorphisms
\[
\label{IV.11.2.1.5}\tag{11.2.1.5}
  \varphi_i:\operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M,N)\otimes_A A'\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A'}(M',N').
\]

Consider now two homomorphisms of rings
\[
  \rho:A\to A^{(1)},\quad\sigma:A^{(1)}\to A^{(2)},
\]
and their composite $\sigma\circ\rho:A\to A^{(2)}$; setting $M^{(j)}=M\otimes_A A^{(j)}$, $N^{(j)}=N\otimes_A A^{(j)}$ for $j=1,2$.
Then the canonical composite homomorphism
\[
  \operatorname{Tor}_i^A(M,N)\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A^{(1)}}(M^{(1)},N^{(1)})\to\operatorname{Tor}_i^{A^{(2)}}(M^{(2)},N^{(2)})
\]
is the same as the canonical homomorphism deduced from $\sigma\circ\rho$; this follows from the fact that, if $L^{(j)}_\bullet$ is a free resolution of $M^{(j)}$, the diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  L_\bullet\ar[r]\ar[d] & L_\bullet\otimes_A A^{(1)}\ar[r]\ar[d] & L^{(1)}_\bullet\\
  L_\bullet\otimes_A A^{(2)}\ar[r] & L^{(1)}_\bullet\otimes_{A^{(1)}}A^{(2)}\ar[r] & L^{(2)}_\bullet
}
\]
is commutative.
\end{env}

\begin{lemma}[11.2.2.2]
\label{IV.11.2.2.2}
Let $(A_\alpha,g_{\beta\alpha})$ be a filtered inductive system of rings, $(M_\alpha,h_{\beta\alpha})$ a filtered inductive system of sets, $A'=\varinjlim A_\alpha$, $M'=\varinjlim M_\alpha$, $g_\alpha:A_\alpha\to A'$, $h_\alpha:M_\alpha\to M'$ the canonical maps.
For every $\alpha$, let $F(M_\alpha)$ be the $A_\alpha$-module of formal linear combinations of elements of $M_\alpha$; let similarly $F(M')$ be the $A'$-module of formal linear combinations of elements of $M'$.
If $h'_{\beta\alpha}:F(M_\alpha)\to F(M_\beta)$ (for $\alpha\leq\beta$) and $h'_\alpha:F(M_\alpha)\to F(M')$ are the $A_\alpha$-homomorphisms deduced from $h_{\beta\alpha}$ and $h_\alpha$ respectively, $(F(M_\alpha),h'_{\beta\alpha})$ is an inductive system of $A_\alpha$-modules and $(h'_\alpha)$ an inductive system of homomorphisms.
Then
\[
  h''=\varinjlim_\alpha h'_\alpha:\varinjlim_\alpha F(M_\alpha)\to F(\varinjlim_\alpha M_\alpha)
\]
is an isomorphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the proof, see Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.}, chap.~II, 3\textsuperscript{e}~\'{e}d., \textsection\,6, n\textsuperscript{o}\,6, cor.~of prop.~10.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Application to the elimination of Noetherian hypotheses}
\label{subsection:IV.11.3}

\begin{theorem}[11.3.1]
\label{IV.11.3.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Then the set $U$ of $x\in X$ such that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$ is open in $X$.
If moreover $\Supp(\sh{F})=X$, $f|U$ is an open morphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The second assertion has already been proved \sref{IV.2.4.6} and has been included here only for reference.

The question being local on $X$ and $Y$, we can suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are affine, and that $f$ is a morphism of finite presentation.
Let $x\in X$ be a point such that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the point $x$.
Applying \sref{IV.11.2.7}, one can suppose that $X=X_0\times_{Y_0}Y$, $f=f_0\times 1$, where $Y_0$ is Noetherian, $f_0:X_0\to Y_0$ a morphism of finite type, $\sh{F}_0$ a coherent $\sh{O}_{X_0}$-Module; moreover, if $x_0$ is the projection of $x$ in $X_0$, one can suppose that $\sh{F}_0$ is $f_0$-flat at the point $x_0$.
Then, by virtue of \sref{IV.11.1.1}, the set $U_0$ of points of $X_0$ where $\sh{F}_0$ is $f_0$-flat is an open neighbourhood of $x_0$; hence $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the points of the inverse image of $U_0$ in $X$ \sref{IV.2.1.4}, and this proves that $U$ is a neighbourhood of $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.3.2]
\label{IV.11.3.2}
\oldpage[IV-3]{133}
Let $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ be two morphisms of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Then the set $U$ of $y\in Y$ such that, for every generisation $y'$ of $y$, $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat at all the points of $f^{-1}(y')$ (i.e.\ such that $\sh{F}\otimes_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$ is flat relative to the morphism $X\times_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})\to Z$) is open in $Y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The same reasoning as in \sref{IV.11.1.5} shows that if $V$ is the set of $x\in X$ where $\sh{F}$ is $(g\circ f)$-flat, $U$ is the set of $y\in Y$ whose every generisation belongs to $E=Y-f(X-V)$.
As $g\circ f$ is of finite presentation, $V$ is open in $X$ by virtue of \sref{IV.11.3.1}, hence ind-constructible; and $f(X-V)$ is pro-constructible in $Y$ \sref{IV.1.9.5}[vii)], hence $E$ is ind-constructible in $Y$.
But it follows then from $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 10.1) that $U$ is the interior of $E$, hence open in $Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[11.3.3]
\label{IV.11.3.3}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, $C$ a $B$-algebra of finite type, $M$ a $C$-module of finite type.
Then the set of $\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec(B)$ such that $M_\mathfrak{q}$ is an $A$-module flat is open in $\Spec(B)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Taking into account \sref{IV.2.1.2}, this is a consequence of \sref{IV.11.3.2} applied to $Z=\Spec(A)$, $Y=\Spec(B)$, $X=\Spec(C)$, $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$.

The results of this subsection will be freed of the Noetherian hypotheses in \sref{IV.11.3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.3.4]
\label{IV.11.3.4}
Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, $\sh{J}$ a quasi-coherent ideal of finite type of $\sh{O}_X$, $Y$ the closed sub-prescheme of $X$ defined by $\sh{J}$, $\sh{F}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_X$-Module of finite presentation.
Suppose $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is $f$-flat.
Under these conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the points of $Y$.
  \item If $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is $f$-flat, $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ is an $(\sh{O}_X/\sh{J})$-Algebra of finite presentation and $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{F})$ is an $(\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X))$-Module of finite presentation.
  \item Suppose $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ $f$-flat (which implies that $\sh{O}_X/\sh{J}$ is $f$-flat).
    Then the set $W$ of $y\in Y$ such that $(\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{F}))_y$ is an $(\sh{O}_X/\sh{J})_y$-module flat is open in $Y$.
  \item Suppose $\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{O}_X)$ $f$-flat; let $S'\to S$ be any morphism, let $X'=X\times_S S'$, $p:X'\to X$ the canonical projection, $Y'=p^{-1}(Y)$ the closed sub-prescheme of $X'$, defined by $\sh{J}'=p^*(\sh{J})\sh{O}_{X'}$, $\sh{F}'=p^*(\sh{F})=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_S}\sh{O}_{S'}$; then, if $W'=p^{-1}(W)|W'$, one has $\operatorname{gr}_{\sh{J}'}(\sh{F}')|W'\cong p^*(\operatorname{gr}_\sh{J}(\sh{F}))|W$, and $\operatorname{gr}_{\sh{J}'}(\sh{F}')$ is an $(\sh{O}_{X'}/\sh{J}')$-Module flat at the points of $W'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The questions being local on $X$ and $S$, one can suppose that $S=\Spec(A)$ and $X=\Spec(B)$ are affine, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, and $\sh{F}=\widetilde{M}$, where $M$ is a $B$-module of finite presentation, $\sh{J}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}$, where $\mathfrak{J}$ is an ideal of finite type of $B$; by virtue of \sref{IV.8.9.1} and \sref{IV.8.5.11}, there exists a Noetherian sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$, an $A_0$-algebra of finite type $B_0$, an ideal $\mathfrak{J}_0$ of $B_0$ of finite type, a $B_0$-module $M_0$ such that $B=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A$, $\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}_0 B$, $M=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A=M_0\otimes_{B_0}B$.
Moreover, $A$ is the inductive limit of its sub-$A_0$-algebras of finite type $A_\lambda$; setting $B_\lambda=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda$, $M_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{B_0}B_\lambda$, $\mathfrak{J}_\lambda=\mathfrak{J}_0 B_\lambda$, $M=\varinjlim M_\lambda$.
Since, by hypothesis, $\operatorname{gr}^*_\mathfrak{J}(M)$ is an $A$-module flat, it follows from \sref{IV.11.2.9} that there exists $\lambda$ such that $\operatorname{gr}^*_{\mathfrak{J}_\lambda}(M_\lambda)$ is an $A_\lambda$-module flat.
To prove that $\sh{F}$ is $f$-flat at the points of $Y$, one can therefore restrict to the case where $S$ is \emph{Noetherian}; but then $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 6.1.2), applied by induction on $n$, proves that the $\sh{F}/\sh{J}^{n+1}\sh{F}$ are $f$-flat, and one concludes by $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 10.2.6).

The proof of (ii) reduces in the same way to the case where $S$ is Noetherian, as does (iii) and (iv).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[11.3.5]
\label{IV.11.3.5}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, $M$ a $B$-module of finite presentation, which is an $A$-module flat.
Then there exists a finite suite $(f_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ of elements of $A$, such that the ideal generated by the $f_i$ is equal to $A$, and that, for $0\leq i<n$, $M_{f_{i+1}}/(\sum_{j=1}^i f_j M_{f_{i+1}})$ is an $(A_{f_{i+1}}/(\sum_{j=1}^i f_j A_{f_{i+1}}))$-module free.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One can also say that the $\mathrm{D}(f_i)$ form a covering of $X=\Spec(A)$, and that, setting $Z_1=\mathrm{D}(f_1)$ and by induction $Z_{i+1}=\mathrm{D}(f_{i+1})\cap\mathrm{V}(f_1 A+\cdots+f_i A)$, the $Z_i$ form a \emph{partition} of $X$ into locally closed sets, $A_{f_{i+1}}/(\sum_{j=1}^i f_j A_{f_{i+1}})$ being the ring of an affine sub-scheme of $X$ having $Z_{i+1}$ as underlying space.

To prove the proposition, one can first, by virtue of \sref{IV.11.2.7}, suppose that there exist a Noetherian ring $A_0$, an $A_0$-algebra of finite type $B_0$, a $B_0$-module of type fini $M_0$, flat over $A_0$, such that $B=B_0\otimes_{A_0}A$, $M=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A$; it is clear that it suffices to prove the proposition for $A_0$, $B_0$, $M_0$, for if the $f_i\in A_0$ satisfy the conditions of the statement, they will satisfy them also for $A$, $B$, $M$.
One can therefore restrict to the case where $A$ is Noetherian.

Note now that if $C$ is a Noetherian ring, $\mathfrak{R}$ its nilradical and $P$ a flat $C$-module, then for $P$ to be a $C$-module free, it suffices that $P\otimes_C(C/\mathfrak{R})$ be a $(C/\mathfrak{R})$-module free.
Note also that if $C$ is a Noetherian \emph{reduced} ring, there exists $g\in C$ such that $C_g$ is integral.
Using now the lemma \sref{IV.6.9.2}: one can define by induction a suite $(f_i)_{i\geq 1}$ of elements of $A$ in the following manner:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[$1\textsuperscript{o}$] $f_1$ is such that $A_1=(A_{\mathrm{red}})_{f_1}$ is integral and $M\otimes_A A_1$ is an $A_1$-module free;
  \item[$2\textsuperscript{o}$] if the ideal $\mathfrak{J}_i$ generated by $f_1,\ldots,f_i$ is $A$, $f_{i+1}=f_i$;
  \item[$3\textsuperscript{o}$] otherwise $\mathfrak{J}_i\neq A$, $f_{i+1}$ is an element not belonging to $\mathfrak{J}_i$ such that $A_{i+1}=((A/\mathfrak{J}_i)_{\mathrm{red}})_{f_{i+1}}$ is integral and $M\otimes_A A_{i+1}$ is an $A_{i+1}$-module free.
\end{enumerate}
As $A$ is Noetherian, the increasing sequence $(\mathfrak{J}_i)$ is stationary, hence there exists $n$ such that $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ generate the ideal $A$, and the $f_i$ answer the question since
\[
  ((A/\mathfrak{J}_i)_{\mathrm{red}})_{f_{i+1}}=((A/\mathfrak{J}_i)_{f_{i+1}})_{\mathrm{red}}.
\]
\end{proof}
