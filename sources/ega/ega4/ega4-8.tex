\section{Projective limits of preschemes}
\label{section:IV.8}

\oldpage[IV-3]{5}

\begin{env}{8.1.1}
\label{IV.8.1.1}
The notion of a projective limit of preschemes was defined in \sref[II]{II.2.4.1};
if the ordered set $\mathbf{L}$ of indices is filtered and increasing, and if the morphisms of the projective system are \emph{affine}, then the projective limit exists, and is a prescheme.
The results of the present paragraph will essentially show that the algebraic geometry of $\sh{O}_y$-preschemes of finite presentation (and the theory of Modules of finite presentation on these preschemes) is essentially equivalent to the algebraic geometry of preschemes of finite presentation over ``sufficiently small'' open neighborhoods of $y$.
A particularly important case, and to a certain extent a classical one, is the one where $Y$ is integral and where $y$ is its generic point, so that $\sh{O}_y$ is nothing other than \emph{the field} $\mathrm{R}(Y)=K$ \emph{of rational functions on} $Y$.
The results of the present paragraph thus allow reinterpreting algebraic geometry over $K$ in terms of algebraic geometry over non-empty open subsets ``sufficiently small'' of $Y$, that is to say, intuitively, in terms of ``families'' of geometric objects indexed by the points of such an open subset.
This point of view has moreover been commonly used for a long time, not only in Algebraic Geometry over algebraically closed fields, but also in the study of arithmetic of varieties defined over a \emph{number field} $K$ (a finite extension of $\bb{Q}$), by considering the latter as the field of fractions of its ring of integers $A$ (``the theory of \emph{reduction modulo} $\mathfrak{p}$'' ($\mathfrak{p}$ a prime ideal of $A$); cf.\ \sref[I]{I.3.7}).
The results of \textsection\textsection~8 and 9 thus provide, among other things, some of the foundations of the language of ``reduction modulo $\mathfrak{p}$'' in arithmetic.

We note that in the example considered here, the morphisms $S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ (for $\lambda\leq\mu$)
are the \emph{canonical open immersions} $U_\mu\to U_\lambda$, and \emph{a fortiori} are \emph{flat} morphisms (but not faithfully flat in general), which explains the interest of the statements that do not make such a restriction.
\end{env}

\oldpage[IV-3]{6}

\begin{env}{8.1.2}
\label{IV.8.1.2}
\medskip\noindent
\emph{b)}
Suppose that the $A_\lambda$ are \emph{fields}, so that $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$ is also a field.
This case arises generally when one starts from geometric data over a field $K$ of any kind (for example $k$ the prime subfield of $K$).
There is then interest in considering $K$ as an inductive limit of its sub-extensions that are \emph{of finite type over $k$}, which allows in many cases reducing questions to the case where $K$ is a finite type extension of $k$.
Using also the method sketched in \emph{a)}, one can then reduce more generally to the case of a base ring $A$ which is an \emph{algebra of finite type over $k$}.

We note that in this example, the morphisms $S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are \emph{faithfully flat}.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{c)}
Suppose one is interested in properties, local over $Y$, of preschemes of finite presentation $Y$ over any prescheme $Y$, which one can then assume to be an affine scheme of ring $A$.
There is then interest in considering $A$ as an inductive limit of its sub-rings which are $\bb{Z}$-\emph{algebras of finite type}, which allows reducing many questions to the case where $Y$ is the spectrum of such an algebra.
This is the basis for the ``Kroneckerian point of view'' according to which Algebraic Geometry reduces to the Algebraic Geometry of preschemes of finite type over $\bb{Z}$ (which one sometimes qualifies as
\end{env}

\oldpage[IV-3]{7}
\begin{env}{8.1.2.cont}
``Absolute Algebraic Geometry'').
This example shows us in particular that for most ``relative'' questions on a prescheme of base $Y$, one can reduce to the case where $Y$ is \emph{noetherian}.

We note that in this example, in contrast to the preceding ones, the morphisms $S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ have in general no particular regularity property.
\end{env}

\begin{env}{8.1.3}
\label{IV.8.1.3}
In example \emph{a)} of (8.1.2), we saw that if $Y$ is an integral prescheme of generic point $y$, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite presentation, then, if the generic fibre $f^{-1}(y)$ is proper over $k(y)$, there is an open neighborhood $U$ of $y$ in $Y$ such that $f^{-1}(U)$ is proper over $U$;
it follows \emph{a fortiori} that for all $s\in U$, $f^{-1}(s)$ is proper over $k(s)$.
One arrives at a need for a converse, ensuring that if $f^{-1}(s)$ is proper over $k(s)$ for ``sufficiently many'' points $s\in U$, then $f^{-1}(y)$ is proper over $k(y)$.
For example, suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are preschemes algebraic over a field $k$ algebraically closed (one can take for $k$ the field $\bb{C}$ of complex numbers, to fix ideas);
one sometimes needs to know that if, for all $s\in Y$, \emph{rational over} $k$, the fibre $f^{-1}(s)$ is proper over $k(s)$, then $f^{-1}(y)$ is proper over $k(y)$, and consequently $f^{-1}(U)$ is proper over $U$ for a neighborhood $U$ of $y$.
This statement will follow easily from the following:
the set $E$ of points $s\in Y$ such that $f^{-1}(s)$ is proper over $k(s)$ is \emph{constructible} (and hence equal to all of $Y$ if it contains the closed points of $Y$, by the Hilbert Nullstellensatz (\sref{IV.10.4.8}));
this amounts to saying that if $f^{-1}(y)$ \emph{is not proper} over $k(y)$, then there exists an open neighborhood $U$ of $y$ such that $f^{-1}(s)$ is not proper over $k(s)$ for all $s\in U$ (cf.\ (\sref{IV.9.6.1}, (iv))).
This example illustrates the interest of systematically developing \emph{constructibility criteria} for the most important notions: this is what will be done in \textsection~9.
\end{env}

\subsection{Projective limits of preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.8.2}

\begin{env}{8.2.1}
\label{IV.8.2.1}
Let $S_0$ be a ringed space, $\mathbf{L}$ a preordered filtered increasing set, $(\sh{A}_\lambda,\varphi_{\mu\lambda})$ an inductive system of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebras (not necessarily commutative), with $\mathbf{L}$ as index set.
We know that, considered as an inductive system of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Modules, $(\sh{A}_\lambda,\varphi_{\mu\lambda})$ admits an inductive limit $\sh{A}$;
denote by $\varphi_\lambda:\sh{A}_\lambda\to\sh{A}$ the canonical homomorphism.
Let $m_\lambda:\sh{A}_\lambda\otimes\sh{A}_\lambda\to\sh{A}_\lambda$ be the homomorphism of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Modules that defines the multiplication in the $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebra $\sh{A}_\lambda$;
the hypothesis on the $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}$ implies that the $m_\lambda$ form an inductive system of homomorphisms,
\oldpage[IV-3]{8}
and as the functor $\varinjlim$ commutes with the tensor product, $m=\varinjlim m_\lambda$ is a homomorphism $\sh{A}\otimes\sh{A}\to\sh{A}$ of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Modules;
by passage to the limit on the commutative diagrams expressing the associativity of $m_\lambda$ and the existence of the identity section in $\sh{A}_\lambda$, one sees that $m$ defines on $\sh{A}$ a structure of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebra and that $\varphi_\lambda$ is a homomorphism of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebras for all $\lambda\in\mathbf{L}$.
Furthermore $\sh{A}$ is the inductive limit of the system $(\sh{A}_\lambda,\varphi_{\mu\lambda})$
\emph{in the category of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebras}, in other words, for every $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebra $\sh{B}$, the canonical map
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0\text{-}\mathrm{Alg.}}(\sh{A},\sh{B})
  \to \varprojlim_\lambda \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0\text{-}\mathrm{Alg.}}(\sh{A}_\lambda,\sh{B})
  \tag{8.2.1.1}\label{IV.8.2.1.1}
\]
which to every homomorphism $f:\sh{A}\to\sh{B}$ of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebras associates the family $(f\circ\varphi_\lambda)$, is \emph{bijective}.
Indeed, one already knows that it is injective and identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_{S_0\text{-}\mathrm{Alg.}}(\sh{A},\sh{B})$ with a part of $\varprojlim \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0\text{-}\mathrm{Mod.}}(\sh{A}_\lambda,\sh{B})$;
it suffices to see that if $(f_\lambda)$ is an inductive system of homomorphisms of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebras, $f_\lambda:\sh{A}_\lambda\to\sh{B}$, its inductive limit $f:\sh{A}\to\sh{B}$, which is by definition a homomorphism of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-\emph{Modules}, is also a homomorphism of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-\emph{Algebras};
but this follows from passage to the inductive limit in the commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
  \sh{A}_\lambda\otimes\sh{A}_\lambda \ar[r,"f_\lambda\otimes f_\lambda"] & \sh{B}\otimes\sh{B}\\
  \sh{A}_\lambda \ar[u,"m_\lambda"'] \ar[r,"f_\lambda"'] & \sh{B} \ar[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\]
and from the fact that the functor $\varinjlim$ commutes with tensor products.
We note finally that if the $\sh{A}_\lambda$ are commutative $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebras, so is $\sh{A}$.
\end{env}

\begin{env}{8.2.2}
\label{IV.8.2.2}
Suppose now that $S_0$ is a \emph{prescheme}, and that the $\sh{A}_\lambda$ are \emph{quasi-coherent} $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebras (commutative);
we know then that $\sh{A}=\varinjlim\sh{A}_\lambda$ is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebra \sref[I]{I.4.1.1}.
Denote by $S_\lambda$ (resp.\ $\sh{A}$) the spectrum of the $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebra $\sh{A}_\lambda$ (resp.\ $\sh{A}$) \sref[II]{II.1.3.1}, and let $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ (for $\lambda\leq\mu$) and $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ be the $S_0$-morphisms corresponding to the homomorphisms $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}$ and $\varphi_\lambda$ respectively \sref[II]{II.1.2.7};
it is clear that $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ is a \emph{projective system} in the category of $S_0$-preschemes.
We note that the $u_{\lambda\mu}$ and $u_\lambda$ are affine morphisms \sref[II]{II.1.6.2}, hence \emph{quasi-compact} and \emph{separated}.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.2.3]
\label{IV.8.2.3}
With the notations of \sref{IV.8.2.2}, the morphisms $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ make $S$ a projective limit of the projective system $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ in the category of preschemes.
Furthermore, if $h:S_0\to T$ is a morphism, making every $S_0$-prescheme into a $T$-prescheme, $S$ is also a projective limit of the system $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ in the category of $T$-preschemes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.2.3.proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{9}
It suffices to show that if $X$ is any $S_0$-prescheme, the canonical map
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(X,S) \to \varprojlim_\lambda \operatorname{Hom}_{S_0}(X,S_\lambda)
  \tag{8.2.3.1}\label{IV.8.2.3.1}
\]
which to every $S_0$-morphism $v:X\to S$ associates the family $(u_\lambda\circ v)$, is \emph{bijective}.
Now, if $g:X\to S_0$ is the structural morphism and if one sets $\sh{B}=g_*(\sh{O}_X)$, which is an $\sh{O}_{S_0}$-Algebra, the map \eqref{IV.8.2.3.1} identifies canonically with \eqref{IV.8.2.1.1} \sref[II]{II.1.2.7}, and the conclusion follows from what we saw in \eqref{IV.8.2.1.1}.

The other assertions of \eqref{IV.8.2.3} are consequences of the following general lemma:
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[8.2.4]
\label{IV.8.2.4}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category, $T$ an object of $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathcal{C}_T$ the subcategory of objects of $\mathcal{C}$ above $T$.
Let $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ be a projective system in $\mathcal{C}_T$; then every projective limit of this system in $\mathcal{C}_T$ is also a projective limit in $\mathcal{C}$, and conversely.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $f_\lambda:S_\lambda\to T$ be the structural morphism.
Suppose that $S$ is the projective limit of $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ in $\mathcal{C}$, and denote by $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ the canonical corresponding morphisms.
Consider then a projective system of $T$-morphisms $w_\lambda:Y\to S_\lambda$, where $Y\in\mathcal{C}_T$.
By hypothesis there exists a unique morphism (in $\mathcal{C}$) $w:Y\to S$ such that $w_\lambda=u_\lambda\circ w$ for all $\lambda$.
The hypothesis that the $u_\lambda$ are $T$-morphisms implies that the morphisms $f_\lambda\circ u_\lambda:S\to T$ are all the same, and this morphism $f$ makes $S$ into a $T$-object.
If $g:Y\to T$ is the structural morphism of $Y$, one has $f\circ w=f_\lambda\circ u_\lambda\circ w=f_\lambda\circ w_\lambda=g$ for all $\lambda$, which proves that $w$ is a $T$-morphism.
Conversely, suppose (with the same notations) that $S$ is a projective limit of $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ in $\mathcal{C}_T$, and consider now a projective system of morphisms (of $\mathcal{C}$) $w_\lambda:Y\to S_\lambda$.
The composed morphisms $f_\lambda\circ w_\lambda:Y\to T$ are then all the same:
indeed, for any two indices $\lambda$, $\mu$, there is an index $\nu$ such that $\lambda\leq\nu$ and $\mu\leq\nu$, hence $f_\nu=f_\lambda\circ u_{\lambda\nu}=f_\mu\circ u_{\mu\nu}$;
as $w_\lambda=u_{\lambda\nu}\circ w_\nu$ and $w_\mu=u_{\mu\nu}\circ w_\nu$, one has $f_\lambda\circ w_\lambda=f_\lambda\circ u_{\lambda\nu}\circ w_\nu=f_\nu\circ w_\nu$ and similarly $f_\mu\circ w_\mu=f_\nu\circ w_\nu$.
If $g:Y\to T$ is the unique morphism thus defined, $g$ makes $Y$ into a $T$-object, and the $w_\lambda$ are $T$-morphisms;
they thus have a projective limit $w:Y\to S$ which is a $T$-morphism, and \emph{a fortiori} a morphism of $\mathcal{C}$;
moreover the first part of the reasoning shows that every projective limit $w'$ (in $\mathcal{C}$) of the projective system $(w_\lambda)$ is necessarily also a $T$-morphism, hence equal to $w$, which finishes proving the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.2.5]
\label{IV.8.2.5}
Under the conditions of \sref{IV.8.2.2}, let $\alpha$ be an element of $\mathbf{L}$, $X_\alpha$ an $S_\alpha$-prescheme.
For all $\lambda\geq\alpha$, set $X_\lambda=X_\alpha\times_{S_\alpha}S_\lambda$, and for $\alpha\leq\lambda\leq\mu$, set $v_{\lambda\mu}=1_{X_\alpha}\times u_{\lambda\mu}$, so that $(X_\lambda,v_{\lambda\mu})$ is a projective system of $X_\alpha$-preschemes, whose index set is formed of the $\lambda\geq\alpha$ in $\mathbf{L}$.
Set also $X=X_\alpha\times_{S_\alpha}S$ and $v_\lambda=1_{X_\alpha}\times u_\lambda$.
Then the $X_\alpha$-morphisms $v_\lambda:X\to X_\lambda$ make $X$ a projective limit of the projective system $(X_\lambda,v_{\lambda\mu})$ in the category of $X_\alpha$-preschemes, or in the category of all preschemes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This will follow from the following general lemma:
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[8.2.6]
\label{IV.8.2.6}
\oldpage[IV-3]{10}
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category in which fibered products exist, $q:T'\to T$ a morphism of $\mathcal{C}$, $\mathcal{C}_T$ (resp.\ $\mathcal{C}_{T'}$) the category of objects of $\mathcal{C}$ above $T$ (resp.\ $T'$).
Let $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ be a projective system (not necessarily filtered) in $\mathcal{C}_T$, and set $S'_\lambda=S_\lambda\times_T T'$, $u'_{\lambda\mu}=u_{\lambda\mu}\times 1_{T'}$, so that $(S'_\lambda,u'_{\lambda\mu})$ is a projective system in $\mathcal{C}_{T'}$.
Then, if $(S,u_\lambda)$ is a projective limit of $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ in $\mathcal{C}_T$, $(S\times_T T',u_\lambda\times 1_{T'})$ is a projective limit of $(S'_\lambda,u'_{\lambda\mu})$ in $\mathcal{C}_{T'}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By hypothesis, for all $\lambda$, one has a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
  S' \ar[r,"u'_\lambda"] \ar[d,"p"'] & S'_\lambda \ar[r,"h'_\lambda"] & T'\\
  S \ar[r,"u_\lambda"'] & S_\lambda \ar[r,"h_\lambda"'] \ar[u,"p_\lambda"] & T \ar[u,"q"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
where we have set $S'=S\times_T T'$, $u'_\lambda=u_\lambda\times 1_{T'}$, $h'_\lambda=h_\lambda\times 1_{T'}$.
Let $Y$ be a $T'$-object, and consider a projective system of $T'$-morphisms $w'_\lambda:Y\to S'_\lambda$, where $w'_\lambda=p_\lambda\circ w'_\lambda$ are $T$-morphisms from $Y$ to $S_\lambda$.
The hypothesis that the $u_\lambda$ are $T$-morphisms implies that the morphisms $f_\lambda\circ u_\lambda:S\to T$ are all the same, and this morphism $f$ makes $S$ into a $T$-object.
By definition of the fibered product, there exists therefore by hypothesis a unique $T$-morphism $w:Y\to S$ such that $u_\lambda\circ w=w_\lambda$ for all $\lambda$.
By definition of the fibered product, there is a unique $T'$-morphism $w':Y\to S'$ such that $p\circ w'=w$.
One has then $u'_\lambda\circ w'=p_\lambda\circ w'_\lambda$ for each $\lambda$; on the other hand, writing that $w'$ is a $T'$-morphism and $w'_\lambda$ a $T'$-morphism, one sees that $h'_\lambda\circ u'_\lambda\circ w'=g'=h'_\lambda\circ w'_\lambda$.
The definition of $S'_\lambda$ as fibered product $S_\lambda\times_T T'$, and the fact that $w'$ is a $T'$-morphism, imply that $w'$ is the unique $T'$-morphism verifying these relations, whence the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[8.2.7]
\label{IV.8.2.7}
Given any ringed space $S$, the inductive limits with respect to any preordered (not necessarily filtered) set $\mathbf{L}$ of (not necessarily commutative) $\sh{O}_S$-Algebras exist by \sref{IV.8.2.1}, and on the other hand, for two homomorphisms of $\sh{O}_S$-Algebras $\sh{A}\to\sh{B}$, $\sh{A}\to\sh{C}$, the tensor product $\sh{B}\otimes_{\sh{A}}\sh{C}$ is the ``amalgamated sum'' in this category.

When $S$ is a prescheme, the tensor product $\sh{B}\otimes_{\sh{A}}\sh{C}$ is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_S$-Algebra whenever $\sh{A}$, $\sh{B}$, and $\sh{C}$ are \sref[I]{I.1.3.13};
one concludes that, in the category of quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_S$-Algebras, the inductive limits for any preordered index set always exist.
This allows generalizing the definition of a projective limit of preschemes and the propositions \sref{IV.8.2.3} and \sref{IV.8.2.5} to the case where the preordered set $\mathbf{L}$ is not necessarily filtered.
\end{remark}

\begin{env}{8.2.8}
\label{IV.8.2.8}
With the notations of \sref{IV.8.2.2}, set $u_{\lambda\mu}=(\psi_{\lambda\mu},\theta_{\lambda\mu})$ and $u_\lambda=(\psi_\lambda,\theta_\lambda)$;
$(S_\lambda,\psi_{\lambda\mu})$ is then a projective system of topological spaces and $(\psi_\lambda)$ a projective system of continuous maps from the spaces underlying $S$ and $S_\lambda$ respectively.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.2.9]
\label{IV.8.2.9}
With the notations of \sref{IV.8.2.8}, the projective limit of the projective system $(\psi_\lambda)$ of continuous maps is a homeomorphism from the space underlying $S$ to the projective limit of the projective system $(S_\lambda,\psi_{\lambda\mu})$ of topological spaces.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{11}
Let $T$ be the topological limit space of the system $(S_\lambda,\psi_{\lambda\mu})$ and set $\psi=\varprojlim\psi_\lambda:S\to T$.
One can reduce to the case where $S_0=S_\alpha$ for an $\alpha\in\mathbf{L}$, and $\lambda\geq\alpha$.

\medskip\noindent
(i) Let us show first that the topology of $S$ is the inverse image by $\psi$ of the topology of $T$;
in other words, if $\pi_\lambda:T\to S_\lambda$ is the canonical map, the map $\psi^{-1}(\pi_\lambda^{-1}(U_\lambda))$, where $U_\lambda$ runs through the open subsets of $S_\lambda$.
Now every open subset of $S$ is by definition a union of open subsets obtained as follows:
one considers an affine open $U_0$ of $S_0$, of ring $A_0$, so that $\psi_\alpha^{-1}(U_0)$ is the open affine of $S$ of ring $A=\Gamma(U_0,\sh{A})$, and one takes an element $f\in A$ and one considers in $\Spec(A)$, identified with $\psi_\alpha^{-1}(U_0)$, the open subset $\mathrm{D}(f)$.
These open subsets $\mathrm{D}(f)$ form a base of the topology of $S$ \sref[II]{II.1.3.1}.
Now, if one sets $A_\lambda=\Gamma(U_0,\sh{A}_\lambda)$, one has $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$ \sref[I]{I.1.3.9}, hence there exists an index $\lambda$ such that $f$ is the canonical image of an element $f_\lambda\in A_\lambda$;
one has then $\mathrm{D}(f)=\psi_\lambda^{-1}(\mathrm{D}(f_\lambda))$ \sref[I]{I.1.2.2}, and as $\psi_\lambda=\pi_\lambda\circ\psi$, our assertion is shown.

\medskip\noindent
(ii) Let us show now that $\psi$ is \emph{bijective}, which will finish the proof.
As $S$ is a Kolmogoroff space, it follows already from (i) that $\psi$ is injective, and it remains to show that $\psi$ is surjective.
One can evidently replace $T$ by an open $\pi_\alpha^{-1}(U_0)$, where $U_0$ is open affine in $S_\alpha=S_0$, hence one can reduce to the case where the $S_\lambda$ and $S$ are affine, in other words $\sh{A}_\lambda$ is the sheaf associated to an $A_0$-algebra $A_\lambda$, and $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$ the canonical homomorphisms.
By definition, an element of $T$ is a family $(\mathfrak{p}_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\mathbf{L}}$ where $\mathfrak{p}_\lambda$ is a prime ideal of $A_\lambda$ and where $\mathfrak{p}_\lambda=\varphi_{\mu\lambda}^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}_\mu)$ for $\lambda\leq\mu$.
One knows then ((5.13.3) and (5.13.1)) that there is a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$ such that $\mathfrak{p}_\lambda=\varphi_\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})$ for all $\lambda\in\mathbf{L}$, which finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

In particular, one has thus shown the

\begin{corollary}[8.2.10]
\label{IV.8.2.10}
Let $(A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\mathbf{L}}$ be a filtered inductive system of rings, and let $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$, $\varphi_\lambda:A_\lambda\to A$ the canonical homomorphisms.
The canonical map $\mathfrak{p}\mapsto(\varphi_\lambda^{-1}(\mathfrak{p}))$ is a homeomorphism from $\Spec(A)$ to the topological space $\varprojlim\Spec(A_\lambda)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[8.2.11]
\label{IV.8.2.11}
With the notations of \sref{IV.8.2.8}, for every quasi-compact open subset $U$ of $S$, there exists an index $\lambda$ and a quasi-compact open subset $U_\lambda$ of $S_\lambda$ such that $U=\psi_\lambda^{-1}(U_\lambda)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This follows from the fact that, by definition of the projective limit topology, the $\psi_\lambda^{-1}(U_\lambda)$ ($U_\lambda$ open quasi-compact of $S_\lambda$) form a base of the topology of $S$, and since the index set $\mathbf{L}$ is filtered.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.2.12]
\label{IV.8.2.12}
With the notations of \sref{IV.8.2.8}, the inductive limit of the system of homomorphisms $\theta_\lambda^\#:\psi_\lambda^*(\sh{O}_{S_\lambda})\to\sh{O}_S$ of sheaves of rings on $S$ is an isomorphism
\[
  \varinjlim_\lambda\psi_\lambda^*(\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}) \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \sh{O}_S.
  \tag{8.2.12.1}\label{IV.8.2.12.1}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
One can evidently suppose the $S_\lambda$ affine;
with the notations of the proof of \sref{IV.8.2.9}, it suffices to see that the inductive limit of the system of canonical maps $(A_\lambda)_{\mathfrak{p}_\lambda}\to A_\mathfrak{p}$ is an isomorphism, which is nothing other than ($0_I$, 5.13.3, (ii)).
\end{proof}

\oldpage[IV-3]{12}

\begin{proposition}[8.2.13]
\label{IV.8.2.13}
Suppose that the morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are open immersions, so that $S_\mu$ identifies with an open subset of $S_\lambda$ for $\lambda\leq\mu$.
Then, for all $\alpha\in\mathbf{L}$, the underlying space of the prescheme $S$ identifies with the subspace of $S_\alpha$ which is the intersection of the $S_\lambda$ for $\lambda\geq\alpha$, and the structural sheaf $\sh{O}_S$ with the sheaf induced by $\sh{O}_{S_\alpha}$ on this intersection;
more generally, for every $\sh{O}_{S_\alpha}$-Module $\sh{F}_\alpha$, $u_\alpha^*(\sh{F}_\alpha)$ identifies with the $\sh{O}_S$-Module induced by $\sh{F}_\alpha$ on $S$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from \sref{IV.8.2.9}, the definition of a projective limit of topological spaces;
moreover the $\psi_\lambda^*(\sh{O}_{S_\lambda})$ are all equal to the sheaf induced by $\sh{O}_{S_\alpha}$ on $S$;
with the notations of the proof of \sref{IV.8.2.9}, the homomorphisms $(A_\lambda)_{\mathfrak{p}_\lambda}\to(A_\mu)_{\mathfrak{p}_\mu}$ are bijective for a system $(\mathfrak{p}_\lambda)$ of prime ideals corresponding to a single point of $S$;
the assertion relative to $\sh{O}_S$ follows from \sref{IV.8.2.12}.
The last assertion then follows from the definition of $u_\alpha^*(\sh{F}_\alpha)$ ($0_I$, 4.3.1).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[8.2.14]
\label{IV.8.2.14}
The results of \sref{IV.8.2.9} and \sref{IV.8.2.12} show that $S$ is the projective limit of the projective system $(S_\lambda,u_{\lambda\mu})$ in the category of \emph{all} ringed spaces (or of all locally ringed spaces).
Indeed, let $Y$ be a ringed space, and consider a projective system of morphisms of ringed spaces $w_\lambda:Y\to S_\lambda$.
If one sets $w_\lambda=(\rho_\lambda,\omega_\lambda)$, the $\rho_\lambda$ form a projective system of continuous maps, and the $\omega_\lambda$ form a system of homomorphisms of sheaves of rings;
in view of \sref{IV.8.2.9}, their limits projective give respectively a continuous map $\rho:Y\to S$ and, in view of \sref{IV.8.2.12}, a homomorphism of sheaves of rings $\omega^\#:\rho^*(\sh{O}_S)\to\sh{O}_Y$ such that $\omega_\lambda^\#=\omega^\#\circ\rho^*(\theta_\lambda^\#)$, which shows our assertion.
\end{remark}

\label{subsection:IV.8.3}

\begin{env}{8.3.1}
\label{IV.8.3.1}
Throughout this paragraph, we assume the conditions of \sref{IV.8.2.2} are satisfied, and we keep the same notations.
\end{env}

\begin{theorem}[8.3.2]
\label{IV.8.3.2}
For all $\lambda$, let $E_\lambda$, $F_\lambda$ be two subsets of $S_\lambda$.
Set
\[
  E=\bigcap_\lambda u_\lambda^{-1}(E_\lambda),\qquad
  F=\bigcup_\lambda u_\lambda^{-1}(F_\lambda).
  \tag{8.3.2.1}\label{IV.8.3.2.1}
\]
Suppose the following conditions are satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] For all $\lambda$, $E_\lambda$ is pro-constructible and $F_\lambda$ is ind-constructible (1.9.4).
  \item[\rm(ii)] For $\lambda\leq\mu$, we have
  \[
    E_\mu\subset u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(E_\lambda),\qquad F_\mu\supset u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(F_\lambda).
  \]
  \item[\rm(iii)] There exists $\alpha\in\mathbf{L}$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact (which implies that $S_\lambda$ is quasi-compact for $\lambda\geq\alpha$).
\end{enumerate}
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] $E\subset F$.
  \item[\rm b)] There exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $u_\lambda^{-1}(E_\lambda)\subset u_\lambda^{-1}(F_\lambda)$ (and then $u_\mu^{-1}(E_\mu)\subset u_\mu^{-1}(F_\mu)$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$).
  \item[\rm c)] There exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $E_\lambda\subset F_\lambda$ (and then $E_\mu\subset F_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$).
\end{enumerate}
\oldpage[IV-3]{13}
The remarks in parentheses in \emph{b)} and \emph{c)} follow from (ii).
Set
\[
  G_\lambda=E_\lambda\cap(S_\lambda-F_\lambda),\qquad G=E\cap(S-F).
\]
Then $G_\lambda$ is a pro-constructible part of $S_\lambda$ (1.9.5, (i)), and by (8.3.2.1) and (ii), one has
\[
  G_\mu\subset u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(G_\lambda)\quad\text{for }\lambda\leq\mu
\]
\[
  G=\bigcap_\lambda u_\lambda^{-1}(G_\lambda).
\]
One is thus reduced to proving the particular case of \sref{IV.8.3.2} corresponding to $F_\lambda=\emp$ for all $\lambda$:
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[8.3.3]
\label{IV.8.3.3}
For all $\lambda$, let $E_\lambda$ be a pro-constructible part of $S_\lambda$ such that, for $\lambda\leq\mu$, $E_\mu\subset u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(E_\lambda)$.
Suppose there exists $\alpha\in\mathbf{L}$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] $E=\bigcap_\lambda u_\lambda^{-1}(E_\lambda)=\emp$.
  \item[\rm b)] There exists $\lambda$ such that $u_\lambda^{-1}(E_\lambda)=\emp$ (and then $u_\mu^{-1}(E_\mu)=\emp$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$).
  \item[\rm c)] There exists $\lambda$ such that $E_\lambda=\emp$ (and then $E_\mu=\emp$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
It is clear that \emph{c)} implies \emph{b)}: since $S_\lambda$ is quasi-compact, so is $S$ \sref{IV.8.2.9};
$E_\lambda$ being pro-constructible, so is $u_\lambda^{-1}(E_\lambda)$ (1.9.5, (vi));
then the filtering decreasing family of pro-constructible sets $u_\lambda^{-1}(E_\lambda)$ has empty intersection, hence (1.9.9) one of them is empty.

Finally, let us show that \emph{b)} implies \emph{c)}.
As $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact and $\mathbf{L}$ is filtered, one can replace $S_\alpha$ by an open affine, hence suppose \sref{IV.8.2.2} that $S$ and the $S_\lambda$ for $\lambda\geq\alpha$ are affine;
one has then (1.9.2.1), for $\lambda\geq\alpha$,
\[
  u_\lambda(S)=\bigcap_{\mu\geq\lambda}u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu).
\]
Hence $E_\lambda\cap u_\lambda(S)=\bigcap_{\mu\geq\lambda}(E_\lambda\cap u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu))$.
Now, as $u_\lambda$ and the $u_{\lambda\mu}$ are quasi-compact, $u_\lambda(S)$ and $u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu)$ are pro-constructible in $S_\lambda$ (1.9.5, (vii)), hence the sets $E_\lambda\cap u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu)$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ form a filtering decreasing family of pro-constructible parts of $S_\lambda$.
As $S_\lambda$ is quasi-compact, the hypothesis \emph{b)} implies that the intersection of this family is empty, hence (1.9.9) one of the sets $E_\lambda\cap u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu)$ is empty, hence $E_\mu\subset u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(E_\lambda)$ is empty.
Q.E.D.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.3.4]
\label{IV.8.3.4}
For all $\lambda$, let $F_\lambda$ be an ind-constructible part of $S_\lambda$ such that for $\lambda\leq\mu$, $u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(F_\lambda)\subset F_\mu$.
Suppose there exists $\alpha\in\mathbf{L}$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] The set $F=\bigcup_\lambda u_\lambda^{-1}(F_\lambda)$ is equal to $S$.
  \item[\rm b)] There exists $\lambda$ such that $u_\lambda^{-1}(F_\lambda)=S$ (and then $u_\mu^{-1}(F_\mu)=S$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$).
\end{enumerate}
This follows immediately from \sref{IV.8.3.3} by passage to complements.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[8.3.5]
\label{IV.8.3.5}
\oldpage[IV-3]{14}
For all $\lambda$, let $E_\lambda$, $F_\lambda$ be two constructible parts of $S_\lambda$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $E_\mu\subset u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(E_\lambda)$ and $F_\mu\supset u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(F_\lambda)$.
Suppose there exists an index $\alpha$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact.
Then, for $E\subset F$ (resp.\ $E=F$), it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda$ such that $E_\lambda\subset F_\lambda$ (resp.\ $E_\lambda=F_\lambda$), in which case we also have $E_\mu\subset F_\mu$ (resp.\ $E_\mu=F_\mu$) for $\mu\geq\lambda$.
This is a particular case of \sref{IV.8.3.2}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[8.3.6]
\label{IV.8.3.6}
Suppose there exists $\alpha$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact.
For $S=\emp$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda$ such that $S_\lambda=\emp$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[8.3.7]
\label{IV.8.3.7}
On a, for all $\lambda$,
\[
  u_\lambda(S)=\bigcap_{\mu\geq\lambda}u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu).
  \tag{8.3.7.1}\label{IV.8.3.7.1}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
It is clear that the first member of \eqref{IV.8.3.7.1} is contained in the second.
Let $s$ be a point of $S_\lambda$ and set $X_\lambda=\Spec(k(s))$;
consider the projective system $(X_\mu,v_{\mu\nu})$ where $X_\mu=X_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S_\mu$ and $v_{\mu\nu}=1\times u_{\mu\nu}$ for $\lambda\leq\mu\leq\nu$;
its projective limit is $X=X_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S$ \sref{IV.8.2.5}.
If $s\in\bigcap_{\mu\geq\lambda}u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu)$, this implies that $X_\mu\neq\emp$ for all $\mu\geq\lambda$ \sref[I]{I.3.4.8};
it follows from \sref{IV.8.3.6} that $X\neq\emp$, hence $s\in u_\lambda(S)$, hence \sref[I]{I.3.4.8}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.3.8]
\label{IV.8.3.8}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] For the morphism $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ to be dominant (resp.\ surjective), it is necessary and sufficient that for $\mu\geq\lambda$ the morphism $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ be dominant (resp.\ surjective).
  \item[\rm(ii)] If, for an index $\lambda$, the morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are flat (resp.\ faithfully flat) for $\mu\geq\lambda$, then the morphism $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ is flat (resp.\ faithfully flat).
  \item[\rm(iii)] Suppose that the morphisms $u_\mu:S\to S_\mu$ are surjective for $\mu\geq\lambda$.
  For $u_\lambda$ to be an open (resp.\ universally open) morphism, it is necessary and sufficient that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $u_{\lambda\mu}$ be an open (resp.\ universally open) morphism.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{15}
(i) As $u_\lambda(S)\subset u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu)$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$, the necessity of the conditions is trivial, and it follows immediately from \eqref{IV.8.3.7.1} that if the $u_{\lambda\mu}$ are surjective, so is $u_\lambda$.
Suppose now the $u_{\lambda\mu}$ dominant for $\mu\geq\lambda$, and consider in $S_\lambda$ an open quasi-compact non-empty subset $U_\lambda$;
then $u_{\lambda\mu}(S_\mu)\cap U_\lambda$ is non-empty for all $\mu$, hence by \eqref{IV.8.3.7.1}, $u_\lambda(S)\cap U_\lambda$ is non-empty, hence $u_\lambda$ is dominant.

We note that it can happen that all the $u_{\lambda\mu}$ are open without it being so for $u_\lambda$ if the $u_\mu$ are not surjective.

\medskip\noindent
(ii) By (i), it suffices to consider the case where the $u_{\lambda\mu}$ are flat;
one can then reduce to the case where $S_\lambda$ is affine, hence the $S_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ and $S$ are also affine, and the assertion follows from ($0_I$, 6.2.3).

\medskip\noindent
(iii) By \sref{IV.8.2.5} and \sref[I]{I.3.5.2}, it suffices to treat the case of open morphisms.
As $u_\lambda=u_{\lambda\mu}\circ u_\mu$ and $u_\mu$ is surjective, one knows that if $u_\lambda$ is open it is the same of $u_{\lambda\mu}$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Top.\ g\'{e}n.}, chap.~I\textsuperscript{er}, 3\textsuperscript{e}~\'{e}d., \textsection~5, n\textsuperscript{o}~1, prop.~1).
\end{proof}

Conversely, to show that $u_\lambda$ is open when all the $u_{\lambda\mu}$ are for $\mu\geq\lambda$,
it suffices to see that for every open quasi-compact $V$ of $S$, $u_\lambda(V)$ is open in $S_\lambda$;
but there exists then $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $V=u_\mu^{-1}(V_\mu)$, where $V_\mu$ is open in $S_\mu$ \sref{IV.8.2.11};
as $u_\mu$ is surjective, one has $V_\mu=u_\mu(V)$ and $u_\lambda(V)=u_{\lambda\mu}(u_\mu(V))$ is therefore open by hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[8.3.11]
\label{IV.8.3.11}
Suppose there exists $\alpha\in\mathbf{L}$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
Then the canonical maps \eqref{IV.8.3.9.2}, \eqref{IV.8.3.9.3}, \eqref{IV.8.3.9.4}, and \eqref{IV.8.3.9.5} are \emph{bijective}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the preceding remark, it follows from \sref{IV.8.3.5} that under the hypothesis that for an $\alpha\in\mathbf{L}$, $S_\alpha$ is \emph{quasi-compact}, the map \eqref{IV.8.3.9.2} is \emph{injective}.
Furthermore:

It follows that these maps are \emph{surjective};
as every constructible part of $S$ is a finite union of sets of the form $U\cap V^c$, where $U$ and $V$ are open and constructible, it will suffice to prove that \eqref{IV.8.3.9.3} is surjective (and also \eqref{IV.8.3.9.4}, by passage to complements).
Now, as the morphisms $S_\lambda\to S_\alpha$ and $S\to S_\alpha$ are separated, the $S_\lambda$ for $\lambda\geq\alpha$ and $S$ are quasi-compact and quasi-separated, and one knows that the constructible open parts in such a prescheme are the quasi-compact open subsets (1.8.1).
The conclusion follows hence from \sref{IV.8.2.11}, except for the map \eqref{IV.8.3.9.5}.
To prove that the latter is surjective, consider a locally closed and constructible part $Z$ in $S$;
$Z$ is then quasi-compact ($0_{\mathrm{III}}$, 9.1.8).
As every point $x\in Z$ admits by hypothesis a quasi-compact open neighborhood $V_x$ in $S$ such that $Z\cap V_x$ is closed in $V_x$, one can cover $Z$ by a finite number of the $V_x$, hence there is a quasi-compact open $U$ containing $Z$ such that $Z$ is closed in $U$;
as $Z$ is also constructible in $S$, it is also constructible in $U$ ($0_{\mathrm{III}}$, 9.1.8).
One knows \sref{IV.8.2.11} that there is an index $\lambda$ and a quasi-compact open $U_\lambda$ of $S_\lambda$ such that $U=u_\lambda^{-1}(U_\lambda)$.
Applying to $U$ (which is a projective limit of the $U_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(U_\lambda)$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$) the fact that \eqref{IV.8.3.9.4} is surjective, one sees that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ and a closed constructible set $Z_\mu$ \emph{in} $U_\mu$ such that $Z=u_\mu^{-1}(Z_\mu)$.
But as the canonical immersion $U_\mu\to S_\mu$ is quasi-compact by hypothesis (1.2.7), each $U_\mu$ is open quasi-compact in $S_\mu$, so that $Z_\mu$ is a constructible part of $S_\mu$;
each of the $Z_{\mu\lambda}$ is of finite presentation on $S_\mu$ (1.6.2).
This finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.3.12]
\label{IV.8.3.12}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, and let, for all $\lambda$, $Z_\lambda$ be a constructible part of $S_\lambda$ such that $Z_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Z_\lambda)$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$.
\oldpage[IV-3]{17}
For $Z$ to be open (resp.\ closed, resp.\ locally closed) in $S$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\alpha$ such that $Z_\lambda$ is so in $S_\lambda$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Cover $S_\alpha$ by a finite number of affine open subsets $U_\alpha^{(i)}$;
then the $U^{(i)}=u_\alpha^{-1}(U_\alpha^{(i)})$ form a finite open affine covering of $S$,
and for $Z$ to be open (resp.\ closed) in $S$, it is necessary and sufficient that each $Z\cap U^{(i)}$ be open (resp.\ closed) in $U^{(i)}$.
As $\mathbf{L}$ is filtered and $S_\alpha$ is affine, hence quasi-compact and quasi-separated, the result follows from \sref{IV.8.3.11}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.3.13]
\label{IV.8.3.13}
Suppose that the morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are flat for $\lambda\leq\mu$, and that there exists $\alpha$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact.
For all $\lambda$, let $Z'_\lambda$, $Z''_\lambda$ be two pro-constructible parts of $S_\lambda$, such that $Z'_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Z'_\lambda)$ and $Z''_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Z''_\lambda)$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$;
suppose in addition that $\overline{Z''_\alpha}$ is constructible in $S_\alpha$.
Let $Z'=u_\lambda^{-1}(Z'_\lambda)$, $Z''=u_\lambda^{-1}(Z''_\lambda)$; for $Z''\subset\overline{Z'}$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $Z''_\lambda\subset\overline{Z'_\lambda}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, one knows that $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ is also a flat morphism for all $\lambda$ \sref{IV.8.3.8};
as $Z'_\lambda$ is pro-constructible, the closure of $Z'_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ (resp.\ of $Z'$) in $S_\mu$ (resp.\ $S$) is equal to $u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(\overline{Z'_\lambda})$ (resp.\ $u_\lambda^{-1}(\overline{Z'_\lambda})$) (2.3.10).
As the $u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(\overline{Z'_\lambda})$ and $u_\lambda^{-1}(\overline{Z'_\lambda})$ are constructible (1.8.2), the conclusion follows from \sref{IV.8.3.2}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Criteria for irreducibility and connectedness for projective limits of preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.8.4}

\begin{proposition}[8.4.1]
\label{IV.8.4.1}
Suppose there exists an index $\alpha$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] If $S$ is not irreducible and if moreover the underlying space of $S$ is noetherian and $S_\alpha$ is quasi-separated, there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $S_\mu$ is not irreducible.
  \item[\rm(ii)] If $S$ is not connected, there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $S_\mu$ is not connected.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $S$ is the union of two distinct closed sets $S'$, $S''$ (resp.\ disjoint non-empty closed sets $S'$, $S''$) in $S$.
In case (i), $S'$ and $S''$ are constructible since the space $S$ is noetherian.
By \sref{IV.8.3.11}, there exist $\lambda\geq\alpha$ and two closed constructible sets $S'_\lambda$, $S''_\lambda$ of $S_\lambda$ such that $S'=u_\lambda^{-1}(S'_\lambda)$, $S''=u_\lambda^{-1}(S''_\lambda)$;
as $S=S'\cup S''$, it follows from \sref{IV.8.3.11} that one can suppose $S_\lambda=S'_\lambda\cup S''_\lambda$;
as $S'_\lambda$ and $S''_\lambda$ are distinct from $S_\lambda$, this proves that $S_\lambda$ is not irreducible.

In case (ii), $S'$ and $S''$ are open quasi-compact, hence, by \sref{IV.8.2.11}, there exist $\lambda\geq\alpha$ and two open quasi-compact subsets $S'_\lambda$, $S''_\lambda$ of $S_\lambda$ such that $S'=u_\lambda^{-1}(S'_\lambda)$, $S''=u_\lambda^{-1}(S''_\lambda)$.
Moreover, $S'$ and $S''$ are at once pro-constructible and ind-constructible, hence constructible (1.9.11), and it follows from \sref{IV.8.3.5} that one can take $\lambda$ such that $S_\lambda=S'_\lambda\cup S''_\lambda$ and $S'_\lambda\cap S''_\lambda=\emp$;
this shows that $S_\lambda$ is not connected.
\end{proof}

\oldpage[IV-3]{18}

\begin{proposition}[8.4.2]
\label{IV.8.4.2}
Suppose that the underlying space of $S$ is noetherian and that one of the two following conditions is satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] For $\lambda\leq\mu$, $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ is dominant, and there exists $\alpha$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact.
  \item[\rm b)] There exists $\alpha$ such that the underlying space of $S_\alpha$ is noetherian, and for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $u_{\lambda\mu}$ is a homeomorphism from $S_\mu$ to a subspace of $S_\lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
Under these conditions, there exists $\lambda$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] For every irreducible component $Y_i$ of $S$ $(1\leq i\leq m)$, $\overline{u_\mu(Y_i)}$ is an irreducible component of $S_\mu$, and the map $Y_i\mapsto\overline{u_\mu(Y_i)}$ is a bijection from the set of irreducible components of $S$ to the set of irreducible components of $S_\mu$.
  \item[\rm(ii)] For every connected component $C_j$ of $S$ $(1\leq j\leq n)$, $\overline{u_\mu(C_j)}$ is a connected component of $S_\mu$, and the map $C_j\mapsto\overline{u_\mu(C_j)}$ is a bijection from the set of connected components of $S$ to the set of connected components of $S_\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first establish the

\begin{lemma}[8.4.2.1]
Under condition \emph{a)} or \emph{b)} of \sref{IV.8.4.2}, there exists $\lambda$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $u_\mu:S\to S_\mu$ is dominant.
\end{lemma}

In case \emph{a)}, this has already been proved without supposing $S$ noetherian \sref{IV.8.3.8}.
In case \emph{b)}, set $Z_\alpha=\overline{u_\alpha(S)}$;
as a closed part of the noetherian $S_\alpha$, $Z_\alpha$ is constructible, and as $u_\alpha^{-1}(Z_\alpha)=S$, it follows from \sref{IV.8.3.5} that there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $Z_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Z_\lambda)=S_\mu$.
But as $u_{\lambda\mu}$ is a homeomorphism from $S_\mu$ to a subspace and the composed map $S\to Z_\mu\to Z_\alpha$ is dominant, it is the same of $S\to Z_\mu=S_\mu$.

The lemma being established, we can suppose that for all $\lambda$, $u_\lambda$ is dominant.

\medskip\noindent
(i) Each $S_\lambda$ is a union of the $\overline{u_\lambda(Y_i)}$, which are irreducible.
On the other hand, if $U_i$ is the complement of $S$ of the union of the $Y_j$ of index $j\neq i$ $(1\leq i\leq m)$, the $U_i$ are pairwise disjoint and $Y_i=\overline{U_i}$ ($0_I$, 2.1.6).
As the space $S$ is noetherian, the $U_i$ are quasi-compact, hence there exists an index $\lambda$ and open subsets $U_{i\lambda}$ of $S_\lambda$ such that $U_i=u_\lambda^{-1}(U_{i\lambda})$ for $1\leq i\leq m$ \sref{IV.8.2.11}.
One concludes that if one sets $U_{i\mu}=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(U_{i\lambda})$ for $\lambda\leq\mu$, the $U_{i\mu}$ are pairwise disjoint, the $U_i=u_\mu^{-1}(U_{i\mu})$ are the same and $u_\mu$ is dominant.
Consequently, no $\overline{U_{i\mu}}$ is contained in another;
hence $\overline{U_{i\mu}}=u_\mu(Y_i)$, which proves that the $\overline{U_{i\mu}}$ are the irreducible components of $S_\mu$, and finishes the proof.

\medskip\noindent
(ii) As the space $S$ is noetherian, the $C_j$ are open and closed in $S$ and quasi-compact;
the same reasoning as in (i) shows that there exist $\lambda$ and open subsets $V_{j\lambda}$ of $S_\lambda$ such that $C_j=u_\lambda^{-1}(V_{j\lambda})$ for $1\leq j\leq n$.
One sees also, as in (i), that if one sets $V_{j\mu}=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(V_{j\lambda})$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$, the $V_{j\mu}$ are pairwise disjoint, and $u_\mu(C_j)$ is dense in $V_{j\mu}$.
Furthermore, it follows from \sref{IV.8.3.4} that for $\mu$ large enough, the union of the $V_{j\mu}$ is $S_\mu$, since every open subset in a prescheme is ind-constructible (1.9.6).
The $V_{j\mu}$ are thus the connected components of $S_\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.4.3]
\label{IV.8.4.3}
Suppose one of the conditions \emph{a)}, \emph{b)} of \sref{IV.8.4.2} is satisfied, the underlying space of $S$ being noetherian;
then, for $S$ to be irreducible, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda$ such that $S_\mu$ is irreducible for all $\mu\geq\lambda$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[8.4.4]
\label{IV.8.4.4}
\oldpage[IV-3]{19}
Suppose there exists $\alpha$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact and that, for $\lambda\leq\mu$, $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ is dominant.
Then, for $S$ to be connected, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda_0$ such that $S_\lambda$ is connected for all $\lambda\geq\lambda_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The condition is sufficient by \sref{IV.8.4.1};
on the other hand, by \sref{IV.8.3.8}, (i), we have seen that $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ is dominant for all $\lambda$ sufficiently large, so if $S$ is connected, since $u_\lambda(S)$ is dense in $S_\lambda$ and connected, $S_\lambda$ is connected.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.4.5]
\label{IV.8.4.5}
Let $k$ be a field, $X$ a $k$-prescheme quasi-compact and connected.
For $X$ to be geometrically connected (4.5.2), it is necessary and sufficient that, for every finite and separable extension $K$ of $k$, $X\otimes_k K$ be connected.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The condition is trivially necessary.
To see that it is sufficient, we note that if $\Omega$ is an algebraic closure of $k$, $X\otimes_k\Omega$ is connected (4.5.1) and, for $k\subset k'\subset k''\subset\Omega$, the morphism $X\otimes_k k''\to X\otimes_k k'$ is surjective.
One is thus reduced, by \sref{IV.8.4.4}, to proving that $X\otimes_k k'$ is connected for every finite extension $k'$ of $k$.
But if $K$ is the largest separable extension contained in $k'$, the morphism $X\otimes_k k'\to X\otimes_k K$ is finite, surjective and radiciel, hence (2.4.5) a homeomorphism, and as $X\otimes_k K$ is connected by hypothesis, so is $X\otimes_k k'$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[8.4.6]
\label{IV.8.4.6}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] The proof of \sref{IV.8.4.2} shows that the conclusion of the proposition is valid if one only supposes that the underlying space of $S$ is noetherian, that there exists $\alpha$ such that $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact, and finally that the $u_\lambda:S\to S_\lambda$ are dominant.
  \item[\rm(ii)] On the other hand, the conclusion of \sref{IV.8.4.2} can fail when the $u_\lambda$ are not dominant for $\lambda$ sufficiently large, even when the $S_\lambda$ and $S$ are noetherian, as the following example shows, taking $\bb{N}$ for the set of indices, all the $S_n$ equal to $\Spec(A\times K)=\Spec(A)\amalg\Spec(K)$, where $K$ is a field, $A$ a $K$-algebra of any kind, and all the morphisms $u_{n,n+1}$ equal to the same morphism corresponding to the homomorphism $(x,y)\mapsto(j(y),y)$ of $A\times K$ into itself, where $j:K\to A$ is the canonical homomorphism.
  One easily verifies that the inductive limit of this system of rings is $K$, the canonical homomorphism $u_n$ corresponding to the second projection $A\times K\to K$.
  One sees then that $S=\Spec(K)$ is irreducible even though none of the $S_n$ are connected.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\subsection{Modules of finite presentation on a projective limit of preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.8.5}

\begin{env}{8.5.1}
\label{IV.8.5.1}
We continue to use the notations and hypotheses of \sref{IV.8.2.2};
we will moreover limit ourselves in this section to the case where $S_0$ is one of the $S_\lambda$, which one can always reduce to.

When, in this section, we consider a family $(\sh{F}_\lambda)$, where, for all $\lambda\in\mathbf{L}$, $\sh{F}_\lambda$ is an $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Module, it will be understood that this family satisfies the condition
\[
  \sh{F}_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(\sh{F}_\lambda) \quad\text{for }\lambda\leq\mu.
  \tag{8.5.1.1}\label{IV.8.5.1.1}
\]
We will then set
\oldpage[IV-3]{20}
\[
  \sh{F}=u_\lambda^*(\sh{F}_\lambda)
  \tag{8.5.1.2}\label{IV.8.5.1.2}
\]
which is an $\sh{O}_S$-Module not depending on the index $\lambda$, by virtue of \eqref{IV.8.5.1.1}.
\end{env}

Let now $(\sh{F}_\lambda)$, $(\sh{G}_\lambda)$ be two such families of $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Modules.
It is clear that the maps $f_\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_\lambda)$ from $\operatorname{Hom}_{\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}}(\sh{F}_\lambda,\sh{G}_\lambda)$ into $\operatorname{Hom}_{\sh{O}_{S_\mu}}(\sh{F}_\mu,\sh{G}_\mu)$ define an inductive system of abelian groups $(\operatorname{Hom}_{\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}}(\sh{F}_\lambda,\sh{G}_\lambda),u_{\lambda\mu}^*)$,
and that the maps $f_\lambda\mapsto u_\lambda^*(f_\lambda)$ form an \emph{inductive system} of homomorphisms of abelian groups
\[
  u_\lambda^*:\operatorname{Hom}_{\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}}(\sh{F}_\lambda,\sh{G}_\lambda)\to
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\sh{O}_S}(\sh{F},\sh{G});
\]
whence, passing to the inductive limit, a canonical homomorphism of abelian groups
\[
  u_{\sh{F},\sh{G}}:\varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}}(\sh{F}_\lambda,\sh{G}_\lambda)\to
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\sh{O}_S}(\sh{F},\sh{G}).
  \tag{8.5.1.3}\label{IV.8.5.1.3}
\]

We note that when $\sh{F}_\lambda=\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$, the condition \eqref{IV.8.5.1.1} is satisfied, and $\sh{F}=\sh{O}_S$;
the homomorphism \eqref{IV.8.5.1.3} gives then ($0_I$, 5.1.1) a canonical homomorphism of abelian groups
\[
  u_{\sh{G}}:\varinjlim\Gamma(S_\lambda,\sh{G}_\lambda)\to\Gamma(S,\sh{G}).
  \tag{8.5.1.4}\label{IV.8.5.1.4}
\]

\begin{theorem}[8.5.2]
\label{IV.8.5.2}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact (resp.\ quasi-compact and quasi-separated) and that, for an $\lambda\in\mathbf{L}$, $\sh{F}_\lambda$ is quasi-coherent and of finite type (resp.\ of presentation finie) and $\sh{G}_\lambda$ quasi-coherent.
  Then the homomorphism $u_{\sh{F},\sh{G}}$ is \emph{injective} (resp.\ \emph{bijective}).
  \item[\rm(ii)] Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
  For every $\sh{O}_S$-Module quasi-coherent $\sh{F}$ of finite presentation, there exist $\lambda\in\mathbf{L}$ and an $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Module quasi-coherent $\sh{F}_\lambda$ of finite presentation such that $\sh{F}$ is isomorphic to $u_\lambda^*(\sh{F}_\lambda)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{21}
(i) One can evidently reduce to the case where $S_0=S_\lambda$ and the morphisms $u_{0\lambda}:S_\lambda\to S_0$ are affine.
Consider first the case where $S_0=\Spec(A_0)$ is \emph{affine}.
Then the assertion (i) is equivalent to the

\begin{lemma}[8.5.2.1]
\label{IV.8.5.2.1}
Let $A_0$ be a ring, $(A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\mathbf{L}}$ an inductive system of $A_0$-algebras, $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$;
let $M_0$, $N_0$ be two $A_0$-modules, and set $M_\lambda=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda$, $N_\lambda=N_0\otimes_{A_0}A_\lambda$, $M=M_0\otimes_{A_0}A=\varinjlim M_\lambda$, $N=N_0\otimes_{A_0}A=\varinjlim N_\lambda$.
If $M_0$ is of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation), the canonical homomorphism
\[
  \varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{A_\lambda}(M_\lambda,N_\lambda)\to\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,N)
  \tag{8.5.2.2}\label{IV.8.5.2.2}
\]
is injective (resp.\ bijective).
\end{lemma}

One knows that there are canonical functorial isomorphisms
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{A_\lambda}(M_\lambda,N_\lambda)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{A_0}(M_0,N_\lambda),\qquad
  \operatorname{Hom}_A(M,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{A_0}(M_0,N)
\]
so that \eqref{IV.8.5.2.2} is nothing other, up to canonical isomorphisms, than the canonical homomorphism
\[
  \varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{A_0}(M_0,N_\lambda)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A_0}(M_0,\varinjlim N_\lambda)
  \tag{8.5.2.3}\label{IV.8.5.2.3}
\]
which to every inductive system of $A_0$-module homomorphisms $\theta_\lambda:M_0\to N_\lambda$ associates its inductive limit.
Now, if $M_0$ is of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation), one has an exact sequence $A_0^n\to M_0\to 0$ (resp.\ $A_0^p\to A_0^n\to M_0\to 0$);
as it is clear that \eqref{IV.8.5.2.3} is bijective when $M_0=A_0^n$, it suffices to use the left exactness of the $\operatorname{Hom}_{A_0}(M_0,P)$ functor and the exactness of the functor $\varinjlim$ (in the category of abelian groups) to conclude.

Let us now pass to the case where $S_0$ is quasi-compact, and let $(U_i)$ be a finite open affine covering of $S_0$;
for all $\lambda$, the $U_{i\lambda}=u_{0\lambda}^{-1}(U_i)$ form a finite open affine covering of $S_\lambda$, and the $V_i=u_0^{-1}(U_i)$ a finite open affine covering of $S$.
To see that $u_{\sh{F},\sh{G}}$ is injective, it suffices, if $f=u_\lambda^*(f_\lambda)$ is such that $f=0$, to show that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $f_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_\lambda)=0$.
By the lemma \sref{IV.8.5.2.1}, for each $i$ there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $f_\mu|U_{i,\mu}=0$;
it thus suffices to take $\mu$ larger than all the $\lambda_i$.

Suppose moreover $S_0$ quasi-separated and $\sh{F}_0$ of finite presentation, and let $f:\sh{F}\to\sh{G}$ be an $\sh{O}_S$-homomorphism.
By the lemma \sref{IV.8.5.2.1}, for each $i$, there exist $\lambda_i$ and a homomorphism $f_{\lambda_i}^{(i)}:\sh{F}_{\lambda_i}|U_{i,\lambda_i}\to\sh{G}_{\lambda_i}|U_{i,\lambda_i}$ such that $u_{\lambda_i}^*(f_{\lambda_i}^{(i)})=f|V_i$ (with the notations of the lemma).
As $\mathbf{L}$ is filtered, one can suppose all the $\lambda_i$ equal to a same $\lambda$.
Note now that $S_\lambda$ is quasi-separated ($0_I$, 5.2.5), and $\sh{F}_\lambda$ is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Module of finite presentation;
as, for every pair of indices $i$, $j$, $U_{ij\lambda}=U_{i\lambda}\cap U_{j\lambda}$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated (1.2.7), it follows from \sref{IV.8.2.5} that for every pair $(i,j)$, there exists an index $\lambda_{ij}\geq\lambda$ such that $u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_{\lambda}^{(i)}|U_{ij\lambda})=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_{\lambda}^{(j)}|U_{ij\lambda})$ for $\mu\geq\lambda_{ij}$;
taking $\mu$ larger than all the $\lambda_{ij}$, the $u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_\lambda^{(i)})|U_{ij\mu}$ coincide in $U_{i\mu}\cap U_{j\mu}$ for all pairs $(i,j)$, and therefore define an $S_\mu$-morphism $f_\mu:\sh{F}_\mu\to\sh{G}_\mu$ such that $f=u_\mu^*(f_\mu)$.

Before passing to the proof of (ii), let us note the following corollaries of (i):

\begin{corollary}[8.5.2.4]
\label{IV.8.5.2.4}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, $\sh{F}_\lambda$ quasi-coherent of finite type, $\sh{G}_\lambda$ quasi-coherent of finite presentation.
Let $f_\lambda:\sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}_\lambda$ be a homomorphism.
For $f=u_\lambda^*(f_\lambda):\sh{F}\to\sh{G}$ to be an isomorphism, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $f_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_\lambda):\sh{F}_\mu\to\sh{G}_\mu$ is an isomorphism.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
One can always suppose $S_\lambda=S_0$; the question being local on $S_0$, one can moreover ($S_0$ being quasi-compact and $\mathbf{L}$ filtered) reduce to the case where $S_0$ is affine, hence quasi-separated.
The condition being trivially sufficient, it remains to show it is necessary: now, by hypothesis, there exists an $\sh{O}_S$-homomorphism $g:\sh{G}\to\sh{F}$ such that $g\circ f=1_{\sh{F}}$ and $f\circ g=1_{\sh{G}}$.
As $\sh{G}$ is of finite presentation, there exist $\nu\geq\lambda$ and a homomorphism $g_\nu:\sh{G}_\nu\to\sh{F}_\nu$ such that $g=u_\nu^*(g_\nu)$ by (\sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i)); one thus has $u_\nu^*(g_\nu\circ f_\nu)=1_{\sh{F}}$
\oldpage[IV-3]{22}
and $u_\nu^*(f_\nu\circ g_\nu)=1_{\sh{G}}$; taking into account that $\sh{F}_\nu$ and $\sh{G}_\nu$ are of finite type, one concludes by (\sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i)) that there exists $\mu\geq\nu$ such that $g_\mu\circ f_\mu=1_{\sh{F}_\mu}$ and $f_\mu\circ g_\mu=1_{\sh{G}_\mu}$, whence the corollary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.5.2.5]
\label{IV.8.5.2.5}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
Suppose that $\sh{F}_\lambda$, $\sh{G}_\lambda$ are $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Modules quasi-coherent of finite presentation.
For $\sh{F}$ and $\sh{G}$ to be isomorphic, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $\sh{F}_\mu$ and $\sh{G}_\mu$ are isomorphic.
Moreover, for every isomorphism $f:\sh{F}\xrightarrow{\sim}\sh{G}$, there exist $\nu\geq\mu$ and an isomorphism $f_\nu:\sh{F}_\nu\xrightarrow{\sim}\sh{G}_\nu$ such that $f=u_\nu^*(f_\nu)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This follows from \sref{IV.8.5.2.4} and from (\sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i)) since every homomorphism $f:\sh{F}\to\sh{G}$ is of the form $u_\mu^*(f_\mu)$ for some $\mu\geq\lambda$ and some homomorphism $f_\mu:\sh{F}_\mu\to\sh{G}_\mu$.
\end{proof}

\medskip\noindent
(ii) Consider first the case where $S_0=\Spec(A_0)$ is \emph{affine}.
Then the assertion is equivalent to the lemma (5.13.7.1).
In the general case, starting from a finite open affine covering $(U_i)$ of $S_0$, one deduces \sref{IV.8.5.2.1} that for each $i$, there exist $\lambda(i)$ and an $\sh{O}_{U_{i,\lambda(i)}}$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation $\sh{F}_i$ such that $u_{\lambda(i)}^*(\sh{F}_i)=\sh{F}|V_i$ (with the notations of the proof of (i)).
As $\mathbf{L}$ is filtered, one can suppose all the $\lambda(i)$ equal to a same $\lambda$.
Note now that $S_\lambda$ is quasi-separated (1.2.7), that $\sh{F}_\lambda$ is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Module of finite presentation;
as, for every pair $(i,j)$, $U_{ij\lambda}=U_{i\lambda}\cap U_{j\lambda}$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated (1.2.7), it follows from \eqref{IV.8.2.5} that for every pair $(i,j)$, there exists an isomorphism $\theta_{ij}:\sh{F}_i|U_{ij\lambda}\xrightarrow{\sim}u_{\lambda,\lambda_{ij}}^*(\sh{F}_j)|U_{ij\lambda}$ such that $u_\lambda^*(\theta_{ij})$ is the identity automorphism of $\sh{F}|(V_i\cap V_j)$;
one can further suppose all the $\lambda_{ij}$ equal to $\lambda$, so that $\theta_{ij}:\sh{F}_i|U_{ij\lambda}\xrightarrow{\sim}\sh{F}_j|U_{ij\lambda}$.
Finally, for all triples $i$, $j$, $k$, if one sets $U_{ijk,\lambda}=U_{i\lambda}\cap U_{j\lambda}\cap U_{k\lambda}$, $U_{ijk,\lambda}$ is quasi-compact, and if $\theta'_{ij}$, $\theta'_{jk}$ and $\theta'_{ik}$ denote the restrictions of $\theta_{ij}$, $\theta_{jk}$ and $\theta_{ik}$ to $U_{ijk,\lambda}$, one has $u_\lambda^*(\theta'_{ik})=u_\lambda^*(\theta'_{ij}\circ\theta'_{jk})$.
There is thus, by (i), an index $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $u_{\lambda\mu}^*(\theta'_{ik})=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(\theta'_{ij}\circ\theta'_{jk})$, hence the isomorphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}^*(\theta_{ij}):u_{\lambda\mu}^*(\sh{F}_i)|U_{ij\mu}\xrightarrow{\sim}u_{\lambda\mu}^*(\sh{F}_j)|U_{ij\mu}$ satisfy the gluing condition, and define on $S_\mu$ an $\sh{O}_{S_\mu}$-Module quasi-coherent $\sh{F}_\mu$ of finite presentation ($0_I$, 3.3.1) such that $\sh{F}$ is isomorphic to $u_\mu^*(\sh{F}_\mu)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{scholie}[8.5.3]
\label{IV.8.5.3}
The result of \sref{IV.8.5.2} can be expressed by saying that if $S_0$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, the category of $\sh{O}_S$-Modules quasi-coherent of finite presentation is determined up to equivalence by the data of the categories of $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Modules quasi-coherent of finite presentation, the functors $u_{\lambda\mu}^*$ between these categories, and the transitivity isomorphisms $u_{\mu\lambda}\circ u_{\lambda\mu}\xrightarrow{\sim}u_{\mu\lambda}$.
In an image-like fashion, one can say that the data of an $\sh{O}_S$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation $\sh{F}$ is ``functorially'' equivalent to the data of an $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation $\sh{F}_\lambda$ for $\lambda$ sufficiently large, and that, if an $\sh{O}_{S_\mu}$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation $\sh{F}''_\mu$ also has $\sh{F}$ as its inverse image, then $\sh{F}'_\lambda$ and $\sh{F}''_\mu$ have the same inverse image in an $S_\nu$ for $\nu$ large enough $(\nu\geq\lambda, \nu\geq\mu)$.
\end{scholie}

\begin{corollary}[8.5.4]
\label{IV.8.5.4}
\oldpage[IV-3]{23}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated; then, for every quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Module $\sh{G}_\lambda$, the canonical homomorphism \eqref{IV.8.5.1.4} is bijective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, it suffices to apply \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i) to the case where $\sh{F}_\lambda=\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$, which is of finite presentation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.5.5]
\label{IV.8.5.5}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, and suppose that $\sh{F}_\lambda$ is an $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation.
For $\sh{F}$ to be locally free (resp.\ locally free of rank $n$), it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $\sh{F}_\mu$ is locally free (resp.\ locally free of rank $n$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The condition being trivially sufficient, let us prove it is necessary.
If $\sh{F}$ is locally free (resp.\ locally free of rank $n$), there exists a finite open affine covering $(V_i)$ of $S$ such that $\sh{F}|V_i$ is isomorphic to $\sh{O}_S^n|V_i$ (resp.\ $\sh{O}_S^n|V_i$) for all $i$.
By \sref{IV.8.2.11} there exists $\nu\geq\lambda$ and for each $i$ a quasi-compact open $U_{i\nu}$ of $S_\nu$ such that $V_i=u_\nu^{-1}(U_{i\nu})$.
As $S_\nu$ is quasi-compact, each $U_{i\nu}$ is a finite union of open affines;
one is thus reduced to the case where $S_0$ is affine and $\sh{F}=\sh{O}_S^n$; one knows then \sref{IV.8.2.5} that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $\sh{F}_\mu$ is isomorphic to $\sh{O}_{S_\mu}^n$ \sref{IV.8.5.2}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.5.6]
\label{IV.8.5.6}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, and consider an exact sequence
\[
  \sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}_\lambda\to\sh{H}_\lambda\to 0
\]
of homomorphisms of $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Modules quasi-coherent, where $\sh{F}_\lambda$ and $\sh{G}_\lambda$ are of finite type and $\sh{H}_\lambda$ of finite presentation.
For the corresponding sequence $\sh{F}\to\sh{G}\to\sh{H}\to 0$ to be exact, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that the sequence $\sh{F}_\mu\to\sh{G}_\mu\to\sh{H}_\mu\to 0$ is exact (in which case it is exact for $\nu\geq\mu$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The fact that the condition is sufficient and the last assertion follow from the fact that the functor $u_\lambda^*$ (resp.\ $u_{\lambda\mu}^*$) is right exact.
To show that the condition is necessary, note that by (\sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i)) there exists $\nu\geq\lambda$ such that the composed $\sh{F}_\nu\to\sh{G}_\nu\to\sh{H}_\nu$ is zero.
Changing notations, one can therefore already suppose that $g_\lambda\circ f_\lambda=0$.
If one sets $\sh{H}'_\lambda=\operatorname{Coker}(\sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}_\lambda)$, one has then a homomorphism $f_\lambda:\sh{H}'_\lambda\to\sh{H}_\lambda$;
by hypothesis, $u_\lambda^*(f_\lambda)$ is an isomorphism, and it follows from \sref{IV.8.5.2.4} that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_\lambda)$ is an isomorphism, which finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.5.7]
\label{IV.8.5.7}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, $\sh{F}_\lambda$ quasi-coherent of finite type, $\sh{G}_\lambda$ quasi-coherent.
Let $f_\lambda:\sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}_\lambda$ be a homomorphism.
For $f=u_\lambda^*(f_\lambda):\sh{F}\to\sh{G}$ to be surjective, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $f_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_\lambda):\sh{F}_\mu\to\sh{G}_\mu$ is surjective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is the particular case of \sref{IV.8.5.6} applied to the sequence $0\to\sh{H}'_\lambda\to 0\to 0$, where $\sh{H}_\lambda=\operatorname{Coker}(f_\lambda)$, which is quasi-coherent and of finite type (taking into account that $\operatorname{Im} f_\lambda$ and $\operatorname{Ker} g_\lambda$ are the reciprocal images of $\operatorname{Im} f_\lambda$ and $\operatorname{Ker} g_\lambda$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$), and in view of exactness to the right of the functors $u_\lambda^*$ and $u_{\lambda\mu}^*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.5.8]
\label{IV.8.5.8}
\oldpage[IV-3]{24}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and the morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ flat.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] Let $\sh{F}_\lambda\xrightarrow{f_\lambda}\sh{G}_\lambda\xrightarrow{g_\lambda}\sh{H}_\lambda$ be a sequence of homomorphisms of $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Modules quasi-coherent, such that $\operatorname{Im} f_\lambda$ and $\operatorname{Ker} g_\lambda$ are of finite type.
  For the corresponding sequence $\sh{F}\xrightarrow{f}\sh{G}\xrightarrow{g}\sh{H}$ to be exact, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that the sequence $\sh{F}_\mu\xrightarrow{f_\mu}\sh{G}_\mu\xrightarrow{g_\mu}\sh{H}_\mu$ is exact.
  \item[\rm(ii)] Let $f_\lambda:\sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}_\lambda$ be a homomorphism of $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Modules quasi-coherent such that $\operatorname{Ker} f_\lambda$ is of finite type.
  For $f=u_\lambda^*(f_\lambda):\sh{F}\to\sh{G}$ to be injective, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $f_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(f_\lambda):\sh{F}_\mu\to\sh{G}_\mu$ is injective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
(i) Taking into account \sref{IV.8.3.8}, (ii), note that, by flatness, $\operatorname{Im} f$ and $\operatorname{Ker} g$ (resp.\ $\operatorname{Im} f_\mu$ and $\operatorname{Ker} g_\mu$) are the reciprocal images of $\operatorname{Im} f_\lambda$ and $\operatorname{Ker} g_\lambda$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ ($0_I$, 6.7.2).
Suppose the sequence $\sh{F}\xrightarrow{f}\sh{G}\xrightarrow{g}\sh{H}$ is exact.
Since $\operatorname{Im} f_\lambda$ is of finite type, there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that the composed $\sh{F}_\mu\to\sh{G}_\mu\to\sh{H}_\mu$ is zero, by \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i).
Changing notations, one can therefore suppose that $g_\lambda\circ f_\lambda=0$.
Then, as $\operatorname{Ker} g_\lambda$ is of finite type, it follows from \sref{IV.8.5.7} that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that the homomorphism $\sh{F}_\mu\to\operatorname{Ker} g_\mu$ is surjective, which finishes proving (i).

\medskip\noindent
(ii) This is the particular case of (i) applied to the sequence $0\to\sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}_\lambda$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[8.5.9]
\label{IV.8.5.9}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $\sh{F}_\lambda$ quasi-coherent of finite type;
let $\sh{G}'_\lambda$, $\sh{G}''_\lambda$ be two quasi-coherent quotients of $\sh{F}_\lambda$, $\sh{G}'_\lambda$ being in addition supposed of finite presentation.
For $\sh{G}''$ to be a quotient of $\sh{G}'$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $\sh{G}''_\mu$ is a quotient of $\sh{G}'_\mu$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By hypothesis, there are two surjective homomorphisms $p'_\lambda:\sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}'_\lambda$, $p''_\lambda:\sh{F}_\lambda\to\sh{G}''_\lambda$;
by exactness to the right of $u_\lambda^*$ and $u_{\lambda\mu}^*$, $p'=u_\lambda^*(p'_\lambda)$, $p''=u_\lambda^*(p''_\lambda)$, $p'_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(p'_\lambda)$, $p''_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^*(p''_\lambda)$ are also surjective;
moreover, if there exists a homomorphism $f:\sh{G}'\to\sh{G}''$ (resp.\ $f_\mu:\sh{G}'_\mu\to\sh{G}''_\mu$) such that $p''=f\circ p'$ (resp.\ $p''_\mu=f_\mu\circ p'_\mu$), this homomorphism is necessarily unique, which shows that the question is \emph{local} on $S_\lambda$, and that one can therefore (as $S_0$ is quasi-compact and $\mathbf{L}$ filtered) suppose $S_\lambda$ affine, hence \emph{quasi-separated}.
It is clear that the condition in the statement is sufficient.
Conversely, if there exists a homomorphism $f:\sh{G}'\to\sh{G}''$ such that $p''=f\circ p'$, it follows from \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i) that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ and a homomorphism $f_\mu:\sh{G}'_\mu\to\sh{G}''_\mu$ such that $f=u_\mu^*(f_\mu)$.
Furthermore, by \sref{IV.8.5.7}, one can suppose $\mu$ taken large enough for $p_\mu$ to be surjective, which finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}{8.5.10}
\label{IV.8.5.10}
To finish this section, for every quasi-coherent Module $\sh{F}$ on a prescheme, denote by $\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F})$ the set of Modules which are quotients of $\sh{F}$ and are \emph{of finite presentation}.
If $\sh{F}_\lambda$ is quasi-coherent and $\sh{G}_\lambda\in\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}_\lambda)$, it follows from the fact that $u_{\lambda\mu}^*$ and $u_\lambda^*$ are exact to the right that $\sh{G}_\mu\in\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}_\mu)$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ and $\sh{G}\in\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F})$;
it is clear that $(\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}_\lambda),u_{\lambda\mu}^*)$ is an inductive system of sets, and that the $u_\lambda^*:\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}_\lambda)\to\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F})$ form an inductive system of maps, whence, passing to the inductive limit, a canonical map
\[
  u_\mathfrak{Q}:\varinjlim\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}_\lambda)\to\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}).
  \tag{8.5.10.1}\label{IV.8.5.10.1}
\]
Furthermore, if $(\sh{F}'_\lambda)$ is a second family of quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$-Modules and if, for all $\lambda$, $\sh{F}'_\lambda$ is a quotient of $\sh{F}_\lambda$, then $\sh{F}'$ is a quotient of $\sh{F}$ and one has a commutative diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
  \varinjlim\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}_\lambda) \ar[r,"u_\mathfrak{Q}"] & \mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F})\\
  \varinjlim\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}'_\lambda) \ar[r,"u_\mathfrak{Q}"'] \ar[u] & \mathfrak{Q}(\sh{F}') \ar[u]
\end{tikzcd}
\tag{8.5.10.2}\label{IV.8.5.10.2}
\]
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.5.11]
\label{IV.8.5.11}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact (resp.\ quasi-compact and quasi-separated).
Suppose that $\sh{F}_\lambda$ is quasi-coherent of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation) for all $\lambda$;
then the canonical map \eqref{IV.8.5.10.1} is \emph{injective} (resp.\ \emph{bijective}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows more precisely from the lemma \sref{IV.8.5.9}.
For the second, consider an $\sh{O}_S$-Module quotient $\sh{G}$ of $\sh{F}$ which is of finite presentation.
It follows from \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (ii) that there exist $\lambda'\in\mathbf{L}$ and an $\sh{O}_{S_{\lambda'}}$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation $\sh{G}_{\lambda'}$ such that $\sh{G}=u_{\lambda'}^*(\sh{G}_{\lambda'})$;

Consider then the canonical homomorphism $p:\sh{F}\to\sh{G}$;

Furthermore, by \sref{IV.8.5.7}, one can suppose $\mu$ taken large enough for $p_\mu$ to be surjective, which finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sub-preschemes of finite presentation of a projective limit of preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.8.6}

\begin{env}{8.6.1}
\label{IV.8.6.1}
Given a prescheme $Y$, we will denote in this section by $\mathfrak{Spr}(Y)$ the ordered set \sref[I]{I.4.1.10} of sub-preschemes of $Y$ which are \emph{of finite presentation} on $Y$ (1.6.1), by $\mathfrak{Spro}(Y)$ (resp.\ $\mathfrak{Sprf}(Y)$) the part of $\mathfrak{Spr}(Y)$ formed of sub-preschemes induced on open subsets (resp.\ closed sub-preschemes) of $Y$, of finite presentation on $Y$;
recall that saying a sub-prescheme $Z$ of $Y$ belongs to $\mathfrak{Spro}(Y)$ means it is induced on an open subset and that the underlying space $Z$ is \emph{retrocompact} in $Y$ (resp.\ that it is closed and that the ideal $\sh{J}$ of $\sh{O}_Y$ which defines it is \emph{of finite type}, which also means that $j_*(\sh{O}_Z)\in\mathfrak{Q}(\sh{O}_Y)$ if $j:Z\to Y$ is the canonical injection) (1.6.1 and 1.4.5).
\end{env}

\begin{env}{8.6.2}
\label{IV.8.6.2}
We keep the notations of \sref{IV.8.2.2} and suppose that $S_0$ is one of the $S_\lambda$.
Let $Y_\lambda$ be a sub-prescheme of $S_\lambda$;
then $Y_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Y_\lambda)$ (resp.\ $Y=u_\lambda^{-1}(Y_\lambda)$) is a sub-prescheme of $S_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ (resp.\ of $S$);
it is induced on an open subset (resp.\ it is closed) if $Y_\lambda$ is so, and is of finite presentation on $S_\mu$ (resp.\ $S$) if $Y_\lambda$ is of finite presentation on $S_\lambda$ (1.6.2, (iii)).
Hence $(\mathfrak{Spr}(S_\lambda),u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1})$ (resp.\ $(\mathfrak{Spro}(S_\lambda),u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1})$, $(\mathfrak{Sprf}(S_\lambda),u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1})$) is an inductive system and the maps $u_\lambda^{-1}:\mathfrak{Spr}(S_\lambda)\to\mathfrak{Spr}(S)$ (resp.\ $\mathfrak{Spro}(S_\lambda)\to\mathfrak{Spro}(S)$, $\mathfrak{Sprf}(S_\lambda)\to\mathfrak{Sprf}(S)$) form an inductive system of maps;
whence, passing to the inductive limit, canonical maps
\begin{align}
  &\varinjlim\mathfrak{Spr}(S_\lambda) \to \mathfrak{Spr}(S)\tag{8.6.2.1}\label{IV.8.6.2.1}\\
  &\varinjlim\mathfrak{Spro}(S_\lambda) \to \mathfrak{Spro}(S)\tag{8.6.2.2}\label{IV.8.6.2.2}\\
  &\varinjlim\mathfrak{Sprf}(S_\lambda) \to \mathfrak{Sprf}(S).\tag{8.6.2.3}\label{IV.8.6.2.3}
\end{align}

\oldpage[IV-3]{26}
Recall \sref[I]{I.4.1.10} that $\mathfrak{Spr}(X)$, for any prescheme $X$, is an \emph{ordered} set by the relation ``$Z$ is a sub-prescheme of the sub-prescheme $Y$'' which we write $Z\leq Y$.
The maps $u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}$ and $u_\lambda^{-1}$ are \emph{increasing} for the corresponding order relations in $\mathfrak{Spr}(S_\lambda)$, $\mathfrak{Spr}(S_\mu)$, $\mathfrak{Spr}(S)$.
Furthermore, one defines an order relation on the set $\varinjlim\mathfrak{Spr}(S_\lambda)$ by writing $\eta\leq\eta'$ for two elements of this set whenever there exist $\lambda$ and two elements $Y_\lambda$, $Y'_\lambda$ of $\mathfrak{Spr}(S_\lambda)$, whose $\eta$ and $\eta'$ are the canonical images, and such that $Y_\lambda\leq Y'_\lambda$;
one verifies easily that this does not depend on the choice of representatives, and that one obtains a \emph{relation of order}.
This being the case, the fact that the $u_\lambda^{-1}$ are increasing implies immediately that the canonical map \eqref{IV.8.6.2.1} is \emph{increasing};
it is the same evidently for \eqref{IV.8.6.2.2} and \eqref{IV.8.6.2.3}.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.6.3]
\label{IV.8.6.3}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact (resp.\ quasi-compact and quasi-separated).
Then the maps \eqref{IV.8.6.2.1}, \eqref{IV.8.6.2.2}, \eqref{IV.8.6.2.3} are \emph{injective} (resp.\ \emph{bijective}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Taking into account the remarks of \sref{IV.8.6.1}, the assertions relative to \eqref{IV.8.6.2.3} follow from \sref{IV.8.5.11} applied to $\sh{F}_\lambda=\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$;
similarly, the assertions relative to \eqref{IV.8.6.2.2} are particular cases of \sref{IV.8.3.5} and \sref{IV.8.3.11}, taking into account that the $S_\lambda$ and $S$ are quasi-compact.
It remains to consider the map \eqref{IV.8.6.2.1}.
Let us first prove that it is surjective when $S_0$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
Let $Z$ be a sub-prescheme of $S$, of finite presentation on $S$;
as $S$ is quasi-compact, it is the same of $Z$, hence there exists a quasi-compact open $U$ of $S$ such that $Z$ is a sub-prescheme closed of $U$, of finite presentation on $U$.
There exists then an index $\lambda$ and a quasi-compact open $U_\lambda$ of $S_\lambda$ such that $U=u_\lambda^{-1}(U_\lambda)$ \sref{IV.8.2.11};
as $S_\lambda$ is quasi-separated, each $U_\lambda$ is of the same type of $U_\lambda$ (1.2.7), and by the same argument as in the case of \eqref{IV.8.6.2.3}, it is surjective.

Finally, to see that \eqref{IV.8.6.2.1} is injective when $S_0$ is quasi-compact, it suffices to prove the following more precise result:
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.6.3.1]
\label{IV.8.6.3.1}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, and let $Z'_\lambda$, $Z''_\lambda$ be two sub-preschemes of $S_\lambda$, of finite presentation on $S_\lambda$.
For $Z'=u_\lambda^{-1}(Z'_\lambda)$ to be majorized by $Z''=u_\lambda^{-1}(Z''_\lambda)$ \sref[I]{I.4.1.10}, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $Z'_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Z'_\lambda)$ is majorized by $Z''_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Z''_\lambda)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[8.6.4]
\label{IV.8.6.4}
\oldpage[IV-3]{27}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, and let $Z_\lambda$ be a sub-prescheme of $S_\lambda$, of finite presentation on $S_\lambda$.
For $Z=u_\lambda^{-1}(Z_\lambda)$ to be a sub-prescheme induced on an open subset (resp.\ a closed sub-prescheme) of $S$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $Z_\mu=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(Z_\lambda)$ is induced on an open subset (resp.\ a closed sub-prescheme) of $S_\mu$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $(U_\lambda^{(i)})$ be a finite open affine covering of $S_\lambda$, and set $U_\mu^{(i)}=u_{\lambda\mu}^{-1}(U_\lambda^{(i)})$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ and $U^{(i)}=u_\lambda^{-1}(U_\lambda^{(i)})$.
If $Z$ is open (resp.\ closed) in $S$, $Z\cap U^{(i)}$ is open (resp.\ closed) in $U^{(i)}$, and conversely.
Since $\mathbf{L}$ is filtered, it suffices to show the corollary when $S_\lambda$ is affine, hence quasi-separated;
but then the result follows from the bijectivity of the maps \eqref{IV.8.6.2.1}, \eqref{IV.8.6.2.2}, and \eqref{IV.8.6.2.3}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Criteria for a projective limit of preschemes to be reduced (resp.\ integral)}
\label{subsection:IV.8.7}

\begin{proposition}[8.7.1]
\label{IV.8.7.1}
Suppose that $S$ is not reduced.
Then there exists $\lambda_0$ such that for $\lambda\geq\lambda_0$, $S_\lambda$ is not reduced.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The question being local on $S_0$, one can suppose $S_0=\Spec(A_0)$ affine, whence $S=\Spec(A)$, where $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$ is the inductive limit of an inductive system of $A_0$-algebras $(A_\lambda)$.
One knows then (5.13.2) that the nilradical of $A$ is the inductive limit of the nilradicals of the $A_\lambda$;
so if it is not zero, one of the $A_\lambda$ contains a nilpotent element $a_\lambda\neq 0$ whose image in $A$ is non-zero, and the image of $a_\lambda$ in the $A_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$ is therefore a nilpotent element $\neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.7.2]
\label{IV.8.7.2}
Suppose one of the following hypotheses is satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] $S_0$ is quasi-compact, the nilradical $\sh{N}_0$ of $\sh{O}_{S_0}$ is an ideal of finite type (which will be the case when $S_0$ is noetherian), and the morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are open immersions.
  \item[\rm b)] The morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are faithfully flat.
\end{enumerate}
Under these conditions, for $S$ to be reduced, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda_0$ such that $S_\lambda$ is reduced for $\lambda\geq\lambda_0$.

Furthermore, in case \emph{b)}, if $S$ is reduced, all the $S_\lambda$ are reduced.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{28}
The last assertion follows from the fact that the morphism $S\to S_\lambda$ is then faithfully flat for all $\lambda$ \sref{IV.8.3.8} and from (2.1.13).
On the other hand, \sref{IV.8.7.1} proves that the condition is necessary in hypothesis \emph{a)};
then \sref{IV.8.2.13}, the underlying space of $S$ identifies with the intersection of the underlying spaces of the $S_\lambda$ (the $S_\lambda$ being identified with sub-preschemes induced on open subsets of $S_0$), and the structural sheaf $\sh{O}_S$ identifies with the sheaf induced by all the $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$;
in particular for all $s\in S$ and for all $S_\lambda$, the local ring $\sh{O}_s$ is the same for $S$ and for all $S_\lambda$.
If $\sh{N}_\lambda$ is the Nilradical of $\sh{O}_{S_\lambda}$, the Nilradical $\sh{N}$ of $\sh{O}_S$ has at each point of $S$ the same fibre $\sh{N}_s$ (nilradical of $\sh{O}_s$) (induced on $S$);
the hypothesis that $S$ is reduced implies by $u_\lambda^*(\sh{N}_\lambda)=0$;
as $\sh{N}_0$ is supposed of finite type, it is the same of $\sh{N}_\lambda$, and the conclusion follows from \sref{IV.8.5.7}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.7.3]
\label{IV.8.7.3}
Suppose one of the following hypotheses is satisfied:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] $S_0$ is a noetherian prescheme and the morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are open immersions.
  \item[\rm b)] The morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are faithfully flat.
\end{enumerate}
Then, for $S$ to be integral, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda_0$ such that $S_\lambda$ is integral for $\lambda\geq\lambda_0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Recall that saying a prescheme is integral means it is at once reduced and irreducible;
the corollary thus follows from \sref{IV.8.7.2} and \sref{IV.8.4.3}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[8.7.4]
\label{IV.8.7.4}
If one does not make hypotheses on the $u_{\lambda\mu}$, it can happen that $S$ is integral even though all the $S_\lambda$ are not reduced and not connected, as the example \sref{IV.8.4.4} shows, where one takes $A$ non-reduced.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Preschemes of finite presentation on a projective limit of preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.8.8}

\begin{env}{8.8.1}
\label{IV.8.8.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{28}
Keeping always the notations and hypotheses of \sref{IV.8.2.2}, we will assume given in this section two $S_\alpha$-preschemes $X_\alpha$, $Y_\alpha$, which defines \sref{IV.8.2.5} by setting $X_\lambda=X_\alpha\times_{S_\alpha}S_\lambda$, $Y_\lambda=Y_\alpha\times_{S_\alpha}S_\lambda$, $v_{\lambda\mu}=1_{X_\alpha}\times u_{\lambda\mu}$, $w_{\lambda\mu}=1_{Y_\alpha}\times u_{\lambda\mu}$ (for $\alpha\leq\lambda\leq\mu$), whose projective limits are respectively $X=X_\alpha\times_{S_\alpha}S$, $Y=Y_\alpha\times_{S_\alpha}S$, the canonical morphisms $v_\lambda:X\to X_\lambda$ and $w_\lambda:Y\to Y_\lambda$ being respectively equal to $1_{X_\alpha}\times u_\lambda$ and $1_{Y_\alpha}\times u_\lambda$.
For $\alpha\leq\lambda\leq\mu$, one has a canonical map $e_{\mu\lambda}:\operatorname{Hom}_{S_\lambda}(X_\lambda,Y_\lambda)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{S_\mu}(X_\mu,Y_\mu)$, which to every $S_\lambda$-morphism $f_\lambda:X_\lambda\to Y_\lambda$ associates the morphism $f_\mu=f_\lambda\times 1_{S_\mu}:X_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S_\mu\to Y_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S_\mu$, and it is clear that $(\operatorname{Hom}_{S_\lambda}(X_\lambda,Y_\lambda),e_{\lambda\mu})$ is an \emph{inductive system} of sets.
Similarly, one has a canonical map $e_\lambda:\operatorname{Hom}_{S_\lambda}(X_\lambda,Y_\lambda)\to\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,Y)$ which to $f_\lambda$ associates $f=f_\lambda\times 1_S:X_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S\to Y_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S$ and $(e_\lambda)$ is an inductive system of maps;
whence, passing to the inductive limit, a canonical map, functorial in $S_\alpha$, $X_\alpha$ and $Y_\alpha$:
\[
  e:\varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{S_\lambda}(X_\lambda,Y_\lambda)\to\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,Y).
  \tag{8.8.1.1}\label{IV.8.8.1.1}
\]
\end{env}

\begin{theorem}[8.8.2]
\label{IV.8.8.2}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] Suppose $X_\alpha$ quasi-compact (resp.\ quasi-compact and quasi-separated), and $Y_\alpha$ locally of finite type (resp.\ locally of finite presentation) on $S_\alpha$.
  Then the map \eqref{IV.8.8.1.1} is \emph{injective} (resp.\ \emph{bijective}).
  \item[\rm(ii)] Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
  For every prescheme $X$ of finite presentation on $S$, there exist $\lambda\in\mathbf{L}$, a prescheme $X_\lambda$ of finite presentation on $S_\lambda$, and an $S$-isomorphism $X\xrightarrow{\sim}X_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{29}
(i) Consider first the case where $S_0=\Spec(A_0)$, $X_\alpha=\Spec(B_\alpha)$ and $Y_\alpha=\Spec(C_\alpha)$ are affine;
then the $S_\lambda=\Spec(A_\lambda)$ and $S=\Spec(A)$ are also affine, with $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$, and the assertions of (i) are equivalent to the

Let $A_0$ be a ring, $(A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\mathbf{L}}$ an inductive system of $A_0$-algebras, $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$;

\[
  \varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{A_\lambda\text{-}\mathrm{alg.}}(C_\alpha\otimes_{A_\alpha}A_\lambda,B_\alpha\otimes_{A_\alpha}A_\lambda)
  \to\operatorname{Hom}_{A\text{-}\mathrm{alg.}}(C_\alpha\otimes_{A_\alpha}A,B_\alpha\otimes_{A_\alpha}A)
  \tag{8.8.2.2}\label{IV.8.8.2.2}
\]
is injective (resp.\ bijective).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[8.8.2.3]
Let $E$ be a ring, $G$ an $E$-algebra of finite type (resp.\ of finite presentation), $(F_\lambda)$ an inductive system of $E$-algebras.
Then the canonical homomorphism
\[
  \varinjlim\operatorname{Hom}_{E\text{-}\mathrm{alg.}}(G,F_\lambda)
  \to\operatorname{Hom}_{E\text{-}\mathrm{alg.}}(G,\varinjlim F_\lambda)
\]
which to every inductive system of homomorphisms $\theta_\lambda:G\to F_\lambda$ of $E$-algebras associates its inductive limit, is injective (resp.\ bijective).
\end{lemma}

These lemmas are proved by reduction to the case of polynomial algebras and quotients thereof, using the universal properties of polynomial algebras and the finite presentation hypothesis.

\medskip\noindent
\oldpage[IV-3]{32}
(ii) follows from (i) and the gluing arguments of \sref{IV.8.5.2}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.8.2.4]
\label{IV.8.8.2.4}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $X_\alpha$ of finite presentation on $S_\alpha$, $Y_\alpha$ of finite type on $S_\alpha$ and quasi-separated on $S_\alpha$ (which will be the case for example if $Y_\alpha$ is also
of finite presentation on $S_\alpha$).
Let $f_\alpha:X_\alpha\to Y_\alpha$ be an $S_\alpha$-morphism.
For $f:X\to Y$ to be an isomorphism, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $f_\lambda:X_\lambda\to Y_\lambda$ is an isomorphism.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[8.8.2.5]
\label{IV.8.8.2.5}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $X_\alpha$ and $Y_\alpha$ of finite presentation on $S_\alpha$.
For $X$ and $Y$ to be $S$-isomorphic, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $X_\lambda$ and $Y_\lambda$ are $S_\lambda$-isomorphic.
Moreover, for every $S$-isomorphism $f:X\xrightarrow{\sim}Y$, there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ and an $S_\mu$-isomorphism $f_\mu:X_\mu\xrightarrow{\sim}Y_\mu$ such that $f=f_\mu\times 1_S$.
\end{corollary}

\oldpage[IV-3]{33}

\begin{scholie}[8.8.3]
\label{IV.8.8.3}
The essential content of \sref{IV.8.8.2} can be expressed by saying that if $S_0$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, the category of $S$-preschemes of finite presentation is determined up to equivalence by the data of the categories of $S_\lambda$-preschemes of finite presentation, the functors $\rho_{\mu\lambda}:X_\lambda\mapsto X_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S_\mu$ (for $\lambda\leq\mu$) between these categories, and the transitivity isomorphisms $\rho_{\nu\mu}\circ\rho_{\mu\lambda}\xrightarrow{\sim}\rho_{\nu\lambda}$.
In an image-like fashion, one can say that the data of an $S$-prescheme of finite presentation $X$ is ``functorially'' equivalent to the data of an $S_\lambda$-prescheme of finite presentation $X'_\lambda$ for $\lambda$ sufficiently large;
if an $S_\mu$-prescheme of finite presentation $X''_\mu$ is such that $X$ is $S$-isomorphic to $X''_\mu\times_{S_\mu}S$, there exists $\nu$ such that $\lambda\leq\nu$, $\mu\leq\nu$ and such that $X'_\lambda\times_{S_\lambda}S_\nu$ and $X''_\mu\times_{S_\mu}S_\nu$ are $S_\nu$-isomorphic.
The fact that $\mathbf{L}$ is filtered implies moreover that if one gives a \emph{finite} family $(X^{(i)})_{i\in I}$ of $S$-preschemes of finite presentation, and a \emph{finite} family $(f^{(j)})_{j\in J}$ of $S$-morphisms between these preschemes (thus $f^{(j)}$ being of the form $X^{(\sigma(j))}\to X^{(\tau(j))}$ where $\sigma$ and $\tau$ are two maps of $J$ into $I$), there is then an index $\lambda\in\mathbf{L}$, a family $(X_\lambda^{(i)})_{i\in I}$ of $S_\lambda$-preschemes of finite presentation and a family $(f_\lambda^{(j)})_{j\in I}$ of $S_\lambda$-morphisms such that $X^{(i)}$ identifies with $X_\lambda^{(i)}\times_{S_\lambda}S$ for all $i$ and $j$;
moreover, if one has a relation $f^{(j)}=f^{(k)}$, one can suppose $f_\lambda^{(j)}=f_\lambda^{(k)}$.
\end{scholie}

\subsection{First applications to the elimination of noetherian hypotheses}
\label{subsection:IV.8.9}

\oldpage[IV-3]{34}

\begin{proposition}[8.9.1]
\label{IV.8.9.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $X$ an $A$-prescheme.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] The following conditions are equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[\rm a)] $X$ is of finite presentation on $A$.
    \item[\rm b)] There exists a noetherian ring $A_0$, a prescheme $X_0$ of finite type on $A_0$, a ring homomorphism $A_0\to A$, and an $A$-isomorphism $X_0\otimes_{A_0}A\xrightarrow{\sim}X$.
    \item[\rm c)] There exists a sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$, which is a $\bb{Z}$-algebra of finite type, a prescheme $X_0$ of finite type on $A_0$, and an $A$-isomorphism $X_0\otimes_{A_0}A\xrightarrow{\sim}X$.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(ii)] If $\sh{F}$ is an $\sh{O}_X$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation, one can suppose the sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$ is such that there exists an $\sh{O}_{X_0}$-Module coherent $\sh{F}_0$ such that $\sh{F}$ is isomorphic to $\sh{F}_0\otimes_{A_0}A$;
  $\Supp(\sh{F})$ is constructible and closed in $X$, and there is a closed sub-prescheme $Z$ of $X$, having $\Supp(\sh{F})$ as underlying space, such that the canonical immersion $Z\to X$ is of finite presentation.
  \item[\rm(iii)] If $Y$ is a second $A$-prescheme of finite presentation, and $f:X\to Y$ an $A$-morphism, one can suppose the sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$ such that there exists a prescheme $Y_0$ of finite type on $A_0$ and an $A_0$-morphism $f_0:X_0\to Y_0$ (necessarily of finite type) such that $f$ identifies with $f_0\otimes 1_A$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) As $A$ is the inductive limit of its sub-rings that are of finite type over $\bb{Z}$, \emph{a)} implies \emph{c)} by \sref{IV.8.8.2}, (ii);
\emph{c)} implies \emph{b)} since a $\bb{Z}$-algebra of finite type is a noetherian ring;
finally, if $A_0$ is noetherian, an $A_0$-prescheme of finite type is of finite presentation (1.6.1), hence \emph{b)} implies \emph{a)} by (1.6.2, (iii)).

The assertion (ii) follows immediately from \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (ii), \sref{IV.8.3.11} and (1.8.2), and the assertion (iii) from \sref{IV.8.8.2}, (i).
\end{proof}

\begin{env}{8.9.2}
\label{IV.8.9.2}
Proposition \sref{IV.8.9.1} and the results of \sref{IV.8.5.2} and \sref{IV.8.8.2} allow extending in many cases to morphisms of finite presentation $X\to Y$ results proved by noetherian techniques assuming $Y$ is \emph{locally noetherian}.
We will see many examples in the sequel and will limit ourselves here to giving a few results of this type.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.9.3]
\label{IV.8.9.3}
\oldpage[IV-3]{35}
Let $A$ be a ring, $M$ an $A$-module of finite presentation.
Then every surjective $A$-endomorphism of $M$ is bijective.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, consider $A$ as the inductive limit of its sub-$\bb{Z}$-algebras of finite type.
It follows from (8.5.2.6) that there is one of these sub-algebras $A_0$ and an $A_0$-module $M_0$ of finite type such that $M$ is $A$-isomorphic to $M_0\otimes_{A_0}A$;
moreover, if $f:M\to M$ is a surjective $A$-endomorphism, one can suppose \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i) that there exists an $A_0$-endomorphism $f_0:M_0\to M_0$ such that $f=f_0\otimes 1_A$;
finally \sref{IV.8.5.7} one can suppose that $f_0$ is \emph{surjective}.
But as $A_0$ is noetherian and $M_0$ an $A_0$-module of finite type (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.}, chap.~VIII, \textsection~2, n\textsuperscript{o}~2, lemma~3) $f_0$ is bijective, and it is the same of $f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.9.4]
\label{IV.8.9.4}
(``generic flatness theorem'').
Let $Y$ be an integral prescheme, $u:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type and locally of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation.
Then there exists a non-empty open subset $U$ of $Y$ such that $\sh{F}|u^{-1}(U)$ is flat over $U$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The reasoning from the beginning of (6.9.1) reduces to proving the

\begin{lemma}[8.9.4.1]
Let $A$ be an integral ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, $M$ a $B$-module of finite presentation.
Then there exists $f\neq 0$ in $A$ such that $M_f$ is an $A_f$-module libre.
\end{lemma}

Indeed, by \sref{IV.8.9.1} there is a sub-$\bb{Z}$-algebra of finite type $A_0$ of $A$, a $A_0$-algebra of finite type $B_0$ and a $B_0$-module of finite type $M_0$ such that $B$ is $A$-isomorphic to $B_0\otimes_{A_0}A$ and $M$ is $B$-isomorphic to $M_0\otimes_{A_0}A$;
by (6.9.2), there exists $f_0\neq 0$ in $A_0$ such that $(M_0)_{f_0}$ is an $(A_0)_{f_0}$-module libre.
As $M_{f_0}=(M_0)_{f_0}\otimes_{(A_0)_{f_0}}A_{f_0}$ and $A_{f_0}=(A_0)_{f_0}\otimes_{A_0}A$, $M_{f_0}$ is an $A_{f_0}$-module libre.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.9.5]
\label{IV.8.9.5}
Let $S$ be a prescheme quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $u:X\to S$ a morphism of finite presentation, $\sh{F}$ an $\sh{O}_X$-Module quasi-coherent of finite presentation.
Then there exists a partition $(S_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$ of $S$ into a finite number of locally closed sets in $S$, having for each $1\leq i\leq n$ a sub-prescheme $S'_i$ of $S$ of finite presentation, having $S_i$ as underlying space, such that if one sets $X_i=X\times_S S'_i$, the $\sh{O}_{X_i}$-Module $\sh{F}_i=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_S}\sh{O}_{S'_i}$ is flat over $S'_i$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Permanence of properties of morphisms by passage to the projective limit}
\label{subsection:IV.8.10}

\oldpage[IV-3]{36}

\begin{env}{8.10.0}
\label{IV.8.10.0}
In this section we keep the general hypotheses and notations of \sref{IV.8.8.1}.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.10.1]
\label{IV.8.10.1}
If there exists $\lambda$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $f_\mu$ is an open (resp.\ universally open) morphism, then $f$ is an open (resp.\ universally open) morphism.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As $X=X_\lambda\times_{Y_\lambda}Y$, the assertion relative to universally open morphisms is a particular case of (2.4.3, (iii)).
Suppose $f_\mu$ open for $\mu\geq\lambda$;
it suffices to see that for every open \emph{quasi-compact} $U$ of $X$, $f(U)$ is open in $Y$.
Now, it exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $U=v_\mu^{-1}(U_\mu)$, where $U_\mu$ is a quasi-compact open in $X_\mu$ \sref{IV.8.2.11};
one has then $f(v_\mu^{-1}(U_\mu))=w_\mu^{-1}(f_\mu(U_\mu))$ \sref[I]{I.3.4.8}, hence $f(U)$ is open in $Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.10.2]
\label{IV.8.10.2}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism.
For $f$ to be universally open, it suffices that, for every integer $n>0$, if one sets $Y_n=Y\otimes_{\bb{Z}}\bb{Z}[T_1,\ldots,T_n](=\bb{V}_Y^n)$, and $X_n=X\times_Y Y_n$, the projection $f_n=f\times 1_{Y_n}:X_n\to Y_n$ is an open morphism.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[8.10.3]
\label{IV.8.10.3}
Suppose there exists $\alpha$ such that:
1\textsuperscript{o} $S_\alpha$ is quasi-compact;
2\textsuperscript{o} the morphisms $X_\alpha\to S_\alpha$, $Y_\alpha\to S_\alpha$ are quasi-compact and the morphism $Y_\alpha\to S_\alpha$ is quasi-separated;
3\textsuperscript{o} for $\alpha\leq\lambda\leq\mu$, the morphisms $u_{\lambda\mu}:S_\mu\to S_\lambda$ are flat;
4\textsuperscript{o} $f_\alpha(X_\alpha)$ is constructible in $Y_\alpha$.
Then, for $f$ to be dominant, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $f_\lambda$ is dominant.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[8.10.4]
\label{IV.8.10.4}
\oldpage[IV-3]{37}
Suppose there exists $\alpha$ such that $Y_\alpha$ is quasi-compact and of finite type on $S_\alpha$.
For the morphism $f$ to be separated, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $f_\lambda$ is separated.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[8.10.5]
\label{IV.8.10.5}
Suppose $S_0$ quasi-compact, $X_\alpha$ and $Y_\alpha$ of finite presentation on $S_\alpha$, and let $f_\alpha:X_\alpha\to Y_\alpha$ be an $S_\alpha$-morphism.
Consider, for a morphism, the property of being:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] an isomorphism;
  \item[\rm(i bis)] a monomorphism;
  \item[\rm(ii)] an immersion;
  \item[\rm(iii)] an open immersion;
  \item[\rm(iv)] a closed immersion;
  \item[\rm(v)] separated;
  \item[\rm(vi)] surjective;
  \item[\rm(vii)] radiciel;
  \item[\rm(viii)] affine;
  \item[\rm(ix)] quasi-affine;
  \item[\rm(x)] finite;
  \item[\rm(xi)] quasi-finite;
  \item[\rm(xii)] proper.
\end{enumerate}
Then, if $\mathbf{P}$ denotes one of the preceding properties, for $f$ to possess $\mathbf{P}$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exists $\lambda\geq\alpha$ such that $f_\lambda$ possesses property $\mathbf{P}$ (in which case $f_\mu$ also possesses it for $\mu\geq\lambda$).

\oldpage[IV-3]{38}
If $S_0$ is moreover supposed quasi-separated, the same conclusion is valid when $\mathbf{P}$ is the property of being:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(xiii)] projective;
  \item[\rm(xiv)] quasi-projective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof \emph{(sketch)}]
The case where $\mathbf{P}$ is one of properties (i) or (v) was not inserted in the statement since, for those cases, the theorem follows from what was already shown in \sref{IV.8.8.2.4} and \sref{IV.8.10.4} respectively.
Moreover, taking into account \sref[I]{I.5.4.1} and (5.3.4), (v) follows also from (iv).
The case (i~bis) follows immediately from (i), using \sref[I]{I.5.3.8} and noting that the diagonal $\Delta_{f_\lambda}$ of $X_\lambda\times_{Y_\lambda}X_\lambda$ is deduced from $\Delta_f$ by base change $S\to S_\lambda$.

We note on the other hand that (vi), (vii) and (xi) are in fact conditions on the \emph{fibres} $f^{-1}(y)$ of the morphisms considered, taking into account the transitivity of fibres by base change \sref[I]{I.3.6.4}:
condition (vi) means that all fibres are non-empty;
condition (vii) that they must be radiciel \sref[I]{I.3.5.8};
and condition (xi) that they must be finite \sref[II]{II.6.2.2} and \sref[I]{I.6.4.4}, taking into account that $f$ and the $f_\lambda$ are morphisms of finite type by (1.5.4, (v)).
The theorem in these three cases will thus be a consequence of a general result on a type of properties concerning only the fibres, which will be established in (9.3.3);
we postpone the proof of the theorem in case (xi) until then, and note that (8.11) and (8.12) will not use the theorem up to (9.3.3) and that (8.11) and (8.12) are not used before (9.3.3).

For the remaining cases, one can reduce to proving that the condition in the statement is necessary, all considered properties $\mathbf{P}$ being invariant by base change (see chap.\ I\textsuperscript{er} and II in the sections concerning each of these properties).
One can moreover suppose $S_\alpha=S_0$ and $Y_\alpha=S_\alpha$, hence $Y_\lambda=S_\lambda$ for all $\lambda\geq\alpha$.
Finally, properties (i) through (xii) are local on $S_0$, hence one can further suppose $S_0=\Spec(A_0)$ affine (and quasi-separated).
We designate by $A_\lambda$ the ring of $S_\lambda$ (resp.\ $S$).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Cases} (ii), (iii), (iv): these follow from \sref{IV.8.6.3} applied to the sub-prescheme $X'$ associated to $f$.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Cases} (viii) \emph{and} (ix): follow from \sref{IV.8.10.5}, applied to the morphism $j:X\to\bb{V}_S^r=\Spec(S[T_1,\ldots,T_r])$ of $S$-preschemes of finite presentation.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Case} (x): By hypothesis, $X=\Spec(B)$, where $B$ is an $A$-algebra which is an $A$-module of finite presentation (1.4.7);
it follows from \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (ii) that there is a $\lambda$ and an $A_\lambda$-module of finite presentation $B_\lambda$ such that the $A$-module $B$ is isomorphic to $B_\lambda\otimes_{A_\lambda}A$.
The $A$-algebra structure on $B$ is defined by an $A$-homomorphism $m:B\otimes_A B\to B$;
by \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i) there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ an $A_\mu$-homomorphism $m_\mu:B_\mu\otimes_{A_\mu}B_\mu\to B_\mu$ such that $m=m_\mu\otimes 1$.
Considering the usual diagrams expressing the associativity and commutativity of $m$, one sees, applying \sref{IV.8.5.2}, (i) again, that one can define on $B_\nu$ a multiplication making it into an $A_\nu$-algebra for $\nu$ taken large enough, so that $X'_\nu=\Spec(B_\nu)$ is $S_\nu$-isomorphic to $X_\nu\times_{S_\nu}S$, by (i), hence there exist $\rho\geq\nu$ such that $X_\rho$ and $X'_\rho$ are $S_\rho$-isomorphic, which finishes the proof in this case.

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Case} (xii): Apply to the morphism $f_0:X_0\to S_0$ the proposition \sref{IV.8.10.5.1} (Chow's lemma for morphisms of finite presentation).

\medskip\noindent
\emph{Cases} (xiii) \emph{and} (xiv): By (xii) and \sref[II]{II.5.5.3} (which is applicable since the $S_\lambda$ are quasi-compact and quasi-separated, taking into account (1.7.19)), it suffices to consider the case where $f$ is quasi-projective.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.10.5.1]
\label{IV.8.10.5.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{39}
(Chow's lemma for morphisms of finite presentation).
Let $A$ be a ring, $X$, $Y$ two $A$-preschemes of finite presentation, $f:X\to Y$ a separated $A$-morphism.
Then there exist two $A$-preschemes $X'$, $P$ of finite presentation and $A$-morphisms $p:P\to Y$, $j:X'\to P$, $g:X'\to X$, such that the diagram
\[
\begin{tikzcd}
  X' \ar[r,"j"] & P\\
  X \ar[u,"g"'] \ar[r,"f"'] & Y \ar[u,"p"']
\end{tikzcd}
\]
is commutative, and such that:
1\textsuperscript{o} $p$ is projective;
2\textsuperscript{o} $g$ is projective and surjective;
3\textsuperscript{o} $j$ is an open immersion.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Application to quasi-finite morphisms}
\label{subsection:IV.8.11}

\oldpage[IV-3]{41}

We propose to show in this section the two following theorems:

\begin{theorem}[8.11.1]
\label{IV.8.11.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism which is proper, locally of finite presentation, and quasi-finite.
Then the morphism $f$ is finite.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[8.11.2]
\label{IV.8.11.2}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, quasi-finite and separated.
Then the morphism $f$ is quasi-affine, and \emph{a fortiori} quasi-projective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[8.11.3]
\label{IV.8.11.3}
We will see below that the proofs of \sref{IV.8.11.1} and \sref{IV.8.11.2}, in the case where $Y$ is locally noetherian, yield another proof of Chevalley's theorem \sref[III]{III.4.4.2}.
\end{remark}

\begin{env}{8.11.4}
\label{IV.8.11.4}
The hypotheses and conclusions of \sref{IV.8.11.1} and \sref{IV.8.11.2} are all local on $Y$ (1.6.1, 1.2.6, \sref[II]{II.5.1.1}, \sref[II]{II.5.4.1} and \sref[II]{II.6.2.2}), hence one can suppose $Y=\Spec(A)$ affine.
One knows that there exists then a sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$, which is a $\bb{Z}$-algebra of finite type, and a morphism of finite type $f_0:X_0\to\Spec(A_0)$ such that $X$ identifies with $X_0\otimes_{A_0}A$ and $f$ with $f_0\times 1$ \sref{IV.8.9.1}.
Furthermore, $A$ can be considered as an inductive limit of its sub-rings containing $A_0$ and which are $\bb{Z}$-algebras of finite type;
using (8.1.2, \emph{c})) and \sref{IV.8.10.5}, (v), (xi) and (xii), one sees that it suffices to prove theorems \sref{IV.8.11.1} and \sref{IV.8.11.2} for $f_0$;
using now the method of (8.1.2, \emph{a})) and \sref{IV.8.10.5}, (v), (ix), (x), (xi) and (xii), one can replace $Y$ by $\Spec(\sh{O}_y)$, where $y$ is any point of $Y$.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.11.5]
\label{IV.8.11.5}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of finite presentation.
The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] $f$ is a closed immersion.
  \item[\rm b)] $f$ is a proper monomorphism.
  \item[\rm c)] $f$ is proper and for all $y\in Y$, $f^{-1}(y)$ is radiciel and geometrically reduced over $k(y)$ (that is to say either empty or $k(y)$-isomorphic to $\Spec(k(y))$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is clear that \emph{a)} implies \emph{b)}.
To see that \emph{b)} implies \emph{c)}, note \sref[I]{I.3.3.12} that for all $y\in Y$, the morphism $f^{-1}(y)\to\Spec(k(y))$ deduced from $f$ by base extension is a monomorphism, hence is injective, and in any case affine.
Moreover, if $A$ is the ring of $f^{-1}(y)$, the homomorphism canonical $A\otimes_k A\to A$ is bijective \sref[I]{I.5.3.8}.
This evidently implies that $A=k$, unless $A=0$.

It remains to prove that \emph{c)} implies \emph{a)}.
It follows first from \sref{IV.8.11.1} that $f$ is finite, then a fortiori $X=\Spec(\sh{A})$, where $\sh{A}$ is an $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra finite.
It suffices then to prove that the canonical homomorphism $\sh{O}_Y\to\sh{A}_y$ is surjective \sref[II]{II.1.4.10}, or equivalently that for all $y\in Y$, the homomorphism $\sh{O}_y\to\sh{A}_y$ is surjective.
But by hypothesis $f^{-1}(y)=\Spec(\sh{A}_y/\mathfrak{m}_y\sh{A}_y)$ \sref[II]{II.1.5.5} is such that the corresponding homomorphism $k(y)=\sh{O}_y/\mathfrak{m}_y\to\sh{A}_y/\mathfrak{m}_y\sh{A}_y$ is bijective;
as $\sh{A}_y$ is an $\sh{O}_y$-module of finite type, the lemma of Nakayama shows that $\sh{O}_y\to\sh{A}_y$ is surjective, which finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[8.11.6]
\label{IV.8.11.6}
If a morphism $f:X\to Y$ of finite presentation is a universal homeomorphism, it is finite, surjective, and radiciel (the converse being true by (2.4.5, (i))).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, $f$ being of finite type, universally closed, and separated by (2.4.4), is proper by definition \sref[II]{II.5.4.1}.
As it is evidently quasi-finite \sref[II]{II.6.2.3}, it is finite by \sref{IV.8.11.1}.
One knows moreover that it is surjective (2.4.2), and evidently radiciel.
\end{proof}

\subsection{New proof and generalization of Zariski's ``Main Theorem''}
\label{subsection:IV.8.12}

\oldpage[IV-3]{43}

\begin{lemma}[8.12.1]
\label{IV.8.12.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $\sh{C}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra, $Z=\Spec(\sh{C})$, which is a $Y$-prescheme affine above $Y$.
Let $g:X\to Z$ be a $Y$-morphism, $\varphi=\sh{A}(g):\sh{C}\to f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ the corresponding $\sh{O}_Y$-homomorphism of $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebras \sref[II]{II.1.2.7}.
Suppose that $g$ is an immersion.
Then, for the closed image of $X$ by $g$ to be equal to $Z$, it is necessary and sufficient that $\varphi$ be injective;
$g$ is then an open immersion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[8.12.2]
\label{IV.8.12.2}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism quasi-separated and of finite type, $\sh{C}$ a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra, $Z=\Spec(\sh{C})$, $\varphi:\sh{C}\to f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ the corresponding $\sh{O}_Y$-homomorphism.
Let $(\sh{C}_\lambda)$ be the filtering increasing family of quasi-coherent sub-$\sh{O}_Y$-Algebras of finite type of $\sh{C}$ (whose $\sh{C}$ is the union \sref[I]{I.9.6.6} and (1.7.9)));
set $Z_\lambda=\Spec(\sh{C}_\lambda)$ and let $g_\lambda$ be the composed morphism $X\xrightarrow{g}Z\to Z_\lambda$.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] The morphism $g:X\to Z$ is an immersion.
  \item[\rm b)] There exists $\lambda$ such that $g_\lambda:X\to Z_\lambda$ is an immersion.
  \item[\rm c)] There exists $\lambda$ such that $g_\lambda:X\to Z_\lambda$ is an immersion.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, when $g_\lambda$ is an immersion, it is the same of $g_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$, and there exists $\lambda$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $g_\mu$ is an open immersion.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[8.12.3]
\label{IV.8.12.3}
\oldpage[IV-3]{44}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism separated and of finite type.
Let $\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$, which is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra \sref[I]{I.9.2.2};
let $\sh{C}$ be the integral closure of $\sh{O}_Y$ in $\sh{B}$, which is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra \sref[II]{II.6.3.4}.
Set $Z=\Spec(\sh{C})$, and let $g:X\to Z$ be the $Y$-morphism corresponding to the canonical injection $\sh{C}_\lambda\to\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$ \sref[II]{II.1.2.7}.
Let $(\sh{C}_\lambda)$ be the filtering increasing family of sub-$\sh{O}_Y$-Algebras quasi-coherent of finite type of $\sh{C}$ (whose $\sh{C}$ is the union \sref[I]{I.9.6.6} and (1.7.9)), and, for all $\lambda$, $g_\lambda:X\to Z_\lambda$ the $Y$-morphism corresponding to the injection $\sh{C}_\lambda\to\sh{B}=f_*(\sh{O}_X)$.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm a)] There exists a factorization of $f$ as
  \[
    X\xrightarrow{f'}Y'\xrightarrow{u}Y
  \]
  where $f'$ is an open immersion and $u$ a finite morphism.
  \item[\rm a')] There exists a factorization $X\xrightarrow{f'}Y'\xrightarrow{u}Y$ of $f$, where $f'$ is an open immersion and $u$ a finite morphism.
  \item[\rm b)] The morphism $g:X\to Z$ is an immersion.
  \item[\rm c)] There exists $\lambda$ such that $g_\lambda:X\to Z_\lambda$ is an immersion.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, when $g_\lambda$ is an immersion, so is $g_\mu$ for $\mu\geq\lambda$, and there exists $\lambda$ such that, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $g_\mu$ is an open immersion.
\end{proposition}

We will say that a morphism $f:X\to Y$, where $Y$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, is \emph{pseudo-finite}, if it is of finite type and satisfies condition \emph{a)} of \sref{IV.8.12.3} (in which case it is necessarily separated).

\begin{corollary}[8.12.4]
\label{IV.8.12.4}
\oldpage[IV-3]{45}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\rm(i)] Suppose $f$ pseudo-finite.
  Then, for every morphism $Y'\to Y$, where $Y'$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $f_{(Y')}:X'=X_{(Y')}\to Y'$ is pseudo-finite.
  \item[\rm(ii)] Let $(U_\lambda)$ be a covering of $Y$ by open quasi-compact subsets.
  For $f$ to be pseudo-finite, it is necessary and sufficient that for all $\lambda$, the restriction $f_\lambda:f^{-1}(U_\lambda)\to U_\lambda$ of $f$ is a pseudo-finite morphism.
  \item[\rm(iii)] Suppose moreover $Y$ noetherian, and $f$ of finite type.
  Then, for $f$ to be pseudo-finite, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every $y\in Y$, the morphism $f_y=f\times 1:X_y=X\times_Y\Spec(\sh{O}_y)\to\Spec(\sh{O}_y)$ be pseudo-finite.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{env}{8.12.5}
\label{IV.8.12.5}
We can now give a proof of Zariski's ``Main theorem'' \sref[III]{III.4.4.3} not using the ``global'' cohomological results of chap.~III, but appealing instead to finer properties of local noetherian rings;
we will moreover generalize the statement of the theorem by eliminating the noetherian hypotheses:
\end{env}

\begin{theorem}[8.12.6]
\label{IV.8.12.6}
(``Main theorem'' of Zariski).
Let $Y$ be a prescheme quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
If a morphism $f:X\to Y$ is quasi-finite, separated and of finite presentation, there exists a factorization of $f$
\[
  X\xrightarrow{f'}Y'\xrightarrow{u}Y
  \tag{8.12.6.1}\label{IV.8.12.6.1}
\]
where $f'$ is an open immersion and $u$ a finite morphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.12.6.proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{46}
By virtue of \sref{IV.8.12.4}~(ii) and the local character (on $Y$) of the notions of quasi-finite, separated, and finitely presented morphism, we can reduce to the case where $Y=\Spec(A)$ is affine.
Applying \sref{IV.8.9.1}, we can suppose that there is a subring $A_0$ of $A$, which is a $\bb{Z}$-algebra of finite type, and an $A$-isomorphism $X_0\otimes_{A_0}A\xrightarrow{\sim}X$, $f$ being identified via this isomorphism with $f_0\times 1$, where $f_0:X_0\to\Spec(A_0)$ is a morphism of finite type;
moreover (\sref{IV.8.10.5}, (v) and (xi)) we can suppose that $f_0$ is separated and quasi-finite;
if we prove that $f_0$ is pseudo-finite, the same will hold for $f$ by \sref{IV.8.12.4}~(i).
Since $A_0$ is then noetherian and the notions of morphism of finite type, separated, and quasi-finite are preserved by base change, it follows from \sref{IV.8.12.4}~(iii) that we can even suppose that $A$ is a local ring, \emph{essentially of finite type over $\bb{Z}$} \sref[I]{I.1.3.8}.
Set $n=\dim(A)$, and proceed by induction on $n$;
for $n=0$, the theorem is evident, $A$ being a field and the morphism $f$ being already finite \sref[II]{II.6.2.2}.
Set $B=\Gamma(X,\sh{O}_X)$; denote by $C$ the integral closure of $A$ in $B$, set $Z=\Spec(C)$ and let $g:X\to Z$ be the $Y$-morphism corresponding to the injective $A$-homomorphism $C\to B$;
by virtue of \sref{IV.8.12.3}, it suffices to show that $g$ is an open immersion.
Let $a$ be the closed point of $Y$, and let $U=Y-\{a\}$;
$U$ is noetherian and all its local rings are essentially of finite type over $\bb{Z}$ and of dimension $<n$;
taking into account the induction hypothesis and \sref{IV.8.12.4}~(iii), we see that the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is a pseudo-finite morphism.
We conclude from \sref{IV.8.12.3} that, if $h:Z\to Y$ is the structural morphism, the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to h^{-1}(U)$ of $g$ is an open immersion.
Set $A'=\widehat{A}$, $Y'=\Spec(A')$, $X'=X_{(Y')}$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$.
Since the canonical morphism $u:Y'\to Y$ is flat, it follows from \sref{IV.2.3.1} that $B'=\Gamma(X',\sh{O}_{X'})$ is identified with the $A'$-algebra $B\otimes_A A'$.
On the other hand, since $A$ is an excellent local ring \sref{IV.7.8.3}, the morphism $u:Y'\to Y$ is regular, and \emph{a fortiori} normal, and consequently \sref{IV.6.14.4} the integral closure of $A'$ in $B'$ is equal to $C'=C\otimes_A A'$.
We thus see that $Z'=\Spec(C')$ is equal to $Z_{(Y')}$ and the morphism $g':X'\to Z'$ coming from the injection $C'\to B'$ is equal to $g_{(Y')}$.
Since $u:Y'\to Y$ is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, to prove that $g$ is an open immersion, it suffices to prove that $g'$ is an open immersion \sref{IV.2.7.1}~(x).
Note now that $u^{-1}(a)$ is reduced to the closed point $a'$ of $Y'$ and consequently $U'=Y'-\{a'\}=u^{-1}(U)$.
If $h':Z'\to Y'$ is the canonical morphism, the fact that the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to h^{-1}(U)$ of $g$ is an open immersion implies that the same holds for the restriction $f'^{-1}(U')\to h'^{-1}(U')$ of $g'$.
\oldpage[IV-3]{47}
Note that $f'$ is a separated and quasi-finite morphism \sref[II]{II.6.2.4};
since $A'$ is complete, we deduce from \sref[II]{II.6.2.6} that $X'$ is $Y'$-isomorphic to a sum $X'_1\sqcup X'_2$, where the restriction $f'|X'_1=f'_1:X'_1\to Y'$ is a \emph{finite} morphism, and $X'_2\subset f'^{-1}(U')$.
It follows that $B'$ is the direct product of the two $A'$-algebras $\Gamma(X'_1,\sh{O}_{X'_1})=B'_1$ and $\Gamma(X'_2,\sh{O}_{X'_2})=B'_2$;
we immediately conclude that the integral closure $C'$ of $A'$ in $B'$ is composed of the integral closures $C'_1$, $C'_2$ of $A'$ in $B'_1$, $B'_2$ respectively, whence $Z'=Z'_1\sqcup Z'_2$, where $Z'_i=\Spec(C'_i)$ $(i=1,2)$;
and the canonical morphism $g':X'\to Z'$ is such that $g'|X'_i$ is the canonical morphism $g'_i:X'_i\to Z'_i$ $(i=1,2)$.
But since $B'_1$ is already a finite $A'$-algebra, we have $C'_1=B'_1$, and $g'_1$ is therefore an isomorphism.
On the other hand, since $X'_2\subset f'^{-1}(U')$ and is open in $f'^{-1}(U')$, we already know that $g'_2$ is an open immersion.
We conclude that $g'$ is an open immersion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[8.12.7]
\label{IV.8.12.7}
When, in \sref{IV.8.12.6}, we suppose that $Y$ is an affine \emph{scheme}, the proof by reduction to the noetherian case shows that, in the factorization \sref{IV.8.12.6.1}, the morphisms $f'$ and $u$ are also morphisms \emph{of finite presentation} \sref[I]{I.1.6.2}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[8.12.8]
\label{IV.8.12.8}
Let $Y$ be a quasi-compact scheme such that there exists an ample $\sh{O}_Y$-Module \sref[II]{II.4.5.3}, $f:X\to Y$ a quasi-finite and quasi-projective morphism.
Then there exists a factorization of $f$ as
\[
  X\xrightarrow{f'}Y'\xrightarrow{u}Y
\]
where $f'$ is an open immersion and $u$ a finite morphism.\footnote{The reader will verify that Corollaries \sref{IV.8.12.8} to \sref{IV.8.12.11} are not used in \textsection~9.}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.12.8.proof}
The hypothesis implies that $X$ is identified with a quasi-compact sub-$Y$-scheme of a $Y$-scheme of the form $Z=\bb{P}^r_Y$ \sref[II]{II.5.3.3}.
There is thus a quasi-compact open neighborhood $U$ of $X$ in $Z$ such that $X$ is closed in $U$;
since $Z$ is a scheme, the canonical injection $U\to Z$ is a morphism of finite presentation ($(\text{\sref[I]{I.1.2.7}})$ and $(\text{\sref[I]{I.1.6.2}})$), so the composite morphism $g:U\to Z\to Y$ is also a morphism of finite presentation (the fact that $\bb{P}^r_Y$ is of finite presentation over $Y$ resulting immediately from the definition \sref[II]{II.4.1.1}).
Let $\sh{J}$ be the quasi-coherent Ideal of $\sh{O}_U$ defining the closed sub-prescheme $X$;
since $U$ is a quasi-compact scheme, $\sh{J}$ is the inductive limit of its quasi-coherent sub-Ideals of finite type $\sh{J}_\lambda$ \sref[I]{I.9.4.9}.
If $X_\lambda$ is the closed sub-prescheme of $U$ defined by $\sh{J}_\lambda$, we thus have $X=\bigcap_\lambda X_\lambda$.
For all $y\in Y$, we thus have $f^{-1}(y)=\bigcap(X_\lambda\cap g^{-1}(y))$, and since the sets $X_\lambda\cap g^{-1}(y)$ are closed in the noetherian space $g^{-1}(y)$, for every $y$ there exists an index $\lambda(y)$ such that $f^{-1}(y)=X_{\lambda(y)}\cap g^{-1}(y)$.
Denote by $E_\lambda$ the set of $y\in Y$ such that the fibre $X_\lambda\cap g^{-1}(y)$ of the restriction of $g$ to $X_\lambda$ is a finite $k(y)$-prescheme.
The hypothesis that $f$ is quasi-finite implies, by virtue of the above, that $Y=\bigcup E_\lambda$.
Now, each of the $X_\lambda$ is, by definition, \emph{of finite presentation} over $Y$;
it thus follows from \sref{IV.9.2.3} and \sref{IV.9.2.6} that the $E_\lambda$ are \emph{constructible} subsets in the scheme $Y$;
since they form a filtered increasing collection, there exists an index $\lambda$ such that $E_\lambda=Y$ \sref[I]{I.1.9.9}, and for this index $\lambda$, the morphism $f_\lambda:X_\lambda\to Y$, restriction of $g$ to $X_\lambda$, is thus \emph{quasi-finite}.
Since it is of finite presentation and separated, we can apply \sref{IV.8.12.6} to it, and $f_\lambda$ thus factorizes as
\[
  X_\lambda\xrightarrow{j_\lambda}Y'_\lambda\xrightarrow{u_\lambda}Y
\]
where $j_\lambda$ is an immersion and $u_\lambda$ a finite morphism.
Since $X$ is a closed sub-prescheme of $X_\lambda$, we have thus proved that $f$ possesses the property \sref{IV.8.12.3}~$a)$, whence the corollary by virtue of the equivalence of \sref{IV.8.12.3}~$a)$ and \sref{IV.8.12.3}~$a')$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.12.9]
\label{IV.8.12.9}
\oldpage[IV-3]{48}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a locally quasi-finite morphism $(\text{\emph{Err}}_{\text{III}}, 20)$.
For every $x\in X$ there exists an open neighborhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$, an open neighborhood $V$ of $y=f(x)$ in $Y$, such that $f(U)\subset V$ and a factorization
\[
  U\xrightarrow{f'}V'\xrightarrow{u}V
\]
of the restriction of $f$ to $U$, where $f'$ is an open immersion and $u$ a finite morphism.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.12.9.proof}
It suffices to take for $V$ an affine neighborhood of $y$ in $Y$, for $U$ an affine neighborhood of $x$ in $X$ contained in $f^{-1}(V)$ and such that $f|U$ is quasi-finite.
The morphism $U\to V$ restriction of $f$ being then affine (hence quasi-projective), we can apply \sref{IV.8.12.8} to it.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.12.10]
\label{IV.8.12.10}
Let $Y$ be an integral and normal prescheme, $X$ an integral prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a birational and locally quasi-finite morphism $(\text{\emph{Err}}_{\text{III}}, 20)$.
Then $f$ is a local isomorphism;
for $f$ to be an open immersion, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be moreover separated.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.12.10.proof}
The second assertion follows immediately from the first and from \sref[I]{I.8.2.8}.
To prove the first assertion, we can suppose $X$ and $Y$ affine and $f$ quasi-finite;
consider the factorization $f=u\circ f'$ of \sref{IV.8.12.8}, which allows us to identify $X$ by $f'$ with a sub-prescheme induced on an open subset of $Y'$.
Since $X$ is integral, we can, by virtue of \sref[I]{I.5.2.3}, replace $Y'$ by the reduced sub-prescheme of $Y'$ having for underlying space $\overline{X}$, so we can suppose that $Y'$ is also \emph{integral}.
Moreover, since $f$ is birational, $u$ is as well.
The conclusion then follows from the following lemma:
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[8.12.10.1]
\label{IV.8.12.10.1}
Let $Y'$ be an integral prescheme, $Y$ an integral and normal prescheme;
then a morphism $u:Y'\to Y$ that is finite and birational is an isomorphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.12.10.1.proof}
Set $\sh{A}=u_*(\sh{O}_{Y'})$, so that $\sh{A}$ is a finite $\sh{O}_Y$-Algebra, $Y'$ being identified with $\Spec(\sh{A})$ \sref[II]{II.1.3.6}.
If $\mathrm{R}(Y)$ is the field of rational functions of $Y$, we thus have, for all $y\in Y$, $\sh{O}_{Y,y}\subset\sh{A}_y\subset\mathrm{R}(Y)$;
but since the ring $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is by hypothesis integrally closed and has $\mathrm{R}(Y)$ as its field of fractions, we necessarily have $\sh{A}_y=\sh{O}_{Y,y}$, whence the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.12.11]
\label{IV.8.12.11}
Let $Y$ be an integral and normal prescheme, $X$ an integral prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a dominant and locally quasi-finite morphism.
Let $K$ and $L$ be the fields of rational functions of $Y$ and $X$ respectively (extension of $K$);
and let $Y'$ be the integral closure of $Y$ relative to $L$ \sref[II]{II.6.3.4};
then $f$ factorizes in a unique way as $f:X\xrightarrow{f'}Y'\xrightarrow{u}Y$, where $f'$ is birational, and corresponds to the identity automorphism of $L$;
$f'$ is then a local isomorphism, and for $f'$ to be an open immersion, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be separated.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.12.11.proof}
The existence and uniqueness of the factorization of $f$ follow from \sref[II]{II.6.3.9}.
It follows from (\sref[II]{II.6.2.4}, (v)), reducing to the affine case, that $f'$ is locally quasi-finite;
moreover, it follows from \sref[I]{I.5.5.1} that, for $f'$ to be separated, it is necessary and sufficient that $f$ be so, since $u$ is affine, hence separated;
the two last assertions are thus consequences of \sref{IV.8.12.10} applied to $f'$.
\end{proof}

\label{subsection:IV.8.13}

\oldpage[IV-3]{49}

\begin{proposition}[8.13.1]
\label{IV.8.13.1}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $(X_\lambda,v_{\lambda\mu})$ a filtered projective system of $S$-preschemes;
suppose that there exists $\alpha$ such that $v_{\alpha\lambda}$ is an affine morphism for all $\lambda\geq\alpha$ (which implies \sref[II]{II.1.6.2} that $v_{\lambda\mu}$ is affine for $\alpha\leq\lambda\leq\mu$), so that the projective limit $X=\varprojlim X_\lambda$ exists in the category of $S$-preschemes \sref{IV.8.2.3}.
Let $Y$ be an $S$-prescheme, and, for all $\lambda\geq\alpha$, let $e_\lambda:\Hom_S(X_\lambda,Y)\to\Hom_S(X,Y)$ be the map which, to every $S$-morphism $f_\lambda:X_\lambda\to Y$, associates $f=f_\lambda\circ v_\lambda$, where $v_\lambda:X\to X_\lambda$ is the canonical morphism.
The family $(e_\lambda)$ is an inductive system of maps, which thus defines a canonical map
\[
  \varinjlim\Hom_S(X_\lambda,Y)\to\Hom_S(X,Y).
  \tag{8.13.1.1}\label{IV.8.13.1.1}
\]

Suppose $X_\alpha$ quasi-compact (resp.\ quasi-compact and quasi-separated), and the structural morphism $Y\to S$ locally of finite type (resp.\ locally of finite presentation).
Then the map \eqref{IV.8.13.1.1} is injective (resp.\ bijective).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.13.1.proof}
Set, for $\lambda\geq\alpha$, $Z_\lambda=Y\times_S X_\lambda$, so that $Z_\lambda=Z_\alpha\times_{X_\alpha}X_\lambda$.
Set also $Z=Y\times_S X=Z_\alpha\times_{X_\alpha}X$;
we know \sref{IV.8.2.5} that, if we also set $w_{\lambda\mu}=1\times v_{\lambda\mu}:Z_\mu\to Z_\lambda$ for $\alpha\leq\lambda\leq\mu$ and $w_\lambda=1\times v_\lambda:Z\to Z_\lambda$ for $\alpha\leq\lambda$, $Z$ is the projective limit of the system $(Z_\lambda,w_{\lambda\mu})$ and the $w_\lambda$ are the canonical corresponding morphisms.
Note moreover that the morphism $Z_\alpha\to X_\alpha$ is locally of finite type (resp.\ locally of finite presentation \sref[I]{I.1.3.4} and \sref[I]{I.1.4.3}).
Finally, we know that
\[
  \Hom_S(X_\lambda,Y)=\Hom_{X_\lambda}(X_\lambda,Z_\lambda)\quad\text{and}\quad\Hom_S(X,Y)=\Hom_X(X,Z)
\]
\sref[I]{I.3.3.14}.
It then suffices to apply (\sref{IV.8.8.2}, (i)) replacing $Y_\lambda$ by $Z_\lambda$ and $y$ by $X_\lambda=S_\lambda$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.13.2]
\label{IV.8.13.2}
With the notations of \sref{IV.8.13.1}, suppose $X_\alpha$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated, and the $v_{\alpha\lambda}$ affine for $\alpha\leq\lambda$;
suppose moreover that $Y=\varprojlim Y_\rho$, where $(Y_\rho,t_{\rho\sigma})$ is a filtered projective system of $S$-preschemes such that, for each $\rho$, the structural morphism $Y_\rho\to S$ is locally of finite presentation.
Then we have a canonical bijection
\[
  \Hom_S(X,Y)\xrightarrow{\sim}\varprojlim_\rho\left(\varinjlim_\lambda\Hom_S(X_\lambda,Y_\rho)\right).
  \tag{8.13.2.1}\label{IV.8.13.2.1}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.13.2.proof}
Indeed, the fact that $Y$ is the projective limit of the $Y_\rho$ implies in particular that the canonical map $\Hom_S(X,Y)\to\varprojlim_\rho\Hom_S(X,Y_\rho)$ is bijective;
and on the other hand, the hypotheses imply, for each $\rho$, the existence of a canonical bijection $\Hom_S(X,Y_\rho)\xrightarrow{\sim}\varinjlim_\lambda\Hom_S(X_\lambda,Y_\rho)$ by virtue of \sref{IV.8.13.1};
whence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}{8.13.3}
\label{IV.8.13.3}
The preceding results allow us to interpret in the theory of preschemes the notions of ``pro-variety'' or ``pro-scheme'' that arise in certain applications (for example in the theory of the local class field following the ideas of Serre~\cite{serre_groupes_proalgebriques} or in the theory of N\'{e}ron on the reduction of abelian varieties~\cite{neron_modeles_minimaux}).
Let us recall briefly here the notion of \emph{pro-object} of a category, referring to chap.~V for more ample developments (we will not in fact use before chap.~V the interpretation that follows, and the reader may thus omit until then the reading of the end of this number).
\oldpage[IV-3]{50}
Given a category $\mathcal{C}$, the category $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C})$ of \emph{pro-objects} of $\mathcal{C}$ has for objects the \emph{projective systems} (in the universe in which we place ourselves) $\mathbf{X}=(X_\mu)_{\mu\in M}$ of objects of $\mathcal{C}$ whose index sets (depending on the projective system considered) are supposed preordered and filtered.
Given two such pro-objects $\mathbf{X}=(X_\mu)_{\mu\in M}$, $\mathbf{X}'=(X'_{\mu'})_{\mu'\in M'}$, the \emph{morphisms} of $\mathbf{X}$ into $\mathbf{X}'$ are by definition the elements of the set $\varprojlim_{\mu'}(\varinjlim_\mu\Hom(X_\mu,X'_{\mu'}))$;
the verification that one can take these sets for sets of morphisms is immediate, the composition of systems of morphisms $u^{\mu}_{\mu'}:X_\mu\to X'_{\mu'}$, $u'^{\mu'}_{\mu''}:X'_{\mu'}\to X''_{\mu''}$, which are inductive in the upper index and projective in the lower index, being done ``argument by argument'', in other words by considering the system of $u''^{\mu}_{\mu''}=u'^{\mu'}_{\mu''}\circ u^{\mu}_{\mu'}$.
\end{env}

\begin{env}{8.13.4}
\label{IV.8.13.4}
Consider a quasi-compact and quasi-separated prescheme $S$, and denote by $\mathcal{C}$ the full subcategory of the category $(\mathbf{Sch})_{/S}$ of $S$-preschemes, formed by the $S$-preschemes $X$ having the following property:
the morphism $X\to S$ factorizes as $X\xrightarrow{g}X_0\xrightarrow{f}S$, where $g:X\to X_0$ is affine and $f:X_0\to S$ is of finite presentation;
we will say for brevity that the preschemes of $\mathcal{C}$ are \emph{essentially affine over $S$}.

Consider on the other hand the full subcategory $\mathcal{C}'_0$ of $(\mathbf{Sch})_{/S}$ formed by the $S$-preschemes of finite presentation, and the category $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C}'_0)$ of pro-objects of $\mathcal{C}'_0$.
We will say that an object $\mathbf{X}=(X_\mu)_{\mu\in M}$ of $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C}'_0)$ is \emph{essentially affine} if there exists $\gamma\in M$ such that for all $\mu\geq\gamma$, the transition morphism $v_{\gamma\mu}:X_\mu\to X_\gamma$ is \emph{affine} (which implies that for $\gamma\leq\mu\leq\nu$, $v_{\mu\nu}:X_\nu\to X_\mu$ is affine).
Note that an object of $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C}'_0)$ \emph{isomorphic} to an essentially affine object is not necessarily itself essentially affine.
We will denote by $\mathcal{C}'$ the full subcategory of $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C}'_0)$ formed by the essentially affine pro-objects of $\mathcal{C}'_0$.

This being so, it follows from \sref{IV.8.2.2} and \sref{IV.8.2.3} that for every object $\mathbf{X}=(X_\mu)_{\mu\in M}$ of $\mathcal{C}'$, the $S$-prescheme $X=\varprojlim_\mu X_\mu$ exists;
moreover, since, for $\mu$ large enough, the morphism $X\to X_\mu$ is affine \sref{IV.8.2.2}, $X$ is essentially affine over $S$ by definition.
Set $X=\mathrm{L}(\mathbf{X})$;
let us show that we have thus defined a \emph{functor}
\[
  \mathrm{L}:\mathcal{C}'\to\mathcal{C}
  \tag{8.13.4.1}\label{IV.8.13.4.1}
\]

Indeed, for any two objects $\mathbf{X}=(X_\mu)$, $\mathbf{X}'=(X'_{\mu'})$ of $\mathcal{C}'$, one has for each $\mu'$ a canonical map
\[
  \varinjlim_\mu\Hom_S(X_\mu,X'_{\mu'})\to\Hom_S(\varprojlim_\mu X_\mu,X'_{\mu'})
\]
\oldpage[IV-3]{51}
defined in \sref{IV.8.13.1.1}, and by the definition of the projective limit, a canonical bijection
\[
  \varprojlim_{\mu'}\Hom_S(\varprojlim_\mu X_\mu,X'_{\mu'})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom_S(\varprojlim_\mu X_\mu,\varprojlim_{\mu'}X'_{\mu'})
\]
whence a canonical map
\[
  \varprojlim_{\mu'}(\varinjlim_\mu\Hom_S(X_\mu,X'_{\mu'}))\to\Hom(\varprojlim_\mu X_\mu,\varprojlim_{\mu'}X'_{\mu'})
  \tag{8.13.4.2}\label{IV.8.13.4.2}
\]
evidently functorial in $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{X}'$, and which completes the definition of the functor $\mathrm{L}$.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.13.5]
\label{IV.8.13.5}
The hypotheses and notations being those of \sref{IV.8.13.4}, the functor $\mathrm{L}$ is fully faithful.
If moreover $S$ is a noetherian prescheme (which already implies that $S$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated \sref[I]{I.1.2.8}), $\mathrm{L}$ is an equivalence of categories.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.13.5.proof}
To say that $\mathrm{L}$ is fully faithful means that the map \eqref{IV.8.13.4.2} is bijective for all $\mathbf{X}$, $\mathbf{X}'$ in $\mathcal{C}'$, which is a particular case of \sref{IV.8.13.2}:
indeed, the structural morphisms $X_\mu\to S$ being of finite presentation, are in particular quasi-compact and quasi-separated, hence the $X_\mu$ are quasi-compact and quasi-separated.

To show that when $S$ is noetherian $\mathrm{L}$ is an equivalence of categories, it suffices, since we already know that $\mathrm{L}$ is fully faithful, to prove that every $S$-prescheme essentially affine $X$ is $S$-isomorphic to an object of the form $\mathrm{L}(\mathbf{X})$ where $\mathbf{X}\in\mathcal{C}'$ $(\mathbf{0}_{\text{III}}, 8.1.5)$.
Now, there is by hypothesis a factorization $X\xrightarrow{g}X_0\xrightarrow{f}S$ of the structural morphism, $f$ being of finite presentation and $g$ affine.
We can thus write $X=\Spec(\sh{A})$, where $\sh{A}$ is a quasi-coherent $\sh{O}_{X_0}$-Algebra \sref[II]{II.1.3.1}.
Now, since $X_0$ is noetherian (since $S$ is noetherian and $f$ is of finite type), $\sh{A}$ is the filtered inductive limit of the family $(\sh{A}_\lambda)$ of its quasi-coherent sub-$\sh{O}_{X_0}$-Algebras of finite type \sref[I]{I.9.6.6}.
Set $X_\lambda=\Spec(\sh{A}_\lambda)$;
the morphisms $X_\lambda\to X_0$ are of finite type, hence of finite presentation since $X_0$ is noetherian, and consequently the same holds for the composite morphisms $X_\lambda\to X_0\to S$ \sref[I]{I.1.6.2};
in other words, the $X_\lambda$ belong to $\mathcal{C}'_0$ and since the morphisms $X_\lambda\to X_0$ are affine, $\mathbf{X}=(X_\lambda)$ is an object of $\mathcal{C}'$ whose projective limit exists and is $S$-isomorphic to $X$ by virtue of \sref{IV.8.2.2}.
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[8.13.6]
\label{IV.8.13.6}
It follows from \sref[I]{I.1.6.2} and \sref[II]{II.1.6.2} that if $X$ and $Y$ are essentially affine over $S$, then so is $X\times_S Y$.
We thus conclude for example $(\mathbf{0}_{\text{III}}, 8.2.5)$ that a $\mathcal{C}$-group is nothing other than a $(\mathbf{Sch})_{/S}$-group which is a prescheme essentially affine over $S$.
On the other hand, finite products exist in the category $\mathcal{C}'$:
indeed, if $\mathbf{X}=(X_\mu)_{\mu\in M}$, $\mathbf{Y}=(Y_\rho)_{\rho\in R}$ are two objects of $\mathcal{C}'$, the products $X_\mu\times_S Y_\rho$ are $S$-preschemes of finite presentation, and taking for transition morphisms $X_\nu\times_S Y_\sigma\to X_\mu\times_S Y_\rho$ the products of the transition morphisms $X_\nu\to X_\mu$ and $Y_\sigma\to Y_\rho$, we see immediately that $(X_\mu\times_S Y_\rho)$ is the product of $\mathbf{X}$ and $\mathbf{Y}$ in $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C}'_0)$;
moreover \sref[II]{II.1.6.2} the transition morphisms thus defined are affine for $\mu$ and $\rho$ large enough, so the product $\mathbf{X}\times\mathbf{Y}$ thus defined belongs to $\mathcal{C}'$.
We conclude as above that a $\mathcal{C}'$-group is a $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C}'_0)$-group that is essentially affine.
\oldpage[IV-3]{52}
We deduce from \sref{IV.8.13.5} that the categories of $\mathcal{C}'$-groups and $\mathcal{C}$-groups are \emph{equivalent} when $S$ is \emph{noetherian}.
It seems plausible that when $S$ is the spectrum of a field $k$, the category of $\mathcal{C}$-groups is \emph{equivalent} to that of $\mathbf{K}$-groups, where $\mathbf{K}$ is the category of $S$-preschemes \emph{quasi-compacts};
in other words, every prescheme in groups over $k$ that is quasi-compact would be essentially affine.
On the other hand, if we denote by $\mathcal{C}'_0\text{-}\mathbf{gr}$ the category of $\mathcal{C}'_0$-groups, it is plausible that the category of $\mathcal{C}'$-groups is equivalent to the full subcategory of $\mathbf{Pro}(\mathcal{C}'_0\text{-}\mathbf{gr})$ formed by the ``essentially affine proalgebraic groups'', that is to say by the projective systems $\mathbf{G}=(G_\mu)_{\mu\in M}$, where the $G_\mu$ are algebraic groups over $k$ and the transition morphisms $G_\nu\to G_\mu$ are affine (which one can also express by saying that $\mathbf{G}$ is an extension of an algebraic group by an affine pro-algebraic group).
The conjunction of these two conjectures amounts to the following:
every prescheme in quasi-compact groups over $k$ is an ``extension'' of an ``algebraic group'' (i.e.\ a prescheme in groups of finite type over $k$) by a prescheme in \emph{affine} groups over $k$.

The only pro-algebraic groups encountered in practice up to now being in fact essentially affine, there will thus undoubtedly be an advantage in substituting the study of general pro-algebraic groups (introduced and studied by Serre~\cite{serre_groupes_proalgebriques2}) by that of schemes in quasi-compact groups over $k$, whose definition is conceptually simpler.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Characterization of a prescheme locally of finite presentation over another, in terms of the functor it represents}
\label{subsection:IV.8.14}

\begin{env}{8.14.1}
\label{IV.8.14.1}
Given a prescheme $S$, we will say again, as in \sref{IV.8.13.4}, that a filtered projective system $(X_\lambda,v_{\lambda\mu})$ of $S$-preschemes is \emph{essentially affine} if there exists $\alpha$ such that $v_{\alpha\lambda}$ is an affine morphism for $\lambda\geq\alpha$.

The following statement, which will be especially useful in chap.~V, gives a converse to (\sref{IV.8.8.2}, (i)):
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[8.14.2]
\label{IV.8.14.2}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $f:X\to S$ a morphism.
For every $S$-prescheme $T$, set
\[
  h_X(T)=\Hom_S(T,X)
\]
so that $h_X$ is a contravariant functor from the category $(\mathbf{Sch})_{/S}$ of $S$-preschemes to the category $\mathbf{Ens}$ of sets $(\mathbf{0}_{\text{III}}, 8.1.1)$, and $X$ an object representing the functor $(\mathbf{0}_{\text{III}}, 8.1.8)$.
The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[a)] $f$ is locally of finite presentation.
  \item[b)] For every filtered projective system $(Z_\lambda)$ of $S$-preschemes, essentially affine \sref{IV.8.13.4} and formed of quasi-compact and quasi-separated preschemes, the canonical map \sref{IV.8.13.1.1}
  \[
    \varinjlim h_X(Z_\lambda)\to h_X(\varprojlim Z_\lambda)
    \tag{8.14.2.1}\label{IV.8.14.2.1}
  \]
  is bijective.
  \item[c)] For every filtered projective system $(Z_\lambda)$ of $S$-preschemes, such that the $Z_\lambda$ are affine schemes, the map \eqref{IV.8.14.2.1} is bijective.
  \item[c$'$)] For every affine open subset $U$ of $S$ and every filtered projective system $(Z_\lambda)$ of $U$-preschemes, such that the $Z_\lambda$ are affine schemes, the map \eqref{IV.8.14.2.1} is bijective.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.14.2.proof}
\oldpage[IV-3]{53}
The fact that $a)$ implies $b)$ is nothing other than \sref{IV.8.13.1};
it is trivial that $b)$ implies $c)$ and that $c)$ implies $c')$.
It remains to see that $c')$ implies $a)$, and since the property $a)$ is local on $S$, we can reduce to the case where $S$ is affine.

Suppose first that $X$ is also affine;
the assertion to prove is then equivalent to the following:
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[8.14.2.2]
\label{IV.8.14.2.2}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra.
In order that, for every filtered inductive system $(C_\lambda)$ of $A$-algebras, the canonical map
\[
  \varinjlim\Hom_{A\text{-alg.}}(B,C_\lambda)\to\Hom_{A\text{-alg.}}(B,\varinjlim C_\lambda)
  \tag{8.14.2.3}\label{IV.8.14.2.3}
\]
be bijective, it is necessary and sufficient that $B$ be an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.8.14.2.2.proof}
It remains only to show that the condition is necessary.
Let us first take for $(C_\lambda)$ the filtered inductive system of sub-$A$-algebras of finite type of $B$, of $B$, so that $\varinjlim C_\lambda=B$.
The fact that \eqref{IV.8.14.2.3} is bijective implies in particular that the identity $1_B$ of $B$ factorizes as $B\to C_\lambda\to B$ for some suitable $\lambda$, which implies $C_\lambda=B$, hence $B$ is an $A$-algebra \emph{of finite type}.
Set then $B=C/\mathfrak{J}$, where $C=A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and $\mathfrak{J}$ is an ideal of $C$.
Then $\mathfrak{J}$ is the filtered inductive limit of its ideals of finite type $\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$;
setting $C_\lambda=C/\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$, and using the exactness of the inductive limit functor $\varinjlim$, we see that $B$ is still isomorphic to the inductive limit of the filtered system $(C_\lambda)$.
There thus exists a $\lambda$ and an $A$-homomorphism $u:B\to C_\lambda$ such that the composite $B\xrightarrow{u}C_\lambda\xrightarrow{p_\lambda}B$ (where $p_\lambda$ is the canonical homomorphism) is the identity.
Set $q_\lambda:C\to C_\lambda$ the canonical homomorphism, and set $t_i=p_\lambda(q_\lambda(T_i))$;
we thus have $p_\lambda(u(t_i))=p_\lambda(q_\lambda(T_i))$, in other words $u(t_i)-q_\lambda(T_i)\in\mathfrak{J}/\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$ $(1\leq i\leq n)$;
if $\rho_{\mu\lambda}:C_\lambda\to C_\mu$ is the canonical homomorphism, there thus exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that the $n$ elements $u(t_i)-q_\lambda(T_i)$ belong to $\mathfrak{J}_\mu/\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$ $(1\leq i\leq n)$;
we thus have $\rho_{\mu\lambda}(u(t_i))=\rho_{\mu\lambda}(q_\lambda(T_i))=q_\mu(T_i)$.
Replacing $\lambda$ by $\mu$ and $u$ by $\rho_{\mu\lambda}\circ u$, we can thus suppose $u(t_i)=q_\lambda(T_i)$ for all $i$, and if $r=p_\lambda\circ q_\lambda$ is the canonical homomorphism $C\to C/\mathfrak{J}=B$, we can thus write $u(r(T_i))=q_\lambda(T_i)$ for all $i$, from which $q_\lambda=u\circ r$.
But this implies necessarily that $\mathfrak{J}=\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$, for if $z\in\mathfrak{J}$, then $r(z)=0$; hence on $B=C_\lambda$.

Now pass to the case where $S$ is affine and $X$ arbitrary;
it amounts, in virtue of what has just been shown, to proving that for every affine open subset $V$ of $X$ and every filtered projective system $(Z_\lambda)$ of affine $S$-preschemes, the map
\[
  \varinjlim\Hom_S(Z_\lambda,V)\to\Hom_S(\varprojlim Z_\lambda,V)
  \tag{8.14.2.4}\label{IV.8.14.2.4}
\]
\oldpage[IV-3]{54}
is bijective.
It is immediate that this map is injective, for if $(v_\lambda)$, $(v'_\lambda)$ are two inductive systems of $S$-homomorphisms $v_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to V$, $v'_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to V$ such that the morphisms
\[
  Z\xrightarrow{u_\lambda}Z_\lambda\xrightarrow{v_\lambda}V,\qquad Z\xrightarrow{u_\lambda}Z_\lambda\xrightarrow{v'_\lambda}V
\]
are equal ($u_\lambda$ being the canonical morphism), then the morphisms
\[
  Z\xrightarrow{u_\lambda}Z_\lambda\xrightarrow{v_\lambda}V\xrightarrow{j}X,\qquad Z\xrightarrow{u_\lambda}Z_\lambda\xrightarrow{v'_\lambda}V\xrightarrow{j}X
\]
(where $j$ is the canonical injection) are equal, which implies $j\circ v_\lambda=j\circ v'_\lambda$ by hypothesis for some suitable $\lambda$, hence $v_\lambda=v'_\lambda$.

It remains to prove that \eqref{IV.8.14.2.4} is surjective.
Let $v:Z\to V$ be an $S$-morphism;
by hypothesis there exists $\lambda$ and an $S$-morphism $w_\lambda:Z_\lambda\to X$ such that $j\circ v$ factorizes as
$Z\xrightarrow{u_\lambda}Z_\lambda\xrightarrow{w_\lambda}X$, and it suffices to prove that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that the morphism
\[
  Z_\mu\xrightarrow{u_{\lambda\mu}}Z_\lambda\xrightarrow{w_\lambda}X
\]
(where $u_{\lambda\mu}$ is the transition morphism) factorizes as $Z_\mu\xrightarrow{v_\mu}V\xrightarrow{j}X$.
Set, for $\mu\geq\lambda$, $U_\mu=u^{-1}_{\lambda\mu}(w^{-1}_\lambda(V))$.
Since the $Z_\lambda$ are quasi-compact and the $U_\mu$, being open, are ind-constructible \sref[I]{I.1.9.6}, we deduce from \sref{IV.8.3.4} that there exists $\mu\geq\lambda$ such that $U_\mu=Z_\mu$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[8.14.3]
\label{IV.8.14.3}
The fact that the map \eqref{IV.8.14.2.1} is injective when $f$ is locally of finite type (\sref{IV.8.8.2}, (i)) naturally leads one to ask whether this result also admits a converse.
This is not the case, even when $S$ and $X$ are affine, for there exist monomorphisms $X\to S$ that are not of finite type \sref[I]{I.2.4.2}, and which therefore disprove this conjecture.
\end{remark}
