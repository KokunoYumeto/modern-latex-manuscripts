\section{Equidimensional morphisms}
\label{section:IV.13}

\oldpage[IV-3]{187}

This paragraph is devoted to the study of the variation of the \emph{dimension} of the fibres of a morphism locally of finite type $f:X\to Y$ (which has already appeared in connection with the ``dimension formula'' in 5.5 and 5.6).
One first proves (13.1.3) that the function $x\mapsto\dim_x f^{-1}(f(x))$ is always \emph{semi-continuous superiorly} in $X$ (Chevalley's theorem of semi-continuity).
One then studies more particularly the morphisms called ``equidimensional'', for which this function is \emph{locally constant}.
Unfortunately, the notion of equidimensional morphism is not stable by base change; this is why in many questions it is more convenient to work with the notion of universally open morphism, which is the object of \textsection\textsection~14 and~15.

\subsection{Chevalley's theorem of semi-continuity}
\label{subsection:IV.13.1}

\begin{lemma}[13.1.1]
\label{IV.13.1.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{188}
Let $Y$ be an irreducible locally Noetherian prescheme, $X$ an irreducible prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a dominant morphism of finite type.
Let $\xi$ (resp.\ $\eta$) be the generic point of $X$ (resp.\ $Y$) and let $e=\dim(f^{-1}(\eta))$.
Then, for every $x\in X$, all the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(f(x))$ are of dimension $\geq e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proposition is immediate when, for every $y\in f(X)$, $\sh{O}_y$ is a \emph{universally catenary} ring: indeed, if $x$ is the generic point of an irreducible component $Z$ of $f^{-1}(y)$, it follows from (5.6.5), joined with (5.2.1), that $e+\dim(\sh{O}_y)=\dim(Z)+\dim(\sh{O}_x)$, but by ($\mathbf{0}$, 16.3.9), one has $\dim(\sh{O}_x)\leq\dim(\sh{O}_y)$, hence the conclusion in this case.

We shall reduce the general case to this particular case.
The question is evidently local on $Y$ and, taking into account (4.1.1.3), it is also local on $X$; one can thus restrict to the case where $Y=\Spec(A)$ and $X=\Spec(B)$ are affine and irreducible, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite type.
Moreover (1.5.4), one can suppose $X$ and $Y$ reduced, hence $A$ and $B$ integral.
Consider $A$ as inductive limit of its sub-$\bb{Z}$-algebras of finite type; it follows then from (8.9.1) that there exist such a sub-algebra $A_0$ and an $A_0$-algebra of finite type $B_0$ such that $B=B_0\otimes_{A_0} A$.
Set $Y_0=\Spec(A_0)$, $X_0=\Spec(B_0)$, and let $f_0:X_0\to Y_0$ be the morphism corresponding to $A_0\to B_0$, so that $f=f_0\times 1_Y$.
It is not \emph{a priori} evident that the prescheme $X_0$ be \emph{integral}, but we shall now see that one can reduce to this case.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[13.1.2]
\label{IV.13.1.2}
Let $Y_0$, $Y$ be two preschemes integral of generic points $\eta_0$, $\eta$, $g:Y\to Y_0$ a dominant morphism, $f_0:X_0\to Y_0$ a dominant morphism such that $f_0^{-1}(\eta_0)$ is irreducible.
Let $X'_0$ be the unique irreducible component of $X_0$ meeting $f_0^{-1}(\eta_0)$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.1.8), and designate again by $X'_0$ the reduced closed sub-prescheme of $X_0$ having $X'_0$ for underlying space.
Suppose that the prescheme $X=X'_0\times_{Y_0} Y$ is integral; then $X$ is isomorphic to $X'_0\times_{Y_0} Y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, if $j_0:X'_0\to X_0$ is the canonical injection, which is a closed immersion, $j=j_0\times 1_Y:X'_0\times_{Y_0} Y\to X_0\times_{Y_0} Y=X$ is a closed immersion.
On the other hand, $X'_0$ contains the fibre $f_0^{-1}(\eta)$; note on the other hand that $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is not empty (\textbf{I}, 3.4.7), hence contains the generic point of $X$; hence the image of $j$ necessarily contains $X$ entirely.
But as $X$ is integral, the only closed sub-prescheme of $X$ having $X$ for underlying space is $X$ itself, hence $j$ is an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

This lemma being established, one can thus suppose that $X_0$ is integral; for every $y_0\in Y_0$, $\sh{O}_{y_0}$ is a $\bb{Z}$-algebra essentially of finite type, hence a universally catenary ring (5.6.4); hence, for every $y_0\in f_0(X_0)$, all the irreducible components of $f_0^{-1}(y_0)$ have dimension $\geq e$ by transitivity of the fibres (4.1.4).
For every $y\in f(X)$, one has $y_0=g(y)\in f_0(X_0)$ and by transitivity of the fibres and (4.2.7), the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$ are the irreducible components of $p^{-1}(Z_0)$, where $Z_0$ ranges over the irreducible components of $f_0^{-1}(y_0)$, and each of the irreducible components of $p^{-1}(Z_0)$ dominates $Z_0$ and has dimension equal to $\dim(Z_0)$.
Taking into account ($\mathbf{0}$, 14.1.5), one sees that $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))=\dim_{x_0}(f_0^{-1}(f_0(x_0)))$, where $F_n(X)=g'^{-1}(F_n(X_0))$, and one is thus reduced to proving the theorem when $Y$ is \emph{Noetherian}, which we shall now suppose.

\begin{theorem}[13.1.3]
\label{IV.13.1.3}
\textnormal{(Chevalley).}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type.
For every integer $n$, the set $F_n(X)$ of $x\in X$ such that $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))\geq n$ is \emph{closed}; in other words, the function $x\mapsto\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))$ is semi-continuous superiorly in $X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{I)} Suppose first that $f$ is locally of finite presentation.
The question is evidently local on $X$ and on $Y$, and one can thus suppose $Y=\Spec(A)$, $X=\Spec(B)$ affine, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
Then by (8.9.1) there is a Noetherian sub-ring $A_0$ of $A$, and an $A_0$-algebra of finite type $B_0$ such that $B=B_0\otimes_{A_0} A$, so $X=X_0\times_{Y_0} Y$, $f=f_0\times 1_Y$.
Let $g:Y\to Y_0$ be the morphism corresponding to $A_0\to A$, $y$ a point of $Y$, $y_0=g(y)$; one knows that $f^{-1}(y)=f_0^{-1}(y_0)\otimes_{\kappa(y_0)}\kappa(y)$, and it follows from (4.2.7) that if $p$ is the canonical projection $f^{-1}(y)\to f_0^{-1}(y_0)$, the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$ are the irreducible components of $p^{-1}(Z_0)$, where $Z_0$ ranges over the irreducible components of $f_0^{-1}(y_0)$, and each has dimension $\dim(Z_0)$.
Taking into account ($\mathbf{0}$, 14.1.5), one sees thus that if $g':X\to X_0$ is the canonical projection, $x$ a point of $X$ and $x_0=g'(x)$, one has $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))=\dim_{x_0}(f_0^{-1}(f_0(x_0)))$, hence $F_n(X)=g'^{-1}(F_n(X_0))$, and one is thus reduced to proving the theorem when $Y$ is \emph{Noetherian}, which we shall now suppose.

One can evidently suppose $X$ and $Y$ reduced (1.5.4).
Considering the set of closed parts $Y'$ of $Y$ such that the theorem holds for the closed sub-prescheme having $Y'$ for underlying space and for $X'=f^{-1}(Y')$, one can argue by Noetherian induction ($\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.2.2), and suppose that the theorem is true for every closed part $Y'\neq Y$ of $Y$.
Let $X_i$ ($1\leq i\leq m$) be the closed sub-preschemes which are the reduced irreducible components of $X$; by ($\mathbf{0}$, 14.1.5), $F_n(X)=\bigcup_i F_n(X_i)$ and one can thus restrict to the case where $X$ is \emph{irreducible}.
If $Z$ is the closed sub-prescheme of $Y$ having $\overline{f(X)}$ for underlying space, $f$ factorises as $X\xrightarrow{f'}Z\xrightarrow{j}Y$, where $j$ is the canonical injection, and $f'$ is of finite type (1.5.4), hence it suffices to prove the theorem for $Z$ and $g$; by the induction hypothesis, one is thus reduced to the case where $Z=Y$, i.e.\ $Y$ is irreducible and $f$ dominant.
Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $Y$ and set $e=\dim(f^{-1}(\eta))$; it follows from \sref{IV.13.1.1} that for $n\leq e$ one has $F_n(X)=X$, and one can thus restrict to the case $n>e$.
But then (9.5.6), there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $\eta$ in $Y$ such that $F_n(X)\subset f^{-1}(Y-U)$; as $Y-U\neq Y$, the induction hypothesis implies that $F_n(X)$ is closed.

\emph{II)} Let us now pass to the general case, still supposing $S=\Spec(A)$ and $X=\Spec(B)$ affine, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite type, hence of the form $B=A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{J}$.
Let $(\mathfrak{J}_\lambda)$ be the family of ideals of finite type of $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ contained in $\mathfrak{J}$; if $X$ and the $X_\lambda=\Spec(A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{J}_\lambda)$ are considered as closed sub-preschemes of $Z=\Spec(A[T_1,\ldots,T_n])$, $X=\bigcap_\lambda X_\lambda$ for the underlying spaces.
If $f_\lambda:X_\lambda\to S$ is the structural morphism, one deduces that $f^{-1}(s)=\bigcap_\lambda f_\lambda^{-1}(s)$ for every $s\in S$, and as the sets $f_\lambda^{-1}(s)$ are closed in the Noetherian space $Z_s$, it exists for every $x\in X$, a $\lambda$ (depending on $s$) such that $f^{-1}(s)=f_\lambda^{-1}(s)$.
Then, for every $x\in X$, $d(x)=\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))$, $d_\lambda(x)=\dim_x(f_\lambda^{-1}(f_\lambda(x)))$, $d(x)=\inf_\lambda d_\lambda(x)$; as the functions $d_\lambda$ are semi-continuous superiorly by the first part of the proof, so is $d$.
C.Q.F.D.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[13.1.4]
\label{IV.13.1.4}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.13.1.3}, the set of $x\in X$ such that $x$ is isolated in $f^{-1}(f(x))$ is open in $X$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, this is the complementary of $F_1(X)$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 14.1.10).
One notes that one thus recovers, under more general hypotheses, the consequence of the ``Main theorem'' of Zariski (\textbf{III}, 4.4.10).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[13.1.5]
\label{IV.13.1.5}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.13.1.3}, suppose also that $f$ is a \emph{closed} morphism.
Then, for every integer $n$, the set of $y\in Y$ such that $\dim(f^{-1}(y))\geq n$ is \emph{closed}; in other words, the function $y\mapsto\dim(f^{-1}(y))$ is semi-continuous superiorly; in particular if $y$ is a specialisation of $y'$, one has $\dim(f^{-1}(y))\geq\dim(f^{-1}(y'))$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[13.1.6]
\label{IV.13.1.6}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two irreducible preschemes, $f:X\to Y$ a dominant morphism locally of finite type.
Let $\xi$ (resp.\ $\eta$) be the generic point of $X$ (resp.\ $Y$) and let $e=\dim(f^{-1}(\eta))$.
Then, for every $x\in X$, one has $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))\geq e$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, the set of $x$ such that $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))<e$ is open by \sref{IV.13.1.3}, and as it cannot contain $\xi$, it is empty.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[13.1.7]
\label{IV.13.1.7}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme quasi-compact, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type.
There exists an integer $n$ such that, for every $y\in Y$, $\dim(f^{-1}(y))\leq n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As there is a finite covering by affine open sets $(V_i)$ of $Y$ such that each $f^{-1}(V_i)$ is a finite union of affine opens, one is immediately reduced to the case where $Y=\Spec(A)$ and $X=\Spec(B)$ are affine, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite type.
If $B$ admits a system of $n$ generators, then $B\otimes_A\kappa(y)$ is a $\kappa(y)$-algebra admitting $n$ generators, hence $\dim(f^{-1}(y))\leq n$ by (4.1.1).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Equidimensional morphisms: case of dominant morphisms of irreducible preschemes}
\label{subsection:IV.13.2}

\begin{env}[13.2.1]
\label{IV.13.2.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{190}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme irreducible, $X$ a prescheme irreducible, $f:X\to Y$ a \emph{dominant} morphism locally of finite type; let $\eta$ be the generic point of $Y$.
One knows (13.1.6) that for every $x\in X$, one has $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))\geq\dim(f^{-1}(\eta))$.
\end{env}

\begin{definition}[13.2.2]
\label{IV.13.2.2}
Under the hypotheses of \sref{IV.13.2.1}, we say that $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$ (or that $X$ is equidimensional over $Y$ at the point $x$) if
\[
  \dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))=\dim(f^{-1}(\eta)).
\]
We say that $f$ is equidimensional (or that $X$ is equidimensional over $Y$) if $f$ is equidimensional at every point $x\in X$.
\end{definition}

It follows from Chevalley's theorem \sref{IV.13.1.3} that the set of $x\in X$ where $f$ is equidimensional is \emph{open and non-empty}.
Moreover, if $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$, all the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(f(x))$ containing $x$ have the \emph{same dimension}, which is $\geq\dim(f^{-1}(\eta))$ (13.1.6) and $\leq\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))$ by ($\mathbf{0}$, 14.1.5).

\begin{proposition}[13.2.3]
\label{IV.13.2.3}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme irreducible locally Noetherian, $X$ a prescheme irreducible, $f:X\to Y$ a dominant morphism locally of finite type; let $\eta$ be the generic point of $Y$, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$, and suppose that one has
\[
\label{IV.13.2.3.1}\tag{13.2.3.1}
  \dim(\sh{O}_x)=\dim(\sh{O}_y)+\dim(\sh{O}_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}\kappa(y)).
\]
Then $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$.
The converse is true if the two members of the inequality (5.6.5.2) are equal, in particular if $\sh{O}_y$ is a universally catenary ring.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This follows immediately from (5.6.5.2) and the inequality $\delta(x)\geq 0$ \sref{IV.13.1.1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{env}[13.2.4]
\label{IV.13.2.4}
Let now, more generally, $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $X'$ a closed irreducible part of $X$ containing $x$, $Y'=\overline{f(X')}$, $\eta'$ the generic point of $Y'$.
Designating again by $X'$ and $Y'$ the reduced closed sub-preschemes of $X$ and $Y$ respectively having $X'$ and $Y'$ for underlying spaces, the restriction $X'\to Y$ of $f$ factorises as $X'\xrightarrow{f'}Y'\xrightarrow{j}Y$, where $j$ is the canonical injection, and $f'$ is of finite type (1.5.4).
Set for short
\[
\label{IV.13.2.4.1}\tag{13.2.4.1}
  A=\sh{O}_{Y,y},\quad B=\sh{O}_{X,x},\quad A'=\sh{O}_{Y',y},\quad B'=\sh{O}_{X',x}.
\]
\end{env}

\begin{lemma}[13.2.5]
\label{IV.13.2.5}
With the notations of \sref{IV.13.2.4}, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item $\dim(B')=\dim(A)+\dim(B\otimes_A\kappa(y))$.
  \item The following relations hold simultaneously:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $\dim(A')=\dim(A)$.
    \item $\dim(B'\otimes_{A'}\kappa(y))=\dim(B\otimes_A\kappa(y))$, in other words $\dim_x(X'\cap f^{-1}(y))=\dim_x(f^{-1}(y))$.
    \item $\dim_x(f'^{-1}(y))=\dim_x(f'^{-1}(\eta'))$, in other words $f':X'\to Y'$ is equidimensional \sref{IV.13.2.2} at the point $x$.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item One has simultaneously the relations:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item $\dim(A')=\dim(A)$.
    \item $\dim(B')=\dim(A)+\dim(B'\otimes_{A'}\kappa(y))$.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item One has the equality
  \[
    \dim(B')=\dim(A')+\dim(B'\otimes_{A'}\kappa(y))-(\dim_x(f'^{-1}(y))-\dim_x(f'^{-1}(\eta')))
  \]
  (a relation that is always verified when $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is a universally catenary ring, by (5.6.5)).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{env}[13.2.6]
\label{IV.13.2.6}
Recall now that the irreducible components $X_i$ containing $x$ are in finite number in $X$ ((5.1.1) and ($\mathbf{0}$, 14.2.1.1)) and that
\[
\label{IV.13.2.6.1}\tag{13.2.6.1}
  \dim(\sh{O}_{X,x})=\sup_i\dim(\sh{O}_{X_i,x}).
\]
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[13.2.7]
\label{IV.13.2.7}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$; one has
\[
\label{IV.13.2.7.1}\tag{13.2.7.1}
  \dim(\sh{O}_{X,x})\leq\dim(\sh{O}_{Y,y})+\dim(\sh{O}_{X,x}\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y,y}}\kappa(y)).
\]
For the two members of \sref{IV.13.2.7.1} to be equal, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist a closed irreducible part $X'$ of $X$ containing $x$ and verifying simultaneously the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If $Y'=\overline{f(X')}$, one has $\dim(\sh{O}_{Y',y})=\dim(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$.
  \item $\dim_x(X'\cap f^{-1}(y))=\dim_x(f^{-1}(y))$ (in other words, $X'$ contains one of the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$ of maximal dimension among all the components).
  It amounts to the same to say that $\dim(\sh{O}_{X,x}\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y,y}}\kappa(y))=\dim(\sh{O}_{X',x}\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y',y}}\kappa(y))$.
  \item $X'$ is equidimensional over $Y'$ at the point $x$, in other words, one has
  \[
    \dim_x(X'\cap f^{-1}(y))=\dim(X'\cap f^{-1}(\eta')),
  \]
  where $\eta'$ is the generic point of $Y'$ (and hence all the irreducible components of $X'\cap f^{-1}(y)$ have the same dimension \sref{IV.13.2.2}).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, one has the equality
\[
  \dim(\sh{O}_{X',x})=\dim(\sh{O}_{Y,y})+\dim(\sh{O}_{X',x}\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y',y}}\kappa(y))
\]
(condition always implied by (iii) when $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is a universally catenary ring).
Moreover, $X'$ is then a irreducible component of $X$ and $Y'$ a irreducible component of $Y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[13.2.8]
\label{IV.13.2.8}
If the two members of \sref{IV.13.2.7.1} are equal, the closed irreducible parts $X'$ of $X$ containing $x$ and verifying conditions (i) to (iv) of \sref{IV.13.2.7} are exactly those for which $\dim(\sh{O}_{X',x})=\dim(\sh{O}_{X,x})$ (which implies necessarily that $X'$ is a irreducible component of $X$).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[13.2.9]
\label{IV.13.2.9}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$.
Suppose that $\sh{O}_{X,y}$ is a universally catenary ring.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item The two members of \sref{IV.13.2.7.1} are equal.
  \item There exists an irreducible component $Z$ of $f^{-1}(y)$ containing $x$, of dimension $\dim_x(f^{-1}(y))$, such that, for every $x'$ in a neighbourhood of $x$ in $Z$, one has
  \[
  \label{IV.13.2.9.1}\tag{13.2.9.1}
    \dim(\sh{O}_{X,x'})=\dim(\sh{O}_{Y,y})+\dim(\sh{O}_{X,x'}\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y,y}}\kappa(y)).
  \]
  \item There exists an irreducible component $Z$ of $f^{-1}(y)$ containing $x$, of dimension $\dim_x(f^{-1}(y))$, such that for the generic point $z$ of $Z$, one has
  \[
  \label{IV.13.2.9.2}\tag{13.2.9.2}
    \dim(\sh{O}_{X,z})=\dim(\sh{O}_{Y,y}).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let us show that $a)$ implies $b)$.
Let $\dim_x(f^{-1}(y))=n$; by \sref{IV.13.2.7}, there exists an irreducible component $X'$ of $X$ verifying conditions (i) to (iv) of \sref{IV.13.2.7}; let $Z$ be an irreducible component of dimension $n$ of $f^{-1}(y)$, containing $x$ and contained in $X'$.
As $\sh{O}_{X,y}$ is universally catenary, since $f^{-1}(y)$ is locally Noetherian, there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ in $X$ meeting no irreducible component of $f^{-1}(y)$ other than those containing $x$, hence (4.1.1.3) $\dim_{x'}(f^{-1}(y))=n$ for every $x'\in U$; it is clear that conditions (i) to (iii) of \sref{IV.13.2.7} are satisfied when one replaces $x$ by any $x'\in U$, and the same is true of condition (iv) since $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is universally catenary; hence the conclusion by \sref{IV.13.2.7}.
Condition $b)$ implies trivially $c)$ by (5.1.2).
Finally, if $c)$ is verified and if $X''$ is an irreducible component of $X$ containing $Z$ and such that conditions (i) to (iv) of \sref{IV.13.2.7} are verified, it is clear that these conditions are also verified when one replaces $X'$ by $X''$ and $x$ by $z$, hence the conditions of \sref{IV.13.2.7} are satisfied, hence $c)$ implies $a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[13.2.10]
\label{IV.13.2.10}
\oldpage[IV-3]{193}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme irreducible locally Noetherian, $\eta$ its generic point, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type, $y$ a point of $f(X)$.
Let $Z_i$ be the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y)$, $z_i$ the generic point of $Z_i$, and consider the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item For $x\in f^{-1}(y)$, one has the relation
  \[
  \label{IV.13.2.10.1}\tag{13.2.10.1}
    \dim(\sh{O}_{X,x})=\dim(\sh{O}_{Y,y})+\dim(\sh{O}_{X,x}\otimes_{\sh{O}_{Y,y}}\kappa(y)).
  \]
  \item For every $i$, one has
  \[
  \label{IV.13.2.10.2}\tag{13.2.10.2}
    \dim(\sh{O}_{X,z_i})=\dim(\sh{O}_{Y,y}).
  \]
  \item For every $i$, there exists an irreducible component $X_i$ of $X$ containing $Z_i$ and such that \emph{the reduced closed sub-prescheme} $X_i$ of $X$ is equidimensional over $Y$ at the point $z_i$  (in other words $\dim(X_i\cap f^{-1}(y))=\dim(f_i^{-1}(\eta))$, where $f_i=f|X_i$).
\end{enumerate}
Then $a)$ implies $b)$ and $b)$ implies $c)$; moreover, if $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is universally catenary, the three conditions $a)$, $b)$, $c)$ are equivalent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[13.2.11]
\label{IV.13.2.11}
The notations being those of \sref{IV.13.2.10}, suppose that $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is universally catenary.
For every $y'\in Y$, let $E(y')$ be the set of dimensions of the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(y')$ and set $d(y')=\dim(f^{-1}(y'))=\sup(E(y'))$.
Then, if the conditions $a)$, $b)$, $c)$ of \sref{IV.13.2.10} are verified, one has $E(y)\subset E(\eta)$, hence $d(y)\leq d(\eta)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Indeed, with the notations of \sref{IV.13.2.10}, $X_i\cap f^{-1}(\eta)$ is not empty and is by construction an irreducible component of $f^{-1}(\eta)$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.1.8).
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[13.2.12]
\label{IV.13.2.12}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item Recall (6.1.2) that the relation \sref{IV.13.2.10.1} is always verified when the morphism $f$ is \emph{flat} at the point $x$.
  \item With the notations of \sref{IV.13.2.10}, suppose that $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is universally catenary and that the morphism $f$ is \emph{proper}; then, if the equivalent conditions $a)$, $b)$, $c)$ of \sref{IV.13.2.10} are satisfied, for the same $d(y)=d(\eta)$, for it follows from \sref{IV.13.1.5} that one has $d(y)\geq d(\eta)$.
  \item The morphism of (12.2.3, (ii)) is \emph{proper} and \emph{flat} and all the local rings are universally catenary; moreover, the two irreducible components $X_1$, $X_2$ of $X$ are \emph{equidimensional} over $Y$ at every point; but $E(y)$ has \emph{two} elements for every $y\neq y_0$, hence $E(y)$ is not constant in $Y$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remarks}

\subsection{Equidimensional morphisms: general case}
\label{subsection:IV.13.3}

\begin{proposition}[13.3.1]
\label{IV.13.3.1}
\oldpage[IV-3]{194}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$.
Designate by $Y_\alpha$ the irreducible components of $Y$ containing $y$.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*)]
  \item There exist an integer $e$ and an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that the image by $f$ of every irreducible component of $U$ is dense in some $Y_\alpha$, and that, for every $x'\in U$, the space $U\cap f^{-1}(f(x'))$ is equidimensional and of dimension $e$.
  \item There exist an integer $e$ and a $Y$-morphism quasi-finite $g:U\to Y[T_1,\ldots,T_e]$ (prescheme that we shall also denote $Y[\mathrm{T}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{T}_e]$ for short) such that the image by $g$ of every irreducible component of $U$ is dense in some irreducible component of $Y[T_1,\ldots,T_e]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is immediate that $a'')$ implies $a)$, for the irreducible components of each of the $U_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(f(x))$ are each irreducible components of one of the $U_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(f(x))$, hence the condition $a)$ is trivially $a'')$.
Let us show next that $a')$ implies $a'')$; one can restrict to the case where $f$ is of finite type and $X$ and $Y$ reduced (1.5.4).
Let $U_\lambda$ be an irreducible component of $U$, and suppose that $f(U_\lambda)$ is dense in $Y_\alpha$; let $x_\lambda$ the generic point of $U_\lambda$; by $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.1.8), $U_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(y_\alpha)$ is the unique irreducible component of $U\cap f^{-1}(y_\alpha)$ containing $x_\lambda$ and is by hypothesis of dimension $e$, equal to the dimensions of all the irreducible components of $U_\lambda\cap f^{-1}(y_\alpha)$, by the hypothesis and \sref{IV.13.1.1}.
But by Chevalley's theorem \sref{IV.13.1.3} and \sref{IV.13.1.1}, the set $V_\lambda$ of $x'\in U_\lambda$ such that $\dim_{x'}(f_\lambda^{-1}(f_\lambda(x)))=e$ is open and contains $x$, and it suffices to take the union of the $V_\lambda$ for an open satisfying the conditions of $a'')$.

Let us now prove that $a)$ implies $b)$; one can restrict to the case where $Y=\Spec(A)$ and $X=\Spec(B)$ are affine and let us prove the following lemma first:
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[13.3.1.1]
\label{IV.13.3.1.1}
Let $Y=\Spec(A)$, $X=\Spec(B)$ two affine schemes, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type, $y$ a point of $f(X)$, and set $e=\dim(f^{-1}(y))$.
Then there exists a $Y$-morphism $g:X\to Y[T_1,\ldots,T_e]$ such that (if one sets $X_y=X\times_Y\Spec(\kappa(y))$, $g_y:X_y\to\Spec(\kappa(y)[T_1,\ldots,T_e])$ is finite.
Moreover, for every such morphism $g$, $g_y$ is necessarily surjective and the set $U$ of $x\in X$ which are isolated in their fibre $g^{-1}(g(x))$ is open in $X$ and contains $f^{-1}(y)$, and by definition the restriction $g|U$ is a quasi-finite morphism.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{i}_y$; the ring $B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is a $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$-algebra of finite type, hence by the Noether normalisation lemma (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.~comm.}, chap.~V, \textsection~3, n\textsuperscript{o}~1, th.~1) there are $r$ elements algebraically independent over $\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, $(t_i)_{1\leq i\leq r}$, of $B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ such that if one sets $C'=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, $B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ is a $C'$-algebra \emph{finite}; one thus has $\dim(B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))=\dim(C')$ ($\mathbf{0}$, 16.1.5) and as $\dim(C')=r$ (5.2.1), one has $r=e$.
As $B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=(B/\mathfrak{p}B)\otimes_{A/\mathfrak{p}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$, one can, multiplying the $t_i$ by a suitable non-zero element $s_i\in B$, suppose that each $t_i$ is the canonical image in $B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak{p})$ of an element of $B$.
Let then $u:A[T_1,\ldots,T_e]\to B$ be the homomorphism such that $u(T_i)=s_i$ for every $i$, and let $g:X\to Y[T_1,\ldots,T_e]$ be the corresponding morphism.
It is clear that by reason of the choice of the $s_i$, $g_y$ is finite.
For every such morphism $g$, it follows from (5.4.2) and (4.1.2.1) that $g_y$ is necessarily surjective.
On the other hand, the set $U$ of $x\in X$ isolated in their fibre $g^{-1}(g(x))$ is open in $X$ by \sref{IV.13.1.4}, and contains $f^{-1}(y)$ and by definition the restriction $g|U$ is a quasi-finite morphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[13.3.2]
\label{IV.13.3.2}
\oldpage[IV-3]{196}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$.
We say that $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$ (or that $X$ is equidimensional over $Y$ at the point $x$) if the equivalent conditions of \sref{IV.13.3.1} are verified.
We say that $f$ is equidimensional (or that $X$ is equidimensional over $Y$) if $f$ is equidimensional at every point $x\in X$.
\end{definition}

It is clear that, for $f$ to be equidimensional at a point $x$, it suffices that $f_\mathrm{red}$ be.
Moreover, the conditions of \sref{IV.13.3.1} show that the set of points where $f$ is equidimensional is \emph{open} in $X$.

One notes that when $X$ and $Y$ are \emph{irreducible}, saying that $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$, by \sref{IV.13.3.1}, $a''$), means that $f$ is dominant and that $\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))=\dim(f^{-1}(\eta))$ (where $\eta$ is the generic point of $Y$); the definition \sref{IV.13.3.2} thus coincides with \sref{IV.13.2.2}.

\begin{proposition}[13.3.3]
\label{IV.13.3.3}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $X_j$ the irreducible components of $X$ (in finite number) containing $x$.
For $f$ to be equidimensional at the point $x$, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every $j$, $Y_j=\overline{f(X_j)}$ be a irreducible component of $Y$ and, designating by $X_j$ and $Y_j$ the reduced closed sub-preschemes of $X$ and $Y$ having $X_j$ and $Y_j$ for underlying spaces respectively, and by $f_j:X_j\to Y_j$ the morphism deduced from $f$ (\textbf{I}, 5.2.2), by $y_j$ the generic point of $Y_j$, that $f_j$ be equidimensional at the point $x$ and that all the numbers $\dim(f_j^{-1}(y_j))$ \emph{be equal}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[13.3.4]
\label{IV.13.3.4}
With the notations of \sref{IV.13.3.3}, set $y=f(x)$.
If $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is universally catenary and if $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$, one has
\[
\label{IV.13.3.4.1}\tag{13.3.4.1}
  \dim(\sh{O}_{X_j,x})=\dim(\sh{O}_{Y_j,y})+e-\deg.\operatorname{tr}_{\kappa(y)}\kappa(x)
\]
where $e$ is the common value of the numbers $\dim(f_j^{-1}(y_j))$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[13.3.5]
\label{IV.13.3.5}
With the notations of \sref{IV.13.3.4}, suppose that $f$ be equidimensional at the point $x$ and that $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ be an universally catenary ring.
If the ring $\sh{O}_{Y,y}$ is equidimensional, so is $\sh{O}_{X,x}$; the converse is true if the image by $f$ of the union of the $X_j$ is dense in a neighbourhood of $y$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[13.3.6]
\label{IV.13.3.6}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme locally Noetherian, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$.
Suppose that the ring $\sh{O}_y$ be equidimensional and that the equality
\[
\label{IV.13.3.6.1}\tag{13.3.6.1}
  \dim(\sh{O}_x)=\dim(\sh{O}_y)+\dim(\sh{O}_x\otimes_{\sh{O}_y}\kappa(y))
\]
holds (cf.\ (13.2.7.1)), and the converse is true if $\sh{O}_y$ is a universally catenary ring.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[13.3.7]
\label{IV.13.3.7}
\oldpage[IV-3]{198}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $Z$ a closed part of $X$.
Then the function
\[
\label{IV.13.3.7.1}\tag{13.3.7.1}
  x\mapsto\operatorname{codim}_x(Z\cap f^{-1}(f(x)),f^{-1}(f(x)))
\]
is semi-continuous inferiorly in $X$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As all the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(f(x))$ containing $x$ have by hypothesis the same dimension (13.3.1), one has, by (5.2.1),
\[
  \operatorname{codim}_x(Z\cap f^{-1}(f(x)),f^{-1}(f(x)))=\dim_x(f^{-1}(f(x)))-\dim_x(Z\cap f^{-1}(f(x))).
\]
But by hypothesis the first term of the right-hand side is \emph{continuous} at $x$ \sref{IV.13.3.1} and the second is semi-continuous superiorly by Chevalley's theorem \sref{IV.13.1.3}; hence the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[13.3.8]
\label{IV.13.3.8}
Let $Y$ be a prescheme, $Y'$ a prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, $g:Y'\to Y$ a flat morphism, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$; if $f$ is equidimensional at the point $x$, then $f'$ is equidimensional at every point $x'\in X'$ above $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The question being local on $X$ and $Y$, one can restrict to the case where every irreducible component of $X$ (resp.\ $Y$) contains $x$ (resp.\ $y$); as the image by $f$ of every irreducible component of $X$ is then dense in an irreducible component of $Y$ (13.3.1, $a'$), one knows (2.3.5) that the image by $f'$ of every irreducible component of $X'$ is dense in an irreducible component of $Y'$.
On the other hand, by transitivity of the fibres (\textbf{I}, 3.6.4), it follows from (4.2.8) and \sref{IV.13.3.1} that, for every $z'\in X'$, the set of dimensions of the irreducible components of $f'^{-1}(f'(z'))$ is the same as the set of dimensions of the irreducible components of $f^{-1}(f(z))$, where $z$ is the projection of $z'$ in $X$; hence the conclusion by \sref{IV.13.3.1}, $a)$).
\end{proof}

\begin{remarks}[13.3.9]
\label{IV.13.3.9}
The property of equidimensionality of a morphism $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{not} stable by arbitrary base change $g:Y'\to Y$, even when $g$ is the canonical injection of an irreducible component $Y'$ of $Y$ into $Y$ ($Y'$ being considered as a reduced closed sub-prescheme of $Y$).
For example, let $k$ be a corps, $A_0=k[S,T]$ the ring of polynomials in two indeterminates, $\mathfrak{p}_1$ and $\mathfrak{p}_2$ the ideals $A_0 S$ and $A_0 T$ of $A_0$; let $A=A_0/\mathfrak{a}$ and $Y=\Spec(A)$, which has two irreducible components $Y_1=\Spec(A/\mathfrak{p}_1)$, $Y_2=\Spec(A/\mathfrak{p}_2)$; take $X=Y_1$, $f:X\to Y$ the canonical injection, which is evidently an equidimensional morphism.
Take on the other hand $Y'=Y_2$, $g:Y'\to Y$ the canonical injection; then $X'=X\times_Y Y'=\Spec((A/\mathfrak{p}_1)\otimes_A(A/\mathfrak{p}_2))=\Spec(A/(\mathfrak{p}_1+\mathfrak{p}_2))$, and $\mathfrak{p}_1+\mathfrak{p}_2$ is a maximal ideal of $A$, hence $X'$ is reduced to a point; the morphism $f':X'\to Y'$ is \emph{not dominant}, hence $f'$ is not equidimensional.

One can also give a counter-example where $X$ and $Y$ are \emph{integral}, $f$ \emph{finite} and \emph{birational} (and \emph{a fortiori} equidimensional by (13.3.1, $b$))), $g:Y'\to Y$ \emph{finite} and \emph{dominant}.

In this example, the ring $A$ is not geometrically unibranch; we shall see in the following paragraph (14.4.6) that such phenomena cannot occur when the points of $Y$ are \emph{geometrically unibranch}.
The lack of stability of the notion of equidimensional morphism greatly restricts its interest, in favour of the notion of universally open morphism, which will be studied in detail in the following paragraph.
\end{remarks}
