\section{Relative finiteness conditions. Constructible sets in preschemes}
\label{section:IV.1}

In this section, we will resume the expos\'e of ``finiteness conditions'' for a morphism of preschemes $f:X\to Y$ given in~(\textbf{I},~\hyperref[subsection:I.6.3]{6.3}~and~\hyperref[subsection:I.6.6]{6.6}).
There are essentially two notions of ``finiteness'' of a \emph{global} nature on $X$, that of \emph{quasi-compact} morphism (defined in~\sref[I]{I.6.6.1}) and that of a \emph{quasi-separated} morphism; on the other hand, there are two notions of ``finiteness'' of a \emph{local} nature on $X$, that of a morphism \emph{locally of finite type} (defined in~\sref[I]{I.6.6.2}) and that of a morphism \emph{locally of finite presentation}.
By combining these local notions with the preceding global notions, we obtain the notion of a morphism \emph{of finite type} (defined in~\sref[I]{I.6.3.1}) and of a morphism \emph{of finite presentation}.
For the convenience of the reader, we will give again in this section the properties stated in~(\textbf{I},~\hyperref[subsection:I.6.3]{6.3}~and~\hyperref[subsection:I.6.6]{6.6}), referring to their labels in Chapter~I for their proofs.

In~n\textsuperscript{os}\hyperref[subsection:IV.1.8]{1.8} and~\hyperref[subsection:IV.1.9]{1.9}, we complete, in the context of preschemes, and making use of the previous notions of finiteness, the results on constructible sets given in~(\textbf{0}\textsubscript{III},~\textsection\hyperref[section:0.9]{9}).

\subsection{Quasi-compact morphisms}
\label{subsection:IV.1.1}

\begin{definition}[1.1.1]
\label{IV.1.1.1}
We say that a morphism of preschemes $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{quasi-compact} if the continuous map $f$ from the topological space $X$ to the topological space $Y$ is quasi-compact~\sref[0]{0.9.1.1}, in other words, if the inverse image $f^{-1}(U)$ of every quasi-compact open subset $U$ of $Y$ is quasi-compact~(cf.~\sref[I]{I.6.6.1}).
\end{definition}

If $\mathfrak{B}$ is a basis for the topology of $Y$ consisting of affine open sets, then for $f$ to be quasi-compact, it is necessary and sufficient that for all $V\in\mathfrak{B}$, $f^{-1}(V)$ is a \emph{finite union of affine open sets}.
For example, if $Y$ is affine and $X$ is quasi-compact, \emph{every} morphism $f:X\to Y$ is quasi-compact~\sref[I]{I.6.6.1}.

If $f:X\to Y$ is a quasi-compact morphism, then it is clear that for every open subset $V$ of $Y$, the restriction of $f$ to $f^{-1}(V)$ is a quasi-compact morphism $f^{-1}(V)\to V$.
Conversely, if $(U_\alpha)$ is an open cover of $Y$ and $f:X\to Y$ is a morphism such that the restrictions $f^{-1}(U_\alpha)\to U_\alpha$ are quasi-compact, then $f$ is quasi-compact.
As
\oldpage[IV-1]{225}
a result, if $f:X\to Y$ is an $S$-morphism of $S$-preschemes, and if there exists an open cover $(S_\lambda)$ of $S$ such that the restrictions $g^{-1}(S_\lambda)\to h^{-1}(S_\lambda)$ of $f$ (where $g$ and $h$ are the structure morphisms) are quasi-compact, then $f$ is quasi-compact.

\begin{proposition}[1.1.2]
\label{IV.1.1.2}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item An immersion $X\to Y$ is quasi-compact if it is closed, or if the underlying space of $Y$ is locally noetherian, or if the underlying space of $X$ is noetherian.
  \item The composite of two quasi-compact morphisms is quasi-compact.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$ is an S-morphism quasi-compact, then the same is true of $f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ for every base extension $g:S'\to S$ of the base prescheme.
  \item If $f:X\to X'$ and $g:Y\to Y'$ are two S-morphisms quasi-compact,
\[
  f\times_S g : X\times_S Y \to X'\times_S Y'
\]
is quasi-compact.
  \item If the composite $g\circ f$ of two morphisms $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ is quasi-compact, and if $g$ is separated, or $X$ locally noetherian, $f$ is quasi-compact.
  \item For a morphism $f$ to be quasi-compact, it is necessary and sufficient that $f_{\mathrm{red}}$ be so.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.6.4}. We note that (vi) is also a consequence of the following more general proposition:

\begin{proposition}[1.1.3]
\label{IV.1.1.3}
Let $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ be two morphisms. If $g\circ f$ is quasi-compact and $f$ surjective, $g$ is quasi-compact.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, if $V$ is a quasi-compact open subset in $Z$, $f^{-1}(g^{-1}(V))$ is quasi-compact by hypothesis, and since $g^{-1}(V) = f(f^{-1}(g^{-1}(V)))$, since $f$ is surjective, $g^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact.

\begin{corollary}[1.1.4]
\label{IV.1.1.4}
Let $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y'\to Y$ be two morphisms, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$; suppose $g$ quasi-compact and surjective \emph{(}resp. dominant\emph{)}. Then, for $f$ to be quasi-compact \emph{(}resp. dominant\emph{)}, it suffices that $f'$ be so.
\end{corollary}

If $g':X'\to X$ is the canonical projection, $g'$ is a surjective morphism \sref[I]{I.3.5.2}, and so $f\circ g'=g\circ f'$; if $f'$ is quasi-compact (resp. dominant), the same is true of $g\circ f'$ since $g$ is quasi-compact \sref{IV.1.1.2} (resp. surjective); as $f\circ g'$ is quasi-compact (resp. dominant) and $g'$ is surjective, we deduce that $f$ is quasi-compact \sref{IV.1.1.3} (resp. dominant).

To abbreviate, we will call a \emph{maximal point} of a prescheme $X$ any generic point of an irreducible component of $X$; if $X=\Spec(A)$ is affine, the maximal points of $X$ are the \emph{minimal prime ideals} of $A$.

\begin{proposition}[1.1.5]
\label{IV.1.1.5}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a quasi-compact morphism. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{a)}]
  \item $f$ is dominant;
  \item for every maximal point $y$ of $Y$, $f^{-1}(y)\neq 0$;
  \item for every maximal point $y$ of $Y$, $f^{-1}(y)$ contains a maximal point of $X$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The equivalence of $a)$ and $c)$ has been proved in \sref[I]{I.6.6.5}. It is clear that $c)$ implies $b)$; on the other hand, if $X'$ is an irreducible component of $X$ meeting $f^{-1}(y)$,
\oldpage[IV-1]{226}
$f(X')$ is contained in the irreducible component $Y'=\overline{\{y\}}$ of $Y$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.1.6); if $x$ is the generic point of $X'$, we necessarily have $f(x)=y$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.1.5); thus $b)$ implies $c)$.

\begin{proposition}[1.1.6]
\label{IV.1.1.6}
Let $f':X'\to Y$, $f'':X''\to Y$ be two morphisms, $X$ the prescheme sum $X'\coprod X''$, $f:X\to Y$ the morphism which coincides with $f'$ in $X'$ and with $f''$ in $X''$. For $f$ to be quasi-compact, it is necessary and sufficient that $f'$ and $f''$ be so.
\end{proposition}

This follows immediately from the definition.

\subsection{Quasi-separated morphisms}
\label{subsection:IV.1.2}

\begin{definition}[1.2.1]
\label{IV.1.2.1}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two preschemes. We say that a morphism $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{quasi-separated} (or that $X$ is \emph{quasi-separated over} $Y$) if the diagonal morphism $\Delta_f=(1_X,1_X)_Y:X\to X\times_Y X$ is quasi-compact. We say that a prescheme $X$ is \emph{quasi-separated} if it is quasi-separated over $\Spec(\bb{Z})$.
\end{definition}

By definition, every separated morphism is quasi-separated, $\Delta_f$ being a closed immersion \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (i)); a \emph{scheme} $X$ is a prescheme quasi-separated, being separated over $\Spec(\bb{Z})$ \sref[I]{I.5.4.1}.

\begin{proposition}[1.2.2]
\label{IV.1.2.2}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item Every monomorphism of preschemes $f:X\to Y$ \emph{(}in particular every immersion\emph{)} is quasi-separated.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to Z$ are two quasi-separated morphisms, $g\circ f:X\to Z$ is quasi-separated.
  \item Let $X$, $Y$ be two $S$-preschemes, $f:X\to Y$ an $S$-morphism quasi-separated. Then, for every base change $g:S'\to S$, the morphism $f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is quasi-separated.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$, $f':X'\to Y'$ are two S-morphisms quasi-separated, then
\[
  f\times_S f' : X\times_S X' \to Y\times_S Y'
\]
is quasi-separated.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ are two morphisms such that $g\circ f$ is quasi-separated, then $f$ is quasi-separated.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$ is a quasi-separated morphism, $f_{\mathrm{red}}:X_{\mathrm{red}}\to Y_{\mathrm{red}}$ is quasi-separated.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

By virtue of \sref[I]{I.5.5.12}, it suffices to prove (i), (ii), and (iii). The assertion (i) is trivial, every monomorphism being separated \sref[I]{I.5.5.1}. To prove (iii), we immediately reduce to the case where $Y=S$ \sref[I]{I.3.3.11} and the assertion follows from the fact that $\Delta_{f_{(S')}}=(\Delta_f)_{(S')}$ \sref[I]{I.5.3.4} and from \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (iii). To prove (ii), consider the projections $p$ and $q$ of $X\times_Y X$ onto $X$; if $i=(p,q)_Z$, we know that the diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  X \ar[r]^-{\Gamma_f} \ar[d]_f & X\times_Z Y \ar[d]^{1\times_Z f} \\
  Y \ar[r]^-{\Delta_g} & Y\times_Z Y
}
\]
is commutative and identifies $X\times_Y X$ as the product of the $(Y\times_Z Y)$-preschemes $Y$ and $X\times_Z X$ \sref[I]{I.5.3.5}. If $g$ is quasi-separated, $\Delta_g$ is a quasi-compact morphism, and the same is true of $\Gamma_f$ \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (iii));
\oldpage[IV-1]{227}
the same is true of $i\circ\Delta_f$ \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (ii)) which equals $\Delta_{g\circ f}$.

\begin{corollary}[1.2.3]
\label{IV.1.2.3}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item Let $X$ be a quasi-separated prescheme. Then every morphism $f:X\to Y$ is quasi-separated.
  \item Let $Y$ be a quasi-separated prescheme. For a morphism $f:X\to Y$ to be quasi-separated, it suffices that the prescheme $X$ be quasi-separated.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

This follows immediately from \sref{IV.1.2.2}, (ii) and (v)) applied to $X$, $Y$ and $\Spec(\bb{Z})$.

\begin{proposition}[1.2.4]
\label{IV.1.2.4}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism, $g:Y\to Z$ a morphism quasi-separated. If $g\circ f$ is quasi-compact, then the same is true of $f$.
\end{proposition}

We know that $f$ is the composite morphism $X\xrightarrow{\Gamma_f} X\times_Z Y \xrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}_2} Y$ \sref[I]{I.5.3.13}; on the other hand, $\mathrm{pr}_2$ is identified with $(g\circ f)\times_Z 1_Y$ \sref[I]{I.3.3.4} and if $g\circ f$ is quasi-compact, the same is true of $\mathrm{pr}_2$ \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (iv)). Finally, we have the commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  X \ar[r]^-{\Gamma_f} \ar[d]_f & X\times_Z Y \ar[d]^{f\times 1_Y} \\
  Y \ar[r]^-{\Delta_g} & Y\times_Z Y
}
\]
\begin{equation*}
\tag{1.2.4.1}
\end{equation*}
which identifies $X$ as the product of the $(Y\times_Z Y)$-preschemes $Y$ and $X\times_Z Y$ \sref[I]{I.5.3.7}. Since by hypothesis $\Delta_g$ is a quasi-compact morphism, the same is true of $\Gamma_f$ \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (iii)); the conclusion follows from \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (ii)).

\begin{proposition}[1.2.5]
\label{IV.1.2.5}
Let $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y'\to Y$ be two morphisms, $X'=X\times_Y Y'$, $f'=f_{(Y')}:X'\to Y'$. If $g$ is quasi-compact and surjective and $f'$ quasi-separated, then $f$ is quasi-separated.
\end{proposition}

Indeed $(X'\times_{Y'} X')=(X\times_Y X)_{(Y')}$ and $\Delta_{f'}=(\Delta_f)_{(Y')}$ \sref[I]{I.5.3.4}; since the projection $X'\times_{Y'} X'\to X\times_Y X$ is quasi-compact \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (iii)) and surjective \sref[I]{I.3.5.2}, one can apply \sref{IV.1.1.4}, which shows that since $\Delta_{f'}$ is a quasi-compact morphism, the same is true of $\Delta_f$.

\begin{proposition}[1.2.6]
\label{IV.1.2.6}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism, $(U_\alpha)$ an open cover of $Y$ such that the preschemes induced $U_\alpha$ are quasi-separated. For $f$ to be quasi-separated, it suffices that each of the preschemes induced by $X$ on the open sets $f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ be quasi-separated.
\end{proposition}

The inverse image $X_\alpha\times_Y X_\alpha$ of $U_\alpha$ is $X_\alpha\times_{U_\alpha} X_\alpha$, with $X_\alpha=f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$, and the restriction $X_\alpha\times_{U_\alpha} X_\alpha$ to $X_\alpha$ of $\Delta_f$ is none other than $\Delta_{f_\alpha}$, where $f_\alpha$ denotes the restriction $X_\alpha\to U_\alpha$ of $f$; by virtue of the definition \sref{IV.1.2.1} and of the local character of the notion of quasi-compact morphism \sref{IV.1.1.1}, for $f$ to be quasi-separated, it suffices that each $f_\alpha$ be quasi-separated. But as by hypothesis the morphism $U_\alpha\to\Spec(\bb{Z})$ is quasi-separated, it amounts to the same to say that $f_\alpha$ is quasi-separated or that the composite $X_\alpha\to U_\alpha\to\Spec(\bb{Z})$ is \sref{IV.1.2.2}, (ii) and (v)), whence the proposition.

To verify that a morphism is quasi-separated, one is thus led to give criteria for a prescheme to be quasi-separated:

\begin{proposition}[1.2.7]
\label{IV.1.2.7}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $(U_\alpha)$ an open cover of $X$ consisting of quasi-compact open sets. The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{a)}]
  \item $X$ is quasi-separated.
  \item For every quasi-compact open subset $U$ of $X$, the canonical immersion $U\to X$ is quasi-compact (in other words, $U$ is \emph{retrocompact} in $X$).
  \item[\emph{b')}] The intersection of two quasi-compact open subsets of $X$ is quasi-compact.
  \item For every pair of indices $(\alpha,\beta)$, the intersection $U_\alpha\cap U_\beta$ is quasi-compact.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The properties $b)$ and $b')$ are trivially equivalent by definition of a quasi-compact morphism. Since a quasi-compact open is a finite union of affine open sets, for two quasi-compact open sets $U$, $V$ of $X$, $U\times_{\bb{Z}} V$ is a quasi-compact open subset of $X\times_Z X$ \sref[I]{I.3.2.7}, whose inverse image by $\Delta_X$ is $U\cap V$, so $a)$ implies $b')$. It is trivial that $b')$ implies $c)$; finally, if $c)$ is satisfied, the $U_\alpha\times_{\bb{Z}} U_\beta$ form a cover of $X\times_{\bb{Z}} X$ by quasi-compact open sets, and we know that for the morphism $\Delta_X:X\to X\times_{\bb{Z}} X$ to be quasi-compact, it suffices that the inverse images of $U_\alpha\times_{\bb{Z}} U_\beta$ by $\Delta_X$ be quasi-compact \sref{IV.1.1.1}; hence $c)$ implies $a)$.
\oldpage[IV-1]{228}

\begin{corollary}[1.2.8]
\label{IV.1.2.8}
Every prescheme $X$ whose underlying space is locally noetherian \emph{(}for example a locally noetherian prescheme\emph{)} is quasi-separated; every morphism $f:X\to Y$ is then quasi-separated.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[1.2.9]
\label{IV.1.2.9}
Let $X'$, $X''$ be two preschemes, $X=X'\coprod X''$ their sum, $f':X'\to Y$, $f'':X''\to Y$ two morphisms, $f:X\to Y$ the morphism which coincides with $f'$ in $X'$ and with $f''$ in $X''$. For $f$ to be quasi-separated, it is necessary and sufficient that $f'$ and $f''$ be so.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, $X\times_Y X$ is the sum of the four preschemes $X'\times_Y X'$, $X'\times_Y X''$, $X''\times_Y X'$ and $X''\times_Y X''$, and $\Delta_f$ is the morphism which coincides with $\Delta_{f'}$ in $X'$ and with $\Delta_{f''}$ in $X''$; the proposition therefore follows immediately from the definitions.

\subsection{Morphisms locally of finite type}
\label{subsection:IV.1.3}

\begin{env}[1.3.1]
\label{IV.1.3.1}
Recall that if $A$ is a ring, a $A$-algebra (commutative) $B$ is said to be \emph{of finite type} if it is generated by a finite number of elements of $B$, or, what amounts to the same, if it is isomorphic to a quotient of a polynomial algebra $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ by an ideal of this algebra. It is clear that for every $A$-algebra (commutative) $A'$, $B\otimes_A A'$ is then an $A'$-algebra of finite type. If $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type and $C$ a $B$-algebra of finite type, $C$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type, for $C$ is a quotient of $B[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and $B$ a quotient of $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m]$, $B[T_1,\ldots,T_n]=B\otimes_A A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ is isomorphic to $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m,T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and is a quotient of $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m,T_1,\ldots,T_n]$, so the same is true of $C$.
\end{env}

\begin{definition}[1.3.2]
\label{IV.1.3.2}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$. We say that $f$ is \emph{of finite type in} $x$ if there exists an affine open neighbourhood $V$ of $y$ and an affine open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $f(U)\subset V$ and such that the ring $A(U)$ is an $A(V)$-algebra of finite type. We say that $f$ is \emph{locally of finite type} if it is of finite type at every point of $X$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[1.3.3]
\label{IV.1.3.3}
If $Y$ is a locally noetherian prescheme and $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type, then $X$ is locally noetherian.
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.7}.

\begin{proposition}[1.3.4]
\label{IV.1.3.4}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item Every local immersion is locally of finite type.
  \item If two morphisms $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ are locally of finite type, the same is true of $g\circ f$.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$ is an $S$-morphism locally of finite type, $f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is locally of finite type for every base extension $S'\to S$ of the base prescheme.
  \item If $f:X\to X'$ and $g:Y\to Y'$ are two $S$-morphisms locally of finite type, $f\times_S g$ is locally of finite type.
  \item If the composite $g\circ f$ of two morphisms is locally of finite type, $f$ is locally of finite type.
  \item If a morphism $f$ is locally of finite type, the same is true of $f_{\mathrm{red}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.6}.
\oldpage[IV-1]{229}

\begin{corollary}[1.3.5]
\label{IV.1.3.5}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite type. For every morphism $Y'\to Y$ such that $Y'$ is locally noetherian, $X\times_Y Y'$ is locally noetherian.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, $f_{(Y')}:X\times_Y Y'\to Y'$ is locally of finite type by \sref{IV.1.3.4}, (iii)), and it suffices to apply \sref{IV.1.3.3}.

\begin{proposition}[1.3.6]
\label{IV.1.3.6}
Let $\varphi:A\to B$ be a homomorphism of rings. For the corresponding morphism $f:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)$ to be locally of finite type, it is necessary and sufficient that $B$ be an $A$-algebra of finite type.
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.3}, taking into account that $f$ is quasi-compact and \sref[I]{I.6.6.3}.

\begin{proposition}[1.3.7]
\label{IV.1.3.7}
For a morphism locally of finite type $f:X\to Y$ to be surjective, it is necessary and sufficient that, for every algebraically closed field $\Omega$, the map $X(\Omega)\to Y(\Omega)$ corresponding to $f$ \sref[I]{I.3.4.1} be surjective.
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.10}.

\begin{env}[1.3.8]
\label{IV.1.3.8}
Let $A$ be a ring. We say that an $A$-algebra $B$ is \emph{essentially of finite type} if $B$ is $A$-isomorphic to an $A$-algebra of the form $S^{-1}C$, where $C$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type and $S$ a multiplicative subset of $C$.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[1.3.9]
\label{IV.1.3.9}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item If $B$ is an $A$-algebra essentially of finite type and $C$ a $B$-algebra essentially of finite type, then $C$ is an $A$-algebra essentially of finite type.
  \item Let $B$ be an $A$-algebra essentially of finite type, $A'$ an $A$-algebra; then $B'=B\otimes_A A'$ is an $A'$-algebra essentially of finite type.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

(i) Let $B=S'^{-1}B'$ and $C=T'^{-1}C'$, where $B'$ (resp.\ $C'$) is an $A$-algebra (resp.\ a $B$-algebra) of finite type and $S'$ (resp.\ $T'$) a multiplicative subset of $B'$ (resp.\ $C'$); then $C'$ is also the form $S'^{-1}C''$, where $C''$ is a $B'$-algebra of finite type, so $C$ is also a ring of fractions of $C''$; as $C''$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type, this proves our assertion.

(ii) If $B$ is a ring of fractions of an $A$-algebra of finite type $C$, $B'$ is a ring of fractions of the $A'$-algebra of finite type $C\otimes_A A'$, whence (ii).

\begin{env}[1.3.10]
\label{IV.1.3.10}
If $B$ is an $A$-algebra \emph{locally} essentially of finite type, $B$ is of the form $C_{\mathfrak{q}}$, where $C$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type and $\mathfrak{q}$ a prime ideal of $C$ (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~II, \S5, prop.~11). Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be the inverse image in $A$ of the ideal $\mathfrak{q}$; if we set $S=A-\mathfrak{p}$, $C_{\mathfrak{q}}$ is also a local ring at a prime ideal of $S^{-1}C$; as $S^{-1}C$ is an algebra of finite type over $A_{\mathfrak{p}}=S^{-1}A$, we see that $B$ is also an $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-algebra essentially of finite type, and furthermore the homomorphism $A_{\mathfrak{p}}\to B$ is \emph{local}.
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[1.3.11]
\label{IV.1.3.11}
If $B$ is an $A$-algebra locally essentially of finite type, $B$ is $A$-isomorphic to an $A$-algebra quotient of a localisation $C_{\mathfrak{q}}$, where $C$ is a polynomial algebra $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and $\mathfrak{q}$ a prime ideal of $C$.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, by definition, $B$ is isomorphic to $C'_{\mathfrak{q}'}$, where $C'$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type; but $C'=C/\mathfrak{b}$, where $C=A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and $\mathfrak{b}$ is an ideal of $C$; so $\mathfrak{q}'=\mathfrak{q}/\mathfrak{b}$, where $\mathfrak{q}$ is a prime ideal of $C$, and $C'_{\mathfrak{q}'}$ is isomorphic to $C_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathfrak{b} C_{\mathfrak{q}}$.

\subsection{Morphisms locally of finite presentation}
\label{subsection:IV.1.4}

\begin{env}[1.4.1]
\label{IV.1.4.1}
Given a ring $A$, we say that an $A$-algebra $B$ (commutative) is \emph{of finite presentation} if it is isomorphic to $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{a}$ for a \emph{finitely generated} ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ of $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$. For every $A$-algebra (commutative) $A'$, $B\otimes_A A'$ is then an $A'$-algebra of finite presentation, being isomorphic to the quotient of $A'[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ by the image of $\mathfrak{a}$ via the canonical map $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]\to A'[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$, which is then an ideal of finite type. If $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation and $C$ a $B$-algebra of finite presentation, then $C$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation. Indeed, let $B=A[S_1,\ldots,S_m]/\mathfrak{a}$ and $C=B[T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{b}$, where $\mathfrak{a}$ (resp.\ $\mathfrak{b}$) is a finitely generated ideal of $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m]$ (resp.\ $B[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$); the ring $B[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ is isomorphic to $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m,T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{a}'$, where $\mathfrak{a}'$ is the image of $\mathfrak{a}$ via the canonical map of $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m]$ to $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m,T_1,\ldots,T_n]$, and thus an ideal of finite type; the ideal $\mathfrak{b}$ of $B[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ is of the form $\mathfrak{b}'/\mathfrak{a}'$, where $\mathfrak{b}'$ is an ideal of $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m,T_1,\ldots,T_n]$; as $\mathfrak{a}'$ and $\mathfrak{b}'/\mathfrak{a}'$ are finitely generated modules, the same is true of $\mathfrak{b}'$, and $C$, isomorphic to $A[S_1,\ldots,S_m,T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{b}'$, is indeed an $A$-algebra of finite presentation. Note finally that if $A$ is \emph{noetherian}, the same is true of $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$, so every $A$-algebra of finite type is then of finite presentation.
\end{env}

\begin{definition}[1.4.2]
\label{IV.1.4.2}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of preschemes, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$. We say that $f$ is \emph{of finite presentation in} $x$ if there exists an affine open neighbourhood $V$ of $y$ and an affine open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $f(U)\subset V$ and such that the ring $A(U)$ is an $A(V)$-algebra of finite presentation. We say that $f$ is \emph{locally of finite presentation} if it is of finite presentation at every point of $X$.
\end{definition}

If $Y$ is locally noetherian, it amounts to the same to say that $f$ is locally of finite type or locally of finite presentation.

\begin{proposition}[1.4.3]
\label{IV.1.4.3}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item Every local isomorphism is locally of finite presentation.
  \item If two morphisms $f:X\to Y$, $g:Y\to Z$ are locally of finite presentation, the same is true of $g\circ f$.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$ is an $S$-morphism locally of finite presentation, $f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is locally of finite presentation for every base extension $S'\to S$ of the base prescheme.
  \item If $f:X\to X'$ and $g:Y\to Y'$ are two $S$-morphisms locally of finite presentation, $f\times_S g$ is locally of finite presentation.
  \item If the composite $g\circ f$ of two morphisms is locally of finite presentation and if $g$ is locally of finite type, then $f$ is locally of finite presentation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\oldpage[IV-1]{231}
The assertion (i) is trivial. To prove (iii), one can restrict to the case where $S=Y$ \sref[I]{I.3.3.11}; let $x'$ be a point of $X_{(S')}$, $s'$ and $x$ its projections onto $S'$ and $X$ respectively, $s$ the common projection of $s'$ and $x$ onto $S$. By hypothesis, there exists an affine open neighbourhood $V$ (resp.\ $V'$) of $s$ (resp.\ of $x$) such that the image of $U$ be contained in $V$ and such that $A(U)$ be an $A(V)$-algebra of finite presentation; let $V'$ be an affine open neighbourhood of $s'$ whose image in $S$ is contained in $V$, and $U'=\Spec(A\otimes_{A(V)} A(V'))$ an affine open neighbourhood of $x'$ whose image in $S'$ is contained in $V'$ and whose ring $A(U)\otimes_{A(V)} A(V')$ is an $A(V')$-algebra of finite presentation.

One knows that (iv) follows from (i), (ii), and (iii) \sref[I]{I.3.5.1}. To prove (v), let us consider a point $x\in X$, and let $y=f(x)$, $z=g(f(x))$. There exists an affine open neighbourhood $W=\Spec(C)$ (resp.\ $V=\Spec(B))$ of $z$ (resp.\ of $y$) such that $g(V)\subset W$ and such that $B$ is a $C$-algebra of finite type. On the other hand, there exists an affine open neighbourhood $V'=\Spec(B')$ (resp.\ $U=\Spec(A)$) of $y$ (resp.\ of $x$) such that $f(U)\subset V'$ and such that $A$ is a $B'$-algebra of finite presentation. Let $s\in B$ be such that the open set $\mathrm{D}(B_s)$ in $V$ is a neighbourhood of $y$ contained in $V'$, and let $U'=f^{-1}(\mathrm{D}(s))\subset U$; we have $U'=\Spec(A\otimes_{B'} B_s)$, and $A\otimes_{B'} B_s$ is a $B_s$-algebra of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.4.1}; on the other hand $B_s=B[T]/(1-sT)$ is a $B$-algebra of finite presentation by definition; so by \sref{IV.1.4.1}, $A\otimes_{B'} B_s$ is a $C$-algebra of finite presentation. One sees therefore that on the other hand $f$ is also locally of finite presentation.

On the other hand, consider the commutative diagram
\[
\xymatrix{
  X \ar[r]^-{\Gamma_f} \ar[d]_f & X\times_Z Y \ar[d]^{f\times 1} \\
  Y \ar[r]^-{\Delta_g} & Y\times_Z Y
}
\]
which identifies $X$ as the product of the $(Y\times_Z Y)$-preschemes $Y$ and $X\times_Z Y$ \sref[I]{I.5.3.7}. If $\Delta_g$ is locally of finite presentation, the same follows for $\Gamma_f$ by (iii); on the other hand, we have the factorisation of $f$ as $X\xrightarrow{\Gamma_f} X\times_Z Y\xrightarrow{p_2} Y$ \sref[I]{I.5.3.13}, where the projection $p_2$ equals $(g\circ f)\times_Z 1_Y$; as $g\circ f$ is assumed locally of finite presentation, the same is true of $p_2$ by (iv), and it will follow from (ii) that $f$ is also locally of finite presentation. One sees therefore that we are led to proving the

\begin{corollary}[1.4.3.1]
\label{IV.1.4.3.1}
Let $g:Y\to Z$ be a morphism locally of finite type; then the diagonal morphism $\Delta_g:Y\to Y\times_Z Y$ is locally of finite presentation.
\end{corollary}

\oldpage[IV-1]{232}
One can restrict to the case where $Z=\Spec(A)$, $Y=\Spec(B)$, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite type; the morphism $\Delta_g$ corresponds then to the \emph{augmentation homomorphism} $\delta:B\otimes_A B\to B$ such that $\delta(x\otimes y)=xy$. In view of the definition \sref{IV.1.4.2}, it suffices to show that the kernel $\mathfrak{J}$ of $\delta$ is an ideal of finite type, which follows from $(\mathbf{0_{IV}}$, 20.4.4).

\begin{proposition}[1.4.4]
\label{IV.1.4.4}
Let $A$ be a ring, $B$ an $A$-algebra, $B'$ a polynomial algebra $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$, $u:B'\to B$ a surjective homomorphism of $A$-algebras. For $B$ to be an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, it is necessary and sufficient that the kernel $\mathfrak{J}$ of $u$ be a finitely generated ideal of $B'$.
\end{proposition}

The condition is sufficient by definition \sref{IV.1.4.1}. To see that it is necessary, note that the morphism $g:\Spec(B')\to\Spec(A)$ is locally of finite type; if $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, the morphism $f:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(B')$ corresponding to $u$ is locally of finite presentation, so $g\circ f:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)$ is locally of finite presentation. But, to prove that the ideal $\mathfrak{J}$ is of finite type, it suffices to prove that $\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}$ is a $\widetilde{B'}$-Module of finite type \sref[II]{II.6.1.4.1}. Returning to the definition \sref{IV.1.4.2}, we are finally led to proving the

\begin{lemma}[1.4.4.1]
\label{IV.1.4.4.1}
Let $\mathfrak{a}$ be an ideal of a ring $C$, $s$ an element of $C$, such that $C_s/\mathfrak{a}_s$ is a $C$-algebra of finite presentation; then $\mathfrak{a}_s$ is a finitely generated ideal of $C_s$.
\end{lemma}

By hypothesis, there exists a $C$-algebra of polynomials $C'=C[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and a $C$-homomorphism surjective $v:C'\to C_s/\mathfrak{a}_s$ whose kernel $\mathfrak{b}$ is of finite type. Let $\rho:C_s\to C_s/\mathfrak{a}_s$ be the canonical homomorphism; for each $i$, there exists $t_i\in C$ such that $\rho(t_i/s^k)=v(T_i)$ (one may suppose that the exponent $k$ is the same for all indices $i$). Consider then the $C_s$-algebra $D=C_s[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$, and the homomorphism of $C_s$-algebras $w:D\to C_s$ such that $w(T_i)=t_i/s^k$; $w$ is evidently surjective, and by the same construction $v'=\rho\circ w:D\to C_s/\mathfrak{a}_s$. On the other hand, every polynomial of $D$ can be written $P/s^m$, where $P\in C'=C[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$, and we have $v'(P/s^m)=(1/s^m)v(P)$; as $1/s$ is invertible in $C_s$, the relation $v'(P/s^m)=0$ in $C_s/\mathfrak{a}_s$ is equivalent to $v(P)=0$, and by the same the kernel $\mathfrak{b}'$ of $v'$ is generated by $\mathfrak{b}$ in the ring $D$. But as $\mathfrak{b}'$ is the image of $\mathfrak{b}$ by the canonical map of $\mathfrak{b}$ to $D$, and the kernel of $w$ is the ideal generated by the $F_i(T_i)$ in $D$; this proves that $\mathfrak{a}_s$ is a finitely generated ideal of $C_s$.

\begin{corollary}[1.4.5]
\label{IV.1.4.5}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two preschemes, $j:X\to Y$ an immersion, $U$ an open subset of $Y$ such that $j(X)$ is closed in $U$, $\sh{J}$ the quasi-coherent Ideal of $\sh{O}_U$ defining the closed sub-prescheme of $Y$ associated to $j$.
For $j$ to be locally of finite presentation, it is necessary and sufficient that $\sh{J}$ be an $\sh{O}_U$-Module of finite type.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[1.4.6]
\label{IV.1.4.6}
\oldpage[IV-1]{233}
Let $\varphi:A\to B$ be a homomorphism of rings.
For the corresponding morphism $f:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)$ to be locally of finite presentation, it is necessary and sufficient that $B$ be an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.1.4.6.proof}
The condition being trivially sufficient, it remains to prove that it is necessary.
If $f$ is locally of finite presentation, it already follows from \sref{IV.1.3.6} that $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type, so there exists a surjective homomorphism $u:B'=A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]\to B$ of $A$-algebras;
it follows from (\sref{IV.1.4.3}, (v)) that the immersion $j:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(B')$ is locally of finite presentation;
hence \sref{IV.1.4.5}, if $\mathfrak{J}$ is the kernel of $u$, the $B'$-Module $\widetilde{\mathfrak{J}}$ is of finite type, and consequently \sref[II]{II.6.1.4.1} $\mathfrak{J}$ is an ideal of finite type.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[1.4.7]
\label{IV.1.4.7}
Let $\rho:A\to B$ be a homomorphism of rings making $B$ a finite $A$-algebra.
For $B$ to be an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, it is necessary and sufficient that $B$ be an $A$-module of finite presentation.
\end{proposition}

We will use the following

\begin{lemma}[1.4.7.1]
\label{IV.1.4.7.1}
If $B$ is a finite $A$-algebra, there exists a surjective $A$-homomorphism of algebras $u:B'\to B$, where $B'$ is a finite $A$-algebra of finite presentation that is a free $A$-module.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\label{IV.1.4.7.1.proof}
Indeed, there is a finite system $(a_i)_{1\leq i\leq m}$ of generators of the $A$-algebra $B$ such that each $a_i$ satisfies a relation $F_i(a_i)=0$, where $F_i\in A[X]$ is a monic polynomial of degree $>0$;
the quotient $A$-algebra $B'_i=A[X]/(F_i)$ is therefore free of finite rank over $A$;
it suffices then to take for $B'$ the tensor product $B'_1\otimes_A B'_2\otimes\cdots\otimes_A B'_m$ and to consider the homomorphism $u_1\otimes u_2\otimes\cdots\otimes u_m:B'\to B$, which is surjective by construction.
Moreover, if $B''=A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and if $b'_i$ denotes the canonical image of $c_i$ in $B'$, the $A$-homomorphism of algebras $v:B''\to B'$ such that $v(T_i)=b'_i$ $(1\leq i\leq m)$ is surjective, and its kernel $\mathfrak{b}'$ is the ideal of $B''$ generated by the $F_i(T_i)$, hence of finite type;
this proves that $B'$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of {\sref{IV.1.4.7}}]
\label{IV.1.4.7.proof}
This lemma being proved, keep the same notation, and let $w=v\circ u:B''\to B$. If $\mathfrak{b}$ (resp.\ $\mathfrak{a}$) is the kernel of $w$ (resp.\ $u$), we have $\mathfrak{a}=v(\mathfrak{b})$ since $v$ is surjective. But \sref{IV.1.4.4}, if $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, $\mathfrak{b}$ is an ideal of finite type of $B''$, so $\mathfrak{a}$ is a finitely generated $A$-module of finite type since $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite type; as $B'$ is an $A$-module of finite presentation, $\mathfrak{a}$ is \emph{a fortiori} a finitely generated ideal of $B'$; by the same, $B$ is a $B'$-algebra of finite presentation, and as $B'$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Morphisms of finite type}
\label{subsection:IV.1.5}

\begin{proposition}[1.5.1]
\label{IV.1.5.1}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism, $(U_\alpha)$ an open cover of $Y$ consisting of affine open sets. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{a)}]
  \item $f$ is locally of finite type and quasi-compact.
  \item For each $\alpha$, $f^{-1}(U_\alpha)$ is a finite union of affine open sets $V_{\alpha i}$ such that each ring $A(V_{\alpha i})$ is an $A(U_\alpha)$-algebra of finite type.
  \item For every affine open subset $U$ of $Y$, $f^{-1}(U)$ is a finite union of affine open sets $V_j$ such that the rings $A(V_j)$ are $A(U)$-algebras of finite type.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.2} and \sref[I]{I.6.6.3}.

\begin{definition}[1.5.2]
\label{IV.1.5.2}
We say that a morphism $f$ is \emph{of finite type} if it satisfies the equivalent conditions of the proposition~\sref{IV.1.5.1}.
\end{definition}

\oldpage[IV-1]{234}

\begin{proposition}[1.5.3]
\label{IV.1.5.3}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of finite type; if $Y$ is noetherian, the same is true of $X$.
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.7}.

\begin{proposition}[1.5.4]
\label{IV.1.5.4}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item Let $j:X\to Y$ be a morphism of immersion. If $j$ is quasi-compact \emph{(}which is the case if $j$ is a closed immersion, or if the underlying space of $X$ is noetherian, or if the underlying space of $Y$ is locally noetherian\emph{)}, $j$ is of finite type.
  \item The composite of two morphisms of finite type is of finite type.
  \item If $f:X\to Y$ is an $S$-morphism of finite type, $f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is of finite type for every base extension $S'\to S$ of the base prescheme.
  \item If $f:X\to X'$ and $g:Y\to Y'$ are two $S$-morphisms of finite type, $f\times_S g$ is of finite type.
  \item If the composite $g\circ f$ of two morphisms is of finite type and if $g$ is quasi-separated or if $X$ is noetherian, $f$ is of finite type.
  \item If a morphism $f$ is of finite type, the same is true of $f_{\mathrm{red}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

This follows immediately (except the case (v) where $X$ is noetherian, proved in \sref[I]{I.6.3.6}) from the definitions and from \sref{IV.1.1.2}, \sref{IV.1.2.4} and \sref{IV.1.3.4}.

\begin{corollary}[1.5.5]
\label{IV.1.5.5}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of finite type. For every morphism $Y'\to Y$ such that $Y'$ is noetherian, $X\times_Y Y'$ is noetherian.
\end{corollary}

This follows from \sref{IV.1.5.4}, (iii)) and from \sref{IV.1.5.3}.

\begin{corollary}[1.5.6]
\label{IV.1.5.6}
Let $X$ be a prescheme of finite type over a locally noetherian prescheme $S$. Then every $S$-morphism $f:X\to Y$ is of finite type.
\end{corollary}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.9}.

\begin{proposition}[1.5.7]
\label{IV.1.5.7}
Let $\varphi:A\to B$ be a homomorphism of rings. For the corresponding morphism $f:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)$ to be of finite type, it is necessary and sufficient that $\varphi$ make $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite type.
\end{proposition}

For the proof, see \sref[I]{I.6.3.3}.

\subsection{Morphisms of finite presentation}
\label{subsection:IV.1.6}

\begin{definition}[1.6.1]
\label{IV.1.6.1}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two preschemes. We say that a morphism $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{of finite presentation} if it satisfies the following three conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item $f$ is locally of finite presentation;
  \item $f$ is quasi-compact (which, together with (i), is equivalent to saying that $f$ is of finite type \sref{IV.1.5.2});
  \item $f$ is quasi-separated.
\end{enumerate}
We then also say that $X$ is \emph{of finite presentation over} $Y$, or a $Y$-\emph{prescheme of finite presentation}.
\end{definition}

The condition (iii) is automatically satisfied if $f$ is separated, or if $X$ is locally noetherian \sref{IV.1.2.8}; when $Y$ is locally noetherian, it amounts to the same to say that $f$ is \emph{of finite presentation} or that $f$ is \emph{of finite type} (the latter condition implying that $X$ is locally noetherian \sref{IV.1.3.3}).
\oldpage[IV-1]{235}

\begin{proposition}[1.6.2]
\label{IV.1.6.2}
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item Every quasi-compact immersion \emph{(}in particular every quasi-compact open immersion\emph{)} is of finite presentation.
  \item The composite of two morphisms of finite presentation is of finite presentation.
  \item Let $X$, $Y$ be two $S$-preschemes, $f:X\to Y$ an $S$-morphism of finite presentation. Then, for every base change $S'\to S$, the morphism $f_{(S')}:X_{(S')}\to Y_{(S')}$ is of finite presentation.
  \item Let $f:X\to Y$, $f':X'\to Y'$ be two $S$-morphisms of finite presentation; then $f\times_S f':X\times_S X'\to Y\times_S Y'$ is of finite presentation.
  \item If the composite $g\circ f$ of two morphisms is of finite presentation and if $g$ is quasi-separated and locally of finite type, then $f$ is of finite presentation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

This follows immediately from \sref{IV.1.1.2}, \sref{IV.1.2.2}, \sref{IV.1.2.4} and \sref{IV.1.4.3}.

It follows in particular from \sref{IV.1.6.2}, (iii)) that if $f$ is a morphism of finite presentation and $U$ an open subset of $Y$, the restriction $f^{-1}(U)\to U$ of $f$ is a morphism of finite presentation. Conversely, let $(U_\alpha)$ be a cover of $Y$ by affine open sets, and suppose that the restrictions $f^{-1}(U_\alpha)\to U_\alpha$ of $f$ are morphisms of finite presentation; then $f$ is evidently locally of finite presentation, quasi-compact by virtue of \sref{IV.1.1.1} and quasi-separated by virtue of \sref{IV.1.2.6}. One can therefore say that the property for a morphism $f:X\to Y$ of being of finite presentation is \emph{local on} $Y$.

If $X$ is a quasi-separated prescheme, every morphism $f:X\to Y$ is quasi-separated \sref{IV.1.2.3}. So, if $f$ is quasi-compact and locally of finite presentation, $f$ is of finite presentation.

In particular:

\begin{corollary}[1.6.3]
\label{IV.1.6.3}
Let $\varphi:A\to B$ be a homomorphism of rings. For the corresponding morphism $f:\Spec(B)\to\Spec(A)$ to be of finite presentation, it is necessary and sufficient that $\varphi$ make $B$ an $A$-algebra of finite presentation.
\end{corollary}

The necessity of the condition follows indeed from \sref{IV.1.4.6}.

\begin{remark}[1.6.4]
\label{IV.1.6.4}
In the definition \sref{IV.1.6.1}, the condition (iii) is not a consequence of the other two. Take for example $Y$ an affine scheme whose underlying space is not noetherian, and let $U$ be an open non quasi-compact subset in $Y$. Let $X$ be the prescheme obtained by gluing two preschemes $Y_1$, $Y_2$ isomorphic to $Y$ along the open sets $U_1$, $U_2$ corresponding to $U$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 4.1.7), so that $X$ is the union of two open subsets isomorphic to $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ (which we identify with the latter), with $Y_1\cap Y_2=U$. Furthermore, there is a morphism $f:X\to Y$ which coincides in $Y_i$ with the isomorphism $Y_i\to Y$ given. It is clear that $f$ satisfies condition (i) of \sref{IV.1.6.1}, being a local isomorphism; it also satisfies (ii), as the inverse image of an open quasi-compact set is quasi-compact; but as $Y_1\cap Y_2$ is not quasi-separated \sref{IV.1.2.7}, $f$ is not quasi-separated.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[1.6.5]
\label{IV.1.6.5}
Let $X'$, $X''$ be two preschemes, $X=X'\coprod X''$ their sum, $f':X'\to Y$, $f'':X''\to Y$ two morphisms, $f:X\to Y$ the morphism which coincides with $f'$ in $X'$ and with $f''$ in $X''$. For $f$ to be of finite presentation, it is necessary and sufficient that $f'$ and $f''$ be so.
\end{proposition}
\oldpage[IV-1]{236}

It suffices to show that for $f$ to possess one of the three properties of the definition \sref{IV.1.6.1}, it is necessary and sufficient that $f'$ and $f''$ possess it; this is evident for the property of being locally of finite presentation, which is local on $X$; for the property of being quasi-compact, this was seen in \sref{IV.1.1.6} and for the property of being quasi-separated, in \sref{IV.1.2.9}.

\subsection{Morphisms of finite presentation and constructible sets}
\label{subsection:IV.1.8}
\oldpage[IV-1]{238}

\begin{env}[1.8.1]
\label{IV.1.8.1}
Let $X$ be a prescheme; one knows that every quasi-compact open subset in $X$ is a finite union of affine open sets, and conversely. For an open subset $U$ of $X$ to be \emph{retrocompact} in $X$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.1), it is necessary and sufficient that for every \emph{affine} open subset $V$ of $X$, $U\cap V$ be quasi-compact. When $X$ is \emph{quasi-separated} \sref{IV.1.2.1}, every quasi-compact open subset in $X$ is retrocompact in $X$ \sref{IV.1.2.7}, so every locally constructible subset of $X$ is retrocompact in $X$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.13); if \emph{in addition} $X$ is \emph{quasi-compact}, there is identity between constructible subsets and locally constructible subsets in $X$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.12), and between constructible open subsets and quasi-compact open subsets $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.5).
\end{env}

\begin{proposition}[1.8.2]
\label{IV.1.8.2}
Let $X$, $Y$ be two preschemes, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism. For every constructible \emph{(}resp.\ locally constructible\emph{)} subset $Z$ of $Y$, $f^{-1}(Z)$ is a constructible \emph{(}resp.\ locally constructible\emph{)} subset of $X$.
\end{proposition}

Suppose $Z$ locally constructible; for every $x\in X$, there will be an affine open neighbourhood $V$ of $f(x)$ such that $Z\cap V$ be constructible in $V$; as the proposition is proved for constructible subsets, $f^{-1}(Z)\cap f^{-1}(V)$ will be constructible in $f^{-1}(V)$, so $f^{-1}(Z)$ will be locally constructible. It suffices therefore to consider the case where $Z$ is constructible, and taking into account $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.3), we are reduced to the case where $Z$ is open and retrocompact in $Y$, in other words such that the canonical injection $Z\to Y$ is a quasi-compact morphism; it suffices then to see that $f^{-1}(Z)$ is \emph{retrocompact in} $X$, in other words that the injection $f^{-1}(Z)\to X$ is a quasi-compact morphism; but this follows from \sref{IV.1.1.2}, (iii)), since $f^{-1}(Z)=Z\times_Y X$ \sref[I]{I.4.4.1}.

\begin{lemma}[1.8.3]
\label{IV.1.8.3}
Let $X$ be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated prescheme, $Z$ a constructible subset of $X$. There exist then an affine scheme $X'$ and a morphism of finite presentation $f:X'\to X$ such that $f(X')=Z$.
\end{lemma}

Since $X$ is quasi-compact, it is a finite union of affine open sets $X_i$ ($i\in I$), and since $X$ is quasi-separated, it follows from \sref{IV.1.2.7} that each of the canonical immersions $g_i:X_i\to X$ is a morphism of finite presentation; one concludes that if $f_i:X'_i\to X_i$ is a morphism of finite presentation, the same is true of $g_i\circ f_i:X'_i\to X$ \sref{IV.1.6.2}, and if $X'$ is the prescheme sum of the $X'_i$, the morphism $h:X'\to X$ which coincides with $g_i\circ f_i$ in each $X'_i$ is also of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.6.5}. Since $Z\cap X_i$ is constructible in $X_i$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.8, (i)), one sees that one is led to proving the lemma when $X_i$ is \emph{affine}, that is a ring $A$. As $Z$ is then a finite union of subsets of the form $U\cap \complement V$, where $U$ and $V$ are quasi-compact open subsets $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.3), one sees, by considering a suitable sum of preschemes, that one can even reduce to the case where $Z=U\cap\complement V$; as $U$ is a finite union of open sets of the form $X_{f_i}$, where $f_i\in A$; let $\mathfrak{J}$ be the ideal of $A$ generated by the $f_i$, and let $Z'$ be the closed affine sub-scheme of $X$ which it defines (and which is by construction of finite presentation on $X$); we have by definition $V=\complement Z'$, so $U\cap\complement V = U\cap Z'$. Consider the affine scheme $X'=Z'\times_X U$
\oldpage[IV-1]{239}
and let $f:X'\to X$ be the structural morphism; one sees that the canonical immersion $U\to X$, which is of finite presentation, and the same is true of the open immersion $U\to X$, which is quasi-compact since $U$ and $X$ are affine; we conclude therefore from \sref{IV.1.6.2}, (iv)) that $f$ is of finite presentation, and we have $f(X')=Z'\cap U$ \sref[I]{I.3.4.8}, which finishes the proof.

\begin{theorem}[1.8.4]
\label{IV.1.8.4}
(Chevalley). --- Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism of finite presentation. For every locally constructible subset $Z$ of $X$, $f(Z)$ is locally constructible in $Y$.
\end{theorem}

Let $y\in Y$ and $V$ an affine open neighbourhood of $y$; as $f$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, the same is true of its restriction $f^{-1}(V)\to V$, so $f^{-1}(V)$ is a prescheme quasi-compact and quasi-separated \sref{IV.1.2.3}; since $Z\cap f^{-1}(V)$ \sref{IV.1.8.1} is constructible in $f^{-1}(V)$, one sees that it suffices to consider the case where $Y$ is affine and $Z$ constructible; but then it will suffice to prove the

\begin{lemma}[1.8.4.1]
\label{IV.1.8.4.1}
Let $Y$ be an affine scheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism quasi-compact and locally of finite presentation; then $f(X)$ is a constructible subset of $Y$.
\end{lemma}

Since $X$ is quasi-compact, it is a finite union of affine open sets; one can therefore reduce to the case where $X=\Spec(A)$, $Y=\Spec(B)$, $B$ being an $A$-algebra of finite presentation. As $Y$ is quasi-compact and separated, it is also quasi-separated, whence \sref{IV.1.8.3}, there exists a morphism of finite presentation $g:X'\to X$ such that $Z=g(X')$; as $f\circ g$ is of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.6.2}, and $f(Z)=f(g(X'))$, we have seen that the question is reduced to proving that $f(X)$ is constructible; since $f$ is of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.6.2}, (iv)), it suffices to apply \sref{IV.1.8.4.1}.

\begin{lemma}[1.8.4.2]
\label{IV.1.8.4.2}
Let $A_0$ be a ring, $(A_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ an inductive system of $A_0$-algebras, $A=\varinjlim A_\lambda$; if $B$ is an $A$-algebra of finite presentation, there exist a $\lambda$ and an $A_\lambda$-algebra of finite presentation $B_\lambda$ such that $B$ is isomorphic to $B_\lambda\otimes_{A_\lambda} A$.
\end{lemma}

By hypothesis, $B$ is isomorphic to an algebra of the form $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{J}$, where the $T_i$ are indeterminates and $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of finite type; let $(F_j)$ ($1\leqslant j\leqslant m$) be a system of generators of $\mathfrak{J}$. Let $\varphi_\lambda:A_\lambda\to A$ be the canonical homomorphism. Since $L$ is filtering, there exists $\lambda$ such that each of the coefficients of the polynomials $F_j$ is the image by $\varphi_\lambda$ of an element of $A_\lambda$. In other words, there exist a system of polynomials $(F_{j\lambda})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}$ of $A_\lambda[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ such that $F_j=\varphi_\lambda(F_{j\lambda})$ for $1\leqslant j\leqslant m$. If $\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$ is the ideal of $A_\lambda[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ generated by the $F_{j\lambda}$, $\mathfrak{J}$ is the image of $\mathfrak{J}_\lambda\otimes_{A_\lambda} A$ in $A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]=A_\lambda[T_1,\ldots,T_n]\otimes_{A_\lambda} A$; the ring $B_\lambda=A_\lambda[T_1,\ldots,T_n]/\mathfrak{J}_\lambda$ answers the question.

We will apply this lemma by considering $A$ as an inductive limit of its sub-$\bb{Z}$-algebras of \emph{finite type}. One sees therefore that there exist such a sub-$\bb{Z}$-algebra $A_0$ of $A$, and an $A_0$-algebra of finite presentation $B_0$ such that $B$ is isomorphic to $B_0\otimes_{A_0} A$; setting $X_0=\Spec(B_0)$, $Y_0=\Spec(A_0)$, so that $X=X_0\times_{Y_0} Y$, $f$ being the projection $X\to Y$; it follows from \sref[I]{I.3.4.8} that $f(X)=g_0^{-1}(f_0(X_0))$; taking into account \sref{IV.1.8.2}, it suffices therefore to show that $f_0(X_0)$ is constructible. In other words, we are finally reduced to proving the
\oldpage[IV-1]{240}

\begin{corollary}[1.8.5]
\label{IV.1.8.5}
Let $Y$ be a noetherian prescheme, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism of finite type. For every constructible subset $Z$ of $X$, $f(Z)$ is a constructible subset of $Y$.
\end{corollary}

One reduces as above to proving that $f(X)$ is constructible. Applying the criterion $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.2.3): one must prove that for every irreducible closed subset $T$ of $Y$, $T\cap f(f^{-1}(T))$ is rare in $T$ or contains a non-empty open subset of $T$; by taking a sub-prescheme of $Y$ having $T$ as underlying space \sref[I]{I.5.2.1} and considering its inverse image by $f$, one is finally (taking into account \sref{IV.1.5.4}, (iii))) reduced to proving that if $Y$ is irreducible and $f$ dominant, $f(X)$ contains a non-empty open subset of $Y$. One can in addition suppose $Y$ affine; then $X$ is a finite union of affine open sets $V_i$, and as $f(X)$ is dense in $Y$, the same is true of at least one of the $f(V_i)$. One can therefore also suppose $X$ affine; if $(X_j)$ is the family (finite) of the irreducible components of $X$, one sees as above that at least one of the $f(X_j)$ is dense in $Y$; one can therefore suppose $X$ irreducible. Finally, replacing $f$ by $f_{\mathrm{red}}$ \sref{IV.1.5.4}, (vi)) one can suppose $X$ and $Y$ \emph{integral}. Then $X=\Spec(B)$, $Y=\Spec(A)$, where $A$ is a noetherian integral ring and $B$ an integral $A$-algebra of finite type containing $A$ \sref{IV.1.2.7}. It suffices to show that there exists $g\in A$ such that, for every $y\in\mathrm{D}(g)$ (that is to say $g\notin\mathfrak{j}_y$) the ideal $\mathfrak{j}_y$ the ideal of $A$ such that $y$ is the intersection of $A$ and of a prime ideal of $B$, it will be shown that $\mathrm{D}(g)\subset f(X)$. Finally it suffices to prove the

\begin{lemma}[1.8.5.1]
\label{IV.1.8.5.1}
Let $A$ be an integral ring, $B$ an integral $A$-algebra of finite type. There exists $g\in A$ such that every homomorphism of $A$ into an algebraically closed field $\Omega$, non-zero on $g$, extends to a homomorphism of $B$ into $\Omega$.
\end{lemma}

But this is a classical result of commutative algebra (Bourbaki, \emph{Alg.\ comm.}, chap.~V, \S3, n\textsuperscript{o}~1, cor.~3 of th.~1).

\begin{corollary}[1.8.6]
\label{IV.1.8.6}
Let $Y$ be an irreducible prescheme, $\eta$ its generic point, $f:X\to Y$ a morphism locally of finite type. If $f^{-1}(\eta)$ is not empty, there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $\eta$ in $Y$ such that $f^{-1}(y)$ is not empty for every $y\in U$. If $\sh{F}$ is an $\sh{O}_X$-Module quasi-coherent of finite type, and if $\sh{F}_\eta=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y} k(\eta)$ is not zero, then there exists an open neighbourhood $U'$ of $\eta$ in $Y$ such that $\sh{F}_y=\sh{F}\otimes_{\sh{O}_Y} k(y)$ is not zero for every $y\in U'$.
\end{corollary}

If $p_y$ is the canonical projection of the fibre $f^{-1}(y)=X\times_Y\Spec(k(y))$ into $X$, we have $\sh{F}_y=p_y^*(\sh{F})$, so $\mathrm{Supp}(\sh{F}_y)=p_y^{-1}(\mathrm{Supp}(\sh{F}))=\mathrm{Supp}(\sh{F})\cap f^{-1}(y)$ by virtue of \sref[I]{I.9.13.1} and \sref[I]{I.3.6.1}, since $\sh{F}$ is of finite type; moreover $\mathrm{Supp}(\sh{F})$ is closed in $X$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 5.2.2), and if $Z$ is a closed sub-prescheme of $X$ having $\mathrm{Supp}(\sh{F})$ as underlying space and $j$ the canonical immersion $Z\to X$, $f\circ j$ is locally of finite type \sref{IV.1.3.4}, (vi)); this proves that the first assertion implies the second. To prove the first assertion, remark first that one can suppose $X$ and $Y$ \emph{reduced} \sref{IV.1.3.4}, (vi)), in other words $Y$ \emph{integral}. Furthermore, if $Z$ is the closed sub-prescheme reduced of $X$ having $\{\xi\}$ as underlying space (for a point $\xi$ of $f^{-1}(\eta)$); one can, as above, replace $X$ by $Z$, in other words suppose $B$ \emph{integral}. Since the morphism $f$ is then dominant, the corresponding homomorphism $\varphi:A\to B$ is injective \sref[I]{I.1.2.7}; the corollary therefore follows finally from the lemma \sref{IV.1.8.5.1}.

\begin{proposition}[1.8.7]
\label{IV.1.8.7}
Let $S$ be a prescheme, $g:X\to S$, $h:Y\to S$ two morphisms of finite presentation, $f:X\to Y$ an $S$-morphism. For every $s\in S$, let $X_s=g^{-1}(s)=X\times_S\Spec(k(s))$, $Y_s=h^{-1}(s)=Y\times_S\Spec(k(s))$, $f_s=f\times 1:X_s\to Y_s$. Then the set of $s\in S$ such that $f_s$ is surjective \emph{(}resp.\ radiciel\emph{)} is locally constructible.
\end{proposition}
\oldpage[IV-1]{241}

Let $E$ be the set of $s$ such that $f_s$ is surjective; the set $S-E$ is equal to $h(Y-f(X))$; but $f$ is of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.6.2}, (v)) so $f(X)$ is locally constructible in $Y$ \sref{IV.1.8.4}; as $h$ is of finite presentation, $h(Y-f(X))$ is also locally constructible in $S$, hence also $E$.

To show that the set $E'$ of $s$ such that $f_s$ is radiciel is locally constructible, we will use the

\begin{lemma}[1.8.7.1]
\label{IV.1.8.7.1}
Let $U$, $V$ be two preschemes; for a morphism $f:U\to V$ to be radiciel, it suffices that the diagonal morphism $\Delta_f:U\to U\times_V U$ be surjective. Moreover, every radiciel morphism is separated.
\end{lemma}

Indeed, $\Delta_f$ being an immersion \sref[I]{I.5.3.9}, is a morphism locally of finite type, and $\Delta_{f_s}:X_s\to X_s\times_{Y_s} X_s$ is deduced from $\Delta_f:X\to X\times_Y X$ by the base change $\Spec(k(s))\to S$. As $f$ is of finite presentation, $\Delta_f$ is also of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.6.2}, (v)); the same is true of $\Delta_{f_s}$ \sref{IV.1.6.2}, (iv)), so it suffices to apply the first part of the proposition to $\Delta_f$, using the lemma \sref{IV.1.8.7.1}.

\subsection{Pro-constructible and ind-constructible sets}
\label{subsection:IV.1.9}

\begin{lemma}[1.9.1]
\label{IV.1.9.1}
Let $(A_\alpha)_{\alpha\in I}$ be an inductive system of rings whose index set is right filtering, and let $A=\varinjlim A_\alpha$. For $A=0$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist an index $\gamma$ such that $A_\gamma=0$ \emph{(}and then $A_\beta=0$ for all $\beta\geqslant\gamma$\emph{)}.
\end{lemma}

Indeed, for every $\alpha\in I$, the canonical homomorphism $\varphi_\alpha:A_\alpha\to A$ transforms the unit element into the unit element; to say that $A=0$ means that $\varphi_\alpha(1)=\varphi_\alpha(0)$, or equivalently that $\varphi_\alpha(1)=0$; as the set is filtering, this is equivalent to saying that there exists an index $\gamma\geqslant\alpha$ such that $\varphi_{\gamma\alpha}:A_\alpha\to A_\gamma$ satisfies $\varphi_{\gamma\alpha}(1)=\varphi_{\gamma\alpha}(0)$, and this last relation is equivalent to $A_\gamma=0$, whence the lemma.

\begin{proposition}[1.9.2]
\label{IV.1.9.2}
Let $B$ be a ring, $(A_\alpha)_{\alpha\in I}$ an inductive system of $B$-algebras whose index set $I$ is right filtering, and let $A=\varinjlim A_\alpha$; set $Y=\Spec(B)$, $X_\alpha=\Spec(A_\alpha)$, $X=\Spec(A)$, and let $u:X\to Y$, $u_\alpha:X_\alpha\to Y$ be the morphisms corresponding to the structural homomorphisms $B\to A$, $B\to A_\alpha$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item For $X=\varnothing$, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist $\gamma\in I$ such that $X_\gamma=\varnothing$, and then $X_\alpha=\varnothing$ for $\alpha\geqslant\gamma$.
  \item We have
\end{enumerate}
\begin{equation*}
\tag{1.9.2.1}
  u(X) = \bigcap_{\alpha\in I} u_\alpha(X_\alpha).
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

The assertion (i) is none other than the translation of \sref{IV.1.9.1}. To prove (ii), note that, since $u$ factorises as $X\xrightarrow{u_\alpha} X_\alpha\xrightarrow{} Y$, the first member is contained in the second. Conversely, let $y\in Y-u(X)$; on a $u^{-1}(y)=\varnothing$, and $u^{-1}(y)$ is the $k(y)$-prescheme $\Spec(A\otimes_B k(y))$; as in the category of $B$-modules the functor $\varinjlim$ commutes with the tensor product, $A\otimes_B k(y)$ is the inductive limit of the system $(A_\alpha\otimes_B k(y))$; the lemma \sref{IV.1.9.1} shows that there exists $\alpha$ such that $A_\alpha\otimes_B k(y)=0$, so $u_\alpha^{-1}(y)=\varnothing$, and hence $y\notin u_\alpha(X_\alpha)$. Q.E.D.

\begin{proposition}[1.9.3]
\label{IV.1.9.3}
Let $X$ be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated prescheme, $(X_\alpha)_{\alpha\in I}$ an open cover of $X$, and for every $\alpha\in I$, set $E_\alpha=E\cap X_\alpha$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{a)}]
  \item There exists a quasi-compact morphism $f:X'\to X$ such that $E=f(X')$.
  \item[\emph{a')}] For every $\alpha$, there exists a quasi-compact morphism $f_\alpha:X'_\alpha\to X_\alpha$ such that $E_\alpha=f_\alpha(X'_\alpha)$.
  \item[\emph{a'')}] For every $\alpha$, there exists an affine scheme $X'_\alpha$ and a morphism $f_\alpha:X'_\alpha\to X_\alpha$ such that $f_\alpha(X'_\alpha)=E_\alpha$.
  \item $E$ is an intersection of constructible subsets of $X$.
  \item[\emph{b')}] $F=X-E$ is a union of constructible subsets of $X$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

It is clear that $b)$ and $b')$ are equivalent. The condition $a)$ implies $a')$ by taking for $X'_\alpha$ the prescheme induced on $f^{-1}(X_\alpha)$ by $X'$ and for $f_\alpha$ the restriction of $f$. To see that $a')$ implies $a'')$, it suffices to remark that $X'_\alpha$ is a finite union of affine open sets; let $X''_\alpha$ be the prescheme \emph{sum} of the $X''_{\alpha\lambda}$ ($\lambda\in L_\alpha$), which is an affine scheme; if $g_\alpha:X''_\alpha\to X'_\alpha$ is the morphism coinciding with the identity in each of the $X''_{\alpha\lambda}$, it is clear that $f'_\alpha=f_\alpha\circ g_\alpha$ on a $E_\alpha=f'_\alpha(X''_\alpha)$ since $g_\alpha$ is surjective. To show that $a'')$ implies $a)$, note that since $X_\alpha$ covers $X$ for $\alpha\in J$, and let $f:X'\to X$ be the morphism which coincides with $g_\alpha\circ f_\alpha$ in each $X'_\alpha$, it is clear that $E=f(X')$ and the hypothesis that $X$ is quasi-separated implies that $j_\alpha$ is quasi-compact \sref{IV.1.2.7}, hence $f$ is quasi-compact \sref{IV.1.1.6} and finality of $f$ \sref{IV.1.1.2}.

It remains to prove the equivalence of $a'')$ and $b)$. Suppose first that $a'')$ holds; for every $\alpha\in J$, where $X_\alpha=\Spec(A)$ is affine, and $E=f(X')$ where $X'=\Spec(A')$ is affine, $f:X'\to X$ a morphism corresponding to a homomorphism of rings $A\to A'$. Note now that $A'$ is the inductive limit of the family $(A'_\lambda)$ of its sub-$A$-algebras of \emph{finite type}, ordered by inclusion; if $X'_\lambda=\Spec(A'_\lambda)$, $f$ factorises as $X'\to X'_\lambda\xrightarrow{f_\lambda} X$ and it follows from \sref{IV.1.9.2}, (ii)) that $f(X')=\bigcap_\lambda f_\lambda(X'_\lambda)$; if one shows that $f_\lambda(X'_\lambda)$ is an intersection of constructible subsets of $X$, the same will be true of $f(X')$. One can therefore restrict to the case where $A'$ is an $A$-algebra of \emph{finite type}. But then, by the theorem of Chevalley \sref{IV.1.8.5}, $f(X')$ is constructible in $X$, whence $b)$.

Let us show finally that $b)$ implies $a'')$. If $E$ is an intersection of a family $(G_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ of constructible subsets of $X$, each $E_\alpha$ is an intersection of constructible subsets $G_\lambda\cap X_\alpha$, which are constructible in $X_\alpha$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.8, (i)), hence we are reduced to the case where $X=\Spec(A)$ is affine. We know then \sref{IV.1.8.3} that for each $\lambda\in L$ there exists a morphism $f_\lambda:X'_\lambda\to X$, where $X'_\lambda=\Spec(A'_\lambda)$ is affine, such that $f_\lambda(X'_\lambda)=G_\lambda$. It suffices then to take for $B'$ the product tensor $A'_J=\bigotimes_{\lambda\in J} A'_\lambda$ (product tensor of $A$-algebras), $X'_J=\Spec(A'_J)$, and let $f_J:X'_J\to X$ be the structural morphism. If $A'=\varinjlim A'_J$, $J$ ranging over the filtering set of finite subsets of $L$, $X'=\Spec(A')$, and if $f:X'\to X$ is the structural morphism, we have $f(X')=\bigcap_{\lambda\in L} f_\lambda(X'_\lambda)$.

\begin{lemma}[1.9.3.1]
\label{IV.1.9.3.1}
Let $A$ be a ring, $A'$ an $A$-algebra of finite type. Then $A'$ is a limit $\varinjlim A''_\mu$ where the $A''_\mu$ are $A$-algebras of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.4.1}.
\end{lemma}

Indeed, one can write $A'=B/\mathfrak{J}$, with $B=A[T_1,\ldots,T_n]$ and $\mathfrak{J}$ an ideal of $B$. But $\mathfrak{J}$ is the inductive limit of the \emph{finitely generated} ideals $\mathfrak{J}'_\mu$ of $B$, contained in $\mathfrak{J}$, ordered by inclusion; as in the category of $B$-modules the functor $\varinjlim$ is exact, we conclude that $A'=\varinjlim B/\mathfrak{J}'_\mu$ up to $A$-isomorphism.

\begin{lemma}[1.9.3.2]
\label{IV.1.9.3.2}
Let $(A'_\lambda)_{\lambda\in L}$ be a family of $A$-algebras, $X=\Spec(A)$, $X'_\lambda=\Spec(A'_\lambda)$, and let $f_\lambda:X'_\lambda\to X$ be the structural morphism. For every finite subset $J$ of $L$, set $A'_J=\bigotimes_{\lambda\in J} A'_\lambda$ \emph{(}tensor product of $A$-algebras\emph{)}, $X'_J=\Spec(A'_J)$, and let $f_J:X'_J\to X$ be the structural morphism. If $A'=\varinjlim A'_J$, $J$ ranging over the filtering set of finite subsets of $L$, $X'=\Spec(A')$, and if $f:X'\to X$ is the structural morphism, we have $f_J(X'_J)=\bigcap_{\lambda\in J} f_\lambda(X'_\lambda)$. If $A'=\varinjlim A'_J$, $X'=\Spec(A')$, and $f:X'\to X$ the structural morphism, we have $f(X')=\bigcap_{\lambda\in L} f_\lambda(X'_\lambda)$.
\end{lemma}

The first assertion follows from \sref[I]{I.3.4.7}; the second follows from \sref{IV.1.9.2}, (ii)).

\begin{definition}[1.9.4]
\label{IV.1.9.4}
Let $X$ be a topological space. We say that a subset $E$ of $X$ is \emph{pro-constructible} (resp.\ \emph{ind-constructible}) in $X$ if, for every $x\in X$, there exists an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $E\cap U$ is an intersection (resp.\ union) of locally constructible subsets of $U$.
\end{definition}

Taking into account \sref{IV.1.2.7} and $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.8 and 9.1.12), the equivalent conditions of the proposition \sref{IV.1.9.3} are expressed by saying that $E$ is \emph{pro-constructible} in $X$, the equivalent conditions on the locally constructible subsets in $X$ being identical to the constructible subsets when $X$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.5).

\begin{proposition}[1.9.5]
\label{IV.1.9.5}
Let $X$ be a prescheme.
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item For a subset $E$ of $X$ to be pro-constructible, it is necessary and sufficient that $X-E$ be ind-constructible.
  \item Every finite union and every finite intersection of pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)} subsets of $X$ are pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)}.
  \item Every intersection \emph{(}resp.\ union\emph{)} of locally constructible subsets of $X$ is pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)}. Conversely, if $X$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, every pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)} subset of $X$ is an intersection \emph{(}resp.\ union\emph{)} of constructible subsets.
  \item Let $(U_\alpha)$ be an open cover of $X$. For a subset $E$ of $X$ to be pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)} in $X$, it is necessary and sufficient that for every $\alpha$, $E\cap U_\alpha$ be pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)} in $U_\alpha$.
  \item Every pro-constructible closed subset $E$ of $X$ is retrocompact in $X$.
  \item Let $f:X'\to X$ be a morphism of preschemes; for every pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)} subset $E$ of $X$, $f^{-1}(E)$ is pro-constructible \emph{(}resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)} in $X'$.
  \item Let $f:X'\to X$ be a quasi-compact morphism; for every pro-constructible subset $E'$ of $X'$, $f(E')$ is pro-constructible in $X$; in particular $f(X')$ is pro-constructible in $X$.
  \item Let $f:X'\to X$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation; for every ind-constructible subset $E'$ of $X'$, $f(E')$ is ind-constructible in $X$; in particular $f(X')$ is ind-constructible in $X$.
  \item Suppose $X$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated; then, for a subset $E$ of $X$ to be pro-constructible, it is necessary and sufficient that there exist an affine scheme $X'$ and a \emph{(}necessarily quasi-compact\emph{)} morphism $f:X'\to X$ such that $E=f(X')$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The properties (i), (ii), (iv), and (iii) (first assertion) follow from the definition \sref{IV.1.9.4} and are valid for any topological space, using the distributivity of intersection and union, and the fact that if $T$ is locally constructible in $X$, $T\cap U$ is locally constructible in $U$ for every open $U$ of $X$. If $X$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated and $E$ is pro-constructible in $X$, there is a finite open cover $(U_i)$ of $X$ by affine open sets such that $E\cap U_i$ is intersection of constructible subsets in $X_i$ for every $i$; $E$ being a union of the $E\cap U_i$, is intersection of finite unions $\bigcup_i F_{i,\lambda(i)}$, for all choices of $\lambda(i)\in L_i$; these unions are constructible in $X$; this proves the second assertion of (iii) and also (ix) by \sref{IV.1.9.3}.

To prove (v), one can restrict to the case where $X$ is affine, and prove that $E$ is quasi-compact for the induced topology of $X$. The properties (vi) and (viii) translate respectively \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (vi) and (vii).

Similarly, to prove (vii), one can restrict to the case where $X$ is affine; then $X'$ is a finite union of affine open sets $X'_i$ and $f(E')$ is a union of the $f(E'\cap X'_i)$, so one can even reduce to $X'$ affine, in which case $X'$ is a union of affine open sets, and $f(E')$ is a union of the $f(E'\cap X'_i)$; hence $f(E')$ is pro-constructible by an application continued.

To prove (viii), one can also restrict to the case where $X$ is affine; then $X'$ is a finite union of affine open sets $X'_i$ and $f(E')$ is a union of the $f(E'\cap X'_i)$, so from (ii) one can restrict to the case where $X'$ is affine; this then follows from the theorem of Chevalley \sref{IV.1.8.4} and (iii).

Finally, to prove (viii), one can still suppose $X$ affine and, by (iv), also restrict to the case where $E'$ is ind-constructible in $X'$; the conclusion then follows from (iii) and \sref{IV.1.8.4}.

\begin{examples}[1.9.6]
\label{IV.1.9.6}
Every \emph{finite} subset of a prescheme $X$ is pro-constructible: indeed, it suffices to consider the singleton $\{x\}$ reduced to a single point \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (ii)) and $\{x\}$ is the image of $\Spec(k(x))$ by the canonical morphism $\Spec(k(x))\to X$, which is quasi-compact; whence the conclusion by \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (vii)). On the other hand, a subset $\{x\}$ reduced to a point is not necessarily ind-constructible; for example, let $A$ be a noetherian integral ring having infinitely many maximal ideals, and let $x$ be the generic point of $X=\Spec(A)$; if $\{x\}$ were ind-constructible in $X$, it would be constructible by virtue of \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (iii)) and would therefore contain a non-empty open subset of $X$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.2.2); but this contradicts the hypothesis that $A$ has infinitely many maximal ideals, by virtue of the theorem of Artin--Tate $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 16.3.3).

Every \emph{closed} subset $Y$ of a prescheme $X$ is pro-constructible, by \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (v)). Every open subset of $X$ is therefore ind-constructible, but an open subset $U$ of $X$ is not pro-constructible in general unless it is \emph{retrocompact}, by virtue of \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (v)).

Finally, if $X$ is a \emph{noetherian} prescheme, hence quasi-separated \sref{IV.1.2.8}, it follows from \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (iii)) and $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.7) that the ind-constructible subsets of $X$ are the \emph{unions of locally closed subsets} of $X$.
\end{examples}

\begin{proposition}[1.9.10]
\label{IV.1.9.10}
Let $X$ be a prescheme. For a subset $E$ of $X$ to be ind-constructible, it is necessary that, for every $x\in X$, the intersection $E\cap\overline{\{x\}}$ be a neighbourhood of $x$ in $\overline{\{x\}}$. If $X$ is locally noetherian, this condition is sufficient.
\end{proposition}

Suppose $E$ ind-constructible, and let $Y$ be the intersection of $\overline{\{x\}}$ and an affine open subset in $X$, containing $x$; there is then a sub-prescheme of $X$ having $Y$ as underlying space \sref[I]{I.5.2.1}, and if $j:Y\to X$ is the canonical injection, $E\cap Y=j^{-1}(E)$ is ind-constructible in $Y$ \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (vi)). Since $Y$ is quasi-compact and separated, $E\cap Y$ is therefore a union of constructible subsets of $Y$ \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (iii)); furthermore, there are two open subsets $U$, $V$ of $Y$ such that $x\in U\cap\complement V\subset E\cap Y$ $(\mathbf{0_{III}}$, 9.1.3). But as $x$ is the generic point of the irreducible space $Y$, $V$ is necessarily empty and we have $U\subset E\cap Y$.

Now suppose $X$ locally noetherian, and let $E$ be a subset of $X$ satisfying the condition of the statement. By virtue of the definition \sref{IV.1.9.4}, one can restrict to the case where $X$ is \emph{noetherian}. Then, for every $x\in E$, $E\cap\overline{\{x\}}$ contains a non-empty open subset of the form $U\cap\overline{\{x\}}$, where $U$ is open in $X$; this shows that $E$ is a union of locally closed subsets, hence is ind-constructible \sref{IV.1.9.6}.

\begin{proposition}[1.9.11]
\label{IV.1.9.11}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $E$ a subset of $X$. The following properties are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{a)}]
  \item $E$ is locally constructible.
  \item $E$ is ind-constructible and pro-constructible.
  \item $E$ and $X-E$ are pro-constructible.
  \item $E$ and $X-E$ are ind-constructible.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

It suffices evidently to prove that $d)$ implies $a)$, and one can restrict to the case where $X$ is affine. Then \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (iii)), $E$ (resp.\ $X-E$) is a union of a family $(E_\lambda)$ (resp.\ $(E'_\mu)$) of constructible subsets of $X$; as the $E_\lambda$ and the $E'_\mu$ form a cover of $X$, by \sref{IV.1.9.9} it follows that there are finitely many $\lambda_i$, $\mu_j$ such that the $E_{\lambda_i}$ and the $E'_{\mu_j}$ form a cover of $X$; this implies that $E$ is the union of the $E_{\lambda_i}$, hence is constructible.

Let $f:X\to Y$ be a surjective morphism, either quasi-compact, or locally of finite presentation. For a subset $E$ of $Y$ to be locally constructible \emph{(}resp.\ pro-constructible, resp.\ ind-constructible\emph{)}, it is necessary and sufficient that $f^{-1}(E)$ be so in $X$.
\end{corollary}

One knows that the condition is necessary \sref{IV.1.8.2} and \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (vi))); to show that it is sufficient, one can restrict (by virtue of \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (iv))) to the case where $X$ is affine, or the case where $f^{-1}(E)$ is pro-constructible, \emph{or} the case where $f^{-1}(E)$ is ind-constructible. If $f$ is surjective and locally of finite presentation, and $f^{-1}(E)$ ind-constructible, $E=f(f^{-1}(E))$ is ind-constructible by \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (viii)), which finishes the proof.

Let $X$ be a prescheme, $\mathscr{T}$ its topology; it follows from \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (i) and (ii)) that the ind-constructible (resp.\ pro-constructible) subsets of $X$ are the open subsets (resp.\ closed subsets) for a topology on $X$, called the \emph{constructible topology} and which we will denote by $\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{cons}}$; we will denote by $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$ the set $X$ equipped with the topology $\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{cons}}$.

\begin{proposition}[1.9.14]
\label{IV.1.9.14}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $\mathscr{T}$ its topology, $\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{cons}}$ the constructible topology on $X$.
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item The topology $\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is finer than $\mathscr{T}$.
  \item The locally constructible subsets of $X$ are identical to the subsets that are both open and closed in $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$.
  \item For every morphism $f:X\to Y$, the underlying map of $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$ to $Y^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is continuous; we denote it $f^{\mathrm{cons}}$.
  \item If the morphism $f:X\to Y$ is quasi-compact, $f^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is a closed map; in particular, if $f$ is quasi-compact and bijective, $f^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is a homeomorphism.
  \item If the morphism $f:X\to Y$ is locally of finite presentation, $f^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is an open map; in particular, if $f$ is bijective and locally of finite presentation, $f^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is a homeomorphism.
  \item For every open subset $U$ of $X$, the topology induced by $\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{cons}}$ on $U$ is identical to the topology of $U^{\mathrm{cons}}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Indeed, (i) follows from the fact that every open set for $\mathscr{T}$ is an open set for $\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{cons}}$ \sref{IV.1.9.6}, and (ii) is a translation of \sref{IV.1.9.11}; (iii), (iv) and (v) translate respectively \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (vi), (vii) and (viii). Finally, to prove (vi), it suffices to remark that the canonical injection $j:U\to X$ is locally of finite presentation \sref{IV.1.4.3}, so $j^{\mathrm{cons}}:U^{\mathrm{cons}}\to X^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is a continuous open map.

\begin{proposition}[1.9.15]
\label{IV.1.9.15}
Let $X$ be a prescheme.
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{(i)}]
  \item For $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$ to be quasi-compact, it is necessary and sufficient that $X$ be quasi-compact.
  \item For $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$ to be separated, it is necessary and sufficient that $X$ be quasi-separated, and then $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is locally compact and totally disconnected.
  \item For $X^{\mathrm{cons}}$ to be compact, it is necessary and sufficient that $X$ be quasi-compact and quasi-separated.
  \item Every point of $X$ admits, for the topology $\mathscr{T}^{\mathrm{cons}}$, an open and compact neighbourhood.
  \item For a quasi-compact morphism $f:X\to Y$ to be quasi-compact, it is necessary and sufficient that the continuous map $f^{\mathrm{cons}}:X^{\mathrm{cons}}\to Y^{\mathrm{cons}}$ be proper.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[1.10.1]
\label{IV.1.10.1}
Let $X$ be a prescheme, $E$ an ind-constructible subset of $X$ \sref{IV.1.9.4}, $x$ a point of $X$. For $x$ to be interior to $E$, it is necessary and sufficient that every generalisation $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.1.2) $x'$ of $x$ belong to $E$, in other words that $\Spec(\sh{O}_{X,x})\subset E$.

The condition is evidently necessary, every neighbourhood of $x$ containing the generalisations of $x$. To see that it is sufficient, one can evidently restrict to the case where $X=\Spec(A)$ is affine, $x$ being a prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$. One knows then \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (ix)) that there exist an affine scheme $X'=\Spec(A')$ and a morphism $f:X'\to X$ such that $f(X')=X-E$. Set $Y=\Spec(\sh{O}_{X,x})$ and $Y'=X'\times_X Y$; the hypothesis means (by virtue of \sref[I]{I.3.6.5}) that $Y'=\varnothing$, in other words $A'\otimes_A A_{\mathfrak{p}}=0$. As $A_{\mathfrak{p}}=\varinjlim A_t$, where $t$ ranges over $A-\mathfrak{p}$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 1.4.5), on a $\varinjlim A'_t=0$, the functor $\varinjlim$ commuting with the tensor product. By \sref{IV.1.9.1}, there exists $t\in A-\mathfrak{p}$ such that $A'_t=0$, and $\mathrm{D}(t)$ is then an open neighbourhood of $x$ contained in $E$.

\begin{corollary}[1.10.2]
\label{IV.1.10.2}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism locally of finite presentation, and let $y\in Y$. For $y$ to be interior to $f(X)$ it is necessary and sufficient that every generalisation $y'$ of $y$ belong to $f(X)$.
\end{corollary}

One knows indeed \sref{IV.1.9.5}, (viii)) that $f(X)$ is ind-constructible in $Y$ and it suffices to apply \sref{IV.1.10.1}.

We will say that a continuous map $f:X\to Y$ is \emph{open at a point} $x\in X$ if the image by $f$ of \emph{every} neighbourhood of $x$ in $X$ is a neighbourhood of $f(x)$ in $Y$.

\begin{proposition}[1.10.3]
\label{IV.1.10.3}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism, $x$ a point of $X$, $y=f(x)$. Consider the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{a)}]
  \item $f$ is open at the point $x$.
  \item For every generalisation $y'$ of $y$, there exists a generalisation $x'$ of $x$ such that $f(x')=y'$.
  \item[\emph{b')}] The image by $f$ of $\Spec(\sh{O}_{X,x})$ is $\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$.
  \item For every irreducible closed subset $Y'$ of $Y$ containing $y$, there exists an irreducible component of $X'=f^{-1}(Y')$ containing $x$ and dominating $Y'$.
\end{enumerate}
Then we have the implications $a)\Rightarrow b)\Leftrightarrow b')\Rightarrow c)$. If in addition $f$ is locally of finite presentation, the four conditions are equivalent.
\end{proposition}

By definition of a generalisation, the image by $f$ of $\Spec(\sh{O}_{X,x})$ is always contained in $\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$, so $b)$ and $b')$ are equivalent. It is immediate that $c)$ implies $b)$, for if $y'$ is a generalisation of $y$, $Y'=\overline{\{y'\}}$ is an irreducible closed subset of $Y$ containing $y$; if $x'$ is the generic point of an irreducible component of $f^{-1}(Y')$ containing $x$ and dominating $Y'$, we have $f(x')=y'$ $(\mathbf{0_I}$, 2.1.5) and $x'$ is a generalisation of $x$. Conversely, $b)$ implies $c)$: indeed, let $y'$ be the generic point of $Y'$ and let $x'$ be a generalisation of $x$ such that $f(x')=y'$; let $Z'$ be an irreducible component of $f^{-1}(Y')$ containing $x'$ (hence also $x$); as $f(x')$ is already dense in $Y'$, it is thus \emph{a fortiori} so for $f(Z')$.

To see that $a)$ implies $b')$, one can evidently restrict to the case where $X=\Spec(B)$ and $Y=\Spec(A)$ are affine, $x$ being a prime ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ of $B$, so that $\sh{O}_{X,x}=B_{\mathfrak{q}}=\varinjlim B_t$,
\oldpage[IV-1]{250}
where $t$ ranges over $B-\mathfrak{q}$. We have, by \sref{IV.1.9.2}, $f(\Spec(\sh{O}_{X,x}))=\bigcap_t f(\Spec(B_t))=\bigcap_t f(\mathrm{D}(t))$, and by hypothesis, for every $t\in B-\mathfrak{q}$, $y$ is interior to $f(\mathrm{D}(t))$, so $f(\mathrm{D}(t))$ contains $\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})$; whence $\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,y})\subset f(\Spec(\sh{O}_{X,x}))$, which establishes our assertion. Finally, when $f$ is locally of finite presentation, $b)$ implies $a)$ by virtue of \sref{IV.1.10.2} applied to the restriction $U\to Y$ of $f$ to an arbitrary open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$.

\begin{corollary}[1.10.4]
\label{IV.1.10.4}
Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism. Consider the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[\emph{a)}]
  \item $f$ is open.
  \item For every $x\in X$ and every generalisation $y'$ of $y=f(x)$, there exists a generalisation $x'$ of $x$ such that $f(x')=y'$.
  \item[\emph{b')}] For every $x\in X$, the image by $f$ of $\Spec(\sh{O}_{X,x})$ is $\Spec(\sh{O}_{Y,f(x)})$.
  \item For every irreducible closed subset $Y'$ of $Y$, every irreducible component of $f^{-1}(Y')$ dominates $Y'$.
\end{enumerate}
Then we have the implications $a)\Rightarrow b)\Leftrightarrow b')\Rightarrow c)$. If in addition $f$ is locally of finite presentation, the four conditions are equivalent.
\end{corollary}

To say that $f$ is open means that $f$ is open at every point $x\in X$, hence the implications $a)\Rightarrow b)\Leftrightarrow b')$ follow from the analogous implications in \sref{IV.1.10.3}, as well as the implication $b)\Rightarrow a)$ when $f$ is locally of finite presentation. The implication $c)\Rightarrow b)$ also follows from the analogous implication in \sref{IV.1.10.3}; let us prove finally that $b)$ implies $c)$. Indeed, let $x'$ be the generic point of an irreducible component of $f^{-1}(Y')$, and let us show that $y'=f(x')$ is the generic point of $Y'$. Let $y''$ be a generalisation of $y'$; it exists by hypothesis a generalisation $x''$ of $x'$ such that $f(x'')=y''$, and as $x''\in f^{-1}(Y')$, we necessarily have $x''=x'$, hence $y''=y'$, which finishes the proof.
