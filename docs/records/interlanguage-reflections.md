## What the current paper contributes

The paper reconstructs AI-assisted interlanguage production as an auditable scholarly method rather than a sequence of prompts. It separates inherited target-language authority, source witnesses, model-prior output, retrieved evidence, editorial decisions, adverse history, and checked effects. It explains why translation can expose defects in mathematical source transcriptions, how an evolving canon prevents terminology drift, how deterministic script projection avoids creating a second uncontrolled witness, and how durable machine state survives session compaction.

The transport package preserves the complete editable paper, claim–evidence matrix, current Interslavic v021 methods and publication evidence, full build and page-render QA, a bounded public task transcript, and the displaced predecessor paper and transport. The wider Zenodo record retains the other interlanguage programmes and earlier evidence rather than selecting them away.

## Scope and limits

The current release proves recorded production, traceable correction, consistency under declared checks, reproducibility within stated conditions, and public access. It does not claim official standardization, native-speaker certification, direct reader-comprehension results, mathematical correctness, peer review, community-standard status, inherent linguistic neutrality, or a universally optimal interlanguage.
