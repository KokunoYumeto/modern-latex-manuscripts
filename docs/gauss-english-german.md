dedication, preface, all seven sections of the *Disquisitiones Arithmeticae*
(Articles 1–366), five historical additions, Tables I–III, the historical
contents, the manuscript appendix, the historical editor's closing note and
both imprints. Historical page references retain the source pagination.

Volume II contains the historical title matter, its first five complete papers:

The complete self-reviews of May 12 and September 19, 1808, March 10, 1817, April 11, 1825, and April 23, 1831, follow these five
papers.

Seven complete reviews of other writings follow: March 11, 1809; March 23, 1812; November 3, 1814; November 7, 1816; August 9, 1817; December 19, 1825; and July 9, 1831. They concern a work on irrational radicals, the factor tables of Chernac and Burckhardt, Erchinger’s construction of the regular seventeen-sided polygon, and Seeber’s positive ternary quadratic forms.

The complete manuscript work *Solution of the Congruence X^m−1 ≡ 0*, the first part of Chapter VI of *Analysis of Residues* (Articles 237–252), follows the reviews.

*General Investigations of Congruences*, Chapter VIII of *Analysis of Residues*, contains Articles 330–375 as printed, including the unfinished ending of Article 375. R. Dedekind's complete historical remarks follow. This portion treats congruences between functions, division and common divisors, prime functions, factorization and counting, power transformations and periods, two proofs of quadratic reciprocity, equal roots and divisors, and separation of factors by degree.

*Further Development of the Investigations of Pure Equations* follows in full as printed, with its title, Articles 1–19 and R. Dedekind's historical remarks. It treats proper and improper roots, products and rational expressions formed from their powers, consistent radical choices, periods, division of ratios and angles, indices and least residues, power expansions, trigonometric period expressions, worked numerical examples and the sign ambiguity in quadratic sums.

The fragment *Proof of Some Theorems Concerning the Periods of Classes of Binary Quadratic Forms* contains both theorems, the unfinished ending of the second proof and Stern's historical remark.

The texts marked [I.] and [II.] of *On the Connection between the Number of Classes into Which Binary Quadratic Forms Are Distributed and Their Determinant, First Paper* are each included in full as printed, with their titles and Articles 1–8. The text marked [II.] was presented in 1837. These texts treat counts of integer points in regions, limiting ratios of counts to areas, and mean numbers of representations by binary quadratic forms. Text [I.] includes prime-factor rules, series and infinite products. Text [II.] gives the boundary-square argument, distinguishes approximation of ratios from approximation of differences, and treats counts with congruence and parity conditions.

Fragments III–X follow in full as printed, with their historical editorial notes. They treat representation counts, average and actual numbers of classes, and the distribution of quadratic residues in eighths and twelfths, including the historical tables. The complete historical remarks on [I.]–[X.], signed by R. Dedekind, discuss circle-counting formulas and quoted rules, divisor and representation counts, the series for π/4, prime elimination and mean values, infinite products and the qualification concerning their rigor, trigonometric summations and class numbers, and hyperbolic sectors for positive determinants. They also give the biquadratic-character argument for 2 and the full class-number relations for quadratic residues in eighths and twelfths, with the correspondence tables and both congruence cases.

*Geometrical Aspect of Ternary Forms* follows with its complete geometric definitions and relations, the ellipsoid and tetrahedron notes, the crystallization law, transformation tables and complex projections, and the hexakisoctahedron formulas, value table and two editable diagrams. Schering’s complete historical remarks include the crystal measurements, the quoted theorem on transformations of an indefinite ternary form, its parameter conditions and the account of the transformation table. 

The posthumous texts [I.] and [II.] of *On the Theory of Biquadratic Residues* follow. Text [I.] contains its title and complete Articles 1–19, including all five editable diagrams. Text [II.], *Preparations for the General Theory of Biquadratic Residues*, contains both preparatory sections (1) and (2) and complete Articles 1–12 as printed. They treat oriented lines, winding and boundary conventions, residue classes, decidents and their composition, the residue 1+i, and the point, square and ligature constructions for biquadratic characters. Their originally German prose is rendered in modern German in the German reader; the original German bodies and diagrams are supplied separately.

Text [III.], *Theory of Biquadratic Residues*, follows in full as printed, with its title and complete Articles 1–14, including the joint heading 7–8. It treats least-residue classes, decidents and the stated reciprocity theorem, coordinate bounds, parity sums and all boundary exceptions. Text [IV.] follows in full as printed, Articles 1–5, with its coordinate relations, two bound tables, intensor sums and sign table; its second method ends after the fourth step. The German reader renders the original German in modern German; the historical originals are supplied separately.

Text [V.], *Theory of Biquadratic Residues*, follows in full as printed, Articles 1–17, with its editable lattice diagram, numerical examples, parity tables and 128-case table. Text [VI.] follows in full as printed, with its title and complete Articles 1–6: complete residue systems, associated quarter systems, the character and its independence, boundary conventions and the four-case construction. Their original German prose is rendered in modern German in the German reader; the historical originals are supplied separately.

## Translation sources and terminology

These English and German texts are AI-translated from Gauss's original-language
editions. Earlier translations are consulted to standardize mathematical
terminology and usage; they are not reproduced as this edition's running text.
The original mathematical formulations remain those of Gauss's source.

H. Maser, German editor and translator, Carl Friedrich Gauss' Untersuchungen
über höhere Arithmetik, Berlin: Julius Springer, 1889, is a reference for
German arithmetical terminology and corresponding passages.
https://archive.org/details/carlfriedrichga00gausgoog

James Caddall Morehead and Adam Miller Hiltebeitel, translators, General
Investigations of Curved Surfaces of 1827 and 1825, Princeton University
Library, 1902 (Project Gutenberg 36856), and Paul Taylor's English translation
of Gauss's second proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra, with corrections by Bernard
Leak, 1983, are references for English mathematical terminology and usage.
Their geometrical and algebraic subject matter differs from these arithmetic
papers; they do not supply replacement text or proofs for them.
https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/36856
https://www.paultaylor.eu/misc/gauss.html

Contemporary German terminology is also informed by Ulrich Görtz, Algebra,
Wintersemester 2021/22, Section 3.1, and Lineare Algebra II, Sommersemester
2021, Section 18.4; and Nikolay Moshchevitin, Eine Bemerkung über positiv
definite quadratische Formen und rationale Punkte, arXiv:1510.06214v3.
These are terminology references, not translations of the present works.
https://math.ug/a-ws2122/sect0006.html
https://math.ug/la2-ss21/sec-quotient-ring.html
https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.06214v3
