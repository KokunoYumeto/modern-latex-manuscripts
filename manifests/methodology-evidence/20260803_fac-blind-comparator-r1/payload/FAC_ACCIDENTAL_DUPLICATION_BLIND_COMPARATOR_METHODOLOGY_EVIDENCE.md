# FAC accidental-duplication blind comparator — methodology evidence control

## Why this evidence exists

The overlap was not planned. Codex translated Jean-Pierre Serre's *Faisceaux Algébriques Cohérents* source-first from the controlling French journal witness and completed, compiled, and reviewed FAC nos. 1--79 before Floris discovered that Piotr Achinger and Łukasz Krupa had already produced an English translation. Floris first supplied the public PDF and then found the public editable source archive. The archive was acquired only after the Codex no.79 boundary was frozen.

That accidental duplication creates a useful chronology-defined held-out comparison. The independently written Achinger--Krupa English could not have influenced the Codex wording, formulas, or source interventions in nos.1--79. It can therefore test the translation-comparator method on genuinely overlapping work rather than on a translation drafted with the comparator open.

This backstory is part of the evidence. It must not later be rewritten as a planned benchmark, an independent French witness, or an external translation used to draft the blind cohort.

## What the experiment tests

For each matched no.1--79 unit, the comparator workflow must distinguish:

1. substantive agreement;
2. harmless target-language variation;
3. a beneficial external convention that remains source-safe;
4. an external mathematical, textual, structural, or TeX regression;
5. a Codex regression requiring append-only repair;
6. an inherited French-transcription deviation;
7. a printed French typo or defect requiring diplomatic preservation plus a disclosed corrected layer.

The corrected French TeX and, where a reading is genuinely ambiguous, the journal image adjudicate every disagreement. Neither English lineage is source authority. Mechanical similarity classes are routing aids only; scalar scores, majority votes, or certification claims are prohibited.

Nos.80--81 were translated after the comparator became available. They are explicitly comparator-assisted and excluded from blind-performance claims; they instead provide a small process control for whether the same workflow rejects bad alternatives without copying them.
