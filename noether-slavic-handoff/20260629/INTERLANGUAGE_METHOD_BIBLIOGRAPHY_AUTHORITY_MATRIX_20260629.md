# Interlanguage method bibliography and authority matrix - 2026-06-29

This artifact strengthens the research/publication lane for AI-assisted technical-register construction, semi-constructed/interlanguage methodology, educational translation, and open-source handoff.

It is not a claim that any constructed or zonal language is ready for canonical Noether publication. It is a source shelf and governance matrix for deciding what kind of evidence, authority, and review each lane requires.

## Authority Matrix

| Lane type | What local validation can prove | What local validation cannot prove | Required external authority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Established natural language with mature mathematical register | Source coverage, render integrity, glossary consistency, term anchor counts, TeX/PDF completeness. | Native idiom, final acceptability, region-specific convention, pedagogy suitability. | Native technical writer, mathematician, translator, or domain reviewer. |
| Low-resource or under-served educational lane | Source discoverability, page anchors, script handling, open-license pointers, reproducible translation workflow. | Community usefulness, classroom adoption, cultural appropriateness, local ownership. | Local educators, language users, subject teachers, and community reviewers. |
| Multi-standard language family lane | Side-by-side evidence, script/crosswalk tables, terminology divergence, shared/blocked terms. | A unified standard, cross-border authority, or legitimacy of collapsing registers. | Reviewers for each standard, plus explicit cross-register governance. |
| Zonal/interlanguage lane | Design rationale, witness comparison, machine-readable lexicon tables, consistency checks, script conversion, error logs. | That the language is acceptable to its communities, politically neutral in practice, or appropriate for canonical publication. | Language-community authority, linguists familiar with the project, and external user review. |
| Constructed-language pilot | Internal grammar/term consistency, reproducibility, examples, parser/renderer checks, versioned governance logs. | Educational legitimacy, community uptake, cultural authority, or suitability as a canonical edition. | Explicit pilot framing, opt-in community review, and no canonical claim without adoption/review. |
| Computational interlingua / MT pivot | Corpus coverage, alignment quality, machine-readable metadata, reproducible training/evaluation artifacts. | Human readability, social authority, or right to act as a language standard. | NLP evaluation plus human/native review for any generated user-facing text. |

## Method Rules for This Project

- Evidence shelves come before translation or revision.
- A term anchor is not a term approval.
- FAIR-style mechanical validation and CARE/TRUST-style authority review answer different questions; do not collapse them.
- Open source means inspectable and forkable, not authorized, accepted, or safe by default.
- For Interslavic/Panslavic work, Latin/Cyrillic sidecars are not optional decoration; they are part of authority and accessibility.
- For Romance bridge ideas, French and Spanish natural-language shelves must stay separate from any later Pan-Romance or Neolatino experiment.
- For Persian-family work, `fa_IR`, `prs_AF`, and `tg_Cyrl_TJ` must remain separate sublanes unless a cross-register rationale and external review justify linking them.
- For Arabic, mathematical register and educational register need native review; local extraction already showed that OCR/text encoding can distort evidence.
- For any proposed educational translation into under-served contexts, the default posture is open-source handoff, not intervention: local users can inspect, reject, fork, correct, or own downstream use.
