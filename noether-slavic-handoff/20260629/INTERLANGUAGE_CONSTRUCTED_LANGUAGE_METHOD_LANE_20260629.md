# Interlanguage and constructed-language method lane - 2026-06-29

This lane is now a core contribution of the Noether multilingual project, not an aside. It belongs under the translation header because it concerns how mathematical register, terminology, authority, and distribution are built when a target is not simply a fully standardized national language with mature technical convention.

## Research questions

- How can AI-assisted workflows construct or stabilize technical registers without pretending to replace native, community, or scholarly authority?
- When does a zonal/interlanguage approach help educational access, and when are parallel translations into existing standards more respectful and useful?
- How should source evidence, term governance, review authority, and open-source ownership be documented for semi-constructed or constructed registers?
- What can the Interslavic/Panslavic lane teach about Romance, Persianate, Turkic, Southeast Asian, or other regional language-family contexts?

## Required discipline

- Keep source witnesses before translation: native mathematical arXiv, university, lecture-note, and open TeX/PDF examples.
- Separate mechanical validation from authority validation. Local scripts can check coverage, scripts, anchors, glossary consistency, and render integrity; they cannot certify living-language acceptability.
- Record term decisions with rationales, alternatives rejected, and community/reviewer status.
- Make script choices explicit, especially for Latin/Cyrillic sidecars, Perso-Arabic/Cyrillic/Latin crosswalks, or any future multi-script register.
- Treat constructed-language artifacts as proposed open materials, not imposed standards.

## Anti-colonial/open-source framing

The educational-translation lane should avoid any claim that outside tooling "solves" a language community. The defensible frame is open-source handoff: source materials, transformation rules, terminology decisions, and validation scripts are inspectable, forkable, correctable, and rejectable by the people who would actually use them.

This matters especially for under-served education contexts. The project may generate useful prototypes, but durable authority must remain with local teachers, writers, technical communities, and language users.

## Publication value

This lane is publishable in its own right as a methodology note alongside the mathematical edition work. It should document:

- Existing zonal/interlanguage projects and what kind of authority each claims.
- Geographic language-family usefulness and limits.
- Educational utility for undergraduate mathematics/physics and open arXiv-style materials.
- Register construction for algebra, invariant theory, number theory, fields, rings, ideals, modules, and representations.
- Distinctions among established-language translation, low-resource technical-register development, semi-constructed standardization, and fully constructed-language experimentation.

## Immediate applications

- Continue the Interslavic/Panslavic Latin+Cyrillic work with explicit authority/review boundaries.
- Consider Romance bridge work only after French/Spanish/native-register evidence shelves are strong.
- Treat Persian/Farsi/Dari/Tajik as a multi-standard, multi-script evidence problem before any unifying proposal.
- Treat Arabic as a register and education-access lane with attention to Modern Standard Arabic, regional educational practice, and mathematical convention.
- Keep speculative constructed-language pilots separate from canonical edition claims unless they receive appropriate review.
